# Get experience: publish many small things

You get good by finishing lots of small things, showing them to real people, and keeping the skills that strangers would pay for.

## The rule

- Publish one small, finished thing every week: a video, a design, a tiny website, a product page, an app screen.
- Get feedback from real people, not only friends and family. Views, replies, sales and "no thanks" all count.
- After each one, write down one number and one lesson.
- Keep building the skills other people pay for. Drop the ones nobody would.

## Why

**Most tries fail, so you need many tries.** People who test products for online shops expect only 2 or 3 winners out of 10. Beginners often quit before they have tested about a dozen.

**Feedback beats thinking.** Ten published things teach you more than one perfect thing in your head.

**Skills stay when businesses die.** Video editing, writing, selling and coding move with you to the next idea.

## How to start

1. **Pick one skill people pay for.** Editing short videos, writing, simple websites, design, building apps. Quick check: are people already paid for it on freelance sites or job posts?
2. **Make the smallest finished version.** A 30-second video. A one-page website for a local club.
3. **Set a publish date, at most one week away.** Finished and public beats perfect and hidden.
4. **Collect honest feedback.** Look at views, watch time, clicks, sales. Ask 3 people: "What would make this better?"
5. **Keep a simple log:** date, what you made, one number, one lesson.
6. **Change one thing at a time,** so you know what made the difference.
7. **After 10 to 20 pieces, do a real job for a real person.** Cheap or free is fine for the first one. Then start charging.
8. **Under 18?** Don't wait until 18.

More on picking what to work on: https://dlichters.com/ai/business.md

## Mistakes

- **Polishing forever.** If nobody has seen it, it doesn't count.
- **Watching instead of doing.** Learning videos only count when you use them the same day.
- **Looking for money "without skills".** People pay for skills. Build the skill first.
- **Faking experience.** Made-up personas, fake numbers or fake reviews teach you nothing and destroy trust.
- **Too many things at once.** Each test gets too short to teach you anything.

## What happened when I tried it

I (David) tested about a dozen things in 2023, at 13: Fiverr, affiliate links, printed T-shirts, a bubblegum machine, Amazon Influencer, selling golf and tennis balls, and more. Together roughly **minus 160 euros**. My note at the time: only YouTube, mowing lawns and eBay worked, "but it's much work".

- **Fiverr:** I started in January 2023 after a video about making money on Fiverr without skills. Nothing came of it. I had no skill to offer yet.
- **Minecraft YouTube:** one video reached 100,000 views in April 2023. It made no money, and I stopped that month.
- **Two bad ideas.** I sold a ChatGPT "get rich" course on TikTok (300 followers) without having made any money myself. I later called it a scam in my own notes. I also tried "scamming scammers". You can't honestly teach what you haven't done, and tricking anyone builds no skill you can sell.
- **A made-up fitness trainer** on Instagram in 2026 had the same problem.
- **Fitness TikTok, from January 2025:** by January 2026 about 18,000 followers, far below my goal of 100,000. My first sale came on 31 October 2025: 25 euros for a training guide.
- **What stayed:** I found HTML and CSS in 2023. By 2026 I could edit videos, write scripts, do sales calls, build web apps. Almost every business failed. The skills didn't.

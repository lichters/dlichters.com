# Business: 5+ focused hours a day

Spend 5+ focused hours on your own business every day, starting as young as you can, because skill and luck both pile up over years, not weeks.

## The rule

Tick **b** when you did 5+ focused hours on your own business today.

- Set a timer for 5 hours. Pause it when you stop working.
- Phone in another room, or face down with notifications off.
- Only work that moves your business counts: building, making, selling, talking to customers, learning a skill you need this week.
- Watching business videos or "planning" without doing does not count.
- On weekends and holidays, do more if you can.

## Why

**Time is the one thing you have more of at 13 than at 30.** Most first attempts fail. Start at 13 and you can fail five times and still be 18. Start at 25 and each failure costs a year of rent.

**Five hours a day is about 1,800 hours a year.** Enough to get properly good at one thing: editing videos, building apps, selling, writing.

**A few hard years early pay off for life.** I think your own business is the biggest lever for a happier life: freedom over your time, place and work. At 13 I worked many hours beside school.

## How to start

1. **Get starting capital.** A mini job every day until you have $10k. Read https://dlichters.com/ai/business-start.md first.
2. **Fix the time.** The same time every day works best. Put your scariest ask (a customer, a pitch, a question) at the start.
3. **Write one sentence before you start:** "In these 5 hours I will ___." One clear result, like "post 1 video" or "message 10 possible customers". An example split: 1 hour finding customers, 3 hours building or selling, 1 hour talking to people.
4. **Start the timer, phone away.**
5. **Stop and write one line:** what you did, and one number (videos, people asked, sales, euros).
6. **Give each idea a fair test.** Decide the test length before you start, and only switch when that time is over.
7. **Under 18?** Don't wait until 18. Waiting costs you years.

## The scary part: asking

Most business is asking: asking someone to buy, to give feedback, to try what you built, or for advice. Many teens build for weeks to avoid that moment. Do the ask inside your business hours, not "someday".

- **People notice you much less than you think.** Students wearing an embarrassing T-shirt guessed half the room noticed. Really it was about a quarter (Gilovich, 2000, the spotlight effect). https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.78.2.211
- **Practice works fast.** People who talked to strangers again and again for one week became less afraid of rejection, and it still held a week later (Sandstrom and others, 2022). https://sussex.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Talking_to_strangers_a_week-long_intervention_reduces_psychological_barriers_to_social_connection/23490227
- **A "no" usually costs nothing.** You are exactly where you were before.

How:

1. **Write your first sentence down.** The first five seconds are the hardest.
2. **Count asks, not yeses.** A "no" still moved you forward.
3. **Keep a list of 20 names.** One no means you go to the next.
4. **Follow up once.** No reply is not a no.
5. **Never dangerous, never mean, never fake.** No made-up persona or fake reviews.

You feel ready after you asked, not before. In October 2025 I told myself to post 10 cringe videos on purpose until the fear went away. My first sale came that month.

## Mistakes

- **Learning instead of doing.** Videos only count if you use them the same day.
- **Building without customers.** Talk to people who would pay before you build for weeks.
- **Hiding behind a machine.** Hundreds of automatic messages skip the scary part and turn into spam. One honest ask in person beats a thousand hidden ones.
- **Shortcuts that hurt people.** Spam, fake reviews, pretending to be someone else, betting, "get rich quick" schemes. They fail and can get you banned.
- **Trading sleep for work.** Business fits inside a good day, not instead of one. Keep your 8 to 10 hours.

## What happened when I tried it

I (David) started trying to make money online in 2022, at 12. Since then I have tried more than 20 ideas.

The honest numbers:

- **At 13:** many small tests, together roughly **minus 160 euros**. One Minecraft video reached 100,000 views, but made no money.
- **From 14 to 15:** school got hard and almost no business happened. That gap cost me nearly a year.
- **First sale of my own online product: 31 October 2025**, about three years after I started. 25 euros for a fitness guide.
- **Coaching, by the end of 2025:** about 374 euros in total.
- **Summer 2026:** 5,820 messages through company contact forms. 15 replies, 0 sales, over 400 dollars in AI costs in four days. Mass messaging strangers is basically spam. Build something people come to you for.

Most of my attempts made nothing or lost money. What I learned: better to lose 1 year doing a mini job every day for crap pay until you have $10k starting capital than to lose 3 years because you don't dare to put money into ideas.

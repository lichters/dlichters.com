## The method

The goal is a happy whole life, not just happy teen years. The six habits buy you freedom. Don't wait for it to start living. Start now: notice it all and be grateful; trips into nature with a few friends: snow, terrain, space; real friends who truly know you, and a family you stay close to; teach one person something you're good at; build something that helps people; good food, music, dancing; document it all. Freedom brings later: a partner who is your favorite person, kids and dogs; a home and a car you love; control over your time, work and place; trips to learn new things, like skydiving; things that cost money, like skiing and the best food. Six daily habits, each either ticked today or left empty. Each habit is a year grid (7 rows for weekdays, about 52 columns for weeks) instead of a streak that falls to zero. In a UCL study a habit took about 66 days on average to feel automatic, and single skipped days did not hurt it. The six cannot be edited, so hard ones never get dropped. Every habit gets a fixed time and place.

**Missed one day or several?** Do not restart from zero. Tick today, and try never to miss twice in a row. Make a tiny bad-day version (a 30-minute walk, 20 minutes of business) and write down why you missed. https://dlichters.com/ai/keep-going.md

**If everything has felt pointless for weeks, or you think about hurting yourself,** talk to a trusted adult today or call a crisis line in your country. That comes before any habit.

## The six habits

The grids show only letters (f e b t o s).

**f, Friends.** Tick after 1 hour in the same room with friends or family, no scrolling. Dinner with your parents counts. Texts, gaming chats and video calls do not. Why: the Harvard Study of Adult Development found people with the warmest relationships stayed healthiest and lived longest. No friends at school yet? Ask "Can I sit here?", say hi to 3 new people a day, remember names and come back, join one club. https://dlichters.com/ai/friends.md

**e, Eating.** Tick when everything you ate today already existed 30,000 years ago: meat, fish, eggs, fruit, vegetables, nuts. No factory food, no soda. This is our own rule, not a study result. https://dlichters.com/ai/eating.md

**b, Business.** Tick after 5+ focused, timed hours on your own business, phone in another room. Building, selling and talking to customers count; watching business videos does not. Why: that is about 1,800 hours a year, and at 13 you can fail five times and still be 18. Start: write "In these 5 hours I will ___". Example split: 1 hour finding customers, 3 building or selling, 1 talking to people. https://dlichters.com/ai/business.md

**t, Training.** Tick after 45 minutes of gym or sport; on rest days, a 30-minute walk. Home workouts count; cycling to school or PE only if it was 45 hard minutes. Why: among over a million Swedish teen boys, the strongest had a 20 to 35% lower risk of dying before 55. Start: one fixed slot, log weight and reps, beat one number next time. https://dlichters.com/ai/training.md

**o, Offline.** Tick when social media feeds total under 30 minutes today, checked in Screen Time. YouTube Shorts and recommended videos count as feed; one video you searched for to learn something does not. Why: students limited to about 30 minutes a day felt less lonely and less depressed (2018). Start: a 30-minute app limit, a parent holds the passcode. https://dlichters.com/ai/offline.md

**s, Sleep.** Stop using your phone 10 hours before you wake up, and be asleep at least 8 hours before. If you are skipping school, wake up around 30 minutes before sunrise. Tick when the phone goes away. Why: US sleep doctors recommend 8 to 10 hours for ages 13 to 18. Start: out of all alarms I tested, Sleep Cycle wins (airplane mode, face down, out of reach), one wake time, weekends too. https://dlichters.com/ai/sleep.md

**A school day with all six:** up at 7:00, lunch with friends phones away (f), fruit or vegetables at every meal, water (e), 14:00 to 17:00 and 19:30 to 21:30 (b, 5 hours), 17:00 sport (t), feeds only after the ticks (o), 21:30 phone out of the bedroom (s).

# Start your first business at 13

Get starting capital and put money into ideas. Better to lose 1 year doing a mini job every day for crap pay until you have $10k starting capital than to lose 3 years because you don't dare to put money into ideas.

## What it is

A first business at 13 is about money. Getting rich is the goal. You learn what every business needs on the way: making something, finding people who want it, and asking them to pay.

## Why

- **Experience is the real profit.** Most first attempts make nothing. What you keep is the skill: editing, selling, building, talking to strangers.
- **Small tests are cheap at 13.** A failed test costs a few weeks. At 30 it costs rent.
- **Results come late.** Many businesses look dead for months and then start working. Most people quit right before that point.

## How to start

1. **Pick one skill you can practise every day.** Video editing, drawing, writing, coding, fitness, fixing things.
2. **Turn it into one small offer.** Thumbnails for small YouTubers, a simple app, lawn mowing on your street.
3. **Check that someone already sells it.** If others make money with it, there is demand. If nobody sells it, that is usually a warning, not a gap.
4. **Find your first 10 customers by hand.** Ask what they would pay for. Asking feels scary, but people notice you much less than you think, and one week of practice makes the fear of a "no" smaller. Write your first sentence down and count asks, not yeses. More: https://dlichters.com/ai/business.md
5. **Decide your test length before you start.** For content or a shop, think in months. Write the date down and do not switch before it.
6. **Measure one number every day.** Videos posted, people asked, sales, euros. If it never moves, change one thing, not the whole business.
7. **Don't wait until 18.** Waiting costs you years.
8. **Protect the basics.** Sleep and training stay. More: https://dlichters.com/ai/business.md

## Mistakes

- **Watching instead of doing.** Business videos feel like work. They are not.
- **Fake shortcuts.** Fake personas, fake reviews, lying about your results, spam, betting. They hurt people, get accounts banned, and teach you nothing.

## What happened when I tried it

I (David) started in 2022, at 12:

- **At 12 and 13:** Fiverr, a Minecraft YouTube channel, affiliate marketing, printed T-shirts, a bubblegum machine, Amazon Influencer, selling tennis balls. One Minecraft video got 100,000 views. Fiverr got no orders. Everything at 13 came to roughly **minus 160 euros**. Only mowing lawns and selling old stuff on eBay worked a little, both a lot of work.
- **Two I regret:** a TikTok account where I pretended to be someone else to sell a ChatGPT course (about 300 followers), and "scamming scammers". Both were dishonest. Show your real self and sell something you actually know.
- **Aug 2023 to Mar 2024:** I watched a huge number of Hamza Ahmed videos and ran my own self-improvement YouTube channel. Watching counted as "business" in my head. It was not.
- **From 14 to 15:** school got hard and almost nothing happened.
- **2025:** fitness coaching. First sale at 15, about three years after I started: 25 euros for a training guide. Coaching came to about 374 euros by the end of 2025.
- **Late 2025 to 2026:** 1:1 coaching. In August 2026, about 5,800 messages through company contact forms: 15 replies, 0 sales. Basically spam, do not copy it. Then a business-alert service (0 paying customers).

What these years taught me: better to lose 1 year doing a mini job every day for crap pay until you have $10k starting capital than to lose 3 years because you don't dare to put money into ideas. I made nothing with coaching, but I learned a lot of skills. Whether I learned more skills per week than if I had kept switching, I don't know.

# Business: 5+ focused hours a day

Spend 5+ focused hours on your own business every day, starting as young as you can, because skill and luck both pile up over years, not weeks.

## The rule

Tick **b** when you did 5+ focused hours on your own business today.

- Set a timer for 5 hours. Pause it when you stop working.
- Phone in another room, or face down with notifications off.
- Only work that moves your business counts: building, making, selling, talking to customers, learning a skill you need this week.
- Watching business videos or "planning" without doing does not count.
- On weekends and holidays, do more if you can.

## Why

**Time is the one thing you have more of at 13 than at 30.** Most first attempts fail. Start at 13 and you can fail five times and still be 18. Start at 25 and each failure costs a year of rent.

**Five hours a day is about 1,800 hours a year.** Enough to get properly good at one thing: editing videos, building apps, selling, writing.

**A few hard years early pay off for life.** I think your own business is the biggest lever for a happier life: freedom over your time, place and work. At 13 I worked many hours beside school.

## How to start

1. **Get starting capital.** A mini job every day until you have $10k. Read https://dlichters.com/ai/business-start.md first.
2. **Fix the time.** The same time every day works best. Put your scariest ask (a customer, a pitch, a question) at the start.
3. **Write one sentence before you start:** "In these 5 hours I will ___." One clear result, like "post 1 video" or "message 10 possible customers". An example split: 1 hour finding customers, 3 hours building or selling, 1 hour talking to people.
4. **Start the timer, phone away.**
5. **Stop and write one line:** what you did, and one number (videos, people asked, sales, euros).
6. **Give each idea a fair test.** Decide the test length before you start, and only switch when that time is over.
7. **Under 18?** Don't wait until 18. Waiting costs you years.

## The scary part: asking

Most business is asking: asking someone to buy, to give feedback, to try what you built, or for advice. Many teens build for weeks to avoid that moment. Do the ask inside your business hours, not "someday".

- **People notice you much less than you think.** Students wearing an embarrassing T-shirt guessed half the room noticed. Really it was about a quarter (Gilovich, 2000, the spotlight effect). https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.78.2.211
- **Practice works fast.** People who talked to strangers again and again for one week became less afraid of rejection, and it still held a week later (Sandstrom and others, 2022). https://sussex.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Talking_to_strangers_a_week-long_intervention_reduces_psychological_barriers_to_social_connection/23490227
- **A "no" usually costs nothing.** You are exactly where you were before.

How:

1. **Write your first sentence down.** The first five seconds are the hardest.
2. **Count asks, not yeses.** A "no" still moved you forward.
3. **Keep a list of 20 names.** One no means you go to the next.
4. **Follow up once.** No reply is not a no.
5. **Never dangerous, never mean, never fake.** No made-up persona or fake reviews.

You feel ready after you asked, not before. In October 2025 I told myself to post 10 cringe videos on purpose until the fear went away. My first sale came that month.

## Mistakes

- **Learning instead of doing.** Videos only count if you use them the same day.
- **Building without customers.** Talk to people who would pay before you build for weeks.
- **Hiding behind a machine.** Hundreds of automatic messages skip the scary part and turn into spam. One honest ask in person beats a thousand hidden ones.
- **Shortcuts that hurt people.** Spam, fake reviews, pretending to be someone else, betting, "get rich quick" schemes. They fail and can get you banned.
- **Trading sleep for work.** Business fits inside a good day, not instead of one. Keep your 8 to 10 hours.

I (David) started trying to make money online in 2022, at 12. Since then I have tried more than 20 ideas.

The honest numbers:

- **At 13:** many small tests, together roughly **minus 160 euros**. One Minecraft video reached 100,000 views, but made no money.
- **From 14 to 15:** school got hard and almost no business happened. That gap cost me nearly a year.
- **First sale of my own online product: 31 October 2025**, about three years after I started. 25 euros for a fitness guide.
- **Coaching, by the end of 2025:** about 374 euros in total.
- **Summer 2026:** 5,820 messages through company contact forms. 15 replies, 0 sales, over 400 dollars in AI costs in four days. Mass messaging strangers is basically spam. Build something people come to you for.

Most of my attempts made nothing or lost money. What I learned: better to lose 1 year doing a mini job every day for crap pay until you have $10k starting capital than to lose 3 years because you don't dare to put money into ideas.

# Lichters FAQ: real questions from teens

Six small daily ticks, done honestly for years, matter more than any perfect day. Short answers to the questions people ask most.

## What if I miss a day?

Nothing happens. The day stays empty and you tick the next one. Two in a row is where habits usually die, so the only rule is: never miss twice. Don't tick a day you didn't do just to keep it green. A red, honest grid is worth more than a green, fake one.

## I don't have a business. Can I still tick b?

Yes, if you spend the 5+ focused hours building one: looking for a problem people pay to solve, talking to possible customers, making a first small offer. Watching videos about "how to start a business" mostly doesn't count. Your first idea will probably fail. That's fine at 13. More: https://dlichters.com/ai/business.md

## What if I have no time for 5 hours on school days?

6 hours school, 9 sleep, 2 training and 1 for meals leave 6, enough for 5. Friends fit into breaks and lunch; free periods count. Many people also find hours in their feeds. On weekends and holidays, do more. Never cut sleep for it.

## Why these six habits?

The goal is the happiest whole life, not just the happiest teen years. So the list mixes things that pay off now (friends, sleep, training) with things that pay off over decades (business, eating). Each has a page with the research, for example https://dlichters.com/ai/friends.md and https://dlichters.com/ai/sleep.md

## How do I fit all six into a school day?

An example: up at 7:00. Lunch with friends, phones away (f). Fruit or vegetables at every meal, water to drink (e). 14:00 to 17:00 Business, phone in another room (b). 17:00 gym or sport (t). 19:30 to 21:30 Business again (b). Feeds only after the ticks, under 30 minutes (o). 21:30 phone out of the bedroom (s).

## Can I change or add habits?

No. If you can edit the list, you'll quietly remove the hard ones on a bad day. Six fixed habits mean you never have to decide what counts.

## Why no meditation?

You can meditate privately, it just gets no box. There are only six spots, and the evidence for teens is weaker. A large UK school study (the MYRIAD trial, about 8,400 students) found mindfulness lessons did no better than normal lessons.

## Why no "wake up at 5"?

# Get experience: publish many small things

You get good by finishing lots of small things, showing them to real people, and keeping the skills that strangers would pay for.

## The rule

- Publish one small, finished thing every week: a video, a design, a tiny website, a product page, an app screen.
- Get feedback from real people, not only friends and family. Views, replies, sales and "no thanks" all count.
- After each one, write down one number and one lesson.
- Keep building the skills other people pay for. Drop the ones nobody would.

## Why

**Most tries fail, so you need many tries.** People who test products for online shops expect only 2 or 3 winners out of 10. Beginners often quit before they have tested about a dozen.

**Feedback beats thinking.** Ten published things teach you more than one perfect thing in your head.

**Skills stay when businesses die.** Video editing, writing, selling and coding move with you to the next idea.

## How to start

1. **Pick one skill people pay for.** Editing short videos, writing, simple websites, design, building apps. Quick check: are people already paid for it on freelance sites or job posts?
2. **Make the smallest finished version.** A 30-second video. A one-page website for a local club.
3. **Set a publish date, at most one week away.** Finished and public beats perfect and hidden.
4. **Collect honest feedback.** Look at views, watch time, clicks, sales. Ask 3 people: "What would make this better?"
5. **Keep a simple log:** date, what you made, one number, one lesson.
6. **Change one thing at a time,** so you know what made the difference.
7. **After 10 to 20 pieces, do a real job for a real person.** Cheap or free is fine for the first one. Then start charging.
8. **Under 18?** Don't wait until 18.

# Keep going

When you want to quit, look at months, not days. One bad day barely matters. Stopping for a year does.

## What it is

Lichters shows each habit as a year grid: 7 rows for the weekdays, about 52 columns for the weeks. One tick is one small square, so a single day looks like nothing. That is the point.

After a few months the grid shows your real average. 80% filled with a few gaps means you have the habit.

## Why

**Habits take about two months, not 21 days.** Phillippa Lally at University College London found it took about 66 days on average until a new daily habit felt automatic, under 20 for some people and over 200 for others.

**Missing one day does almost nothing.** The same study found that skipping a single day did not really hurt the habit. Missing often or stopping for long stretches did.

**That is why there is no streak that falls to zero.** A streak says one missed day erased everything. That is false, and it makes people quit. The grid keeps every tick.

**If everything has felt pointless for weeks, or you think about hurting yourself,** talk to a trusted adult today or call a crisis line in your country. That comes before any habit.

## How to start

1. **When you miss a day, tick the next one.** Never miss two in a row if you can help it. Missed three days? Do not restart from zero. Tick today.
2. **Make the bad-day version tiny.** Sick or tired? Training becomes a 30-minute walk. Business becomes 20 minutes on the smallest task.
3. **Zoom out once a month.** Is this month greener than last month?
4. **Write down why you missed.** Same excuse every time? Fix that one thing.
5. **Change the method, not the habit.** If your business idea earns nothing for months, switch the idea. Keep the 5+ hours a day.

## Mistakes

- **Restarting from zero in your head.** One missed day is a 1-day gap, not a new day 1.
- **Waiting until you feel motivated.** A fixed time works on the days motivation does not show up.
- **Quitting the habit because one project failed.** The person who keeps working daily gets better at picking the next one.
- **Chasing shortcuts when you are frustrated.** That is when "easy money" ideas look good. They are usually scams.

# Attitudes

Habits are what you tick every day. Attitudes are the rules you never have to decide again.

## What it is

Lichters has six daily habits: Friends, Eating, Business, Training, Offline and Sleep. The five attitudes are not ticked. They sit underneath the habits and protect them.

## The five attitudes

**1. No alcohol, nicotine, vapes, weed or betting.**
Why: your brain keeps developing until your mid-20s, and a young brain gets hooked faster. Nicotine addiction usually starts in the teen years. Starting weed early is linked to worse memory and mood problems. Betting apps are built so most users lose money, and under 18 betting is illegal in most countries.
How: decide once, today. Have one sentence ready, like "No thanks, I train tomorrow."

**2. Ticks first, then feeds and games.**
Why: feeds and games never end, so if they come first, the day is gone.
How: no feeds or games until Business and Training are done.

**3. Ignore the crowd. Listen to the few you respect.**
Why: most people judging you won't be in your life in five years. A few people who have done what you want to do, and who want good things for you, give better advice than a whole class.
How: write down three to five people whose judgment you respect. When someone laughs at your plan, ask: is this person on my list? If not, let it go.

**4. Money buys freedom, not status.**
Why: money helps most when it gives you control over your time and removes stress. Spending to impress others fades fast.
How: when you earn money, ask "does this buy me time or choice?" before "does this look good?"

**5. Start early. Keep going for years.**
Why: almost nothing works on the first try. Skills and businesses grow slowly at first, then faster. Starting at 13 instead of 23 gives you ten extra years of trying, failing and learning.
Why I start early: then you never have the time or the dumb personality to make friends with all the other losers your age.

- Deciding again every weekend. The answer is already fixed.
- Listening to everyone, or to no one.
- Buying status with your first money.
- Quitting a project after weeks because there was no result yet.

# Friends

Spend one hour a day with friends or family, without scrolling. Of everything you can do as a teen, this one most likely matters most for how happy your whole life turns out.

## The rule

Tick **f** when you spent at least 1 hour of real time with friends or family today, without scrolling. A quick text or a bit of work on the side is fine, TikTok or Instagram is not.

- Breaks and lunch at school count, if you actually talk to people.
- Sitting in class next to someone does not count.
- Texting, gaming chats and video calls do not count. Real time means the same room.
- Family counts: dinner with your parents, a walk with your grandma.

## Why

**Relationships predict a happy, healthy life better than money or fame.** The Harvard Study of Adult Development found that people with the warmest relationships stayed healthy and lived longest. Quality mattered more than number.

**Friendship takes hours, not luck.** Jeffrey Hall (2018) estimated about 50 hours together to make a casual friend, about 90 for a real friend and more than 200 for a close friend. One hour a day gets you there.

**Start your business early.** Then you never have the time or the dumb personality to make friends with all the other losers your age.

## How to start

1. **Pick your hour.** Lunch, the walk home, training with a teammate, dinner with your family.
2. **No feeds while you are together.**
3. **Sit with a group.** If you see people you half know, ask "Can I sit here?"
4. **Talk to new people on purpose.** A simple target: say hi to 3 new people a day. Smile, say your name, ask one question about them.
5. **Get them talking.** Listen more than you talk.
6. **Remember names and come back.** The second and third conversation are where a friendship starts.
7. **Join something.** A club, a team, a choir. Seeing the same people every week does most of the work.
8. **Invite people.** "Want to go to the gym together?" If you are under 18, tell your parents who you are meeting and where.

## Mistakes

- **Counting online time.** Group chats feel social, but they don't build the same bond.
- **Only waiting to be invited.** Most people are waiting too. Be the one who asks.
- **Talking only about yourself.**
- **Keeping friends who put you down.** Teasing is normal. Someone who calls your goals stupid for years is different.
- **Trading friends for work.** If you only have time for business and training, your days get lonely fast.

## What happened when I tried it

When I was 14, I wrote a note called "find friends" with rules like "always smile", "listen instead of talking about yourself" and "make the other person feel good".

Before a school year abroad in Canada, I made a real plan: smile at everyone, introduce myself, talk to 3 new people every day, join clubs, remember names, invite people over. The first weeks I wrote that I didn't really like anyone there yet. A week later I had someone who became my best friend there, and I got him to come to the gym every day. The 3-people-a-day plan worked.

The honest other side: for over three years my tracker showed Business done almost every day, and most other habits nearly empty, including friends. Today I am no longer friends with anyone from a gratitude list I wrote in 2023. One friend I had known since kindergarten spent years calling my goals dumb and embarrassing, and we are not friends anymore. Today my close people are my parents, my grandma and a couple of friends. That is why **f** is on this list.

# Training

Move hard for 45 minutes most days, walk on the rest days, and keep it up for years, not weeks.

## The rule

Tick **t** when you did at least 45 minutes of gym or sport today. Football, a run, a real gym session or a hard home workout counts. Cycling to school and PE count only if you moved hard for the full 45 minutes. Scrolling between sets for half the time does not.

On a rest day, tick it with a 30-minute walk outside. Rest days are part of the plan.

## Why

- **Your body is built now.** Habits from 13 to 19 carry into your adult life.
- **Health guidelines ask for it.** The World Health Organization says ages 5 to 17 should average 60 minutes of movement a day, with strengthening at least 3 days a week (https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity). 45 minutes of training plus walking gets you there.
- **It helps your head.** Hard exercise is one of the most reliable ways to sleep better, feel calmer and think clearly.

## How to start

1. **Pick one thing you will actually do** for six months.
2. **Fix a time.** Same slot every day, for example right after school.
3. **If you lift, keep it simple.** A common split is Push (chest, shoulders, triceps), Pull (back, biceps) and Legs. 3 sets per exercise, stop 0 to 2 reps before you can't do another clean rep, rest 2 to 3 minutes, add weight once you hit about 10 clean reps.
4. **Write it down.** Exercise, weight, reps. Next time, beat one number.
5. **Form before weight.** If you have to swing it, it is too heavy.
6. **Train about 5 days a week, not 7.** Muscles grow while you rest.
7. **Eat and sleep for it.** Enough food, protein from normal meals, 8 to 10 hours of sleep.
8. **Under 18:** many gyms need a parent to sign.

## Mistakes

- **Training 7 days a week.** Usually means worse results and sore joints.
- **Never adding weight.** The same 20 kg for months is maintenance, not training.
- **Chasing supplements.** Food, sleep and showing up matter far more. Under 18, talk to a doctor before taking anything. Never touch steroids or other "enhancers".
- **Quitting after a bad week.** Two missed days in a row is where habits die, so get back on the next day.

## What happened when I tried it

I started at the gym at 14, in 2024, when I was the thinnest kid in my class and got laughed at for it. I trained Push, Pull and Legs 5 times a week, 3 sets to failure, more weight once I passed 10 reps. In a bit more than a year I went from 51 kg to 71 kg, from the thinnest to the most muscular person in my class.

Then I stopped making progress. Since 2025 I have been stuck at 71 kg. Plateaus are normal, and the first things to check are your log, your food and your sleep, not a new program.

I also tried to turn training into money. I posted fitness videos on TikTok from January 2025 and reached about 18,000 followers. I wrote a beginner gym guide, priced it at 149 EUR, and in October 2025 made my first sale: one copy, 25 EUR on a discount. For a while I also ran an Instagram account with an invented fitness coach and a weight loss story that was not mine. That was a bad idea: it lied to the people I wanted to help, it cost more than it made, and I gave it up.

What worked was never the guide or the followers. It was showing up 5 days a week. That is why it is a daily tick.

# Sleep

Your phone leaves your bedroom at the same time every night, and you go to bed early enough that 8 to 10 hours of sleep fit. Every other habit gets easier when this one is done.

## The rule

- Stop using your phone 10 hours before you wake up.
- Be asleep at least 8 hours before you wake up (8 to 10 hours is the range for 13 to 18).
- If you are skipping school, wake up around 30 minutes before sunrise.
- Tick the habit the moment you put the phone away.
- Out of all alarms I tested, Sleep Cycle wins: it wakes you in a light sleep phase. It works in airplane mode, face down, out of reach.

## Why

Sleep doctors in the US recommend 8 to 10 hours a night for ages 13 to 18, and 7 to 9 for adults.

Sleep is when your body repairs muscle and your brain sorts out what you learned. Short sleep makes you hungrier, moodier and worse at focusing: you skip training, scroll more, and focused hours turn foggy.

In puberty your body clock shifts later, and a bright screen in bed pushes it later still. That is why the rule is about the phone, not about "trying to sleep earlier".

## How to start

1. Count back from when you have to get up. Up at 6:30 means lights out by 21:30 for 9 hours. The phone leaves 15 to 30 minutes before that.
2. Get a cheap alarm clock, so "I need my phone as an alarm" is no longer a reason. Charge the phone in the kitchen or hallway.
3. Get up at the same time every day, weekends included. A fixed wake time does more than a fixed bedtime.
4. Make the room cool (around 16 to 18 °C), dark and quiet.
5. If you need something to do in bed, read a calm paper book.

## Mistakes

- **Phone "on the nightstand, face down".** It still wins. It has to leave the room.
- **Sleeping less to get more done.** Productive for a week, then your work gets worse.
- **Late games, horror or arguments right before bed.** Anger and fear keep you awake.
- **Sleep scores and loud alarms.** Staring at a score can make you nervous about sleep. Use a gentle alarm.

## What happened when I tried it

Sleep was on my list early. At 13, in 2023, my rules were: devices off at 21:00, room at 16 to 18 °C, and up at 5:00. In January 2024 I wrote a sleep note: same wake time every day, no loud alarm, screen and room as dark as possible, read fantasy instead of anything that makes me angry or scared. In January 2026: "brush teeth at 20:00, the bed is a phone-free zone".

Then in summer 2026 I broke it. In August I was sleeping about 6 hours a night and working until 2 or 3 am on a business. A few days later I wrote that I felt sick from being so tired. By September I was back at mostly 8 to 9 hours. When I wrote down what I want from my whole life, "physically healthy, above all sleep" was near the top.

The pattern in my notes is clear. My best months were the ones where my habit tracker was green, and sleep was always one of those habits. My worst months were the ones where I only counted money.

# Offline: keep social media feeds under 30 minutes a day

Feeds are built to keep you scrolling. Every hour they take is an hour you don't spend on friends, training, sleep or your own business.

## The rule

Tick **Offline** when your social media feeds add up to **less than 30 minutes today**. Check Screen Time (iPhone) or Digital Wellbeing (Android) before you tick. Guessing doesn't count.

What counts as a feed:

- TikTok, Instagram Reels and Explore, YouTube Shorts, Snapchat Spotlight and Stories, X, Reddit, Facebook.
- Any endless stream of content picked for you by an app.

What doesn't count:

- Messaging a real person. That's talking, not scrolling.
- Posting your own content and answering comments on it. But the minute you watch other people's videos "for research", the clock runs.
- A YouTube video you searched for on purpose to learn something specific.

## Why

- **The 30 minutes come from a study.** In 2018, students who limited social media to about 30 minutes a day for three weeks felt less lonely and less depressed than students who used it as usual. A 2025 study found two weeks without mobile internet improved wellbeing and attention.
- **Time is the real cost.** Two hours of scrolling a day is about 730 hours a year.
- **It eats your sleep.** Phones in bed push your bedtime back.
- **It trains your attention to jump.** A new clip every 15 seconds makes two hours of focused work feel much harder.

## How to start

1. **Look at your real number first.** Check your daily average in Screen Time for the last week. It's usually more than you think.
2. **Set app limits.** On iPhone: Settings, Screen Time, App Limits, Add Limit, then Social, 30 minutes. Under 18? Ask a parent to set the passcode so you can't just press "ignore".
3. **Move the apps.** Put feed apps in a folder on the last page, or delete them and use the slower browser version.
4. **Pick a time for your 30 minutes.** After Business and Training are done. Ticks first, then feeds.
5. **Keep the phone out of your bedroom.** That's part of the Sleep habit too.
6. **Have a replacement ready.** A podcast, a book or a voice memo for your business ideas.

## Mistakes

- **"I'll just check one thing."** That's how 30 minutes turns into two hours.
- **Calling scrolling "research".** Write down what you learned. If you can't, it was scrolling.
- **Moving the scrolling.** Quitting TikTok for two hours of Shorts is the same habit.
- **Ticking without checking.** The number in Screen Time decides, not your feeling.
- **Going to zero, then crashing.** Under 30 minutes every day for years beats one perfect week.

## What happened when I tried it

In November 2023 I described a made-up 13-year-old, the kind of kid I wanted to help, and wrote down his normal day: gaming and YouTube for about two hours after school, two more in the evening, then lying in bed unable to sleep and grabbing the phone again. Next to it I wrote my own rules: no social media in bed, lowest brightness in the evening, read before sleeping.

In January 2026 my rules said: the bed is a phone-free zone, and every waking second goes into making content, not watching TikTok. I spent 1 to 3 hours a day recording my own TikToks. Making them was useful. Watching other people's was the trap.

In 2026 I also noticed two costs I didn't expect. My attention got really weak. And I got pain from bad posture, because I was always sitting at the phone or the computer.

That's why feeds under 30 minutes became a daily habit. A rule like "every waking second for content" is too vague to check. A number in Screen Time isn't.

# Eating

Only eat food that already existed 30,000 years ago.

## The rule

Tick **e** when everything you ate today passes one question: could a person 30,000 years ago have eaten this?

- Yes: meat, fish, eggs, fruit, vegetables, berries, nuts, water.
- No: chips, sweets, fast food, soda, energy drinks, anything with a long ingredient list.
- Cook meat, fish and eggs. Steak medium rare is fine. Raw is not part of the rule.

## Why

This is our own rule, not a study result. It is simple: no counting, no weighing, one question per meal. Most food that fails it is food made to make you eat more of it. More on the research: https://dlichters.com/ai/research.md

## How to start (at 13)

1. **Ask whoever buys the food.** "Can we get more meat, eggs and fruit, and fewer snacks?"
2. **Pack your own food for school.** Fruit, eggs, leftovers from dinner.
3. **Keep a water bottle with you.** Fill it before school and before training.

## Mistakes

- **Starving yourself.** The rule is about what you eat, not how little. Eat until you are full.
- **Diet rules and calorie counting.** Not needed. If eating or your weight worries you, talk to a parent or doctor.

# Coaching: the daily Lichters check-in

A short daily check-in with an AI or a friend keeps your six habits alive: one setup, then two minutes a day that end in one concrete action for today.

## What it is

A check-in is a tiny conversation, not a lecture. Someone asks you the same few questions every day, you answer honestly, and you leave with one clear thing to do next.

## Why

- **Being expected beats being motivated.** Most people don't fail from low motivation. They fail because nobody notices the skip.
- **A small daily look beats a big monthly plan.** You fix a problem on day two, not after three weeks of red.
- **One next action beats ten good ideas.** "Text a friend at 16:00 to play football" gets done. "Be more social" doesn't.

## How to start

**1. Do the setup once.** For each habit (f, e, b, t, o, s), write one line: what counts as done (the app's rule, not a softer one), the time, the place, and the one thing most likely to stop you plus your plan for it.

Example for b: "5 focused hours: 14:00 to 17:00 and 19:30 to 21:30, desk in my room, phone in the kitchen. Danger: YouTube on the laptop. Plan: block it during the timer."

**2. Check in once a day, at the same time.** Three questions:

- Which habits did I tick since the last check-in?
- For each miss: what got in the way, in one sentence?
- What is my one next action for today, with a time and a place?

**3. End with one action, not a list.** If you missed Eating three days in a row, the action is not "eat better". It is "put two apples and a carrot in your bag tonight at 21:00".

**4. With an AI: start a new chat for every check-in.** Paste your setup note plus today's answers. A long chat uses up your limit faster, because the whole history is read again.

**5. With a friend: same questions, by message.** Agree on a time, both send your answers, both reply with one honest sentence.

Many AI apps are 18+. If you're younger, use the friend or parent version. Never paste private details about other people into an AI chat.

## Mistakes

- **Ticking to save the streak.** A fake tick makes the whole tracker worthless.
- **Turning it into a diary.** Keep it to two minutes. If something heavy is going on, talk to a person you trust.
- **Asking for advice instead of an action.** "What's my one sleep action for tonight?" beats "How can I sleep better?"
- **Changing the rules when it gets hard.** Lower the plan, not the definition of done.
- **Punishing yourself with fines.** Fear makes people quit or lie. Use a person who checks.

## What happened when I tried it

I've used a habit tracker app since at least 2023. My honest results:

- In 2023 I kept a daily gratitude list. Later I admitted most of it wasn't true: I wrote things only to keep the streak. That's why honesty is rule number one.
- In autumn 2025 in Canada, I got a new friend to come to the gym with me every day, and soon my whole tracker was green again.
- Around new year 2026 I set a rule: if my week isn't 100% green, I give away 100 € on Monday. I wouldn't recommend it.
- In summer 2026 I worked hard for about a month mostly because someone expected a call from me at a fixed time.
- In September 2026 I asked an AI to coach me. Business had been green for years, but the other habits were almost empty. So the first goal wasn't more Business. It was one single week with every habit ticked.

# Friends

Spend one hour a day with friends or family, without scrolling. Of everything you can do as a teen, this one most likely matters most for how happy your whole life turns out.

## The rule

Tick **f** when you spent at least 1 hour of real time with friends or family today, without scrolling. A quick text or a bit of work on the side is fine, TikTok or Instagram is not.

- Breaks and lunch at school count, if you actually talk to people.
- Sitting in class next to someone does not count.
- Texting, gaming chats and video calls do not count. Real time means the same room.
- Family counts: dinner with your parents, a walk with your grandma.

## Why

**Relationships predict a happy, healthy life better than money or fame.** The Harvard Study of Adult Development found that people with the warmest relationships stayed healthy and lived longest. Quality mattered more than number.

**Friendship takes hours, not luck.** Jeffrey Hall (2018) estimated about 50 hours together to make a casual friend, about 90 for a real friend and more than 200 for a close friend. One hour a day gets you there.

**Start your business early.** Then you never have the time or the dumb personality to make friends with all the other losers your age.

## How to start

1. **Pick your hour.** Lunch, the walk home, training with a teammate, dinner with your family.
2. **No feeds while you are together.**
3. **Sit with a group.** If you see people you half know, ask "Can I sit here?"
4. **Talk to new people on purpose.** A simple target: say hi to 3 new people a day. Smile, say your name, ask one question about them.
5. **Get them talking.** Listen more than you talk.
6. **Remember names and come back.** The second and third conversation are where a friendship starts.
7. **Join something.** A club, a team, a choir. Seeing the same people every week does most of the work.
8. **Invite people.** "Want to go to the gym together?" If you are under 18, tell your parents who you are meeting and where.

## Mistakes

- **Counting online time.** Group chats feel social, but they don't build the same bond.
- **Only waiting to be invited.** Most people are waiting too. Be the one who asks.
- **Talking only about yourself.**
- **Keeping friends who put you down.** Teasing is normal. Someone who calls your goals stupid for years is different.
- **Trading friends for work.** If you only have time for business and training, your days get lonely fast.

## What happened when I tried it

When I was 14, I wrote a note called "find friends" with rules like "always smile", "listen instead of talking about yourself" and "make the other person feel good".

Before a school year abroad in Canada, I made a real plan: smile at everyone, introduce myself, talk to 3 new people every day, join clubs, remember names, invite people over. The first weeks I wrote that I didn't really like anyone there yet. A week later I had someone who became my best friend there, and I got him to come to the gym every day. The 3-people-a-day plan worked.

The honest other side: for over three years my tracker showed Business done almost every day, and most other habits nearly empty, including friends. Today I am no longer friends with anyone from a gratitude list I wrote in 2023. One friend I had known since kindergarten spent years calling my goals dumb and embarrassing, and we are not friends anymore. Today my close people are my parents, my grandma and a couple of friends. That is why **f** is on this list.

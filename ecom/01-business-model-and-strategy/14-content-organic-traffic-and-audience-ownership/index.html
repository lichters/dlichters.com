Content, organic traffic and audience ownership

Section 1.14 · 14 of 23 in this chapter · 11 min read

In short

  • Content (articles, product pages, videos) works alongside paid ads. It does not replace them.
  • Pay freelancers (self-employed writers hired per job) to write content. Do not write it all yourself.
  • Start with a test of 10 articles, about $1,000 to $1,500 in total.
  • Build your own email list and followers. You can reach them for free, while paid ads stop when the budget stops.
  • Do not depend on one traffic source. Ad accounts can be blocked with little warning.
This section has 11 parts. Tap one to open it.

Content's role in the marketing mix

1 min
  • Content marketing does not replace paid advertising. It adds another channel next to paid ads.
  • A store's marketing system is a set of connected parts that improve each other when they work together.
  • Content sits at the centre of this system. It makes several other parts work better.
  • Content creates options. It gives you more ways to attract customers, grow the store and keep customers.
  • This is similar to how money creates options in life.
  • The value comes from the options content opens. Content is not valuable only as content.
  • Content builds trust before the first purchase.
  • Shoppers compare your store with large online marketplaces (huge shopping sites with many sellers). They need a reason to buy from you.
  • Detailed product pages, product videos and educational articles give you a selling point that large marketplaces rarely match.
  • An article that ranks for a relevant keyword keeps bringing visitors. "Ranks" means it appears high in search results. A "keyword" is a word or phrase people type into Google.
  • The article keeps bringing visitors for as long as people still search for the topic.
  • Each paid click stops when the budget stops.
  • Content is therefore a one-time production cost with long-term traffic. Paying per visit is different.
  • Organic traffic (free visitors from search and social media, with no ad money) costs time instead of money. It can grow slowly.
  • Paid traffic (visitors you buy with ads) can start immediately. It scales with the size of the market.
  • Use both together. A store should not rely on only one of them.

Outsourcing content production

1 min
  • Business owners should create only a minimal amount of content themselves. Their time is better spent promoting content.
  • Outsource content creation to freelancers. This is especially true for small to medium e-commerce businesses.
  • Doing it in-house leads to frustration and no progress.
  • A standard content plan is two pieces per month. **
  • An ultimate guide (a very long, complete article on one topic) can replace both monthly pieces. **
  • This works when the guide is planned as a truly epic piece. It then becomes the only content for that month, because its promotion lasts longer. **
  • A dropship store (a store that sells products shipped directly from the supplier, so it holds no stock itself) can grow from zero to over $20,000 monthly revenue (money coming in per month).
  • It can do this using content marketing alone, without paid advertising.
  • It builds high-quality content through outsourced creation and systematised templates (repeatable, step-by-step formats).
  • Starting budget reference for content marketing (what to expect to spend):
Item Cost
Ahrefs (an SEO tool that finds keywords; SEO means making pages easier to find in search) 14-day free trial, enough for the keyword process
Writers (Upwork, a website where freelancers are hired) $50 to $100 per 1,000-word article
Native English editors (editors who speak English as a first language) $20 to $25 per hour
Sales copywriter (a writer of selling text) for headlines and meta descriptions (the short preview text shown under a search result) $40 to $100 per hour

Optimising headlines and meta descriptions about $2,000

When to start: sequence with paid traffic

1 min
  • You can keep building your audience before you sell.
  • Or you can start selling now and keep building your audience at the same time.
  • Both approaches are valid. It depends on your specific situation.
  • If pay-per-click ads (ads where you pay each time someone clicks) are still profitable, scale them fully first.
  • If you are looking for alternatives, start content as an additional traffic channel.
  • When paid channels such as PPC (pay-per-click), Amazon and Facebook ads are unavailable in a niche (a small, specific market), content marketing can become the main source of traffic.
  • This reduces dependence on platforms that restrict the category.
  • Build an audience before the product launch. Post educational content, such as recipes or health advice, on social platforms.
  • This also reveals what the target audience wants.
  • Community building comes after the first sales.
  • Focus first on the first 10 sales, then 20. Build community with the customers who arrive afterwards.
  • Do not write blog posts to get a supplier approved. **
  • Blog posts do not help approvals. They only delay revenue. **
  • Blog content belongs in the SEO phase, once organic traffic is being built. **

Case: building the audience before the first offer

1 min
  • A homebrewing test brand (a shop for brewing beer or wine at home) had a Shopify site (an online store platform) that was parked. It was ready, but had no products yet.
  • The owner built the audience first.
  • A Facebook page was created.
  • A meme-style image (a funny picture with text) was posted with a caption asking people to like and share it.
  • Then a Facebook ad was run with only an engagement objective (the goal is likes, comments and shares) at about $5 per day.
  • The post reached about 46,000 people.
  • It received about 3,100 likes, 71 comments and 349 shares.
  • The cost per engagement (the cost of each like, comment or share) was below one cent.
  • After a few weeks, about 3,600 people had engaged on Facebook.
  • About 5,300 people had engaged on Instagram.
  • These people formed the seed audiences (starter groups of people who already engaged, used as a base for future ads).
  • No offers were planned for roughly six weeks while content was produced.
  • The later plan was to use dropshipping for expensive products.
  • Private label (your own brand, made by a supplier) would be used for mid-priced products.
  • A seasonal subscription box (a box sent on a schedule that changes with the seasons) was also planned.

SEO and blog use

1 min
  • Do not sell ad space on your blog to other companies.
  • Use the traffic and articles to sell your own products instead.
  • Check domain authority (a site-strength score from Moz) to judge a website.
  • Compare the domain authority of sites you could get links from (links that point to your site) against each other.
  • Use the top sites in your niche as the comparison, instead of a fixed minimum score.
  • Paying for Google Ads (paid ads shown in Google search) to promote blog posts only makes sense at several million dollars in annual revenue. **
  • Before that level, it is wasted money. **
  • Building organic traffic through SEO is an important part of high-ticket dropshipping (selling expensive products shipped from the supplier). **
  • It should not be postponed indefinitely in favour of paid traffic alone. **
  • A high-ticket dropshipping business that depends heavily on paid traffic is exposed. **
  • It is exposed as soon as ad costs rise or performance drops. **
  • Building organic traffic through SEO is a common weakness. **
  • It is easy to neglect when paid advertising feels more comfortable. **

Owning the audience and the email list

1 min
  • There are only three types of traffic: traffic you own, traffic you control, and traffic you don't control.
  • The goal for all three is to convert them into owned traffic, such as an email list or a follower base.
  • Growing owned traffic is the core task of the business.
  • Owned traffic (an email list, followers or past customers) can be contacted at no extra acquisition cost (the cost of winning a new customer).
  • Repeat sales to this audience are essentially pure profit.
  • Owning an audience you can contact at any time is the long-term goal of marketing. **
  • Traditionally, this audience is kept on an email list. **
  • Paid traffic, ads and SEO reach people you do not own. **
  • So they do not give the same security. **
  • Traffic you don't control includes social media, search engine optimisation, guest blogging (writing articles for other sites), YouTube and guest interviews (being interviewed on other people's shows).
  • Send all of it to your own blog.
  • The top section of your blog captures email addresses.
  • Once visitors give an email address, they become owned traffic.
  • A dropshipping store usually runs direct response marketing (ads that push for an immediate sale).
  • This is unlike brands that build awareness first.
  • Cold visitors (people who do not know your store yet) rarely buy on the first visit.
  • So the store needs a way to keep interested visitors who have not bought yet.
  • Email and SMS (text messages) capture that group and move them toward a purchase.

Risks and dependence on a single channel

1 min
  • Content marketing carries its own risks.
  • It takes sustained effort, meaning steady work over a long time.
  • Treat it as an addition to paid channels, not a guaranteed replacement for them.
  • Results arrive slowly.
  • A single article rarely changes sales.
  • Plan it alongside other traffic sources from the start.
  • Do not depend on one traffic source.
  • Google Ads accounts can be suspended (blocked) with little warning.
  • This is especially true in industries platforms consider risky.
  • A store that gets all its visitors from one paid channel can lose nearly all of them within a day.
  • Combine paid channels such as PPC and Shopping (Google Shopping, product ads in search) with content marketing and email.
  • A business that relies only on paid ads depends on a single traffic source. **
  • If the paid channel fails, for example through account problems or rising costs, traffic can stop entirely. **
  • Content gives the business other ways to attract visitors. **
  • Relying only on pay-per-click ads and influencer marketing (paying people with audiences to promote you) is a long-term risk. **
  • A high-ticket business without content tends to have a higher CPA (cost per acquisition, the money spent to win one customer). **
  • It also has limited traffic options. **
  • Be skeptical of anyone who says content marketing is unnecessary, especially when they sell paid-ad training. **
  • Such claims often hide a commercial motive, meaning they want to sell you something. **
  • If someone says they tried content marketing and it failed, the content was most likely not done properly. **
  • This is not evidence that content marketing itself does not work. **

Facebook groups and community channels

1 min
  • Facebook groups cannot be made read-only (locked so members can only read, not post).
  • So set new posts to require approval to stop questions.
  • Move ongoing support to community channels organised by topic.
  • These channels stay current.

YouTube as an asset

1 min
  • A YouTube channel with many subscribers and steady engagement adds value to an online business. **
  • That value matters if the owner later sells the business. **
  • Each extra traffic source, including YouTube, makes the business less dependent on a single channel. **
  • Case: this drop-shipping store owns none of its products, produces none and holds no stock. **
  • It sold grow lights (lamps for growing plants) from many brands. **
  • Prices ran from about $150 up to about $1,000. **
  • Over roughly two years, it built a YouTube channel with reviews, buyer's guides and top-7 lists of its range. **
  • It also embedded these videos on its website. **
  • Results: the channel reached about 22,000 subscribers. **
  • Single videos drew about 21,000 to 35,000 views. **
  • The videos probably reached millions of views in total. **
  • The website receives tens of thousands of visits per month. **
  • The video descriptions link to the products. **
  • The closing section promotes the affiliate program (a scheme where others earn a commission for sending you sales). **

Faceless AI channels

1 min
  • A faceless AI-presented channel (a channel with no real person on camera) works well in a niche (a small, specific market).
  • It works best where a specific language audience has little content of the type it needs.
  • The unmet demand is the opening.
  • The creator does not need to appear on camera.
  • That lowers the barrier for people who are not comfortable with their own face.
  • Case: a new faceless YouTube channel was built entirely with AI tools.
  • The AI made the portrait, script, voice, avatar animation (a moving digital presenter) and subtitles.
  • The operator had no on-camera presence.
  • After two days, the two uploaded videos had about 1,200 views, about 200 likes and 18 new followers across TikTok.
  • The operator used the channel to practise organic TikTok growth (growing without ads) with a target of 1 million views.
  • AI-presented videos can be turned into digital products.
  • Examples are full video courses on a topic, product tutorials with a speaking presenter, or audiobooks made from text.
  • An audiobook version can be offered as a free bonus with an ebook (a digital book).
  • This adds value for customers.

Short-form social presence

1 min
  • Case: a new account started posting on 5 November with a single new product.
  • The first uploads got about 500 views.
  • Later uploads got about 3,000, 1,000, 1,000, 3,000, 9,000 and then 26,000 views.
  • After five days of uploads, the account had about 800 followers.
  • One video used the hook (the opening line that grabs attention): Why did you give me this free product?
  • That video reached about 375,000 views within three days.
  • That one video raised followers to about 6,000.
  • Viewers began commenting that they wanted the product.
  • At least one sale was reported.
  • A later upload used the hook Do you see yourself needing this?
  • It reached about 23,000 views within three hours.
  • Product accounts are easier to grow than personal accounts.
  • Their content stays on topic and focused.
  • Build separate product accounts rather than relying on a personal profile.
  • One creator reached 100,000 followers in about one month on a personal account.
  • That creator did it by uploading every day.
  • Personal accounts were considered harder to grow than product accounts.
  • The product account is the default recommendation.
  • A personal account can still grow fast with daily uploads.
  • Consistent daily posting is a key growth driver, the main thing that makes an account grow.
  • Most competitors post inconsistently.
  • Consistent upload frequency with slightly better content can outperform many established accounts (accounts that have been around a long time).

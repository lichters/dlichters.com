Relationship-based growth: Dream 100, partners, affiliates and influencers

Section 1.13 · 13 of 23 in this chapter · 19 min read

In short

  • Ask for nothing at first. Give value to partners who already have an audience, and let them get to know you before you ask.
  • Affiliates (partners who earn a commission only when they make a sale) are a safe extra income. Add them only after your store already makes money.
  • Take a business partner only if they bring a large network or a rare skill. Paying a helper for one task is usually cheaper.
  • Partner only with companies whose customers match yours, and only when the partnership suits your community.
  • Karo deals can add sales to a high-ticket store without stock risk, but a new store should start with the free influencer side.
More key points
  • A cause model gives one product per sale and stays affordable.
  • Community events can grow your business and network; the largest drew about 400 entrepreneurs.

Why relationship-based growth works

1 min
  • In an established market, the established players already hold customers' attention, meaning the time and focus people give to a brand. Build relationships with these leaders and use their trust, instead of building all your trust from zero.
  • Customer attention drives revenue directly. Whoever has the most attention in a market can charge the highest prices and make the most sales.
  • Relationship-based growth is a separate channel from direct marketing. Direct marketing means paid methods such as Google Ads, social media ads and email marketing. Relationship-based growth is just as powerful for getting customers.
  • Most online stores work alone. This is especially true of high-ticket dropshipping stores. High-ticket dropshipping means selling expensive products, where the supplier ships each order straight to the customer. These stores miss partnership chances in their own industry.
  • Established figures in your market are willing to partner. They share access to their audience because both sides gain value from the relationship.
  • Relationship-based business growth is a traditional method. It has been proven for centuries and has built successful businesses. It is still rarely used in online stores, even though it works.
  • High-ticket dropshippers usually enter established markets instead of creating new ones. The brand or product may have existed online or offline for 10 to 40 years or more. So the influential voices and audiences that solve the same problems already exist. You can partner with them.

Transferred influence and intent-based traffic

1 min
  • Transferred influence means borrowing trust. You use the credibility and existing audience of people who already have influence in your niche (a small, specific market). They send their visitors to your store.
  • Transferred influence adds a traffic source on top of search traffic. Search traffic is where shoppers look up your products, brand or SKU (stock keeping unit, the product code) on Google or Bing.
  • Intent-based traffic comes from shoppers who already search for a product or brand. Partner-based traffic reaches people through trusted third parties, where they already spend time. Both sources complement each other.
  • The top-ranked partner in a niche is usually a long-term target, not a first contact. Start outreach with the many smaller partners. The goal is many relationships, not one big deal.

Dream 100 outreach and engagement

4 min

Dream 100 means a list of 100 ideal prospects (potential partners) for your business.

Principles

  • Before you ask a high-value prospect for a partnership or sale, build familiarity first. Do this by engaging with their content over time. Direct cold outreach (an unrequested message with a sales pitch) tends to trigger a spam reaction and put people off.
  • Engagement works because an audience-facing person notices and remembers people who interact with them repeatedly. When you reach out later, the contact feels natural, not like a stranger's cold message.
  • In this phase, engagement should give value and ask questions. Do not request anything. Do not ask for a sale or partnership yet. Help the prospect's audience where you can and show real interest in their work.
  • Treat relationship building like a normal conversation with a new acquaintance. Learn what they do and what they are interested in, and engage with that. Stay genuine. Do not pretend to be someone you are not.
  • Dream 100 partnerships must benefit both sides. A relationship that only serves one party tends to end.
  • Frame every outreach as a win-win offer, not a request for help.
  • Use the Dream 100 exercise for high-value targets. Use the affiliate recruitment email template for general affiliate recruitment outside your Dream 100 list.
  • Use the same outreach message whether you contact a potential affiliate by email or on social media (Facebook, Instagram and similar). The message stays consistent across all contact channels.

Step-by-step engagement

Procedure:

  1. Find your Dream 100 prospects. Follow each one, subscribe to their email list, or join it.
  2. Engage with their content on the channels where they are active: Facebook groups and forums, YouTube, Instagram, blogs and email.
  3. Keep this up over time. Only after familiarity has built should you contact them directly about working together.

Facebook groups and forums

  • Engage by answering members' questions with useful, experience-based information from your market.
  • Do not promote your product in replies. Promotional answers usually get you removed from the group.
  • Group and forum owners want questions answered without having to do it themselves. Consistent helpful replies are visible to admins (the people who run the group), who can track the most engaged members. This builds goodwill with the people who run the community.
  • At this stage, do not contact the owner or admin. Your target is regular, helpful participation in the community itself.
  • Keep your personal profile and business page separate. The personal profile follows Dream 100 contacts, joins their groups and forums, and handles relationship-building.
  • The business Facebook page is used only to interact with other pages you run. Some groups refuse page accounts and block a page that tries to join. So the page should never be the one that joins.

YouTube

  • When a prospect you follow publishes a new video, watch it and leave a comment.
  • Either say what you liked about it, or ask a genuine question if the video taught you something and left you with one.

Instagram

  • Check your feed for posts from your Dream 100 prospects from the last couple of days. Comment on them.
  • Say what you like, ask a question, or offer a relevant suggestion. You do not need to react to every post.

YouTube and Instagram algorithms

  • An algorithm is the system that decides who sees which content. These algorithms give more reach to content that gets more engagement.
  • Creators therefore value regular, genuine comments from people who engage with them. This is why consistent commenting builds goodwill.

Blogs and websites

  • When a prospect publishes a new post, read it and leave a comment.
  • Say what you found interesting, add your own view, or ask a question that invites the author to reply and continue the conversation.

Email

  • Reply to the newsletters and emails your prospects send you. Thank them, say what you liked, or ask a question.
  • Always sign with your name so they begin to recognise it.
  • Email is usually the best long-term channel for ongoing contact with a prospect. Prospects send newsletters to subscribers, and replies keep the relationship active.

Respectful disagreement

  • When a prospect publishes something you disagree with, you may give respectful, constructive feedback in a comment.
  • Thank them for the content and say what was good. Then explain clearly why you think a specific point is wrong. Most audience-building creators appreciate this.

Working a prospect toward partnership

  • Most Dream 100 prospects already earn from their audience. They do this through coaching, advertising on their site or forum, or their own products that do not compete with yours.
  • If a prospect does not monetize (earn money from their audience), helping them grow their audience is still a valid offer.
  • The list of monetization options is not complete. There are no fixed limits on what a partnership can be.
  • The real task is a conversation about how both businesses can work together for mutual benefit. Any outcome both sides accept is the right one.
  • One prospect can be worked through several partnership types over time. Start with one option and add others as the relationship grows.

Joint ventures and joint promotions

1 min
  • Joint content: create a piece of content together and promote it to both audiences. Or have both sides run ads to the same content.
  • Agree in advance on how much ad spend each side will contribute.
  • A joint venture (JV, a deal where two businesses work together on one offer) works with a prospect who sells a complementary product in the same market. A complementary product fits with yours but is not a direct competitor.
  • Build a deal where customers get a bundle (one package) of both products. The bundle does not have to be physically packed together. Promote it to both audiences.
  • Joint ventures cross-pollinate audiences. In plain words, each side's customers join the other side's list.
  • This is why joint product promotions are attractive to both partners.
  • A small ad purchase can be a starting point. For example, rent ad space for one month.
  • If it works, the relationship can grow into ongoing deals, such as sponsored emails (paid mentions sent to their list) or other joint promotions.

Affiliate programs: why they work

1 min

An affiliate is a partner who promotes your products and earns a commission (a percentage of each sale).

  • Affiliate programs let you use other people's audiences to drive sales.
  • You pay commissions only when a sale completes. You never pay for traffic itself.
  • This makes affiliate marketing less risky than paid advertising. With paid ads, you pay for clicks whatever the outcome.
  • Affiliates generate qualified traffic (visitors who are likely to buy) by recommending your products to an existing audience. That audience already trusts them on a particular topic.
  • When they make a recommendation, their audience's trust transfers to your brand.
  • Affiliates naturally recommend only products that fit their audience. Content creation takes a lot of effort and investment.
  • Poor-fit recommendations waste their work and generate no sales or commission. They also risk burning the audience trust that is their main business asset.
  • Affiliate marketing works best as an extra revenue stream added to a dropshipping store. It is not a standalone business.
  • Affiliate marketing is an optional add-on for a store that is already live and profitable.
  • It should not be a requirement before launch or before the first sales.
  • An older affiliate website with no traffic and no audience is usually better left aside. Start a new store with this model instead.
  • An active site with real traffic, for example 1,000 visitors a day, can be extended with the model.
  • Affiliate programs suit stores that already do roughly $1 million or more in annual revenue. They help grow from about $1 million to $2 million.
  • Affiliate programs are not a launch tactic for a new store. Refersion (an affiliate software platform) is the platform recommended for them.

  • Amazon Associates (Amazon's affiliate program) commissions were cut to about 1 to 2% on most product categories.
  • This creates a competitive opportunity. Dropshippers can recruit Amazon affiliates by offering equal or better commission rates.

Economics and incentive design

  • The 25% commission rate is an investment in the business, not a cost. (Course D)
  • Paying high commissions for member referrals aligns the company's goal with the members' goal. The company wants to help people build real businesses. Members get a reason to grow the community.
  • Case: a member referred a friend to Course D and received a $1,000 referral commission.
  • He invested the entire amount as startup capital for a dropshipping store. He is now generating about $100,000 in monthly revenue.

Scope rules

  • With affiliate sales, the customer buys from the brand. So you do not own the customer and cannot market to them later.
  • Affiliate promotions can run on a drop-ship store only for complementary products that do not compete with your own supplier products.
  • Affiliate items earn a much smaller margin (profit per sale). They cannot be advertised with the same Google Shopping campaigns as your drop-ship products.
  • Google Shopping ads (product ads on Google) can only promote products you sell directly. So affiliate products cannot be used in Shopping campaigns.
  • Affiliate offers are only worth adding for complementary products you do not sell. An example is online lessons for products sold in the store.
  • Affiliate offers should never be a competing product from the same niche.
  • Shopify stores can carry affiliate deals in two ways. Shopify is an online store platform. The two ways are promoting affiliate offers on the store, and partnering with affiliates who send traffic.
  • Both ways add income beyond product margin.
  • Affiliate cross promotion (promoting another brand's product to your customers) is useful when a brand will not approve you as a dropship supplier. (Cross promotion means promoting each other's products.)
  • Example: a monitor brand sells to a standing desk store's customers. Affiliate payouts are lower than dropship margins. So use this route for accessories and upgrades customers still want.

Procedure: Amazon affiliate store on a WordPress theme

WordPress is a website builder. A theme is a ready-made design for it.

  1. Sign up for the Amazon Associates affiliate program. Copy your unique tracking ID (the code that records your sales).
  2. Buy and install the Shopper Press WordPress theme.
  3. In the WordPress admin dashboard (the control panel of your site), enter your Amazon Associates information and tracking ID in Shopper Press.
  4. Choose the Amazon categories you want to sell. For example, guitar and its subcategories.
  5. Let Shopper Press import the products, including images, prices and descriptions.
  6. Match the theme branding to your dropship store. Customers should feel they are still on the same brand.
  7. Check that the add to cart button sends customers to the Amazon cart with your tracking ID attached.

Creators testing products

  • Some creators test many products on their own accounts. They earn affiliate commissions from the ones that take off.
  • This works only when the test content is cheap to produce and the affiliate program pays for the sales.

Recruiting affiliates

1 min
  • Affiliates do not find or approach ecommerce sites on their own.
  • Successful affiliate programs need active recruitment. You identify potential partners and pitch the program to them.
  • The Dream 100 exercise is the main method for finding affiliates. It naturally surfaces partnership chances during outreach conversations.
  • Warrior Forum and JVZoo are job-board platforms, places where affiliate offers are posted.
  • These platforms work best for limited-time product launches. They are less useful for lasting affiliate partnerships in traditional high-ticket dropshipping.
  • When searching YouTube for affiliates in your product category, check the video descriptions.
  • Look at the links there for affiliate URL parameters (extra codes added to a web address that track sales). YouTube creators embed affiliate links in their video descriptions.

Influencer partnerships

2 min

Scaling

  • Once one influencer partnership works, scale to several in parallel, in different niches or markets. The seller reports working with three influencers at once.
  • Influencer partnerships can include paid services beyond promotion. Examples are TikTok organic growth (growing followers without ads), content creation and product sourcing.
  • Offering these skills to influencers as added value gives your brand a reason to win the deal over competing brands.
  • For a direct-to-consumer (DTC, selling straight to customers) brand grown mainly through influencers, about 90% of sales success was attributed to influencer marketing.
  • Influencers were used mostly at the top of the funnel. The funnel is the path from first view to purchase. The top brings in large volumes of qualified traffic. Influencers were not used only as social proof (other people's praise) at the bottom.

Readiness and budget

  • Before paying for influencer traffic, set up and test the full sales funnel. The funnel includes: store, email capture (collecting email addresses), retargeting (showing ads to people who already visited), branded search ads (ads on your brand name) and SEO (search engine optimisation, getting found on Google).
  • Without a working funnel, influencer spending is largely wasted.
  • Influencer marketing does not produce quick results.
  • Expect the first year to be a costly testing phase. Some spending will not pay back. Plan the budget for that.
  • Spending very large sums, such as around $100,000 on one big-name creator, is risky.
  • This is risky unless the product is exceptional and hard to find. Social proof alone rarely justifies it.

Product fit by platform

  • Some products do not scale profitably through paid ads. For those, influencer campaigns may be the main way to keep sales going.
  • Dropshipped products often convert poorly through Instagram influencers. Converting means turning viewers into buyers. Buyers often convert poorly once they realise the product is dropshipped.
  • Products from a real small brand that makes its own goods tend to convert better on Instagram.
  • TikTok influencer audiences appeared to buy more often than Instagram audiences for dropshipping products.
  • The speaker presented this as an observation, not a proven rule.

Creator whitelisting and owned characters

  • Paid creator whitelisting means running ads from a real creator's account. Whitelisting is the term for this.
  • It requires a revenue share (a cut of sales) and negotiation.
  • A self-owned fictional character avoids that cost and dependency. So it suits testing or a new brand with a small budget.
  • Creator whitelisting is worth considering when more money is available.

Business partners and partnership stores

3 min

Whether to take a partner

  • A business partnership works only when both partners are committed to a bigger vision. They must also have the time to work on it. The combined result should exceed what each could do alone.
  • Two partners who each want to work about 10 hours per week in a lifestyle business (a business built to fund a relaxed life) usually do not benefit. This is especially true when they are in different time zones.
  • A business partner should bring more value than hours. They should have skills that complement yours.
  • For example, one partner may be strong at supplier approvals, phone calls and closing deals (getting the final yes). The other builds websites that convert.
  • Choose a partner who has both succeeded and failed in other ventures.
  • A partner who has only seen success may give up when sales fall short of expectations.
  • Business partnerships rarely suit a new dropshipping store. The exception is when a partner brings a large network or a rare, specific skill set.
  • A 50/50 partner takes half the profit forever and half the business value on a sale.
  • Outsourcing a task is paid once.
  • A business partnership is worth considering mainly when the partner brings a large existing network or a rare, specific skill set.
  • It is also worth it when the partner is a spouse in a close family arrangement.
  • Otherwise, paying an outside provider for a specific task is usually cheaper. Giving a partner a permanent share of profit and of the business's sale value costs more.

Procedure: agree before starting a business partnership (in writing)

  1. Write down each partner's exact role and responsibilities, so no two people do the same work.
  2. Agree on a shared long-term vision and goals before starting. A partnership is very hard to exit once the business is successful.

Partnership stores (separate projects)

  • An experienced store owner can grow income by partnering with trusted people from their own circle who are interested in the model.
  • The owner oversees operations. Each partner does their assigned part.
  • The partnership store is a separate business from the owner's main store.
  • A done-for-you web design service delivers a fully finished, ready-to-sell store.
  • You can use it to launch partnership stores quickly, without building them from scratch.
  • In a partnership store, do not give the partner equity (ownership share) or any share of your main business.
  • You are only a co-owner of the new, separate store.
  • Do not bring a partner into your main income source.
  • A split main business with a partner who is not pulling their weight is a much bigger problem than a separate project. Partnership stores work best as future projects.
  • Spreading income across several partnership stores diversifies risk. Diversifying means not depending on one source.
  • After three or four such stores, they may even earn more than the main store.
  • So you are not locked into a single business partnership for your lifestyle income.

Procedure: partnership model for additional stores

  1. Make sure your first store is already built, has suppliers and traffic set up, and generates the income you need.
  2. Confirm you understand the model and process well enough to run it yourself.
  3. Identify people you know and trust who are interested in starting a business with you.
  4. Choose a new, separate store project for the partnership. Do not use your main business for this.
  5. Have the store delivered through a done-for-you web design service, or have your team build it.
  6. Give the partner their part of the work while you oversee the operations.
  7. Share ownership of the separate store, so both of you earn from it and both receive a payout if it is sold.

Case: partnership stores after a first exit (an exit means selling your business)

  • A store owner first built a fully owned store and sold it for a large lump sum.
  • He then invited applicants from his own audience to join him. He gathered a group in one location and helped them build stores together as partnerships.
  • He earned from those stores and later sold them as well.

Case: consultant turned partner

  • A dropshipping store was built with consulting help from the start. It reached about 250,000 USD in sales in its first three months.
  • The consultant stayed on for the first year. Then they became a partner to scale the business.
  • In the second year of that partnership, annual revenue reached about 11 million USD before the partnership ended.

Brand, community and cause deals

1 min
  • A brand deal is a partnership between your brand and another company.
  • Complementary companies sell things that go well with yours but are not the same thing. Examples are breweries and tourism operators.
  • Brand deals become easy once your audience trusts the brand. The audience is the people who see your content.
  • Your email list (people who gave you their email address and want your messages) and your followers (people who follow your social media accounts) count as audience.
  • Such companies often approach the brand themselves.
  • The brand should only accept partnerships that suit its community, the group of people who follow and trust it.

Brand partnerships on Karo

1 min
  • Karo is a platform that links brands with influencers (people with an online audience who promote products).
  • Brand partnerships on Karo can complement a high-ticket dropshipping store. High-ticket means expensive products. Dropshipping means selling products that a supplier ships straight to the buyer, so you hold no stock.
  • Karo partnerships fill gaps in your catalogue (the list of products you sell) with products you cannot source (find and buy from a supplier).
  • This adds sales opportunities without inventory risk (the danger of paying for stock you cannot sell).
  • It also adds sales without fulfillment work (packing and shipping orders).
  • Do not use Karo's brand partnership side as a first step if the store is new.
  • Its benefits need sales history (a record of past sales) and an existing customer base (people who have already bought from you).
  • Start with the free influencer side to test the account.

Cause-linked model

1 min
  • A cause-linked model means each sale also helps a good cause.
  • The brand donates one product for each item sold, through a partner organization (a charity or group that receives the product).
  • Example: one pair of reading glasses per item sold.
  • A small per-sale donation is affordable. It gives customers and influencers a real reason to support the brand.

Community events

1 min
  • Hosting community events can grow a business and its network at the same time.
  • Example: digital nomad conferences (big meetings for people who work remotely while travelling), held in several countries.
  • The largest event had about 400 entrepreneurs attending. Entrepreneurs are people who run their own businesses.
  • The series ran seven events across Thailand, Las Vegas and Mexico before COVID (the coronavirus pandemic) ended it.

Seasonality and Q4 planning

Section 1.15 · 15 of 23 in this chapter · 17 min read

In short

  • Q4 (October to December) is the biggest sales season of the year. Plan promotions and stock around it.
  • Start planning promotions many weeks ahead. Do not wait for the last week before a holiday.
  • Target warm audiences only: people who already know your store, such as email subscribers and recent visitors.
  • For a first store, sell products that sell all year. Seasonal products cover only part of the year.
  • Keep your ads running if return on ad spend stays stable. Tell customers about delays before they buy.

Planning the Year Around Q4

2 min
  • Q4 means the fourth quarter of the year: October, November and December.
  • E-commerce means buying and selling online.
  • Q4 has been the largest quarter for US retail e-commerce sales in almost every year.
  • The exception is 2020. In 2020, lockdowns (government stay-at-home orders) pulled extra demand into another quarter.
  • "Unadjusted" means the raw numbers. They are not adjusted for normal seasonal swings.
  • The US Census figures used here are unadjusted.
  • One source covers 2016 to Q2 2025. Another source covers 2016 to 2025.
  • Plan promotions and inventory around Q4.
  • Inventory means the stock you hold. Treat Q4 as the highest-volume season of the year, meaning the season with the most orders.
  • Sales rise in Q4 even without action.
  • Total buying goes up. Shoppers buy gifts for themselves and for others. They compare prices online. They also find smaller stores through competitors' promotions. Competitors are rival stores.
  • A live store with no promotions gets only a small Q4 lift.
  • Planned promotions turn that lift into a large spike.
  • The spike needs coordinated promotions on the website, search ads, social ads and email, all at the same time.
  • Search ads are paid ads shown in search results. Social ads are paid ads on social media. Email means messages to your subscriber list.
  • The promotion effort covers five channels. A channel is a way to reach customers.
  • The five channels are: content and SEO, the website, Google Ads, Meta Ads and email.
  • SEO means search engine optimization, which helps your pages rank in Google search. Google Ads is Google's paid ad system. Meta Ads is the paid ad system for Facebook and Instagram.
  • A well-prepared Q4 can bring in roughly the revenue of two other quarters combined.
  • That result justifies the extra work of planned promotions. Revenue means total sales money.
  • For one brand, about 89 percent of sales from October to mid-December were Christmas gifts.
  • So raise ad spend in this period. Ad spend means money paid for ads.
  • Q4 order volume for winning products began picking up from September.
  • Winning products are products that sell well.
  • The surge arrived in the weeks before the peak season. A peak season is the busiest selling time.
  • Q1 is the weakest period for e-commerce. Q1 means January to March.
  • Q2 to Q4 is the growth season.
  • Test products in Q1 to find winners. A test product is a product tried on small ads to see if it sells.
  • Then scale the winners through Q4. Scale means increase sales and spending on them.

Peak Revenue Months and Peak Count

1 min
  • Count the revenue peaks across the year. A peak is a short period of very high sales.
  • Examples include Black Friday (the big sale day after US Thanksgiving), Mother's Day, Father's Day, Memorial Day weekend or back to school.
  • Aim for at least three peaks per year.
  • If only one or two appear, add promotions or product drops to reach three. A product drop is a planned release of new products.
  • For established dropshipping stores, November is usually the highest-revenue month of the year.
  • Dropshipping means a supplier ships products straight to your customer. You do not hold the stock yourself.
  • November includes Black Friday and Cyber Monday. Cyber Monday is the online-deals Monday after Thanksgiving.
  • October typically runs at normal levels.
  • In December, the first two weeks usually bring most of the month's sales.
  • The week before Christmas is the only real slump. Late orders cannot arrive in time to be gifts.
  • Sales soften again around New Year.
  • Promotions often start between December 28 and December 31.
  • They lead into a strong January.
  • So plan inventory and ad budgets for that window. An ad budget is the money you set aside for ads.
  • A store set up correctly should not stop selling in December.
  • Treat the holiday period as a normal month, not a reason to pause.

Launch Readiness and Lead Times

2 min
  • Lead time means how long something takes before it is ready.
  • Seven weeks is enough to build and launch a complete store ready for Q4 traffic, if you work efficiently. Traffic means visitors to your store.
  • Older advice (2021).
  • Build the store around a sustainable business. A sustainable business can grow over the next decade.
  • Do not chase a short trend, such as a holiday season.
  • A late-starting holiday store can still launch in a few weeks.
  • But it is a last-minute effort. Do not make it the main reason to build the store.
  • Older advice (2022).
  • Start planning Q4 promotions many weeks ahead.
  • Do not wait for the final week before a holiday.
  • Planning five promotions across about three months takes time.
  • A store launched a few weeks before Halloween still has time to build a list and run them. A list means your email subscriber list.
  • Planned promotions capture more holiday spending. So plan them several weeks ahead, not days before the holiday.
  • Setting up the store before a Q4 promotion still leaves time to build an email list.
  • Use abandoned carts and exit overlays to build it. An abandoned cart is a shopping cart left without buying. An exit overlay is a pop-up that appears when a visitor tries to leave.
  • Do this before the first promotion goes out.
  • Black Friday and Cyber Monday planning should begin in September.
  • Campaigns, promotions and messaging should be ready before the October and November peaks. A campaign is an organized push of ads and emails.
  • Winter products need to be ready for the next peak season in advance.
  • Plan a store relaunch in October or early November for Black Friday. A relaunch means reopening with new products or a new look.
  • Preparing new creatives and an improved store before the season matters more than reacting during it. Creatives are the ad images and videos.
  • Plan seasonal products for the whole late-year period, not only for Black Friday.
  • Buyer demand can arrive later in December.
  • One seller made almost nothing on Black Friday. That seller earned most of the season's results from the Christmas period.

Supplier Approvals in December

1 min
  • Approved suppliers are suppliers you are cleared to order from.
  • If you already have approved suppliers, start Google Ads in December.
  • Do not wait until after Christmas.
  • If you are still waiting on supplier approvals in December, expect slow replies.
  • Staff are on holiday.
  • Use the time to improve the website, build the supplier list, set up social pages and optimize conversions.
  • A conversion is a visit that ends in a sale. Optimize means improve, so more visitors buy.

Black Friday, Cyber Monday and Late-Year Timing

1 min
  • About 50 percent of e-commerce brands launched their Black Friday sale around November 17.
  • Launching then is roughly mid-pack, meaning the middle of the group.
  • A dip in sales before Black Friday is normal for almost all stores.
  • Ride the holiday momentum up to the last order date for Christmas shipping. Momentum means the energy and buying that build up.
  • The last order date is called the shipping cutoff. It is the last day to order and still arrive in time.
  • Keep your messaging aligned with that end date.
  • In early November, shift storefront and evergreen campaigns toward gift guides and gifting messaging.
  • A storefront is your shop pages. Evergreen campaigns are always-on ads and emails.
  • Do not lead with discounts at this point.
  • This keeps demand going before a formal offer.
  • Holiday promotions such as Black Friday can add sales even for new stores.
  • They are not only for stores with a loyal customer base. A loyal customer base means customers who come back often.
  • Traffic costs rise during these periods. Traffic cost means what you pay in ads for each visitor.
  • Check margins before running them. Margin means the profit left after costs, as a share of the price.

Holiday Messaging and Email

1 min
  • Plan holiday messaging as one calendar.
  • Give each event a separate wording set.
  • Use this order: Halloween, Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Christmas and New Year.
  • Each event needs its own angle. An angle is the main message or reason to buy.
  • This lets subscribers tell the sales apart. Subscribers are people on your email list.

Event Core angle Wording focus

Cyber Monday Online-only deals Short deadlines, extended or last-chance pushes
Christmas Gifting and holiday mood Gift framing, countdown to shopping cutoff
New Year Fresh start Resolutions, new-year savings
  • Sequence matters across the season.
  • After Black Friday, the Cyber Monday message should give a new reason to buy. Do not repeat the same deal.
  • New Year messages work best after Christmas. Frame them as a fresh start.
  • Do not run a sales-driven push in a new store without a plan.
  • Send at least one promotional email to the list with a discount code for the holidays.
  • A discount code is a code customers type at checkout to get a lower price.

Audiences and Remarketing

1 min
  • An audience is a group of people your ads target.
  • Promote to warm audiences only. Warm audiences are email subscribers and people who visited the site in the last 180 days.
  • Exclude recent customers.
  • Cold audiences are people who do not know the brand. They are not the target of holiday promotions.
  • Warm audiences are far more likely to buy. They also cost almost nothing extra to reach.
  • Most products and businesses, including B2B, pick up around the holiday season.
  • B2B means business-to-business, selling to other companies.
  • Consumers buy gifts, and companies spend before year-end.
  • Build your email list and audiences before the holidays, not only in December.

Q5: The Post-Black Friday Window

1 min
  • Q5 is an informal label. It means the roughly three weeks between Black Friday/Cyber Monday and New Year.
  • Many competitors pull back on ads and activity in this window.
  • Competition for ad space drops. Ad space is the places where ads are shown.
  • Suppliers have more capacity to take on new accounts.
  • During this low-competition window after Black Friday, keep and increase what already works.
  • Do not launch new ad platforms or untested experiments. Untested means not yet proven.

Ad Costs and Competition

1 min

  • The sources describe ad costs at different times. They do not agree on one trend.

  • Use each value for its own period.

  • Re-check against current platform costs. A platform is an ad system such as Google or Meta.

  • CPMs (cost per thousand impressions, the price for 1,000 ad views) and CPC rates (cost per click) fell on Google and Facebook Ads.

  • Larger advertisers reduced budgets because of economic uncertainty.

  • This created better-priced inventory, meaning cheaper ad space.

  • The result was more traffic at the same ROAS. ROAS means return on ad spend, the money earned for each unit of money spent on ads.

  • 2023 expectation: A downturn is an economic slowdown.

  • When ad costs fall during a downturn, the plan is to hold revenue steady.

  • This means more visitors buy for the same or lower ad cost.

  • This was the expectation for 2023. Re-check against current platform costs.

  • 2023 observed level: Ad costs had returned to roughly pre-pandemic levels. Pre-pandemic means before COVID-19.

  • During COVID, ad costs were unusually cheap. Many local businesses cut their ad spending then.

  • Competition in dropshipping stayed about as intense as during the pandemic.

  • Product availability had recovered.

Economic Downturns and Recessions

2 min
  • During an economic slowdown, buyers with high incomes tend to keep buying.
  • So do buyers of products that solve a clear, important problem.
  • Favor these two groups when the economy is uncertain.
  • A recession is a sharp, lasting economic slowdown.
  • During a recession, e-commerce growth dipped by about 13 percent compared to the previous year.
  • Stores that stay profitable can still grow, at a lower rate.
  • Products that solve a real problem or have clear use cases keep selling during a downturn.
  • This holds regardless of price.
  • Novelty products are new, fun items that buyers do not need. They need higher perceived value to sell well. Perceived value is how valuable the buyer thinks the item is.
  • A recession significantly affects only stores with very high volume.
  • Typically this means above $1 million over two or three months.
  • Low-priced products of $24 to $29 with no clear use case are the most affected.
  • A store with low fixed costs can lower ad spend in a downturn. Fixed costs are bills you pay every month, whether you sell or not.
  • Such a store does not pay bills to stay open.
  • It keeps making money at lower volume.
  • It grows again when spending recovers.
  • Older advice (2019).
  • Launch the store now. Do not wait for supply chain problems or inflation to ease. Supply chain problems are delays in making or moving goods. Inflation means prices rising over time.
  • People keep buying. Suppliers still have products to sell.
  • The business adapts to market conditions as they change. Market conditions means how the market is doing.
  • When sales fall at the same time across many stores in the same market, first check whether the drop is market-wide.
  • Compare your revenue trend with other stores and niches. A niche is a narrow product area. Revenue trend means how your sales money moves over time.
  • This happened with many US e-commerce stores from June to July 2021.
  • Longer supplier lead times and shipping problems can add to a slowdown.
  • But any problem specific to your own store still needs its own fix.
  • Older advice (2021 to 2022).

Procedure for downturns and supplier closures

Supplier closures are times when a supplier stops shipping for a while.

  1. Maintain current ad spend during economic downturns if ROAS is stable.
  2. Accept orders with clear customer notification about delays.
  3. Follow up with customers manually via phone and email.
  4. Concentrate on building remarketing email lists and audiences.
  5. When the economy recovers, execute aggressive remarketing campaigns to this built-up audience.
  • Do not pause ads during supplier closures or economic uncertainty if return on ad spend stays stable.
  • Instead, clearly notify customers of shipping delays before purchase.
  • Build remarketing audiences for future campaigns.
  • Older advice (2020).
  • and apply to different conditions.
  • Lowering ad spend in a downturn fits a store with low fixed costs.
  • Keeping spend fits a store where ROAS holds.

Seasonal Products and Seasonal Niches

2 min
  • For a first store, sell products that can be sold year-round.
  • Do not mix in seasonal ranges. A range is the set of products you sell.
  • Running two seasonal product sets doubles the work.
  • That work covers suppliers, traffic analysis, conversion checks and page optimization.
  • Traffic analysis means checking where visitors come from. Conversion checks mean checking how many visitors buy.
  • Seasonal pairs such as sun and snow products can work in principle.
  • They are not recommended for a beginner.
  • Seasonal niches are not recommended as a first store.
  • They lack year-round profitability. Profitability means making money.
  • They can be added to a portfolio of stores after the core business is established. A portfolio is your group of stores.
  • They should not be the starting point.
  • Older advice (2021).
  • Seasonal products lose most of their sales for roughly three months a year.
  • Test other products to cover the shortfall.
  • Products that are too expensive for a new brand to sell at scale are a poor choice.
  • This holds even if they are profitable per unit. Per unit means for each item sold.
  • Expect several test products to fail before one scales.
  • For a seasonal product, run an aggressive offer during the off-season.
  • The off-season is when the product does not sell well. An aggressive offer is a strong discount or deal.
  • A deep discount covers the sales gap. It can be sustained without large losses.
  • Also target a region currently in its season.
  • For example, target the Southern Hemisphere (the half of Earth south of the equator) when the Northern Hemisphere is off-season.
  • Use a pre-season sale before a market's season starts.
  • This can extend sales across most of the year.
  • Check shipping times before targeting a distant region.
  • Shipping of 10 to 15 days lowered results when the supplier had no warehouse in that region. A warehouse is a storage building for stock.

Case: UK seasonal dropshipping brand

  • A UK seasonal dropshipping brand started with about £300 of personal capital and no savings.
  • The first product sold well immediately.
  • But scaling it outside its selling season lost a lot of money.
  • A later good season allowed scaling.
  • The business later took on a business partner.
  • Current figures cited: about £30k monthly revenue and about £5k monthly profit.
  • The next target is £10k monthly profit.
  • The owner learned web design, graphic design and advertising himself.

Holiday Niche Stores

1 min
  • A holiday niche store can be broad within its theme.
  • It can sell anything connected to the occasion.
  • It does not need one narrow product.
  • Open it quickly.
  • Reach out to influencers, meaning people with large followings who promote products.
  • Run ads to test before expanding the range.
  • Ads sold holiday products.
  • But their profit margins were lower than those from influencer marketing.
  • Compare margin per channel before deciding where to spend. A channel is a way of reaching customers.
  • For a holiday such as Valentine's Day, about three weeks before the date was considered enough time.
  • Use that time to open a themed store, send influencer outreach and start ads. Outreach means messages asking people to promote you.
  • If creators do not reply, run paid ads to test the offer. Creators are people who make content.
  • Do not wait.
  • Summer products should be items such as sandals, women's summer bags and accessories.
  • Avoid swimwear like bikinis. Swimwear carries a risk of platform bans, meaning the ad or shop platform removes your account.
  • A sandal product sold in 2020 was advertised with Facebook ads in Australia and New Zealand, not the US.
  • It performed well, meaning it sold well.
  • Before reusing an older strategy, check what has changed in the market.
  • Then adjust it.

Chinese New Year and Supplier Closures

1 min
  • Launching products just before or during Chinese New Year risks delayed orders, complaints and disputes.
  • Chinese New Year ends on February 10.
  • Use the time before the holiday to research products and build product pages and ads.
  • Then launch several products after February 10.
  • Before a holiday such as Chinese New Year, suppliers may close.
  • So stock preparation has to start early. Stock means products on hand.
  • If stock runs out during the holiday, a pre-order model keeps sales going.
  • Pre-order means customers buy before the product is in stock.
  • Sales continue until restocking, which means getting more stock.
  • If no winning product is available during a period such as Chinese New Year, stop running ads.
  • Use the time to learn more about dropshipping.
  • Digital products are an alternative during that time. Digital products are items delivered online, such as downloads.
  • They can be tested with ads.
  • For ad handling during supplier closures, see in Economic Downturns and Recessions.

Business-to-Business Year-End

1 min
  • B2B sales also tend to rise before year-end.
  • Profitable companies spend their remaining cash on purchases. This lowers the tax they would owe.
  • Plan B2B offers and outreach for the final months of the year.
  • Do not plan only for consumer holidays.

Case Studies: Launch and Black Friday Results

1 min
  • A store launched in April with a launch event.
  • It reached over $50,000 in total revenue and over 500 orders by Black Friday. Orders means purchases.
  • About two-thirds of its month's revenue came from Thursday to Monday.
  • Its VIP email day was its best day. A VIP email is sent to your best customers.
  • Returning launch customers bought again six months later.
  • The owner kept the ad budget conservative, meaning careful and low. The owner used email heavily.
  • A store ran a four-day Black Friday sale this year.
  • The year before, it ran 14 days.
  • Still, it grew about 20 percent year over year. Year over year means compared with the same period last year.
  • It did this with lower ad spend and better profit.
  • It planned to keep its working campaign running.
  • It also planned to build cash flow before adopting a new structure. Cash flow means money coming in and going out.

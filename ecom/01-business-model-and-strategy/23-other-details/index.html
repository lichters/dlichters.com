Section 1.23 · 23 of 23 in this chapter · 8 min read

In short

  • Store fixes come in order: 8 quick steps, one at a time, on a store that already sells.
  • If a store loses money, find the real cause first. Spending more on the same setup does not fix it.
  • Good supplier relationships and well-built product pages take real work. That work separates winning stores from the rest.
  • Direct mail (printed letters sent by post) works for any product with real value, because buyer reactions stay the same.
  • AI tools help most with research, store building, ads, customer work and decisions.

Quick wins on an established store

1 min
  • Apply these steps in order to a store that already sells.
    1. Add abandoned cart emails (emails to shoppers who left items in their basket without buying).
    2. Add "buy" and "online" to product title tags (the page title shown in search results).
    3. Make sure Google Shopping feed monitoring is set up daily (checking the product list you send to Google every day). Split product types into separate campaigns (separate ad groups).
    4. Negotiate provider discounts by phone. Providers are Shopify (the software your store runs on), your payment processor (the company that handles card payments), and PayPal. Also review annual billing (paying once a year).
    5. Rewrite meta descriptions (the short summary under a search result) using these rules: capitals, unique per page, sales-letter style (written to sell), rich snippets (extra details in search results, such as stars or prices), and a phone number where relevant.
    6. Disable pagination (splitting a product list over several pages).
    7. Set up uptime monitoring (a tool that alerts you when your store goes offline).
    8. Validate Google Analytics e-commerce tracking (check that Google Analytics, a traffic and sales tracker, records sales correctly). Then remove PayPal Express buttons (quick-pay buttons).
  • A store doing about $1 million in revenue (total sales) applied three of these quick wins. These included titles, Google Shopping structure and meta descriptions. It added about $200,000 in revenue within three months.
  • A separate store cut its Shopify costs by $100 per month with one phone call.
  • That saves over $1,000 per year.

Return fees

1 min
  • Return fees (charges for sending an item back) spread first in the US.
  • Then they spread to Australia, and more slowly to the UK and EU.
  • Large fashion retailers such as ASOS and Zara now charge them.
  • Expect fee-based return policies to become the norm in each market, at a different speed in each.

Return on investment

1 min
  • Return on investment (ROI) means the profit you get compared with the money you put in.
  • Example: a $2,000 retreat investment (a paid event; flights, hotel and ticket combined) should generate $20,000 in annual profit through applied strategies. That is a 10x return, meaning ten times the money put in.

Diagnosing a store that does not make money

1 min
  • if a store is not making money, find the cause.
  • The cause is either traffic quality and conversion (how many visitors buy), or product and supplier quality.
  • Spending more money on the same setup does not fix it.
  • Do not close a store before the root cause (the real underlying reason) has been checked.

Competitive position

1 min
  • The business is relationship-based, meaning it depends on personal ties with suppliers and partners.
  • That is a real barrier against copycats (others who copy you).
  • Supplier relationships, optimized product pages (pages tuned to sell), traffic setup (how visitors reach the store) and ad accounts all take work.
  • That work is what separates the stores that win.

Platforms and emerging channels

1 min

  • VR means virtual reality, 3D worlds you enter with a headset. Meta is the company behind Facebook and Instagram. The target was 500,000 users.

Applying dropshipping methods to other online businesses

1 min
  • Dropshipping means selling products that a supplier ships straight to the buyer, so you hold no stock.
  • Human psychology (how people think and decide) and platform mechanics (how sites and apps work) carry over from dropshipping to other online businesses.
  • Only the objective changes.
  • For example, a lead-generation campaign (an ad campaign that collects contact details of interested people) books sales calls instead of making a direct product sale.

Direct mail

1 min
  • Direct mail (printed letters or leaflets sent by post) sells very different products with the same method.
  • This works because buyer reactions stay the same across products.
  • A seller who understands those reactions can apply one approach to any new offer.
  • Direct mail reaches buyers quickly and at low cost in good and bad markets.
  • It shows early whether a product sells, so a campaign can still run within the selling season.
  • This only works for products with real merit (real value) and enough people who want them, or who can be made to want them.
  • Management can check every claim before a direct mail piece goes out.
  • That makes written promises easier to control than a salesman's spoken pitch.
  • Small mailings let a seller test an idea cheaply before committing to a large budget.
  • Direct mail can prepare a sales team's prospects (people who might buy) before a visit. Salespeople then meet buyers who already know the offer.
  • The same letter technique can help a business sell to its own staff and partners.

AI-first dropshipping

1 min
  • An AI-first dropshipping system uses artificial intelligence (software that creates and analyzes content).
  • It is designed for speed and leaner operations (fewer people and less work) across product research, store build and ad creative (ad images and text).
  • This is why its milestones (target checkpoints) are set aggressively, meaning very ambitiously.
  • The old approach of guessing, testing blindly, or spending weeks building a store is replaced by AI-supported work.
  • That AI work speeds up each stage.
  • The goal is faster decisions and less wasted effort.
  • AI dropshipping can be organized into five areas where AI tools add leverage (extra power):
    • product research
    • store creation
    • creatives and content
    • customer experience
    • data-based decisions
  • Each area is a place where manual work (work done by hand) or guesswork can be replaced by tools.

High-ticket dropshipping course: scope and audience

1 min
  • The growth phase (the stage for scaling a business that already runs) applies to an existing business.
  • The build phase (the stage for starting a new business) applies only to someone starting a new business.
  • So a store already running should focus on growth content.
  • The course is aimed at people who already run a high-ticket dropshipping business (selling expensive products that a supplier ships to the buyer), not at beginners.
  • Learners still in the launch stage (when a business first opens) are told to start with the build-phase material.
  • The program targets stores trading for a few months to several years.
  • A store at either end of that range can use the grow-phase material.
  • A store still being set up should begin with the build phase.
  • Sources differ on where to start, and both apply in their own case.
  • The course is framed for existing owners.
  • A beginner only needs to focus on the Build phase at the start.
  • Existing owners use the course directly, and beginners start with Build.
  • Training is meant to stay useful well after launch, including one or two years into a running business.
  • A beginner can leave advanced material until the business has grown.

High-ticket dropshipping course: structure

1 min
  • The course has two phases.
    • A build phase, which covers setting up and launching the business.
    • A grow phase, which covers scaling traffic (more visitors) and conversion (the share of visitors who buy).
  • An existing business owner can skip the build-phase parts.
  • The Build phase covers building and launching the business.
  • The Grow phase covers marketing and outsourcing (handing tasks to other companies or people) after launch.
  • The high-ticket dropshipping process is described as a sequence (order) of five steps: build the business, then launch it, then grow it.
  • The build phase is the first stage.
  • The unit (the source section) names only build, launch and grow. The other two steps are not named in this source.
  • in the members area (the paid, members-only part of the site), the Build phase parts appear first. They sit at the top of the page, below the welcome message, under a heading called Build Phase.
  • The course has about six Build phase parts, which must be completed in numbered order.
  • The growth stage has about ten parts, each covering a single side of marketing for high-ticket dropshipping.
  • That count keeps rising over time.
  • Named areas:
    • Conversion rate optimization (improving the share of visitors who buy).
    • Backlink acquisition (getting links from other sites).
    • Content marketing.
    • Search engine optimization (SEO, making pages easy to find in search).
    • Pay-per-click advertising (paying for each click on an ad).
    • Email marketing.
    • Retargeting (showing ads again to people who already visited).
  • There is no fixed order for working through the grow-phase parts.
  • Start with the weakest area of the business, or with a traffic channel (a source of visitors) not yet tested.

Buying an existing store or business

Section 1.22 · 22 of 23 in this chapter · 5 min read

In short

  • Building a store yourself is usually a better choice than buying one.
  • Buying only makes sense when you have more money than time, and you must check every number yourself.
  • Learn to build a store first, so you can judge one that is for sale.
  • An old web address alone is rarely worth paying for. Check the real sales history before paying for any store.

Build versus buy

  • Building from scratch means starting a store yourself, with nothing bought. It is generally preferred to buying one.
  • A store with roughly 10,000 to 40,000 in profit can often be rebuilt within a few weeks. So buying it is rarely worth the price.
  • Buying a finished store is usually more expensive than building one.
  • So building and growing the store until it earns income is the more common route.
  • Build online stores from scratch rather than buying existing ones.
  • Buying an existing online business is generally not recommended for most buyers.
  • E-commerce means selling products online. E-commerce businesses are easily replicated, meaning others can copy them quickly.
  • Starting a new store is usually a better use of several hundred thousand dollars than buying one (2024).
  • Buying a profitable store at a high multiple rarely makes sense. A multiple is a price set as a number of times the store's profit.
  • A store that sells about one order per day is achievable with focused work and little money. This source does not state the multiple itself.
  • A better route is to find which niches (narrow product markets) and stores are for sale.
  • Then check their numbers and rebuild a comparable model yourself.

When buying can make sense

  • Drop shipping means a supplier ships products straight to customers, so you hold no stock. Buyers purchase an existing drop shipping store to:
    1. shorten their learning curve (the time it takes to learn the work),
    2. trade money for time,
    3. use momentum (the speed the store has already built up), or
    4. put a unique advantage of their own to work.
  • For high-ticket dropshipping (selling expensive products this way), the choice depends on your resources.
Your position Usually the better route Source
More money than time Buy an existing store. It saves setup time, but you inherit processes (the ways the business works) you did not design and must review them carefully.
More time than money Build from scratch.
  • Only content sites are worth buying. A content site earns from articles or posts, not products.
  • The content site must have established traffic (regular visitors), such as a forum or blog running for around 15 years.
  • Pure e-commerce stores are not worth buying.

Who should not buy yet

  • Buyers without prior experience building stores often overpay.
  • Their sales often fall soon after purchase, because they cannot judge the quality of the store.
  • Build a store first. Learning to build a store makes it much easier to evaluate one that is for sale.

Verifying figures before paying

  1. Verify (check) the seller's figures. Also check supplier relationships (how the seller works with product suppliers), supplier lead times (how long suppliers take to deliver) and the sales trend (whether sales go up or down over time).
  2. Sellers can present numbers that hide problems. Check everything against the underlying data, meaning the original records.
  3. Do your own check of revenue (total money from sales) and margins (the share of each sale kept as profit).
  4. Many small online sellers keep poor financial records. So the figures they report are often unreliable.
  5. Check the length of the sales history (how many months or years of sales records exist) before you agree on a multiple. See the valuation benchmarks below.

Valuation benchmarks

A valuation is an estimate of what a store is worth. A benchmark is a typical reference point.

Store or situation Benchmark Applies when Source
Established profitable store Around $500,000 or more Store with a long, proven profit record
Small, unproven store Roughly $5,000 to $20,000, risky for beginners Little or no track record
Store with roughly 10,000 to 40,000 in profit Often rebuildable within a few weeks, so buying is rarely worth the price Comparing purchase price with build cost
Store with about one year of sales history About 30 times monthly net profit (profit left after all costs) is typical (2024) Standard valuation for a store with roughly a year of sales
Store with less than one year of sales history More risk for the buyer, usually a lower multiple (2024) Short or unproven sales history
  • The 2020 price points are older. They show only rough orders of magnitude (rough sizes, not exact prices).
  • For a current valuation of a store with known monthly net profit, use the 2024 multiple.

Aged domains

An aged domain is a web address that has existed for years.

  • Buying aged domain names is generally not worth the money in drop shipping.
  • Spending large amounts on several aged domains has not paid off.
  • An established store with an aged domain, existing backlinks (links from other websites to yours) and running traffic lets you grow quickly.
  • These are the growth levers, meaning the ways to increase sales.
  • An aged domain with authority (trust that search engines give a site) does not guarantee fast search rankings (your place in Google results).
  • So budget time for SEO (search engine optimization, getting found on Google), even after buying.
  • Both statements apply to different things.
  • An aged domain bought on its own has not been worth the money.
  • An aged domain that comes with a store that already has backlinks and traffic can speed up growth.

Shopify Exchange and broker listings

Shopify Exchange is an online marketplace for buying and selling Shopify stores (online shops built on the Shopify software). A business broker is a person who sells businesses for owners. A listing is an advertisement for a business for sale.

  • Stores listed for sale on Shopify Exchange were often overpriced, had little or no sales history and carried unrealistic valuations. This was seen around its 2019 launch.
  • Check the real revenue history and the asking price (the price the seller wants) critically before buying a store there.
  • On a business broker listing for a dropshipping store, the sale includes the domain (web address) and all site contents and files.
  • The URL (web address), Google Analytics access (a tool that shows visitor numbers) and profit-and-loss statement (a report of income and costs) are visible only after you unlock the listing.
  • Review the listing carefully before buying.

Risks for buyers

  • You inherit processes you did not design and must review them carefully.
  • You can overpay and watch sales fall, because you cannot judge quality without building stores yourself.
  • The seller's numbers can hide problems.
  • Reported revenue and margins are often unreliable when the seller kept poor records.
  • Small, unproven stores sold for roughly $5,000 to $20,000 are risky for beginners.
  • An aged domain does not guarantee fast rankings.
  • E-commerce businesses are easily replicated, which weakens the case for paying for one.

Learning due diligence

Due diligence means careful checking before a purchase.

  • The podcast Acquisitions Anonymous has its host vet two businesses a week (check them carefully) as if buying them.
  • It is a useful way to learn due diligence before purchasing an online store.

Choosing a business model: dropshipping, high-ticket, FBA, private label, digital and affiliate

Section 1.1 · 1 of 23 in this chapter · 23 min read

In short

  • Dropshipping means a supplier stores and ships the products, so you need little money to start.
  • At the start, pay the supplier only after the customer has paid you, so no cash goes out before money comes in.
  • The real risk is wasted ad spending, so tie your spending to results you measure.
  • Some figures here are old. Check current supplier prices and Amazon fees before you decide anything.
  • A dropshipping store is slower to build as a brand, but it can give steadier income if you treat it as a long-term brand.
More key points
  • Affiliate marketing means promoting other people's products and earning a commission (a share of each sale) when your visitor buys.
  • Print on demand (items printed only after someone orders them) earns very little per sale, so treat it as a brand add-on, not a profit source.
  • Depending on one marketplace is risky, because one account ban can end your business.
  • Dropshipping (a supplier ships each order to the buyer, so you never hold stock) is not dead, but stores without a brand plan often fade within two or three months.

What dropshipping is

2 min
  • Dropshipping is a fulfillment model, meaning a way for someone else to pack and ship your orders.
  • You sell products without holding inventory, which means without keeping stock on hand.
  • You hand fulfillment to a supplier. This reduces upfront costs, meaning the money you must pay before you earn.
  • Compared with traditional retail (selling to customers in a shop) or wholesale (selling in bulk to other businesses), it minimizes financial risk.
  • Dropshipping is not a separate business model. It is a fulfillment method.
  • Research, selling and store building follow the same process as any other online store built on Shopify (software for running online shops).
  • The model here is online-only, with no physical store locations.
  • After each sale, the supplier ships the order directly to the customer.
  • In dropshipping, the store owner never buys or stores inventory.
  • Each order is sent to a supplier or fulfillment company. That company picks (grabs) the item, packs it and ships it straight to the customer.
  • The store owner only builds the website, attracts buyers and passes on order details.
  • A dropshipping business rests on three parts: an online store where sales happen, supplier fulfillment that ships orders, and traffic (visitors) that brings buyers.
  • Each part must work before the business can make a profit.
  • Dropshipping is a modern version of retail: a store sells products made by other manufacturers, as department stores do.
  • Being the middleman between manufacturer (the maker) and customer is normal in retail and accepted by customers.
  • Dropshipping is only a fulfillment method, so the customer experience is largely the same as with any other brand.
  • The supplier ships the order directly, and the store owner stays responsible for the sale.
  • With approved suppliers, the retailer holds no inventory for most products.
  • Orders go to suppliers after each customer purchase, and the supplier ships directly to the customer. (2023.)
  • In a non-stocking dealer setup (a dealer who does not keep stock), the supplier keeps the products in stock and ships orders to your customers.
  • Your store handles marketing, order handling and customer service. You do not hold inventory.
  • Dropshipping gives the seller no product control.
  • You cannot change or design the product. The supplier decides what is stocked and can remove a product at any time.
  • Your job is marketing and customer service. You can only suggest product changes to the supplier.

High-ticket and low-ticket

1 min
  • High-ticket dropshipping means selling higher-priced products online, while a supplier ships them directly to the customer. The seller does not hold stock.
  • Beginners should confirm they understand this model before they search for products.
  • High-ticket dropshipping combines these benefits:
    • Low startup risk: under $500 to start.
    • An evergreen model, meaning it sells steadily over time.
    • Profit of $300 or more per sale.
    • A sellable branded asset, meaning a brand that has value and could be sold.
    • A time to first profit of about 30 days.
    • Skills that transfer, meaning skills you can use in other online businesses.
  • Low-ticket dropshipping sells $10 to $100 items from China, or via eBay or Amazon. It keeps low risk and transferable skills, but it lacks the other benefits above.
  • Dropshipping usually targets items above $200, and sometimes items above $750 to $800.
  • At those prices, the supplier ships and the store owner provides all customer service.
  • Use this range for high-ticket products.
  • Note on price thresholds: the two sources use different cut-offs.
  • The $10 to $100 range describes low-ticket products.
  • The $200 and above range describes the usual dropshipping target.
  • Both are older or undated advice, so check current supplier prices before you set a product range.

How orders and payments flow

2 min

Basic cycle

  1. Open a store and attract customers to it.
  2. When a customer orders, place the same order with your approved supplier.
  3. The supplier ships directly to the customer, so you never hold inventory.
  4. Pay the supplier only after the customer has paid you.
  5. You manage the website and marketing while the supplier handles fulfillment.

Full order flow

  1. The customer pays on your website by card or PayPal, and the charge is immediate.
  2. The payment provider (the service that takes the money) sends it automatically to your bank account.
  3. Only after you receive the customer's money do you place the order with your supplier.
  4. The supplier invoices you, meaning it sends you a bill.
  5. You pay the supplier.
  6. The supplier ships the product directly to the customer, who receives it at home or at their business.
    • The cycle repeats for each order.
    • Repeat customers can be sold other items.

Order flow with an app-based supplier

  1. The customer places an order on the online store, for example one built with Shopify.
  2. The store owner forwards the order information to a supplier or fulfillment company. This is usually done through that company's app, in a few clicks.
  3. The supplier picks the product from the manufacturer or stock, checks its quality, packs the order and ships it directly to the customer's door.

Summary: the store is built, the customer orders, the order is passed to the supplier, and the supplier handles sourcing (finding and buying products), packing and shipping.

Payment timing and cash

  • Payment flows first from the customer to the seller, at the time of purchase.
  • The seller then orders from the supplier and pays for the product afterwards.
  • So no large stock is bought upfront.
  • Payment gateways (services that process card payments) commonly pay out the sale amount on the same day, minus small fees.
  • This means the seller has the money before paying the supplier.
  • At the start, pay each supplier only after the customer's payment has been received.
  • This way, no cash goes out before money comes in.
  • Paying suppliers before the customer's funds reach your bank account is an option to consider later, once the business is more experienced and earns more.
  • Dropshipping is self-funding: customer payments cover each supplier order.
  • So profit can be taken at the end of each month.
  • A physical product business requires fronting cash, meaning paying for stock before you earn from it.
  • Profit only comes after a difficult cash cycle (the time your money is stuck in stock before it returns) is passed.

Who is involved

  • The model has three players: the customer, you as the seller, and the supplier.
  • Freight companies (shipping companies) handle the physical delivery.
  • It is simple in structure, but that does not make it easy to run.
  • Marketing is the engine of the business.
  • Without marketing, customers never find the website, so no sale or service can happen.
  • High-ticket dropshipping is therefore effectively a marketing business that sells through a store.

Benefits

3 min

Low capital (little money put in) and low risk

  • Dropshipping has a low barrier to entry, meaning it is easy to start.
  • No inventory is required. Upfront costs and risk are reduced.
  • No physical storefront is needed.
  • You can test new products and markets without large commitments, such as big spending or big stock orders.
  • Because no inventory is bought in advance, the seller avoids the risk of unsold stock.
  • The seller can spend the budget on marketing and selling, instead of on warehousing (storing stock).
  • Dropshipping eliminates overstocking and understocking risk, meaning holding too much or too little stock.
  • You test products and niches without financial exposure, meaning without money at real risk.
  • You keep complete control over inventory levels.
  • Low-priced products are a lower-risk way to start, because they need little capital and no storage.
  • Choosing items that ship directly from the supplier keeps inventory risk near zero.
  • Dropshipping is a low-risk way to gain business experience.
  • The main costs at the start are ad spend (money spent on ads) and the Shopify subscription (the regular fee for the software).
  • The real risk is wasted ad spend, so keep spend tied to measured results.
  • Dropshipping is commonly recommended as a starting point for beginners.
  • It needs little money, a laptop and an internet connection. It needs no physical store, warehouse or upfront stock purchase.

Speed, scale (growing sales) and flexibility

  • Dropshipping enables quick scalability, meaning you can grow fast.
  • You can expand product offerings rapidly.
  • You keep more control over operations.
  • You reduce warehouse and shipping overhead (the regular costs of running them).
  • You can reach better profit margins, meaning the share of each sale you keep as profit, because of lower fixed costs (costs that stay the same each month).
  • Dropshipping enables rapid pivoting (quickly changing direction) to market trends.
  • You can expand or scale back product offerings quickly.
  • Offering many different products (diversified offerings) reduces financial risk.
  • This flexibility is a core competitive advantage, meaning a main edge over rivals.
  • Dropshipping allows a seller to switch products and suppliers easily.
  • Many product ideas and niches (small markets) can be tested without the cost of managing inventory.
  • A dropshipping store can list as many products as needed.
  • Several offers in the same niche can be sold to existing and new customers.
  • Dropshipping removes the operational burden (the daily workload) of inventory management and fulfillment.
  • The capacity freed by outsourcing these tasks can go to revenue-generating activities (work that brings in money), such as marketing, customer acquisition (winning new buyers) and sales.
  • These activities directly drive growth.
  • Dropshipping applies the lean startup logic, a way of starting small and testing first.
  • The seller tests an existing market product first.
  • Then the seller customizes branding and holds inventory only after the product proves demand.

Control and margin

  • Selling on your own store avoids marketplace commissions, which are fees a platform takes from each sale.
  • This allows higher prices or better margins than on platforms that take a cut of each sale.
  • Owning the storefront gives the seller control over marketing, pricing and all other business aspects.
  • The seller is not bound by a marketplace's rules.
  • Dropshipping removes warehousing and shipping overhead.
  • So margin can be put into marketing rather than storage.
  • This is presented as a reason for higher profit margins than inventory-based models (businesses that hold stock).
  • Dropshipping gives higher margins and more control. (2023.)
  • You own the customer, can cut costs by negotiating with suppliers, and can run every type of ad.
  • On an own store, the seller receives the customer data, which marketplaces typically keep.
  • This data supports strategic decisions, community building and repeat sales (customers buying again).

Skills and transfer

  • Dropshipping is a starting point for acquiring skills and generating cash.
  • The same skills can open other businesses, such as a local marketing agency or a coaching business.
  • Once you can sell and market well, those skills transfer (carry over) to other work.
  • Examples include agencies, coaching, consulting, online education, buying other e-commerce businesses, or building your own brand.
  • The underlying sales and marketing concepts stay the same across products, even when tactics change.

Risks and limits

1 min

Limits of the dropshipping model

  • You sell products that competitors also sell, so the product cannot be your differentiator, meaning the thing that sets you apart.
  • Better marketing and better customer service than competitors lead to more sales of the same products.
  • Dropshipping is a real business that has existed for at least 15 years and is still viable (able to work).
  • It is cheaper to start than many other models.
  • It still requires real marketing and real customer service.
  • Dropshipping is a different proposition from selling your own brand.
  • As a reseller (someone who sells others' products), you compete as one of many brands in a category.
  • So relying on a single brand is risky. If customers do not want it, nothing sells.
  • Dropshipping from China is not a long-term, sustainable business model.
  • Do not keep running an unprofitable China-based store to recover your investment.
  • Instead, focus on brand dropshipping with US-based suppliers.
  • Dropshipping stores often fade after two or three months.
  • Brands that last test consistently after a winning product and invest in customer experience.
  • Platforms such as Facebook do not ban dropshipping as a model.
  • They judge account quality, customer ratings and copied content, so the way the store operates is what matters.

Cash flow and ad spend

  • Combine short-term cash flow (money in and out) with long-term recurring revenue (income that repeats).
  • Cash from the first sale funds acquisition (paying to win customers), while repeat revenue produces the profit.
  • A churn-and-burn model (spend heavily to win customers, then move on) depends on constant cash for traffic and is stressful.
  • A long-game-only model (slow, long-term focus) suits high-ticket sales that rely on referrals (customers recommending you) and high margins.
  • The real risk in dropshipping is wasted ad spend, so keep spend tied to measured results.

Building your own brand versus dropshipping

2 min

Risks of starting a brand from scratch

  • Starting a brand from scratch carries five major risks:
    1. High upfront investment in product development and inventory.
    2. Difficulty choosing a product or niche with sufficient market demand.
    3. A time-consuming process of building a website, sourcing materials and manufacturing.
    4. Difficulty attracting customers and gaining traction in crowded markets.
    5. Risk of overestimating demand and holding excess stock that cannot be sold.
  • Building a product and brand from scratch takes four to six months from idea to product.
  • It requires upfront investment in development and inventory.
  • It risks choosing a product with insufficient demand, or overestimating sales and ending with excess stock.

Lean startup approach

  • The lean startup method (from Eric Ries' book The Lean Startup) says a business should validate the product before investing heavily.
  • Validate means proving the product works and that buyers want it. This is called product-market fit (the product matches what buyers want).
  • Test before you spend big on production or marketing.
  • Use a minimum viable product (the simplest version you can test) and a proof of concept (a small test that shows the idea can work).

Brand over the long term

  • A dropshipping brand takes longer to build, but it can produce steadier, more predictable income.
  • It gives the owner more time and stability.
  • The trade-off is lower speed to launch and more upfront patience.
  • Running a store to scale a single product and then shutting it down is the disposable model.
  • The disposable model limits long-term income.
  • Plan the store as a multi-product brand from the start, even if it begins as dropshipping.
  • Dropshipping remained viable in 2023, but it needed more brand work to stay sustainable.
  • Owners who treat the store as a long-term brand, not a short-term sales vehicle, are better positioned.
  • In the business model that sells other brands' products through your store, the store itself is the brand.
  • It does not involve introducing your own private-label products (products sold under your own brand).

Compared with Amazon FBA (Fulfilled by Amazon: Amazon stores and ships your products) and private label

3 min

Private label on Amazon

  • With Amazon FBA you own the private label product.
  • So you set the price and decide how much inventory to hold.
  • Unlike dropshipping, you cannot blame a supplier when an item is out of stock, because the inventory risk is yours.
  • Amazon FBA handles storage, shipping, returns, refunds and most customer service for the seller.
  • This lets one person or a small team scale a product without a large staff.
  • In FBA, the seller's core jobs are:
    • Product selection.
    • Finding a manufacturer.
    • Managing inventory levels.
    • Building a high-converting product page (a page that turns visitors into buyers).
    • Driving traffic, often with Amazon ads.
  • Inventory management is the seller's biggest responsibility, especially before Q4 (the last three months of the year, the holiday season).
  • One FBA product can reach about $10,000 profit per month.
  • This takes work and is not an overnight process.
  • Most sellers become profitable within one to three months of starting, once the first inventory order is sold.
  • Compared with dropshipping, FBA sellers source and own their product.
  • FBA sellers face a longer and trickier supplier phase.
  • They depend on Amazon as a third-party platform (a site run by someone else).
  • Overseas suppliers add time zone differences and a language barrier. The seller must plan for both.
  • FBA private label products usually sit in a lower price range where impulse buying (buying on a whim) is strong.
  • Dropshipping usually targets items above $200.
  • Amazon pays sellers directly into their bank account every two weeks in eligible countries, whatever bank the seller uses.
  • The payout schedule supports cash flow planning for the business.
  • Case: a seller in the course group sold 430 units (single items) in two weeks, generating almost $9,500 in sales at an average price of $22 per unit.
  • Over that span, the sales data moved from 430 to 513 units, and the two weeks together brought in almost $10,000 in sales.
  • This shows that a sub-$25 product (under $25) can still produce solid revenue when the volume is consistent.
  • Case: a different seller sold 129 units in one week for $5,000 in sales at an average price of $43 per unit.
  • Results like this show that a price around $40 to $45 can still generate strong revenue without needing a very high unit count.
  • Many new sellers aim to sell under $10, but this is a poor strategy for Amazon FBA.
  • The course recommends choosing a product that can carry enough profit per unit to fund paid advertising.
  • Amazon FBA carries these risks:
    • High competition and saturation in popular categories (too many sellers with the same products).
    • Very thin price margins.
    • Amazon controlling customer data and branding.
    • A 20 to 30 percent commission on each sale.
    • Risk of account suspension or restriction for policy violations.
  • Selling on Amazon requires bulk inventory and carries higher risk.
  • Margins can be as low as 15% or $5 to $10 per unit.
  • Amazon keeps customer data and takes a cut of revenue. This makes building a brand harder than in dropshipping.
  • The 15% commission figure comes from a 2020 comparison. Marketplace commissions were the benchmark (the standard to compare against) for Shopping Actions (a Google shopping program).
  • Amazon was cited as charging 15% of the sale price.
  • The original Shopping Actions commission averaged about 12% and ranged from roughly 8% to above 12%.

  • The 15% figure comes from a 2020 comparison.
  • The 20 to 30% figure comes from Source C.
  • The 15% value is older. Check the current fee schedule before you set prices.

Digital products

2 min

Why digital products

  • Digital products have no fulfillment costs and no shipping delays.
  • Digital products can educate buyers.
  • They are a separate business model that requires its own research on creation, advertising and finding products to sell.
  • Digital products such as ebooks (electronic books) give a gross margin of roughly 90 percent, meaning the share of each sale left after the product's direct cost.
  • The product is created once and then sold repeatedly without extra cost per order.
  • The only ongoing cost is traffic (visitors), paid or organic (free).
  • Digital products such as ebooks and digital planners carry no fulfillment cost.
  • This makes them profitable at low margins while still delivering the product instantly.
  • Note on digital margins: the two sources describe the margin in different ways.
  • One source gives a gross margin of roughly 90% for ebooks.
  • Another describes them as profitable at low margins because there is no fulfillment cost.
  • Both apply: the first describes the margin on each sale, the second describes the profitability of low-priced items.
  • Digital products have instant fulfillment. The buyer receives a download link (a web link to the file) or the PDF (a document file) by email right after purchase.
  • There are no shipping delays or lost-parcel problems.
  • Because fulfillment is easy and customers get what they expect, customer support load (number of help requests) and refund impact are smaller than with physical shipped products.
  • Digital downloads let a seller monetize traffic (make money from visitors) from countries the physical product cannot ship to.
  • Designs with no written instructions avoid a language barrier.
  • Use digital offers to capture buyers that a physical-only product would lose.
  • Market saturation (too many sellers) is not a real concern for knowledge products.
  • Each new author can add value through a different point of view on the same topic.
  • Digital products can run alongside physical dropshipping products in the same business.
  • Copywriting skills (writing sales text) transfer to both.
  • When selling the same digital product in another country, adjust the pricing, the payment providers and the wording to that market.
  • Expertise in how one country differs from another, such as its payment setup and local terms, is itself a value that can be turned into an ebook.
  • Digital products such as Lightroom presets (saved photo-editing settings) sold well.
  • Many people lack the skill or time to edit photos themselves, so they buy presets to adjust.
  • Choose digital products that solve a clear problem for the buyer.

Product size rules

  • A recipe ebook should not be sold below 20 pages.
  • Other digital products can be shorter. For example, a two-page Facebook ads strategy is fine.
  • A shorter product is fine as long as the buyer gets the full value they need.

Starting with an information product

  • A first online venture can be a low-cost info product (a product that teaches), such as a self-published e-book (published by yourself).
  • Its main use is to learn how online sales work.
  • Asking an established operator (someone with a running business) what they sell and how they market it can lead to a more proven model, such as dropshipping.

Affiliate marketing

1 min

Model and types

1 min
  • In affiliate marketing, you promote a product that you neither create nor fulfill (fulfill means ship it and handle the order).
  • You earn a commission, which is a share of the sale price, when a visitor you referred buys it.
  • For digital products sold through affiliate directories such as ClickBank (online lists of digital products for affiliates), the merchant (the company that owns the product) handles refunds and customer questions.
  • A personal brand, where you become the known face of the business, is built on consistent content.
  • Examples include a blog, a podcast, or regular guest posts (articles or talks on other people's sites or shows).
  • A personal brand builds credibility (trust in you) and a loyal audience that follows you across products.
  • A personal brand is a long-term model with a slow start and more time commitment.
  • Once established, you can keep promoting new products in your niche (a narrow topic area, such as home gardening).
  • A funnel-based affiliate business (a set of steps that moves a visitor from a page to a purchase) has no personal face.
  • A funnel-based business builds a weaker audience connection, but it launches faster.
  • You can set up several niche sites (small websites on one narrow topic).
  • Most of the work is front-loaded, meaning done at the start.
  • Afterward, the sites run largely on autopilot (on their own, with little help) and need reduced maintenance.
  • Digital affiliate offers include downloads, software subscriptions such as website builders, and email autoresponders (tools that send automatic emails).
  • Digital affiliate offers also include non-online offers such as cruises.
  • Choosing among these depends on what your audience already searches for and wants to buy.

Print on demand

1 min
  • Print on demand means items are printed only after an order comes in. It is very hard to make meaningful money with.
  • Profit per sale is tiny.
  • It is better treated as a brand-building add-on than as a profit source.
  • Personal items such as clothing and bedding have high return rates (many buyers send them back) when fit or feel does not match expectations.
  • A brand merchandise store (a shop selling products with your brand name) sells inexpensive items such as t-shirts, mugs and hats.
  • Such a store has very thin margins (little money kept after costs).
  • Keep it for brand merchandise rather than treating it as a profit source.
  • Print on demand is a community-building add-on, not a primary revenue source.
  • Add it only after a store has a loyal audience that wants branded products.
  • Print on demand is not recommended.
  • Product prices are too low, margins are too thin, and ads are hard to run profitably.
  • There is too little profit per sale for the work involved.
  • Note on print on demand: the 2020 sources differ in strength. One treats it as merchandise only, one as a community add-on, and one as not recommended.
  • The 2025 source gives the most current position: a brand-building add-on, not a profit source.

Marketplaces

1 min

Marketplace selling as a separate model

1 min
  • Marketplace selling means selling on large shopping sites that host many sellers, such as Amazon.
  • Marketplace selling is a separate model from dropshipping (a supplier ships each order to the buyer, so you never hold stock).
  • The seller lists products on large retail marketplaces without stocking them.
  • The seller fulfills an order only once it sells.
  • Etsy suits small handmade or home businesses.
  • Etsy is less suited for building a branded store than Shopify (a platform for running your own online store), which gives more control over brand presentation (how your brand looks to buyers).
  • Depending on a single marketplace is a single point of failure (one weak spot that can stop everything).
  • An account suspension (a block on your account) or restriction for a policy violation (breaking the platform's rules) can shut the whole business down.
  • A seller who runs their own website keeps the business alive regardless of what any one marketplace decides.
  • Marketplaces such as Amazon can remove a seller's account and destroy the business overnight.
  • An own website gives control over the brand, the customer data and the option to sell the business later.

Is dropshipping still viable?

2 min
  • Viable means it can still make real money.
  • Dropshipping is not dead.
  • Claims that it has died come up every year, while e-commerce (buying and selling online) keeps growing.
  • It would only die if people stopped buying online.
  • Dropshipping is a real business that has existed for at least 15 years.
  • Dropshipping is still viable and is cheaper to start than many other models.
  • Dropshipping is a short-term model only when done poorly.
  • Done correctly, it builds a brand and customer knowledge that can be used later to sell your own products.
  • Dropshipping stores often fade after two or three months.
  • Brands that last test consistently after a winning product (a product that sells well).
  • Brands that last also invest in customer experience.
  • Platforms (websites and apps that host ads and shops) such as Facebook do not ban dropshipping as a model.
  • Platforms judge account quality, customer ratings and copied content.
  • After the pandemic (the global COVID-19 health crisis), e-commerce adoption normalized, meaning it returned to the historical 20-year growth trend.
  • The spike during lockdown (government stay-at-home orders) created excess capacity (more business capacity than needed).
  • Shopify laid off 1,000 people (10% of its workforce) on July 26, 2022.
  • The layoff happened because growth returned to normal rather than to a sustained high trajectory.
  • Shopify's public data reportedly showed about a 10% revenue increase (more money from sales) for the average store in Q1 2025 (the first three months of 2025) compared with Q1 2024.
  • Use this only as a general market signal (a broad sign, not proof for your own store).
  • AI dropshipping keeps the same business model as regular dropshipping: selling products online without holding inventory (stock).
  • The difference is that AI (artificial intelligence, computer tools that make decisions and create content) is applied at every step of the process.
  • The seller can move faster, smarter and leaner.
  • Between 2016 and 2018, vendors flooded AliExpress listings with lengthy delivery times.
  • AliExpress is a large online marketplace that connects buyers with overseas sellers.
  • The approach paid off because Facebook advertising was inexpensive, yet the buyer experience suffered.
  • Branded dropshipping took off from 2019 through 2021.
  • Quicker delivery, stronger ad creatives (the images and videos used in ads) and bespoke storefronts (custom-made online shops) set the successful operators apart.
  • Most workflows (the step-by-step way work gets done) stayed hands-on, meaning done manually by people. Experimentation moved slowly.
  • From 2023 onward, the AI era allows a business to run with fewer people, lower costs and data-driven decisions (choices based on numbers) instead of intuition alone (gut feeling).

  • "Not dead" applies to dropshipping as a whole.
  • "Fades after two or three months" applies to stores run without a brand plan.
  • The short-term outcome depends on how the store is built.

Choosing a business model

1 min
  • When choosing an online business model, compare the options on three criteria (tests).
  • Check whether it can be run remotely (from anywhere, online).
  • Check how much startup capital (money needed to begin) it needs.
  • Check whether it can be built part-time next to a job.
  • Common options to compare are affiliate marketing, selling a service, selling online education and selling physical products through e-commerce.
  • Physical-product dropshipping can meet all three criteria for someone who still has a job and fixed financial obligations (bills or payments due every month).
  • Dropshipping with approved suppliers (vetted companies you have checked) and low-priced products suits a first step with low capital.
  • Choose the model that matches your remote working needs, your starting capital and the time you can give alongside other obligations.

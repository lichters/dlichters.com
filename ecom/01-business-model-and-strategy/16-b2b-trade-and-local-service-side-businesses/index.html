B2B, trade and local-service side businesses

Section 1.16 · 16 of 23 in this chapter · 12 min read

In short

  • Start small: sell hands-on help to shop owners first, then one-on-one coaching. Build a paid online course only after many clients ask the same questions.
  • Decide before a sale what you will and won't do, and what the buyer must do.
  • Outside client work needs at least $25,000 paid upfront. Local ad management usually costs $2,000 to $5,000 a month.
  • Business customers (B2B) need a separate "To the Trade" page. Build it early if you chase them, or wait until they start asking for it.

Sequencing income from expertise

1 min
  • Expertise is your skill or knowledge. You can turn it into money in stages.
  • Stage 1: sell hands-on work to other store owners.
  • Stage 2: sell one-on-one coaching.
  • Stage 3, later: sell a structured online course. A course is worth building when the same questions and problems keep coming up across many clients.
  • Dropshipping means selling products you never stock. The supplier ships each order to the buyer.
  • A dropshipping business and a service business can work together.
  • The service business brings in cash flow, which is money coming in. It also teaches you skills.
  • Income covers your living costs. Education builds the skills you need for the bigger vision.
  • Running an e-commerce store (an online shop) trains you in several areas at once:
    • marketing
    • logistics (moving and delivering products)
    • customer service
    • project management
    • web design
  • Pick the part you enjoy most. Sell it as a service, such as a copywriting agency (writing sales text), a video editing agency, or a web design agency.
  • Skills from dropshipping can be sold to other businesses. These include:
    • building websites and online stores
    • running ads
    • copywriting
    • creating offers
    • handling customer service
  • An agency is a small business that sells services to other businesses. You can build one alongside your store or after it.
  • Running a service agency next to a dropshipping business gives you a second income stream. You also learn while testing other people's products.
  • Example: one operator dropshipped a Swiss Invisalign offer (clear teeth aligners) by running ads for a dentist. Meanwhile he grew his own brands.

Setting boundaries and contract rules

1 min
  • A contract is the agreement that sets the rules between you and a client.
  • Before a sale starts, write down:
    • what you will do during the sale and after it
    • what you will not do
    • what you expect from the buyer
    • what the buyer expects from you
    • how you will meet each expectation
  • Clear boundaries let you earn well from limited hours. They also leave time for your personal life.
  • Your income depends heavily on how much work you accept. Breaking your own limits to close a deal usually costs you more later.
  • Accept outside client work only with a minimum upfront fee of $25,000. Upfront means paid before the work starts.
  • Higher fees tend to filter out difficult clients. They also limit the time taken away from your own products.
  • Take on consulting work (paid advice) or service work only when:
    • The client can realistically earn back significantly more than your fee
    • you like the product or niche (the market you serve)
  • Ask prospective clients (people who may hire you) whether they care about your effort or only about the result.
  • Clients who care only about the result fit performance-based pay. This means you get paid when results happen.
  • Only accept deals where you are paid when you win.
  • Pricing and contract structure are among the main levers for earning more while working fewer hours.
  • Plan them before you start selling, not after.

Choosing a local service agency

1 min
  • A dropshipping operator with little cash can fund the business by selling services to local businesses.
  • Local services include websites, online stores and social media setup.
  • Reinvest the fees into ad budgets (the money you spend on ads).
  • Your existing dropshipping and paid-ads knowledge is the core asset of this service business.
  • When cash is short, help local businesses in your city sell products with a web store.
  • This turns your dropshipping knowledge into a paid service. You don't have to stop the business.
  • Offer local clients a small set of clear services:
    • a social media presence
    • a website
    • an online store
  • Once the client is happy with the setup, upsell paid ad management. Upselling means offering an extra or bigger service.
  • A website is useful to a client who only wants to communicate their brand.
  • An online store fits a client who wants to sell online but lacks the setup or time.
  • Build your sales pitch around these two needs. This makes the sales talk easier.
  • Start a local agency by choosing one city and focusing on clients there.
  • Expand abroad only after your home city is established.
  • Countries such as Canada and Australia were planned for later. A remote cold-caller (someone who calls people who did not ask for a call) was considered for time-zone differences, which are different clock times in each country.
  • Do a first project for free for a friend, family member or early contact.
  • Free work builds a portfolio (a collection of your past work) and proves quality.
  • Early free work can lead to referrals. Referrals bring in your first paid clients.

Building websites and stores for clients

2 min

Tool choice

  • Shopify is a tool for building online stores.
  • Squarespace and Wix are website builders. They are drag-and-drop tools, so you move blocks with the mouse and need no code.
  • For a simple client who needs only text and pictures, use Squarespace. It has attractive designs at a fair price. The client can also edit the site themselves.
  • Use Wix when the client needs more custom layout. Examples are text in specific places or embedded video (a video placed inside the page).
  • Choose a drag-and-drop builder such as Squarespace or Wix for service websites.
  • Use Shopify for any client who needs to sell products online.

  • A small agency can offer a complete package to a client:
    • a Shopify store
    • product photos
    • a photo shoot
    • offers

Pricing ad management for local clients

1 min
  • After a two-week ad test shows results for a local client, you can charge a monthly fee to manage their Facebook ads.
  • The range is $2,000 to $5,000 per month, depending on the client's size.
  • Discuss the fee when you visit the client's office to review the results.

Note: The $25,000 minimum upfront fee applies to outside client work in general. The local ad-management range above is much lower and applies to local retainers (fixed monthly fees for ongoing work). Use whichever figure matches the engagement.

Building a sales team and reselling skills

1 min
  • Freelance sales partners (independent people who work for many clients) can be recruited locally.
  • Train them on how to acquire customers.
  • Pay them a commission (a share of each sale) for every client they bring in.
  • Pay a commission to a client-referral partner, meaning someone who sends you new clients.
  • Let referred clients pay the agency's virtual assistants (remote helpers) through the agency website.
  • The agency keeps a commission on each engagement.
  • The agency model can grow by recruiting and training freelancers in your country.
  • Pay them per client.
  • This lets you scale (grow) the sales work without making every call yourself.
  • Skills such as writing product descriptions, blog posts and ad copy with ChatGPT (an AI writing tool) can be sold.
  • Sell them as a copywriting or freelance service to local businesses. This is an extra income source.
  • You can get any skill you lack from Fiverr or Upwork (websites where freelancers offer their work). Then resell it to clients.
  • Example: a store owner resold a service to a local dentist. A Fiverr freelancer made tooth animations. The owner added a markup (an extra amount on top of the cost) and delivered the result to the client.

Combining services with physical products

1 min
  • A service business can be combined with physical products on one store.
  • Example: sell an installation service locally. Subcontract (hire another firm for) jobs in other areas. Sell the product to buyers who want to install it themselves.

B2B fundamentals

1 min
  • B2B means business to business: you sell to companies.
  • B2C means business to consumer: you sell to people.
  • The full process of building a store applies to B2B niches just as it does to B2C niches.
  • Examples of B2B products include safety cones, bags of cement and commercial refrigerators.
  • B2B orders are usually much larger than B2C orders.
  • The extra customer service effort they need is generally worth it.
  • B2B and B2C both produce successful stores. No clear rule shows that one always earns more.
  • Income depends on the whole plan:
    • niche
    • supplier
    • ad optimization (improving ads over time)
    • execution (doing the work well)
  • The niche is only one part of it.
  • B2B buyers rarely convert through a normal add-to-cart flow. Converting means a visitor becomes a buyer. Add-to-cart is the normal step where a shopper puts an item in their basket.
  • A few large orders a year can matter more to revenue than many small retail sales.
  • Wholesale means selling in large amounts at lower unit prices to resellers.
  • Wholesale accounts usually do not fit a dropship store. A buyer can simply order from the supplier directly.
  • Offering wholesale only makes sense if you:
    • private label the products (put your own brand on them), or
    • run your own dropship program
  • B2B can come first.
  • A founder started with a B2B-only website for hotels and boutiques (small specialty shops).
  • He received two wholesale orders through Instagram, from a tea room and an organic store.
  • The wholesale demand proved the product was wanted.
  • The logos of those accounts can be shown on the website to build credibility (trust).
  • B2B can come first when early orders come from hotels or boutiques.
  • A clean B2C site can then serve as a credibility page for wholesale accounts.
  • B2B can eventually reach 80 to 90 percent of revenue.

Heavy and high-ticket B2B

1 min
  • Sell heavy products to businesses only if the margin (the money left after costs) covers the shipping cost.
  • Heavy items can lose money at low prices, even when margins look fine on paper.
  • Before you list a heavy B2B product, compare three numbers:
    • The item price
    • The wholesale cost
    • The shipping cost
  • Check whether it still leaves a healthy profit after free shipping (where the store pays shipping).
  • B2B high-ticket sales (expensive sales) are not sold through social ads directly.
  • Example: about $120,000 to $150,000 per container (a large shipping box).
  • Generate leads first. A lead is a possible buyer who showed interest. Get leads through an email list or a sales call.
  • Then qualify them. This means checking that the lead is real and can pay.
  • Then run a longer consultative sales cycle. This is a longer sales process where you give advice. Work with the economic buyer (the person who controls the money) and the decision makers (the people who approve the purchase).

Finding business buyers

1 min
  • For B2B sales, LinkedIn advertising (ads on a professional social network) is expensive and hard to make profitable.
  • Instead, create a business profile.
  • Join relevant associations and groups.
  • Post content on the company page.
  • To find business buyers, one-to-many methods reach more buyers with less effort than one-to-one emails or cold calls.
  • One-to-many methods include:
    • dedicated trade pages
    • LinkedIn content
    • group participation
  • The same marketing that works for consumers also applies to B2B.
  • Business buyers find an e-commerce store through the same channels as consumers, such as:
    • Google search
    • remarketing ads (ads shown again to people who visited before)
  • They need no separate setup to find you.
  • Add a dedicated trade page only after business inquiries start arriving. (source 2025)
  • To reach business buyers for a niche product:
    1. Identify online and offline trade publications (magazines and sites for an industry) that already reach the people who buy that product.
    2. Propose a partnership. The publication promotes your offer, and its audience earns a commission on sales.
    3. Expect this to be harder for products that apply to every business, because no single trade audience fits them.
  • Paid traffic training (learning to buy visitors with ads) applies to service businesses too, with some changes.
  • A new store should start with a small ad budget to find what works.
  • Scale up (spend more) only after results show.
  • Government and school vendor registration (signing up as a supplier) is handled through the form each agency or body sends.
  • The source found nothing beyond completing that form was needed.

Building a To the Trade page

2 min

Template for the page:

Create a separate page on the store titled something like To the Trade. Describe the products for business buyers, state that volume discounts and custom orders are offered, and add a contact form, which Shopify can provide. Link the page in the footer menu, because business buyers often want to contact the seller before ordering in bulk.

Procedure for a B2B or trade page:

  1. Add a short paragraph stating that you work business to business with a specific trade or niche.
  2. Add a contact form with name, email, phone, products of interest, quantity needed and required delivery date.
  3. Show logos of past business customers to add credibility.

Procedure for capturing business buyers on a B2C store:

  1. Add a "To the Trade" page that lists logos of past business customers.
  2. Add a contact form that asks for the quantity needed, the products wanted, and the date they are needed by, so the seller can send a quote.
  3. Expect bulk orders and price them with that in mind.

Procedure for a trade or wholesale tab:

  1. Add a clearly labeled trade or wholesale tab or button to the website.
  2. Show photos of gyms or businesses that already use your product type.
  3. Add a call to action (CTA), meaning a clear request for the visitor to act, and a simple form that asks about the buyer's needs.
  4. Call the people who submit the form to start the conversation and tailor the order to their needs.

Note on placement: The footer link in the 2019 template and the header link in the 2020 advice both make the page easy to find.

  • The header is the top part of the website. Use it for business owners who browse the main navigation.
  • The footer is the bottom part of the website. Use it for buyers who look for contact details before ordering in bulk.

Note on timing: The older advice (2018 to 2022) recommends building the trade page up front to capture bulk buyers.

  • The 2025 advice waits until business inquiries start arriving.
  • The older approach fits a store that is actively courting bulk buyers.
  • The 2025 approach fits a store whose main traffic is consumer-driven and that has no inbound trade demand yet. Inbound means people come to you on their own.

Note on B2C stores with business customers: If you sell B2C but also get business customers, keep the store positioned as B2C. Add a separate "To the Trade" page for business buyers. Link that page in the header navigation so business owners see it.

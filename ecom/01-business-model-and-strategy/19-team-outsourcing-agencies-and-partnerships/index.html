Playbook › 1. Business model and strategy

Team, outsourcing, agencies and partnerships

Section 1.19 · 19 of 23 in this chapter · 12 min read

In short

  • Learn the core skills yourself first. Hand off tasks only after you know what good work looks like.
  • Keep product choice, ads and product descriptions in your own hands. These shape your brand.
  • Hire a virtual assistant (VA, a remote helper) only when monthly profit reaches roughly 3,000 to 4,000 EUR.
  • Do not hire an agency to build ads for a new store. Agencies rarely get results from zero.

Skills to build before delegating

1 min

  • Six core skills are needed for a dropshipping business. Dropshipping means selling products that a supplier ships to your customer, so you do not hold stock.

  • The six skills are niche selection (picking a narrow market), Shopify store development (building the shop on Shopify, an online store tool), copywriting (writing words that sell), deal-making (negotiating prices and terms), paid advertising on Google and Facebook (ads you pay for each time they are shown or clicked), and search engine optimization (SEO, getting found in free search results).

  • A weakness in any one of them limits the whole business.

  • A beginner first builds skills in product research (finding products to sell), store building and marketing.

  • Do this before chasing revenue (money coming in).

  • The business has three linked parts: product, store and marketing.

  • A weak result in one part usually shows up as a weak point in the funnel (the path from first visit to purchase).

  • Check the three parts in that order.

  • Build the business on learned skills, not only on tools.

  • Useful skills include consumer psychology (how people think and buy), advertising best practices, and design principles.

  • Design principles include color theory (how colors work together), layout, and Gestalt proximity (items placed close together look related).

  • Persuasion principles include scarcity (limited supply makes things seem more wanted) and reciprocity (people feel they should return a favor).

  • Books cited as starting points are The Mini MBA (a short business-basics book) and Cialdini's Six Principles of Persuasion (a book on how people are influenced).

Delegation rules

1 min

  • Spend your time on tasks that directly produce revenue, such as writing sales copy or making sales videos.

  • Hand off or drop tasks you are not strong at. They take time away from the work that earns money.

  • The biggest beginner mistake is trying to do everything in the business yourself.

  • Map out which roles you enjoy and keep those.

  • Outsource the rest, meaning pay someone outside to do it.

  • Doing everything alone can lead to burnout (exhaustion from overwork).

  • Delegate only tasks that someone else can do without lowering the quality of the business.

    • Keep in-house: brand-shaping work (work that defines your brand, your business identity), such as choosing products, running ads and writing product descriptions.
    • Hand off: supplier negotiation, email replies, order fulfillment (packing and shipping orders) and tasks the owner dislikes.

  • A first-time seller should not outsource all content creation to a freelancer right away.

  • Outsourcing everything blocks the owner from learning the platform and advertising.

  • Outsourced content tends to cost more in the long run and rarely matches what the owner could produce.

  • Learn to make the content and run ads yourself first.

  • Then hire, once you know what good output looks like.

  • Advertising skills are the foundation for hiring well.

  • The owner must know consumer psychology, target audience needs and platform mechanics (how the ad platforms work).

  • You need this knowledge to judge the work of anyone producing content.

  • Learn these before delegating.

Time budgeting

1 min

  • Target: a semi-automated online store (mostly run by tools that repeat tasks) that the owner can run in 30 minutes or less per day.

  • It does not need to be fully hands-off.

  • It should not require daily involvement from sunrise to sunset.

  • Once a store is running, maintenance (upkeep) can be handled in 15 to 30 minutes per day.

  • Later, it can be handled in a few hours per week.

  • Building the store is far more work than this. It includes supplier calls and setup.

  • **

  • Picking (taking items from stock), packing and posting every order yourself can take 2 to 3 hours a day.

  • This is a poor return on time.

  • It becomes the limit on growth.

  • Progress tends to happen in phases.

  • Phase one: time is traded for money through hands-on work.

  • Phase two: money is reinvested to buy systems and help that generate more money.

  • Moving from phase one to phase two requires systemizing (writing down and repeating what works).

Automation order

1 min

  • Automate first the repeatable business processes.

  • Automation means tools that do repeat tasks for you.

  • Repeatable processes run the same way every time. They can be copied without deep understanding.

  • Automation comes before outsourcing, and it can be started very early.

  • Only add automation and outsourcing once the store is already profitable (earning more than it spends).

  • The core setup tasks must also be completed.

  • A store that has not reached that stage is not ready for these steps.

  • The sources set different conditions for timing:

    • Single repeatable processes: automate early, with no profit requirement stated.
    • Wider automation and outsourcing: only after profit and completed core setup.

VA hiring timing

1 min

  • Once a store is profitable and orders are being fulfilled, the first reinvestment worth making is hiring a virtual assistant (VA, a remote helper).

  • A VA takes over recurring day-to-day tasks.

  • Hire only when the business can afford it.

  • Consider hiring a VA once the store has steady orders.

  • Consider it once monthly profit reaches roughly 3,000 to 4,000 EUR.

  • At that level, outsourcing time-consuming work frees the owner to focus on ads and the offer (the deal you present to buyers).

  • Order handling uses a different trigger.

  • Move order handling to suppliers or a team as early as possible.

  • Picking, packing and posting can take 2 to 3 hours a day.

  • Case: a non-technical parent (someone without tech skills) went from hand-selling (selling personally, one by one) to a delegated store (one run by hired helpers).

Team structure and partnerships

1 min

  • Team at scale (a large, growing business): 35 people across 3 stores.

  • Of the 3 stores, 2 are actively operating and 1 is in growth phase.

  • The business handles hundreds of orders per month.

  • This size allows specialization (each person doing one kind of work), delegation, and delegation of hiring to team leaders.

  • Large dropshipping operations at nine-figure revenue (at least 100 million dollars a year) commonly show this pattern:

    • They run five to ten stores in parallel rather than one.
    • They use several suppliers, sometimes one store per supplier.
    • They test new products weekly, seeing which go well.
    • Teams usually number 10 to 20 people, mixed across countries such as the Philippines and Pakistan.
    • A single-country team shares too much information in its own language.
    • Supplier choice is secondary to the ads and testing.
    • The business needs enough capital to fund the cash flow (money moving in and out).

  • Stores run day to day by partners can each reach over 15,000 USD in revenue.

  • The owner can keep the stores without daily operations.

  • This model separates ownership from operating work.

  • Handing over operations means giving the daily running of a store to someone else.

  • A store can be outsourced once it reaches roughly 50,000 to 80,000 per month in revenue.

  • This was given as the target before handing over operations and opening another store.

  • Partnership: a founder with limited capital (little money) but strong skills can partner with someone who has money but no time.

  • Together they can run a joint store (one owned by both).

  • Such partnerships can be found through local meetups (in-person groups) or existing relationships.

  • Skills, time and money are interchangeable levers (things you can swap for each other).

  • A missing resource can be replaced by another.

Agency evaluation and incentives

2 min

  • An agency is an outside firm you pay for work.

  • Structure agency incentives (the pay rules that reward the agency) so both parties profit from growth.

  • When the business succeeds and you earn more, the agency should also earn more.

  • Misaligned incentives create friction, because the vendor's (the outside provider's) financial interest diverges from yours.

  • Your account size relative to the agency's other clients affects priority and risk:

    • If you are their largest client, you get high attention.
    • But you risk being deprioritized (moved down the list) if they land a bigger client.
    • If you are one of many small clients, your account may be deprioritized for their bigger accounts.

  • An agency's value lies in time and experience with ad accounts (the accounts that run your ads on Google or Facebook).

  • A person who has run ads for two years knows much more than someone who only studied them for months.

  • Hire an agency only once scaling (growing ad spend and sales) exceeds your own consistent knowledge.

  • Until then, learning and testing yourself is the better path.

  • Most ad agencies have little real experience with high-ticket dropshipping (selling expensive products).

  • High-ticket is a very small share of e-commerce (online selling).

  • An agency that claims e-commerce expertise may never have run ads for high-ticket products.

  • High-ticket has a different customer journey (the steps a buyer takes before purchase) than low-ticket e-commerce (cheap products).

  • High-ticket also has thinner margins (less profit per sale).

  • Agencies used to high margins often struggle with this.

  • **

  • Before revenue exists, invest in consulting, coaching or mastermind learning (small groups of owners who learn together).

  • Do not hire a paid traffic agency (a firm that buys visitors with ads) at this stage.

  • Once the business has revenue, calculate whether an outside Google Ads specialist can add enough profit to justify the fee.

  • Do not hire a marketing agency to run or build ads for a new store.

  • Agencies generally cannot create results from zero.

  • Outsourcing makes sense only after the owner's own systems are already profitable.

  • **

  • Example of when an agency that scales ads profitably may be worth discussing:

    • A store with about $100,000 monthly sales.
    • $10,000 monthly ad spend (money spent on ads).
    • $20,000 monthly net profit (profit after all costs).

  • Such a stage is reached only after months of proven results.

  • **

Outsourcing ads

1 min

  • Do not outsource paid ads until the campaigns (ad runs) are set up.

  • Wait until they deliver a return on ad spend (ROAS, money earned for each dollar spent on ads) that you are satisfied with.

  • Outsourcing earlier risks paying agencies whose model relies on signing many clients for a few months.

  • Even when outsourcing paid ads, learn the full traffic process (how visitors reach your store) first.

  • This lets you understand what the agency does and what a campaign should look like.

  • A marketing agency that offers done-for-you paid ads (ads run entirely for you) should be hired only after the campaigns are already profitable.

  • Its job is to maintain or improve them.

  • Start paid growth services and additional marketing only after the store is live.

  • The store should already have traffic and sales.

  • This way, optimization (making results better) is based on real data.

  • Do not outsource paid ads until you have made them profitable yourself.

  • An outside specialist can usually take an account from one to two or maintain a working account.

  • They rarely build it from zero.

  • **

  • Keep control of ad budgets (money set aside for ads) in your own hands.

  • Handing spending to an outside agency without oversight can quickly produce overspending and debt.

Coaching and mentors

2 min

  • Single coaching sessions (one-off expert calls) are one hour long and recorded.

  • Clients receive the recording afterward for reference. **

  • Single coaching sessions can address any aspect of the client's high-ticket dropshipping business.

  • Topics range from initial setup and build-phase questions through marketing, outsourcing, systems and finance management. **

  • Monthly coaching frequency was chosen after testing various intervals (time gaps between calls).

  • It gives enough time between calls to implement (carry out) changes.

  • It allows a meaningful review of progress one month later.

  • Frequent sessions would mean clients complete too little work. **

  • Master Plan 12-month monthly coaching structure **:

    1. First call: analyze the current business state or building-stage progress, and set topic priorities for the coming months.
    2. Assign specific action items (concrete tasks) to complete in the next 30 days.
    3. Point the client to step-by-step guidance for each action item.
    4. Next monthly call: review progress on action items, discuss obstacles encountered, assess completion status, and discuss why any items were not completed.
    5. Identify the next business opportunity or priority to focus on in the following month.
    6. Repeat the monthly cycle for 12 months.

  • Master Plan monthly coaching gives a competitive advantage (an edge over rivals).

  • It does this by providing business-specific, market-specific and product-specific guidance.

  • It deepens general program content without replacing core instruction. **

  • Master Plan 12-month coaching fits two groups:

    • Entrepreneurs (business owners) in the build-toward-launch phase, getting ready to open.
    • Established operators (people already running a business) seeking structured growth guidance over a defined period. **

  • Coaching calls can save large amounts of time, money and energy.

  • You can learn from other operators' mistakes.

  • Treat them as a core business resource rather than an optional extra.

  • Many dropshipping courses sell ready-made material plus a dead community (an inactive group).

  • Students hit a wall (get stuck) when applying the material alone.

  • A paid program should provide live expert review at each major decision point.

  • It should not offer theory alone.

  • Use a mentor or course coach to review the product, offer and targeting (who you show ads to) before scaling.

  • A mentor who knows the product can provide better guidance on audience choice.

Avoiding scammers

1 min

  • Do not respond to cold emails (unasked-for emails) from Shopify "experts" offering design, SEO or marketing services.

  • These companies use volume models (mass sending to huge lists) to contact all new Shopify stores.

  • Delete the emails.

  • If you need help, contact official Source A support or use vetted service providers (checked and approved ones).

  • Unsolicited direct messages (messages you did not ask for) in online communities are a common scam pattern.

  • This is especially true when they ask for money, login details or your store's results.

  • Do not share account information or pay anyone who contacts you first.

AI tools

1 min

  • AI (artificial intelligence, computer programs that generate text and ideas) tools can help you.

  • ChatGPT (an AI chat tool) Plus is the paid version.

  • Plus unlocks plugins (add-on tools), web access and more capable models (smarter AI engines), which the basic free version lacks.

  • Plus costs around 20 dollars per month.

  • It is worth considering once automation tools such as Zapier (a tool that links apps so they run tasks together) are used.

  • Web browsing in ChatGPT may be temporarily disabled.

  • The model's knowledge may only reach back to 2021.

  • Check current news and market information with a live search source.

  • AI tools help with brainstorming (generating ideas), brand name ideas, and graphic mockups (draft visuals) for team review.

  • They also help with drafting email templates (ready-to-edit email drafts).

  • Supplier relationships and direct customer relationships still need people.

  • The trust they build over time cannot be replaced by a tool.

  • AI tools can speed up product research, generate product descriptions and ad copy (short ad text), and support ad targeting (choosing who sees your ads).

  • They reduce guesswork and manual hours.

  • They do not replace the operator's own decisions.

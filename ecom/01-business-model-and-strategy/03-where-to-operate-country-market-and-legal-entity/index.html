Where to operate: country, market and legal entity

Section 1.3 · 3 of 23 in this chapter · 23 min read

In short

  • Pick a market where people have money and buy a lot online, not one where visitors are cheap.
  • If your own country has a real online shopping market, sell there. If not, the US is the default.
  • Use suppliers in the same country as your customers to keep shipping and import costs low.
  • Test one country at a time, and keep the strongest one as your base.
  • Form an LLC (a company that separates your personal money from the business) from the start if you can. A sole proprietor (a one-person business) is only a shortcut.
More key points
  • Related stores go under one company (LLC). Unrelated niches get separate companies.
  • Never mix personal money with business money.
  • Each company needs its own EIN (a US tax number for businesses) and its own bank account.
  • While income is small, register where you already live. Don't spend dozens of hours researching the cheapest place first.

Choosing the home or target market

1 min

Principles

2 min
  • Choose a market by its buying power, not by cheap traffic (visitors sent to your store).
  • Buying power means how much money people have to spend.
  • A country where people have little money to spend is a poor place to test a product.
  • A market where people buy a lot online is a good candidate.
  • This is especially true for cheap items with fast delivery.
  • Dropshipping (you sell, and the supplier ships to the buyer, so you do not keep stock) can run in a country with an established e-commerce market (online selling that is already common). **
  • Examples of such countries are the US, UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand and European countries. **
  • Where e-commerce is less developed, you can still run the business in the US from abroad. This adds setup steps and costs. **
  • The US is the easiest large market for this. **
  • The model works only where e-commerce is established and Google Shopping (product ads shown on Google) is running. **
  • In countries with underdeveloped e-commerce, the local store may not work yet. **
  • To check whether your country has a real domestic (home-country) e-commerce market, ask one question. **
  • Do you regularly buy online from websites based in your own country? **
  • If yes, local suppliers and payment options probably exist. **
  • If you mostly buy from overseas sites, a store aimed at local customers will be harder to run. **
  • Entering a new country should follow research of that market, not assumptions. **
  • Countries such as Australia and the UK were once advised against but later proved successful. **
  • So a country like Spain can be a good market if your own research supports it. **
  • The country-specific demand test (a check of how many people want the product in that country) is an addition to the core product research method.
  • It is not a replacement.
  • Every other part of the core research method still applies when you target a foreign market.
  • A core dropshipping blueprint (the basic step-by-step plan) can be run from any country.
  • International sellers should keep most of their effort on the core process.
  • Treat country-specific adjustments as small add-ons, not a separate model.
  • Choose the countries you advertise to before you set prices.
  • The main markets at the time were the US, UK, Australia, New Zealand and Canada.
  • Each market needs its own price in local currency (the money used in that country).
  • A price that looks normal in one currency can look far too cheap or expensive in another.
  • The local currency for the 800 price minimum is the currency of the country you operate from. **
  • If you are based in a country without an established e-commerce market, the reference is USD (US dollars) and the business is set up in the US. **

Home-country default in detail

1 min
  • For a first high-ticket business, the best default is to run it from the country where you live now. **
  • This keeps shipping, customs and currency complexity low. **
  • Customs means border checks and import taxes.
  • If your country has an established e-commerce market, you can run high-ticket dropshipping there. **
  • If you live in a country without an established e-commerce market, do not start the business there. **
  • Choose a different target market instead. **
  • When your country has no e-commerce market, the default alternative is the US market or the market of the country where you hold citizenship. **
  • The second option only applies if that market is workable for you. **
  • For a first store, sell in the country where you live if it has a real domestic e-commerce market. **
  • Local suppliers, local currency and local payment accounts avoid cross-border payment problems (problems when money moves between countries). **
  • Expansion into other countries can wait until the first business runs well. **
  • For a first store, a home-country market can keep the business simpler when enough suppliers there will sell to dropshippers. **
  • Cross-border sourcing (buying from a neighbouring country) is a workable alternative. **
  • But it adds currency and shipping questions to the pricing. **
  • A business can be run across borders, with the business registered in one country while the operator (the person running it) lives in another. **
  • Partnerships with owners in different countries, such as Australia and the US, are also possible. **
  • Example: a first store in home lighting (chandeliers, pendant lights and wall lights) sourced its products in the same country and sold to local customers. **
  • Starting with domestic sourcing and domestic buyers is a simple way to launch a first store in a single market. **

Step-by-step: choosing a first market (high-ticket, Source B method)

1 min
  1. Determine the country where you currently live.
  2. Check whether that country has an existing e-commerce market.
  3. If it does, run your first high-ticket business there with local suppliers, products stocked in local warehouses and local customers.
  4. If it does not, use the US market or the market of your citizenship country as your default target.

**

1 min

  • For instance, an Australian living in Thailand could run either an Australian-market or a US-market business. **
  • Thailand and Singapore are cited as places where residents often lack an established e-commerce market. **

Europe

1 min
  • EU markets are easier for a beginner to enter than the US, Canada, UK and Australia.
  • The EU is the European Union, a group of European countries.
  • CPMs (the cost to show an ad 1,000 times) are much lower there, and competition is lower.

Asia

1 min

  • The reason: the market is less proven and needs more effort.

  • Whether to enter depends on your goals, your risk tolerance (how much risk you accept) and your available time.

Cross-border shipping, sourcing and pricing

1 min
  • Cross-border means across national borders.
  • High-ticket dropshipping is generally a domestic business, because sellers rarely ship products from their home country across borders. **
  • Some regions, such as parts of Europe, are exceptions. **
  • Cross-border shipping of high-ticket products adds cost through bulkier packages. **
  • It also adds higher freight rates (shipping prices for large goods), customs duties (import taxes) and currency conversion (changing money). **
  • These costs can significantly raise the price for either the seller or the buyer. **
  • Launching a cross-border high-ticket model without pricing in freight, customs duties and currency conversion can leave little or no margin (profit left per sale). **
  • Check these costs before choosing an international setup. **
  • Your customers should be located in the country where your supplier stocks the products. **
  • So the supplier's location determines your target market. **
  • For example, US-based products are sold to US customers. **
  • For a store that targets a country where you do not live, use suppliers located in that country. **
  • Shipping and tariffs (import taxes) from abroad are the main reasons this matters. **
  • Dropshipping from a non-US country works if the supplier ships from a US warehouse or distribution center (a large storage site). **
  • The store can then be run under a US company set-up. **
  • Check the choice of entity (the legal business type) with a forum or professional before setup. **
  • Expansion from North America into the EU is often impractical, because shipping can cost around ten times the North American rate. **

Market testing and expansion order

1 min

Testing

1 min
  • Validate (check that it works) a product in a large market such as the USA or Canada first.
  • Then take the same proven product to another market.
  • The same product can perform very differently across countries.
  • Breaking ad results down by country showed the United States outperforming all other markets for a Facebook-driven store (a store whose sales come mainly from Facebook ads).
  • The seller concentrated spend on the US for four to five months before testing other markets again.
  • Worldwide targeting combined with free shipping made profit difficult to manage.
  • The worldwide spend was reduced and budgets moved to the top 5 to 10 countries.
  • Scaling down a weak region can protect margin (profit per sale) while the strongest markets grow.
  • Choose test and expansion markets by buying power, not only by cheap traffic.
  • Treat the first international order as a milestone that proves the model can scale.
  • Selling the same product outside the home market shows that the business model can grow simply by adding volume and reach.
  • When a product works in one country, expand to other European markets such as Germany and France.
  • Use a separate store for each market.
  • Use a separate ad account or agency account to gather those markets' data, rather than mixing them in one store.

Business entity types

1 min

Sole proprietor

2 min
  • A business entity is the legal form your business takes.
  • A sole proprietorship makes the owner and the business legally the same.
  • Personal and business assets (money and property) are mixed and exposed to business liabilities (debts and legal claims).
  • A sole proprietor is a business run under the owner's personal name with no formal registration. **
  • An LLC (limited liability company) is formed through formal documents with the state. **
  • An S-corp (a tax setting for a company) is a tax designation applied to an existing LLC. **
  • A corporation (a company owned by shareholders) is generally not needed for a dropshipping business. **
  • You can legally start a US dropshipping business as a sole proprietor. **
  • It is wiser to form an LLC from the beginning. **
  • Later conversion to an LLC after selling a business adds work. **
  • Choose a generic business name, form the LLC in your state, and do not spend long deciding. **
  • Any structure works if you commit and move forward. **
  • A business can start as a sole proprietor with an EIN, and an LLC is not required at the beginning.
  • Sole proprietor, LLC and S-corp each have trade-offs, so compare them before choosing.
  • Many beginners start as a sole proprietor, form an LLC and later an S-corp with an accountant once sales come in.
  • A general business name works for several future stores.
  • Many founders start as a sole proprietor using a personal bank account.
  • They later form a corporation with a business bank account and an accountant once profits arrive.
  • A new business can also set up separate entities from day one if it wants full separation.
  • The legal entity choice is a personal decision. **
  • Many owners start as a sole proprietor because the risk is low, and then set up a corporation once the business is making money. **
  • An LLC is the recommended structure from the start.
  • A sole proprietorship is only a reasonable shortcut for someone with almost nothing to lose who wants to avoid an extra filing (extra paperwork sent to the government).

LLC

2 min
  • An LLC is a separate business entity that shields personal assets from the business.
  • Outside the US, a dropshipping business usually has two legal structure options: a sole trader or a limited liability company. **
  • Names differ by country, but Commonwealth countries (a group of countries linked to Britain) use fairly similar structures. **
  • The legal structure, whether sole trader or company, is not visible to customers. **
  • It affects liability and taxes, not the storefront (the store pages customers see), checkout or customer experience. **
  • Use a generic business entity name that is not tied to one niche.
  • Related niches such as desks and chairs can share one entity.
  • An unrelated niche such as greenhouses should get its own new entity.
  • An LLC name does not have to appear on the website. **
  • Many stores show only the store name, and the legal entity can stay private. **
  • The LLC itself does not need a website.
  • Websites are built only for sales stores.
  • A new business name should be the umbrella company (one parent company) that owns related stores.
  • Use a different company name for an unrelated niche.
  • Each store should have its own generic-named company rather than a store name in the company name.
  • A business address that is not the owner's home address is recommended.
  • Do not use a virtual address (a mail-only address) for Google Business Profile (Google's free business listing).
  • That is because it is not required and could be a concern for Merchant Center (Google's product data tool).
  • Consider using a DBA (a "doing business as" name, a public trading name) when the LLC name differs from the store name.
  • That way the store is legally doing business under its public name.
  • A DBA can officially link a store URL (web address) to the business entity.
  • The legal entity name is what goes on banking and tax forms and into the business information in Shopify settings.
  • The store name can differ.

S-corp

1 min
  • For a US business that is in it for the long term, an LLC elected to be taxed as an S-corporation is considered the best structure.
  • Part of the income is paid as a set salary and the rest as distributions (profit payouts), which are taxed at a lower rate.
  • This can save money.
  • The S-corp tax election (your choice to be taxed this way) can be made from the start of the business.
  • It does not have to wait until the business has grown.
  • Whether it makes sense depends on the owner's situation, so a tax professional should confirm it.
  • An S-corp tax election becomes more advantageous as the business earns more profit. **
  • So it is typically considered after revenue grows, not at the start. **

Sources differ on timing. Use when a tax professional confirms that an early election fits your situation. Use as the default, which is to consider the election after revenue grows.

Co-mingling

1 min
  • Mixing personal and business funds (called co-mingling) removes the liability protection of a business entity.
  • Even when a personal account is used as a temporary workaround, keep the money flows (money in and out) separated where possible.

Entity grouping for related and unrelated niches

1 min

An entity is a separate legal company. An LLC (limited liability company) is a common type. It keeps your personal money apart from business debts.

Core rule

1 min
  • For related niches (for example office chairs and desks), operate under a single LLC.
  • For completely unrelated niches (for example office furniture and greenhouses), create separate business entities.
  • Separate entities keep money apart. This is called compartmentalizing finances.
  • Separate entities also make it simpler to sell individual stores later.
  • Closely related businesses (for example modern office desks and modern office chairs) share one business entity.
  • They also share one bank account and one credit card.
  • Unrelated businesses each get their own business entity, bank account, and credit card.
  • To decide if two niches are related, ask: Would the same customer buy from both?
  • Also ask: Would I cross-promote them? Cross-promote means promoting each store to the other store's customers.
  • Group related stores under one LLC.
  • Put unrelated niches into separate LLCs.
  • This keeps money from being mixed.
  • This also simplifies accounting (keeping the financial records).
  • It also makes it easier to sell related stores as a bundle (one package) later.
  • Each niche should stand as its own business line.
  • Related niches that customers can cross-promote can sit under one parent company (the company that owns the others).
  • Unrelated niches need a separate entity.
  • This lets stores be sold together as a network (a group of connected stores).

Related stores

1 min
  • Stores in related niches are grouped under one LLC.
  • This LLC name is used on supplier forms, the bank account, and tax filings.
  • Each store still keeps its own brand and domain (the web address, like yourstore.com).
  • Related stores that are cross-promoted can share one business entity.
  • They can also share one EIN.
  • Example: a trampoline store and a bounce house store, which would be cross-promoted, share one corporation (a type of company).
  • They also share one EIN.
  • Closely related stores sell better as a bundle or network. Buyers value a group of stores in one niche.
  • Keep a separate legal entity for each group of related businesses.
  • This keeps accounting clean.
  • It also lets the group be sold as one unit.
  • **
  • Stores in the same network of related niches should sit under one company.
  • A future buyer of one store usually wants a non-compete covering the whole network. A non-compete is a legal promise not to run a competing business.
  • **
  • Sites in related niches that target the same customers can share one LLC.
  • **
  • Stores that are indirectly related, for example through cross-promotion, should be kept under one business entity.
  • This way they can be sold together later.
  • One company entity can own several stores with different store names and domains.
  • The company's assets are the domains, the store design, the customer lists, and the supplier relationships.
  • So a single entity can hold multiple stores.
  • One company can own several store domains.
  • Form one company, such as a US LLC.
  • Build each store under it.
  • Each store can run as its own brand under the same legal entity.
  • **
  • One LLC can own several niche stores.
  • But each store should keep its own identity and accounts.
  • **

Unrelated stores

1 min
  • Unrelated business areas, such as a store in a different niche, get a separate LLC.
  • This keeps the books and accounting clean.
  • It also makes it simpler to sell one business later without affecting the others.
  • A store in an unrelated niche should get its own LLC and EIN.
  • This lets its accounting be separated if it is sold later.
  • Example: a horse saddle store that is unrelated to the trampoline and bounce house stores gets a new LLC.
  • It also gets a new EIN.
  • A store in an unrelated niche should be under a separate LLC.
  • This way it can be sold or audited (officially checked) without affecting others.
  • **
  • A new, unrelated niche should go under a separate entity.
  • This applies even if the same owner runs it with the same skills.

Brand building, private label and moving beyond dropshipping

Section 1.18 · 18 of 23 in this chapter · 22 min read

In short

  • Your own brand is a later step. Start only with products that already make steady profit.
  • Check the numbers before you spend: sales every day, for weeks, with real profit left over.
  • Building your own brand costs much more money and time. Decide on purpose.
  • Keep your current store running. Its income pays for the brand.
  • Start with one product. Add more only after it proves itself.
More key points
  • Brands grow slowly. Expecting a full brand on day one wastes time and money.
  • Groups of products (collections) usually come after several months, not the first week.
  • A branded holiday can collect emails and build a community.
  • A brand built for value can aim for about 1 million dollars in sales before it is sold.
This section has 12 parts. Tap one to open it.

Deciding When to Build a Brand

2 min
  • A business grows in three ways: it gets more customers, it raises the average order value (AOV, the average amount one order brings in), or it raises the lifetime value (LTV, the total money one customer spends over time).
  • Private labeling (a factory makes the product, and you sell it under your own brand) helps with all three. That is why it is worth checking as a model.
  • Private label manufacturing is optional.
  • It moves the business away from a lifestyle model (a small business that fits around your life instead of growing big). **
  • It needs more time and money.
  • It should be a deliberate decision.
  • Private labeling is a separate profit center (a second source of profit) that you add on top of a dropshipping business. It does not replace dropshipping. Dropshipping means your supplier ships each order straight to the buyer, so you hold no stock.
  • The owner owns the brand and keeps the margin (the money kept from each sale) that a reseller (someone who sells other people's products) would otherwise give away.
  • Own-brand work is a next-stage step, not a starting step.
  • It is meant for advanced sellers who already run a profitable business with regular sales.
  • Adding own branded products is the natural next step after a dropshipping business is successful and growing.
  • It is worth considering when the owner wants the next level of growth for a profitable high-ticket store (a store that sells expensive products).
  • Dropshipping is the lowest-risk way to test products before building a brand.
  • Own-design brands need far more upfront investment (money spent before the first sale) and time.
  • The owner should first understand the general theory of private labeling.
  • Then they should choose one of the few possible directions that fits their model.
  • Private labeling works very differently from business to business.
  • Examples come later, not as a single template.
  • Use dropshipping as the cash engine (the main money source) that funds the brand.
  • Private-label the successful products and order them in bulk (large quantities at once).
  • Once they have made around 50,000 to 100,000 in profit, that cash can go into a long-term brand.

Thresholds and Readiness Signals

1 min

Each threshold applies to a different decision. Check them in the order they appear.

Decision Threshold Units
Confirm the product is really profitable 2-4 weeks of consistent daily sales with confirmed profits, not just order volume (number of orders)
Invest heavily in branding 10 sales per day (70 per week) or more; below that, the cost-benefit (whether the gain is worth the cost) does not justify the investment
Switch from dropshipping to branding with bulk stock Consistent 30 to 50 orders per day for at least 1 to 2 months; below that, buying stock is a large risk
Start a first private label product The dropshipping store is profitable, traffic (visitors) is consistent, and you can run two businesses at once. The move is about lifestyle tradeoff and workload, not only revenue. **
  • Invest in stock carefully, even after proof of demand (evidence that people want it).
  • Reorder only what sells out quickly.
  • This keeps the risk of unsold stock (inventory that does not sell) low.
  • Brand building needs a large upfront investment, including minimum order quantities (MOQ, the smallest order a factory accepts).
  • Start with winning products that generate profit.
  • Move to your own brand once you know which products you can stand behind.
  • Finding a suitable product for a brand took about 2.5 years in one case.
  • The owner waited until confident in the product.
  • Plan for a long validation period (time spent checking that the product works and sells) before building a brand.
  • Keep dropshipping running while building a brand on the side.
  • Dropshipping generates the cash flow (money coming in) that funds the brand.
  • Shutting down a winning dropshipping business to focus on a brand risks losing that cash flow if the brand fails.

Capital and Cost Planning

1 min
  • An own-brand e-commerce business needs about $15,000 to $20,000 set aside before launch.
  • That money is needed because manufacturing and advertising must be paid before sales arrive.
  • This applies to building a brand, not to dropshipping.
  • Importing your own products from overseas, instead of dropshipping, needs a much larger budget.
  • Often tens of thousands of dollars go into inventory (stock you hold).
  • The seller also pays fulfillment centers (warehouses that store and ship goods).
  • The seller uses a shipping agent (a company that books transport) to book ocean freight (shipping by sea), for example from China to the US.
  • Own-design fashion brand costs:

Designs and custom branding, at start About 10,000-15,000 USD
Influencer (a person with an audience who promotes products) and celebrity content for launch About 5,000-8,000 USD

Patent (legal protection for a design), sampling (making test versions) and tech pack (a detailed design sheet for the factory) About 3,000-4,000 USD, about 3-4 months or longer; depends on location and target market
  • Thank-you cards and tags designed on Fiverr (a freelancer website) typically cost under 5 USD.
  • A good design can cost up to about 20 USD.
  • A custom box design commissioned on Fiverr or Upwork (freelancer websites) typically costs between 20 USD and 100 USD.

Dropshipping-to-Brand Path

3 min
  • There are two proven strategies.
  • For dropshipping, find a winning product and scale it (grow its sales) across multiple countries.
  • For branding, build a brand alongside dropshipping.
  • Some very large sellers run only dropshipping and avoid branding.
  • The choice depends on what the owner wants.
  • Moving from general stores (stores that sell many kinds of products) to a single brand is a later step.
  • Building a brand alongside general stores took about five to six months in the example given.
  • Path from dropshipping to own brand:
    1. Test products and niches (narrow market groups) without large inventory or upfront costs.
    2. Use customer feedback and insights to pivot (switch direction) to the products or niches with the most potential.
    3. Once a product shows market reaction, improve the offer, for example with a better deal or extra products.
    4. Customize packaging and branding, for example with thank-you cards and inserts (small leaflets in the box) from the fulfillment provider (the company that ships your orders).
    5. Optimize the funnel (the path a buyer takes from ad to purchase), product offering and marketing with customer feedback to build a loyal community and recognizable brand.
  • Build the direct-to-consumer (DTC, selling straight to buyers on your own site) base first.
  • Add major retail (big stores that sell your product) later.
  • When choosing retail partners, pick a few good ones, support them, and expect them to support you in return.
  • Full sequence for a branded dropshipping business:
    • Understand the business model.
    • Learn suppliers and fulfillment.
    • Research and validate products.
    • Build the store.
    • Run and test ads.
    • Add recurring revenue (income that repeats, such as subscriptions).
    • Produce content.
    • Scale with systems and automation (tools that run tasks for you).
  • Skills learned in high-ticket dropshipping carry over to other models.
  • These skills include Google Shopping (shopping ads on Google), SEO (getting free traffic from search results), email marketing and retargeting (showing ads again to people who already visited).
  • They carry over to models like Amazon FBA (Fulfillment by Amazon, where Amazon stores and ships your stock) or an own-brand product line.
  • The owner earns money while building these skills. **
  • High-ticket dropshipping can lead to an own brand.
  • Once a seller knows the customers and the market, they can source their own products directly from a manufacturer.
  • They can buy in bulk and sell at a higher margin.
  • **
  • Sell established brands first (well-known names), then build your own.
  • This keeps the store earning while you learn.
    • Start by selling other brands' products.
    • Then private label your own brand once you know your best sellers.
    • Sales from established brands show what customers search for and buy. **
    • Blind shipping (the supplier ships your order without their name on it) and private labeling do not automatically bring traffic.
    • A new brand name is not searched.
    • Build your own brand after you have proven sales with the supplier's brands. **
    • Pattern: sell established brands in the same niche on a store.
    • Introduce your own brand on that store once it has traffic, and later move it to its own site.
    • The known brands bring the early traffic and sales that fund the launch.
    • Example for a new store: sell three established yoga mat brands first.
    • Add your own mat line after six months.
    • Then launch the line on a separate site.

Private Label Versus White Label

2 min
  • Private label means your own brand made for you by a factory. White label means a standard factory product that you sell under your name.
Question Private label White label Units
Per-unit cost (cost of one item) Usually higher, because the manufacturer has no standing production setup for a unique product Lower, because large batches are run
Speed and entry Slower and harder to launch Usually easier and faster
Minimum order quantity (the smallest order a factory accepts) Higher Lower
Supplier location , May offer American suppliers, depending on the product
  • Sourcing for private label shares most of the dropshipping strategy.
  • The main difference is supplier sourcing (finding and choosing the factory).
  • The rest of the approach is essentially the same. **
  • For commodity products (plain, common goods) with little that can be changed, starting with white label can make sense.
  • Once the business has traction (steady sales and growth), the seller can begin changing the product.
  • They can then move toward private label.
  • There is no universally correct model.
  • The choice depends on the product type and the seller's budget and speed.
  • A common path is to start with white label and later move to private label.
  • Private labeling in a dropshipping business can be done in two ways.
  • You can build your own brand for the main product on your store.
  • Or you can private label smaller accessories and add-on items used alongside it.
  • The second route is usually easier to start because the investment is smaller.
  • Private labeling a high-ticket product you already sell can be done by recreating that main product.
  • Or you can build around accessories and smaller items.
  • Recreating the main product is the more expensive route because it requires significant upfront capital.
  • Example, 3D printer filament (plastic thread used to print 3D objects): customers need filament to print, so the gap was colors not available in the market.
  • Options included producing custom colors, licensing sports-team colors, or licensing logos for 3D printed items. Licensing means paying for permission to use a name or logo.
  • Filament sold on a subscription model creates recurring revenue.
  • Sourcing and production:
    • The brand bought its own stock directly from factories in bulk-style batches.
    • It never used dropshipping or print-on-demand (products printed only after an order).
    • The model was to order a small quantity, sell it, reinvest, and reorder in larger amounts.
    • Quality outranks marketing: a good product with weak marketing will outperform a weak product with strong marketing.
    • Expect repeated sample rounds and improvements.
    • Make sure the first version (MVP, minimum viable product: the first basic version) is already genuinely good, not merely functional. **
    • Product differentiation (making your product different from others) requires systematically listening to customers over time.
    • Listen through sales, support contacts, surveys and notes, instead of copying competitors.
    • The answers to what to change come from documented customer requests.

First Own-Brand Launch

2 min
  • Test the concept cheaply first.
  • Use AI tools (software that creates content) to generate the visual concept of a brand quickly.
  • Test it in the market before real design or product spend (money spent on the actual product).
  • Testing with print-on-demand style runs or small batches lets you test a concept at low cost.
  • The choice is not all or nothing.
  • Validate first, then move forward step by step.
  • Not being in the market yet can be turned into an asset.
  • A first-drop exclusivity message (a note that the first release is limited), a notify-when-to-order list and lead capture (collecting customers' emails) can build hype (excitement) before stock arrives.
  • Refine everything after money starts coming in and the concept is proven.
  • Do not try to perfect the brand, website and community before the first customers arrive.
  • Finish product development (samples, final product, quality checks) before starting social media campaigns and paid advertising.
  • Pre-sales (taking orders before stock exists) only make sense after you have already launched collections and worked with the supplier before.
  • Launch private label directly after a product proves itself on TikTok.
  • In one case, a product was taken to a private-label brand with a designed label.
  • It was then pushed through TikTok organic (free posts) and TikTok ads (paid ads).
  • First private label product procedure. **
    1. Begin with a very small range, possibly just one product under the new brand name.
    2. Choose it based on the current best sellers, what customers ask about, and shopper feedback.
    3. Keep out-of-pocket costs (money you pay yourself) low, since private label typically requires little upfront spend.
  • Selling in person during the early phase can bring cash while online sales are still being figured out.
  • In one example, a brand sold its product at music festivals.
  • Becoming the known category builder (the brand people link with that type of product) in one niche makes marketing leaner and more efficient.
  • It also gives a foundation to expand into adjacent products (related products) later.

Amazon FBA and Marketplace Ownership

2 min
  • Amazon FBA (Fulfillment by Amazon, where Amazon stores and ships your stock) lets you build an owned brand.
  • You build an owned brand instead of dropshipping for others.
  • Ownership allows higher profit margins per sale.
  • Ownership gives control over pricing and over who can resell the product.
  • Ownership opens the potential for wholesale distribution channels (selling in bulk to other stores).
  • Ownership lets you sell the brand as an asset (something of value you can sell later).
  • With dropshipping, you build someone else's brand.
  • FBA means you private label a product and ship it to Amazon's warehouses.
  • Amazon handles storage, packing, shipping, customer support, returns and refunds.
  • You remain responsible for finding the product and sourcing the supplier.
  • You also manage inventory (your stock), optimize the listing page (your product page) and drive traffic.
  • FBA requires upfront capital to buy your first inventory batch from the supplier.
  • Unlike dropshipping, which is free to start, this capital is recoupable (you can get it back).
  • If a product underperforms, you can sell remaining inventory at wholesale price (a bulk, lower price) to recover costs.
  • Start with one product, not several.
  • After one year with only two products in supplements, profit reached $17,000 per product per month.
  • Managing few products allows focused marketing and better results than spreading effort across many SKUs (separate product versions).
  • FBA benchmarks:
Metric Value Units
Realistic monthly profit, one well-researched product $10,000 to $20,000+ per month
Time to profitability after launch 2-3 months
Sales notifications compared with dropshipping About 10x more frequent
  • Daily FBA operations:
    • Check Seller Central (Amazon's seller control panel) for units sold and inventory remaining.
    • Respond to customer emails.
    • Review Amazon ad campaign conversion rates (the share of clicks that become sales) and spend.
    • Check emails again before dinner.
  • Some platform tactics are tolerated despite Terms of Service prohibitions (platform rules), such as HTML formatting (code tricks that style a listing) on Amazon.
  • If a tactic offers minimal performance gain and carries account risk, removing it is worth the cost.
  • Account safety is more valuable than micro-optimizations (tiny improvements).
  • For entirely new, invented products without existing market BSR data (Amazon's sales-rank numbers), run your own branded website alongside the Amazon listing.
  • This drives external traffic and validates demand independently.
  • Build the website first, test traffic and conversion, then commit to full manufacturing and FBA inventory.
  • This reduces inventory risk compared with Amazon-only launches, where interest cannot be measured until after manufacturing.
  • Selling as a marketplace seller (a site where many sellers sell, such as Walmart) mostly builds the marketplace's business rather than your own. **
  • Building your own store is the better long-term asset.
  • Galaxus is the largest marketplace in Switzerland, reported to have about one million daily visitors.
  • Sellers can apply and must meet its listing requirements.
  • Sellers can gain organic sales (sales that come free, not from ads) from it.
  • A branded product sold in Switzerland can be a long-term asset.
  • Plan to run it for three to four years.
  • Then possibly sell it to a larger seller or retailer.
  • Partnerships with other brands add value to such a sale.

Brand Growth Case Studies

5 min
  • Beard-oil brand (brand beyond the product): The company started with a basic product and an existing supplier connection.
  • Instead of only running Google Shopping listings for a low-priced product, the owner built a brand around a mission of self-improvement for men.
  • The brand offered education, inspiration and community beyond the product.
    • Instagram reached about 140,000 followers with photos of beards and grooming.
    • People tagged friends.
    • The hashtag (a searchable tag, like #beard) had about 62,000 posts on Instagram.
    • A YouTube channel with almost one million subscribers covered lifestyle and self-improvement rather than beards.
    • A blog published skincare and lifestyle content.
    • Merchandise (items with the brand name on them) sold well.
    • A website community membership at $30 per month added recurring income.
    • The core product sold for about $25.
    • The core product was not pushed with paid ads.
  • Timeline expectation: Plan for about 12 to 18 months before a brand-led business becomes very profitable.
  • Monetization (making money from the audience) is often delayed for months while the audience grows.
  • Ad spending can stay small during this phase, so the main investment is time and content.
  • Sunglasses brand (direct-to-consumer first):
    • Starting point: three co-founders each put in $10,000 ($30,000 total).
    • They spent it all on the first product order and had no marketing budget.
    • The sunglasses launched at $40.
    • 25% off brought the effective price (the price actually paid) to $32.50.
    • After almost a full first year without profit, the list price was doubled to $85 with the same product quality.
    • The price allowed the brand to scale (grow).
    • The founders cut their other side projects to focus on one brand.
    • Revenue was $350,000 in the distracted year (2015), $6.5M in 2016, with a $12M target for 2017.
    • By then the brand had sold over 300,000 pairs.
    • It had about 336,000 Instagram followers from 1,585 posts.
  • Back-stretcher brand (single product to over $1M per month):
    • Starting point: a Facebook ad for one orthopedic back stretcher (a device that stretches the back), first seen on 8 November 2020.
    • The store had a basic one-page site and a generic logo.
    • Phase 1 (first two to three weeks): the owner recorded all ads himself on a phone with custom content.
    • Each ad clip was at least about 1 minute 20 seconds.
    • The hook (the opening part that grabs attention) was the first three clips.
    • He added a chiropractor review (a review from a back-care expert).
    • Phase 2 (weeks two to three onward): a branded landing page (the page visitors arrive on) with GIFs (short looping animated pictures) and infographics (pictures that show facts).
    • The page had a quoted testimonial (a customer quote) near the top.
    • It had a clear USP (unique selling point) with a money-back guarantee, and one-line benefit bullets.
    • A branded image style with guarantee badges (small trust icons) was added.
    • Phase 3 (about six months): the store had promoted only that one product.
    • In June 2021 a second product (orthopedic pillow) was tested with normal drop-shipping footage (dropshipping video clips of the product).
    • Two to three new products were launched in August 2021.
    • More launches followed in July and later, building collections such as neck, back, hand, foot and knee health.
    • Pricing: the main product started at $49.95.
    • Supplier cost was $6 to $7 per single unit, about $2 to $3 in bulk.
    • It was later raised to $89.95 with no drop in sales after more investment in custom content and images.
    • Bundles and upsells: a sleep bundle at $79.95 versus a regular combined price of $126.
    • Cart upsells (extra offers in the cart) and post-purchase offers (offers after buying) were added.
    • Traffic and economics: about 500k to 600k visits in peak months.
    • Estimated AOV (average order value) of about $80, likely higher with upsells.
    • Estimated revenue above $1M per month at 2.5% conversion (the share of visitors who buy) and $80 AOV.
    • Ad spend around $250k at $0.50 CPC (cost per click), implying roughly 4x ROAS (return on ad spend: sales per dollar spent on ads).
    • The source notes this is above the typical Facebook ROAS of 2 to 2.5 while scaling.
    • Later steps: a Pinterest ad channel (a paid ad source on Pinterest) for the same product reportedly drove an estimated 3,000 to 5,000 visits a day.
    • The brand was described as sellable for 3x to 5x its profit.
  • UK luxury streetwear brand (slow, self-funded build):
    • Founded in 2017 by a founder who kept a full-time job at a high street bank.
    • He invested about 60% of his salary.
    • He spent a year or two developing the base, designs and trial-and-error work.
    • The real business started around 2019.
    • He began production with a minimum order of 50 hats.
    • He reinvested sales into larger reorders.
    • Early growth came from organic Instagram posts and influencers promoting the brand.
    • Scaling came with paid advertising and personal networking with celebrities and their teams.
    • Over the years he estimated total investment plus ad spend at over a quarter million, up to half a million, currency not stated.
    • The brand grew to about 185 products on sale.
    • It reached around 1 million pounds annual revenue.
    • It launched new collections every two to three months.
    • Production was in China and Pakistan.
    • It had a customer-paid return policy (the buyer pays return shipping), with a return rate (the share of orders sent back) of about 20 to 25 percent.
  • Pet supplement brand (multi-channel, selling on several platforms at once): A private label brand was sold on Amazon and Chewy (a pet store website).
  • It had a smaller Shopify presence (a store on the Shopify platform).
  • This mix was the scaling focus at the time.
  • Pet supplements such as glucosamine (a joint-support ingredient), vitamins and fish oil for dogs and cats are an example of a product line used in this model. **
  • Private label inside a high-ticket store: Private labeling was used inside a high-ticket dropshipping store, where the store's own products were made under its own brand.
  • The result was described as very good, though details stay vague due to an NDA (a secrecy agreement). **
  • Adult coloring book brand (exit value, what the brand sold for): The brand was built and later sold for roughly 350 to 400 USD.
  • The source did not remember the exact figure. **
  • Production moved from China to the US during this brand's lifetime.

Product-Line Expansion

1 min
  • A product line is the group of products a brand sells. Expanding it means adding more products over time.
  • Start with one product. Build the brand around it.
  • Add complementary products. Complementary products fit well with the first one. They solve the same or a similar problem.
  • Do not launch a large catalog at the beginning. A catalog is the full list of products in a shop.
  • Add products gradually, as the first one proves itself. Proving itself means it sells and works well.
  • Sub-niches are small parts of a market. Collections are groups of related products in one shop section.
  • Together, sub-niches and collections turn one hero product (the main product) into a one-stop shop (one place that solves the whole problem) for one problem area.
  • This raises average order value (the average amount each customer spends per order) and repeat buying (customers buying again).
  • This structure usually comes after several months, not in the first week.
  • Brands grow slowly and step by step. Iteratively means repeating small steps.
  • Each new product adds to the niche. A niche is the small market the brand serves.
  • Customer loyalty (customers who keep coming back) grows from consistent service.
  • Expecting a full brand from day one wastes time and money on hundreds of unproven product pages. A product page is one web page that sells one product.
  • Become the known category builder (the brand people see as the leader that created a product type). This gives a foundation to expand into adjacent products (nearby products) later.

Brand Holidays and Community

1 min
  • Create a branded holiday or event. This is a special day or event that only your brand runs.
  • The goal is to build community (a group of people who follow and care about the brand).
  • Use it to collect emails (gather the email addresses of people who want to hear from you).
  • In the example, a mid-September holiday was modeled on a big retail event (a large shopping event run by stores).
  • The promotion was announced at the bottom of a regular content email (an email with useful information, not a sale).
  • It appeared as a small PS line (a short extra note at the very end of an email, marked "PS").
  • That line led to a page that asked only for an email address. This qualified interested people, meaning it marked the people who were truly interested.
  • Brand holiday promotion, Stacks Day (mid-September):
    • The brand built custom packages around its customer avatars. An avatar is a made-up profile of a typical customer.
    • The brand reached out to other brands and asked them to donate products. A brand that gives products is called a donor.
    • Each donor was asked for a commitment of 24 units. Units are single items.
    • In return, the brand promised to promote each donor to its list. Its list is its email subscribers.
    • The brand also offered each donor a coupon (a discount code).
    • The brand sold out of almost all packages (gift bundles) except the traveler package.
    • It made $30K profit in one day. Profit is the money left after costs.
    • This was possible because the products were donated.

Long-Term Value and Exit

1 min
  • A brand built for long-term value (value that lasts for years) can aim for about 1 million dollars in sales before it is sold.
  • A buyer pays for the audience (the people who follow the brand) and the customer base (the customers who have already bought).
  • Early years may run at break-even (no profit and no loss) while the brand builds awareness (how many people know the brand).
  • Dropshipping stores (shops that sell products they do not stock; the supplier ships them to the customer) are judged differently.
  • They are usually tested for profit from the start.
  • A brand sold in Switzerland can be run for three to four years.
  • It can possibly be sold later to a larger seller or retailer (a store that sells to shoppers).
  • Brand partnerships (agreements with other brands to work together) add value to the sale.
  • Sell or partner from strength. This means doing it when the brand is doing well.
  • Partnerships with other brands add value when the brand is sold, as in the Swiss example.

Further details

1 min
  • A branded business can earn from many sources. These are called income streams (different ways of earning money):
    • Dropshipped high-ticket products (expensive products sold without holding stock).
    • Private-label products (products made by a manufacturer and sold under your own brand name).
    • Merchandise (branded items such as shirts or mugs).
    • Paid community memberships (people pay to join your group).
    • Affiliate or referral links to related products (links that earn a commission when someone buys through them).
    • Sponsored videos (videos paid for by another company).
    • Group trips (trips taken together with customers).
  • Diversifying income streams (spreading earnings across several sources) is a main reason to build the brand rather than only dropship.

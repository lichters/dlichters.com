Gifting and seeding campaigns

Section 13.9 · 9 of 21 in this chapter · 9 min read

In short

  • Gift products to 30 to 50 creators first, because most of them will not post.
  • Out of 50 messages, about 10 creators post. Plan for that loss.
  • Pay only creators who already engage with your brand on their own.
  • Get permission to reuse creator content before you run it in paid ads.
This section has 8 parts. Tap one to open it.

Gifting campaign phases and funnel benchmarks

2 min
  1. Gift products to a broad list of creators (people who make content online). Record each creator's details in a spreadsheet (an online table, here Google Sheets). Start with 30 to 50 creators.
  2. Expect attrition (people dropping out at each step). From 50 outreach DMs (private messages on social media), roughly 30 reply, 20 want the product, and 10 post on Instagram.
  3. Track which creators engage with the brand (like, comment, or share) on their own. These smaller creators are the ones to move into paid partnerships.
  • A funnel (the steps from first contact to final result) benchmark (a typical reference number) for creator outreach: of 50 DMs sent, about 30 reply, about 20 want the product, and about 10 post on Instagram.
  • Plan for roughly a 60 percent loss from reply to posting. Start with more creators than you need.
  • Arithmetic from the same benchmark:
    • Reply to post is a loss of about 67 percent (30 to 10).
    • Product request to post is a loss of about 50 percent (20 to 10).
    • DM to post is a loss of about 80 percent (50 to 10).
    • The stated 60 percent does not match the 30-to-10 figure exactly. Keep both, and use the stage figures to see where creators drop out.

Numbered campaign procedure:

  1. Gift the product to a broad list of 30 to 50 creators. Record each creator's details in a Google Sheets list.
  2. Send outreach DMs and track replies. From 50 DMs, expect roughly 30 replies, 20 who want the product, and 10 who post on Instagram.
  3. Watch which creators engage with the brand on their own, without prompting (without being asked). These are the genuinely interested ones.
  4. Invite the genuinely engaged creators, usually the smaller ones, into paid partnerships.
  5. For a challenge or campaign (a set of posts around one theme), choose paid creators from the pool (group) of people already gifted and engaged with the brand.
  6. Secure usage rights (written permission to reuse the content) before running any creator content through paid media, such as Facebook ads (ads you pay for).

Gifting outreach messages and offers

2 min
  • Common mistake: a custom cushion brand sent a short email offering a free custom-designed cushion in exchange for showing it on Instagram.
    • The email had only a website link.
    • It gave no details about what the brand wanted, the product options, or the deliverables (the exact items the creator must produce).
    • Creators receive many such emails every day. They will rarely do gifted campaigns (campaigns where the creator gets a free product) for an unknown brand.
    • So generic gifting outreach like this is ignored.
  • Template for a gifting offer:
Hi [CREATOR NAME],

I saw your [SPECIFIC SERIES OR VIDEO] and loved [SPECIFIC DETAIL]. My name is [YOUR NAME] and I work on creator partnerships at [BRAND].

We would like to send you [GIFT PRODUCT] for your next [PROJECT]. In return, we are looking for one Instagram story and one TikTok post showing how you use it. Please let me know whether you are interested, and feel free to ask any questions before deciding.

Best regards,
[YOUR NAME]
  • Formula for a gifting offer:
    • The creator's name.
    • A specific reference to their work.
    • The gift and what it will be used for in their project.
    • The exact return asked, for example one Instagram story and one TikTok post.
    • Stating the gift and the expectation together lets the creator judge the offer in one reading.
    • Example 1: Hi Lena, your upcycling series on small bathrooms is great, and we would like to send you an oak wall panel for your next project. In return we would love one Instagram story and one TikTok post.
    • Example 2: Hi Sam, your cookware reviews are exactly the style of our customers, so we would like to send you our new cast-iron pan. We are looking for one Instagram reel and one story in return.
  • Gifting procedure for influencer outreach (influencers are creators with a large following):
    1. Find the creator's PO box address (a postal mailbox address) in their Instagram bio or YouTube description.
    2. Send a product personalized for them, such as a version with their name or a cutout (a cut-out version of their photo).
    3. Include a short note saying the gift is from you, with your social media handle (your username).
    4. Do not ask for promotion. Smaller creators often promote the gift for free as a goodwill gesture (a kind act with no payment expected).

Seeding relationships before any request

1 min
  • Seed your product (send it free, with no strings attached) to major influencers in your niche (your narrow market). Include websites, YouTube creators, Instagram and TikTok creators, larger businesses, magazines, blogs and forums. Send the product and ask for honest feedback.
  • Older advice: when seeding influencers, ask for nothing in return. Do not request a post.
    • Build a real relationship first.
    • Transactional requests (asking for something in exchange for something) for posts rarely lead to results.
    • This applies to seeding established influencers.
    • It conflicts with the gifting offer format above, which states the return asked.
    • Use the seeding rule when there is no relationship yet and the aim is trust.
    • Use the offer format when the deliverables are defined and the creator is being asked to produce content.

Non-posting losses

1 min
  • About 20 percent of influencers receiving product did not post. Plan for this loss, and test enough influencers to find reliable ones.
  • Read this figure as the loss among influencers who already received product.
    • The funnel figures are higher for cold outreach (first contact with people who did not know the brand).
    • About 50 percent of creators who asked for product did not post.
    • About 80 percent of all DMs did not lead to a post.

Selecting creators for paid work

1 min
  • Gifting cannot guarantee coverage (cannot promise posts). Use it to find genuinely interested creators.
    • Creators who engage organically (on their own, without paid help) are the best candidates for paid collaboration.
  • For a challenge or campaign, choose creators from the pool of people already gifted and engaged with the brand.
    • They are more authentic.
    • They need less briefing (instructions) on brand image and voice (the brand's look and way of speaking).
  • Do not pick creators only because they are viral (suddenly very popular) at the moment. Choose creators who genuinely invest in the brand.
  • Content produced by campaign creators can be pushed through paid media, such as Facebook ads, but only if you have the usage rights to that content.

Selling at cost instead of free product

1 min
  • If free products are not feasible (not possible), offer an influencer the product at cost (at the price it costs you). Ask for shout-outs (public mentions) and links in return. For example, a $1,000 paddle board can be offered at $500. This is older advice from Source A (2021).
  • Influencer marketing is rarely done with free products.
    • Instead, sell the product at cost to influencers. You lose no money and still get exposure (people seeing your brand).
    • This is older advice from Source A (2020).
    • The 2020 source treats at-cost sales as the usual approach.
    • The 2021 source treats it as a fallback (a backup plan) when free product is not feasible.

Shipping and packaging

1 min
  • Influencer marketing works best when the product ships quickly, since slow shipping damages the influencer's reputation.
  • A custom box can be designed for the product by a freelancer (a self-employed designer) on Fiverr (an online marketplace for freelance work) for about $5 to $10.
    • A supplier (a company that makes the goods) can quote about $0.50 per box for around 100 units (pieces).
    • That costs about $50 in total.
    • Creators showing the custom box in their videos build a stronger brand impression than plain shipping.

Launch seeding with multiple creators and markets

3 min
  • Launch sequence with untapped-market creators (creators in markets nobody has sold to yet):
    1. Pick three or four influencers in an untapped market, such as certain European countries, Australia or New Zealand.
    2. Send each influencer a brief (short set of instructions) that specifies the posting day, time, and the points to mention.
    3. First video: the influencer uses the product without introducing it. This creates a rumor (talk people spread) and vibe (feeling or mood) around it.
    4. Second video: announce the launch date and offer a discount, such as 20%, to people who sign up to the email list (a list of addresses for news) before launch.
    5. Use a signup form on the Shopify store (an online shop platform) to collect email, name and optionally phone number. In exchange, offer an incentive (a reward for signing up) of 10% to 20% off.
    6. Let the influencer announce a time-limited sign-up discount, for example valid for one to two hours.
    7. Warm up the new email list (build interest) with benefit-driven emails (emails about what the customer gains) before the launch date.
    8. Sell at launch while stock lasts (until stock runs out). Then plan the next hype (a new wave of excitement) once stock and customization are ready, often 10 to 30 days later.
  • Case: a pre-launch influencer campaign (before the product is released) for a product not yet on sale built a large email list through a signup offer. Then it sold 20K to 60K per day on launch day.
    • Sales were stopped once stock ran out, so existing orders could be fulfilled (shipped).
  • Launching with several mid-size creators in the same region at the same time makes a product look widely discussed.
    • Pick three to four creators with roughly 10K to 200K followers (people who follow their account) each.
    • Their audiences overlap, with about 10 percent of one creator's followers also following another.
    • Their combined posts reach people that no single creator would reach alone.
  • Test a product that works in the US with US influencers. Then launch it in a European country, such as France, with a local influencer.
    • This approach was used in 2020 and produced sales.
    • This is older advice.
  • Case, Mary Roost (a multivitamin supplement brand, a brand selling daily vitamin pills):
    • The brand gained a large TikTok push (lots of attention on TikTok, a short-video app) for its multivitamin hair growth products through a gifting campaign.
    • It sent out large amounts of product.
    • It offered affiliate commissions (a payment for each sale made through a creator's link) to people who posted videos. It did not pay flat fees (fixed payments) to creators.
    • The campaign targeted several audience groups with separate creators:
      • a health-focused mom,
      • a fitness and supplements enthusiast,
      • a health-field expert giving a scientific explanation,
      • a younger woman reporting fast hair growth.
    • Once the topic trended (became popular online) and TikTok Shop (TikTok's built-in shop) offered affiliate links, buyers and more creators joined in. This created a domino effect (one thing triggering the next).
    • The campaign showed that it does not cost much. Sending product and contacting creators can be enough to start.
  • Principle: a gifting campaign can target several audience segments (groups of people with shared interests) at once.
    • Select one creator per segment. Each creator speaks to that segment's specific interests or concerns.
    • Pairing product gifting with affiliate commissions motivates creators to post without flat-rate fees.

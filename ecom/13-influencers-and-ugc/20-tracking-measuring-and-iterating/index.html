Playbook › 13. Influencers and UGC

Tracking, measuring and iterating

Section 13.20 · 20 of 21 in this chapter · 4 min read

In short

  • Track the visitors and sales from each influencer. Keep a partner only if they make you money.
  • Give each influencer their own discount code, so you can see which sales they bring.
  • Judge results over several posts, not one. Likes alone are not sales.
  • Measure in the same order every time, then repeat only the partners who pay.

Measuring Influencer Results: Principles and Priorities

  • Track the visitors (traffic) that come from influencer partnerships (paid deals with people who have followers). This is the most important step to see if they make money.
  • It shows whether a partnership earns enough to continue long-term.
  • Some influencers become so profitable that they stay active advertising partners for multiple years.
  • Count referrals (visitors sent by someone's link or post) from brand ambassadors (people who promote your brand) as their own traffic source.
  • Keep this source separate from paid social (ads on social apps) and search (ads on search engines).
  • This traffic should be better quality than cold paid traffic (visitors from ads who do not know your brand).
  • It can feed back into the top of the funnel (the first step, where people first hear about you) to bring in more customers.
  • Judge TikTok results over several posts, not one.
  • Engagement (likes, comments and shares) on influencer content can rise and fall sharply.
  • Attention spans are short and trends move quickly, so expect results to fluctuate.
  • Organic (free, not paid) Instagram posts that reached over 200 followers within a week show that people are interested in the content.
  • This is not proof of sales on its own.
  • A cited survey says 86% of businesses using influencer marketing effectively report an immediate or long-term effect on their brand.
  • The source and the exact meaning of "effective" are not stated. Treat this figure as a rough hint only.

Platform Analytics and Engagement Data

  • Check the built-in analytics (the free statistics each platform shows) for each influencer post:
    • Instagram Insights
    • Facebook Insights
    • Twitter Analytics
    • YouTube Analytics
  • Record for each post: comments, shares, likes and interaction rate (the share of people who react to the post).
  • Ask each influencer to send their engagement figures (comments, shares, likes, interaction rate) for the campaign posts.
  • This lets you compare their numbers with your own platform data.

Influencer Platforms and Campaign Analytics

  • Influencer marketing platforms (tools for running influencer deals), such as Influency, Aspire IQ and Tracker, give detailed campaign analytics.
  • These include reach (how many people saw the content), engagement, and numbers for each collaboration.
  • These platforms are most useful when you manage the whole campaign through them.
  • If the campaign ran through such a platform, pull (collect) its numbers for reach, engagement and each collaboration.

Brand Mentions and Audience Feedback

  • Track brand mentions (people naming your brand online) over the campaign period.
  • Use a social listening tool (software that finds online posts about a topic), or search manually for the brand, keyword, hashtag (a word tagged with #) or campaign name.
  • Survey the audience and collect feedback (surveys, social media polls).
  • Use the feedback to find out where they first heard of the influencer and how they see the campaign.

Sales, Conversion and Unique Codes

  • Track sales and conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy) for each campaign.
  • Give each creator a unique discount code (a code only that creator shares), so sales can be traced back to that creator.
  • Ideally, build the code from their name.
  • A 25% code was affordable at that brand's margins (the profit left after costs).
  • Any unique code, even free shipping, serves the same tracking purpose.
  • Each influencer's own coupon code (discount code) tracks results and strengthens the relationship with the creator.
  • Any coupon works, even free shipping, as long as it is tied to one specific influencer.
  • Use one identical spelling of each creator name across all ads.
  • Consistent names let you make reports per creator. They also make creator commission payments (money paid for the sales they bring) easier to match.
  • An influencer post with no sale can still be valuable. It counts if it earns a follower, an email or a visit that you can retarget (show ads to later).
  • Plan this fallback before you pay for the post.

Campaign Evaluation Sequence

Measure each influencer campaign in this order:

  1. Check the platform's built-in analytics for each influencer post (Instagram Insights, Facebook Insights, Twitter Analytics, YouTube Analytics).
  2. Record comments, shares, likes and interaction rate.
  3. Ask the influencer to send their engagement numbers as well.
  4. Monitor brand and hashtag mentions with a listening tool, or search manually for the brand, keyword or campaign name.
  5. Pull detailed campaign metrics from the influencer marketing platform, if one was used.
  6. Track sales and conversion metrics.
  7. Survey the audience.
  8. Compare results from before and after the campaign.
  9. Compare results with your other marketing channels.

Deciding Which Partners to Repeat

  • Use the results and audience insights (what your audience responds to) from each campaign to decide which influencer strategies to repeat.
  • Choose future partners and formats (types of posts) based on what has resonated most with your audience.
  • Use the sequence above on every campaign before deciding. This way, comparisons across creators rest on the same measurements.
  • Influencers whose traffic and sales prove profitable can remain active advertising partners for multiple years.

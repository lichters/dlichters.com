Using UGC in ads, store and social channels

Section 13.19 · 19 of 21 in this chapter · 6 min read

In short

  • Real customer photos and videos often beat polished ads.
  • Use real customer material in retargeting, which means ads shown again to people who already saw the product.
  • Only boost new accounts. Boosting old, successful posts can kill their free sales.
  • Borrowed clips from others risk copyright strikes, so use them only for a short time.

Paid ads: why UGC works

  • UGC (user-generated content, meaning real customer material such as reviews and photos of people using the product) builds trust. It works especially well in retargeting ads, where it answers the doubts of people who already saw the product.
  • Meta (the company behind Facebook and Instagram) favors content that looks natural. Over-produced ads still run but cost more, so real customer photos and videos, or phone-shot footage, often perform better.
  • Use real customer videos or photos in retargeting where possible. A customer describing their experience often outperforms a brand message.
  • Spark Ads (a real post from a creator's account, run as an ad) from influencers (people with large followings who promote products) tend to outperform regular brand ads. They look like organic content (free, unpaid posts) from a trusted creator, not like a sales message. This lowers viewer resistance (how much people push back) and makes the ad feel more credible.

Paid ads: where the traffic goes

  • Turn collected UGC into paid ads. Cold audiences (people who have never seen the brand) rarely buy on direct product pages. So send paid traffic (paid visitors) to the influencer's profile or to a lead magnet page (a free offer that collects an email address) instead of the product page.

A creative is the ad itself, such as a video or an image.

Audience Creative Send traffic to Source
Cold UGC video ads with real creators Influencer's profile or lead magnet page
Retargeting (people who already saw the product) Real customer videos or photos Not specified in the source

Paid ads: whitelisting and brand pages

  • Brand pages (the company's own accounts) perform well with UGC video ads that feature real creators (people who make content for brands). These creators come from TikTok Shop creator programs (TikTok's own program linking brands with creators) or official Instagram influencers.
  • Whitelisting (running ads through a creator's or persona page, an account built around a character, instead of the brand's own page) means exactly that.
  • The brand's page can still host the UGC content, so both approaches are used together.

Paid ads: boosting organic content

  • Boosting (paying to push a post) with Coins (TikTok's paid boost currency) or Spark Ads on a brand-new account can speed up reaching the right audience. This works during the new account advantage window (extra early reach for new accounts), which lasts roughly the first one to two weeks. Use this boost only on new accounts.
  • Do not boost old accounts with established organic performance (proven free reach). Boosting old successful organic posts has been reported to kill organic sales.
  • Use organic videos as ad creatives only if they already reached around 50,000 to 100,000 views and carry real social proof (signs that other people trust it, such as comments and likes). Very new organic videos can lose organic reach when boosted through Spark Ads.
  • Both conditions apply together: the view threshold decides which videos qualify, and the account-age rule decides whether boosting is safe at all.

Paid ads: borrowed clips and copyright

  • Using UGC clips taken from other brands or TikTok for ads carries a risk of copyright strikes (warnings that you used someone else's content, which can lead to account removal). Keep this short term, such as a couple of weeks at 10 USD per ad set per day (50 USD total). An ad set is a group of ads that shares one budget. Replace the borrowed clips with your own UGC once the product gains traction (starts selling well).

Ad iteration: fixing low-attention ads

  • Ads with low attention (few people watch or stop to look) can be improved rather than discarded. Layer a UGC video with a voice-over (spoken narration added over footage) or new hooks (the first seconds that grab attention). A single hook change can lift attention and sales.

Converting winning UGC into other formats

  • For books, UGC AI ads (UGC-style ads made with AI) produced higher purchase intent (how much people want to buy) and better traction (a growing response) than static image ads (still pictures) in the tests. Static image ads still work, but UGC AI ads are the better default for this product type.
  • Convert a proven UGC AI ad concept (an idea that already worked) back to image ads, in this order:
    1. Confirm the UGC AI concept has produced many purchases and steady profit.
    2. Build image ads that use the same concept, angle (the point of view of the ad) and pain point (the problem the customer has).
    3. Run the image ads alongside the UGC AI ads and compare soft metrics (views and clicks) and hard metrics (sales and profit).

Product pages and testimonial sections

  • Place customer photos in a dedicated section (its own separate block) on the product page, not mixed into supplier product photos. This section is one of the most important elements for conversion (turning visitors into buyers).
  • Customer photos also work in blog posts where a product image fits. A standalone customer photo gallery page (a page with only customer photos) is a weaker use of the photos than product pages and emails.
  • In testimonial sections (blocks of customer quotes) and product images, use raw UGC photos of real customers in real-life settings instead of high-quality studio shots. Visitors relate more to real people, and reported results show that such content converts better than polished lifestyle images.

Review proof blocks

  • Pattern (a repeatable layout): place review screenshots (pictures of reviews) from the store, a marketplace (a shared shopping site that sells many sellers' products) or a product page over a blurred version of the product. Show the store URL (web address) in the same frame. It works because it puts several genuine reviews in one image while the product stays visible in the background.
    • Example 1: Five screenshots of five-star reviews with the URL of the store at the bottom.
    • Example 2: Three reviews from a marketplace over a blurred kitchen counter with the product.

Messenger social proof

  • Formula (a fill-in pattern): name a real customer with their location, state that they like the product, and mention one specific benefit (one concrete advantage). Messenger is Facebook's chat app. Real names and places increase trust more than generic praise.
    • Example 1: Maria from Ohio told us this kettle heats up in under three minutes, and she uses it every morning.
    • Example 2: Dan from Texas wrote that his backpack survived a full semester of daily use.

Video bubbles on the website

Video bubbles are small round video previews. Embed (place code so the content appears on your site) a bubble group (a set of bubbles shown together) on the website, as follows:

  1. Create the video campaigns (groups of ads with one goal) that will be shown as bubbles.
  2. Group the campaigns together into one bubble group (playlist, a set of videos shown together) per topic.
  3. Embed the bubble group on the website.
  4. Customize the bubbles to match the look and feel (colors and style) of the website.
  5. Update the content later without re-embedding, because embedded bubbles are fully dynamic (they change when you update them).
  6. Reuse any video that is already embedded in email marketing campaigns.

Shoppable social galleries

Shoppable means customers can buy straight from the picture. Add a carousel (a row you swipe through) of social content (posts from social media) to the website, as follows:

  1. Pull the content directly from your Instagram or TikTok account onto the website.
  2. Tag each video with the specific products shown in it.
  3. Place the carousel on the home page, collection pages (pages that group products) or product pages. There it works like an endless scroll (a never-ending feed) for product discovery (finding products).
  4. Let the customer check out from the carousel with a single click.

Scaling case: UGC as the main lever

Scaling means growing sales.

  • Starting point: a brand scaled mainly on bottom-of-funnel static ads (ads aimed at people ready to buy, using still pictures) for about two years, with three UGC videos.
  • Change: UGC video was introduced a couple of months earlier.
  • Result: monthly revenue (money coming in each month) rose about 5x with both formats combined.
  • Conclusion: UGC is the main scaling lever (the thing that grows sales most), while bottom-of-funnel statics help at small scale or on top of existing UGC.

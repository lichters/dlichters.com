Playbook › 13. Influencers and UGC

Building and qualifying the prospect list

Section 13.4 · 4 of 21 in this chapter · 8 min read

In short

  • Build a list of 50 to 80 creators before you contact anyone.
  • Only pick creators with at least 3% engagement (likes and comments compared with their followers).
  • Pick websites and forums with more than 10,000 visits a month.
  • Check every name, link and email from an AI tool by hand before you use it.
  • Some of these rules are from 2021-22, so check them before you rely on them.

Setting Up the Tracker and Channel Mix

2 min

Follow these steps to set up the tracker. A tracker is a spreadsheet or document that keeps your list in one place. Older advice, 2021-22.

  1. Create a tracker document to organize your Dream 100 prospects. Dream 100 means your list of 100 best-fit targets.
  2. Identify prospects across multiple channels (social media, email, communities, forums). A channel is any place where you can reach people.
  3. Follow them on their platforms. A platform is a website or app where people post.
  4. Subscribe to their email newsletters.
  5. Join their Facebook groups and online forums.

  • Target platforms for Dream 100 are:

    • blogs and websites
    • online forums, including the forum owners and moderators (the people who manage the forum)
    • Facebook groups
    • Instagram
    • YouTube
    • LinkedIn, but only for B2B businesses (B2B means selling to other businesses)

    Other channels relevant to a niche (a small, focused market), such as Pinterest for interior design, can be added. Older advice, 2021-22.

  • Facebook pages (business profiles) with large followings are excluded from Dream 100 targets.

    • Reason: page posts reach under 10% of followers. Reach means how many people see a post.
    • Page audiences are also mostly unengaged unless you run ads (paid promotion). Older advice, 2021-22.

  • Facebook group admins (the people who run a group) are valid targets.

    • Their posts reach at least around 50% of members as notifications (alerts on the phone or email). Older advice, 2021-22.

  • A creator who posts on several platforms can be entered more than once.

    • A single person can have several entries.
    • Some niches only have YouTube, Instagram and Facebook groups. That is acceptable.
    • Aim for a reasonably balanced spread across channels when the market allows it. Older advice, 2021-22.

  • Instagram reach is moving closer to Facebook reach, but it is still much better than Facebook pages.

    • LinkedIn and YouTube also reach more of their followers than Facebook pages.
    • So an active creator on these platforms is more valuable. Older advice, 2021-22.

  • A large forum can be split into subsections (smaller sections).

    • If one section matches your target market better than the whole forum, enter that section as its own tracker row.
    • Put it next to the parent forum (the main forum). Older advice, 2021-22.

Pure Creators and Sources Without a Website

1 min
  • Pure creators (people with no website or other online presence) usually earn from:
    • ad revenue (money from ads on their content)
    • sponsorships (paid deals with brands)
    • viewer donations
    • membership platforms (paid subscription sites)
  • They typically form a single entry in the matrix (the grid of rows in your tracker). That entry has only a YouTube or podcast property.
  • This is acceptable, because the entry still shows where your buyers watch. Older advice, 2021-22.

Sourcing Experts and Creators

1 min

  • Experts in a niche include anyone with a large audience there.

    • Examples: large YouTube channels, large blogs, or pro athletes with big followings.
    • Identifying them is useful for ad targeting (choosing who sees your ads) and for partnership opportunities. Older advice, 2021-22.

  • Example: a seller of running shoes would:

    • search Google for the top runners with large followings
    • look up lists of experts in the running niche
    • search YouTube for running channels that have many followers

    The creators found this way go into the Experts Followed field (one column in the tracker). Older advice, 2021-22.

  • Build a list of potential influencer (a person with a following who recommends products) and authority partners:

    1. Search Pinterest or Google for people already using or showing your product.
    2. Note each person's name and category, such as social media influencer, actor, artist, celebrity or doctor.
    3. Record each person's social media handle (their username) next to their name.

  • Creator discovery and contact procedure:

    1. Find one well-known creator in your niche as a starting point.
    2. Study the accounts that like and comment on that creator's posts, to find more creators with the same audience.
    3. Search Google for the creator's name plus email to find contact details.
    4. If the bio (the short profile text) has no email, send a direct message (a private message in the app). Keep the first message short and specific, because high-profile creators receive many messages.
    5. Use sales CRM tools (software that manages contacts) that scrape (automatically collect) contact information, as a supplement to manual search.
    6. For high-level creators, contact their manager or agency (the people who handle their business deals).

YouTube Checks

1 min
  1. Open the Videos tab and check upload dates to confirm regular posting.
  2. Check the view counts and comments on recent videos.
  3. Open the About section for the creator's name, website, email and social links.
  4. Note the channel's subscriber count (people who follow the channel) and whether it is focused on your niche.

Older advice, 2021-22.

  • Quick credibility check on the About page:
    • Note the join date.
    • Note the lifetime view count (total views since the channel began) in the Stats section.
    • Combine these with the subscriber count and recent video views before adding the channel to the tracker. Older advice, 2021-22.
  • Upload frequency: the ideal is weekly uploads. A channel that uploads roughly monthly can still be a valid target if its recent videos keep consistent view counts. Check the upload dates on the Videos tab to judge this. Older advice, 2021-22.

Forum and Website Checks

1 min

  • Judge engagement (how much people interact) by checking whether recent posts receive answers and views. Older advice, 2021-22.

  • A blog with many posts but no comments generally has a weak audience. Older advice, 2021-22.

  • Check monthly traffic with a domain tool. Aim for more than 10,000 visits per month as a safe level. Older advice, 2021-22.

  • Domain traffic tools for checking a website's monthly visits when researching Dream 100 targets:

    • Ubersuggest
    • Ahrefs
    • Semrush

    Older advice, 2021-22.

  • A Dream 100 target who is a moderator or owner of a forum or Facebook group is often only visible after joining. So the admin's name can be filled in later. Older advice, 2021-22.

Facebook Group Checks

1 min
  • Facebook group search results show:
    • whether a group is Public or Private (Private means only members can see posts)
    • its member count
    • its posts per day
  • In a private group, you cannot see the discussion before joining.
    • Read the group rules.
    • Judge activity from the listing stats first. Older advice, 2021-22.

Finding Contact Details and Advertising Pages

1 min

  • For a blog or website, look for the owner's name on the About Us page. Look for any contact details on the Contact page. Then record them in the tracker.

    • If the site has no about or contact information, the name can be left blank for now. Older advice, 2021-22.

  • On a blog or media site's contact page, look for a separate podcast, media, advertising or partnership email before using the general contact address.

    • Send guest pitches (offers to write an article for their site) and partnership pitches to that role-specific address.
    • It usually reaches the person who handles those requests.

  • Look for advertising pages on websites and forums, labelled such as Advertise or Advertise with us.

    • They often list banner ads (picture ads across the top or sides), sponsored listings or business listings.
    • Record the advertising page URL (web address) in the tracker.
    • Advertising can be a future traffic source (a source of visitors) alongside the relationship. Older advice, 2021-22.

  • Forums and websites often have an advertise page listing:

    • banner ads
    • single product listings
    • affiliate or multi-product store listings (affiliate listings pay a commission on sales)
    • spot or location pages (pages about one place)
    • business listings

    Record the advertising page link and the types of placements offered in the tracker. Older advice, 2021-22.

Verifying AI-Generated Lists

2 min
  • AI-generated partner lists (lists made by an AI tool) can contain:
    • broken websites
    • wrong or missing emails
    • names that do not match
  • Verify every entry manually before outreach.
  • One AI run returned only 30 rows. A rerun asking for 100 rows returned 42.
    • If the output is short, keep the usable rows.
    • Build the rest through manual research.
  • Prompt for generating the list:
As the proprietor of [STORENAME], you are assembling a list of 100 potential partnership targets: public figures, media properties, independent publishers, video creators, course developers, industry events, social media personalities, podcast producers, and professional organizations whose audiences overlap with your [PRODUCTS] customer base. Record this list in a spreadsheet with four columns: (A) the category describing the partner type (Public Figure, Media Property, Writer, Video Creator, Course Developer, Event, Social Media Creator, Podcaster, or Professional Group), (B) the person's or organization's name, (C) their web presence address if available, and (D) their contact email if obtainable. Complete the list with 100 entries.
  • Verification procedure for each AI-listed entry:
    1. Open each URL the AI listed. If the site is broken or flagged unsafe (marked dangerous by the browser), search the person or business name to find the working site.
    2. Confirm the site matches the name. Note the audience location, for example a site based in one country with a US audience.
    3. Find the contact page or email. Use Cmd+F (Ctrl+F on Windows) to search the page for contact.
    4. If there is no email address, record the contact form (a web form to message them) or an Instagram account for direct messages.
    5. Check blog posts for named outside authors. If none appear across several posts, mark guest posts as no.
    6. Check the sidebar (the side column of the page), footer (the bottom of the page) and blog for current ads. If none are running, mark paid ads as NA and note any empty ad space.
    7. Look for an advertising or media section, and for emails shown as images, which AI tools cannot read. Copy those addresses manually.
    8. Enter the contact details, dates and notes in the sheet.

Expanding the List and Qualifying Partners

1 min
  • Before outreach, open the partner's about or partners page. Look for other companies they already work with.
    • Add those linked companies to the list as new rows.
    • They are likely to be relevant to your niche too.
  • If a prospect gets annoyed when you respectfully disagree with a specific point in their content, treat that as a sign they may be a poor long-term partner.
    • Creators who care about delivering value to their audience usually welcome honest, constructive feedback. Older advice, 2021-22.

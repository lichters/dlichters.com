Finding influencers and creators

Section 13.5 · 5 of 21 in this chapter · 13 min read

In short

  • Find creators first on the apps themselves, such as Instagram, TikTok and YouTube.
  • Check how people react to a creator's posts before you contact them.
  • Only contact creators in a niche you know well.
  • Find more creators through the ones you already found.
  • Use paid tools only when time is short, and check every list with a second tool.
This section has 14 parts. Tap one to open it.

Choosing a Discovery Method

1 min
  • Start with manual search on native platforms. Native platforms are the apps themselves, such as Instagram, TikTok and YouTube.
  • Manual search is the main method. It lets you check each creator's engagement, meaning how much people react to their posts, and the talk around them, directly on the platform.
  • Use third-party tools as a backup. These are outside services, not the apps themselves.
  • Use them mainly when time is short.
  • Once you find one suitable influencer, find more through the creators they work with. Also check the accounts the platform suggests on their profile.
  • This builds a chain of relevant creators.
  • AI-generated lists are only one source. An AI list is a list a chatbot writes for you.
  • Also search Google, YouTube and Instagram for your niche terms. Your niche is the small market your product fits.
  • Check which sites, channels and accounts already publish for your audience. This shows real web presence (how often and where you appear online) and quality that an AI list cannot show.
  • Start with your own network. Use local contacts, people you know where you live, to reach creators in the target market. The target market is the group of buyers you sell to.
  • Ask them to promote the product there.

Building the Long List

1 min

Source A's outreach procedure (2021 material, older; the approach still applies). Outreach means contacting people to ask them to promote your product.

  1. Search keywords around your niche, for example "best kayak accessories", "kayak reviews" or "how to choose a kayak". Keywords are the words people type into search.
  2. Find bloggers, YouTubers, Instagram creators and Twitter users who discuss your products.
  3. Make a large list of potential influencers. Expect rejection from most of them.
  4. Contact 20 to 30 influencers with personalized outreach by email, Twitter or Instagram. Personalized means each message is written for that one person.
  5. Assess each influencer on content quality, follower engagement, posting frequency and audience relevance. Follower engagement is how much their followers react. Posting frequency is how often they post. Use Alexa.com or similar tools for this check. Alexa.com is a website that estimates how much traffic a site gets.

If a few channels dominate the results for a keyword, rerun the YouTube search with variations. Variations are changed versions of the same search. For example, add "advanced" to a beginner term. This removes generic tutorial videos and surfaces more creators in the niche. (Source B 2021-22 material, older.)

  • Only contact influencers in a niche you understand.
  • You must be able to judge the product and give real value.
  • Avoid chasing unrelated niches.

Native Platform Search

1 min

Instagram hashtag search (free)

  1. On Instagram, search a specific hashtag in your niche, such as hair, nails or acne. A hashtag is a word with # that groups posts on one topic.
  2. Choose hashtags with about 50K to 100K posts. 50K means 50,000.
  3. Open the Top posts tab, not Recent posts, to see the most engaged creators.
  4. Check each creator's profile. Avoid those who promote many products.
  5. Check the engagement rate and the comments before you make a shortlist. Engagement rate is how many likes and comments a post gets compared to its follower count. A shortlist is your short list of best options.

Instagram hashtag research without paid tools

  1. Search hashtags related to your product.
  2. Open the top results and note the creators or posts that appear.
  3. Check each creator's profile and calculate their engagement rate with free online tools.
  4. Keep only creators with an engagement rate of at least 3%.
  5. Read the comments to spot bought likes and generic or fake comments. Bought likes are likes paid for, not from real people. Generic comments are copy-paste comments that could fit any post.

TikTok

  • Search a product name on TikTok to see which brands drive trends.
  • Other examples cited include chlorophyll drops added to water in health and wellness. Chlorophyll is the green pigment in plants.
  • A candida cleanse product (candida is a yeast) prompted many creators to post about lifestyle changes.
  • The product detail page in the TikTok Shop explorer lists the creators who promote a product. The explorer is TikTok's built-in product search.
  • The page shows each creator's revenue (money earned from sales), sales and video output over the last 30 days. Video output is how many videos they posted.
  • The page also shows the highest-revenue videos for that product.

Instagram Explore, TikTok Discover and YouTube Search are the native tools for the manual method. Explore and Discover are the built-in browse pages of each app.

YouTube Research

1 min

Keyword-based channel research

  1. Make a list of 40 to 50 keywords (search words). Cover your niche, the problem your product solves and what your target audience searches for.
  2. Search each keyword and filter the results to show channels only, not videos. A channel is one creator's page with all their videos. A filter is a setting that narrows results.
  3. Keep channels with 10,000 to 100,000 subscribers. Subscribers are people who follow a channel.
  4. Check upload frequency. A channel should post at least two videos per month. Weekly or daily posting is better.
  5. Check the view rate. This is how many views a video gets compared to the subscriber count. Recent videos should reach at least 15% to 20% of the subscriber count. 50% or more is a strong sign.
  6. Check the comments. At around 50,000 views, a video should have at least a 2% comment rate. That is roughly 100 to 200 comments, and they should be genuine, meaning real and not fake.
  7. Find the contact in the About section, the channel's info page. An email or a linked Instagram or TikTok account is often listed there.

Note on the comment figures: at 50,000 views, a 2% comment rate would be about 1,000 comments, not 100 to 200. The source gives both values without matching them up. Use the 2% rate as the stricter check. Treat 100 to 200 as the minimum the source states for that view count.

Channel size. Sources give different thresholds, meaning different cutoff numbers.

  • Step 3 above uses 10,000 to 100,000 subscribers.
  • Another source considers YouTube creators to be up to roughly 100,000 subscribers, and sometimes above that.

VidIQ is a free tool that checks YouTube channel engagement and trending videos. Use it during YouTube research.

Paid Influencer Platforms and Tools

1 min
  • Modash.io is a paid tool for finding Instagram influencers. It gives detailed analytics (numbers that show performance) and engagement data for each creator.
  • Modash.io is considered accurate.
  • Modash is a paid influencer search tool with good results.
  • Influencity, AspireIQ and Tracker are influencer marketing platforms. A platform is a service that connects brands with influencers.
  • These platforms show performance data, audience demographics (who the followers are, such as age and country) and engagement rates.
  • These platforms list hundreds of thousands of influencers.
  • Searching with set criteria is much faster than finding candidates by hand.
  • These platforms help most at the start of an influencer search. They filter and compare many influencers with data, instead of searching by hand.
  • They are especially useful for brands just starting with influencer marketing.
  • Influencer databases with filters: Upfluence, Aspire and Traackr. A database is a big searchable list. Filters narrow results by set rules.
  • These databases are useful when time is short.
  • But they only show creators who have signed up to the tool.
  • Social listening tools: Hootsuite, Sprout Social or Brandwatch. Social listening means watching what people say online about a topic.
  • Use them to monitor conversations about relevant topics. Use them to identify creators already engaging with your topics or with your brand.
  • Fiverr is a site for hiring freelancers, meaning independent helpers.
  • You can hire someone on Fiverr to find influencer lists to a specific brief.
  • A brief is your written set of rules. It covers country, niche, follower range (how many followers) and minimum engagement rate.
  • Check any list from any source against a second analytics tool.

Working with a platform, in order

  1. Sign up and create an account.
  2. Define your search criteria.
  3. Search for influencers that match the target audience.
  4. Review the platform profile of each candidate.
  5. Reach out and connect with the influencer.
  6. Negotiate the deal.
  7. Track the campaign performance.

Influencer Directories

1 min
  • Collabstar is an influencer directory, a searchable list of creators. Use it to find people.
  • Contact and pay creators directly, not through the directory.
  • InfluSpine is a French influencer database.
  • It needs a paid plan, reported at about €40 per month.
  • It works best with a translation tool for non-English profiles.
  • InfoSpy is a French influencer-discovery tool.
  • It shows placement history (past brand deals), follower growth, engagement rate and audience demographics.
  • Its paid version unlocks country, follower and language filters, audience breakdowns and contact email addresses.

Existing Customers With an Audience

2 min

Karo's influencer side can scan your existing customers. The following steps are from Source B's 2021-22 material:

  1. Sign up for the influencer partnership side. This is the minimum recommended setup.
  2. Click "Get Started" and follow the setup wizard. A wizard is a series of step-by-step setup screens.
  3. Connect your Shopify account.
  4. Let Karo scan your existing customers. Optionally, feed in your email list through an email tool such as Klaviyo. Optional means you may skip it. Klaviyo is an email tool.
  5. Review the flagged customers who have an audience.
  6. Check whether they are relevant for your Dream 100 list. A Dream 100 list is a list of 100 ideal influencers or partners.
  7. Contact them directly, because they already know and bought your product.

Why this works:

  • Customers with an audience are a good starting point. They have already had a good experience and own the product.
  • Influencers already in your customer base or email list are a shortcut in a Dream 100 process. They already have your product and are easy to approach.
  • Sort them into the Dream 100 list after you discover them.
  • Karo's influencer side also covers creators and press or media who like your brand, not only social media influencers. Press means journalists and publications.
  • A customer who runs a blog, podcast or publication can be approached through the same process.
  • Karo's Shopify integration (a connection between two tools) can read email lists from tools such as Klaviyo.
  • This means both your customer base and your email subscribers can be screened, meaning checked.

Outreach pattern for existing customers (Source B 2021-22 material, older). Approach them as a warm lead, meaning someone who already knows you. Thank them as a customer, point out that you noticed their audience, and propose exploring a way to promote your products to it.

Thanks for being one of our customers. We noticed you have a following that fits our brand. Would you be open to talking about a partnership where you share our products with your audience?

Limits of this method:

  • For a young store with few customers, Karo's discovery may find nothing.
  • It is still worth setting up early, because the next sale could come from an influencer.
  • A store with many sales can still have no customers with an audience.
  • So the free test (testing at no cost) is the only way to know.
  • Karo's network figures were recorded in 2021 to 2022. The network is all the brands and creators Karo links.
  • Use these figures only as a rough scale reference. They are not current figures.
  • The figures: about 33,598 brands, 1,098,986 products, 6,853,585 influencers and 170 countries.

Trend Discovery and Competitor Check

1 min
  • Search a product name on TikTok to see which brands drive a trend.
  • The TikTok Shop explorer product page shows which creators promote a product.
  • It also shows their 30-day revenue, sales and video output.
  • Competitor-comparison videos performed reasonably well in one case. A competitor is a brand selling to the same buyers.

Agencies and Management

1 min
  • Avoid agencies that represent influencers for a fee. An agency is a company that manages creators and takes payment.
  • Agencies often put many brands in front of the same audience.
  • This causes burnout, meaning the audience gets tired of ads.
  • Find hidden opportunities manually instead. (Source A 2021 material, older.)
  • High-level influencers usually go through their management or an agency. You may never speak to the person directly.
  • Mid-level and micro-influencers are usually reached directly or through an assistant.
  • This allows a closer one-to-one relationship.

Both rules fit together. The first rule is about agencies that represent creators for a fee. The second describes how to reach top-level creators who sit behind management.

Creator Size

1 min

The sources disagree on the right size. Use each figure for the situation it describes.

  • Under 50,000 followers and one country or language: these creators are easier to work with.
  • They usually have an audience but no product or paid-placement income (money from brands paying to feature them) yet.
  • Avoid influencers who already sell their own course, product or brand.
  • Micro-influencers near the target market. When the market is a specific country, find a micro-influencer near it.
  • Have them film the video, so the accent and context fit the audience.
  • A creator from a different country can reduce trust among local customers.
  • Big influencers outside the US. For influencer marketing, target big influencers in countries other than the US.
  • Examples include Germany, France, the Netherlands and Sweden.
  • Example range. A specific niche makes the search more precise. For example, beauty influencers in Germany with 50,000 to 200,000 followers.

The sources give no single rule for choosing small or big creators. The market decides. Small creators fit one country or language. Big-creator advice applies to markets outside the US.

Regional and Non-English Markets

1 min

Choosing the market

  • Focus on one country or language and one niche when searching.
  • Non-English markets such as Spanish, German or Dutch face less competition than English.
  • Untapped markets (markets few brands use yet), such as certain European countries, Australia and New Zealand, are often better for influencer launches.
  • The US is less ideal, because beauty products are already widely seen there.
  • Strong influencer markets were listed as the UK, France, the Netherlands, Germany, Canada and Australia.
  • In Europe, the Netherlands, Germany and France were ranked strongest, with Belgium acceptable.

Working in a non-English market

  • The influencer's posts and your website must both be in their native language, the language they grew up with.
  • Use a human translator for the website, meaning a real person, not an automatic tool.
  • Message the influencer on the platform where they are most active.
  • If TikTok is blocked or restricted in your own country, hire creators in other countries.
  • They can shoot content and manage your TikTok accounts.
  • The course operator used this approach for brands that could not access TikTok locally.

Tools for non-English discovery: InfoSpy for French creators and InfluSpine for French profiles, with a translation tool for non-English profiles.

Creator Outreach Case: Thomason

1 min

Thomason is a platform connecting sellers (businesses that sell products) with lifestyle bloggers. These bloggers write reviews and promote products across their blog, Facebook, Instagram and Twitter.

  • Offering free products, or charging $197 for a $40 product, drew backlash (strong public criticism) from the community.
  • Even charging $2 per blogger produced 50% non-completion rates. Non-completion means bloggers did not finish the job.
  • A real review needed $100 to $300 per quality blogger.
  • ROI was poor. ROI means return on investment: money earned compared to money spent.
  • The time investment was not worth it.
  • The seller stopped using the platform.
  • They switched to Feedback Genius email automation, a tool that sends emails automatically.
  • They also switched to organic, sales-driven reviews, meaning free reviews from real buyers.

Product-Specific Case: Teddy Bear Product

1 min

A seller was testing a teddy bear product. Ads were hard to make work, so the seller turned to influencers.

  1. Target accounts that post videos with kids, and check the audiences of those accounts.
  2. Reach out to moms through those accounts.
  3. Share a demo video of the product with a child reacting. Moms who saw it were more willing to promote it.
  4. Comparison videos against a known competitor also performed reasonably well.

Commissioning UGC Creators

Section 13.16 · 16 of 21 in this chapter · 8 min read

In short

  • Test each creator with a small order or sample video before you pay for more.
  • Give every creator a clear script (the exact words and actions) and a short brief (an instruction sheet) before filming.
  • Match the creator's age, gender and country to your customer, so viewers can relate.
  • Agree on a fee before filming, and secure the right to use the footage before it is made.
This section has 14 parts. Tap one to open it.

Sourcing creators on marketplaces

1 min
  • UGC (user-generated content) means short videos that look like a real person made them, not a polished ad. A creator is the person who films them.
  • Four platform routes are listed: Upwork, Fiverr, the TikTok Creator Marketplace and Billow. A marketplace is a website where freelancers (self-employed people who take jobs) offer their work.
  • An agency (a company that does the work for you) that finds creators on incense.com is a fifth route.
  • Billow is spelled "Billo" in one place and "Billow" in another.

Hiring a UGC creator on Upwork

1 min
  1. Post a job that describes the new e-commerce project (your online shop) and the number of creatives (ad videos or pictures) you need.
  2. Send all applicants a Google Form (an online questionnaire) with questions on expectations, salary and experience.
  3. Shortlist the answers. This removes about half of the applicants.
  4. Interview the rest on Zoom (a video call app).

Working with a freelancer on Fiverr

1 min
  1. Find a UGC creator on Fiverr who has relevant experience, such as a background in journalism (writing news stories).
  2. Send the product. Allow about one week for the creator to receive it before filming.
  3. Send the creator a script or a clear steer (a clear direction) on the angle (the main point of the video) before filming starts.
  4. Review the footage (the raw video recorded) and test it against the existing hooks (the first few seconds that catch attention) and angles.

Another way to start on Fiverr:

  1. Search for UGC on Fiverr.
  2. Scroll through the freelancers offered.
  3. Choose the freelancer whose style fits your product, reviewing their past work.
  4. Start working with them.
  5. If the result is good, continue the collaboration long term.

Sourcing on the TikTok Creator Marketplace

1 min
  1. Open creatormarketplace.tiktok.com and create an account to get full platform access.
  2. Create a campaign (a job post for creators) and enter the store and product information.
  3. Wait for creators to apply to the campaign.
  4. Approve the creator you want to work with.
  5. Ship the product to the creator.
  6. Receive the UGC video.

Ordering a video on Billow

1 min
  • Billow offers four video types: video ad, TikTok short, product showcase or honest review.
  • One quoted order costs about $119.
  1. Create an account on the Billow website.
  2. Click Get a video.
  3. Select an existing product you used before, or create a new product.
  4. Choose the video type: video ad, TikTok short, product showcase, or honest review.
  5. Choose how many creators should work on the product.
  6. Check the final price, which updates after every change, and adjust the settings.
  7. Choose the format: portrait (standing up), landscape (lying flat), vertical, or square.
  8. Choose whether to add subtitles, animations, or your own pictures.
  9. Set the preferred creator age so it matches your target customer (the person you want to sell to).
  10. Set the preferred creator gender. For a product for women only, deselect male and keep female.
  11. Choose the creator interests that fit the product.
  12. Add additional notes for the creator.
  13. Click Next step and complete the payment.
  14. Within 2-3 days, creators apply to the task.
  15. Filter the applicants and choose the creator you like most.
  16. Within 1-2 weeks after the creator receives the product, you get the finished video.

Billow order options:

  • Get a video
  • Product (existing or new)
  • Video type (video ad, TikTok short, product showcase, honest review)
  • Number of creators
  • Format (portrait, landscape, vertical, square)
  • Subtitles
  • Animations
  • Added pictures
  • Preferred creator age
  • Preferred creator gender (deselect a gender to exclude it)
  • Creator interests
  • Additional notes
  • Next step (payment)

Creators start applying after payment 2-3 days
Finished video arrives after the creator receives the product 1-2 weeks

Using an agency

1 min
  • An agency can research the product and the store, write scripts and send them to creators found on incense.com.
  • Creator fees are paid separately from the agency fee.
  • An agency can write briefs or scripts for creators.
  • The creators shoot the ad videos, so the store owner only reviews the finished videos.
  • Once the store is scaling (growing fast), this moves content production out of the owner's hands.

Writing the brief and script

1 min
  • Write a detailed, well-written ad script (the exact words and actions for the video) before you outsource (pay others for) the video.
  • The script should show the problem, give the solution, educate, create curiosity and build desire (the wish to buy).
  • Fiverr creators are often generalists (people who do many kinds of work) rather than ad copywriters (people who write ads).
  • So a great script gets great videos, and a poor one wastes money.
  • Send the script before the product ships.
  • A creator needs about one week to receive the product before filming.
  • So writing and sending the script during that wait lets filming start as soon as the product arrives.

Raw scene clips

1 min
  • Ask creators for raw 3-5 second scene clips, not a finished 60-second video. "Raw" means unedited, straight from the camera.
  • Each scene is quick to record. This makes the work cheap for creators.
  • Short scenes also give you more material to put together yourself.

Creative brief math

1 min
  • One 60-second brief is split into 12 scenes of about 5 seconds each.
  • The 12 scenes are: 3 hook scenes, 3 product-introduction scenes, 3 benefit scenes and 3 CTA scenes. A CTA (call to action) is the line that tells people what to do next, such as "shop now."
  • Choosing one of 3 hooks, one of 3 intros, one of 3 benefit clips and one of 3 CTAs gives 81 combinations (different mixes). The maths is 3 x 3 x 3 x 3 = 81.
  • About 20 high-quality videos can be picked from these combinations.
  • So a $60 creator fee works out to about $3 per video.

Raw clip request template:

Subject: Raw clips for [PRODUCT NAME]

Hi [CREATOR NAME],

We would like to work with you on [NUMBER] short scene videos for [PRODUCT NAME]. Please do not send a finished 60-second video. Instead, record separate raw clips of about 3-5 seconds each, one scene per clip, with no editing and no music.

Please record these scenes:
- 3 hook scenes: [HOOK ANGLE 1], [HOOK ANGLE 2], [HOOK ANGLE 3]. Keep the first moment strong and simple.
- 3 product-introduction scenes: the product in your hand, an unboxing or first use of [PRODUCT NAME].
- 3 benefit scenes: show [BENEFIT 1], [BENEFIT 2] and [BENEFIT 3] in use. Speak or show the benefit clearly.
- 3 call-to-action scenes: you holding the product and saying [CTA LINE, e.g. shop now or check the link].

For each scene, use a plain background and keep the phone steady. Send all clips separately so we can combine them ourselves.

Thank you,
[YOUR NAME]
  • Some UGC creators now charge more for raw clips. The brand does the editing, so the creator's output becomes more valuable.
  • Agree on a clear fee for the number of raw clips before the shoot.

Matching creators to the audience

1 min
  • Audience demographics (facts such as age, gender and country of your viewers) matter in UGC. People relate to people who look like them.
  • If you target Gulf countries (such as the UAE and Saudi Arabia), consider whether the creator's ethnicity and accent match the audience.
  • Test both approaches and measure performance (how well the ads sell).
  • Match the creator's age and gender to the target customer, so viewers can relate to the person showing the product.
  • For a female-only product, exclude male creators.
  • When the product cannot be sourced in your own market, hire creators in the target country. The content then looks local and relatable.
  • Creators in the same market are easier to brief for raw scenes.
  • Hire creators from the country you are targeting, and use a local accent or voice.
  • One example showed that UK-accented UGC for UK customers brought most sales.

Case: a UK-accented creator for a UK audience

1 min

  • A store where about 90% of buyers came from the UK added TikTok UGC made by a creator with a British accent.

  • Sales rose sharply.

  • The seller then offered a simple digital (electronic) user guide, made with ChatGPT (an AI writing tool) and one or two images.

  • The guide was given as a free gift with purchase, listed at 19.95 so the free gift looked valuable.

  • Earlier buyers who had missed the guide bought it once they heard about it.

  • The seller then gave it to them free as extra service.

  • Reviews were five stars.

  • The seller had at one point reported a conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy) of 11.11%.

  • The seller planned to run the same ads on Facebook and to add picture ads.

  • For organic growth (free growth from your own posts, not paid ads) on Instagram or TikTok, hire a creator to produce several videos.

  • You post these videos on your own account. Relatable people using the product help the audience connect with the store.

Vetting creators

1 min
  • Freelance sites such as Fiverr often produce poor-quality UGC.
  • Test candidates with a sample or test video before committing.
  • Expect many misses before you find a good creator.
  • Reviews alone do not guarantee video quality.
  • A freelance video seller with thousands of five-star reviews delivered three unusable videos.
  • Test a seller with a small order before committing.

Contracts, volume and rights

1 min
  • Set UGC work as a daily output over a month, for example at least three videos per day.
  • This lets the creator calculate total volume and quote a price.
  • Negotiate after the first month, based on quality.
  • Hire creators to make videos you post on your own accounts, since their own feeds (the posts on their profile) may be weak.
  • Talented creators with low views can be better value than popular ones.
  • Hire a mediocre (average) creator and train them for a few weeks.
  • Then work with a top creator at roughly a quarter of the price.
  • Long-term contracts usually bring better rates.
  • When paying influencers (people with a large online following) for video, commission (order and pay for) clips you can run as paid ads.
  • Do not settle for one-off posts on their own channel.
  • Secure the right to use the footage in your own campaigns before the video is made.

Group chats, content calendars and audits

1 min
  • Create a group chat for UGC creators who deliver on a regular schedule, for example two videos per month each.
  • The group lets creators network (meet and share ideas) with like-minded creators. This makes the partnership more valuable to them.
  • JotForm (an online form tool) can serve as a content calendar (a plan of what to post and when) and creator management system.
  • Creators submit content through a form that is open only on set days, such as Tuesdays and Fridays. This keeps submissions organised.
  • Run a content audit (a check of all content) before outsourced content goes live.
  • This catches off-brand posts (posts that do not match your brand), weak messages and compliance risks (risks of breaking platform or advertising rules).
  • Start new hires by having them produce test TikToks (trial videos) and review them.
  • Then give them posting rights (permission to publish).

Playbook › 13. Influencers and UGC

Briefs, contracts and usage rights

Section 13.11 · 11 of 21 in this chapter · 13 min read

In short

  • Send the creator a written brief before filming. It holds the brand voice, the video type and the goal.
  • List every piece of content you expect in the deliverables list. Anything not listed can be disputed later.
  • Get every deal in writing before work starts, including the fee and payment terms. A phone call alone is not enough.
  • Set dates early, and ask the creator to keep the post online for at least 30 days.
  • Get written permission to reuse the video, with the time period, the platforms and any renewal fee.

Why the Brief Comes First

1 min
  • An influencer brief (a written task sheet for the creator) should tell the creator the brand voice (how the brand sounds).
  • The brief should also tell the creator the type of video wanted.
  • The brief should also tell the creator the goal of the content, from the first contact.
  • The brief matters most for creators the brand has not worked with before. These creators need more guidance.
  • A precise brief at the start cuts down on edit rounds (back-and-forth changes to the video).
  • The more the creator understands before filming, the fewer changes are needed.
  • Sending complete information before filming is the most reliable way to avoid wasted time.
  • Complete information also keeps the creator aligned with the brand.
  • Complete information also gets a video that matches what the brand wants.
  • The brief must clarify expectations: the key message (the main point the video must make).
  • The brief must also name the target audience (the people the video is for) and the brand tone.
  • Examples of tone: family friendly with no swearing, luxury and polished, or playful with bright colours.
  • Doing this upfront prevents videos that clash with the brand.
  • The fee (the payment) must always appear in the brief.
  • The fee is normally agreed before the brief is sent.
  • The brief is sent once the collaboration and the rate (price per post) have been discussed.

Brief Structure

1 min

Build an effective influencer brief in this order:

  1. Campaign overview: campaign name, goal, purpose, and key dates and deadlines for deliverables (the pieces of content owed).
  2. Brand background: tone of voice, target audience, demographics (facts about the audience such as age, gender and location) and interests that inform the video.
  3. Key messaging: the core message, any call to action (what you want viewers to do next, such as buy or click), and the specific points the creator must mention.
  4. Content guidelines: format ratio (the shape of the video screen), whether on-screen text (words shown over the video) is allowed, required video length, and rules such as no swearing.
  5. Content freedom parameters: a springboard (a starting point of video types and options, not a script), example content, and room for the creator to brainstorm and add their own ideas.

Section-level rules:

  • The campaign overview states the campaign name, the goal, the purpose and the key dates.
  • The campaign overview also states the deadline for each deliverable.
  • The creator needs this from the first message.
  • The brand background section defines the tone of voice, the target audience and the audience's demographics and interests.
  • This lets the creator film for the right viewers.
  • Key messaging names the core message and any call to action.
  • Key messaging also lists specific points the creator must mention, such as a product's number of blades or settings.
  • Content guidelines set concrete rules.
  • These rules cover the format ratio, whether on-screen text is allowed, and the video length (for example 30 seconds).
  • Language limits are also rules, for example no swearing.
  • Be as specific as the brand needs.

Creative Freedom and the Springboard

1 min
  • Set firm rules where the brand needs them.
  • Leave the creator room to keep their own voice. That voice is why their audience follows them.
  • Give tighter guidance at the start. Loosen it once the creator understands the brand.
  • Give the creator a springboard instead of a shot-by-shot script (a list of every camera shot to film).
  • Offer one video type, or a choice of types, for example day in my life, get ready with me or tutorial.
  • Include example videos from other creators whose look fits the brand.
  • A rigid shot-by-shot list limits the creator.
  • A rigid list also reduces the chance of getting content that performs well.
  • Brands that control every detail often miss trending, high-performing content.
  • Content freedom gives the creator a brainstorming space (room to pitch ideas).
  • If an idea drifts from the brief, redirect it with a reason and a starting point.
  • Do not reject the creator's idea outright.
  • Brands that want great content should trust the creator's expertise.
  • The brand hired the creator for their skills and for the style that caught the brand's eye.
  • Keep main points specific but flexible.

Deliverables and Timeline

1 min
  • List deliverables at the top of the brief with the exact number of each piece.
  • For example: one Instagram grid post (a photo in the profile's photo grid), one TikTok post with paid media behind it, and one Instagram story (a short post at the top of the app).
  • Anything not listed can be disputed later.
  • Leaving a piece out, such as the Instagram story, leads to the creator saying they did not know it was expected.
  • Listing every piece removes that argument.
  • Set dates for idea approval, first draft, final edits and posting.
  • Approving the idea early saves the creator the most time.
  • The dates may shift during production.
  • The creator must know the dates from the start.
  • The timeline sets three dates:
    1. The first edit or draft due date.
    2. A feedback window for client changes.
    3. The go-live date (the day the post goes public).
  • Allow more than the minimum time for edits when the deadline is tight.
  • Example: in a sample influencer timeline, the first edit was due on 4 Nov, client feedback on 5 Nov and the post went live on 9 Nov.
  • A window this short is tight. Allow more time for edits whenever the schedule permits.
  • If the creator needs a physical product before filming, make the first-edit date depend on the product's arrival.
  • Say so in the timeline, for example as "first edit (pending product arrival)".
  • This stops the creator from being held to a date they cannot meet.

Platform Format and Testimonial Videos

1 min

Write the influencer brief and choose the content format using each platform's audience and content style:

  1. Identify the target audience's demographic and the content style that works on the platform.
  2. Pick the format that fits the audience. For example: short video on TikTok, visual posts and stories on Instagram, or long-form tutorials and reviews on YouTube.
  3. Write the brief around that format, so the content fits the audience you want to reach.

Produce video testimonials (recorded reviews from real users) with individual reviewers in this order:

  1. Work with each reviewer individually, not with a generic script.
  2. Send a template or brief listing the topics the reviewer must cover, such as premium natural ingredients or high bioavailability (how well the body absorbs an ingredient).
  3. Specify the lighting and other production requirements.
  4. Spend time crafting the review. The more time spent on the brief and the review, the better the result.
  • Video testimonials work best when the brief tells the reviewer exactly which product claims to make.
  • This way, the brand's differentiators (what sets the product apart) appear in every review.
  • Give reviewers a template that covers all required claims and production details.
  • When a product launches in a new market, put that launch next to the main product feature in the creative section.
  • The creator then covers both the feature and the goal of making the market aware of the launch.

Gifting and Creator Relationships

1 min
  • Start creator collaborations with a gifting stage (sending free product to creators).
  • Send product to many creators. Then build relationships with those who respond well and continue with them.
  • Creators feel connected to a brand through their direct contact there.
  • A good relationship with that contact makes creators more likely to post stories. It also makes them more likely to keep the brand voice in their content.

Negotiation and Written Agreement

2 min

Negotiate and document an influencer collaboration in this order. Once the influencer shows interest, the steps are the same:

  1. Open a transparent, two-way dialogue. Both sides talk openly about compensation, content requirements, timelines, contract terms and anything else specific to the deal.
  2. Document everything in writing. This includes compensation, scope of work (what work is included), deliverables, payment terms and all expectations.
    • A phone or video call alone is not enough.
  3. Provide the resources they need, such as the product, a photographer or samples. Or pay for them.
  4. Share brand information, service details and the content you create.
    • This lets their content stay true to their own style while matching your brand.
    • Both sides share the same objective.
  5. Respond quickly. Stay available to questions, concerns and collaboration issues so the campaign runs smoothly.
  6. Send a written brief covering the project, campaign, outline (the structure of the work), overview and costs.
  7. Thank the creator. Aim for a long-term relationship, even if you pay them or send free product.

Rules for the agreement:

  • Every influencer deal must be written down before work starts.
  • The written deal must include compensation, deliverables, payment terms and expectations.
  • A phone or video call is never enough.
  • Every point, including pay, deliverables and payment terms, must be sent to the creator.
  • Verbal or call-only agreements leave room for disputes (arguments).
  • Use a short written creator brief that explains the full project.
  • The brief covers the campaign, the outline, an overview of the work and the cost.
  • Send it in writing with the rest of the agreement.
  • For products priced above about $30 to $40, use a written agreement with an influencer.
  • Without one, some keep the product and never post.
  • Without one, you have no proof and no way to get the product back.
  • Brand information lets a creator stay true to their own style while adjusting slightly to your brand.
  • Sharing it is what makes the final content align with your message.

Small-Deal Contract and Approval

1 min

Contract steps for a simple post deal:

  1. Send the influencer a short contract: a one-page, three-paragraph digital agreement confirming the product and the post.
  2. Ship the product and confirm it was received.
  3. Receive the photo and caption (the text under the photo) for approval before the post goes live.
  4. Confirm the post includes your company name in the first eight words of the caption.
  5. Track whether the post was published and how long it stays visible.
  • Approve a new creator's first photo and caption before it goes live.
  • Once a micro-influencer (a creator with a smaller following) has proven reliable, per-post approval can be dropped.
  • Dropping per-post approval lets a brand keep a pool (a group) of about 1,000 creators available.

Post Duration and Visibility

1 min

Sources give different minimum durations. Use the value that matches the deal:

Situation Duration Source
Ideal: post stays on the feed (the profile's list of posts), since new followers scan the profile At least 30 days
30 days not possible Anything beyond a seven-day window is considered acceptable
Pay per post for very short durations, such as two hours Not recommended: does not build lasting brand value
Sponsored (paid) deal with a written agreement that keeps the video online Several weeks or months; one deal ran about six months with three to five posts
  • Ask influencers to keep the post on their feed for at least 30 days.
  • New followers scan the profile, so they will see the post only if it stays up.
  • Brands that pay per post for very short durations, such as two hours, do not build lasting brand value.
  • Prefer influencers who will keep the content visible over time.
  • Include a written agreement that keeps the video online for a set period, such as several weeks or months.
  • Some creators delete posts shortly after being paid.
  • One deal in this context ran for about six months with three to five posts.
  • For a sponsored deal, one good review video is usually enough.
  • A creator with a steady audience already gets a consistent number of views.
  • Requesting three uploads per creator is not necessary.

Promotion Brief

1 min
  • Prepare a written promotion brief with the exact story slides (the screens of an Instagram story), coupon details (discount code details), shipping terms and posting schedule.
  • Influencers do not know how to promote on their own.
  • Use a cheat sheet (a short reminder sheet) listing the templates and questions for the VA (virtual assistant, a remote helper) to follow.

Ownership, Usage Rights and Renewal

1 min

Clarify usage rights (permission to reuse the content) in this order:

  1. Clarify who owns the creator's content.
  2. Specify the usage duration in the agreement.
  3. Specify the platforms where the content will be posted.
  4. Credit the creator in the caption of every post that uses the content.
  5. If the usage should continue, reach out to the creator before the term ends.
    • Agree on a renewal at an additional fee.
    • Ideally, the renewal option is already written into the contract.

Usage rights clause, to fill in the bracketed placeholders:

USAGE RIGHTS CLAUSE (fill in the bracketed placeholders)

Usage term: The brand may use the Content for [number] months, starting on the date the first post containing the Content is published on the Creator's social media account. When the term ends, the brand must stop using the Content unless both parties agree in writing to a renewal. Any renewal requires an additional fee of [amount] for each extra [period, e.g. month or 30 days].

Ownership: [State who owns the Content and what license the brand receives to use it under the terms of this clause.]

Territory: [e.g. Worldwide].

Formats and media: All digital and electronic media and formats, including but not limited to: the brand's own social media accounts; websites, including the brand's site, third-party websites, and e-commerce product pages; other digital transmission or delivery methods, including email marketing and digital advertisements. This covers use on computer and mobile device platforms.

Credit: The brand will credit the Creator in the caption of each post that uses the Content.

Renewal: [Describe the renewal option, the notice period, and the renewal fee.]
  • A usage term can be renewed with a short extension (more time added) instead of a new contract.
  • For example, a 30-day extension can be agreed with the creator before the current term expires.
  • The renewal option and its fee should already be written into the original agreement.

Crediting Creators

1 min
  • Always credit the creator in the post caption when reposting their content.
  • This is a low-cost way to build goodwill and trust with the creator.

Paid Usage and Boosted Posts

1 min
  • If the brand plans to boost (pay to show to more people) the creator's TikTok post as a paid ad, state that paid media is behind the post in the deliverables list.
  • This tells the creator in advance that the video will run as an ad.

Supplier and Third-Party Video Permission

1 min
  • Before using supplier videos on social media, confirm the supplier has given permission.
  • Download the video from the supplier's Instagram only with their consent.

UGC Brief Prompt

1 min

Use this prompt to generate a UGC brief (a brief for user-generated content, video that looks like it was made by an ordinary customer) from the brand voice conversation:

Using our brand voice and brand guidelines, create a UGC content brief for [PRODUCT NAME]. Include a background on our target consumer [short description], do's and don'ts for the vibe of the content, the filming style, and suggested locations. Keep the brief short and ready to send to a UGC creator together with our brand guidelines document, which includes colors, fonts and style.

Send a UGC brief to a creator in this order:

  1. Generate a UGC brief from the brand voice conversation. It covers the target consumer background, do's and don'ts for the vibe, filming style and locations.
  2. Add the brand guidelines document with the colors, fonts and style.
  3. Send both to the UGC creator, so they can produce content that matches the brand.

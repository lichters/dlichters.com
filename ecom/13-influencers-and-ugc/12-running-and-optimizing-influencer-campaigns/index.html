Playbook › 13. Influencers and UGC

Running and optimizing influencer campaigns

Section 13.12 · 12 of 21 in this chapter · 11 min read

In short

  • Give every influencer campaign one clear goal, such as a product launch or a sale.
  • Check the creator's draft (first version) against that goal before anything goes live.
  • Post at the same time on your channels and the creator's channels.
  • Get permission before running paid ads on a creator's post, and keep those ads separate from your own posts.

Campaign Setup and Objectives

2 min

Campaign procedure. The two Source C versions of this procedure are merged here. Each step lists the ids it draws on.

  1. Set one specific objective for the campaign. Examples: a new product launch, an upcoming sale, brand awareness (people knowing your brand), or a new product promotion.
  2. Tell the influencer (a person with followers who posts about products) the objective. Both sides must understand the goal.
  3. Request a draft (a first version of the content) before anything is published.
  4. Review the draft. Confirm it matches the campaign objective before publication.
  5. Create the content together with the influencer. It is a joint post, published alongside their own posts. Agree in advance on what will be posted and which results it should generate. Confirm both sides agree on the message and the intended result before the campaign goes live.
  6. Publish at the same time on your own social media and on the influencer's social media.
  7. Analyze the results (look at what happened and why). Document what worked. Use these insights to choose future influencer collaborations that fit your audience.

One clear objective per campaign. Every influencer campaign needs one clear, specific objective before it starts. Typical objectives are a new product launch, an upcoming sale, or promoting new products. Without a defined objective, the influencer's content has no direction. The campaign also cannot be measured properly.

State the objective explicitly. Tell the influencer the objective. They must know what the brand wants to achieve. They must also know which product or sale is involved. The influencer should know the specific objective before content is created.

Review every draft against the objective. Never let influencer content go live without first reviewing a draft. Check the draft against the campaign objective. Do not only check general quality. Confirm its message serves the chosen goal (brand awareness, sales, or a specific product) before it goes live.

Co-create the post. Create the post together with the influencer. Agree on the message and the intended result before launch. This keeps the content aligned with the campaign goal. Shared understanding reduces the risk of off-message posts. A joint post also gives the brand control over the campaign message.

Respond quickly during the campaign. Answer influencers quickly. Handle their questions and scheduling issues in time. Slow or unavailable brand contacts make the campaign feel badly run to the creator. This can hurt both the result and the working relationship.

Coordinated Posting and Timing

1 min

Post on both channels at once. Publishing the same campaign at the same time on your channels and the influencer's channels maximizes reach (how many people see it) and impact. Coordinated posting across both audiences works better than posting on only one side. Running both channels together is more successful than either alone.

Protect the measurement day of a major post. For a major influencer post:

  • Do not run other promotions on the same day.
  • Do not let other influencers post on that date.
  • Ask the influencer to post within their own two-hour window, so traffic can be measured clearly.

Approve the first post with a new influencer.

  • Approve the photo and caption before publication.
  • Require your company name in the first eight words of the caption.
  • Approve all photos and captions for high-priced products.

Seasonal window. The period between Black Friday and Valentine's Day is a strong season for influencer marketing. The influencer receives a product, a script and a coupon code to drive sales.

Scale with micro-influencers in bulk. Micro-influencers are creators with small, targeted audiences. The goal was about 1,000 micro-influencers available to post at any time. Micro-influencers work best in bulk, because each one reaches a small, targeted audience.

Promo Scheduling and Story Frameworks

2 min

Five-story Instagram promotion framework. Each campaign runs over five stories, in this order:

  1. Introduce the product: its benefits and the problem it solves. Frame it as a personal story, such as answering questions about where the product came from.
  2. Demonstrate the product in use, such as showing how to apply a cream or use a hair tool.
  3. Share a coupon code (a code buyers type at checkout to get a discount) for a limited number of buyers, such as 50 discount codes. Add any free-shipping offer. Mention scarcity (the feeling that the offer is running out).
  4. Ask the influencer to show how to apply the coupon code on the website. They can use a screen recording or a smartphone.
  5. Repeat the coupon code and remind followers of the deadline.

Timing of the follow-ups:

  • 24 hours later: post a story saying the coupon has sold out. Promise another batch later.
  • 24 hours after that: post a story announcing an extra batch of 30 codes, a last-chance message, and extra value such as a bonus.

Story script template. Adapt the wording to the product.

Story 1: 'Hey guys, I got a lot of questions about this. People kept asking where I got this. I got it from this brand. It does [benefit] and it helped me with [problem]. This is the product, here is what it does and which issues it solves.'
Story 2: The influencer demonstrates the product in use, for example applying a cream or using the hair tool, on camera.
Story 3: 'Hey, a lot of you have been asking, and I have good news. I reached out to the brand and got a discount code for the next 50 buyers. You get 50 percent off with my code, plus free shipping on orders above [amount]. Enjoy.'
Story 4: Screen recording or smartphone video showing how to go to the website and enter the coupon code.
Story 5: 'Just a reminder, here is the coupon code again.'
Follow-up after 24 hours: 'A lot of you have asked for this coupon. Sorry, it has expired. We will have to wait for the next batch, but I will keep you posted if I can get another one.'
Another follow-up after 24 hours: 'Good news, I got 30 extra codes and I cannot get any more. If you missed it last time, this is your last chance, plus extra value from the brand.'

Serial progress videos. A creator posts a daily check-in (a short update each day) on a product's results. This brings viewers back to follow the next update. This format works best for products where results can be shown over several days.

Discount Codes and Exclusive Offers

1 min

Follower-only codes. Exclusive coupon codes or special offers are available only to an influencer's followers. They create a sense of privilege and special access. This increases redemption rates (the share of codes that get used). It also strengthens follower loyalty.

Limited batches. The five-story framework uses small, capped batches (50 codes, then 30 extra). It announces sellouts and deadlines to create scarcity.

Organic-to-Paid Engagement Posts

1 min

Growing a Facebook or Instagram page with a promoted post. This is the Source A 2024 approach. Organic-to-paid means you start with a free post, then pay to boost it.

  1. Find trending memes or content seen on Facebook, Twitter or Instagram. Adapt it to the target customer's audience.
  2. Publish it as a post on the Facebook business page.
  3. In Meta Ads Manager (Meta's tool for building Facebook and Instagram ads), create a new campaign with the goal set to Engagement (likes, comments, shares and other reactions).
  4. Choose "use existing post" in the ad builder. Select the published post.
  5. Set a daily budget of about 3 to 5 dollars (the most you spend per day). Narrow the targeting (choosing who sees the ad) to the ideal customer: age range, gender, location and interests.
  6. Let it run for a few days. Then set the budget to zero and turn the campaign off, since engagement can continue for months.

Partnership Ads, Spark Ads and Whitelisting

2 min

Spark Ads boost content designed to convert. Spark Ads are TikTok ads that boost a post. A Spark Ad can give an early boost to a video designed to convert (lead to a sale). Organic reach (views you get for free) may then take over. Videos can be posted organically first and promoted later. Or they can be created from the start for promotion.

Spark Ads procedure (TikTok).

  1. Have an influencer create and post content featuring your product on their own TikTok page.
  2. Ask the influencer to whitelist that specific post. Whitelisting means giving you permission to run ads through their post. This requires only the authorization code (a permission code) for that one post, not their account login.
  3. Run the influencer's post as a Spark Ad from your ad account.
  4. Keep your own organic posts separate from the Spark Ad. This avoids damaging their organic reach.

Do not Spark-boost your own winning organic posts. Spark Ads run on the same brand page can kill organic reach. Run Spark Ads from an influencer's post or from a separate page. Do not run them from your own successful organic posts.

Spark Ads are TikTok-only. Spark Ads can only run on TikTok placements (the spots where an ad appears). If a campaign also uses placements such as Pangle or Audience Network (other ad networks), Spark Ads need a separate campaign limited to TikTok.

Partnership ads. Partnership ads run a collaborator's post as a paid ad. They need the other account's permission before they are set up. The ad runs through the partner's post under that account's identity.

Your own character page versus creator whitelisting. A character page is a page with a made-up character that your brand runs. Running ads from your own character page avoids paying a creator revenue share (a cut of earnings paid to the creator). It also removes dependence on a page owner who can withdraw or change the terms. Creator whitelisting runs ads from a real creator's account. You have no control over that account. The trade-off of a character page is that you carry all page and account risk yourself.

Whitelist pages for video ads. Whitelist pages also work for video ads, especially UGC (user-generated content, made by real people rather than the brand). A video that looks like a real person testing the product feels native (like normal content, not an ad). It gets watched rather than scrolled past.

Matching faces.

  • When a UGC video shows a face, pair it with a whitelist character of similar look, such as the same gender and similar hair.
  • With several UGC videos featuring different people, use different whitelist pages. Match each page to its UGC face.
  • A similar look is acceptable. A copy of a real creator's face is not.

Handling Audience Saturation

1 min

Move to paid and UGC when an audience is saturated. Influencer partnerships depend on the audience. A saturated audience has seen the same offers too often. When one influencer's audience is saturated, shift to paid ads and UGC to reach new people.

Repurposing Creator Content

2 min

Why repurpose. Repurposing means reusing content in new places. Reusing creator content reduces production cost, since the video already exists. It also increases reach and authenticity. The audience sees different people with different experiences. They do not see the same brand voice every time.

Organic videos from unpaid creators. Organic here means free, with no payment. When a product becomes a trend, creators often post organic videos about it without compensation. If people spontaneously talk about the product, the brand can ask to reuse those videos. Only reuse them after the rights are agreed in writing.

Case: a viral candida cleanse product on TikTok. Viral means spreading very fast. The brand that drove the movement worked with several creators. Each took a different angle. The brand's figures are not given. It is not stated which creators were paid. Many creators posted without compensation. Five example videos:

  1. A creator tried the viral cleanse and posted a daily check-in, with day 4 as one update.
  2. A creator made a comedic video with a funny trending audio (a popular sound clip) about sugar cravings going away. 2b. The second creator's video was trend-based, with no voiceover (spoken narration). It used a trending audio.
  3. A creator unboxed the product and walked through her expectations.
  4. Another daily check-in on day 4, which viewers returned to follow.
  5. An instructional video about what to eat and which practices to follow.

Takeaway: when customers join a conversation for free, the brand can reuse those videos on its own social channels. It must first get the rights.

Choose candidates by engagement. Check engagement metrics (numbers such as likes, comments and shares). Look at both followers and non-followers. This shows which content performs best with the broader audience.

Prefer evergreen content (content that stays useful for years). Choose video that still makes sense when shown years later. Avoid renewing rights to trend-based videos. They lose relevance once the trend ends.

Check brand fit before reuse. Confirm each creator video matches the brand's values and aesthetic (look and style) before reusing it. The question is whether the video shows how the brand wants to be seen.

Re-publish on other platforms. A UGC video can be reused on another platform. Change the title, description or thumbnail (the preview image). These affect click-through rate (the share of viewers who click). The video itself can stay the same.

Long-Term Partnerships and Scaling

1 min

One creator over many one-offs. A one-off is a single short deal. A long-term partnership with one creator usually delivers more value than many short one-off collaborations with different creators. The relationship produces more connected and authentic content.

Ambassador deals and the fade-out. An ambassador deal is a long-term partnership where the creator represents the brand. Long-term ambassador deals can keep producing sales while a creator stays popular. Their audience keeps trying to copy them. Expect the partnership to lose value when the creator's personal brand (their own fame) fades. Plan the budget for that window.

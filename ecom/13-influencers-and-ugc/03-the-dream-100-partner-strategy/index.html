The dream 100 partner strategy

Section 13.3 · 3 of 21 in this chapter · 4 min read

In short

  • Dream 100 means finding about 100 people or businesses who already reach your customers, then building friendships with them.
  • Let them send their visitors to you. Do not fight them for the same people.
  • Free partner deals need no money up front. Paid deals cost money or product up front.
  • Do not post ads straight into their groups. Ask the group owners to partner with you instead.
  • Do not offer free products to big media or "as seen on" brands. It costs a lot and brings little back.

The Dream 100 Concept

  • The Dream 100 strategy (a plan built around 100 key partners) finds the top players already active in your niche (your small market, such as one type of product or buyer).
  • Top players can be celebrities, websites, blogs, YouTubers (people who post videos on YouTube), course creators, events, Instagram accounts, podcasters (people who host audio shows) and associations (groups that share one interest or trade).
  • You build relationships with them, so they send their traffic (visitors) to your store.
  • You do not compete with them for the same audience.
  • This traffic source depends on relationships. It needs constant nurturing (steady care) and tweaking (small changes).
  • It can bring in steady sales. That is why it is called a "gold mine channel."
  • Find people, businesses or organizations that already have an engaged audience (people who read, watch or listen and interact) of your target customers (the buyers you want).
  • Then build relationships with them.
  • This lets you gain some of their reach (how many people they reach) and trust while you grow your own audience.
  • Partnerships with influencers (people with a large following who recommend products) work because target customers trust, respect and listen to that person.
  • The word "influencer" describes this narrowly. Think of it as borrowing an existing audience's trust.
  • The concept has been described in sales and marketing (selling and promoting) books for a long time.
  • Many businesses grow this way, through relationships with others who serve the same target customer.
  • It is used less in e-commerce (online selling) than it should be.
  • It is a well-tested process.

The Four-Step Process

  1. Research: Find the people, businesses or organizations that already have an engaged audience of your target customers.
  2. Follow and contact: Follow them and reach out to them.
  3. Build a relationship: Build a relationship with them.
  4. Explore collaboration: Explore ways to work together with them.

Building the Dream 100 List

  • A Dream 100 list is a list of the top 100 people and organizations in your industry that already reach your ideal customers.
  • It works like a master supplier list (your main list of sources), but for influencers and referral partners (people who send buyers to you).
  • Blogs, companies and communities count, not just individuals.
  • The method builds a fixed list of about 100 high-reach partners (partners with large audiences) in one niche.
  • Examples are influencers, community administrators (people who run the group) or companies.
  • Approach them for partnerships. Do not send single cold emails (emails to people who do not know you) or post ads directly in their communities.
  • Any online place where your target audience already spends time belongs on the list.

Partner Types

  • Dream 100 partners can be:
    • Celebrities
    • Websites
    • Blogs
    • YouTubers
    • Course creators
    • Industry events
    • Instagram accounts
    • Podcasts
    • Online stores that are not direct competitors
    • Associations or online communities centered on your niche

Associations Find them by searching Google for your niche name plus "association."
  • To reach a whole segment (a group of similar buyers) at once, such as interior designers, find the communities where they already gather.
  • Those communities are Facebook groups, Reddit communities, blogs and YouTube channels.

Partnership Forms

  • Free relationships need no upfront payment.
  • Free forms include:
    • Guest podcast appearances
    • Guest blog posts
    • Speaking at industry events while representing your store
    • Affiliate deals, where the partner earns a commission (a cut of each sale)
  • Paid relationships mean putting money or product in upfront.
  • Paid forms include:
    • Sponsored YouTube videos
    • Instagram posts
    • Event sponsorships
    • Celebrity video collaborations
    • Paying a creator for a recorded video

Association Partnership Procedure

  1. Search Google for your niche name plus the word "association."
  2. Contact each association by email or phone. Introduce yourself and say where you are calling from.
  3. Offer the association's members a bonus gift or a discount on your products.
  4. Ask the partner to add a link to your store on their website, with your promo code (a discount code).
  • The link brings customers from the partner.
  • The link also gives you a backlink (a link from another site to yours). Backlinks help SEO (search engine optimization, the work of getting found in Google).
  • This works whatever you sell.

Why Relationships Beat Cold Outreach

  • Cold outreach means contacting people who do not know you yet.
  • Partner credibility (how much people trust a partner) transfers to purchase decisions. Customers recognize the name and face of a partner they trust.
  • The partner's endorsement (public recommendation) can move buyers who would never find your store through search alone.
  • One-to-one cold emails to single companies are manual (done by hand) and need long back-and-forth conversations.
  • Partnership outreach reaches many people at once. It is usually more efficient, because many inbound cold emails get ignored.
  • Posting ads directly in groups usually gets them removed.
  • Instead, approach the group administrators and the owners of channels or Instagram pages about a partnership.

Approaches to Avoid

  • Do not reach out to media or "as seen on" companies (brands that claim to be featured in the media) first. The cost is high and the benefit is small.
  • If a company asks for free products in exchange for a credit (a public mention of your name), offer the products at wholesale cost (the price you pay your supplier) instead of giving them away.

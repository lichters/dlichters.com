Customer-Generated content and reviews

Section 13.18 · 18 of 21 in this chapter · 14 min read

In short

  • Ask every buyer for a review, and make it easy: a text, photo or video all count.
  • Wait until the customer has had time to use the product before you ask.
  • Pay a small refund as thanks: about $2 for a photo, $4 for a selfie, $15 for a video.
  • Ask permission before you use any customer photo, video or review in marketing.
This section has 16 parts. Tap one to open it.

Principles: why buyer content matters

2 min
  • Video reviews (short videos of customers talking about the product) are a trust format that few competitors use in many niches. A niche is a narrow market. Publishing them can make your trust element (the part of the page that makes shoppers feel safe) stand out. Collect the largest number of reviews in your category as well, and state the count next to any claim about it.
  • Loyal repeat customers can become advocates (fans who promote your store). Advocates bring in new traffic, add credibility, and raise the brand's status in its market. Treat advocacy as a deliberate growth channel, not as a side effect of good service.
  • User-generated content (UGC, meaning material made by site visitors, such as reviews and customer questions) adds unique text to product pages. It also shows search engines that the store is a trusted source for those products.
  • Customer photos can replace generic supplier images on a product page and make the store look unique. Three customer photos of the same product are enough to start using this on the site. They can raise the conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy).
  • UGC-style photos (photos that look like ordinary customer snapshots) of real-looking customers holding the product act as social proof (signs that other people trust the product), next to reviews.
    • Rotate several variations on the product page or review platform to keep the set fresh.
  • Collect UGC-style reviews from real customers instead of borrowing them from other sites. Real customer photos, videos and text are more trustworthy than copied material.
  • Paying customers $2 to $15 in refunds for content can replace creator content that costs $50 to $100 or more per piece. A creator is a paid content maker. Customer-made content looks raw and native, meaning it looks like it belongs on social media. So it often performs better in ads than polished content ordered from creator platforms.
  • A review without a review badge (a small label that marks a verified review) is still better than no review at all. Use the reviews you can collect, even if they do not carry the badge.

Setting up the request system

1 min

  • Set up a video review request system and include it in every review request. Video reviews are ideal, but image reviews are also a strong form of social proof.

  • Brands that use both request types usually ask for a photo first, then send a review request afterwards. The same buyer then gets two separate post-delivery emails (emails sent after the parcel arrives). The choice still depends on the product type and what buyers are willing to share.

  • Ask for testimonials (written or recorded praise from customers) several times and in different formats, such as text, photo and video. More emails and more request types give a higher chance of collecting them.

  • Collect testimonials by following up after each first order. Turn satisfied buyers into sources of quotes. Each new testimonial becomes proof for the next mailing.

  • Collect customer reviews after each sale:

    1. Contact every buyer by phone or email.
    2. Ask for a review.
    3. Ask for a product photo taken with their phone, if they are comfortable sharing it.
    4. If not, accept a text-based review.
    5. Place the reviews on the product pages.

  • Use a post-purchase review service (an app that asks buyers for reviews after they buy) to collect unique store-level reviews for social proof. Yotpo, Shopify Reviews and Judge.me are recommended review apps. Yotpo was noted as expensive at the time.

Source A 14 to 21 days after the product is received Review and testimonial emails, giving the customer time to use the product

  • Send one short email to everyone who has already received their product. Put the store name and order number in the subject line (the title line of an email).

Request email templates

1 min

Use this template for the photo request after delivery. Words in double curly brackets, such as {{ first_name }}, and words in square brackets are placeholders. Fill them in with your own details.

Subject: Enjoying your [Product Name]?

Hi {{ first_name }},

We noticed your order of [Product Name] has been delivered, and we hope you are loving it!

We would love to see it in action. You can reply to this email with a photo of your [Product Name] in use, or post your photo on Instagram or Facebook using #[StoreHashtag] and tag our page @[StoreHandle]. Photos from our customers may be featured on our website.

Thank you for being part of the [Store Name] community!

[Your Name]
[Store Name]
  • Use a branded hashtag (a word tag starting with #, built from your store name), for example #[StoreName]. Ask buyers to tag the store page handle (the account name starting with @), for example @[StoreHandle]. The hashtag and tag give friends who see the post a direct path to the store.

Outreach to customers who have not reviewed

1 min
  • Write one or two sentences in the body. Ask for feedback on the product and their experience buying from your store. Do not add a link or a photo request.
  • When a customer replies, add the product-specific review to Judge.me as if it came from the customer. If the reply is about the store rather than the product, add it to the general store reviews area in Judge.me.
  • Do not ask customers directly to leave a Google review. If a customer's reply is positive and you want a Google review, thank them. Then ask them to post the same message on your Google business profile (your store's listing on Google), and link them to it.

Collecting photos from buyers

1 min

Procedure for an automated photo request:

  1. Wait a few days after the customer's order has been delivered before contacting them.
  2. Send an automated email (sent by software, not typed by hand) asking the customer to share a photo of the product in use.
  3. Let the customer reply to the email with the photo, or post it on social media with the store hashtag and a tag of the store page.
  4. Collect the photos and use them on the website as product images.
  • Photo requests need the product in the customer's hands, with the face visible and smiling. Reward the photo with a refund or cashback (money back). This shows the customer is happy with the result.
  • Create at least three different UGC variations for review photos. Each one should have a different person, angle and setting. Varied people holding the product read as real customers and give the review set more credibility.
  • Share customer photos on Facebook and Instagram with the customer's first name, location, the product name and the store name. Posts can be scheduled (set to publish later), for example once per month.
  • When customers have sent photos of themselves with the product, use those images in preference to studio shots (professional photos taken in a studio). Customer images show the product in real use.
  • Recruit creators or customers, for example in gyms, to film first-time product use reaction videos. Prioritize volume of UGC, because it both builds trust and fills the creative pipeline (the stock of new ad material).

Fashion niche: Instagram customer photos

1 min

A niche is a narrow market. Fashion is one niche.

Procedure for the fashion store:

  1. Build out the Instagram page for the fashion store (Instagram is a photo sharing app). This is essential in the fashion niche.
  2. Post your own photos of the products.
  3. Ask customers to submit their photos of the products they bought.
  4. Add a 'shop Instagram' link in the store header so visitors can buy directly from the posted photos.
  • Customer photos on social media provide social proof and are a major part of the strategy of successful stores. Collecting as many real customer photos as possible is especially valuable in the fashion niche.
  • An Instagram page with about 4,500 genuine followers drove sales when customers were asked to tag the brand. A post from an influencer (a person with a large social media audience) with about 127,000 followers also brought some sales.

Video reviews and video content

1 min
  • Only ask for a video review from customers who sound very positive during the call. Video requests are not for every customer.
  • Customer video reviews are UGC that the business gets to keep and reuse. They can also generate organic traffic (free visitors who find you through search, not paid ads) when posted to a YouTube channel, which may rank in Google search.
  • Customer video reviews can run as Facebook video ads and as YouTube video ads. They are rarer to collect than written reviews. They are still worth using when available.
  • Video reviews are a trust format that few competitors use in many niches, so publishing them can make your trust element stand out.

Procedure for turning customer feedback into video content:

  1. Ask existing customers for a video review.
  2. Collect the videos with a tool such as Video Peel.
  3. Edit a video only if it needs editing.
  4. Publish it on YouTube and link to the product in the description.

Post-engagement testimonial campaign

1 min

Procedure:

  1. Create a post and run it as a post-engagement campaign. This is an ad built from a post that people already liked, commented on or shared. It does not show on the store's page feed, but it can be used in ads.
  2. Ask commenters to upload a photo of themselves with the product, or to write a short review if photos feel too hard.
  3. Connect the campaign to Minichat (a messaging tool), so each commenter is sent a gift automatically, such as free shipping or a small coupon.
  4. Reuse the post with its comments and photos as social proof in later campaigns.
  • A store ran a video post as a post-engagement campaign for under one week, with a budget of a couple of hundred dollars. The post reached 17,000 views and earned 120 likes, almost 200 comments and 23 shares. The result was presented as strong social proof that could keep working as an evergreen post (a post that keeps working for a long time).

Incentives

1 min

Sources use different rewards. Each applies to its own setup:

Source Incentive When it applies Units
Source C Automatic 15% discount for a review with a picture LUX Reviews app request
Source C Refund per submission type (see table below) UGC generator form ,
Source C Discount on the next order for a photo of the customer wearing the product (stated as 50, likely percent) Selfie-style customer photo campaign
Source A Gift sent automatically, such as free shipping or a small coupon Minichat post-engagement campaign
  • A refund is preferred over an extra free item, because an extra free item takes longer to deliver.
  • Reward by submission type (adjust for your margins, meaning the profit you keep after costs):
Submission type Reward
Product photo $2 refund
Selfie with the product $4 refund
Video testimonial $15 refund
  • Some branded products with many creator-style reviews are backed by discounts offered to customers in exchange for reviews. This builds social proof quickly. Before copying this, check platform rules on incentivized reviews (reviews that come with a reward). Many marketplaces (shopping sites that host many sellers) prohibit them.

UGC generator flow

1 min

Basic version:

  1. Create a JotForm (an online form tool) where customers submit their custom content and grant you marketing rights.
  2. After the order is delivered, send an email asking the customer for UGC, linking to the form.

Full flow, in this order:

  1. Trigger the flow (the event that starts it) when the tracking app reports the order as delivered.
  2. Wait 1-2 days after delivery, so the customer has used the product.
  3. Send an email saying you want to showcase their happiness to other customers. Ask for a photo, selfie or video testimonial.
  4. Offer a reward, preferably a refund on their order, since an extra free item takes longer to deliver.
  5. Collect submissions through a form such as Jotform, with a category choice, an email field, a file upload and a consent checkbox.
  6. Use reviews directly, but ask for permission before using any content in marketing.
  • UGC Jotform form structure: first a category field to pick the submission type (product photo, selfie with product, video testimonial). Then an email field so submissions can be verified. Then a file upload field for the content. Finally a consent checkbox before submission. The form can be cloned from a template and the store name replaced.
  • For a customer selfie with the product, use the caption formula [customer's situation] + [product] = [solution or benefit]. Use it as the UGC form's prompt text. It works because the customer sees their own face in a solved situation, which makes them more willing to submit. Examples: Your cozy evenings + our blanket = warmth without the bulk and Your dog's morning walk + our leash = hands-free control.

Consent and marketing permission

1 min

Consent checkbox text for using customer content in marketing. A consent checkbox is a box the customer ticks to say yes.

Your positive feedback and experience with [STORE NAME] have been invaluable to us. We believe featuring your content on our website and social media channels can show other customers the value and benefits of our products. Tick the box below if you agree that we may use your submitted photo, selfie or video for marketing purposes.

[ ] Yes, I agree that [STORE NAME] may use my submitted content for marketing.
  • Use reviews directly, but ask for permission before using any content in marketing.

Supplier review permissions

1 min
  • Ask the supplier (the company that makes or ships your product) before using their reviews. This includes paid or influencer reviews. If they approve, add any disclaimer (a short legal notice) the supplier requires on your store.

Template for asking a supplier to reuse their reviews:

Hi [Supplier name],

I noticed your website has reviews for [product name or product number]. We don't have any reviews for your products yet. Would it be okay if we displayed these reviews on our website? Thank you for your help.

Best regards,
[Your name]

Procedure for sourcing supplier and customer reviews:

  1. Check the supplier's website for reviews or testimonials already published. Ask the supplier if you may reuse them.
  2. After approval, ask suppliers for reviews and images they can share for your product pages.
  3. Ask customers for reviews after purchase through a follow-up sequence (a set of timed emails). Incentivize them to leave one.

Importing reviews

1 min

Procedure for importing AliExpress reviews into LUX Reviews (a review app):

  1. Install the LUX Reviews app. Choose the Beginner plan (upgrade to Growth or Unlimited when scaling), start the trial and select take beginner instead.
  2. Set store primary language (English), and choose star color (brand or gold).
  3. Set review request timing to 28 days and the 15% picture-review discount.
  4. Drag the LUX import bookmark (a saved browser shortcut) to the bookmarks bar.
  5. On AliExpress (an online shop for supplier products), open the product. Click the bookmark and import to the store product URL (the web address of the product page).
  6. Filter to four stars and up, all countries, translated to English.
  7. Import reviews one by one after checking text and picture, then click finish importing.
  8. Check that the reviews and the product page show the imported count and rating.
  • Import only supplier reviews rated four stars or higher, from all countries, translated to English. Review each one before importing, to check the text and pictures look good.

Q&A on product pages

1 min
  • The SMPS Product Question and Answer app lets shoppers ask questions on product pages. A Q&A app is a question-and-answer tool. The free plan is enough for new stores. Paid plans add an FAQ page (a page of common questions and answers). The same developer also offers a Google Shopping feed app. A feed is a product list sent to a shopping site. This app sends products to Google Merchant Center (Google's product list tool).

Procedure for handling a product question in the Q&A app:

  1. A shopper submits a question on the product page with a name and email address.
  2. The store owner gets a notification that a question was asked.
  3. Write the answer in the app dashboard (the control panel), or delete the question if it is not suitable.
  4. Publish the answer. It appears on the product page and is emailed to the shopper.
  5. Use the dashboard to see the number of questions, the most-viewed questions, and pending questions (questions still waiting for an answer).
  • Enter questions that customers ask by phone, live chat or email into the Q&A app yourself, and answer them. Publish those answers on the product page when they would help future shoppers. The answers also support SEO (search engine optimization, meaning getting found in Google).

Pinterest user content

1 min
  • About 70% of brand engagement on Pinterest is user-generated. Pinterest is a site where people save and share images, called pins. User-generated means it happens without the brand posting anything itself. Stores should therefore make it easy for visitors to share their products.
  • Customers who save and share products from a store's site can generate traffic and sales without any action from the store owner. A visible Pin It button (a button that saves a product to Pinterest) is the simplest way to enable this passive sharing.
  • Entering pinterest.com/source/ followed by your store domain (your web address), for example pinterest.com/source/www.yourdomain.com, shows all pins that users have saved from your website. It is a free way to see who is already sharing your products.
  • Once you have found the users who have pinned your products, comment on their pins, follow their accounts and start a conversation with them. These users have already shown interest in your product. That makes them warm potential customers (people who may buy soon).

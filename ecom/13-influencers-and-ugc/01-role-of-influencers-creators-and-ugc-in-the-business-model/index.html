Role of Influencers, Creators and UGC (user-generated content) in the Business Model

1 min

Where Influencer Marketing Fits

1 min
  • Influencer marketing is an advanced tool for later stages of a business. It is not a starting tactic for a new store.
  • Influencers are one of the core traffic sources in this model. Traffic means visitors to your store.
  • Use influencers alongside SEO (search engine optimization, getting free visitors from Google) and paid search (ads shown on Google search). Do not use them instead.
  • Consistent revenue (money that arrives steadily) comes from paid ads, meaning ads you pay for.
  • Influencers add brand (how people recognize and feel about your company).
  • Influencers also add social proof (other people's approval that builds trust).
  • Influencer marketing is a long-term channel. It needs patience, consistency and admin work (paperwork and organizing).
  • Over time, it tends to be more profitable, consistent and reliable than relying on paid traffic alone.
  • Affiliates (partners who earn a cut of each sale), influencers and high-authority blogs (blogs that Google trusts a lot) rarely send meaningful sales early on.
  • Focus first on Google Ads (paid ads on Google) and Meta remarketing. Meta is the company behind Facebook and Instagram.
  • Remarketing means showing ads again to people who already visited your store.
  • Once the business has momentum (steady growth), use paid affiliate deals and a referral program (a reward for customers who bring new buyers).

When to Start (Stage-of-Business Timing)

1 min
  • Validate the product with Facebook ads before investing in influencers. Validate means proving that real buyers exist.
  • Then choose influencers with an engaged audience (followers who actively like and comment).
  • Influencer work is best used to build social proof and brand awareness (how many people know your name) once sales exist.
  • Start commissioning (paying for) UGC after a product is validated.
  • Here, validated means ads on Facebook or TikTok (a short-video app) already bring real buyers.
  • The goal is to improve conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy) and prepare for scaling (growing sales and ad spend while keeping profit).
  • When building a brand with purchased stock (products you buy and hold yourself) instead of dropshipping (selling products the supplier ships directly, without you holding stock), hire creators from the start.
  • Creators make authentic (real-feeling) videos that connect people with the brand faster.
  • Early growth came from finding the most ideal customer.
  • Early growth also came from growing an Instagram page organically (free, without paid ads). Instagram is a photo and video social app.
  • Early growth also came from getting people and influencers to promote the brand.
  • This worked well only in the earlier social media environment.
  • Influencer marketing and referral marketing suit later stages, after validation, when sales already exist.

Why Influencers Work

1 min
  • Influencer marketing transfers trust: the trust an influencer already has with followers passes to a brand.
  • Recommendations often feel more genuine than traditional advertising.
  • Influencer marketing is a useful way to reach a new audience and bridge the gap to consumers.
  • This is especially useful for new brands.
  • The reason: the creator already has an established audience, and the content feels authentic.
  • Influencer marketing suits new brands because influencers already have an audience.
  • That audience helps bridge the gap between a new brand and its customers.
  • Its authentic, relatable content tends to build brand awareness and visibility (how easily people see your brand).
  • Its effect must still be measured.
  • Influencer partnerships drive brand growth mainly because they put a recognizable face behind the brand.
  • This raises visibility and credibility (how much people trust you).
  • Shoppers often check a brand's social profiles (its pages on social apps) to confirm it is legitimate.
  • Shoppers also want to see real people who have used the product.
  • Content posted on the creator's own page carries more trust than content they only deliver for paid media (ads you pay to show).
  • This is because it uses the audience relationship the creator has built.
  • An influencer's large reach can expand brand awareness and visibility on social platforms.
  • It brings the product in front of people who would not otherwise see it.

Creative Reuse

1 min
  • Influencer collaborations give brands access to content that already resonates (connects well) with an audience.
  • You can reuse that content as shareable material to amplify (boost) the brand's message.
  • Influencers are skilled at making content that resonates with their audience.
  • Brands can reuse that content as shareable, compelling material to amplify their message.

Expected Returns and Volatility

1 min
  • Influencer marketing has high ROI (return on investment, money earned back for each unit spent) potential if successful.
  • When it works, the return can exceed any other paid platform.
  • Success is not guaranteed.
  • It requires strong research skills.
  • Influencer marketing gets traction (visible results, people starting to buy) more cheaply and quickly than paid ads.
  • Influencer sales come in spikes (sudden high-sales days) and are hard to predict.
  • So they are a gamble for direct sales.
  • Influencer-driven sales are volatile (they jump up and down a lot).
  • Do not expect steady daily revenue from influencers alone.
  • Example: an influencer-led store (a store whose sales came from influencers) sold about 16,000 euros in one day.
  • The profit margin (the share of each sale left as profit after costs) was 50%.
  • It was a one-day spike.
  • The sales came from influencer marketing only, with no other ads running.
  • This is one case and a spike, not a steady baseline (normal level).
  • A survey figure cited for influencer marketing states that 86% of people who use it effectively report an immediate or long-term effect on their brand.
  • No survey source is named.
  • Use it only as a rough indication.
  • Verify it before relying on it in a plan or report.
  • About 50% of consumers look for recommendations from others before they buy.
  • So influencer content works best high in the sales funnel (the path from first seeing a brand to buying) to bring in qualified traffic (visitors likely to buy).

Trends by Period

1 min
  • Influencer marketing was described as regaining strength in 2023 after a weaker period in 2021 and 2022.
  • So it was treated as a channel to test again.
  • Check the current market before relying on older assumptions about any channel.
  • Older advice from 2019 to 2022 on influencer effectiveness should be treated as dated (old).
  • The 2023 recovery means it must be re-tested rather than assumed.

Brand-Image Tradeoffs

1 min
  • Influencer marketing gets traction more cheaply and quickly than paid ads.
  • But it may devalue the brand image (make people think less of it).
  • Paid ads give more control over how the brand is perceived.
  • Celebrity endorsements (famous people praising a product) paid for across many unrelated products have damaged the credibility of testimonials (customer reviews and quotes).
  • Prefer ordinary customers describing their own experience.
  • Avoid famous names praising products they have not truly used.

Influencer marketing Faster, cheaper traction Lower entry cost Lower; may devalue brand image Spiky, hard to predict

Affiliates and Referral Partnerships

1 min
  • Affiliate partnerships (partners who earn a share of each sale) with blogs and creators can bring traffic and sales.
  • They are a useful growth channel once a product is validated.
  • Once the business has momentum (steady growth), use paid affiliate deals and a referral program (a reward for customers who bring new buyers).

Testing Influencer Traffic

1 min
  • When paid ads produce few add-to-carts (a visitor putting a product in their shopping cart), influencer content can be tested as an alternative source of traffic.
  • In the discussed case, it generated more add-to-carts and at least one purchase.

Seeding and Budget Split

1 min
  • In a first year with almost no money, one brand split its marketing budget 75% to influencer seeding (sending free products to creators).
  • The other 25% went to remarketing.
  • The reasoning was that traffic it paid for had to be captured again.
  • This is one brand's choice in a low-budget situation, not a general rule.

Choosing Influencers by Niche

1 min
  • Influencers are useful for a print-on-demand niche (a narrow market where items are printed only after an order) only when the niche is open to influencer marketing.
  • The influencers must also not be too large (not macro, meaning very large followings).
  • Choose influencers whose audience fits the niche.

Digital Products

1 min
  • Digital products, such as eBooks (digital books sold as files), can be promoted through influencers instead of paid ads.
  • This approach was planned but not yet tested.

UGC Practice

1 min
  • User-generated content (UGC, videos and posts made by real people, not the brand) builds trust.
  • Prospects (people who might buy) believe other buyers' experiences more than polished brand ads.
  • In dropshipping, raw clips (unpolished, unedited footage) from real people often move a viewer to the next step faster than traditional advertising.
  • For UGC after validation, give the creator the product.
  • Let them name the store in the video to build more trust.

Common Mistake

1 min
  • Celebrity endorsements paid for across many unrelated products have damaged the credibility of testimonials.

Further details

1 min
  • For holiday campaigns (sales pushes around holidays), influencer marketing on TikTok, Instagram, YouTube (a video app) and Twitter (a short-post social app) was described as the most profitable approach, ahead of paid ads.
  • Twitter ads and influencers were described as especially strong.

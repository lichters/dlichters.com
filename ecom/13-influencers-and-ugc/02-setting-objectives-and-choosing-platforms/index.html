Playbook › 13. Influencers and UGC

Setting objectives and choosing platforms

Section 13.2 · 2 of 21 in this chapter · 7 min read

In short

  • Pick one clear goal before you start, and tell the influencer that goal.
  • Know your target audience (the people most likely to buy) before you search for creators.
  • Choose the platform first, then find creators who are active on it.
  • Check each creator's engagement numbers and past work before you pay them.
This section has 10 parts. Tap one to open it.

Setting One Campaign Objective

1 min
  • Pick one specific goal before you start an influencer campaign. An influencer is a creator who posts to a large audience. Goals include launching a new product, promoting an upcoming sale or building brand awareness (people knowing your brand exists).
  • Tell the influencer the goal, so both sides work toward the same result.
  • Set the goal before you choose an influencer. Brand awareness, engagement (likes, comments and shares) and conversions (people who actually buy) each need a different kind of influencer.
Objective Influencer to look for Reason Source ids
Brand awareness Broad reach and a large, diverse audience A high-profile influencer can introduce the brand to many new potential customers.
Engagement An active audience whose followers regularly comment, like, share and re-post This audience may be smaller than a broad-reach audience but is more involved.
Sales and conversions A track record of shaping purchase decisions, such as in-depth product reviews, product usage demonstrations or exclusive discounts for followers Purchase decisions are the target.
New product launch An influencer who has previously introduced new products to their audience Consider a promotion or giveaway with them.
  • For awareness, a high-level influencer (a famous creator) may still be worth it when you need impressions (how many times people see a post), brand recognition and press.
  • Press features (articles about your brand) and backlinks (links from other websites to yours) can drive traffic (visitors to your shop).
  • Those same collaborations can also improve search visibility (how easily people find you in search results).
  • Creators match different goals. A creator with a highly engaged community who starts conversations on most posts suits community engagement.
  • A creator who dedicates videos to product reviews suits driving sales conversions.

Defining the Target Audience First

1 min
  • Define your target audience before you search for influencers.
  • Describe the audience's demographics (facts such as age, gender and location), interests and behaviour (what they do). These decide which influencer will connect best with your customers.
  • Examples of audience-first matching:
    • A fitness brand aimed at health-minded young adults should look for a fitness, nutrition or wellness influencer.
    • Those followers should already share that interest. Do not pick only the influencer with the biggest following.
    • The influencer then fits the brand's values, and their followers are more likely to share the buyer's interests.
    • A tech brand should look for an influencer who does gadget reviews, tech tips and gaming content.
    • Or an influencer who regularly publishes product reviews and covers the latest gadgets.
    • That audience is tech-savvy (comfortable with technology) and more likely to be interested in technology products.
    • A fashion brand with bold, edgy designs aimed at young buyers should look for a streetwear or experimental fashion influencer.
    • That influencer's aesthetic (visual style) should match the brand.
    • That style fits the brand's look and can attract new customers who match its designs.
  • The right influencer depends on the brand's niche (its specific market) and region.
  • A kitchenware brand may work with food bloggers, while a wellness brand may work with fitness coaches or lifestyle creators.
  • There is no universal list of influencers for every brand.
  • Start with micro-influencers (creators with a small, focused following).
  • Test at least five different influencer categories before choosing a focus.
  • Categories can include fashion, beauty, fitness, pets, mommy bloggers and photographers.

Influencer Selection Procedure

1 min

Follow these five steps in this order:

  1. Define the target audience by demographics, interests and behaviour.
  2. Set a clear campaign goal: brand awareness, engagement, conversions or another specific objective.
  3. Use an influencer marketing platform (a search tool for finding creators) to find candidates that fit the audience and goal.
  4. Check each candidate's social media channels on every platform they use, and check whether their content style suits your audience.
  5. Analyze the influencer's engagement metrics (numbers such as likes, comments and shares) before collaborating.
  • The influencer marketing platforms in step 3 are search tools for finding creators.
  • They are a different thing from the social platforms covered in the next sections.

Platform First or Influencer First

1 min
  • Audiences differ a lot by platform (the app or website where content is posted).
  • So judge a creator's audience on TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat or YouTube separately.
  • Choose the platform for the campaign first, then find creators present on it.
  • Start by choosing the social platforms your brand will use, and do your research on those platforms first.
  • Only after that, find potential influencers who are active on them.
  • Then judge each candidate on content, engagement and past collaborations (earlier paid work with brands).
  • These two rules agree on the order of platform and creator.
  • The selection procedure above uses "platform" for influencer search tools.
  • So keep the two meanings apart when briefing your team.

Instagram

1 min
  • Instagram is mostly used by millennials (young adults) aged 18 to 34.
  • Instagram is strongly visual, so it is built around pictures.
  • Instagram works well for photos, very short videos, Stories (short posts that disappear on their own) and links in Stories.
  • It is a strong choice when you want high engagement.
  • Instagram has a shop feature (a built-in store on the account).
  • A shop can be attached to the account, so products can be sold directly from the platform.
  • Influencer content can drive direct product sales.
  • Instagram's "Your Algorithm" control (the setting that decides which posts you see) accepts plain-language requests about the content a user wants to see.
  • A baby-product brand could ask for parenting advice, baby care and children's DIY videos.
  • This surfaces creators whose audiences match its target customers.
  • Instagram remains the largest audience for most influencers, even when they also post on TikTok.

YouTube

1 min
  • YouTube has a broader audience.
  • YouTube favours long-form content (long videos) such as tutorials, reviews and entertainment.
  • Viewers who watch a full video are more likely to buy.
  • So YouTube has a high conversion rate (the share of viewers who buy).
  • YouTube viewers often arrive with search intent (they search for a specific product or solution).
  • This high purchase intent (they are close to buying) gives YouTube influencer content strong conversion potential.
  • That is why YouTube suits product-focused influencer content.
  • YouTube is sometimes used as an SEO platform (a place that helps you get found in search results).
  • YouTube videos can support SEO (search engine optimization, getting found on Google).
  • A YouTube creator with large view counts can still underperform for a physical product.
  • This happens if the audience is young and often lacks its own payment method.
  • Check the buyer age before paying for a YouTube collaboration.

TikTok

1 min
  • TikTok's core audience is Gen Z (the youngest adults and teens).
  • Its short-form content (very short videos) spreads trends quickly.
  • TikTok favours very short, fast content.
  • TikTok's perceived authenticity (how real and trustworthy creators seem) is high.
  • Users often buy directly from authentic creators.
  • TikTok Shop allows a shop to be attached to a TikTok account.
  • TikTok Shop is a newer function.
  • It has shown strong sales results and growth.
  • TikTok influencers can deliver about three times the results of an Instagram influencer.
  • They can do this at about half the price.
  • Once they have a following, they start receiving sponsorship offers themselves.
  • TikTok creators can be cheaper than Instagram creators.
  • So a TikTok-focused campaign can be proposed as an alternative to Instagram.
  • The two figures above come from different sources and make different claims.
  • The first is a results and price claim.
  • The second is a cost and reach comparison.
  • Neither claim says how it was measured.
  • So test with your own data before relying on them.

Snapchat and Pinterest

1 min
  • Snapchat and Pinterest also exist but were left out of the main comparison.
  • Instagram, YouTube and TikTok were chosen for their engagement, sales and conversion results.
  • Snapchat and Pinterest are additional social channels.
  • For influencer vetting (checking creators before you hire them), check Instagram, YouTube and TikTok first.
  • Add other channels only when the target audience clearly uses them.
  • For a fashion sunglasses brand, Pinterest was used only as a repost channel (a place to share content again).
  • Snapchat was hard to penetrate (hard to get results on).
  • So the paid focus went to Facebook and Instagram.
  • Pinterest group boards have more than one contributor and are marked by a multi-person icon in the corner.
  • Some have thousands to over 100,000 followers.
  • Getting pins (saved posts) onto them can reach a much larger audience than your own profile.
  • To move existing audiences onto Pinterest, post a message on your other social accounts, such as Twitter or Facebook.
  • In that message, invite followers to follow your Pinterest profile or a new board.

Matching Content Style to Platform

1 min
  • Match the platform to the content style and the audience.
  • TikTok suits quick, short-form, entertaining content for a younger, trend-driven audience.
  • Instagram suits visually appealing images, videos and Stories.
  • Let the chosen platform shape the influencer brief (the instructions you give the creator).
  • The format delivered should fit how the audience on that platform watches and reads content.
  • For influencer marketing of clothing, try-on hauls (videos of a person trying on several items) perform best.
  • Unboxing content (opening a package on camera) comes next.
  • Unboxing content can be used as reels (short vertical videos) and Stories.

Brand's Own Channels

1 min
  • The platforms used to vet influencers, Instagram, YouTube and TikTok, can also serve the brand's own presence.
  • Plan the brand's own channels alongside influencer partnerships.
  • Each platform's format and audience should guide what the brand posts there.

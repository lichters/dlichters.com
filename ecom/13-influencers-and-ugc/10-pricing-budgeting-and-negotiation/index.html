Playbook › 13. Influencers and UGC

Pricing, budgeting and negotiation

Section 13.10 · 10 of 21 in this chapter · 10 min read

In short

  • Do not set a price from follower count alone. Look at the whole package.
  • Check the engagement rate first: the share of followers who like, comment or share. It often matters more than size.
  • Write down who may use the content, and how, before you pay.
  • Test with smaller creators before you commit a large budget.
  • Pay more for exclusive or paid-ad use than for a single organic post.

Fee drivers

1 min

Audience size and engagement

1 min
  • An influencer (a person with an online audience who promotes products) is paid according to several factors. The follower count (how many people follow the account) alone does not set the fee. A larger audience usually commands a higher price, but that link is not reliable, so audience size must be weighed against the other factors.
  • The engagement rate (the share of followers who like, comment or share) often matters more than follower count. Example: an account with about 500,000 followers and a 20% engagement rate can ask for more than an account with 1 million followers and a 5-6% engagement rate, because its audience is more active and more likely to buy. Engagement rate is a strong signal of what a creator can charge.
  • Price the whole package, not audience size alone. Niche fit (how closely the creator's narrow topic area matches your product), content quality, engagement rate and the campaign's brand objective (the goal you want your brand to reach through a planned marketing push, called a campaign) together determine what is fair and justified.
  • For UGC (user-generated content: videos made by creators for a brand) that the creator posts on their own account, follower count and engagement rate significantly raise the price. The engagement rate on the creator's Instagram or TikTok account should be above 4-5%. If the brand only uses the video itself, follower count and engagement matter little.

Niche and expertise

1 min
  • Creators who serve a narrow niche or are true specialists charge more, because few creators cover that niche and the market is less saturated (less crowded with competitors). Confirm that the creator's niche and expertise level match what you are budgeting for.
  • Influencers who are one of only a few covering a specific task usually charge more. True specialists in their domain charge even higher rates.

Content quality and track record

1 min
  • Content quality can justify a higher fee. Relevant factors are production value (how polished the filming and editing are), brand alignment (how well the creator fits your brand), how long the creator has worked as an influencer, how much content they have produced, and how well they produce it.
  • A creator with a proven track record (a history of good results), high engagement and a recognised name in the industry, who works in content full time, will usually ask for a higher fee. Leaders in their niche, such as a well-known tech creator, can command a premium because their expertise adds to the collaboration's success.
  • A fair fee is a judgment across audience size, engagement, niche expertise, content quality and the brand's goal. Weigh all of these before deciding what is justified.

Usage rights

1 min
  • Define the usage term (the rules for how you may use the content) clearly. State the exclusivity (only your brand may use it) and the usage rights (permission to use the content) you need. Exclusive use, or the right to repurpose (reuse for another purpose) content across channels and ads, raises the fee.
  • Price exclusive usage rights, and the right to reuse content in paid ads (ads you pay to show) or across several channels, separately. Charge a higher fee than for organic use (unpaid posts) on one channel. Write the granted rights into the contract.
  • Document in writing which rights are granted to whom.
  • Royalty (a share of the money earned instead of a flat price) alternative: a content creator who makes ad material for a brand can be paid about 3% of the revenue the creative generates in paid ads, instead of a flat fee. This works only when the brand can attribute (link sales to a source) ad revenue to that content.

Deliverables and campaign complexity

1 min
  • Define deliverables (the exact work the creator must hand in) in detail: the exact scope of posts, stories (temporary posts that vanish after a short time), costs and creative work the creator must deliver. Each additional element raises the compensation.
  • Assess campaign complexity when pricing. Low-effort campaigns leave room to negotiate a lower fee. Campaigns that need photographers, videographers or specific locations justify higher compensation.
  • Price UGC by use case (what the content will be used for). Videos for paid ads need higher production quality and justify a higher budget. Organic TikTok content can use lower production quality if it is engaging and attention-grabbing.
  • The platform (the app or site where content is posted) or company used to source the creator (where you find and hire them) is a major price factor, alongside the use case and the creator's reach (how many people see their posts).

Pricing procedure

1 min
  1. Define the usage term clearly: state the exclusivity and the usage rights you need for the content. Exclusive use or the right to repurpose content across channels and ads will raise the fee.
  2. Negotiate the terms openly with the influencer so both sides are satisfied, and document in writing which rights are granted to whom.
  3. Assess the campaign's complexity: low-effort work leaves room to negotiate the price, while high effort such as photographers, videographers or specific locations requires higher compensation.
  4. Define the deliverables precisely (posts, stories, creative work). Each additional element should increase the compensation.
  5. Evaluate the track record: well-known leaders in their niche who make their living from this work charge more, because their expertise contributes to the success of the collaboration.
  6. Negotiate the final compensation with all of these points factored in, and check that the price fits your budget.

Market benchmarks

1 min
  • Influencers charge anywhere from about $50 to about $1 million per collaboration. Before reaching out, determine where your budget sits within that range.
  • Know the market benchmarks (typical prices to compare against) for audience size, engagement rate, content quality and your niche before negotiating. Without them you risk overpaying or underpaying.
  • Adjust the market rate for campaign-specific factors such as the influencer's expertise, the complexity of the work and any exclusive rights. Reflect these adjustments in the contract.

Budget sizing

1 min

Budget procedure

1 min
  1. Assess the overall marketing budget and set a reasonable portion for influencer marketing without compromising the strategy or the business.
  2. Prioritise influencers within that budget. If an influencer is out of reach, choose another who fits both budget and goals.
  3. Research industry standards for audience size, engagement rate, content quality and your niche so you know the usual rates.
  4. Adjust the market rate for expertise, campaign complexity and exclusivity, and reflect this in the contract.
  5. Communicate openly about the budget and any constraints during negotiation.
  6. Show flexibility through long-term collaboration, mutual promotion (both sides promote each other) or affiliate options (the creator earns a commission on each sale they bring).
  7. Emphasise the mutual benefit, especially the unique value your brand brings to the influencer's audience.
  8. Discuss a long-term partnership, in which the rate can be lowered for an extended period.

Sizing rules

1 min
  • The influencer budget should be only a reasonable portion of the total marketing budget. Size it so that it does not compromise the overall marketing strategy or the business.
  • Never choose an influencer whose price is outside your budget, even if you strongly want them. Look for an influencer who fits both your budget and your campaign goals.
  • Test with smaller creators before committing large budgets. A high fee for a large influencer can return far less than expected: one reported $15,000 post returned about $2,000 in sales.

Flat versus performance pay

1 min
  • Flat fee: pays a fixed amount per deliverable. Use it when the deliverables and usage rights are fixed in advance and the brand cannot attribute sales to the content.
  • Royalty: about 3% of the revenue the creative generates in paid ads. Use it only when the brand can attribute ad revenue to that content.
  • Small fixed payment plus affiliate commission (a share of each sale the creator brings): works when the budget is limited. The creator earns a base amount for the post and more for each sale, which aligns their incentive with yours.
  • Affiliate commission for micro-influencers: the offered range is 6-7%.
  • Worked example: a kayak seller offers a local kayaker with a blog or Instagram account 3-5 free kayaks plus a 6% affiliate commission per referral (a sale the creator sends you). The creator profits from promoting to their niche audience without large upfront costs.
  • Flat rate for all creators: if a brand pays one flat rate to all creators, for example $500 per creator, state in the brief (the written task instructions) or negotiation that there is no room to negotiate.

Story versus feed pricing

1 min
  • Story placements (a spot in the temporary stories section) are usually much cheaper than feed posts for the same creator. For accounts with roughly 30,000-50,000 followers, a story post is often the better buy for predictable reach.
  • Story posts give predictable reach near the creator's average story views. A feed post (a permanent post on the main profile) or TikTok post can go viral but depends on video quality.
  • Choose story posts when consistency matters. Choose feed posts when you want high variance (results that swing a lot, up or down). You can also negotiate both.

Seasonal rates

1 min
  • Creators charge more during the fourth quarter (October to December) and around holidays. Negotiate and book posts before the season.

Negotiation tactics

1 min

Opening and concessions

1 min
  • Start with a low offer and increase only if the creator pushes back. Creators who work with you once often accept lower rates the next time.
  • Open communication about your budget and any constraints often leads influencers to come down in price, especially when they want to work with the brand.
  • Negotiate the terms openly so that both sides are satisfied.
  • Ask for the creator's rate directly. For a paid Instagram post, contact the account by direct message (a private message) and ask for the creator's rate for a post that gives your store a shout-out (a public mention).
  • For industry event sponsorships (paying to be featured at a trade event), ask for the costs first, because some are expensive and others are affordable.

Non-cash and incentive offers

1 min
  • Non-cash offers (value that is not money), such as long-term collaboration, mutual promotion or affiliate marketing, show flexibility and can bridge a gap in the fee.
  • Micro-influencers can be offered a combination of incentives:
    • free products;
    • affiliate commissions of 6-7%;
    • exclusive coupon codes (discount codes) for their followers;
    • free content creation help (guides or product reviews);
    • interviews featuring their expertise.

Mutual value and partnership

1 min
  • If your brand gives the influencer's audience unique value, the influencer may be more willing to work with you and adjust their rates. Emphasise this mutual benefit in the negotiation.
  • Long-term partnerships lower the cost per collaboration. Influencers value regular income, and the brand gains a reliable partner who keeps working with them over time.

Outreach messages and sequences

Section 13.8 · 8 of 21 in this chapter · 18 min read

In short

  • Send DMs first. Use email only when no DM route exists, or when the creator lists an email address (then email first, then a DM).
  • Write each message for one creator. Never send the same copy-pasted text to many creators.
  • Ask for one clear action per message. Do not talk money in the first message.
  • Wait 3 days, send a follow-up. After 2 more days, send one last follow-up. Then stop.
  • Plan for 2% to 3% of influencer messages to get a reply (an influencer is an online creator with an audience).
More key points
  • Contact a large list. Low reply rates are normal, so volume brings results.
  • Do not judge the campaign by the first few contacts. The first 20 messages may bring few or no replies.
  • Split test (compare two versions to see which works better), changing one element at a time. Each message needs one call to action (the one thing you ask the reader to do).

Channel: DM or Email

1 min
  • Send outreach as a DM (direct message, a private message inside the app) where possible. DMs are faster, get more replies, and reach the creator directly. An email may go to a manager who slows the reply. Use email only when DMs are not possible.
  • Use email as the first contact when the creator lists an email address. Then follow up with a DM. Instagram DMs are often buried and rarely read.
  • Use email for influencer outreach because it looks more professional. A DM can work only when the sender has a verified account (confirmed by the platform) with a large follower base. Otherwise, lean toward email.
  • Which rule applies:
Situation Rule to follow Source
A DM route exists and the email address is not listed DM first ,
The creator lists an email address Email first, then a follow-up DM ,
The sender has a verified account with a large follower base DM is acceptable
The sender has no verified large account Email
  • Outreach emails should come from a personal-style address (it looks like a person's name, not a company inbox) on a separate outreach domain (a second web address used only for sending). Details are in the sender section below.

Platform Choice

1 min
  • Outreach works best on the platform where a creator is least active or has the fewest followers. They reply more often there.
  • If you find a creator on one platform, check their other accounts. Message the one with the smallest following.

Sender Identity and Outreach Domain

1 min
  • Send from a personal-style address on a separate domain. The format is firstname.lastname@ followed by the separate outreach domain.
  • Sign with a title such as Marketing Officer and the company name.
  • The source says this setup significantly raises open rates (how many people open the email) and reply rates (how many people answer).

Vetting Before Outreach

1 min
  1. Build a list of influencers that fit the criteria. Criteria: one country or language, one niche (one topic, such as skincare), a follower range, a minimum 5 percent engagement rate (the share of followers who like or comment), and no own product yet.
  2. Verify the engagement rate and follower trend (whether followers are growing or shrinking) with a second analytics tool (a program that measures account data). Also check placement history (their earlier paid posts for other brands).
  3. Send the first outreach email from a personal-style address with a marketing-officer signature, using an automated sequence (a set of messages sent on a schedule).
  4. Once there is a positive reply, send a due-diligence template (a checking message; due diligence means checking facts before a deal). Ask the influencer for audience countries, age split, gender split, and store reviews. This confirms the analytics data.
  5. Move the conversation to a call, since influencers avoid direct partnership pitches by email.
  6. Agree a deal structure (the payment and work terms) and calculate what you can pay. Do this before sending a promotion brief (a document that describes what to post).

Personalisation

1 min
  • Tailor every outreach message to the individual creator. Mention one specific piece of their content they did well.
  • Never send the same copy-pasted message to many influencers. Each message should pick out something specific the creator did well. Explain why that content fits your brand and campaign.
  • Address each creator by name. Mention a specific part of their work that matches your brand. Creators can tell a personal message from a mass one right away.
  • Mass emails with unfilled merge fields (placeholders, such as a gap where the creator's name or handle, meaning username, should be) in the greeting are spotted at once as copy-paste outreach. Creators send them straight to spam (the junk folder).
  • Each message should name a specific reason the creator fits, such as their style or feed (the stream of posts they show). Generic praise does not work. A specific reason makes the request look personal and gives the creator a reason to reply.
  • State how the collaboration fits your brand. Also state how it helps the influencer reach their own goals. This match of interests is a key reason creators accept.
  • A handwritten note from a founder or CEO (the head of the company) that refers to a specific recent video of the creator makes a PR package (a press package: product plus information sent to creators) far more persuasive. It raises the creator's willingness to work with the brand. The note works because it shows real interest in what the creator makes, not only in the product.

Opening Lines

1 min
  • An opener such as "we would love to work with you" with no details gets an immediate rejection from creators.
  • Opening formula: the creator's name, one concrete reference to a recent video or post, and the single reason the brand fits their audience. It works because it proves the message was written for that particular person.
    • Example 1: Hi Maya, the drawer-organization video you posted last week made me rethink how we lay out our product range, and I would love to talk about a collaboration.
    • Example 2: Hi Jonas, the way you broke down your marathon training plan in your last series is exactly how our running-gear customers think, so I would like to discuss a campaign with you.

Core Message Formulas

3 min

  • Short DM formula: three sentences.

    • Sentence 1: introduce yourself and your brand.
    • Sentence 2: praise one specific piece of their work.
    • Sentence 3: explain why they fit your current campaign and how the collaboration serves both sides.
    • Keep the message brief and clear.
    • Example A (fitness supplement brand, 2026): Hi Lena, I'm Marco from Northfield Nutrition. Your morning-routine series on creatine timing was the clearest thing I've seen on the topic. Our new electrolyte launch fits your audience of home-workout viewers, and I think a paid collaboration would work well for both of us.
    • Example B (pet accessories store): Hi Tom, I'm Sara, founder of Pawline. Your slow-motion clips of your rescue dog on the trail are great, especially the one with the harness. We're launching a reflective leash in March that suits your outdoor content, and I'd love to talk terms if you're open to it.

  • Four-part DM formula: a very short message with four parts:

    1. A brief introduction of you and your brand.
    2. A specific expression of admiration for their work.
    3. A clear explanation of why they fit your current campaign.
    4. A simple next step.

    The creator can read it in seconds and sees that you actually looked at their content.

    • Example 1: Hi Lena, I'm Sam from a home-kitchen brand. Your slow-motion knife-skills video made me rethink how we show product texture. We're launching a cookware set for home cooks next month and your audience is a close match, so would you be open to a short call about a paid collaboration?
    • Example 2: Hey Marco, I run a small outdoor gear shop. Your honest review of a beginner trail pack stood out because you showed what failed too. We're building a spring campaign for first-time hikers and I think your style would carry it well. Can I send you the details?

  • Ask for one clear action per message. Ask whether they are interested, or request their media kit after they have shown interest. A media kit is a press-style sheet with audience facts and prices. Several requests in one message make the reply harder.

  • Eight-step structure:

    1. Find the creator's contact details. An email address in the bio (profile text) is preferred because it feels more professional.
    2. Open with praise for a specific piece of their work, in two or three lines.
    3. Pitch your product and explain how it helps their audience.
    4. Mention the collaboration and the benefit it gives them.
    5. Use the headline "brand collaboration opportunity" so the message feels like a big opportunity to them.
    6. If there is no reply, follow up by email.

  • Outreach message pattern: the first message should be short and personal, not a sales pitch.

    • Name something specific you admire in their work.
    • State the shared space or audience in one line.
    • Mention that you see possible joint opportunities.
    • Ask directly whether they would join a short call.
    • Example: Hi Sam, I enjoyed your piece on home coffee gear, and our customers overlap a lot. I think there is a good way for us to work together. Would you be open to a 20-minute call next week?

  • Outreach procedure:

    1. Reach out in the channel you already use with the prospect. Examples: email if you have their address, a Facebook message to a group admin, or an Instagram direct message.
    2. Send a short introduction. It says you like their work, that you are in the same space, that you see possible opportunities to work together, and asks whether they would be open to a call.
    3. If they agree, agree on a time for the call.
    4. Hold the call and explore how you can work together.

  • Collaboration discussion steps:

    1. Use email templates to reach out when relevant.
    2. Start a discussion about collaboration opportunities.
    3. Frame it as finding mutual benefit (a win for both sides).
    4. Ask directly: "How can we work together?"
    5. Identify specific collaboration possibilities during the discussion.

Full Cold-Outreach Templates

3 min

Cold outreach means messaging someone who does not know you yet.

Template: trial offer for paid advertising on blogs, sites, channels, and events.

I recently discovered your [blog/site/channel/event] and am interested in advertising my e-commerce store through your platform. Our approach to partnerships typically begins with a complimentary seven-day trial period. During this week, I monitor how the ads perform and what traffic they generate. Based on these results, I decide whether to proceed with a formal advertising contract. I have run this trial approach with five or six different properties and moved forward with contracts for most of them. You can visit my store at [yourwebsite.com]. Would this trial period align with how you prefer to work with new advertisers? If so, we can get started.

Comment-Based Openers

1 min
  • Expect that most influencers will not accept a proposal from the first direct message. Plan to contact a large list, because a low response rate (few replies) is normal.
  • Comment-based procedure:
    1. Send an email to the creator if an email address is listed.
    2. Send a follow-up DM on Instagram or TikTok.
    3. If there is no email or DM option, comment on the creator's most recent video with a short collaboration message.
    4. Be among the first 10 to 15 commenters. Creators tend to reply to the first comments and to new messages.
  • Comment opener template:
Check your DM, let's work together! (Alternative openers: 'Collaboration?' or 'Do you take sponsors now?')
  • Post this opener as a comment on the creator's newest video so it is among the first comments.
  • Reply template for a creator who commented on your post:
Thanks so much for commenting! We would love to send you [PRODUCT] for free if you'd like to promote it on your socials. Reply here if you're interested and I'll send you the details.

Offer Messages: Gifting, Affiliate, and Podcasts

1 min
  • Gifting template (product under a value cap, meaning a set price limit, or a specific product):
Hey there, I'm a big fan of your videos. I'm writing on behalf of my company, [brand name]. Should you be open to it, we'd like to ship you [a product under $800 in value, or the specific product I want featured]. In return, you'd produce a video, publish it on your YouTube channel, and give us a shout-out saying you got the item from [brand name] and that [brand name] is a strong place to shop for anyone in your audience who wants [product].
  • Affiliate template (commission-based, no upfront cost to the creator). An affiliate earns a commission (a share of each sale) and pays nothing upfront:
Hello, I'm reaching out on behalf of [brand name]. If it interests you, we run an affiliate program: you share a link to our shop and collect [5% or 7%] of the sale for each purchase that comes through it. Payment happens only when clicks convert into orders, so trying it costs you nothing.
  • Podcast pitch: pitch with specific episode ideas that match what the audience cares about, such as market trends or a new brand. Offer to appear as a guest (a speaker invited onto someone else's show). Each accepted appearance sends traffic (visitors) to your store.
  • Influencer outreach agents (people you pay to do outreach for you) are inexpensive to hire. More outreach generally produces more promotions, more posts, and more sales. Even if the owner is better at picking talent, outreach volume still drives deals.

Existing Customers and Experts

1 min
  • Outreach template to an existing customer who has an audience. Adapt the bracketed placeholders before sending:
Hey [First name], we just realized you are one of our customers, so it is great to see you here. I noticed you have an audience of [audience description, e.g. surfing followers on Instagram]. If you had a great experience with us, we would love to explore whether we could work together to talk about our products with your audience. It is a simple conversation, and [tool or channel name] can help us work out the details. Would you be open to a quick chat?
  • Expert quote request template. An expert quote is a short statement from an expert, used in your article:
Hi [Expert name], I'm writing a post on [topic] for my audience, and your work on [area of expertise] is why I'd like your input. Would you be willing to send a short quote of two to three sentences answering this question: [specific question]? I'll link back to your site, credit you by name, and share the published post with my audience. Thanks for considering it, [Your name], [Your business name]
  • Expert follow-up template after the roundup post (an article that collects quotes from several experts) is published:
Hi [Expert name], the roundup post on [topic] is now live at [link], and your quote is included. If you're happy with how it looks, I'd really appreciate you sharing it with your audience. Thanks again for your time, [Your name], [Your business name]

Terms and Timing of the Deal Conversation

1 min
  • Do not negotiate terms in the first outreach message. Start the terms talk only after the influencer has replied and shown real interest. Terms include compensation (pay), content, timelines, and contract details.
  • State the core terms in the first message: deliverables (what the creator must produce), timeline, and whether the campaign is paid. Compensation can wait for the negotiation stage. Everything else should be open from the start. Creators receive many emails daily and avoid vague messages that need several exchanges.
  • Both rules apply together: name the deliverables, timeline, and paid status in the first message. Hold the compensation negotiation until the creator has replied.
  • Case: a technology brand launched a wireless mechanical keyboard and mouse set in a special colour edition. It wanted creators to promote it as a social activation.
    • The agency's casting producer (the person who finds and books creators) emailed a creator with the product link, a description of the campaign, and a request for the creator's content ideas.
    • The offer listed the deliverables: 1 TikTok post with a paid media push (paid ads that boost the post). It also included a 6-month usage term (how long the brand may reuse the content) across the brand's channels, with worldwide territory and all digital formats.
    • The timeline: product and content brief sent as soon as possible. First edit on 4 Nov (pending product arrival). Client feedback on 5 Nov. Content live on 9 Nov.
    • Because the email was complete and clearly a legitimate inquiry, the creator responded immediately. Rates were negotiated afterwards.
    • Knowing the brand beforehand helped but was not required.

Follow-Up Sequence and Timing

1 min
  1. Send the first outreach mail introducing yourself, your brand, and the niche. Ask whether the creator is interested.
  2. If the creator replies positively, send the second mail with the collaboration brief and ask for their media kit.
  3. If there is no reply after 3 days, send the first follow-up.
  4. If there is still no reply 2 days after the first follow-up, send the second follow-up.
  5. The sequence has no step after the second follow-up.
  • Timing: wait 3 days after the first outreach mail before the first follow-up. Then wait 2 days after the first follow-up before the second follow-up.
  • Keep the same thread (the chain of messages in one conversation) so the creator sees the earlier messages.

Response-Rate Expectations

1 min

Response rate means the share of people who answer your message. Cold outreach means contacting someone who does not know you yet. A paid placement is a sponsored spot, such as a post on a blog, site, channel or at an event. A trial offer is a small first deal to test whether a partnership works. A funnel is the chain of steps from first email to final deal.

Measure Value Source When it applies
Typical response rate for influencer outreach 2% to 3% General planning; contact volume must be high
Replies from a list of 100 contacts About 10 reply; 3 to 4 agree to your terms Prospecting session with 100 influencers
Outreach emails to meetings and partnerships About 1,000 emails lead to about 8 meetings and 1 profitable partnership Full funnel from email to profitable deal
Accepted paid influencer offers (cold, general) From 30 influencers contacted, expect 1 to 2 to accept; plan for rejection as the baseline Baseline for cold influencer outreach
Trial-offer method for paid advertising Works 9 out of 10 times Standard trial offer method for paid placements (blog, site, channel, event)
  • The numbers in the table measure different steps: replies, agreements, meetings and accepted offers. Read each number against its own step.
  • A careful research session takes about two to three hours. A research session is one block of time spent finding and checking creators.
  • Most creators you contact early will not reply. Expect this. The first 20 outreach messages may bring few or no replies. Keep building your list. Do not judge the campaign by the first few contacts.
  • Early outreach to creators was manual. The team searched Instagram by category and sent individual emails with short, specific subject lines. The subject line is the email's title. Reply rates were higher in the early years. They dropped as competition for creators grew.

Volume and Split Testing

1 min
  • Split-test your outreach templates. A template is a ready-made email you reuse. A template that works in one market may fail in another. A market is the group of buyers you sell to.
  • Keep the first email short. Aim for any positive reply, such as a request for more information or a yes.
  • Contact a large list when prospecting. Prospecting means searching for and contacting new possible partners. Low response rates are normal, and volume drives results.
  • Each message should contain one clear call to action. The call to action is the one thing you ask the reader to do. Split tests should change one element at a time.

Affiliate and ambassador programs

Section 13.13 · 13 of 21 in this chapter · 12 min read

In short

  • Find partners who already review or sell products like yours, using Google searches and Facebook groups.
  • Most partners start at 5 to 10 percent of each sale. Newer stores get fewer partners at first.
  • Give every partner a unique link with a tag, and state the cookie duration (how long the credit lasts).
  • Early on, a link alone brings few sales. Offer more, such as a free product, or pay for ads.
  • Use the ready-made templates for outreach and Facebook posts. Always list the commission, cookie duration and tracking.

Recruiting Affiliates

1 min

An affiliate is a partner who promotes your product and earns a cut of each sale they bring.

Where to Find Affiliates

1 min
  • Start with your Dream 100 list (your list of ideal prospects, the best-fit people and sites to contact). Look for possible affiliates there.
  • Search Google directly for more affiliates in your market.
  • Check whether each candidate fits your market and your products.
  • Reach out by email (using templates) or by phone call.
  • Present your affiliate program offer.
  • Affiliate partners sometimes run offers as banner ads (image ads on a website). You can find them during Dream 100 outreach or through searches on forums and Google.
  • A prospect who already runs affiliate deals on a site, YouTube channel or other platform can join your affiliate program.
  • They earn a commission (the cut paid per sale) on each sale.
  • They gain new offers to share with their audience.
  • Use an affiliate or commission plan to reward influencers and buyers who promote the product.
  • Give them a referral code (a code that shows who sent the buyer). This keeps fans engaged after the first purchase.
  • Wholesale accounts (businesses that buy in bulk), such as hotels, cafes and shops, can share the product with their own followers.
  • This is a free channel for reaching buyers.
  • Ask each new wholesale partner to promote the product.
  • Ask each new wholesale partner to let you show their logo on the website.

Finding Content Creators via Google

3 min

Principle

  • Affiliate content creators make material for the research stage, when buyers are still gathering information. Examples: reviews, how-to guides and top-10 lists.
  • They do this instead of targeting commercial-intent keywords (search words from people ready to buy).
  • Reason: substantial content performs better with research-phase audiences.

Search procedure

  1. Search Google using research-stage keywords: "best [product type]" and "top 10 [product type]". This finds content creators who review or compare products in your category.
  2. Look at the results for review and comparison websites.
  3. Click through to these sites to see which products they recommend and whether they use affiliate links.

Mapping the full landscape

  • Test several search variations on Google, such as "best [product]", "top 10 [product]" and related modifiers (extra words that narrow a search).

  • This finds all affiliate review and comparison sites in your product category and geographic market.

  • The result is the full landscape (the full set of possible partners).

  • To map the landscape, search with these variations:

    1. "best [product type]"
    2. "top 10 [product type]"
    3. "[product type] reviews"
    4. "[product type] comparison"

    For price-sensitive categories (categories where buyers care a lot about price), also search "best [product type] under $[price]" (e.g., "best headphones under $200"). Review the first 2-3 pages of results for each search. This finds all major affiliate publishers (site owners who publish reviews) in your niche (a small, focused market).

Evaluating candidates

  • Look for reviews that compare many products (typically 7 or more), with detailed feature breakdowns, score ratings and pros/cons for each product.
  • High-effort, comprehensive content shows a serious publisher worth approaching.
  • Thin comparison pages are lower priority.
  • Major media publications (for example New York Times or PC Magazine) often rank in "best [product]" searches.
  • They usually do not show visible affiliate offers, because of their editorial policies (their internal rules for reviews).
  • Focus outreach on independent review sites and content creators where you can confirm active affiliate links. Skip large publishers.

Confirming an active affiliate link

  1. Hover your mouse over the "See Price" button or product link. Read the URL (web address) shown in the bottom-left corner of the browser.
  2. If the URL is unclear, right-click the link and select "Inspect" or "Inspect Element" (a browser tool that shows a page's code).
  3. In the HTML code (the code that builds the page), locate the href= attribute containing the full destination URL. The href= part holds the link address.
  4. Look for an affiliate tag parameter in the URL (e.g., "tag=rtings" for Amazon, or a similar pattern for other platforms).
  5. If a tag parameter is present, the link is active. If the URL ends at the product ID (the product's code number) with no additional parameters, it is not an affiliate link.
  • Affiliate links contain a tag parameter in the URL. The tag names the affiliate partner and tracks commissions.
  • Amazon affiliate links use the format tag=storeidentifier.
  • Links without this parameter are not affiliate links.
  • In Amazon affiliate links, the text after tag= is the affiliate's identifier.
  • That identifier activates the affiliate commission tracking system (the system that records sales from each link).

Outreach template: message to a potential affiliate

Hi [Name],

I came across your [article title/topic] and was impressed by your thorough analysis of [number] products in this category.

I noticed you're currently promoting products through [their affiliate program, e.g., Amazon Associates]. I sell [product category or specific products from their review], and I can offer you commission rates that are competitive with or better than what you're earning through [their current program].

[Add one of these based on your situation:]
- I carry the exact same products you reviewed.
- We offer similar high-quality products that fit perfectly with your audience.
- Our products align with the criteria you outlined in your review.

Would you be open to discussing a partnership? I'm happy to share details about commission rates, cookie duration, and partner support.

Looking forward to connecting.

[Your Name]
  • When contacting a content creator, propose a partnership. Mention that you sell the same or comparable products.
  • Offer rates that may be better than or competitive with their current Amazon or other program rates.

Negotiation

  • After you pitch a potential affiliate, expect negotiation (talks about the terms).
  • They may accept your terms, ask for a change to the commission or the cookie duration (how many days after a click the partner still gets credit for a sale), or decline.
  • Stay professional whatever the outcome.
  • A rejection today can become a partnership later.

Recruiting in Facebook Groups

1 min

Procedure

  1. Go to Facebook and search for groups focused on affiliate marketing.
  2. Join established affiliate-marketing communities that allow merchant recruitment posts (posts where a seller looks for partners).
  3. Post your affiliate offer in the group.
  4. Reply to interested affiliates who comment or send direct messages (private messages).
  5. Share your affiliate program details with qualified partners.

Groups (as listed in the source, 2021-22; member counts may be outdated)

Facebook Group Members Purpose
Affiliate Marketing 45,000+ General affiliate recruitment and promotion
I Am Affiliate 6,500+ Work-at-home and digital advertising community
Affiliate Niche Builders Active Niche-specific affiliate partnerships
Affiliate Pro Community Active Professional affiliate network
  • Before posting, check the group's pinned posts (posts stuck to the top) or description.
  • They show whether merchant recruitment posts are allowed.
  • Some groups limit how often you can post, require a specific format or ban direct links.
  • Breaking these rules can get your post removed or get you removed from the group.

Post template

Hi everyone,

We're recruiting affiliates to promote [product category] to their audiences.

**Affiliate offer details:**
- Product category: [Specific type, e.g., over-ear headphones, fitness trackers]
- Price point: $[price range]
- Commission: [X]% per sale
- Cookie duration: [X] days
- Tracking platform: [Affiliate software name, e.g., Refersion, Impact]

If you think your audience would be interested, send me a DM and I'll get you all the details to get started.

Thanks!
  • When posting an affiliate offer in a Facebook group, include these items:
    • The product type or vertical (the market category)
    • The retail selling price (the price customers pay)
    • The commission rate you offer
    • The cookie duration and tracking system details
    • a clear call-to-action (a direct request, such as "message me") asking interested affiliates to message you for program information

Affiliates Who Buy Traffic

1 min
  • Some affiliates buy their own paid traffic (visitors bought with ads).
  • They treat the program as a business.
  • They judge a program by one thing: whether the commission leaves them profit on offers that convert (turn visitors into buyers).
  • They do not judge it by the size of the catalog (your product list).

Commission Rates and Cookies

1 min

1 min

  • 5-10% (2020) and 5-7% (2023) fit standard programs.
  • 8-10% plus a free product (2022) fits recruiting through an app, where a link alone brings nothing.
  • Up to 30% (2023) fits only a case where an affiliate is won with a strong offer.
  • 10-15% fits a revenue-based ambassador setup (a fan who promotes the brand and is paid a share of sales).
  • Source A's 2020 and 2022 advice is older.

Tiered Rates by Partner Status

1 min
  • Affiliate commission structure: 6% for new affiliate partners.
  • 7% for long-term partners who have consistently performed.
  • Long-term influencer partners earn passive revenue (money that keeps coming in without new work).
  • Their articles written years ago keep earning through affiliate commissions and ad placements.
  • Paying 7% to experienced partners versus 6% to newcomers rewards the compounding value (value that grows over time) of evergreen content (articles that stay useful and keep earning for years).

Setting the Rate by Promotion

1 min
  • Set the commission based on how the affiliate promotes you. Examples: email to their list, sidebar placements (ads in a side column), articles, social media or a footer link (a link at the bottom of a page).
  • The more prominent the promotion, the higher the justified rate.

Cookie Duration

1 min
  • Cookie duration is stated in every offer, e.g., "Cookie duration: [X] days".
  • It must be included in outreach and group posts.

Timing

1 min
  • Affiliate programs work best once a store has steady sales and history.
  • Early affiliate traffic is usually a small share of total traffic.
  • Expect few positive replies from bloggers and YouTube creators until the store has an established sales history.
  • Affiliates are hard to win early, because a new store has no reputation.
  • Paid sponsored placements (paid posts or ads on relevant sites, such as a blog post or sidebar image) tend to get results faster at the start.

Payment Logic

1 min
  • The store pays nothing until link clicks turn into sales.

Affiliate Software and Setup

1 min

Refersion

1 min
  • Refersion is affiliate software. It integrates directly with Shopify (online store software) and can run affiliate and influencer programs.
  • It has been used successfully for this purpose.
  • Affiliates log into Refersion.
  • They browse all available banners in its Creatives section (ready-made ad images).
  • They can select any banner they want at any time.
  • They can test different versions to see which drives more clicks.
  • They do not need to request new assets or wait for custom creation.

Referson / Shopify app

1 min
  • Referson is an app on Shopify that runs an affiliate program.
  • You choose the commission percentage and the cookie duration.
  • It works well when your margins (the profit left after costs) allow commissions.

Shopify Collabs and other platforms

1 min
  • Track affiliate and brand-ambassador deals with Shopify Collabs (Shopify's tool for working with creators) or another affiliate platform.

Shopify listing partners (complementary stores)

1 min
  • Complementary stores (shops that sell products that go with yours, not direct rivals) can list your product for you.
  • You do not need to provide any product data.
  • You fulfill (ship) the order through your supplier.
  • You pay them an affiliate-style cut.
  • This works best for products where you can afford an affiliate fee.

Tracking links and tags

1 min
  • Use the tag parameter in affiliate URLs to identify the partner and track commissions.
  • See "Finding Content Creators via Google" above for examples.

Banners and Creatives

1 min
  • Partners with banner ads place image ads across website headers (the top of a page), sidebars or other spots.
  • Create banner ads either tied to specific products or as general business-wide promotions.
  • General business banners work well for affiliate placements.
  • Partners earn a commission whenever their visitors buy anything from you.
  • You create and provide all banner creatives.
  • Affiliates do not create banners for themselves.
  • They expect ready-made, professional options they can use right away on their sites.
  • Affiliates can choose and test banners in the Refersion Creatives section at any time, without requesting new assets.

Tiers and Brand Ambassadors

1 min

Affiliate and Ambassador Commissions

1 min
  • Tiers are levels of partner status, such as Gold and Platinum.
  • Affiliate or brand-ambassador deals pay about 10% to 15% of revenue (total money from sales) from each sale generated through a unique link.
  • Track these with Shopify Collabs or another affiliate platform.
  • Formula: Commission = 15% of the revenue from orders placed through the influencer's link.
  • Example: 100K followers with 2% buying gives 2,000 buyers whose purchases form the revenue base.

Upgrading Tiers

1 min
  • Upgrade affiliates from a Gold to a Platinum ambassador tier once they reach a sales threshold (a set sales target).
  • The upgrade motivates them to bring more sales.

Micro-Influencer Affiliate Program

1 min
  • A micro-influencer is a creator with a small but active audience.
  1. Find small influencers who are active on TikTok or Instagram.
  2. Gift them one product.
  3. They promote it on their platforms.
  4. They receive a commission for every sale made through their channel.

Referral Programs

1 min
  • A referral program lets existing customers refer family and friends.
  • Both the referrer and the friend get a discount, such as a set percentage off.
  • A referral program works better early on than recruiting affiliates.
  • Referral programs can give customers ready-made social posts or emails to share with friends and followers.
  • This turns existing customers into a channel (a route for reaching buyers) that spreads the offer.

Brand Ambassadors and Loyalty

1 min
  • A hourglass funnel (a sales path shaped like an hourglass) turns new buyers into brand ambassadors (fans who recommend the store).
  • Their referrals bring higher quality traffic (better visitors) back to the top of the funnel.
  • Those visitors convert (turn into buyers), which brings more customers.
  • Use an affiliate or commission plan to reward influencers and buyers who promote the product with a referral code.
  • This keeps fans engaged after the first purchase.

Affiliate Apps and Incentives

1 min
  • An affiliate app only works when you recruit influencers or sites with a strong offer.
  • A strong offer might be a free product and a commission around 8 to 10 percent.
  • A link alone generates no sales.

Affiliate Partnerships with Complementary Brands

1 min
  • Reach out to magazines, blogs and authority websites (sites your market trusts) that you already have a relationship with.
  • Invite them to become affiliates.
  • Ask whether they would write a review as an affiliate.
  • Affiliate partners can be asked to add your product to their recommended product list.
  • This is a separate step from the review request.
  • Find indirect competitors (businesses that sell something related, not the same product) in your niche. Example: drum lessons if you sell drum kits.
  1. Identify indirect competition products related to your niche.
  2. Search Google for "best [related product/service]."
  3. Visit top results and look for "Partner Program," "Affiliate Program," or a similar link in the footer.
  4. Email them directly with your store information and ask about affiliate terms.
  5. Negotiate the commission structure (fixed dollar or percentage per sale).
  6. Get a unique affiliate link and promote it on relevant product pages.

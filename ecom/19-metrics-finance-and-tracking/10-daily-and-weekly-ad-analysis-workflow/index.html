Playbook › 19. Metrics, finance and tracking

Daily and weekly ad analysis workflow

Section 19.10 · 10 of 23 in this chapter · 32 min read

In short

  • Check money spent and profit first. Then find where people stop: noticing the ad, clicking, wanting it, or buying.
  • Judge an ad by its ROAS (money back for each unit spent) against break-even ROAS. Above break-even means profit.
  • Change one thing at a time, and test for at least 3 days before deciding.
  • Do not add budget to ads that Meta is not funding. Find out why first.
  • Judge your ads over seven days or more, never by one day.
More key points
  • Daily numbers swing up and down. This is normal, so do not panic over one slow day.
  • Check results by country, age and gender before you change anything.
  • Keep a written record of every change, so you know what caused a result.

Reporting layers: snapshot and funnel

1 min
  • Split the ad view into two column groups: snapshot and funnel.
  • Snapshot metrics show how performance looks at one point in time.
  • Funnel metrics show how performance changes as viewers move from awareness (noticing the ad) to action (buying). The funnel is the path from first seeing the ad to buying.
  • Snapshot columns:
    • Ad set name (the group of ads that share one budget and audience)
    • Last significant edit
    • Daily budget
    • Amount spent
    • Result for the chosen objective (the goal you picked, such as purchases)
    • Cost per result
  • Funnel columns:
    • Impressions (how many times the ad was shown)
    • CPM (cost per 1,000 impressions)
    • 3-second video plays
    • Hook rate (share of impressions that reach 3 seconds of video)
    • Hold rate (share of impressions that keep watching)
    • Unique CTR (share of people who click, counting each person once)
    • Unique clicks
    • Cost per click
    • Adds to cart
    • Purchases
    • Conversion rate (share of visitors who buy)
    • ROAS (return on ad spend: money back for each unit spent)
  • Together, these columns show where the funnel wins or loses.
  • Ad analysis builds a picture from inference (reasoning from clues), not from hard rules.
  • Combine several metrics. Reason from the evidence instead of judging by one number.
  • Low spend and early data make single metrics especially unreliable.
  • ROAS is not the main goal number (north-star metric) for ad analysis on its own.
  • ROAS adds up return across all user actions. It is a major driver of overall profitability.
  • Purchases and ROAS matter most.
  • Break-even ROAS is the ROAS where you neither make nor lose money.
  • Above break-even ROAS is profitable. Below it is not.
  • Soft metrics (hook rate, hold rate, CTR, which is click-through rate: the share of viewers who click) are improved only when bottom-of-funnel sales are already profitable.
  • Bottom-of-funnel sales are the last steps before buying, such as cart and purchase.
  • Soft metrics are also worked on when weak sales need their cause found.

Daily snapshot check

2 min
  • Review profit and loss (money in minus money out) and ad performance first thing each morning. Do this before working through the task list.
  • Knowing spend and profit before creative work starts keeps daily decisions tied to real numbers.
  • Keep a reference document with ad metric guidance and annotated example images (pictures with notes) for the regular ad review.
  • Turn any review action from that document directly into a task so it is not lost.
  • Each day, review for each ad: cost per add to cart, CTR, CPC, CPM and the engagement rate ranking.
  • Cut losers early when soft metrics stay weak.
  • Keep an ad running only if its signals improve from day to day.
  • Amount spent is the strongest signal of where the platform sees opportunity. Read it before judging.
  • Do not force budget into ads the algorithm is not funding. The algorithm is Meta's system that picks who sees each ad. Diagnose why these ads are not getting spend.
  • Efficiency without scale is a vanity result (a number that looks impressive but is small). For example, 10x ROAS on $5 of spend.
  • Look for ads that are both efficient and funded.

Metrics and targets

2 min
  • ROAS = Revenue / Ad spend. Example: 14,580 / 456 = 31.97, while the report showed a ROAS of 31.
  • Confirm your sheet uses the same definition and rounding (how numbers are rounded) before setting targets.
  • A ROAS target of 20x means revenue should be 20 times the ad spend.
  • Blended ROAS (ROAS across all sales, not one product or ad) is the right level for judging a campaign. Judge it together with product profitability.
  • Low ROAS on a product with few clicks is a sample-size problem (too little data to judge). It is not a verdict.
  • Track ROAS and revenue against separate targets, because each can be missed independently.
  • If ROAS is above target but revenue is below, the account is efficient but lacks volume. Bid more aggressively and accept lower ROAS to gain sales.
  • If ROAS falls, find the cause by checking which campaigns or ad sets lost efficiency.
  • Compare each campaign's actual cost per sale, ROAS and cost per click with the calculated target values before changing anything.
  • If the campaign performs below target, revise the bid, the price or the conversion rate.
  • If it has room below target, you may be too cautious and can bid more aggressively.
  • A campaign can be above breakeven and still have room to improve.
  • If CPA (cost per acquisition, here the cost per purchase) is too high relative to the target, work on the funnel: ad creative, average order value (average money per order) and landing page (the page people arrive on).
  • When CAC (customer acquisition cost: what it costs to win one customer) looks poor, the ad with the highest spend is still the main signal of what works. It should guide the next step.
  • Do not judge the whole account by one weak metric.
  • For an awareness campaign (a campaign to get people to notice you) without conversion tracking yet (no sales measurement set up), judge performance on video views and cost per view.
  • Do not use ROAS or revenue for this, because they cannot be measured yet.
  • Set a cost-per-view KPI, such as 0.05 USD, to check whether the cost of views stays in line.

Ad analysis workflow, in order

1 min
  1. Review snapshot metrics first (daily budget, total spend, results, cost per result) to see if the ad is profitable.
  2. Use funnel metrics to pinpoint where the problem is (attention, interest, desire or action stage).
  3. Use the AIDA framework (also written ADA in this text): Attention, Interest, Desire, Action. Check hook rate (attention), CTR (interest), conversion rate (desire), and ROAS (action).
  4. If a weak area is identified, do a creative review against the checklist.
  5. Form a hypothesis (a clear guess to test) about improvement.
  6. Create a test ad with a single change, keeping the control (the original ad, unchanged, for comparison).
  7. Run it for 3 days minimum.
  8. Evaluate lift (the gain compared with the control).

Protocol for comparing ad sets

1 min
  1. Compare interests against each other using funnel metrics, starting at the top of the funnel (hook rate, which measures attention).
  2. Move down to interest (CTR), then desire (add to cart, conversion rate) and action (ROAS).
  3. Note where each ad set falls behind. Focus improvement effort on the weakest stage.

Meta video analysis, in order

1 min
  1. Spend distribution: rank ads by amount spent. Compute each share of total, utilisation against budget, spend per day and delivery status.
  • Measure how concentrated spend is in the top 3 ads.
  • Check whether new ads are ramping (gaining spend) or starved (getting none).
  1. Delivery status: count ads by status and find the share of spend on Active ads.
  • Explain why Learning Limited ads are stuck: audience too small, budget too low, or creative underperforming.
  1. Ad age: bucket ads (group them by age) and compare performance by bucket.
  2. Efficiency: compare ROAS and CPA against targets, and segment them.
  3. Funnel: build the cost cascade (the cost at each step, from CPM to cost per purchase) and the stage conversion rates. Find the weakest stage per ad.

  7. Give next-best-dollar and next-best-week recommendations.
  8. Write the executive summary and an ad-by-ad scorecard.

Stages

1 min
  • Read the funnel in three stages: attention (hook rate on the first three seconds), interest (click-through rate, the share who click), and desire (conversion rate, the share who buy).
  • A weak stage points to where the funnel breaks down.
  • AIDA maps the funnel in these steps:
    • Attention = hook rate
    • Interest = click-through rate
    • Desire and Action = conversion rate (add to cart and purchase)
    • Overall result = ROAS
  • Check the stages in order to find the weakest point.
  • A high hook rate only shows that viewers pay attention. It does not prove that the traffic will buy.
  • A lower hook rate can still convert well if the audience is the right one.
  • So always read hook rate together with CTR and conversion rate.
  • Judge a product first by hook rate (also called look rate) and CTR on ads.
  • If both are low, change the creatives (the ad visuals and wording) or accept that the product does not attract interest.
  • Ad performance is judged first by CTR, CPM and CPC.
  • Only if these look healthy should the store and funnel be analysed for weak points.

Metric combinations

1 min
  • Strong attention + interest + desire = strong ROAS.
  • Strong attention + interest + weak desire = normal ROAS.
  • Suspect an ad-landing-page mismatch (the page does not match what the ad promised), technical problems, high shipping costs, low trust or high price.
  • Weak attention + good interest + good desire = test new hooks (the first seconds of the ad).
  • The problem is not painful enough.
  • Strong attention + weak interest + good desire = test messaging in the middle of the ad, or refine targeting (narrow down who sees the ad).
  • CTR and CPC good, but people rarely add to cart or conversion rate is very low.
  • The ad is working. The store or landing page is the problem.
  • The fix is store messaging, a better offer, or a more educational page. It is not a new ad.
  • Hook rate and CTR good, but sales missing: the problem is usually the store.
  • Keep working on the store before dropping the product.
  • CTR and CPC look good, but sales are missing: the problem is usually the store, not the ad.
  • Fix the website and offer first. Then judge the product by ROAS.
  • Strong CTR but no sales: the offer is usually the problem.
  • Change the offer before the ads are changed.
  • On TikTok, a CPM of about $5 to $7 is acceptable.
  • If CPM is fine but CTR is low, the problem sits at the creative level.
  • Use CPM, CPC and CTR together to find which part of the funnel fails.

Creative and iteration

1 min
  • When iterating ads (making changes and testing again): if an ad runs acceptably with one or two improvable elements, replace only those specific elements.
  • If it performs poorly overall, test a completely new concept.
  • In video retention (how long people keep watching):
    • A large drop before the 25% mark points to a hook problem.
    • A large drop between 25% and 50% points to weak middle content.
    • A large drop between 50% and 75% points to a problem in the pitch or offer.
    • Strong retention through 75% combined with low completion (few people watch to the end) points to a weak call to action.

Sessions and sales

1 min
  • Read sessions (visits to your store) and sales for the same date range together.
  • If sessions rise sharply (for example from about 50 to 100-120 per day) while sales fall to zero, the traffic or the page has a problem.
  • The rise in sessions is not a success signal.
  • Check whether certain weekdays consistently convert worse for the store before judging a test by one day.
  • One operator found Wednesdays were weak for revenue. So tests should run across several days.

Diagnosing the primary constraint

1 min

Mindset

1 min
  • Identify the one primary constraint (the single thing blocking growth).
  • More budget does not fix a creative constraint, and more creative does not fix a funnel constraint.
  • Treat creative as the main lever (the strongest thing you control) after Andromeda (the name used in this text for Meta's ad system). Let the platform handle targeting, bidding and budget allocation.
  • Diagnose the one constraint that blocks scale (growing spend) and focus there. The others are secondary.
  • More creative does not fix a funnel constraint, and more budget does not fix a creative constraint.
  • Choose the constraint from the symptoms below.

Data windows

1 min

Ads have three levels.

  • A campaign is one ad project with its own budget.
  • An ad set is a group inside a campaign that shares one audience.
  • An ad is one picture or video people see. The picture or video itself is the creative.

A data window is the number of days you look at together.

Kill timing and cutting waste

1 min

To kill an ad means to switch it off. Soft metrics are early signs, such as clicks and watch time.

  • When a campaign is unprofitable, check the ad set and ad level first.
  • One audience or creative can drag down the whole campaign. Kill that ad set or creative.
  • Cut losers early when soft metrics stay weak.
  • Keep an ad running only if its signals improve from day to day.
  • Ad fatigue means people have seen an ad so often that results drop. Check ad age (how many days an ad has run) as the trigger for review (see Diagnosing the primary constraint).
  • Video retention (how long viewers keep watching) drop-offs (the points where they leave) show where to fix or cut.
  • After auditing (checking in detail) two weeks of ad spend, about 35 percent was bloat (waste) that did not lead to sales.
  • Cutting the bloat and reinvesting into the remaining 65 percent that performed is a practical way to reduce waste.
  • Remove a country from targeting (choosing who sees the ads) only after it has spent enough without results.
  • Test the interests (topics the audience likes) that produced sales.
  • Look for places that waste money, and cut them.
  • Look for places that work, and spend more there.

Review cadence

Country, age and gender breakdowns

3 min

A breakdown splits results into groups, such as gender, age or country. Ads Manager is Meta's ad dashboard.

Breakdown procedure after changing the store and pausing ads

  1. Return to Ads Manager and use Breakdown on each ad.
  2. Break down by gender.
  3. Break down by age. Compare ranges such as 18-34, 45+ and 65+, and track results in a spreadsheet.
  4. Break down by country when targeting several countries.
  5. Break down by placement (where on the platform the ad shows).
  6. Focus budget on the best-performing gender, age and countries. Relaunch new ad sets with those settings.

Country checks

  • Verify that targeted countries actually receive spend.
  • If one country gets no visits despite being set up, reduce or test the countries separately.
  • When targeting several countries, a country with high spend and low CTR or high CPM is a sign to drop or pause it.
  • The US in Q4 (October to December) is one example where CPM can be high.
  • Countries with cheap sales (for example, one sale for 3 USD of spend) can be given priority.
  • If a store's reported CPC is high, but CPM is low and CTR is high, one country is usually driving the cost.
  • A per-country breakdown reveals which one is inflating the CPC.

Checking where spend goes after 4 to 5 days:

  1. Open the ad account.
  2. Use the breakdown button at the top right to see spend by country.
  3. If a country has high spend, high CPM and no sales, exclude it from future testing and move budget to the other markets.

Find the best-converting country:

  1. Open the sessions report in your Shopify account. Shopify is the online store software. A session is one visit.
  2. Add filters for country. A filter shows only the rows you pick.
  3. Compare the conversion rate per country.
  4. Move budget to the countries that perform best. Start a new campaign that targets only the strongest country.
  • Case: a product test with a 200 USD ad budget produced only 2 sales.
  • The country report showed both sales came from Australia on only 7 USD spend. The rest of the budget went to the US and UK.
  • The recommendation was to start a new campaign targeting only Australia, because its conversion rate was good.
  • To compare countries after launching ads, break down store sessions by country in the Shopify analytics report (the store's stats page).
  • Add the columns you need. Then compare the conversion rate for each market to decide where to scale.
  • Budget followed the wrong market: most spend went to Australia while the UK had better sales.
  • In one test case, 20 spent on UK ads produced six sales. The UK deserved the larger share of budget.
  • Compare sales and conversion rate per market before deciding where to spend.

Age, gender and buyer data

  • Gender and age breakdowns show where to shift budget.
  • In the example, women aged 45 to 54 performed very strongly.
  • Women aged 25 to 34 had lower revenue and a weaker ROAS.
  • Men aged 45 to 54 reached a ROAS of 11.68, above target. That justified bidding more aggressively (offering more money to show the ad) on that segment (audience group).
  • Of 20 sales from a jewellery test, 18 came from the UK.
  • All buyers were 35 or older, with slightly more women than men.
  • Look at the country, age and gender of buyers to refine the next interest and audience choices.
  • Check sales data, including the customer's country, age and gender, after each spike (a sudden jump in sales) to decide the next target audience.
  • Sales by country can reveal an unexpected market.

Automated and scheduled reports

4 min

Report principles

  • A useful report for paid traffic (visitors you pay for with ads) shows the data for a decision within a few seconds.
  • The insight should stand out without scrolling through raw lists (unprocessed rows).
  • Reports that need manual effort to pull (download) and format (arrange) cost time every month.
  • Manual effort also slows down decisions.
  • A report should take about one second to get.
  • If building it means downloading data, pasting it into a spreadsheet (a table of numbers) and formatting it by hand, the setup is wrong.
  • For example, a one-hour manual report done four times a month costs four hours.
  • If it takes two hours each time, it can cost up to eight hours.
  • Build reports from high level to low level: account, then campaign, then ad group or ad, then device and other dimensions.
  • A dimension is a way to split data, such as device (phone, computer or tablet).
  • Each step narrows the problem. The optimiser (the person adjusting ads) knows where to act without digging through the account.
  • A good dashboard (a summary screen) shows what to manage and the overall health of campaigns, keywords (search words) and ads.
  • It works without a login to the ad platform.
  • Both native dashboards (built into the platform) and spreadsheet dashboards can refresh daily, weekly or even every hour.
  • AI can analyse ad data, predict trends and suggest adjustments.
  • The goal is to replace decisions based on gut feeling (guessing) when scaling profitably (growing spend while staying in profit).

Automated daily reporting (example)

  1. Every morning, pull the previous day's metrics from the ad account: ad spend, impressions, conversions (sales) and ROAS.
  2. Summarise them in plain language.
  3. Post the summary to a Slack channel (a group chat).
  4. At the same time, add the raw numbers to a master Google Sheet.
  5. Update the creative testing spreadsheet automatically.

This creates a live executive dashboard (a summary screen for managers) that updates itself without you reviewing data.

  • Zapier is a tool that links apps. It can automate tasks between them.
  • For example, it can push new Shopify orders into a spreadsheet every day.
  • It can potentially move TikTok data into a spreadsheet, but this has to be verified.

Google Sheets with Supermetrics

  • Supermetrics is an add-on (an extra tool) for Google Sheets.
  • It pulls ad data from platforms such as Google Ads, Bing Ads and Facebook Ads into a spreadsheet.
  • A dashboard can combine several platforms. For example, it can sum Google and Bing spend in one top-level cell (the main box on the sheet).
  • It is the recommended tool for building reporting dashboards in Google Sheets.

Account-level Supermetrics setup:

  1. Open Google Sheets and start the Supermetrics add-on.
  2. Select the AdWords (Google Ads) account and sync it (link it so the data updates).
  3. Start at the highest level and work downward.
  4. Set the date range to the last four months, including the current month.
  5. Add the key metrics: costs, clicks, conversions, conversion rate and revenue.
  6. Break the data down by month and show all-time values.
  7. Sort the output and insert it into the sheet.
  8. Add a custom metric (a number you calculate yourself): cost per view (cost divided by video views), when video campaigns run.

Ad-level drill-down for video campaigns

  1. Choose the last 30 days.
  2. Add cost and video views (times people watched the video).
  3. Split the rows by video title (or ad ID) to see each ad.
  4. Add a cost-per-view column, convert it to dollars, and apply conditional formatting (an automatic colour rule) that flags values greater than 0.05. Flagging means highlighting.
  5. Add device as a further dimension to see which devices overpay. For example, computers may show a higher cost per view than mobile.

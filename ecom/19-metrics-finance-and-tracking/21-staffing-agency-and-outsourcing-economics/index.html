Staffing, agency and outsourcing economics

Section 19.21 · 21 of 23 in this chapter · 12 min read

This section has 11 parts. Tap one to open it.

Financial reporting before any hire

1 min
  • Before you hire an agency, put proper financial reporting in place: ROAS, contribution margin by channel, fixed costs and incremental profit. Without this data you cannot align incentives or measure whether the agency adds value.
  • Establish one shared reporting tool or dashboard as the source of truth for all metrics. This prevents disagreements about performance and keeps both parties accountable to the same numbers.
  • Percentage of ad spend is a vanity metric, not a performance metric. It rewards dollars spent rather than business outcomes and can create misaligned incentives with your agency.

Hire cost against profit

2 min
  • Break-even revenue for a hire = monthly cost of the hire ÷ profit margin. Worked examples from the lesson: a $5/hour entry-level hire at 160 hours per month costs $800, and at a 20% margin the business needs $800 / 0.20 = $4,000 monthly revenue to cover it. A $15/hour US hire at 160 hours costs $2,400, so $2,400 / 0.20 = $12,000 monthly revenue is needed.

Hire cost comparison (160 hours per month, 20% profit margin assumed):

Entry-level, from another country $5 $800 $4,000 2 to 4 (about one order per week covers it)
Experienced, US-based $15 $2,400 $12,000 6 to 8 (or 2 to 3 per week; one per month for higher-ticket items)

Order counts assume order values in the range of the example.

The hire at $5/h frees 40 owner hours per month (10 per week); the hire at $15/h frees 80 hours per month (20 per week).

  • A hire that costs profit can still be the right decision when the owner's freed hours are worth more than the profit lost. Compare what the owner could do with 10 or 20 extra hours per week against the wage cost before judging a hire.
  • Budgeting determines when you can afford each hire, such as one, three or ten virtual assistants, a store manager or a PPC agency. Use your numbers to decide when to hire, not your hopes. **
  • Describe who manages the finances in 10 years, either an in-house finance team or an outsourced provider. Also consider which options the profit creates, such as developing your own products when you currently resell, or investing in assets like a warehouse or a network of warehouses. **

Agency fee math

3 min
  • Hiring an agency increases fixed costs and raises the break-even revenue threshold. If the agency does not generate incremental contribution margin equal to or exceeding its fee, you make less profit than if you managed the work yourself.
  • Example: with a 25% contribution margin, justifying a $10,000 monthly agency fee requires $40,000 in incremental revenue that month. This is break-even before considering other business benefits.
  • Spend-to-retainer ratio should be at least 1:7 (seven dollars of ad spend per one dollar of agency fee), ideally 1:10 or higher, for the agency to be profitable for your business.
  • The ideal spend level is $10,000 to $15,000 per month in ad spend for every $1,000 paid monthly to an agency, balancing efficiency with scale.
  • Standard agency compensation is about 5% of monthly ad spend, or 10% when the agency handles broader services beyond media buying, such as creative, strategy or reporting.
  • Agency fees must be counted in your return on ad spend. The agency has to raise your conversion rate and performance enough to cover its monthly fee, otherwise the fee eats into your profit. **
  • Evaluate agency performance over a typical period of 60 to 90 days. This gives both parties time to learn processes and results to emerge, while remaining short enough to make quick decisions.

Example: ROAS Agency Package B

  • ROAS Agency Package B costs $250 per month, has no set-up fee, and charges a 4% ad spend fee. The sources do not state whether the 4% is added on top of the $250 monthly fee, so confirm this with the agency contact before committing.
  • Estimated monthly cost of ROAS Agency Package B, if the fees are additive: $250 + (4% × monthly ad spend). Example: at $10,000 monthly ad spend, the fee is $400 and the total is $650 per month.

Deciding whether to hire a specialist

  1. Record your current monthly ad spend.
  2. Record your current ROAS and calculate your monthly revenue from it (revenue = ad spend × ROAS).
  3. Record your profit margin, or your cost of goods, to derive the margin.
  4. Calculate your current monthly profit after ad spend.
  5. Estimate the spend and ROAS a hired specialist would need to reach, add the agency fee, and compare the resulting profit with your current profit to decide whether hiring is profitable.
  • Monthly profit = revenue × profit margin − ad spend − agency fee. Revenue equals ad spend × ROAS, so a higher ROAS raises revenue at the same spend. Use this to test whether a proposed improvement in spend or ROAS covers the fee.
  • Starting point: monthly revenue of $7,000 at a 20% profit margin, which the lesson treats as about $400 profit per month after ad spend, or $4,800 per year. Scenario: a specialist doubles monthly ad spend from $1,000 to $2,000 and improves ROAS from 7 to 10 at the same margin, which gives $20,000 revenue instead of $7,000. Adding a $1,200 monthly service fee, the lesson states profit rises from $400 to $825 per month, roughly doubling it and freeing the owner's time for SEO, email marketing and conversion work.
  • Verification note: the $400 figure only reconciles if the margin is applied to revenue minus ad spend. Under the same formula the scenario gives $800 rather than $825, so recheck the numbers in your own model before relying on them. Also, 20% of $7,000 alone is $1,400, so the $400 figure implies the margin is applied after ad spend; confirm which definition you use.

Compensation and incentives

1 min
  • Base compensation plus incentives avoids a conflict of interest. If an agency is paid as a percentage of ad spend and you reduce spend to improve profitability, its income falls while its labour may stay the same or increase, which creates natural resistance to spending discipline.
  • Measure the outcomes that trigger payment, such as completed installations or delivered orders with no cancellation. A salesperson with fewer sales and more completed installations can earn more than one with many unfinished sales.

Virtual assistants: time tracking

1 min
  • Track and record how you spend your time every day for 30 consecutive days to establish a baseline and identify patterns in your work. **
  • Do not analyse time-tracking data until you have at least 30 days of tracking. Analysing after only one or two weeks is premature and will not reveal actual work patterns. **
  • Time-tracking analysis reveals activities that consume more time than you realised. Some may be unnecessary, and others may be reduced to free hours for higher-priority business activities. **
  • A team member, such as a virtual assistant, who spends much time every week on profit tracking but never reviews the results is wasting time and effort. Always check what time and team effort cost against what they return. **
  • Give the virtual assistant access to ad accounts, fulfilment costs and Shopify costs. Have them produce a monthly Excel sheet for accounting.

Virtual assistants: sourcing channels

1 min
  • Virtual Staff Finder is a matchmaking service for offshore assistants. It charges a one-time flat fee of about $400 to $500, delivers about three candidates matching your criteria, sends three more if none fit, and offers no trial period. You pay the hire directly, so it suits general skill sets better than very specific ones.
  • Freelance marketplaces such as Odesk, Elance and Freelancer, plus Craigslist and onlinejobs.ph for postings, give you a large pool and trial periods. Because you do the screening yourself, this path takes more time, and you should hire about three people to keep one or two.

Virtual assistants: hiring funnel

1 min
  1. Post a job ad that requires a specific detail, such as a unique question or a local reference, that every applicant must include in their title, email or first message.
  2. Reject everyone who misses the required detail without reading their resume.
  3. Require a timed, objective test, such as a free online grammar test, and reject those who skip it or do not send the result.
  4. Interview at least three candidates for each hire, in a tight time window.
  5. Hire and train in batches of two or three, then keep the winner and release the rest.
  • Use more than one hidden requirement in the ad. Applicants on large marketplaces are used to a single one and copy-paste their replies, while requiring extra effort filters out people who do not read messages carefully.
  • Grammar test thresholds used for Philippine-based staff: 70 or better for roles without phone or client contact, and 80 or better for roles with phone or client contact. The test takes about an hour.
  • Interview at least three candidates for every position, with two as an absolute minimum, so you can compare them. Do not hire the first acceptable person you meet.
  • Arriving late to an interview is a disqualifier. Schedule interviews in a tight block, about 15 minutes apart, so a missed slot does not waste a whole day.

Virtual assistants: checking comprehension

1 min
  • The yes-sir problem: in some cultures a yes can mean maybe or no, because people avoid disappointing you. Do not ask whether they understood, since they will almost always say yes.
  • To check comprehension, ask the candidate to repeat the task back in their own words and to explain exactly how they would do it. Later in the interview, ask about a detail you mentioned at the start to test attention.

Apprentices

1 min
  • Apprentice hiring example: 17 people applied after an ad that required a completed form and a short video. Four or five dropped out at that stage, so the founder never had to screen their resumes.
  • Recruit apprentices through communities of lifestyle entrepreneurs, forums and Facebook groups, not general job boards like Monster, whose users look for conventional office jobs.
  • Apprentice package: a starting stipend of $1,000 per month, with housing, food, laundry and daily necessities covered. The effective cost to the employer is about $1,500 per month, and pay rises later.
  • Arrange housing and daily life before a relocating apprentice arrives, and keep them close during the first phase so that training and the move are as easy as possible. The less friction for the apprentice, the more willing they are to relocate.
  • For apprentices, the final interview should focus on personal fit and whether you can work well together, since they are hired more for fit than for a specific skill set. Fit matters because an apprentice is an important, long-term hire.

Team cost in profit targets

2 min
  • Monthly profit must cover team salaries. For one owner, a $1,500 per month team manager required at least about $15,000 of profit per month to be affordable.
  • Starting point: a solo owner building a drop shipping business earns about $20,000 per month with no team, 80% COGS, and an allocation of 10% profit, 10% owner salary, 10% taxes, 30% staff and 40% operating expenses.
    1. Step 1: the owner hires a customer service VA at about $1,000 per month, which leaves about $200 of the $1,200 staff budget.
    2. Step 2: when revenue doubles to about $40,000 per month and the VA is overloaded, the owner adds an order processing VA at about $600 per month, bringing staff pay to $1,600 and leaving about $800 in budget. The owner stops answering customer and order questions and uses the time for marketing work.
    3. Step 3: the owner's maximum revenue capacity rises to about $50,000 with the target set to about $75,000. At about $50,000 per month a catalogue manager is hired at about $1,200 per month to fix outdated product information that was sending customer questions to the wrong place.
    4. Step 4: at about $75,000 per month with about $1,700 to $1,800 left in the staff budget, the owner hires an SEO person at about $800 per month and then a PPC person. PPC comes first because it pays back faster, while hiring an agency would cost more.
    5. Step 5: the owner trains a hire through the marketing modules and grows revenue to about $125,000 per month, with about $3,900 of unused staff budget. The owner adds a sales rep at about $1,000 per month and splits sales from customer service. Capacity is then raised again, with the target set to about $250,000.
    6. Step 6: with budget building up, the owner lowers the staff allocation to 20% and resumes taking profit and owner salary.
  • **
  • The max revenue capacity and target capacity figures in the Profit First planning sheet do not feed any formula. They only show the team which level the business is aiming for, so budget calculations must be done with the allocation percentages and staff pay alone. **

Reporting requirements for outside help

2 min
  • Send ad performance stats to the support team every 24 hours once ads are live, and keep doing so daily until told otherwise. Reporting is the most important ongoing obligation in the testing phase.
  • The first three days after launch are the most critical reporting period, so stats for Day 1, Day 2 and Day 3 must be sent without gaps. Early data is what allows a losing product or ad to be caught before significant budget is lost.
  • Failing to report that ads are running lets spend continue unnoticed, and advertisers have lost several hundred dollars this way. Stats shared earlier could have saved more than $100 in such cases, and this happens several times a week.
  • Outside help depends entirely on visible data. Without daily stats, the support team cannot tell an advertiser to pause a product or turn off ads before losses grow, so the advertiser must share the numbers first for any intervention to be possible.

Measuring automated support in Gorgias

  • In Gorgias, Statistics > Live > Overview only loads periods of one day or less and shows "Failed to retrieve statistic" for longer ranges. For multi-week automation results, open Statistics > Automation > Automation Add-on, choose a range such as the last 28 days, and export the flow data as CSV.
  • Analytics for the Automation Add-on: Statistics > Automation > Automation Add-on (from the dropdown in the top left). Key metrics: total add-on interactions, automation rate, volume per flow, automated add-on interaction, served by an agent, drop-off, and per-flow performance (Quick response flow, Article recommendation, Track order, Report order issue, Return order, Cancel order). Data can be exported as CSV.
  • Automation rate = automated add-on interactions ÷ total interactions in the same period. Example: 59 automated and 9 handled by an agent make 68 total, and the report displayed an add-on automation rate of 86%.
  • A typical agent reply to a single ticket takes at least 30 seconds, usually about 1 to 1.5 minutes and up to 2 minutes, so each automated interaction saves that time.
  • Estimated agent time saved = automated interactions × minutes an agent would need per ticket. A single reply-wait-reply ticket takes about 1 to 1.5 minutes and up to 2 minutes, so 59 automated interactions save roughly 59 to 88 minutes at 1 to 1.5 minutes each.
  • Case: a new skincare brand (Toti) on Gorgias Automate. Starting point: a brand with a short history, using quick response flows, order management flows, a flow builder return policy flow, help centre, contact form and auto reply rules. Results after 28 days: 68 total interactions compared with 28 days before; 86% of interactions were resolved by add-on features; 59 interactions were automated and 9 needed an agent. Quick response flows: 52 automated and 7 not. The average agent reply time for a single "where is my order" ticket is about 1 to 1.5 minutes, or up to 2 minutes, because the agent replies, waits and replies again. Automation saved 59 agent interactions in 28 days.

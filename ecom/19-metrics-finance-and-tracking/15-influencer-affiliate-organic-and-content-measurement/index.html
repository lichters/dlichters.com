Playbook › 19. Metrics, finance and tracking

Influencer, affiliate, organic and content measurement

Section 19.15 · 15 of 23 in this chapter · 29 min read

In short

  • Set one clear number to win or lose by before a campaign starts.
  • Check real engagement (people who actually react) before you pay an influencer. Do not trust follower count alone.
  • Give every partner its own tagged link, so you can see which sales came from them.
  • Judge TikTok content by sales per million views, not by views alone.
  • A Trial Reel is a test reel. Most of its viewers do not follow you, so judge it against your usual reels, not against fixed numbers.
More key points
  • Revenue per million views is the sales you earn for every million views. Under about 1,000 USD per million in a richer country is weak.
  • Views alone mean little. Check sales, where viewers live, and sales per view.
  • If sales are zero, check each step from store visit to payment before you blame the product.

Set goals and KPIs before launch

1 min
  • A KPI (key performance indicator) is a number you track to see if you reach a goal. Set KPIs before an influencer campaign starts, so you have exact targets to check against. Without targets, there is nothing specific to measure the campaign against afterwards.
  • A clear campaign goal is the base for accurate measurement and a fair judgment of success. It also shows how each influencer partnership helped the overall marketing plan.
  • If the goal is sales, track sales and conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy) directly. Conversion rate is the key measure for sales from an influencer campaign. If the goal is brand awareness (people knowing your brand), focus on awareness measures. Tracking conversions matters less then.
  • Set the success measure before a TikTok test starts. A test account can be judged on views, but still check the result against sales per million views. A video can go viral and still fail to bring sales.
  • Record followers, views and orders as separate numbers. A test can add tens of thousands of followers while the product still loses money. Audience growth must not hide a weak product.
  • Track sales per million views as the success measure for TikTok content. A video can go viral and still fail this measure. That means the product or offer does not convert.
  • Test a product by measuring sales per million views. A product can go viral and still not convert. Only this number shows whether the product is worth scaling (spending more to grow it).

Check influencers before a collaboration

1 min

  • Real engagement (people liking, commenting and sharing) matters more than a high follower count. Check engagement before you work with an influencer.

  • Track engagement rather than follower count. Likes, comments, tags and shares show whether posts reach the right audience. Bought followers from low-value countries do not buy.

  • A follower target can be unrealistic and easy to fake. Paid followers from cheap regions have no engagement. So the KPI should measure the engagement of followers.

  • Review an influencer's platform analytics (their own statistics inside the app) before you collaborate. Engagement quality can strongly affect how much your customers buy.

  • Check engagement with this procedure:

    1. Ask the influencer for their native analytics (built-in statistics): Instagram Insights, YouTube analytics or TikTok analytics.
    2. Review the engagement snapshot on each platform the influencer uses.
    3. Decide on the collaboration only after reviewing these numbers.

    Most influencers share these analytics for free when asked.

  • Ask the influencer to send the engagement numbers of their post (comments, shares, likes, interaction rate, meaning how many people react compared to how many see it). Do this in addition to checking the platform analytics yourself.

  • Social Blade gives a cross-platform overview (one view across all channels) of an influencer. It is especially useful before collaborating with a larger influencer.

  • Do not buy likes, comments or engagement from SMM panels (websites that sell fake engagement). Fake engagement distorts the data and may mislead the platform. Some sellers use it to lower CPM (cost per 1,000 views), but this is not recommended.

Platform analytics tools

1 min
  • Built-in tools show a post's or account's engagement and audience data: Instagram Insights (business account), Facebook Insights, Twitter Analytics, YouTube Analytics and TikTok Analytics. They show comments, shares, likes and interaction rate.
  • In the TikTok app, open Creator tools > Analytics. The overview shows video views, profile views, likes, comments and shares. The per-video panel shows total play time, average watch time (how long people watch on average), watch full video and new followers.
  • Instagram's Edits app shows total Reels views, reach (how many different people saw it) and net follower change (new followers minus lost ones) over a chosen period, such as 60 days. Tap a single reel to see its own insights.
  • Instagram added weekly and monthly recaps and celebration tools in Insights to highlight milestones with followers.
  • Social listening tools (tools that track mentions) show how often a brand, hashtag, keyword or campaign name is mentioned. Brand Watch is paid and widely used. Mention is an automated tool that counts brand and hashtag mentions. Without a listening tool, count mentions by hand: search the brand name, a keyword or the campaign name on each platform during the campaign, and note how often it appears on each specific platform.

Set up tracking for each creator and partner

2 min
  • Use UTM tracking (short tags added to a link that show where a visitor came from) on every link sent to a paid influencer for an ad. This tracks traffic from that source accurately. The format is:
?utm_source=[influencer_name]&utm_medium=banner&utm_campaign=trial
  • Example: for an ad placed on the source's own site the UTM tracking link is:
?utm_source=yourstore&utm_medium=banner&utm_campaign=trial
  • Use UTM links for bloggers and creators whose results cannot be read from coupon codes alone.
  • Create a UTM link for every partner. Give the tagged link to partners running paid ads, so clicks can be credited to that placement in Google Analytics (a free tool that measures website visits).
  • Create a separate affiliate or landing link for each creator or channel. (An affiliate is a partner paid a commission on sales. A landing link is the page a visitor arrives on.) Then check Shopify's order source report (a Shopify page showing where orders came from) for orders per channel, such as Instagram, TikTok and other sources. Referral Candy also reports sales for each link.
  • In Referral Candy (a referral app), create an affiliate link for any product in the store and give the creator a referral link. Grant the creator temporary staff access restricted to orders, so they can only see sales from the agreed period.
  • Affiliate tracking links or codes show which sales came from a specific influencer, so the sales source is visible directly. Affiliate marketing tools show which source or influencer each sale came from when an influencer shares a tracked link or code.
  • Promo codes help track creator-driven sales. But many buyers do not enter them, so codes capture only part of the impact. Do not use promo codes as the only success measure for long-term partnerships.
  • The embed code (a short snippet of code you paste into a website) for each Refersion banner (Refersion is affiliate software) contains that partner's unique affiliate tracking code. When a customer clicks the banner, the link carries the code automatically. The sale is credited and the commission paid without the partner placing a custom link.

Two conventions appear in the sources. Use each for its own purpose. Do not mix them in one report.

Affiliate economics and attribution

2 min
  • Affiliate payouts (commission payments) are processed in batches throughout the month or once monthly, not automatically. Payments are delayed from sale completion to check that customers are genuine. They are typically held back for up to 30 days.
  • Affiliate software gives each affiliate a unique tracking link. A cookie (a small file in the visitor's browser that remembers the link) credits them for sales within a set timeframe. If a customer clicks the link but buys after the cookie window (the time limit) closes, you get the sale but the affiliate gets no commission.
  • Set the cookie duration to 30 days, 60 days or unlimited. High-ticket products (expensive products) often convert over 2-3 months. A 30-day window risks not crediting the affiliate who created the first interest.
  • Affiliate marketing gives controllable, predictable costs, unlike paid traffic. You know your exact commission rate per sale in advance. This supports cash-flow forecasting (predicting money coming in and going out) and financial planning. Paid advertising has variable daily costs beyond your control.
  • E-commerce businesses typically allocate 10% of annual revenue to Google Ads. Affiliate marketing is more cost-efficient, because you pay commissions only on completed sales, not for clicks or impressions (each time an ad is shown).
  • Organic traffic (free visitors from search and posts, not paid ads) usually has a lower cost per acquisition (CPA, the cost to win one customer) than paid traffic. Each click carries no direct cost. Use organic and other non-paid channels alongside paid ads, especially for traffic too expensive to target with paid ads.
  • Example: if a referred prospect (a person sent to you by a partner) pays 4,000 USD for Course D membership, the referrer receives 1,000 USD in commission, which is 25%.
  • During the Course D booking call, the field labelled 'Did someone recommend you?' must contain the referrer's Discord name (Discord is a chat app). Otherwise the referral is not tracked and the commission is not paid.
  • About 10 new members join Course D each month through referrals from existing members, not through paid advertising.
  • A creator's story with about 5,000 views and an audience that closely matches the niche (a small product group) can achieve around 20 to 100 sales. Small numbers of sales can still justify the spend when the product margin (profit per sale) is strong.

Paid partner placements: ROAS and CPC

1 min
  • CPC (cost per click) equals the monthly ad cost divided by the number of clicks. Example: 200 USD per month with 100 clicks gives a CPC of 2 USD.
  • ROAS (return on ad spend) equals sales generated divided by ad spend. Example: 200 USD of ad spend that brings in 2,000 USD in sales gives a 10X ROAS.
  • A 10X return on ad spend is treated as an acceptable threshold for paying a partner for an ad placement.
  • Partners usually do not charge per click. Calculate CPC yourself by dividing the monthly ad cost by the clicks counted in Google Analytics for that partner's UTM link.
  • Example: a partner charging 500 USD per month brought 2,000 USD in sales with a 5 USD CPC, which is a 4X ROAS. After the free trial, drop this partner, because it does not make money.
  • Partner placement procedure:
    1. Open the UTM builder and enter the destination URL, which is your store page.
    2. Set campaign source to the ad type, for example banner ad.
    3. Set campaign medium to the ad format, for example banner.
    4. Set campaign name to the partner's domain without http or the.com ending, for example surfingwaves for surfingwaves.com.
    5. Generate the link and give that tagged link to the partner for paid ad placements.
    6. After the campaign, count the clicks in Google Analytics, calculate CPC from the ad cost, then calculate ROAS from the sales for that month.
    7. Keep the partner only if the ROAS justifies the monthly cost.
  • Banner ads (image ads on a site) on forums and niche sites can be surprisingly cheap, sometimes around a couple of hundred dollars per month. This makes constant visibility in front of a target audience affordable for many early businesses.

Measure influencer campaign results

2 min
  • Compare the same metrics before and after the influencer campaign. This before-and-after check is especially important for a first influencer campaign.
  • Benchmark (compare against a standard) influencer results against other channels, such as Facebook ads, TikTok ads and Google Analytics data. This shows whether the influencer budget is worth the spend compared with paid channels.
  • To measure which influencer generated conversions:
    1. Survey customers and ask where they first heard of the influencer or brand. Or run audience surveys or social media polls to gauge how the campaign was perceived.
    2. Check brand analytics for traffic and click-through rate (the share of people who click after seeing it) coming from the influencer.
    3. If affiliate links are used, read the sales source data from the affiliate tool.
  • Influencer log procedure, kept in one spreadsheet that works as the influencer database:
    1. Record the influencer's name and whether they have posted for you before.
    2. Log each step: contacted, replied, product sent, product received, photo sent for approval, and post executed.
    3. Record whether the influencer sold anything.
    4. If there were no sales, judge whether the impressions (views) and content quality were worth the cost.
    5. Keep all of this in a spreadsheet, which functions as the influencer database.
  • Judge influencer performance with several measures together: total traffic and social impressions, Google Analytics traffic, new emails collected, and the mix of new versus existing buyers. Judging on direct sales alone undervalues brand awareness.
  • Do not spend a large sum on one high-profile post without a way to measure its results, such as coupon codes or real-time analytics. Without measurement, the spend can feel like money lost.
  • Organic reach is not the same as conversion. Judge a product by orders per thousand views and by whether it is a real purchase. Do not judge by follower growth or comment volume alone.
  • Use total reach to see how many people the content is shown to and whether a creator is worth the cost. If the content will be reused in paid ads (repurposed), its quality matters more than the size of the creator's audience.
  • Benchmark creator content costs at 500 to 5,000 USD per million views. Use this range as a minimum when judging the economics of view-based content.
  • Creator ROI (return on investment) procedure:
    1. Measure the return on investment for each influencer.
    2. Compare the influencers and identify the ones giving the highest return.
    3. Shift part of the budget toward those top performers and increase the posting cadence (how often they post) with them.
  • Track engagement metrics per platform for each repurposed video. This shows which videos to renew, keep running or stop.
  • Record multi-product TikTok tests per post in a spreadsheet, not by account totals. Aggregate account views hide which product is working. A weekly review is enough.

Audience sentiment and indirect value

1 min
  • Judge creator campaigns first by audience sentiment (the mood and quality of the comments and the conversation the post starts), before counting likes. A post that triggers buying questions and positive discussion is worth more than a high like count.
  • Likes, comments, shares and total interactions are the standard social engagement metrics. They are not the main success measure for creator campaigns. Track conversion rate (how many people take the desired action after seeing the creator's content) for each creator where it can be measured. For long-term partnerships this is harder to track, so read it alongside sentiment and reach.
  • Some creator value is indirect. Repeated product exposure builds brand recall (people remember your brand), so people may later buy in a store or without a code. For long-term partnerships, accept this effect as part of the return. Do not expect it to show in direct tracking.
  • Use shares as the main metric for audience-building content. A share shows that people will pass the content on and that its reach will grow. Watch cost per engagement (the cost for each reaction) as a secondary check.
  • Targeting is working when cost per engagement falls to almost nothing. In the example, a post had a cost per engagement under one cent. Judge posts by engagement and shares before looking at sales.
  • Genuine engagement for a campaign matters more than a high follower count. Check engagement before working with an influencer.
  • Rule of thumb: about 10% of likes should convert into followers. An account with only about 1% like-to-follow conversion is performing badly, even if views are high.

TikTok video signals and diagnostics

1 min
  • A winning video clearly outperforms the rest of the account. Example: if most videos get about 1,000 views and one gets 30,000, that video is the account's winner. Let it guide new content.
  • Once analytics show high average watch time, a high completion rate and a good engagement ratio, create more videos of that type.
  • Store sessions (visits to your shop) are the diagnostic. Millions of views with few store sessions means the product or video does not create interest. Many sessions without sales point to the store itself. Check the sessions-to-views relationship before changing the store.
  • When a video gets many views but few store sessions, the cause is most often the product itself, not the store. Curious viewers who see value click through. Check store sessions for each viral video.
  • Use TikTok analytics to check watch time, which videos perform, and trends from the last 7 days. This data shows which videos to recreate or which direction to take.
  • Keep a written log of the upload time of each video. Then compare times with results. Re-upload a winning idea at the same time it previously went viral.
  • Check the follower gender split in TikTok analytics to decide who your videos are made for. In the example, followers were 61.1% female and 38.9% male.
  • New TikTok accounts may not show retention charts (graphs of how long people keep watching) and can register views incompletely. In one example, an account showed about 30 million views when the real count was 80-90 million. Verify numbers on an older account before drawing conclusions.
  • Track the four engagement signals together per video: likes, comments, shares and bookmarks (saves). Strong numbers across all four matter more than a high like count alone, because TikTok distribution (how widely TikTok shows a video to others) responds to all four.

Comments to views A healthy engagement ratio of 1 comment for every 10 views (1:10) 1:10

  • Worked example: 250,000 views with 25,000 likes gives a 1:10 ratio, still in the good range. The same 250,000 views with only 5,000 likes gives 1:50, a weak ratio that signals the video will likely be pushed less widely.
  • Case: a video with 24.5 million views and 3.2 million likes had a ratio of about 1:8, with many comments, shares and bookmarks. This was a standout result, because ratios that good are rare at that view count. The video was 8 seconds long and reached about 20 million views within 24 hours of first going viral. The creator attributed this to combining a strong ASMR element (soft, tingling sounds), good timing and a strong hook (the first seconds that grab attention).
  • Case: a 51 million view video with about 3 million likes had a ratio of roughly 1:17. It performed because it was a long video with very high watch time. High watch time can offset a weaker like ratio.

Watch time and retention

1 min
  • Average watch time is the most important metric for judging a video and for TikTok distribution. It can exceed the video's length when viewers rewatch it. The goal is for average watch time to exceed the video's actual length. A video with a weak like ratio can still be pushed widely if its watch time is strong.
  • Completion rate (the share of viewers who watch to the end) is the second key signal. Benchmarks: above 50% is good, around 65% is strong, and up to about 80% is achievable for smaller videos. Completion rates tend to fall as a video reaches millions of views.
  • Watch-through percentage tends to decline as a video is pushed to larger audiences. A strong rate at small scale does not guarantee the same rate at scale. Track the percentage over time and do not expect it to stay constant.
  • Watch-time check: average watch time divided by video length. Example: a 12-second video with a 15.4-second average watch time gives about 1.28. This is strong, because the result is above 1.0 and viewers replay the clip.
  • Example: a 12-second video had an average watch time of 15.4 seconds, considered very good because viewers rewatched it. Average watch time above the video length is the target benchmark.
  • Watch retention (how much of the video viewers keep watching) is the key metric for short video. Aim for viewers to watch to the end, or at least more than 50% of the video, because higher retention improves distribution.

Traffic sources and distribution

1 min
  • Track the share of views from the For You Page (TikTok's main feed for new viewers), not from your own followers. A high For You share, reported around 97% in one example, signals real growth to new viewers. Views mainly from your profile suggest limited reach.
  • In per-video analytics, the traffic source labelled 'Reached audience' shows the share of views coming from the For You Page. A rising share means the video reaches viewers who do not follow the account.
  • Per-video traffic source analytics usually appear within about 24 hours of posting, sometimes up to two days. Detailed analytics were reported to require a minimum follower threshold of around 1,000 followers. The exact number was unclear.
  • Choose Instagram posting times from the audience activity data in Instagram Insights. The best time differs for every account. There is no universal best hour.

Creator Fund and platform payouts

1 min
  • The TikTok Creator Fund (TikTok's payment program for creators) paid about 30 dollars per million views on average. One month of Creator Fund earnings reached about 2,000 dollars. Eligibility requires at least 10,000 followers.
  • Creator Fund payments are not recommended for product accounts, because joining may reduce organic reach (free reach without paid ads). Consider joining only after you have proven products and an established account.
  • Reach was reported to drop near the end of the year when Creator Fund budgets appeared to run low, then recover after the fund reset in the new year. This is an unverified observation and should be treated as a warning sign.
  • Snapchat Spotlight (Snapchat's public video feed) payouts now require at least 10,000 unique views and snaps (Snapchat posts) submitted on at least 5 days. Plan the posting schedule around both conditions.

Instagram Trial Reels

2 min
  • A Trial Reel is a test reel. Instagram shows it first to non-followers, meaning people who do not follow your account.
  • Find your Trial Reel results at Profile > Reels tab > 'Drafts and trial reels' > 'Trial reels'. All uploaded Trial Reels are listed there.
  • Open a reel and tap 'View more insights' to see the full data.
  • The results screen shows the banner 'This trial reel is being shown to non-followers' and a 'Share to everyone' button.
  • Use 'Share to everyone' only after the results show the reel is performing well.
  • Example Trial Reel results, to compare with a typical reel:

  • Compare them with your typical reel, not with fixed numbers.
  • In the example, 8,000 views was high performance for a Trial Reel.
  • Even 3,000 views still meant reaching 3,000 people who had never seen the account.
  • Viral reach (spreading very widely and fast) is unlikely on Trial Reels, because they reach only a small group at first.
  • Share a Trial Reel to the main feed only when its watch-through rate (the share of viewers who watch to the end) and interactions beat your typical reel.
  • In the example, a 74.1% watch-through rate beat a typical 68.6%.
  • A 7-second average watch time (the average time each viewer watched) also signalled that the reel would likely do well on the feed.
  • Trial Reel results take time to build, because the reel reaches a limited, non-follower group.
  • Do not judge a reel on its first hours of data.
  • In Instagram's reel insights, the view rate past the first 3 seconds can be filtered by All, Followers or Non-followers.
  • For a Trial Reel, read the Non-followers value, since that is the audience it reaches.
  • For a Trial Reel, use the 'Accounts reached' figure (how many different accounts saw it).
  • Compare that figure with total views.
  • Judge the reel by how many new accounts it reached, not by view count alone.

Revenue per million views and product decisions

1 min
  • Revenue per million views is the money earned per million views.
  • Formula: total sales divided by the number of millions of views.
  • Example: 10 million views and 7,000 USD in sales gives 700 USD per million.
  • Use it to compare products and angles (the message or hook of a video), not just raw views.
  • Example: 5,000 USD in sales on 8 million views gives 625 USD per million views, below the 1,000 USD benchmark (a reference number to compare against).

  • The variant with the most views had the lowest revenue per 1,000 views.

Cases

1 min
  • Viral case: a store uploaded 12 TikTok videos and went viral on day four.
  • The video reached about 1.5 million views in the first 12 hours and about 600 sales that day.
  • The video itself converted poorly (few viewers bought) because it was a joke with a negative angle. Sales were about 500 the next day.
  • The account was then shadowbanned (the platform secretly limited its reach), and sales slowed sharply.
  • Race Car case: an honesty-angle video (its message was that the product does not even work) had about 1.8 million views.
  • It gained about 1,500 followers in 24 hours.
  • The store price was 30 USD and only 2 orders were confirmed, against an expected 10 to 15 orders.
  • Takeaway: the marketing angle drew attention but did not convert. The product itself was a weak buy.
  • Facebook ad case: a Facebook ad for a simple household product reached about 2.3 million views and drew strong comments and buyer interest.
  • The creator saved the video and planned to test it both as a TikTok ad and as an organic post (an unpaid post).
  • The video was about 16 seconds long. It used background music and a call to action (a clear instruction to viewers, such as to buy).
  • It showed the product solving a small everyday problem.
  • The product's European AliExpress listing (its product page on the AliExpress shopping site) had over 5,000 orders.
  • Another listing of the same product showed about 213 orders.
  • Low-converter case: in one reviewed case, a product video reached about 10 million views and produced around 3 sales.
  • Another account with about 12 million views produced 10 orders.
  • Only a few sales per million views was the signal that the product was a weak converter (a product that rarely turns viewers into buyers), despite the huge view counts.

Store funnel after traffic spikes

1 min
  • A funnel is the path from store visit to sale. A traffic spike is a sudden jump in visitors.
  • Example from one week of a viral TikTok campaign: about 1,220 sessions, 57 add-to-cart events (a visitor puts an item in the cart) and 47 checkouts (the payment step).
  • Result: zero sales.
  • Viral views (22 million in 36 hours on one video) did not produce purchases, because the traffic was not relevant (not the right people).
  • Compare these funnel numbers against the expected conversion rate before concluding the product is the issue.
  • Organic traffic (free visitors, not paid ones) is less targeted than paid traffic.
  • Viewers of a viral video may visit out of curiosity.
  • Track the funnel from sessions to add-to-cart to checkout to sales.
  • Compare those rates to paid benchmarks when judging a product.
  • In Shopify's product admin (the back office where you manage products), the Insights panel stays empty until the product has had recent sales.
  • It cannot judge a newly embedded product video (a video placed on the product page).
  • In the meantime, use the LyveCom story numbers (views, click-through rate, add to cart, checkout revenue) to judge video performance. LyveCom is a video tool for online stores, and click-through rate is the share of viewers who click.
  • LyveCom's analytics suite (its set of reports) shows revenue attribution (which video led to which sales).
  • It also shows the engagement rate for all video content, and the effect of video on conversion, average order value (the average amount each buyer spends) and session time (how long visitors stay).
  • Use it to see which content has the highest impact on the store.

Organic content, SEO and content ROI

2 min
  • Organic content means free posts and articles that bring in visitors without paid ads.
  • SEO (search engine optimisation) means getting found in Google search.
  • ROI (return on investment) means what you earn back from what you spend.
  • Monitor traffic sources and performance:
    1. Use Google Analytics (Google's free visitor-tracking tool) to track organic, referral (visitors from other sites), social and direct (visitors who type your address) traffic.
    2. Set goals for traffic composition (the mix of traffic sources). Target: 80% organic.
    3. Review monthly which keywords (search words) and pages drive conversions (sales or sign-ups).
    4. Identify underperforming content (content doing poorly) and improve or remove it.
    5. Test changes in small steps and measure impact.
  • Content ROI takes time.
  • Blog articles and SEO improvements do not generate immediate traffic.
  • Plan for 2-6 months before seeing significant organic traffic increases.
  • The long-term payoff is free, recurring traffic from Google, unlike paid ads.
  • Compare the cost of content with paid traffic.
  • A blog post of about 500 USD can cost as much as a Google Ads budget (money for Google's paid search ads) spent within a month or even a single day.
  • A well-ranked post (one that shows high in search results) can drive traffic for years.
  • Direct sales are hard to attribute (credit to a cause) to a single article.
  • So measure content by the number of email signups it produces.
  • Compare signups per article to decide which topics to repeat.
  • In Google Analytics, create a goal (a tracked result) for visits to the newsletter confirmation page (the page people see after they sign up).
  • Use it to see which pages send the most visitors to that page.
  • Monthly content review:
    1. List all articles.
    2. Sort them by email signups.
    3. Use the top performers (the best ones) to choose keywords in the keyword tools and create similar articles.
  • Use Google Search Console (Google's free tool showing how your site appears in search) and Google Analytics data together to audit SEO (check how well your SEO works).
  • Set measurable goals first, then pick the actions that match how competitive those goals are.
  • For SEO opportunity analysis (finding where you can gain ground), use a Search Console window (the date range of data) of about 30 to 95 days.
  • Use 90 days as a practical default.
  • Windows of 7 to 28 days are too slow or too small to be reliable.
  • Periods much longer than 90 days blend performance across a year and hide recent changes.
  • Search Console > Performance shows by default the past three months of organic impressions (how often your pages appeared in search) and clicks.
  • A new store will not show meaningful data for the first weeks.
  • Google Analytics must be installed on the store to track organic traffic behaviour, such as bounce rate (the share of visitors who leave after one page) and time on page.
  • On Shopify, it is very easy to install.

Link-building tracking

1 min
  • Link building means getting other websites to link to yours.
  • Outreach (contacting site owners to ask for links) produces a growing volume of data over time.
  • Record each contact, date, subject and result from the start.
  • Keep them retrievable (easy to find again) for future campaigns.
  • Store outreach tracking files both online and as a physical copy.
  • The online copy is in Google Drive (Google's online file storage). The physical copy is on an external or portable hard drive (a separate disk you can carry).
  • Data kept in only one place can be lost.
  • Repeat outreach to the same contact is acceptable over time.
  • But only with enough spacing between messages.
  • Four Google Drive template sheets (ready-made spreadsheets to copy) are used for outreach tracking:
    • Master Database
    • Outreach Batch folder
    • No Email Database
    • Success Database
  • Keep a very large master spreadsheet stored online in Google Drive, but edit it offline in Excel (Microsoft's spreadsheet program).
  • Big online spreadsheets in Google Drive process slowly, while Excel handles very large files quickly.
  • Ahrefs (an SEO tool for checking links) crawls (automatic page scans by a bot) showed that many links are uncrawled (not scanned) or blocked by robots.txt (a file that tells bots which pages to skip).
  • Read crawl totals with that in mind.
  • The Ahrefs data explorer (its data viewer) can export URL lists (lists of page addresses) with columns such as Page Rating, traffic and HTTP code (a number showing how a page responded).

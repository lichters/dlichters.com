UTM tracking and attribution

Section 19.8 · 8 of 23 in this chapter · 23 min read

In short

  • Tracking is never perfect. Pick one method and use it every time.
  • Judge each traffic source by the profit it brings, not by visitor count.
  • Set up sales tracking before the store gets visitors.
  • Ask customers how they found you, because phone and text sales are missed by Google.
  • Check ad platform numbers against your own store numbers before you trust them.
More key points
  • Each tool has one job. Do not use one tool for another's job.
  • Judge total ad results with your store or accounting software: total ad spend against total revenue.
  • Google Analytics and Google Ads will show different revenue. In the example, the gap was almost 46 percent.
  • Sales often get lost when a shopper clicks an ad on one day and buys days later. This missing credit is the most common cause of gaps.
  • Treat "unavailable" entries as missing data, not as a real traffic source.

Attribution principles and their limits

2 min

  • Digital marketing channels affect each other. They do not work alone.

  • PPC (pay-per-click ads, where you pay for each click) campaigns create extra results beyond their own numbers. They change traffic patterns and results in other channels.

  • Perfect tracking is impossible. Modern shoppers compare stores, visit several stores, and take time before they buy. So it is unclear which touchpoint (each place a customer meets your brand) should get the credit for a sale.

  • Attribution means giving credit for a sale to a traffic source (where a visitor came from). It is always imperfect. Customers use several channels and touchpoints over several days before they buy.

  • Choose one tracking and attribution model. Use it in every reporting period. Do not switch methods.

  • All tracking systems have blind spots. For example, cookies (small files that remember a visitor in the browser) get lost, and journeys cross several sessions (separate visits). So consistency matters more than perfect accuracy.

  • Judge a campaign by total revenue compared with total ad spend. Do not rely only on last-click data. Last-click means the sale is credited to the last click before buying.

  • Track revenue by traffic source from the start. Early tracking shows which sources make profit and which waste time. Test sources. Keep the ones that consistently make money. Drop the rest.

  • Judge each source by revenue and profit. Do not judge it by visitor count. A source that sends many visitors but no sales is a cost, not a win.

  • Set up sales tracking before the store gets traffic. Then every sale can be traced back to the ad or source that brought it. **

  • Google does not record sales made by phone, text or live chat. Track them yourself.

    1. When you take an order, ask each customer how they found the store.
    2. Optionally, add a short survey to the order confirmation page, such as a simple form asking how the customer found you.
    3. Write down answers such as Google search, Bing search or a Facebook ad.

  • Tracking for Facebook, Google and email is often incomplete. Part of the reason is Apple's iOS 14 limits and changed tracking rules from around 2022. (iOS 14 is Apple's 2021 privacy update that limits tracking.)

  • Ask buyers how they found the store in a survey after they buy. Compare their answers with the revenue the ad platform reports. Do not trust either number alone.

Multi-session journeys and double counting

1 min
  • Most e-commerce sites earn more revenue from multi-session customers (people who visit more than once) than from first-time buyers who buy in one visit.
  • Here is a realistic journey over several days and channels:
    • Monday, the customer clicks a shopping campaign.
    • Tuesday, they click a search retargeting ad (an ad shown again to someone who visited before) and become a lead (someone who gave contact details but has not bought).
    • Wednesday, they buy through an email.
    • Thursday, they share a referral code (a code a friend uses to get credit).
    • A friend sees the site, clicks a YouTube retargeting ad, and buys.
    • The result is 2 real purchases, but 4 to 5 channels claim credit.
  • When several platforms track the same sale on their own (email, YouTube, shopping, referral software), each one counts it. This gives 4 to 5 counted sales when only 1 or 2 real purchases happened.
  • Journeys can span many days and several touchpoints. So the time from first ad to purchase is often long. In one example, one stage took 5 days and 2 hours. The next took 2 days and 22 hours. Customers sometimes take 7 to 10 or more days to buy.
  • Typical paths include: seeing a Facebook or Instagram ad, then getting an email or SMS (text message), then returning through a retargeting ad, then visiting from a Google search, or finishing through an abandoned cart email (an email sent to someone who left items in their basket). Judge attribution across the whole path, not just the last click.
  • Shoppers often need several visits or ad clicks before buying. So many sales take several steps. Simple reports give credit only to the last touch, so earlier touchpoints are missed. A high share of multi-step sales is normal. It does not mean your setup is broken. Single-click reports always undercount earlier touchpoints.

Direct and unattributed traffic

1 min
  • Direct/None traffic (visits with no recorded source) comes from customers who return through browser history or from links in text messages. In mature stores, this channel shows 15 percent of attributed revenue. But it is mostly impossible to measure as a real traffic source.
  • Direct/None means visits with no recorded referral source. Examples: returning visitors who open the site from their history, clicks on untracked links, or links shared in private messages. In the example store, it was the third largest traffic source. It made 12 to 13 percent of total revenue.
  • Direct traffic in analytics (reports of where visitors came from) can come from untracked links, bookmarks, or links saved from email. It can be large. Keep it separate from paid ad traffic when you read results. **
  • Do not try to scale or optimize Direct/None. It hides untracked touchpoints. Use it only to compare channels with each other.
  • Direct/None can make up a large share of total sales in a Shopify store (an online store platform). So revenue by channel in Google Analytics will not add up to the store's total revenue. Use it to compare channels. Do not use it to match totals.
  • Direct/None in Analytics usually means the final buying visit. Google cannot link that purchase to the earlier visits where the customer first found you. Move this revenue to the sources that really brought the customer. Usually these are organic search (free Google results) or paid search. **
  • Estimating the share of unattributed revenue (sales with no known source) by source. **
    • When Shopify records revenue that Analytics cannot assign to a source, estimate each source's share. Do this by calculating its share of new users. Then apply the same percentage to the unassigned revenue.
    • Example: if Google Ads brought 72.9% of new users, assign 72.9% of the unassigned revenue to Google Ads.

Abandoned cart, email and message-driven sales

1 min
  • UTMs (tags added to the end of a link that label its source) track email-driven sales. They appear as parameters at the end of URLs. This lets Shopify Messaging and Google Analytics report them.
  • Abandoned cart recovery emails come from an earlier visit through another source. Credit the sale to the source that first brought the customer, not to the email. **
  • Multi-touch attribution tools (tools that spread credit over several touchpoints) can add email and SMS touchpoints from platforms like Klaviyo and Attentive (email and text tools). This links a sale to an earlier ad, even when the last touch before buying was a message.

Reallocating revenue and cross-channel effects

1 min
  • Judging each channel only by its own clicks and sales misses cross-channel effects. For example, a YouTube campaign can lift search click-through rates (the share of people who click) or total traffic. Cutting a channel that looks weak alone can reduce results in the channels that depend on it.
  • When a new Google Ads search campaign goes live, total traffic may rise slowly over several weeks, not right away. Judge this lift by the overall traffic trend over that period. Do not look only at the clicks each campaign reports.
  • To compare paid Google Ads traffic with organic search and email, use the channel comparison in Google Analytics. To see which single ads convert and how often, use Google Ads data. Do not mix the two views when you judge one campaign.

Attribution models and windows

1 min
  • Google Ads uses a 30-day lookback window by default. A lookback window is how many days back a sale can be linked to an ad. Google links calls to website visits from the previous 30 days.
  • Last-click attribution is the standard model for call conversions in Google Ads. It gives credit to the final ad or source the person clicked before calling.
  • Meta's default for this setup credits a purchase to an ad if the person clicked it within 7 days or viewed it within 1 day. A view means the person saw the ad but did not click. Keep these defaults for remarketing campaigns.
  • The click window stays long on purpose. A person may click a remarketing ad days earlier and buy later. That ad still reached them. A view counts because the ad reminds the person of the store, even without a click.
  • For influencer marketing, use a 30-day attribution window, not a one-day click window. Influencers usually need several exposures, often three or more touches, before a customer buys.
  • Retargeting apps often claim sales using a 30-day view window. This credits them with sales they did not create. Check app attribution against your own analytics before you trust it.
  • Account hygiene: use 7DC / 1DEV attribution (7DC means 7-day click. 1DEV means 1-day engaged view). Also exclude website buyers from the last 180 days, and exclude existing Shopify customers from targeting.

AMER and attribution settings

1 min

AMER means Acquisition Marketing Efficiency Ratio. It measures how well the whole business gets new customers. Settings below are compared against it.

  • AMER shows business-level truth. Attribution settings should be judged against it. Ad platform numbers alone should not decide which setting is better.
  • If an account is optimized (tuned to improve) for a metric that does not follow AMER, it is not chasing the real business result. Change the attribution setting or optimization target to one that follows AMER.
  • Platform-wide changes by Meta cause a temporary drop in AMER across the whole industry. Results return to normal only slowly, and never fully to the old level. Expect a dip after any platform change. Do not read it as a permanent failure of your account.

1-day click Too narrow for most accounts. It did not capture enough sales for fast decisions. Caution.
1-day click plus 1-day view Showed a higher match in the chart. Its window is too short for most accounts.
7-day click plus 1-day engage-through (a view where the person engaged with the ad) After the redefinition, it matched AMER better than the other tested settings. Seven-figure cohort (a group of stores with seven-figure revenue), March 2026.

  • A group of seven-figure stores, tracked in March 2026, showed AMER dropping sharply at the March 18 attribution change. It then recovered only partly over the following weeks. Before the change, 7-day click followed AMER closely. Afterwards, the two split apart.

UTM parameters: what they do and how to name them

2 min
  • UTM parameters are free extra fields added to the end of an ad's link. When a visitor clicks, they pass the source, medium, campaign, content and term to tools such as Shopify reports or Google Analytics.
  • UTM tracking shows which campaign, ad set and ad a sale came from. Compare these sources in Shopify reports, or in Google Analytics if it is connected to Shopify.
  • UTM tracking has limits. Customers often buy on a later visit, from another device, or after several touchpoints like ads, emails, texts and Google searches. These paths can break the link to the original ad, so UTM data loses some sales.
  • Use UTMs to cross-check Facebook numbers against real store data.
  • Use one naming rule. Name ad sets by date, audience, country and placement. Name ads after their video or angle. The names flow into the URL parameters, which keeps results readable.
  • Use UTM parameters on every TikTok ad URL. Name campaigns, ad groups and ads the same way, so the parameters can match Shopify orders.
  • Give every ad, headline and offer its own key. A key is a unique coupon, code, address or phone number. This lets each response be traced to its source. Without keys, you cannot tell which creative (the ad image or video) performs best.
  • UTM parameters show conversion rate per campaign and ad set. This is more precise than country-level data for budget decisions. Check UTM tracking when deciding which ad set or campaign gets more money within a country.
  • Some sellers do not connect the Meta pixel (tracking code from Meta installed on the store). Instead, they track each campaign with UTM parameters. They read which campaign converts from the UTM data. This lets them test across several ad accounts without linking the pixel to each one.
  • Small brands rarely need custom parameters. Bigger brands, and users of GA4 (Google Analytics 4) or Triple Whale (a paid tracking tool), should follow the parameters their tool requires.
  • Audience segment URL parameters are set for all sales campaigns in the advertising settings. They are not set per ad. So they do not need to change for each new ad.
  • The UTM Link Builder creates tracking URLs with utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign. These let analytics attribute visits from ads, emails and links to their source.

UTM strings and setup steps by platform

2 min

Shared Meta/Facebook ad-level string. and are the same string, so they are merged here:

utm_source=Facebook&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign={{campaign.name}}&utm_content={{ad.name}}&utm_term={{adset.name}}
  • Add UTM parameters at the ad level so each ad's traffic is tracked by campaign, ad and ad set. Use exactly this parameter string.
  • Note: sources give different rules for the same fields. The string above maps utm_content to the ad and utm_term to the ad set. Procedure sets utm_term to the ad set name. Procedure sets utm_content to the ad set name and utm_term to the ad name. Choose one rule and use it everywhere. (See.)

Meta procedure in the ad's website section (Ads Manager, Facebook's ad dashboard):

  1. Click Build a URL parameter.
  2. Set campaign source to facebook.
  3. Set campaign medium to cpc (cost per click, the standard label for paid clicks).
  4. Set campaign name to the dynamic value campaign.name from the dropdown.
  5. Add the ad set name and the ad name as dynamic values from the dropdown.
  6. Set utm term to the ad set name.

The parameters are added to the URL. Shopify then receives which campaign, ad set and ad sent the visitor.

Free UTM setup for a Facebook ad:

  1. In Facebook Ads Manager, open the ad level of the campaign. Do this after adding the page, creative, and website URL (the landing page or product page).
  2. Open the section called Tracking.
  3. In the UTM parameters area, either use a pre-built UTM link from a website, or use Facebook's own UTM builder.
  4. Fill in Campaign source: Facebook, FB Marketing, or any consistent label you prefer.
  5. Fill in Campaign medium: CPC (cost per click) or the placement information.
  6. Fill in Campaign name with the campaign name.
  7. Fill in Campaign content with the ad set name.
  8. Fill in UTM term with the ad name.
  9. Save the URL, so the parameters are added when someone clicks Shop Now or any other button.

Meta setting in Source E's course:

  • Go to Ad > Tracking > URL parameters > Build a URL parameter. Add utm_source with the value ((campaign.id)) or the campaign name. Add utm_medium for the medium, such as Facebook Feed. Add utm_campaign for the campaign or promotion. Add utm_content for the ad variant. Dynamic values like ((ad.id)) can be inserted from the campaign, ad set or ad level.

Checking UTM results in Shopify

1 min
  • Go to Reports and search for "sales through marketing" or sessions attributed to marketing. Select the exact time range, for example today. Then add or view the columns UTM term, source, campaign name, campaign medium and content. The columns stay empty until traffic arrives with UTM parameters.
  • Shopify's sales-through-marketing report, together with Google Analytics, shows which source, medium, campaign, content and term brought each visit or sale when UTM links are used. It is the free way to see campaign-level results without an extra subscription.

Meta ad-level tracking with UTMs after iOS changes

1 min
  • After Apple's iOS privacy changes, Facebook and Shopify struggle to track what happens after the click. The destination link should carry the campaign, ad set and ad names as URL parameters. Then Shopify can show which ad set and ad each customer came from.
  • Accurate attribution is needed to decide whether to kill, keep or scale a campaign or ad set. Without knowing which campaign or ad set made each sale, you cannot scale a profitable account with confidence.

TikTok and Pinterest UTM setup

1 min

TikTok:

  • UTM links work for TikTok ads too. Paste a UTM link into the store link field at the ad level, instead of a plain store URL. Use the same parameter logic as for Facebook.
  • On TikTok, UTM parameters are needed because native TikTok tracking was considered unreliable. Compare Google Analytics with TikTok ad data. Duplicate only the campaigns that perform well. (Date not stated in source.)
  • Use UTM parameters on every TikTok ad URL. Tracking on iOS is limited, and TikTok data may not match Shopify.

Pinterest string added to the destination URL:

?utm_source=pinterest&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=%7Bcampaignname%7D&utm_term=%7Badgroupname%7D&utm_content=%7Badid%7D
  • Destination URL: use the product page link. Add UTM parameters with a question mark, so you can track which campaign the sales come from.
  • Destination URL: enter the product page link. Then add a UTM tag starting with a question mark, so each sale can be traced to its campaign, ad set and creative.
  • Pinterest tracking is delayed, because the platform takes time to record sales. UTM parameters show the source of each sale. They are enough for a simple test with one campaign and one ad group.

iOS and platform undercounting

1 min
  • Since Apple's iOS 14 update in 2021, Facebook Ads Manager sales numbers for campaigns and ad sets have become less reliable, especially for Apple devices. Many accounts see tracking accuracy of roughly 60 to 80 percent. So do not decide using Ads Manager data alone.
  • Since iOS 14, Facebook reporting undercounts sales. The coach estimated that only about 70 percent of real purchases may show up there. So the dashboard can show a return of 1.3 when the real value is closer to 1.6. Use an independent tracking source to check the numbers.
  • Facebook misses some sales when users switch devices or browsers, or when cookies are not stored. Complex tools such as Triple Whale can track the full customer journey. But they are not needed for the basic 80 to 90 percent tracking rate.
  • After iOS tracking changes, Meta can track fewer users, so its algorithm (its automatic ad-delivery system) is less precise. Retargeting still works but needs different setups. Advertisers have moved toward broad audiences with Advantage+ campaigns (Meta's automated campaigns). These receive all creatives and handle retargeting internally.
  • Tracking accuracy figures depend on how they are measured:
    • 60 to 80 percent: the accuracy many accounts see in Ads Manager.
    • About 70 percent: the coach's estimate of purchases that show up.
    • 80 to 90 percent: the basic tracking rate that does not need a complex tool.
  • UTM tracking is not 100 percent accurate after the iOS update. But it still shows which campaign, ad set and ad a sale came from.

Paid multi-touch attribution tools

2 min

Tools and what they do:

  • Paid multi-touch attribution tools include Triple Whale, Northbeam, Cometly, Converge and Trackify. They track customer journeys across Facebook, TikTok, Google, email and SMS. Triple Whale also shows platform ROAS (return on ad spend, meaning sales divided by ad spend) next to its own ROAS.
  • Triple Whale's dashboard lists campaigns in a layout similar to Facebook Ads Manager. It shows spend and ROAS per campaign. Platform ROAS and the tool's ROAS can be compared side by side on each row. Any gap is easy to see.
  • Triple Whale is better for attribution. Its AI on a higher paid plan is strong. Use TrueProfit when your main goal is profit tracking. Use Triple Whale when your main goal is attribution.
  • Google Analytics custom report setup is free. Paid attribution tools are usually expensive. Google Analytics with a properly installed tag (tracking code) is still the standard for high-ticket dropshipping (selling expensive items you do not stock) in most cases. **

Example of platform versus tool ROAS:

  • A Facebook campaign with 244 USD spend showed a ROAS of 0.84 in Facebook Ads Manager. Triple Whale showed 1.30 for the same campaign. Another campaign showed 1.22 on Facebook and 2.75 in Triple Whale. A campaign that looks unprofitable on the platform can be profitable once tracking is corrected. That changes the kill-or-scale decision.
  • Simple case: an account spends 200 USD on Facebook ads and earns 400 USD in sales, so it is profitable. Without campaign or ad set level tracking, the owner cannot tell which ad set made the sales. So they cannot decide which one to scale.

Platform versus analytics reconciliation

4 min

Three data layers and their jobs:

  • Three data layers answer three different questions. A data layer is one tool that reports numbers. The store platform (the system that runs your online shop) or accounting software (for example Shopify, Xero, QuickBooks) shows total ad spend against total revenue. Google Ads shows campaign-level return on ad spend (ROAS: how many sales come back for each dollar spent). Google Analytics compares traffic channels with each other. A traffic channel is a source of visitors, such as search, social media or email. Each layer has its own job. Do not use one for the others.
  • Use the accounting or store platform to judge overall advertising efficiency. That means total ad spend compared with total revenue.
  • Use Google Analytics to compare traffic sources with each other. Use Google Ads to improve Google Ads campaigns. Treat the revenue number of one source as a comparison figure, not as its exact true value.

Worked gap example:

  • In the example store, for the same period, Google Analytics reported 376,593 USD in revenue and Google Ads reported 552,255 USD. The gap was about 175,000 USD, or almost 46 percent of the Analytics figure. A difference like this is normal, because each tool tracks conversions differently. A conversion is a completed goal, such as a purchase.
  • When you compare revenue between Google Analytics and Google Ads, check the exact same date range. Different periods alone can create a large gap before any tracking difference is considered.
  • A shopper may click one ad on one day and buy days later through another ad or channel. The earlier click often is not recorded correctly. This missing credit is the most likely reason for differences between platforms in active campaigns (campaigns that are running now). When you compare data levels, always consider assisted conversions first. An assisted conversion is a sale that an earlier click helped create, even though a different click got the final credit.

Multi-Channel Funnels and conversion paths:

  • Google Analytics Multi-Channel Funnels (a report that shows the order of clicks before a sale) and attribution reports (reports that decide which click gets credit for a sale) show the different sequences of touchpoints that lead to a conversion. A touchpoint is any point where a shopper meets your ad or site. These reports try to close some data gaps, but information is still lost.
  • In the 30-day example, the store made 1,670,000 USD in revenue from 361 conversions. In the Multi-Channel Funnels view used, 209 of these conversions (about 60 percent) were multi-step, multi-click conversions. "Multi-click" means the buyer clicked several times before buying. The Multi-Channel Funnels report only showed conversions with more than two steps.
  • The author estimated, without data to back it up, that the true share of multi-click conversions in that store was probably closer to 70 percent. Treat this as an unverified guess.
  • Conversion paths labeled "unavailable" mean the data is missing. In the example, "unavailable" was the top conversion source and the seventh, appearing 142 times in the list. Do not read these entries as a real traffic channel.
  • To count "unavailable" entries in the Multi-Channel Funnels conversion path list, set the list to show 250 rows and search for "unavailable". The number of matches shows how much data is missing in that list.
  • Google Analytics is typically more accurate than Google Ads for conversion reporting. It uses a different tracking method and better recognizes when one purchase results from several earlier visits. (Older guidance, 2021-22.)

Google Ads revenue and Analytics choice:

  • The Google and YouTube analytics integration attributes sales to their source. Attributing means giving credit for a sale to the place it came from. Sources include Meta (Facebook and Instagram ads), organic Google (free search results, not paid ads), alpha campaign or beta campaign. Use these attributions to judge whether each channel is profitable.
  • Google Analytics compares traffic channels. Google Ads reports campaign ROAS. Use the two only for their own job, as set out above.

Meta reporting checks:

  • Meta's Purchase ROAS = attributed purchase revenue divided by amount spent. Meta's value is approximate. It is based on purchases credited to the ads. This includes sales through Shops, Marketplace, Pages or Messenger (Meta's shop, marketplace, page and chat features). Some values may be estimated by third-party technologies (tools made by other companies).
  • Meta shows a notice about changed attribution reporting for clicks and engagement results. Engagement means likes, comments and shares. Check the "About this update" notice before comparing results across time periods.
  • Default Meta ad-set attribution and reconciliation: use the account's own store data as the reference, as set out in the iOS (Apple's phone system) and tool sections above. An ad set is a group of ads inside one campaign.

Offline sales, phone calls and keyed responses

2 min

Google Ads call tracking:

  • Offline sales are sales made outside the website, such as by phone. Set up call tracking in Google Ads (a way to see which ads lead to phone calls) when you receive more than 30 calls per month. This helps you understand their source and performance.
  • Calls shorter than 20 seconds do not count as conversions. This filters out very brief or incomplete calls.
  • Each website visitor is given a unique tracking phone number. When the same person returns to the site, they see the same number. This lets their actions be credited to their original traffic source.
  • Google shows each visitor a forwarding number (a stand-in number that passes calls to your real line) in place of your actual phone number. When a visitor dials the forwarding number, Google immediately routes the call to your real number and records it as a conversion.
  • Google Ads can import offline conversions, such as sales made by phone. This means phone-based sales are counted alongside online conversions in campaign results.

Phone sale attribution procedure (Google Ads):

  1. Store each customer's contact details in a CRM tool (customer relationship software, a customer database) such as Klaviyo or Wicked Reports. This links the customer to their web activity when they call.
  2. Look up the caller in the CRM to see their click history.
  3. Confirm from the tagged links (web links with tracking tags added) that the customer came from a Google ad.
  4. Upload the offline sale with its click data in the offline conversions section of Google Ads, so the sale is attributed to the campaign.

The process is cumbersome but gives Google the data it needs.

Keyed and coupon-based response tracking:

  • A keyed response is a reply traced to one specific ad, for example through its own phone number, code or coupon. Advertisers without tracking often pay two to thirty-five times what their sales should cost, because they never see which ads produce the cheapest results. Keyed comparison can reveal cost gaps of several times between ads for the same product.
  • Changing ad size, pictures or type without a traced comparison often raises cost per reply (how much you spend for each reply) while adding no extra sales. A bigger or more decorative ad must be proven to bring proportionally more sales.
  • Reply cost depends on how a coupon is redeemed (used). In one test, mailed sample requests cost about 70 cents each. The same ads with coupons presented at a local store produced inquiries for 18 to 22 cents each.

Store display test procedure:

  1. Choose two comparable towns.
  2. Run the window or store display (a product shown in a shop window or in the store) in one town only.
  3. Compare total product sales in both towns, not only the sales of the display store.
  4. Keep the display spend only if total sales rise.

Platform reporting quirks and delays

1 min
  • TikTok's displayed spend in the ad manager (the dashboard where you see ad costs) is lower than the true spend. It also updates a minute or two late. A campaign showing $50 spent may have actually spent $60 to $80. So check and control payment manually (by hand).
  • Pinterest (an image-sharing site with ads) results take six to seven days to appear. That is slower than TikTok or Facebook. UTM parameters (short tags added to a web link that show where a visit came from) on the destination URL (the web address people land on after clicking an ad) are the main way to track sales by campaign. Sales can appear with a delay because the platform records them slowly.
  • Pinterest's tracking delay is a platform recording issue. It is not a loss of sales. UTM parameters show the source of each sale.

Campaign-level UTM use for budget decisions

1 min
  • Use UTM parameters to decide which ad set or campaign deserves more budget within a country. Do not rely on country-level data alone.
  • Sellers can compare campaigns across several accounts by reading UTM data. They do not need to link the pixel (a small piece of tracking code on your site) to each account.

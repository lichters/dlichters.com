Playbook › 19. Metrics, finance and tracking

Email, retargeting and owned-channel metrics

Section 19.14 · 14 of 23 in this chapter · 23 min read

In short

  • Keep UTM tracking on and Smart Sending off for every email, so results are measured the same way everywhere.
  • Track earnings per subscriber (money per person on your email list). About $2 per subscriber per month is already twice a typical list.
  • A working email system earns about $1 per name on your list per month.
  • Check each email by its own numbers, not the whole series. Compare the two branches side by side before you change anything.
  • Log every abandoned checkout in one sheet and call within 5 minutes. Calling that fast gives 21 times the chance of a qualified lead.
More key points
  • Don't use the 500,000 leads figure. It is an unverified estimate.
  • Facebook's numbers are not the whole story. Measure revenue in Klaviyo (an email tool) using a cold-traffic segment (people who don't know the brand yet).
  • Done right, return on ad spend (money earned per dollar spent) is about double the default figure.
  • Name every giveaway list the same way. Then you can remove earlier entrants later.
  • Track baseline numbers (your starting point) to see if content and email really work.

Email setup and tracking settings

1 min
  • Shopify Messaging (Shopify's built-in email service) includes 10,000 free emails per month. Extra emails cost $1 per 1,000.
  • Keep UTM tracking on (tags added to links that show where each visit came from) for every email in every flow and campaign. A flow is an automatic email series. A campaign is a one-time send.
  • Keep Smart Sending off (a setting that can hold back an email if the person got another one recently) for every email in every flow and campaign. This way, email traffic and sales are tracked the same way, and every send follows the schedule set in the flow or campaign.
  • Consolidate customer data for retention (keeping customers buying again), loyalty (rewards for repeat buyers) and analytics (measuring results) into one view. Many separate tools make it hard to know what is working and how to improve it.

Email revenue and earnings per subscriber

1 min
  • Earnings per subscriber (money each person on your list brings in) = monthly email revenue ÷ active subscribers. A subscriber is someone on your email list. Active subscribers are those who still engage.
  • Example: a list earning $70,000 to $130,000 per month with about 6,000 subscribers returns roughly $11 to $22 per subscriber per month.
  • An average list is closer to $1 per subscriber per month.
  • Track earnings per subscriber and compare it with the average for your market. About $2 per subscriber per month is already twice a typical list.
  • Rule of thumb when communication funnels run correctly (a funnel is a set of emails that leads people toward buying): about $1 of profit per month per name on the list.
  • Examples of that rule: 200 names ≈ $200/month, 1,000 names ≈ $1,000/month, 10,000 names ≈ $10,000/month.
  • Some markets earn more than this rule of thumb.
  • A list that grows without offers (a deal or product pitch) attached to its emails produces no revenue. Track the subscriber count and revenue per subscriber.
  • Also measure opens (how many people open an email) and clicks (how many click a link in it) alongside sales. This shows which types of story work.
  • ** Judge email revenue against spend. Email is commonly judged by revenue returned per dollar invested (money back for each dollar spent).
  • ** Track results per email, so optimisation (making things better) decisions rest on data.

Lead value

1 min
  • A lead is a person who has given you their contact details. A well-run lead generation machine (a setup that collects leads) should earn about $2 per lead per month through email follow-up in the first year.
  • The source range for this figure is printed as "$2-$2".
  • A correctly running lead generation system earns about $2 to $5 per lead per month on average.
  • Example: 1,000 leads emailed twice a week should produce roughly $2,000 to $5,000 in email revenue per month.
  • Use this as the baseline (starting point) to judge the system against.
  • Use the $2 figure as the early target for the first year.
  • Use the $2 to $5 range as the baseline for a mature system (one that has been running for a while).

Sign-up rates and welcome-flow benchmarks

1 min
  • With a sign-up pop-up (a box that asks visitors for their email), expect roughly 8 to 15 newsletter sign-ups per 100 visitors.
  • Plan the email list size with this range.
  • Average email open rates are about 20 to 30%.
  • SMS (text messages) open rates are about 60 to 80%.
  • SMS converts better (turns more visitors into buyers), but it is harder to collect. Phone numbers are harder to get than emails, and compliance rules (legal rules for texting) apply.
  • Case: one test store, about two months old, from 23 November to 23 December.
    • Total sales were about $17,000.
    • 28% of revenue came from Klaviyo email (Klaviyo is an email tool). It was shown as 15% from flows and 14% from campaigns.
    • In the last 30 days the store made about $14,000, with about 24% from Klaviyo.
    • Of the roughly $2,000 from flows, about $1,447 came from the welcome flow (the automatic emails new subscribers get first).
    • Only the basic flows existed: welcome and abandoned checkout. An abandoned checkout is a shopper who left before paying.
  • Basic welcome and abandoned checkout flows were estimated to add about 20 to 30% to a store's revenue.
  • This is an estimate from one case, not a guaranteed result.
  • In Klaviyo's flow list, use the Revenue and Revenue per recipient (money per person who received the email) columns.
  • Use them to decide which welcome flow to clone (copy). The flow with the highest revenue is the proven base for a new welcome series.

Flow and campaign metrics

1 min
  • Klaviyo flow analytics (the reports for automatic emails) show: Waiting (people still on their way to the email), Delivered (emails that arrived), Skipped (people who did not get it), Open Rate, Click Rate, Placed Order Rate (share of people who bought after the email) and Revenue per email.
  • The time windows are 'Analytics (30 days)' and 'Last 365 days'.
  • The 30-day view suits recent tests.
  • Judge each flow email by its own open rate, click rate, placed-order rate and revenue. Do not judge by the flow total. This makes weak messages visible.
  • A path is one of the branches of a flow. Compare path 1 and path 2 email results side by side before changing anything.
  • A 9.5% click rate on a pain-point abandoned cart email (an email that names the customer's problem) is a very good result.
  • This 9.5% is a useful benchmark (reference number) for that email type.

A/B testing and confidence

1 min
  • An A/B test shows two versions to split groups to see which works better. A variant is one version.
  • In a completed subject line test (two email titles compared) on the offer email, the winning variant had an open rate about 19.8% higher.
  • The winner was chosen manually, meaning by a person.
  • Confidence levels by sample size per variant (how many people saw each version):
Sample size per variant Confidence
Around 1,000 About 80%
Larger samples About 95%
Very large samples About 98%
  • Confidence is the likelihood that a change judged positive really is positive.

Email frequency testing

1 min
  • ** Sending too many emails raises unsubscribes (people leaving your list). Sending too few raises them too.
  • ** If people unsubscribe or mark emails as spam (report them as junk) while receiving one email per month or less, they have likely forgotten the brand. They see the email as unfamiliar.
  • ** A steady decline in open rate can indicate too many emails and subscribers becoming blind to the sender.
  • ** Check click-through rate (share of people who click a link) and email conversion rate (share of people who buy) next. Track email performance in Google Analytics (Google's free website statistics tool).
  • Older procedure (2021-22): How to test email frequency:
    1. Start with 4 automated emails per week for days 0 to 30, 2 to 3 per week for days 30 to 60, and 1 per week from day 60.
    2. Increase frequency gradually rather than all at once.
    3. After each change, check unsubscribes first. If they rise, reduce frequency.
    4. Check open rate over time. A steady decline suggests too many emails are being sent.
    5. Check click-through rate and email conversion rate in Google Analytics.
    6. Keep experimenting until results plateau (stop improving). This shows how far frequency can be pushed.

Deliverability baselines and list health

2 min
  • Deliverability means whether your emails actually land in people's inboxes.
  • Before leaving the old provider (the email service you are moving away from), record a deliverability baseline (your starting numbers). Record open rate, click rate, bounce rate (emails that failed to arrive), unsubscribe rate and spam complaints over 30, 60 and 90 days.
  • Use these figures as the minimum expectation for the new provider.
  • Keep the deliverability target for the affected provider (a mail service such as Yahoo that is hurting delivery) above a 30% open rate.
  • Once it stays above that level, begin reducing the exclusion (the pause on sending to a group).
  • The example used a Yahoo segment (a group of contacts with Yahoo addresses) of 135 unengaged profiles (contacts who never open emails) that were not landing in inboxes.
  • Stop sending to a contact segment when its open rate falls to around 15% and its click rate falls to around 0.5%.
  • Read the thresholds together:
    • 30% open is the recovery target for an affected provider.
    • 15% open with 0.5% click is the stop-sending point.
    • 20 to 30% is the typical average open rate.
  • A campaign with about 40% open rate and about 50 clicks was treated as healthy in one example.
  • Good overall results can still hide a sharp drop in a single age bucket (a group of contacts sorted by age). Check the buckets too.
  • After a large send (sending to many people at once), an unsubscribe rate higher than usual can still be acceptable. This is true if it stays within your normal margins.
  • Check it against the provider breakdown before changing sending.
  • Audience breakdown totals can exceed campaign totals. A recipient in several lists or segments is counted once for each membership (each list they belong to).
  • Do not add up segment numbers to get campaign totals.
  • Open rate for an audience bucket = recipients who opened ÷ total recipients in that bucket.
  • Example: 141 opens out of 430 recipients gives 32.8%.
  • Bounce rate = bounced recipients ÷ sent recipients. Delivered = sent − bounced.
  • Example: in a send of 999 recipients with 2 bounces, the bounce rate was 0.20%.
  • In that same example, 997 recipients (99.8%) were delivered.
  • The Performance by inbox provider chart (inbox provider means the mail company, such as Gmail or Yahoo) on a campaign's Deliverability tab lets you hide either the Open rate or the Click rate line.
  • Comparing one metric at a time across providers makes one weak provider easier to spot.
  • Read open rate and click rate together for each age bucket. A bucket with lower opens can still have strong clicks.
  • In one example, the 31 to 60 day bucket fell to about 40% opens but kept a good click rate. This suggests the people still opening remained engaged.

Customer satisfaction and surveys

2 min
  • Net Promoter Score (NPS, a score for how likely customers are to recommend you) is the best metric alongside review ratings for measuring how much customers love you.
  • The NPS survey question is: "How likely are you to recommend to friends and family?"
  • CSAT (customer satisfaction score) formula: (number of "very satisfied" + "satisfied" responses) ÷ total survey responses × 100, giving a percentage.
  • Neutral, dissatisfied and very dissatisfied answers stay in the denominator (the bottom number of the division) and count against the score.
  • CSAT options are typically a five-point scale: very satisfied, satisfied, neutral, dissatisfied, very dissatisfied.
  • The survey usually asks one question: how satisfied the customer is with the support received.
  • CSAT ratings below 4 (on a 5-point scale) are the most valuable negative feedback to review. They show what blocks a high satisfaction score.
  • Some reviewers also look at ratings under 5 as a broader signal.
  • Thematic analysis of CSAT text responses (sorting written comments into recurring topics):
    1. Export all CSAT scores as a CSV file (a spreadsheet file) from the helpdesk (the customer support software).
    2. Upload the file to a thematic analysis tool based on natural language processing (software that reads written text), such as Thematic.
    3. Review the resulting keywords, pain points and recurring themes.
    4. Choose one or two specific themes to focus on at a time.
  • The Gorgias Satisfaction view (Gorgias is a helpdesk tool) lists every ticket (a customer support request) rated 4 stars or lower.
  • This is wider than the below-4 threshold used for analysis.
  • Filter to ratings under 4 before deciding which tickets to review. Otherwise the review includes neutral-leaning feedback that does not point to a clear problem.
  • Post-purchase surveys (questionnaires sent after buying) that ask why the customer bought and what they will use the product for reveal the real audience.
  • These answers can feed Facebook interest targeting (choosing ad audiences by interests).
  • ** Send automated customer feedback surveys using recommended tools. Set up conditional thank-you emails (sent depending on the answer) for survey completers (people who finished the survey). Decide on a reward structure for participation.
  • ** Track the share of customers who leave a review as a key metric. Compare it before and after starting the post-sale call program (phone calls after purchase).

Abandoned-checkout tracking sheet

1 min
  • Logging every abandoned checkout in a shared sheet (a spreadsheet everyone can see) shows how many carts exist. It shows when they were contacted, what happened on each call and how many were recovered.
  • Shopify's default abandoned checkout view only shows whether an email was sent and whether the cart was recovered.
  • That default view hides the reasons for lost sales.
  • Tracking sheet layout (headers in row 1, which are the column titles; row 2 contains only dashes as a separator):
Column Field Filled by
A Date automation
B Name automation
C Phone number automation
D Email automation
E Cart value automation
F Products automation
G Shipping automation
H Recovered paid-order automation or team member
I Link to cart automation
J Notes phone team
  • Columns can be added or removed, but these ten cover the core needs of a tracking sheet.
  • An example sheet also includes a column showing whether the customer was emailed. The standard automated column set does not include this.
  • This status column shows which carts received outreach (contact) before a call was made.
  • Review the sheet once a day or every few days. Check how many carts were abandoned, when outreach happened, what came of each contact, and how many were recovered.
  • Regular review makes gaps in the process visible early.
  • Reviewing the sheet after several weeks reveals causes of low recovery. Examples: slow response times, not leaving voicemails (recorded phone messages), or customers citing high prices.
  • Each cause points to a specific process change.

Retargeting ROAS and conversion benchmarks

2 min
  • Retargeting means showing ads again to people who already visited your store. Cold traffic means people who have never visited. ROAS means return on ad spend (money earned for each dollar spent on ads).
  • Retargeting achieves 2 to 10 times higher conversion rates (share of visitors who buy) than cold traffic.
  • This is because users who have already interacted with the site are further along in the purchase journey.
  • The top end for direct-response e-commerce conversion (sales from ads that ask for a direct purchase) is described as 2 to 3%.
  • Most visitors who are interested but do not buy can be recovered by retargeting, for example via Google.
  • Retargeting makes it reasonable to keep an ad with low attention and click-through rate (share of people who click the ad) running.
  • The reason: the cold traffic it brings can later be retargeted as a larger audience.
  • Meta (the company behind Facebook and Instagram) remarketing campaigns should deliver the highest ROAS of the ad channels used.
  • This held even after Apple's iOS 14.5 update (an Apple privacy update). Small budgets aimed at highly qualified visitors produce a very high return on ad spend.
  • Judge remarketing budgets by ROAS.
  • ** Facebook retargeting below 1x ROAS (1x means you get back exactly what you spent) is the threshold for cutting spend.
  • ** A Facebook remarketing campaign should reach at least 10x return on ad spend.
  • ** 20x to 50x is typical for strong campaigns.
  • ** Below about 20x points to audiences that are too broad, or to frequency (how often one person sees the ad) that is too high.
  • Use the 1x threshold to decide when to cut spend.
  • Use the 10x minimum to judge whether a campaign is performing.
  • Allocate roughly 10% of your daily Shopping Ads budget (Google's product ads) to Google Display remarketing (ads shown on other websites to past visitors).
  • Example: a $10/day Shopping budget gives $2/day for remarketing.
  • ** Facebook DPA example (DPA means dynamic product ads, which show the exact products someone viewed): $100 on Google Shopping versus about $10 on DPA remarketing. Remarketing is a smaller share of spend early on.
  • Shopify notifies Meta when someone buys on the store. That buyer is added to the purchase audience (a saved group of people for ads).
  • The 7-day purchase audience always holds buyers from the last 7 days. The same logic applies to the 30- and 180-day versions.

Promotion budgets for warm audiences

2 min
  • Warm audiences are people who already know your brand, such as visitors and people who engaged with your pages.
  • Audience budget formula: Audience budget = audience size × frequency × remarketing CPM ÷ 1,000.
  • CPM means cost per 1,000 impressions (1,000 views of an ad). The result is the total spend needed to show the ads that many times to that many people.
  • Example, Google: 50,000 people × 6 impressions × $1.50 ÷ 1,000 = $450 total campaign budget, about $150 per day over 3 days.
  • Example, Facebook: 130,000 people (Facebook website visitors, Instagram and Facebook engagement audiences combined) × 6 impressions × $5 ÷ 1,000 = $3,900 total campaign budget, about $1,300 per day over 3 days.
  • Worked budget example (180-day visitor audiences, frequency 6, 3-day promotion):
Audience Size Frequency Remarketing CPM (historic) Budget
Google: Website Visitors - 180 Days 50,000 6 $1.50 $450
Google total 50,000 6 $1.50 $450
Facebook: Website Visitors - 180 Days 50,000 6 $5.00 -
Facebook: Insta Engagement - 180 Days 40,000 6 $5.00 -
Facebook: FB Engagement - 180 Days 40,000 6 $5.00 -
Facebook total 130,000 6 $5.00 $3,900
  • Daily budgets for this 3-day promotion: Google $150/day and Facebook $1,300/day.
  • ROAS means return on ad spend. Budgeting is done to get the highest ROAS possible.
  • Frequency is the average number of times each person sees the ad.
  • CPM is the cost per 1,000 impressions.
  • Calculating the ad budget for a promotion:
    1. Count each warm audience. In Facebook Audience Manager, read the size of the 180-day website visitor, Facebook engagement and Instagram engagement audiences. In Google, open the shared library of audiences.
    2. Set a frequency target, typically 6 to 10 impressions per person for a 3 to 5 day campaign.
    3. Look up the historic CPM (the cost per 1,000 views from past campaigns) for warm audiences in the Facebook Ads and Google Ads accounts, using the CPM column.
    4. Apply the formula for each platform and set the budget.
  • For a 3 to 5 day promotion, aim for a frequency of about 6 to 10. That means each person sees the ad 6 to 10 times.
  • This is the planning figure for budgeting.
  • Historic CPM for warm audiences was roughly $2 to $5 in the example account. It was $1.50 on Google and $5 on Facebook.
  • Use the actual CPM from your own accounts, not a planning assumption of about $10. The $10 figure is set high on purpose.
  • Set promotion campaigns with a total campaign budget and a start and end date, not a daily budget.
  • Spend usually starts slightly lower on the first day. It ramps up (increases) toward the end as the platform gathers data.
  • For a first test with a limited budget, use 7-day or 30-day audiences instead of 180-day audiences. At least 30-day audiences are recommended.
  • In the example, 7-day audiences on both platforms came to about $78 in total.
  • Measuring promotion impact:
    1. Pull the tracked promotion revenue from Klaviyo, Facebook and Google.
    2. Compare the combined total with the total revenue for the same month. This shows the promotion share of sales.
    3. Check return on ad spend per platform to see whether the ad spend was efficient.

Google Analytics audiences

3 min
  • Google Analytics is Google's free website statistics tool. An audience is a saved group of visitors.
  • Google Analytics audiences for remarketing should be built from normal traffic.
  • The audience pool grows from qualified visitors (real, interested people) rather than artificial numbers (fake or inflated visits).
  • When creating Google Analytics audiences, enable remarketing in the audience definition.
  • Otherwise the audience cannot be used for remarketing on Google properties (Google sites and apps that show ads).
  • In Google Analytics, a purchaser audience uses the condition transactions greater than 0.
  • It works only when enhanced e-commerce tracking (extra order data sent from the store) is active.
  • It is typically built as a 7-day and a 180-day list.
  • Google Analytics can compare revenue and conversion rate of first-time visitors against returning visitors.
  • This shows how much retargeting can lift overall conversion.
  • In one example, returning visitors who came back more than once converted at a rate 50% higher than visitors who came only once.
  • Build a customer avatar (a profile of your typical buyer) in Facebook Analytics:
    1. Open the newer Facebook Analytics, which offers segments (groups of people) based on user actions on your site.
    2. Create a segment of people who purchased in the past 28 days or the past 90 days.
    3. Review the page likes and demographics (basic facts such as age and gender) of that purchaser segment.
    4. Use those results to describe your customer avatar.
  • Older procedure (2021-22): Create Google Analytics audiences and send them to Google Ads:
    1. Go to Admin > Property Settings > Audience Definitions > Audiences.
    2. If remarketing is not enabled yet, click Enable remarketing, then Next step.
    3. Under Audience destinations, select your Google Ads account as the destination before saving. This imports the audiences into Google Ads as targeting options.
    4. Ignore the default All Users audience.
    5. Click + New audience, then Create new.
    6. Build the conditions in the Audience Builder.
    7. Name the audience and click Next step. If Google Ads is linked, select it as the destination and send the audience there.
  • ** In the example Google Analytics audience list, the All Users audience has a membership duration of 30 days. Membership duration is how many days a visitor stays in the audience.
  • ** Audience Builder (the tool that creates audiences) categories in Google Analytics: Demographics (age 18-24, 25-34, 35-44, 45-54, 55-64, 65+; gender female, male, unknown; language), Technology, Behaviour, Date of First Session, Traffic Sources, Enhanced E-commerce, and Other Category (Affinity Category, In-Market Segment, Location).
  • Affinity Category means long-term interests. In-Market Segment means people actively looking to buy.
  • ** Location targeting under Other Category > Location lets you segment by city, region, country, subcontinent or continent.
  • Use location targeting for offers limited to certain areas, such as free shipping only where you can afford it.
  • ** Behaviour options: Sessions, Days Since Last Session, Transactions per user, and Session Duration per user. A session is one visit.
  • Example: an audience of users with more than three sessions on the website.
  • ** Session Duration is set per session, for example Session Duration > 30 seconds.
  • Conditions can be layered with AND (both must be true) or OR (either can be true).
  • Date of First Session can limit the audience to visitors since, before, after or between dates.
  • ** Enhanced E-commerce audience conditions include revenue per user, amount spent, products bought, brand and variant (a version of a product, such as a size or colour).
  • With enough volume, you can build an audience of people who bought one product and offer them relevant upsells (a bigger or extra product offer).
  • ** To target by page, change the condition dropdown from its default to Page, then choose Behaviour.
  • Use contains with part of the URL (the web address). For Shopify product pages, enter products, or enter the product name slug (the readable part of the web address) after the last slash for one specific product.

Giveaway measurement

3 min

  • A funnel is the set of steps a visitor takes, from ad to sale.

  • The funnel method was reported to generate about 500,000 leads. A lead is a person who gives their contact details, here an email address.

  • This figure is an unverified estimate. It was not supported with data.

  • Facebook ad set results from the example giveaway campaign (values as shown). An ad set is a group of ads with one audience and one budget.

  • What the Facebook terms mean:

    • Reach is how many different people saw the ad.
    • Frequency is how often each person saw it, on average.
    • Cost per result is the money spent for each action Facebook counts.
    • Amount spent is the total money spent.
    • Impressions are the total times the ad was shown.
    • CPM is the cost per 1,000 impressions.
    • Link clicks are clicks that took people to the website.

    • CTR (click-through rate) is the share of viewers who clicked.
    • Website purchases are the sales Facebook counted.

  • Klaviyo is an email marketing tool. Its results for the same giveaway:

    Klaviyo metric Value
    Leads 18,726

  • What the Klaviyo terms mean:

    • Net new sales are the new sales Klaviyo counts.
    • A 30-day window is the time limit: sales count only within 30 days.
    • Revenue is the money from those sales.

  • Facebook reported about $40,000 in revenue for the giveaway ad set. Klaviyo's 30-day revenue was $16,715.70.

  • Klaviyo's default view (the standard report you see first) undercounts. Email-driven purchases arrive with a delay, so some sales come after the report counts them.

  • Purchases from email flows (automatic email series) may not track straight away. Real revenue from giveaway traffic can be about double what Klaviyo reports by default.

  • Measuring only cold traffic reveals this hidden revenue.

  • Measure true giveaway revenue in Klaviyo. A segment is a saved group of people, filtered by rules. Follow these steps:

    1. Build a segment of people who were added to the giveaway list, filtered by date added.
    2. Keep only people who had not placed an order before the giveaway. This removes past customers the brand already knows.
    3. Keep only people who placed at least one order after entering the giveaway. Non-buyers generate no revenue.
    4. Exclude everyone who appears on the lists of earlier giveaways and campaigns. Exclude means remove from the group.
    5. Read the revenue and net new sales of this segment. They measure absolute cold traffic.

  • When giveaway revenue is measured correctly with cold-traffic segments, return on ad spend (ROAS) is about double the default figure. Cost per purchase is about half.

  • Return on ad spend from the same spend, two ways. ≈ means "about equal to."

    • Platform-reported ROAS = $39,535.20 ÷ $10,832.32 ≈ 3.65
    • Klaviyo 30-day ROAS = $16,715.70 ÷ $10,832.32 ≈ 1.54
    • Cost per Klaviyo lead = $10,832.32 ÷ 18,726 ≈ $0.58, if every entrant counts as a lead

  • Meta is the company behind Facebook and Instagram ads. Meta counted 306 website purchases.

  • Klaviyo's 30-day window showed only 145 net new sales. That is about 47% of the Meta count.

  • Klaviyo revenue since the giveaway ($108,424.26) was about 6.5 times its 30-day revenue ($16,715.70).

  • So the attribution window (the rule for crediting a sale to a source, and how long it counts) changes the reported result a lot.

  • Many advertisers abandon instant giveaway funnels. They only read the default platform numbers and cannot see the real results.

  • Measure revenue through the email platform with a cold-traffic segment before judging the funnel.

  • Name every giveaway audience list in Klaviyo with country, campaign sequence number, year and product. An example is "UK 1 2023 Product Giveaway."

  • Consistent names make it possible to build exclusion segments. These remove earlier campaign entrants later.

Owned-channel baselines

1 min
  • Owned channels are places you control, such as your website and email list. They are not paid ads.
  • A baseline is the starting number you compare future results against.
  • Track baseline numbers for organic traffic (free visitors from search and posts) in analytics (the tool that counts website visitors).
  • Also track opt-ins per day or week. An opt-in is a person who agrees to receive emails.
  • Also track remarketing ROAS (ads shown to people who already visited or bought) and units sold.
  • Then monitor how these numbers grow over time. They show whether content and email work.

Playbook › 2. Niche and product research

Specialty product types: books, digital products, print-on-Demand and gadgets

Section 2.20 · 20 of 24 in this chapter · 26 min read

In short

  • Set the research filters in the given order and keep revenue at 0 to 10K. Larger ranges show too many products.
  • A good book has a unique method, a strong parent worry, and proof of sales, such as an old book or a famous author.
  • Avoid risky areas for beginners: legal, tax, finance, trading, food, emotional-intelligence and psychology books, and supplements.
  • Never copy another seller's ad or ebook. Make your own version.
  • Pick a problem people already pay to solve, then study the sellers who already sell to it.
More key points
  • Test before you build: sell at 19 USD first, and write the ebook only after people pay.
  • Set your price so you still keep money after ad costs.
  • Personalized products need more work and shipping care, so beginners should start simpler.

Kalodata book research: filter setup

1 min

Kalodata is a sales-tracking tool. Use it to find products that are selling.

The base filter set for US book research is shown below.

Filter Value
Dates Last 30 Days
Category Books, Magazines & Audio
Revenue(S) 0-10000
Revenue Source (Content) Video
Revenue Growth Rate >30%
Avg. Unit Price($) 30-100
Region United States

Set the filters in this order:

  1. Open the product search in Kalodata and set Country to United States.
  2. Set Category to Books, Magazines & Audio. Choose the top-level category, not the subcategories.
  3. Set Dates to Last 30 Days.
  4. Set the Revenue ($) filter to 0 to 10K. A 0 to 100K range gives too many products, so it is not used as the working range.
  5. Set Revenue Source (Content) to Video.
  6. Set Revenue Growth Rate to 30% or more.
  7. Set Avg. Unit Price ($) to 30 to 100. This is the average price per item.
  8. Leave all other filters untouched. This includes launch date, commission rate (the cut the platform takes from each sale), creator number and similar settings.
  9. Click Submit to apply the filters. Then click Save selected filters to keep the setup for reuse.
  10. Scan the results. Discard products in the excluded categories, bundles and multi-book listings. Then check the revenue trend and the highest-revenue videos. Validate the candidates with Reddit, Amazon and emotion valence research. Emotion valence means whether people feel positive or negative about a topic.

Revenue range: which value applies when

1 min

The guidance gives different revenue ranges. Each one applies to a different step.

Range Where it applies Source
0 to 10K (0-10000) Filter setting in the base set and in the step-by-step procedure ,
0 to 100K Not used as the working range; returns too many products
About 0 to 8K to 10K Practical working range; winners in this filter are typically low-revenue products, often below about $1,000 over the period
0 to 8K Sweet spot for manual selection
Up to 5K or 8K Only if the manual scan is still manageable; above these values the list grows too quickly to check every product

Revenue growth rate

1 min
  • A growth rate above 30% narrows results to products whose sales are rising fast.
  • One expert argued that a higher threshold also means higher margins, because costs are lower. Margin is the profit kept from each sale. This is not proven, so check unit economics (the profit on each single item after costs) before you act on it.
  • Growth rate alone does not decide a product. A book with a negative growth rate can still be a winner.
  • A trend that looked strong in the past may no longer hold. Check the current revenue trend. Treat it as a signal, not a guarantee.

Ranking screenshot workflow

1 min

Use this procedure to send Kalodata ranking pages to Claude. A ranking page is a list of products sorted by a number, such as revenue.

  1. In Kalodata, set the filters for the category, revenue range, growth rate and unit price.
  2. On Windows, press Windows + Shift + S (the screenshot shortcut). Drag a box around the ranking table.
  3. Switch to the Claude chat and paste the screenshot.
  4. Switch back to Kalodata and go to the next ranking page (page 2, 3, and so on). Repeat steps 2 to 3 for each page.
  5. Once you have sent about six pages, give Claude the research prompt.
  • Ranking screenshots can run past six pages. Capturing more pages gives the AI a fuller picture than a shortened sample.
  • Six pages is a time-saving shortcut, not a rule.
  • Zoom the browser out before you capture. This lets the full ranking table fit into one screenshot, including product titles and revenue columns.

Research prompt for ranking screenshots

1 min

Use this prompt unchanged after the screenshots are sent.

Hey Claude, find me a winning product that is in Kalodata. I am sending you screenshots of the Kalodata ranking with these filters: last 30 days, category Books, Magazines & Audio, revenue 0 to 10000, revenue source video, revenue growth rate above 30%, average unit price 30 to 100, region United States. Read up the demand metrics (revenue, revenue per day, revenue growth rate, items sold, launch date, creator count, creator conversion ratio, video revenue) and find which kids (genre) book has a unique mechanism and solves a specific pain point for parents and children. You may look into TikTok; I want it to be specifically organic content without paid ads. Check whether the book's author has large YouTube subscribers, or whether the book is an old book that has been in the industry for quite some time, and tell me which of these justifies its authority. Also look into the Meta Ad Library: find out whether any competitor is running the same niche title or the same book or author, and tell me how many paid Meta ads each competitor is running. Mark clearly which numbers come from my screenshots and which you could not verify, because you cannot query the Meta Ad Library directly. Then cook it for me and make me money: give me the product concept, the niche reasoning, and the next test steps.
  • Organic content means posts that are not paid ads.
  • The Meta Ad Library is the public list of ads running on Facebook and Instagram.
  • Before judging a niche in a ranking, remove unrelated genres such as romance, Bible and survival titles. They add noise.
  • Only then look at the remaining titles for real organic traction (steady sales from unpaid posts).

Book winner profile

1 min

A winning book candidate should meet several criteria at once.

  • A unique mechanism. This is the special method that makes the book different.
  • A specific, emotionally charged parent pain point. This is a clear, strong problem a parent wants solved.
  • Authority from a big author with large YouTube subscribers, or from an old book that has sold for many years.
  • Organic traction on TikTok without paid ads.

Authority can come from two sources:

  • An old book. This is one published many years ago, for example in the 1980s. Its long market presence builds trust with buyers.
  • A well-known author with a large YouTube audience.

The winner profile for books is an older title by an aged author with a long market history and proven validation. Strong signals are high YouTube subscriber counts in the niche and active Reddit discussion in the genre niche. Reddit is an online discussion forum.

For book niches, the strongest profile is an older title by an author born in the 1950s or 1960s (the baby boom generation), or in the 1970s or 1980s (late millennials). The author should have a long market history and proven validation. Their long-running sales are a demand signal. High YouTube subscriber counts in the niche add to it.

Pain point and mechanism

1 min
  • A strong parent pain point combines a visible problem with a deeper fear behind it.
  • For learn-to-read products, the visible problem is a child who guesses words. The deeper fear is that the parent will fail at teaching reading.
  • Decodable text is a useful mechanism. Decodable text uses only letter-sounds the child has already learned.
  • Because of this, the child reads rather than guesses.
  • Decodable text also makes the parent's job scripted. This means the parent follows set steps. No teaching expertise is needed, and the product gets a clear hook.
  • Understand what the author or the ad says and which problem the buyer wants solved, before you test a book.
  • The meaning and the audience matter more than the translation or the format of the product.
  • Judge each book on whether it solves a durable problem for a clear buyer.
  • Some books tied to specific US requirements stay in demand for years. Others are short-lived fads.
  • A book with a map or content tied to a region far from the target market is hard to sell.
  • Example: a map centered on the Middle East is difficult to sell to a US audience, unless the buyers want to travel there.

Niche tailwinds and sub-niches

1 min
  • A wider trend can strengthen demand for a niche. A tailwind is a trend that pushes sales forward.
  • In the learn-to-read example, US parents are rejecting whole-language reading methods (teaching by memorizing whole words) and looking for phonics (teaching by letter sounds). This is a tailwind for decodable products.
  • Children's books cover many sub-niches. A sub-niche is a smaller slice of a market.
  • The sub-niches are: parenting and family, learn-to-read, early activity cards, financial literacy workbooks, Bible stories, handwriting and writing, and emotional or puberty guidance.
  • Each sub-niche can be tested separately.
  • The same niche logic can apply to other book genres, such as gardening, construction or handyman DIY.
  • In the US, handyman and DIY books can have demand. Hiring a contractor is expensive, so people try repairs themselves when they can.
  • This reasoning comes from one expert and is specific to the US market.
  • Books in the kids, parenting, nursing, survival, independence and practical skills categories tend to sell well. They often appear among the stronger candidates.
  • Confirm each product with the revenue trend and sales data. Do not assume the category always works.
  • Books are a beginner-friendly niche. They are less crowded than supplements, beauty and pet niches.
  • Lower competition and cheaper CPC and CPM make test budgets less painful. CPC is the cost per click. CPM is the cost per 1,000 views.
  • Spanish-language books are an untapped category, because few sellers test them.
  • This is an opportunity for sellers who have translation and creative skills.

Book niches to avoid

1 min
  • Avoid legal, regulation and tax books.
  • Avoid financial and investment books, and trading or chart books. These niches do not convert well. Trading content is too subjective.
  • Financial books are specifically excluded.
  • Avoid food and beverage (F&B) books for beginners. They show high competition and did not perform in the tested cases.
  • Avoid EQ, emotional intelligence, psychology and inner-child books. EQ means emotional intelligence, the skill of reading and handling feelings. These niches did not perform in the tested cases.
  • Case: an EQ and psychology book was tested with about $1,000 in ad spend. It produced little or no profit, so the test was a net loss. Avoid this niche when the budget is small.
  • Example: a farming book and a plumbing book were tested and did not work. Some books in these categories do convert.
  • Testing a book in an unfamiliar subject can waste budget without a clear reason to expect demand.
  • Skip books sold as bundles. A bundle is several books sold as one.
  • Skip multi-book listings with different content. They are hard to run as a single product, even when the listing has high revenue.

Sourcing book candidates

2 min

Pull three product candidates in the same week, one from each source.

  1. Kalodata, filtered to Spanish. Filter by language, select Spanish, then find book ads that are already converting. "Converting" means they lead to sales.
  2. Library or Taobao data. Taobao is a large Chinese shopping site.
  3. Amazon or eBay bestsellers.
  • Besides Kalodata, product ideas can come from local libraries. Use the books with the highest borrow rates.
  • Taobao can also be used. Look for items with the highest purchase counts.
  • The bestseller lists on Amazon and eBay are another source.
  • Building an original product that follows a public method or blueprint avoids rights-holder risk. A rights holder owns the legal rights to the content.
  • An original product can stand on its own, instead of copying an existing book.

Spanish-language book workflow

  1. In Kalodata, filter products by language, for example Spanish.
  2. Find reading-book ads that already convert in that language.
  3. Convert the narration to English with an ElevenLabs voiceover swap. ElevenLabs is an AI voice tool. A voiceover swap replaces the spoken audio.
  4. Replace the book cover with an English version using GPT image editing. This is an AI tool for editing images.
  5. Convert the ad itself to English.

Taobao book workflow

  1. Open Taobao and search for books.
  2. Sort the results by the highest number of purchases.
  3. Rip the video and test it. To rip means to download and copy a video from another seller's ad.
  4. If you can produce custom ads, do that for a better result.

Amazon and eBay book workflow

  1. On Amazon and eBay, sort books by the highest sales count.
  2. If no VSL rips are available to pull from, build a custom VSL yourself. A VSL is a video sales letter, a long sales video.

Ripping ads: conflicting guidance

The guidance disagrees on copying competitor ads. Both points apply, depending on the step:

  • Ripping a video and testing it is described in the Taobao and Amazon/eBay workflows above.
  • Do not rip ads just for the sake of ripping. Understand the ICP and USP before you test. ICP means ideal customer profile, the type of buyer you want. USP means unique selling point, what makes your product different.
  • Kalodata is used to find the competitor ads that sellers of a product run. Copying those ads creates risk of DMCA takedown notices (US copyright removal requests) and competitor reports.
  • Custom covers and custom UGC avoid matching them. UGC means user-generated content, videos that look like real customers made them.

In practice, the guidance favours understanding the ICP and USP first. Then build custom ads when possible.

Books: running an uncustomized book

1 min
  • The default is to customize every book before selling it. Do not list the generic version as it is.
  • Customization can cover the title, cover, interior content and overall look.
  • Customizing books is a strongly recommended best practice, not a hard rule. The seller makes the final decision.
  • The recommended default is to customize each book you run.
  • Running a book straight off the shelf means you sell the same product as every other seller. This weakens your difference.
  • Skipping customization saves time, but that speed is lost if competitors undercut your identical listing.

Test-first approach for an uncustomized book:

  1. List the book unchanged to test demand quickly.
  2. If it sells, decide to customize it.
  3. Create a new, completely separate book listing with the changed title, cover or content. A customized version cannot simply replace the original.

The trade-off is speed now versus extra work later.

Supplements

2 min
  • Avoid the supplement category unless you have significant cash reserves.
  • Competitors in supplements generate $1M+ monthly revenue. They can use predatory tactics, which are unfair moves meant to hurt you.
  • These tactics include mass-buying your entire inventory on day one. They also include opening all units, which makes them unsellable.
  • Other tactics include posting 2,000 negative reviews, then returning everything for a refund. This erases your investment.
  • This risk is too high for new sellers with limited capital.
  • In niches where most advertisers have poor quality scores and low CTRs, the platform can penalize new advertisers from the start. A quality score is the ad platform's rating of your ad. CTR means click-through rate, the share of viewers who click. Supplements are one such niche.
  • Expect a weaker starting position and factor it into the budget.
  • Beginners should first test broad, mass-appeal products, such as gadgets.
  • Supplements and other complex niches should be tested only after you have cash flow and marketing experience.
  • Ingredient-led supplement products, such as ashwagandha, were skipped during screening. Such products need extra scrutiny before testing.

Supplement winner screening (after filters are set)

  1. Skip the first five result pages. Start the review at page 6.
  2. Skip products whose video ads are TikTok Shop-only. These depend on the TikTok Shop platform.
  3. Skip products whose ads are mostly in Spanish if you are a beginner. If you do run them, customize the landing pages and translate everything into Spanish. A landing page is the page a buyer reaches after clicking an ad.
  4. Check the Last 30 Days revenue graph. Prefer a curve that rises and falls with recovering peaks. Avoid a flat or steadily rising line.
  5. Open the Video & Ad list and check the mix of top-funnel, middle-funnel and bottom-funnel ads. Top-funnel ads create awareness. Middle-funnel ads make people consider. Bottom-funnel ads push people to buy.
  6. Skip the product if nearly all ads are bottom-funnel with no variety.
  7. Check that the ads use a wide range of avatars across genders, ages and ethnicities. An avatar is the person shown in the ad.
  8. Judge the editing and creatives of the strongest ads. Note which videos have the highest conversion rates.
  9. Compare 30-day revenue, units sold, price, shipping fee and commission rate against the test budget.
  10. Check the Last 90 Days graph. If the product peaked and is now slowing, rethink the plan or skip it.
  11. Decide to build custom ads rather than copy a competitor. Plan for roughly one to two weeks before the first launch.
  12. For supplements, consider timing the test toward the Q4 peak around Black Friday and Christmas. Q4 means October to December, the busiest shopping season. Black Friday is the big shopping day after Thanksgiving.

Electronics and gadgets

Gadgets sell at higher prices High perceived value; a strong supplier connection is essential because of defect risk

  • Example: a gadget seen selling on X (formerly Twitter) was reported to be doing around $5K in daily sales.
  • A visible, broad-appeal product like this is a signal worth testing when a beginner is researching what to launch.
  • Gadget niches such as locks often get attention without converting.
  • Treat them as low-priority tests unless you have seen them convert.

AliExpress is a global online shop that sells from many suppliers. For electronics on AliExpress, choose products with:

  • An average rating of at least 4.7.
  • At least one listing with more than 1,000 orders.
  • About 50 to 100 plus ratings.

Digital products: principles and niche selection

2 min

Digital products are files sold online, such as ebooks.

  • Digital products such as ebooks have no fulfillment cost. Fulfillment means storing, packing and shipping orders.
  • Digital products also deliver instantly, which makes them a low-cost way to test offers.
  • Digital products often have low competition.
  • A narrow niche topic that solves a specific problem can be tested with ads. It may stand out with little rivalry.
  • Choose a niche that can make money, where buyers are willing to spend.
  • Choose a niche where you have real knowledge, or the willingness to learn it quickly.
  • Without knowledge you cannot provide the value that makes a buyer pay. Avoid a product about a subject you know nothing about.
  • Example niche categories with proven demand include health (weight loss, fitness, healthy living).
  • Other examples include financial investment, making money online, and dating and relationship advice.
  • Hobbies are also examples, such as chess, where buyers may pay 20 to 30 dollars for a good ebook.
  • Language learning and personal development are also examples.
  • Ebook niches suggested for testing include dating advice for young men.
  • Ebook niches suggested for testing also include dating advice for women above a certain age.
  • Hobby niches are the easiest place to add information product offers after a physical sale. An information product is a course, guide or ebook. Buyers already spend money on learning and community around a hobby.
  • Before choosing a niche, check whether paid courses, ebooks or memberships already exist. This shows buyers will pay for extra value.
  • Digital products such as Lightroom presets, Apple themes, iPhone wallpapers and ebooks sell well. Lightroom presets are photo-editing settings.
  • They sell best when they match a passion or niche of the seller.
  • They can be made on Fiverr if you do not create them yourself. Fiverr is a site for hiring freelancers.
  • Pinterest, Etsy and Facebook were named the best platforms for digital product sales. Pinterest is an image board app. Etsy is a marketplace for handmade and digital goods.
  • Etsy ads can be run via Google.
  • Sales counts shown on Etsy listings can serve as a demand signal. For example, one design selling 200 times versus another selling 7,000 times.
  • Compare counts across similar listings before choosing which digital product to test.
  • Etsy hosts digital products such as iPhone icon packs and digital planners.
  • These products have no fulfillment cost. The buyer receives a download link to a file.

Digital products: ebook sourcing and reselling

2 min
  • PLR means private label rights. A PLR ebook can be bought, branded with your name and resold.
  • PLR Hustle is a site where PLR ebooks can be bought, downloaded, branded and resold.
  • ChatGPT can also be used to write an ebook from a chosen outline.
  • Free ebook libraries can supply ebooks for resale.
  • They can also supply ebooks used as free upsells. An upsell is an extra offer sold after the first purchase.
  • Rebrand the ebook with a new cover before use. Check the content first.
  • Use only free-to-use stock images and legally usable templates.
  • Do not download or resell other brands' ebooks or copyrighted content.
  • Digital products like printable planners or icon packs need no shipping or fulfillment.
  • Resellers must check copyright.
  • Ask the original seller for permission, or hire a designer on Fiverr to create a very similar product.
  • Etsy can be used to download and study existing ebooks in a niche.
  • Use this to learn the structure and gaps, to see how ebooks are built and what value they include.
  • Never copy the content.

The guidance disagrees on reselling ebooks from free libraries. Use material whose license allows resale, such as PLR or free-to-use content. Never resell others' copyrighted ebooks.

Turning a free PLR ebook into a sellable product

  1. Download the ebook and check that the content is strong.
  2. Replace the cover and reformat the layout in Canva. Canva is an easy design tool.
  3. Export the final PDF for delivery.

Done properly, this takes roughly one hour. Sending the unchanged file is not recommended.

Setting up a PLR or private-label digital product

Private-label means the product carries your brand.

  1. Find digital products by checking free PLR download sites and ebook libraries as a base. Then validate demand with Google Trends, a tool that shows search interest over time.
  2. Create the product with a copywriter or ebook specialist on Fiverr. Make it specific to the niche and the trend, such as seasonal editions.
  • Use seasonal or multiple editions of a digital product, such as winter, summer and fall editions. A later edition can serve as an upsell.
  • Bundle editions by category, such as relationships, growth mindset or abundance mindset.
  • Free ebooks work well as upsells.
  • Offering a bonus ebook worth about $20 with a purchase increases conversion. Conversion means the share of visitors who buy.
  • Automate delivery through an app that sends a PDF immediately after each order. This is preferred over manual sending.

Digital products: validation and competitor research

1 min

Ebook product research

1 min

An ebook is a digital book that you sell as a file.

  1. Look at what big brands sell and which problems they target.
  2. Choose problems that buyers actively face, such as gut health, skin, dental health or joints in pets.
  3. Build one ebook per problem.
  4. Check that the solution helps before you create the full product.
  • A recipe ebook for one problem should contain 15 to 20 recipes. With 20 recipes, a buyer who uses one recipe every second day has enough content for more than a month.

Niche and competitor validation for a digital product

1 min

A niche is one small group of buyers with one shared interest. Validation means checking that an idea works before you spend money on it.

  1. Choose a broad niche (a large topic) with proven money potential that you understand.
  2. Narrow it to a sub-niche (a smaller group inside the topic) with a specific problem people want solved. Examples: weight loss for men above 40, memory loss, anti-aging or gut health.
  3. Find the top five competitors (other sellers) selling digital products in that sub-niche.
  4. Analyse each competitor's offer (what they sell, for what price, with what promise), copy structure (the order of the sales text), headlines (the big titles), price and guarantee (the promise to refund).
  5. Build a better offer with more value and a stronger angle (the main reason a buyer should pick you).
  6. Create the ebook cover first, and sell before the ebook is written.
  7. Once orders come in, create or source the ebook (make it yourself or get it from someone) and fulfil it (deliver it to the buyer).

Find competitors by searching these places:

  • ClickBank (rank top offers by gravity, ClickBank's score for sales).
  • Digistore24 (a marketplace for digital products).
  • Warrior Plus (a marketplace for digital products).
  • The Facebook Ads Library (Meta's public list of ads; search the problem term to see who runs ads).
  • TikTok (who promotes a product on the problem).
  • Google (first two result pages).
  • Twitter/X.

ClickBank is the largest affiliate marketing marketplace and shows the top offers by gravity. Affiliate marketing means selling someone else's product for a share of each sale. Open the offers to study how they name the problem and persuade the buyer. Use it to learn new offers and copy structure, never to copy content.

Pre-sale validation before writing the ebook

1 min

Pre-sale means selling before the product exists.

  1. Create a book cover and a table of contents (the list of chapters).
  2. Run ads (paid online adverts) to an offer priced at 19 USD per ebook.
  3. Only after people actually pay, write the full ebook, for example with ChatGPT (an AI writing tool).
  4. As an alternative, buy a private label rights ebook (PLR, a ready-made ebook you may rebrand and resell) from PLR Hustle, download it, add your own branding (your name, logo and style), and resell it.

A low-cost ebook test (a small, cheap trial) at about 19 USD per book was run to see whether people spend money on the offer before the full product is created.

Pricing digital products for ads

1 min

Margin means the money you keep after costs.

  • Before you sell a product with ads, check two things: the competitor's price, and the acquisition cost (what it costs to get one customer through Facebook ads).
  • Then set your price so your margin stays large enough.
  • Example: if one customer costs 13 dollars to acquire, do not price the ebook at 15.
  • Example: a competitor selling an ebook on quitting gaming addiction charged 27 dollars and used a personal story on the sales page (the web page that sells the product). The response was judged a good but improvable offer.

Digital products: stock video and organic reach

1 min

Organic reach means views you get for free, without paying for ads.

  • Digital products can be promoted by downloading free stock videos (ready-made clips) from Pexels, adding a quote on top, and posting them as short-form videos (short clips) for organic reach.
  • This aims at zero ad spend and zero fulfillment costs (the costs of delivering the product), but it takes more time than paid ads.

Stock video steps:

  1. Download a free video from Pexels that fits the product topic.
  2. Add a short quote on top of the video that explains what the product helps with, and in which situation.
  3. Upload the video as Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts (short video feeds on Instagram and YouTube).
  4. Post consistently to generate organic traffic (free visitors).

Digital product variants: monthly plans and white-label

1 min

White-label means a product made by someone else that you put your own name on.

  • Custom phone icon packs (sets of small pictures for phone screens) can work as a digital product for an influencer's niche.
  • The designs can be commissioned (ordered and paid for) from Fiverr (a marketplace for freelance workers).
  • Offering a monthly plan that adds new icon sets every month or season turns a one-time sale into recurring revenue (money that comes back regularly).
  • For a physical product (a real item that is shipped) in a specific niche, such as photography gear, a white-label item like a tripod can be branded with the influencer's name. This gives the influencer a product that fits their audience.

Print-on-demand designs

1 min

Print-on-demand means an item is printed only after a customer orders it.

  • Print-on-demand designs sell when they tie into a trend or a strong emotional interest, such as a football team.
  • Use names, quotes, players or memes (funny images or text) that suggest a team instead of official logos, to avoid trademark problems (legal trouble over protected brand names or logos).

Pattern for trend-based slogans (phrases tied to what is popular right now):

  • Formula: take a well-known phrase or catchphrase from a popular show or trend, and change it to describe an everyday activity that many people share at the same time.
  • Why it works: buyers recognize the reference and enjoy wearing a joke that matches their current situation, so the product sells during that moment.
  • Examples in the same pattern: "Remote and rested" for people working from home, and "Snooze and bake" for people stuck indoors during a lockdown.

Customized and personalized products

1 min

Customized jewelry and products

1 min
  • Customizable products with multiple options (a main product with its own versions, and personalization features) sell better because buyers perceive higher value.
  • Customizable products such as engraved jewelry (jewelry with a name or message cut into it), bracelets, necklaces and frames can perform well, especially on TikTok.
  • Customization creates an emotional attachment for the buyer.
  • Jewelry products require only still photography (photos, not video) for ad creatives (the ad visuals).
  • Video content is optional.
  • Customizable jewelry performs well because it combines jewelry's photo-only requirement with personalization appeal (the pull of a product made for one person).
  • Customized products sell better in a narrow niche (a small, specific group) than as generic items (plain, not personalized ones).
  • Examples of narrow niches: jewelry tailored to a specific region or country, a specific religion, or a specific language.
  • Pet products are another customization option.
  • Customized jewelry tends to sell better when it targets a narrow group, such as a necklace featuring a specific country's map, a religious symbol, or text in one language.
  • Choose the audience first, then design the piece around it.
  • Customized or personalized products, such as gifts for a sister, mother or father, can work better than broad jewelry because they target a narrower buyer with less competition (other sellers).
  • Broad jewelry has many competitors and is harder to sell.

Finding a customization niche

1 min
  1. Search small, broad keywords (short search words) such as "engraved", "custom", "bracelet" or "necklace" on the marketplace or research tool.
  2. Repeat with other short keywords to find more listings (product pages).

Production, delivery and beginner fit

1 min
  • Customers accept longer delivery times for customized products, so the product page (the web page where the item is sold) can state 7-12 days or up to 7-15 business days (working days, without weekends).
  • For made-to-order products (made only after the order), such as custom furniture, state the production lead time (the time needed to make the item) on the product page, for example four to six weeks.
  • Customization options can be added with the Bold product options app (an add-on tool for product choices), which has a 14-day free trial (a free test period).
  • Custom-sized or custom-configured products (built to the buyer's size or setup) sold via dropshipping (a model where a supplier ships the item for you, so you hold no stock) work this way: the customer chooses options, the supplier builds the item to order, and then ships it to the customer.
  • Personalized products often sell well, but they are not recommended for complete beginners.
  • They need more production effort and logistics (ordering, packing and shipping) to arrive undamaged (in good shape).
  • In the experience behind this text, personalized products work best in the US market.

Two signals, read together: customized gifts can outperform (sell better than) broad jewelry, but personalized production is not recommended for complete beginners. Start with a narrow customized niche only once production and logistics are under control.

Category-specific warnings

1 min
  • Before you sell a smart home device, such as a light-bulb security camera (a camera built into a light bulb), confirm these points:
    • Whether it needs a subscription (a regular paid plan).
    • How footage (camera recordings) is stored, for example an SD card slot (a small memory card slot) for local playback (watching on the device itself, without the internet).
    • What the companion app (the phone app that goes with the device) requires.
  • Check these points in the product listing or the manufacturer's information (the maker's documents) so you can answer customer questions.
  • For electronics that the buyer uses on the body, order a sample and test it yourself before listing.
  • Or check the product description and manual for safety and performance.
  • This prevents selling a poor-quality product that causes chargebacks (forced refunds taken back through the buyer's bank) and disputes (complaints).
  • Gadget niches such as locks often get attention without converting (without leading to sales).
  • Treat them as low-priority tests (small tests, not your main focus) unless you have seen them convert.
  • Ingredient-led supplements, such as ashwagandha (a herbal supplement), need extra scrutiny (careful checking) before testing.

Ad Spy Tools: Roles and Limits

3 min
  • The Meta Ad Library is the most complete public ad archive for dropshipping research, because advertisers cannot hide their ads from it, while third-party spy tools can miss ads that advertisers pay to conceal. Use it as the primary source for finding products.
  • No spy tool shows every ad, and some products appear in only one tool. Each tool has its own library, so use several tools for a more complete list.
  • Some spy tools do not update their databases and keep showing the same products every day. Supplement them with manual searches in the Ad Library and on supplier marketplaces.
  • No tool gives exact competitor store data or sales for a product. Use the Ad Library and keyword searches to estimate competition. Spy tools with a product section show some stores that sell a product, but they miss many of them.
  • Product research with spy tools works best by studying the best sellers of competitor stores and the ads they run, rather than hunting for new products every day. Products with proven sales are a better starting point than constantly chasing new ones.

  • Old Facebook winners from the past few years are a strong source of product ideas, because products that performed well as Facebook ads may not yet have been tested with organic TikTok content.
  • If a spy extension reports that a Facebook account has too few ads to analyze, create a separate Facebook account for product research and keep it apart from the business account.

Meta Ad Library: Setup and Basic Search

2 min
  • The Meta Ad Library is a free public tool for checking which ads run for a brand or keyword, filtered by country and active status. It is used to validate products and to find winning ads for inspiration.
  • Log into a Facebook account before using the Ad Library, because without a login the results may not show everything. Start from the Ad Library page, choose the country, then open the All ads view.
  • Steps for checking a product in the Meta Ad Library:
    1. Open the Meta Ad Library.
    2. Set the country filter to United States.
    3. Set the active status filter to Active ads and the ad type and media type filters to all.
    4. Search the product or brand keyword.
    5. Count the results and check the launch dates and Library IDs of the ads.
    6. Open the ads and check the impression label: ads marked with a low impression count (under 100 impressions) are weak performers.
    7. Look for the ads with more impressions and with multiple advertisers running similar creatives, since these are the likely winners.
  • The Ad Library can show which products competitors advertise. Search the product idea and sort by newest to see which products have recently started running ads. Use this as a market check before testing.
  • The Ad Library shows which stores run ads for a product. If other stores run the same product creatives with their own landing pages and product titles, that helps validate demand.
  • Searching for an exact product name can return nothing, because some ads only run on general pages. When the exact product is not found, look at the ads of large niche stores in the same category to see what the market runs.
  • Searching by hand gives scattered and incomplete data. A paid ad spy tool with a large library and product filters produces more candidates and more reliable scaling data in less time. The aim is a list of at least 10 candidates, ideally 10 to 15.
  • Facebook Ads Library searches with niche keywords, such as kids toy or kids gift, show which ads are running in a niche and are used for manual niche and competitor checks.
  • Ad spy search by product keyword for a specific audience: AdSpy filters for female 40+ fashion research are Gender = female, Age = 40 to 65+, Country = United States, and Ad text = a product keyword such as shoe, sneakers, boot or slippers. To see a specific competitor store's ads, search by its URL.

Keyword and Offer Searches

2 min
  • Keyword searches in the Ad Library are the first filter for dropshipping ads. Use typical offer phrases such as 30 percent off, 35 percent off, 50 percent off and free shipping. Broad phrases like percent off capture most discount ads, while 70 or 80 percent off are rarely used by dropshippers but can still be tested.
  • A single broad keyword is not enough on its own. The keyword percent off returned more than 50,000 results for the United States without other filters. Adding the Active ads filter and a narrow date window reduces the list to a workable number.
  • Combine several keywords in one search with OR logic, using the pipe character, to see ads containing any of the phrases, such as percent off together with get yours today. Use quotation marks for exact-phrase search when you need ads containing the exact phrase, such as free shipping.
  • Brand and dropship ads often appear side by side in the Ad Library. Large non-product advertisers such as brands, non-profits and political groups add a lot of noise. Use specific keywords, narrow date windows and product-related ads to filter them out.
  • Free research workflow with keywords:
    1. Prepare a list of 5 to 10 keywords per product.
    2. Search each keyword in the Facebook Ad Library, the main free source for Facebook competitors.
    3. Review the last one to two months of data, including the previous month, to see who is running ads.
    4. Note each competitor, its ad count, creative reuse and launch dates in a sheet.
    5. Open the competitor's Facebook page and search its name in the Ad Library to check whether it is scaling.
    6. Check the competitor's landing page and copy.
  • Search Google, Amazon, AliExpress, Alibaba and TikTok with the same keywords as the Facebook Ad Library, and compile the competitors found outside the ad tools, since ad spy tools do not cover every store.
  • Winning Hunter is not needed for a beginner's basic keyword check; the Ad Library workflow above is enough to start.

Date Windows and the Active-Ad Filter

3 min
  • Set the Active ads filter in every research session, because it hides ads that have stopped running. Running ads show what other sellers are currently scaling or testing, which is the signal being looked for.
  • The Ad Library sometimes shows results that do not match the active filters, for example older ads when a recent date window is set. Re-check the filters and dates before trusting a result.
  • A short recent window with the Active ads filter shows which products other sellers are scaling right now, rather than just testing. Ads launched in the last few days give a current signal of demand.
  • Long-running active ads are a signal of profitability, because advertisers rarely keep ads running for months without return. An ad launched in March that is still active in May suggests the product is profitable for that seller, although a forgotten ad can also stay active.
  • Advertisers do not scale ads for products that fail to make money, so active advertising by others is a signal of profitability. Check which products have sustained ad activity before choosing what to test.

  • Ad Library date presets are Today, Yesterday, Last 7 days, Last 14 days, Last 30 days, Last 90 days, Last 180 days, Last 365 days and All Time.
  • Filter for ads launched in the last 1 to 2 weeks, using about one month as the widest window. The aim is to find products that competitors have only just started to scale.
  • Choosing a window that is too recent, such as April, mostly shows products still in testing or early scaling without product market fit. For a proven product, use an older window such as 1 to 31 March or 1 January to 31 March.
  • Filter for product ads created in the last 1 month, and extend to 1.5 to 2 months only if needed. Recently launched ads show which products are currently working for other dropshippers.
  • Winning Hunter has a Last seen date filter, separate from the Ad creation date filter. Use it to find ads still being shown recently, regardless of when they first launched.
  • Check when an advertiser's ads started before trusting their ad count. Ads that began only a day or two ago are not yet proof of profit, while ads that have run for about a week or more are a stronger signal.
  • Change the date range in the Facebook Ad Library to look at past ad counts for a product. A product that once had over 1,000 active ads in an earlier window has scaled before, even if the current count is lower.

Days Running and Video Filters

1 min
  • Set Days running to a minimum of seven days. An ad that has run that long suggests the product is either working or at least breaking even.
  • Dashboard filter ranges include Days running from 0 to 365, Video length from 0s to 3600s, Product Price Range from 0 to 5000, and Products on Store from 0 to 100000. Combine them with Store Traffic (Monthly Visits) and Scaling to narrow results to stores that are already proven.
  • Set Media type to Videos, because the goal is to recreate video ads that already perform.
  • Set the Video length filter to a long range, with the default range 0 to 3600 seconds, to find VSLs. Long-video filtering works well for finding sales-video funnels.
  • Use the Ad copy length filter to surface native-style ads, which tend to carry long body copy.
  • Leave Page type blank, so advertorials, listicles, quizzes and blog-style funnels appear next to product pages. This lets you study the full funnel a brand uses.
  • Choose a niche relevant to your search (the example used Health) and leave the Apps and Theme filters blank. Winning Hunter warns that the niche filter may not show all ads, so use it only when it saves time.

Scaling thresholds for one product: 20 or more active ads for scaling, 30 or more for solid proof of concept, and 50 or more for likely high revenue.

  • Products with 2 to 10 active ads: a seller with 10 to 15 active ads is usually still testing.
  • Products with a very high count that appeared only days earlier may be a short burst of testing; a product whose ads run weeks or months later is a stronger signal of sustained performance.
  • Ads that have run for a week or more strengthen the signal from a high active-ad count.
  • Skip products with very few active ads, such as 18, when comparable products in the same search have many more active ads. Come back to them later instead of testing them now.
  • A product with many active ads that drops to zero may simply have been stopped or replaced by the seller. It can still be tested in other markets, but its success probability is lower.
  • A falling number of active ads signals declining demand or quality. Treat such products as lower priority, even if they were strong in earlier research.
  • Track how many active ads a competing brand runs for a product over time, and check again after about two months. A clearly falling number signals the trend is fading and demand is being lost.
  • A product that had over 1,000 ads a few days ago and now shows fewer active ads may still be a proven winner. Check the Ads Library history and the active ad count together before rejecting a product.
  • A product that started running ads about a month earlier and already shows around 645 active ads is scaling very fast and is worth shortlisting. Compare its start date with its current active-ad count to judge growth speed.

Reading Ad Counts Correctly

2 min
  • Read the active ad count as a signal of intent. Around 10 to 15 active ads usually means a seller is still testing, while a steady flow of several new ads per day suggests the seller has found audiences that convert and is putting budget behind them.
  • Multiple active ads running for a long time, combined with recent edits or new ads started within days, are stronger evidence than a single high count. A competitor who recently started or edited ads is actively testing and spending.
  • Check recent activity: when the latest ad was started or edited shows whether money is still being spent.
  • The Ad Library counts an ad that runs in several ad sets once per ad set, so an ad in five ad sets shows as five ads. A high count means the advertiser tests the product heavily, not that many separate creatives exist.
  • The unfiltered total on a competitor's page can be several times higher than the active count. For one product, 2,200 total against 640 active was shown. Apply the Active ads filter before counting.
  • Some tools show how one creative's ad count grows over time, for example from single digits to several hundred ads for the same creative. Rising counts mean the advertiser is scaling that creative and the product is probably profitable for them. Check the date range to confirm the growth happened recently.
  • A product with a high and growing ad count is a usable proxy for profitable demand, because advertisers do not keep paying for ads that do not perform. This makes ad volume a practical filter.
  • Sorting by active ads is the first filter for shortlisting candidate dropshipping products.
  • Sorting by percentage change in ad count shows which products are gaining ad volume fastest, not only which have the most ads.

  • A competitor running 5 or more ads with the same creative is probably scaling that creative, which usually means the product was profitable for them.
  • A competitor running 10 to 15 ads with the same creative is in an active scaling phase and is a strong competitor signal worth tracking.
  • For a product with ads at a scaling threshold, count the same creative across the advertiser's page to judge whether the product is being scaled or only tested.
  • Mark every competitor's scaling status (Y/N). Competitors who scale ads show the product is currently profitable for them. Count this with the competition and engagement numbers when deciding to test.

  • Products with a large number of likes are easy to miss. If filters return too few products, lower the thresholds and widen the date range to about 45 days.
  • Likes and comments can be bought, so do not rely on engagement numbers alone. Check the comment section to see if buyers are real and whether they complain about quality.
  • Use the comment section to judge genuine demand. People tagging friends on an ad is a strong sign of real engagement. Repetitive comments or fake-looking profiles suggest bought engagement, and the ad should be discounted.
  • Engagement without sales is a weaker signal. A competitor ad shows market fit when its comments contain buying questions and people tag friends to buy.
  • Positive comments such as buyers asking about the product, sharing reviews and tagging friends signal real demand. Products with strong comments can be ranked higher.
  • A product with many positive comments about the concept, buyers asking for details, people tagging friends and reviews from real buyers is a good sign. Comments praising the concept or saying buyers want the product show real demand.
  • Many repeated short comments of 2 to 3 words posted by different accounts are a red flag, because bots may have added them. Ignore such comment sections as evidence of demand.
  • Comments that criticize product quality are a red flag and should lead you to drop the product.
  • Compare the engagement and comments of a competitor's winning ad across a second tool, such as PPSpy, Shop Hunter or DropiSpy.

Page Age and Advertiser Quality

1 min
  • An older Facebook page is not automatically a warning sign. If a page created years earlier is adding many active ads quickly, for example rising from about 30 to about 57 active ads in one day, the product may still be worth testing.
  • Page age of a product's store is a soft guideline, often cited as four to six months. An older page is not an automatic deal breaker. A page older than a year that runs high-scale ads may have a well-trained pixel that is harder to compete with.
  • Competitor Facebook page age does not matter much for product selection, because pages and ad accounts get banned often and are replaced. Judge a product by its active ads and store, not by the advertiser's age.
  • A product that was popular a year or two earlier can return. Check its current ad activity instead of dismissing it because of its age.
  • A declining percentage in AdLib may indicate the advertiser's account was banned and recently restarted.
  • Avoid products when the competitor is a general store testing many products and the active ad count for the specific product is unclear. Without a clear count, you cannot tell whether the competitor is scaling it or profitable.

Store and Competitor Tracking

1 min
  • Peakster has a store tracking feature showing a competitor store's revenue, last orders, and each last-sold product with its name and image, which reveals winning products.
  • To confirm a store found through Peak Star store research:
    1. Check the store's active ads and scaling pattern in the Meta Ad Library.
    2. Open the About section of the store's page and read the page creation date.
    3. Find the same winning ad in a second tool, such as PPSpy, Shop Hunter or DropiSpy, and compare its engagement and comments.
  • Use a competitor's store to find more candidates:
    1. Open the advertiser's page in the Facebook Ad Library.
    2. Note the other products they currently run ads for.
    3. Screen each new product the same way: count active ads, check ad age, check the store, and infer the target country.
  • When one competitor store sells several products, the product with the most active ads is usually the one they are scaling. Use that product's ads as the reference for your own test.
  • Each competitor's ad activity in the Ad Library: open the competitor's Facebook page, search its name in the Ad Library, and check whether it is scaling.
  • Recheck ad counts after a few days to confirm the ads are still running.
  • Check whether the store runs other products with many active ads, which shows what it is testing.
  • Peak Star-style tracking is used to monitor a competitor's selling pace on a specific product.
  • Shop Hunter confirms a product is actually selling before it is tested with ads.

Competitor Screening Workflows

6 min

Product screening workflow (Ad Library)

  1. Search the product in the Facebook Ad Library and count the active ads.
  2. Skip the product if it has fewer than about 50 active ads or only inactive ads.
  3. Check the advertiser's store: offer, price, and whether it is a product you would test.
  4. Infer the targeted country from the store currency and language.
  5. Find the supplier product via AliExpress using the camera (image) search.
  6. Add the product to the test list with the Ad Library link and the store link.

Product research procedure in the Facebook Ad Library

  1. Open a product list sorted by performance or by number of active ads.
  2. Open each product's store link and note the product, price, offers and bundles.
  3. Open the Facebook Ad Library, find the product or store, and count the ads marked as active.
  4. Check when the latest ad was started or edited; recent activity shows that money is still being spent.
  5. Note the countries targeted in the ads.
  6. Review the ad copy and videos, and save videos for reference.
  7. Compare the store's problem and solution messaging, description and offers with what the ads promise.
  8. Add the product to the research list with its store link and its Ad Library link.

Product research in the Meta Ad Library, in order

  1. Search for the product or brand.
  2. Filter for ads that are currently active.
  3. Sort results by number of active ads.
  4. Keep only products with more than 100 active ads, since fewer usually means weak demand.
  5. Open the competitor's store and check its offer, product description and landing page.
  6. Check whether the store runs other products with many active ads, which shows what it is testing.
  7. Recheck the ad counts after a few days to confirm the ads are still running.

Product research with Afterlib

  1. Sort the Afterlib product list by active ads.
  2. Keep only products with more than 100 active ads in the Facebook Ad Library.
  3. Open the product page and check whether the product clearly solves a problem and whether the store has room for a better page.
  4. Search the product in the Facebook Ad Library and confirm its active ad count and the date the ads started.
  5. Check which countries the ads run in and choose the test market from there.

Product research process for Facebook and Meta

  1. Choose an ad spy tool and set its filters.
  2. List all potential products the filtered results show, aiming for at least 10 and ideally 10 to 15 candidates.
  3. For each product, open the competitor page and find its Facebook Ad Library page.
  4. Count the active ads for that specific product on the Ad Library page.
  5. Record the data in the product research sheet: product name, competitor store link, Ad Library link, active ads, AliExpress link, COGS, competitor selling price and break-even ROAS.
  6. Use the recorded COGS and selling price to calculate break-even ROAS and rank the candidates.

Competitor count per product (scaling analysis)

  • Only count active ads that belong to the specific product when doing the scaling analysis, excluding ads for other products on the same competitor page. Record the adjusted active ad count in the sheet for each candidate.
  • On a competitor's Facebook Ad Library page, apply the Active ads filter before counting. Count only the ads for the specific product and subtract ads for other products the advertiser tests on the same page.

Facebook Ad Library competitor research (full workflow)

  1. Open the Ad Library and log into your Facebook account.
  2. Select the country, for example United States.
  3. Select All ads.
  4. Enter a discount or offer keyword in the search field, for example percent off.
  5. Open the filters and set Active ads so only currently running ads appear.
  6. Set the Impression date range to a window of two to three days for recent scaling products, or to a window of about one to three months (for example 1 to 31 March) for proven older products.
  7. Scroll the results and note the products that show a high number of ads using the same creative.
  8. Open each candidate's ads, copy the ad link, and check the advertiser's full ad count in the Ad Library.
  9. Record each candidate and rank products against your product criteria before testing them one by one.

Find long-running products

  1. Open the Ad Library and select the United States (or another chosen country) as the country.
  2. Select All ads.
  3. Enter keywords such as percent off, free shipping, or get yours today.
  4. Set the Active ads filter.
  5. Set the Impression date filter to a past window of one to three months, for example 1 March to 31 March, to find products whose ads have been running a long time.
  6. Review the results and look for products whose ads are still active from that window.
  7. Check whether the seller is still scaling, and note the product if the ad is still running.

Verify a candidate's ads and store

  1. Click View ads, Go to page, or the three-dot menu and Copy ad link on the candidate ad.
  2. Open a new browser tab and paste the link to see the page that runs the ad.
  3. Go back to the Ad Library, select United States, then All ads.
  4. Search for the advertiser's page name to see the total number of ads that page runs.
  5. Count how many ads use the same creative and copy to judge whether the product is being scaled or only tested.
  6. Open the store and check the landing page and product proposition before deciding to test.

Find products to test through the Ad Library as a buyer

  • Click an ad on Facebook or Instagram, visit the advertiser's website, add a product to cart, start checkout and leave. Repeat, so the platform shows more ads from that niche. The same approach works on TikTok.
  • Click an ad that appears in the feed, land on the product page, add the product to cart and go to checkout. Expect Meta to show more ads for similar products afterwards, which signals a market.

Shortlisting volume

  • Examples from the lesson: a magnetic absorption product with 686 active ads and a clothing item with 672 active ads were both kept for testing. A home decor item with 390 active ads and a product with 385 active ads were also shortlisted.
  • A product with 1,000 or more active ads is a strong trust signal that it works, so it is worth testing.

Trend research on Facebook and TikTok, as taught

  1. Check spy tools (Peak Star, Droppy Spy) and the ad platforms for products that worked in the last month.
  2. Check which products worked in the same period of the previous year, since seasonal products that worked last year often work again because they tie to events.
  3. Check products from two years earlier in the same season for ideas that could be improved.
  4. Build a list of 10 to 15 candidates with basic criteria and margin checks.
  5. Run competitor analysis on each candidate.

Products that worked before

  1. Filter products with your conditions for roughly the last six weeks.
  2. Go through all matches until none are left.
  3. Go back to previous years and look for products that worked then and could work again now.

Trend example

  • A trend product rose from 618 ads on 27 November to 1,423 ads on 10 December, a sharp jump within about two weeks. A rapid rise in ad count like this signals that demand is growing.

Market and Country Checks

2 min
  • Check competitors in the Meta Ad Library filtered by your target country before launching there. If many advertisers already run the same ad in that market, expect higher competition.
  • Searching by country, for example United States, reveals products that can be tested in another market.
  • Checking competitors in a new country with the Facebook Ad Library:
    1. Open the Facebook Ad Library.
    2. Set the country filter to the target country.
    3. Search a broad keyword for the category around the product, such as women's care or healthcare.
    4. Search the exact product name.
    5. Note which brands run ads for the product, what they sell, how their ads are built, and on which platform they run them.
    6. If the product has no scaling competitors in that country, the market may be open; if strong brands already sell it, study their ads and offers before entering.
  • When a product is over-run in one market, look at the same product sold in another physical form, such as a drink mix versus drops. That form usually has its own video ads from other sellers, which gives a fresh pool of creatives to study.
  • Markets to avoid for the US research filters: Exclude the European and UK countries GB, DK, NL, DE, NO, SE and FR from the country filter when researching the US, so non-US ads do not crowd the results.
  • Set Countries to the target markets in the spy tool, for example Australia and UK, with the Language filter left open.
  • Countries used in the spy-tool filter for broad research: USA, Canada, Australia, UK and Germany, with Language set to English.
  • Smaller markets such as Australia and New Zealand return a comparatively small library and can be used for a quicker overview; Germany and the UAE were also named as options. The United States produces a very large result set, so extra filters are needed.
  • Infer the targeted country from the store's currency and language.
  • Choose the test market from the countries the ads run in.
  • Home decor items are a good niche for picture-only ads, because they are visually self-explanatory and cheaper to advertise than video.

Winning Hunter Meta Ad Library Filters

1 min

Country, language and technology

  • In the Winning Hunter Meta ad library search, set Countries to the US, set Technology to Shopify, and set Languages to English while excluding the other listed languages. This keeps results on the target market's native-language ads.
  • Leave the EU adspend filter unused when targeting the US, because it mainly covers ads running in Europe.

Store traffic

  • The Store Traffic (monthly visits) filter helps find stores that are booming. Beginners without e-commerce or dropshipping experience should skip it, because high-traffic products are often crowded and harder to enter.

Saved presets

  • Winning Hunter's Meta filter panel includes saved presets such as Week's biggest winners, European winners, US winners, Dropship Ads, Advertorials, Native ads and Quiz Funnels. Clicking a preset loads its filter set, and Save current filters stores your own filter combination for reuse.

Ad card data

  • Winning Hunter ad cards show estimated spend, reach, average daily spend, estimated monthly store traffic and estimated product revenue. They also show how many duplicate copies of a creative exist, which helps judge whether an ad and its product are worth copying.

Full product research procedure in Winning Hunter's Meta ad library

  1. Set Countries to the US and exclude the European and UK countries.
  2. Set Technology to Shopify.

Ad-Level Filters and Product Qualification

1 min
  • Use the Ad Library and the TikTok spy data together to qualify products.
  • Check which countries the ads run in before choosing a test market.
  • Use specific keywords, narrow date windows and product-related ads to remove brand and non-product noise.
  • The method can produce a list of 15 to 25 potential products in one session. Rank them by criteria fit and test the strongest candidates first.
  • A product with no ad scaling signal yet, such as one with only a few ads, should not be treated as a winner based on its appearance alone. Wait for evidence from the Ad Library count or the ad running time before investing.
  • Do not choose products on gut feeling or because they look new and untapped. Pick products already advertised on the same ad platform and to the same audience you plan to use, because sales on a marketplace like AliExpress do not prove the product works on Meta.
  • When a product with many active ads suddenly drops to zero, check whether the seller stopped testing or switched products.
  • Use the ad count, the store and the Ad Library history together. Judge the product by its active ads, not by the advertiser's page age.

Adapting Proven Creatives

2 min
  • When researching a source niche for ads, match the emotion or fear the ad triggers rather than the product category. For example, a hair-loss ad that makes viewers worry about visible decline can be a model for a skin product that addresses a similar self-image concern.
  • The second method adapts proven videos from a different niche that trigger the same emotion as your product, for example taking health-related winners for beauty or acne-related winners for skincare. It is recommended most, because it lets you copy a proven concept without copying a competitor's exact product.

Procedure: find proven videos in a similar niche with the Meta Ad Library (Method 2)

  1. Pick a niche that triggers the same emotion as your product (for example, acne and skincare, or health and beauty).
  2. Open the Meta Ad Library with the filters of the brand tool.

  • Winning-creative replication when you cannot order the product:

    1. Download the competitor video.

    3. Test the altered version.

  • When a competitor has many creative ads, replicate the winning creative by ordering the product and producing custom content. This is the best first step before editing a competitor's video.

Tool Overview and Limits

1 min
  • Meta Ad Library: Shows the ads competitors currently run and whether each is still active. Check it just before launch, because a discontinued creative may mean poor results or an account issue.
  • Meta Ad Library limits: It shows page transparency details such as active ads and page creation date, but not likes, comments or engagement. Check engagement on the Facebook page or through a tool.
  • Tools and the Ad Library don't connect: Peekster and similar tools cannot show the full Facebook Ad Library data because they scrape Facebook pages rather than the Ad Library. The two views cannot be linked directly.
  • Ad spy tools show ads, not sales: Tools such as AfterLib show Facebook Ads Library data (ads running), not actual sales or revenue. Ad count is used as a proxy for profitability, because advertisers only keep scaling products that make money.
  • Store trackers are unreliable: Store trackers gave unreliable data when tested on the coach's own stores. Check winning products through Ad Library ad counts instead.
  • Peekster name variants: The same tool appears as Peekster, Peeksta and PeakStar in the sources, and PP Ads as PPAds.

Finding Competitors Through Keyword Searches

2 min

Common mistake

  • Pasting the full product name or title straight into the Ad Library search usually does not find competitors. Search terms should come from words the ad actually says.

Keyword method (Meta Ad Library)

  1. Open a video ad for the product and listen closely to the spoken words.
  2. Choose a specific keyword said in the ad, such as a problem or benefit term. Skip generic phrases, greetings and your own name.
  3. Type the keyword into the Ad Library search and set Active status to "Active ads" so only currently running ads show.
  4. Narrow results with the available filters, then open each store that appears and check whether it sells the same product.
  5. Scroll down the results and keep finding more stores from the same ad.
  6. Repeat steps 2 to 5 with keywords from other ads until you have a list of competitor stores.
  7. Check the quality of each store before using its design, as described in the quality check procedure.
  • Keyword rules: The Ad Library matches words spoken or shown in ads, so a keyword must really appear in the video. Pick words from the ad itself, not from the product description or your own assumptions.
  • Distinctive terms work best: Keywords should be distinctive terms tied to the product's problem or benefit, not everyday speech. Ordinary words match many unrelated videos and create a noisy list, while a distinctive term returns a short list of stores running similar offers.
  • Example: For a rice-based serum ad, the spoken phrase "nose pores" was typed into the Ad Library, which surfaced several other stores selling a similar product. Each store found this way was reviewed as a possible competitor.
  • Product name as a second search: If the product name is said in the ad, searching the name directly is quicker than side keywords and usually returns more competitor ads. Use it as an efficient second search after the distinctive keyword search.
  • No unique keyword: If a product has no unique spoken keyword, search its general product type name, such as the drink or supplement category, because the ad will almost always mention it. This works for simple, generic products such as an instant tea powder.
  • Keep searching: One product can have many competitor stores. Keep searching with new keywords from different ads rather than stopping at the first matches.
  • Competition check: If only two to three competitors already run the same product, skip it and move to the next one.

Scaling Signals from Active Ad Counts

2 min

Thresholds by source

Sources define the counting unit differently. L1177 counts active ads for a single creative, while L1183 and L1316 count ads for a product or store. Use the per-creative count to judge one ad and the product total to judge a store overall.

20 to 30+ active ads for the same product Scaling and likely profitable Per product
190+ active ads Very strong scaling Per product
Hundreds of active ads Probably profitable for competitors; worth testing Per product or store
More than about 1,000 active ads Saturated in that market; skip unless entering a new market Per product or store
  • Use these counts as market responsiveness signals.
  • Under 30 is not scaling: Require at least 30 active ads for a single creative before treating a competitor as scaling.

Percentage progression

  • Shortlisting threshold: A product's ad count should show progression above 400%. Values around 370% or 3.4% are too low, while progression around 1,100% to 1,600% makes a strong candidate.
  • 14-day growth: Ad activity that has risen roughly 100 to 300 percent over the last 14 days is a strong signal of a product starting to take off. Track it with an ad library or ad spy tool.
  • AfterLib progression column: The key column is progression, meaning how many new ads a product or store has launched recently. A rising number of new ads signals a product gaining traction.

Ad count growth over time

  • A competitor's ad count can grow quickly, so the number of ads a store runs over time shows how hard it tests. In one example, a store went from 3 to 115 ads over the period shown in AfterLib.
  • A competitor running the same product in several countries at once while adding more ads over time is scaling. A product with a rising ad count across multiple markets is a stronger test candidate than one with a single ad.
  • A competitor's ad that has run for a month is proof that a product sells. Running the same product in other countries is fine, and you do not need to be the first seller.

Inactive ads

  • Red flag: If ads have stopped (inactive), treat the product as a red flag and do not test it.
  • Past winners: A product with many inactive ads may have scaled well in the past year, but inactive ads mean it is not being tested now. Flag such products for later review instead of treating them as current winners.

Seasonal caution

  • Early in the year many merchants cut back on ad spend, so competitor ad counts can look lower than normal in January. Re-check a product's ad activity a couple of weeks later before deciding whether to test it.

Single-image ads

  • A competitor running only a single image ad for a product is often sourcing it from AliExpress. The same product, or a similar design, can then be sourced and tested with the same simple ad style.

Page Age

1 min
  • Preferred age: Prefer competitor pages created less than four months ago, ideally four to six months. Older pages have a warmed-up pixel and a data advantage that makes them hard to beat with the same data.
  • Acceptable range: Four to six months is acceptable, per the procedure in.
  • Fresh entry: For Facebook-based dropshipping research, check the page creation date in the Ad Library (About section). A page created within the last 4 to 5 months signals a fresh market entry with a level playing field and high potential. An older page, around 1.5 years, is a weaker signal.
  • Older example (March 2022 page): A competitor with a page created in March 2022 and more than one and a half years in the market had a warmed-up pixel. An identical product launched later was not a safe copy, even though it scaled with 31 active ads. (Older example, from 2022-era research.)
  • Fast-scaling principle: A recently created competitor page that already runs many active ads is a sign of fast scaling. This points to a positive market response and profitability (product-market fit). Entering the same product under those conditions gives a higher chance of reaching similar momentum.

Engagement Checks

1 min
  • Recent ads with engagement: An ad launched within the last one to two months that already gets a good number of likes and comments signals that the market is engaging with the product.
  • Engagement comes from the page: Because the Ad Library shows no likes or comments, check engagement on the Facebook page or through a tool.
  • Recent launches with few competitors: Search PP Ads and similar tools for products launched in the last two weeks with strong engagement, and check how many competitors run ads on them. A new product with few competitor ads is easier to test than a saturated one.
  • Weak stores can still be tested: A product can be added to the list when its store has weak reviews or poor design, as long as ad activity is strong. The store can then be improved with better pages and ads.

Competitor Store Tracking

1 min
  • Find the store's best seller: To see which product a competing store is scaling, compare the active ad counts of all its products in the Facebook Ad Library. The product with the most active ads is usually the store's best seller, and it is the one worth researching first.
  • Use the video library: Use the Ad Library's video library and filter to the exact product rather than relying on store trackers.
  • Check sales with the store link: Track the competitor's sales in Peekster by pasting the store link to confirm real revenue. AfterLib does not show sales, only ad activity.
  • Store review checklist (AfterLib):
    1. Open the product research tool and filter for currently trending products.
    2. Check the progression, meaning how many new ads are being launched.
    3. Note which countries the store is targeting.
    4. Open the store in a new tab and compare prices across variants and styles.
    5. Check the offers, for example "buy 2 for 10% off" or "buy 3 for 15% off," and whether a gift box option is included.
    6. Save the product links for later testing.

Filtering Cadence and Shortlist Routine

1 min

Daily shortlist routine

  1. Rank candidate products by their current number of active ads in the ad library.
  2. After about 10 to 20 minutes, re-rank the same candidates by percentage change in ad count to see which are scaling fastest.
  3. Build a shortlist of 5 to 10 products from both lists that pass the criteria for growth, niche fit and data validation.

Bulletproof method (three criteria)

Use Peekster and the Facebook Ad Library together:

  1. In Peekster, open the Facebook ad section and set the filters: country United States, sort by first seen date, minimum 300 likes, minimum 150 to 200 comments, English, suitable post type.
  2. Pick ads launched within the last one to two months that show good likes and comments.
  3. Open the advertiser's page and check the page transparency section to find the page name.
  4. Open the Facebook Ad Library, search for the page under all ads, and count the active ads for the single creative.
  5. Require 30 or more active ads for a single creative. Fewer suggests a testing phase; 30 or more suggests scaling.
  6. Check the page creation date and prefer pages created less than four months ago. Four to six months is acceptable.
  7. Track the competitor's sales in Peekster by pasting the store link to confirm real revenue.
  8. Only test a product that meets all three criteria: scaling, new page, and sales.

Ad-count check before testing

  1. Search the product or the store running it.
  2. Count the active ads.
  3. If ads have stopped (inactive), treat it as a red flag and do not test it.
  4. If hundreds of active ads run, the product is probably profitable for competitors and worth testing.
  5. If more than about 1,000 active ads run, treat the product as saturated in that market and skip it unless you enter a new market.

Common Mistakes

  • Relying on store trackers: Store trackers gave unreliable data when tested on the coach's own stores.
  • Treating inactive ads as current winners: Inactive ads mean a product is not being tested now, even if it scaled in the past.

More rules worth knowing

1 min

  • An ad count of 1493 on a single product (September 22 baseline) indicates strong market interest; high ad volume from a brand signals viability for testing.

  • When testing a new product: (1) Identify a winning store running ads, (2) Get inspired by their page design, (3) Test with their video creatives first, (4) After sufficient sales, create your own original content.

  • A competitor with a rising count of new ads over the last 30 days is showing a scaling pattern and is worth saving for later review. Use the Performance view for consistency and Trending for fast-moving products.

  • A competitor ad that has been running for a long time is a sign the product is profitable. Check on the competitor's Meta page how long the ad has been live before validating a product.

  • The Facebook Ad Library, or ad-analysis tools, show the ads of competitors and give the ad link you need for product research. The AliExpress link helps locate a supplier product, and CJ can be used as an alternative source.

Playbook › 2. Niche and product research

Finding products via marketplaces and other sources

Section 2.19 · 19 of 24 in this chapter · 14 min read

This section has 14 parts. Tap one to open it.

Set your criteria before you search

1 min
  • Narrow the search to products you understand or care about, then set the criteria a product must meet before you search any source.
  • Source choice matters less than the criteria. Search anywhere (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, ad spy tools, physical stores), but check every candidate against the same list.
  • For Amazon FBA, the ideal price point is $9 to $40. Use $50 as the upper limit only if the cost of goods allows a 50% profit margin, because customers show less purchasing friction in this range.
  • Prioritize small, light products. Lower weight and size reduce Amazon FBA storage fees and shipping costs, which directly improves margin.
  • Check private label potential before investigating further. Most products can be white-labeled with a custom logo and packaging, so confirm this first.
  • Products found only on a distributor's site or in a foreign market still need a supply path. Products made in China can be stocked in a US warehouse, so look for US-based companies that ship from stock in the target market. **
  • Avoid very large or expensive-to-ship products when choosing items to source.

Amazon Best Sellers: research procedures

2 min

Procedure A: category scan

  1. Go to Amazon and search Google for "Amazon Best Seller."
  2. Click the Best Sellers link.
  3. Select a top-level category, such as Toys and Games or Kitchen and Dining.
  4. Scroll through the top 100 products and look for repeated items. Multiple knife listings, for example, indicate a hot category.
  5. For each product of interest, click to open its details.
  6. Check the Best Seller Rank in the top-level category, not the subcategory.
  7. Note the number of reviews.
  8. Record the price point.
  9. Assess whether the product could be private labeled (logo change, packaging change).

Procedure B: Best Sellers to ideas

  1. Open the Amazon Best Sellers page, which lists the top 100 products in each category.
  2. Drill into subcategories to see what sells within each one.
  3. Use these lists to generate product ideas.
  4. Brainstorm in parallel with store visits, recent purchases and questions to friends, using the same logic as dropshipping research at a lower price point.

Procedure C: keyword demand check

  1. Search Amazon for the product keyword, for example "resistance bands."
  2. Open the first 10 or more results.
  3. Scroll to the bottom of each product page and note the BSR in the main category.
  4. If most results have a low BSR (typically under 5,000 in most categories), demand is good, and it is worth looking at competitors and suppliers.
  5. If most results have a high BSR, the product is probably not worth pursuing.

Amazon sales signals

  • Movers and Shakers lists the biggest sales gainers of the past 24 hours and is updated frequently, so checking it daily reveals new products.
  • Best Sellers can be filtered by category on the left side. It shows the top sellers per niche with their review counts. Products that sell well on Amazon can also be sold in markets where Amazon is less dominant.
  • Amazon usually does not run strong promotions such as buy-two-get-one-free. A store can compete with bundles and special deals on products sold on Amazon. The product may be unknown to the target audience, so ads raise awareness.

Best Sellers Rank (BSR)

2 min

What BSR is and where to find it

  • BSR is Amazon's ranking that estimates how many sales a product makes. The lower the number, the more it sells. It can be checked on any Amazon product page.
  • BSR appears at the bottom of every Amazon product listing page. It is the most reliable indicator of whether a product has real demand and is worth entering.
  • On the product page, BSR appears in the product details section just above the reviews, at the bottom of the page. Read the top-level category ranking (for example, Arts and Crafts), not the subcategory ranking.

Read the top-level category only

  • Check BSR in the top-level category only. A product ranked #1 in a subcategory may not sell well if its top-level BSR is 40,000.
  • Do not judge demand from a small subcategory ranking. A product ranked #2 in a subcategory may compete with only two or three products. Judge demand from the main top-level category.

BSR targets and their conflicts

The sources give different thresholds. Each applies under different conditions:

  • General target, 1 to 5,000 (top-level category): This range indicates sufficient sales velocity.
  • General starting point, under 5,000: The usual target is under 5,000, but the threshold depends on the category. Use it as a starting point and adjust it for the category.
  • Low-demand categories, stricter cut-off: In categories such as Musical Instruments, 5,000 is far too loose. Use a stricter cut-off, for example under 1,000.
  • Sports & Fitness: A BSR of 5,000 or lower means roughly 10 to 15 units sold per day.
  • Sales equivalents, which differ by source: A BSR of 2,000 in a top-level category equals about 45 sales per day. The category table below gives different figures for a BSR of 5,000. The sources do not say which category the 45-per-day figure refers to, so use the figure for the category you are researching.

  • Prioritize BSR over review count. A product ranked 10th with a low BSR (selling 10 to 20 units daily) is more valuable than a product ranked 1st with many reviews but a high BSR (selling only 1 to 2 units daily). High sales volume creates room for growth even in competitive conditions.

Competition: reviews and listings

1 min
  • Avoid products with 1,000 or more competitor reviews, which indicate heavy competition. The ideal competitor review count is 200 to 300, which indicates an established market without excessive saturation.
  • On Amazon, a product needs at least one listing with a 4+ rating and at least 20 reviews. Drop it if any listing has 5,000+ reviews with a 4+ rating. The two thresholds are both kept as stated; the source does not say how they combine.
  • Example: a listing with about 1,400 reviews and a rating near 4 stars was estimated at roughly 10,000 orders. Heavy Amazon sales signal demand, but they also make organic TikTok sales harder.

Amazon category trees and idea lists

1 min
  • Find ideas by checking your own purchase history (credit card and bank statements for online purchases), walking through department stores and Walmart, photographing potential products, and browsing Amazon's Top 100 bestsellers by category and subcategory to find under-tapped products.
  • Marketplace category trees, such as Amazon's, cover almost every category sold online. Browse them for individual products or small niches within large categories. **
  • Browsing path: Departments > Industrial & Scientific > Robotics, then drill into the subcategories to see individual products. **
  • On an Amazon results page, use the Sort by dropdown and choose Price: High to Low to see the most expensive products in a category first. **
  • Case: a robotics subcategory showed 146 results. Sorting by price (high to low) revealed only a few expensive products, so the subcategory did not look rich in high-ticket items. Subcategories with few high-priced listings are weaker candidates. **
  • Amazon's Industrial & Scientific department is split into many subcategories, including Cutting Tools, Fasteners, Food Service Equipment & Supplies, Lab & Scientific Products, Material Handling Products and Professional Medical Supplies. Drill into each one for individual products or small niches. **
  • Example: in the Robotics subcategory sorted by price (high to low), the top items were a robot arm kit (uArm Swift Pro), an indoor GPS starter set (Marvelmind), a 1350W DC power supply (Venom Pro Power) and a robot arm (Tinkerkit Braccio). Only a few items at that price appeared among 146 results, so the subcategory looked thin for high-ticket products. **
  • Free category pages on large marketplaces such as eBay and Amazon are good sources for product ideas. Search their category lists for product types you could sell. **

Libraries and physical retail

1 min
  • Local libraries show real demand without ad spend. Look up the books with the highest borrow rate, or ask staff which titles are borrowed most. This works in many countries, including Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, the Philippines, the US, Canada, Germany and Europe.
  • Libraries are a free source for market ideas. Their special-interest magazine collections often include back issues, and their non-fiction books are sorted by topic, which makes interest areas easy to find. **
  • Seeing a product stocked in physical retail, such as a seasonal costume store, is a validation signal that demand exists beyond social media. Check whether a trending gadget is also sold in shops before judging it a fad.
  • Products sold mainly through physical retail stores are often not yet dropshipped online. A product with that profile, a good idea and proven demand can be tested by calling suppliers. **
  • Many products seen in large retail stores are sourced from AliExpress. Spotting them offline and checking their supplier is a valid method.
  • A product featured on a TV investment show may be worth checking as a sourcing idea if it is not yet on AliExpress. When it appears there later with only a few sellers, the number of competing listings stays low, which makes it easier to test.
  • As Seen On TV (free to use) is a source of product ideas. The speaker says almost anything listed there performs well, but this is an opinion, not a tested rule.

Store marketplaces: Flippa and sold stores

1 min
  • Flippa lists Shopify stores for sale together with their revenue and profit figures, so it can be used to find products and niches that already sell. Browse its listings at least weekly to spot stores with working products.
  • Shopify once ran its own marketplace where every store figure was verified by Shopify. Flippa offers verified listings that work the same way. Apply the verified-listing filter in the left-hand panel and ignore unverified profit claims.
  • Procedure:
    1. Open flippa.com and browse Shopify store listings.
    2. Apply the verified-listing filters in the left-hand panel.
    3. Read the verified monthly profit or revenue of each listing.
    4. For promising listings, copy the store domain and open the store.
    5. Study the store's products, niche and price points.
    6. If no domain is available, photograph the product image and use AliExpress image search to find the same product.
    7. Search the brand in the Facebook Ad Library and check how long its ads have been running.
    8. Check the age of the store and repeat this review at least weekly.
  • Case: a Shopify home decor store was listed on Flippa for $1,000,000. The seller claimed Shopify had verified the data and showed about $100,000 profit per month. This was not independently checked. The analyst used AliExpress camera (image) search on the store photos to find the matching supplier products. Then a Shopify best-selling collection URL was appended to the store domain to show the top sellers. The first four best-selling products were tested in Switzerland and Germany. The store sent visitors to a collection page instead of a single product page, and the ads were carousel ads showing several products with the offer "buy two, get the third free." The campaign performed well. A relaunch was planned about one month ahead of spring, because home decor demand rises in spring, while the cold, snowy months had produced little interest.
  • While browsing listings, also look at other niches, including print on demand, which can be tested as an additional business model.

Print-on-demand stores on Flipper

1 min

Procedure:

  1. Create a free account on flipper.com and log in.
  2. Go to Browse, then Websites.
  3. Search for "print on demand," or a keyword such as "fashion," and click Search.
  4. Sort by highest price, highest revenue or most profitable.
  5. Open a store to read its monthly profit, profit margin, years in business and annual revenue. Some listings hide their figures.
  6. Open the About Us page, which shows the niche and sometimes admits to using print on demand. Stores without an About Us page are harder to assess.
  7. Read the product descriptions and check what the store sells, since descriptions can reveal the fulfillment method.

AliExpress

1 min
  • AliExpress orders come mostly from Russia, other Asian countries and parts of Europe. Sales there say little about buyers in the US, UK, Canada or Australia. Use AliExpress for sourcing and quality checks, not as proof of demand in your target market.
  • The AliExpress app camera works as a reverse image search. Photographing a product, such as a shoe, shows matching AliExpress listings. This takes you from an ad found in AdSpy to a supplier listing within minutes.
  • Multiple listings of the same product with a rating of 4.6 or higher and at least 50 reviews indicate that quality is probably acceptable. More reviews make this stronger. Ordering a sample is the most certain check, but it takes time.
  • AliExpress and Temu hold many products, but search with a clear idea of what you want, such as personalized jewelry or fashion items. Browsing without a plan is slow.

Temu

1 min
  • Product and content research sources include competitor ads, Amazon, AliExpress, Alibaba, TikTok, YouTube and Temu. Temu is cited as offering good product pictures, which are better than AliExpress pictures.
  • Temu best sellers can be used to find trending dropshipping products. Cross-check each product on TikTok before testing it.
  • Cross-check procedure:
    1. Take a product from the best sellers list.
    2. Search TikTok for active organic accounts that show it.
    3. Confirm proof of content by tracking the store or placing an order and checking the order number.
    4. If it is proven, look for a different angle for organic posts, ads or both.
  • Check whether a new marketplace such as Temu offers dropshipping integrations and fulfillment before treating it as a supplier option. Confirm it works for your business model first.

Shein

1 min

Procedure for a trending dropshipping product:

  1. Search Google for "bestsellers Shein," which leads to Shein's bestseller pages.
  2. Browse the bestsellers, starting with categories that performed well in earlier products.
  3. Take a phone photo of a promising product.
  4. Open AliExpress on the phone and use its camera search to find the same product.
  5. Check the supplier that appears and source the product from it, for example through CJ Dropshipping.
  • Trending products on Shein are often picked up by TikTok users under 25, so the same products can be advertised on Facebook in markets outside the United States. Products that already trend on one platform can be a useful source of ad products on another.

CJ Dropshipping and Alibaba

1 min
  • Look for products on CJ Dropshipping and AliExpress that look eye-catching and fit your product criteria. Use CJ to find products, then compare suppliers on AliExpress, since AliExpress often offers a wider variety.
  • The date when a CJ product price was last updated is not a reliable sign that a product is inactive. Judge the product by its ad results and test it.
  • Alibaba is listed among product and content research sources.

Trend tools and free sources

2 min
  • Google Trends: Choose a country, type in a broad niche such as "fitness," and look at the related keywords and rising searches. Check which sub-topics are rising.
  • Procedure:
    1. Choose a country in Google Trends (for example, the United States).
    2. Type in a broad niche such as "fitness."
    3. Scroll down to the related keywords and check which sub-topics are rising.
    4. Search those keywords or products on AliExpress or Amazon.
    5. Check whether the product sells well there before testing it.
  • Exploding Topics (explodingtopics.com) lists currently rising trends and lets you filter by category. Use it to check whether a product or niche is growing.
  • Instagram hashtag search can be an extra source of product ideas alongside spy tools such as Peak Star and Droppy Spy. Add promising candidates to the same ranked research list.
  • Jungle Scout shows niches and products that Amazon FBA sellers research, which can be used to find products to source on AliExpress.
  • Seller Pulse shows how many sales a product gets from a specific country. Use it to check market opportunity for a design or product before investing in branding.
  • Dsmoz is a database of niches and suppliers, with close to 400 niches and about 10,000 suppliers at the time of the call, and it was being rebuilt. Niche and supplier lists go stale quickly, so still find current suppliers by researching the future competitors of a niche.
  • Callow is a source to check for items that already sell well, including seasonal kids' toys ahead of the Q4 Christmas period. Looking there lets a beginner spot proven demand without guessing.
  • Wayfair: Best-selling products on competitor stores can be spotted before launch by checking collection pages sorted by "recommended" or best-selling on large retail sites such as Wayfair.
  • Google Shopping: Shopping ads show a product picture, title, price and retailer. Retailers upload product data to Google to advertise. The dedicated Shopping tab is useful for fast price research and is also a future marketing channel. **
  • Google Shopping, broad search: Search the broad niche term without filters and study the first page. Google's first results reflect the products most often bought for that search term. **
  • Use trend tools to filter candidates, not to decide on a product. Combine them with TikTok and Temu research.
  • Stay open-minded and look beyond supplements. Products already selling well in other channels or marketplaces reduce guesswork.

Validate before testing

1 min

Procedure, from trend to supplier:

  1. Find a trending product on Amazon Movers and Shakers or Amazon Best Sellers.
  2. Open the AliExpress app and tap the camera function.
  3. Upload a photo of the product.
  4. Review the similar products AliExpress returns and choose a supplier listing.
  5. Test the product with ads.

Procedure, cost and competition check:

  1. Narrow down to products you understand or care about.
  2. Use the Koala Inspector extension (a free Shopify spy app with freemium searches) to find competitor stores selling similar products.
  3. Right-click the product image and select Google Lens (built into all browsers) to reverse image search.
  4. Examine the results for cheap suppliers and specifically search AliExpress.
  5. Use the AliExpress image search browser extension to find suppliers only on that platform.
  6. Add the product to the supplier's cart and complete a fake checkout with a fake address to reveal the full cost, including hidden shipping fees.
  7. Add the supplier's product price plus shipping cost.
  8. Compare the total against competitor selling prices to verify profitability.
  9. Check the product against your selection criteria before adding it to your list.
  • Example: a product featured on a TV investment show, if it is not yet on AliExpress, can be checked again later; few sellers on AliExpress means fewer competing listings.

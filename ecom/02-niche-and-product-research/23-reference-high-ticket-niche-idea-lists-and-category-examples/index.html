Playbook › 2. Niche and product research

Reference: high-Ticket niche idea lists and category examples

Section 2.23 · 23 of 24 in this chapter · 33 min read

In short

  • Check every product idea for demand, suppliers, profit and shipping before you build a store on it.
  • Choose the buyer first, then the product.
  • A product on a list is only an idea. Verify it yourself.
  • Search with the correct name for products with spelling variants, and count each product once.
  • Older sources and single examples are ideas to test, not proof.
More key points
  • Test a niche (a narrow product market) before you build a shop. Check price, suppliers, competitors, repeat buying and search volume.
  • Heavy or expensive items lose money on shipping or need phone sales. Check the real cost of shipping before you start.
  • Views are not sales. Check whether a product makes sales from its views.
  • Old data (2021 to 2022) can be wrong. Check it against today's numbers first.
  • A product that sells only in one season, or cannot be advertised on Google, is a fail.

Using the category pool

2 min
  • The list has 500 high-ticket niche ideas. High-ticket means expensive products, and a niche is a small, specific market. The list is a brainstorm pool of product categories, not a final verdict.
  • Every candidate still needs checks for demand (how many people want it), supplier availability (whether a supplier, the company that makes or stocks the product, can deliver it), margin (the share of each sale you keep as profit) and shipping before a store is built around it.
  • The list is sorted into B2B (business-to-business, selling to companies), family, fitness, hobbies, kitchen, living room, medical, office, outdoors, pets and sports. Use it for ideas, not to copy entries one by one.
  • Each top-level category points to a buyer. Family means parents, Medical means patients and caregivers, Office means workplaces, Pets means pet owners, and Outdoors or Sports means homeowners and athletes. Define the buyer before choosing a product.
  • Choosing a product only because it is on the list skips the supplier check. The list gives no sourcing (finding where to buy), cost or shipping data. Find these separately for each candidate.
  • Some products appear in more than one category. Baby Rockers is listed twice under Family, Greenhouses appear under Hobbies and Outdoors, and Coffee Tables appear under Living Room and Office. Count each product once when comparing categories.
  • The list has spelling variants, such as Break Makers (most likely bread makers) and Childrens Desks & Chairs. Search suppliers and marketplaces with the corrected term, or you will miss results.
  • Some entries overlap heavily, such as Treadmills, Anti-gravity Treadmills and Aquatic Treadmills. Group overlapping items into one sub-niche (a smaller part of a niche) before comparing demand between them.
  • The example products in a pricing example illustrate a math pattern. They are not product recommendations. Many new sellers copy the same popular niches, so look for your own angle within a chosen niche.
  • A product that passes all niche and product tests can work even if it sounds unusual, such as dining room tables. Run the product through the full criteria before deciding.
  • Typical product types for the gimmick-led model (a model built on a novelty product that grabs attention) include LED galaxy projectors, portable mini printers, unusual kitchen tools and unique pet toys. These are deliberately extreme examples used to explain the category. They are not a verified shortlist.

Home, living room and furniture

2 min
  • High-ticket niche categories to consider include rugs, stand-up desks, leather club chairs, ergonomic office chairs (designed for comfort and body posture), horse saddles, 3D printers, espresso machines, Smith machines (gym machines with a fixed bar) and Pilates reformers (Pilates machines).
  • Rugs and furniture are strong dropshipping categories. Dropshipping means you sell a product, and the supplier ships it straight to the buyer, so you do not hold stock. These categories offer wide variety, can be personalized to customer specifications, and customers expect a wait for delivery.
  • Furniture is generally a good niche at face value. It must still pass the checks for size, price and target demographic (the group of people you aim at by age, gender or income). Some furniture will fail the price check even though the category is good.
  • Cribs and bedroom furniture sets are good niches because buyers do not need to see, feel or touch them before delivery. If the range has many colors and sizes, buyers are usually willing to wait for delivery.
  • A refrigerator niche is weak because most buyers choose from four or five brands. A wine refrigerator niche has many styles and brands and may suit online selling.
  • Product categories with higher price points, worth testing as niches, include 3D printers, laser engraving machines (machines that burn designs into material), CNC machines (computer-controlled cutting machines), horse saddles, ergonomic office furniture, stand-up desks, leather sectionals (large sofas made of several parts), swing sets and backyard playgrounds. Verify their actual prices and margins before relying on them.
  • More higher-ticket candidates to price-check include Persian rugs, leather club chairs, leather sofas and espresso machines. Intuition suggests the price points may be good for most of them. Espresso machine pricing was explicitly left unverified and needs checking.
  • Stand-up desks and leather club chairs were judged possible candidates for the upper middle class (a higher-middle income group). Neither showed clear signs of appealing to wealthy or low income buyers in the review.

Rugs

1 min
  • Rugs are a good niche. There are many expensive options and a wide range of sizes, colors and patterns. Buyers cannot easily find a specific size or pattern at local home improvement stores, so they search online. The price must still cover shipping for large, heavy items.
  • After an AI query, these sub-niches were found for the broad rug category:
    • hand-knotted or hand-woven rugs (tied or woven by hand, knot by knot)
    • rugs with premium materials such as wool, silk or wool-silk blends (mixes of both)
    • traditional heritage rugs (old, classic styles) such as oriental and Persian
    • larger area rugs (rugs sized for a room's floor)
    • collectible or antique rugs, which were judged not relevant
  • Persian and oriental rugs suit older upper middle class households. Collectible and antique rugs were judged not relevant.
  • The rug price and profit figures for Persian, woven and oriental rugs are listed under Price and profit illustrations below.

Lighting, home improvement and kitchen

1 min
  • Lighting and home improvement niches work well with enough product variety and customers who accept waiting for delivery.
  • Lighting and home improvement products are considered good niches.
  • A home seller in Australia used eBay and Facebook. Small kitchen and kids' items sold well and shipped cheaply, including cookie cutters, kids' clothing and spiralisers. Such products fit a low-cost, low-storage start. They are examples to check against current demand, not a fixed shortlist.

Air purifiers

2 min
  • Some air purifier brands use no filters. They rely on a patented (legally protected) air sterilization technology, so there are no filter costs. Such units suit small rooms. They are not recommended for serious gas or odor removal or for reducing household dust.
  • Airpura air purifiers are modular (parts can be swapped or added), so a buyer can reconfigure the unit later. They are priced fairly for their features. Weak points: filter changes need a screwdriver and turning the unit upside down, and there is no filter change monitoring.
  • Alen air purifiers suit general buyers as an all-purpose unit at a moderate price. They have a lifetime warranty (coverage for as long as you own the unit), very easy filter changes, quiet operation and low energy use. They are not recommended for serious gas and odor removal.
  • Austin Air is popular with allergy-focused buyers because it spends its budget on filtration rather than extras. It has above-average particle filtration and very good gas and odor filtration. Its filters are hard to change, requiring a screwdriver and turning the unit upside down.
  • Blueair's HEPASilent technology (a combined filter system) uses electrostatic (electric charge to catch particles), carbon (traps gases and odors) and HEPA-type filtration (a fine filter that traps tiny particles). It gives high filtration with no ozone (a gas some purifiers release). Its units carry the highest CADR (clean air delivery rate, how fast a unit cleans air) certified by AHAM (an industry group that tests air purifiers) in the category. They come in sizes for different rooms, including quiet bedroom models. Newer models add filter life monitoring and pre-filters (an extra first filter for big particles).
  • QuietPure air purifiers use HEPA filtration to remove dust, allergens and VOCs (volatile organic compounds, gases from paints and cleaners) while running very quietly. They are the most budget-friendly of the brands in that comparison. Their range includes a basic Home model, a Home plus Smoke model and a Whisper model.
  • Winix air purifiers combine a space-saving design, easy filter changes, automatic air quality monitoring and Energy Star compliance (an energy-efficiency label) with above-average filtration at a low price. They are not recommended for serious gas and odor removal.

Sport, outdoors and hobbies

1 min
  • A competitor surfboard category was split into hybrid, longboard, mid-length, pro series and shortboard. This breakdown is a useful starting list for sub-collections.
  • Snowboard niche case: the audience was split into beginners struggling to get started and intermediate to advanced riders in different situations. Targeting snowboarders specifically, rather than general sports, gave a more focused message tied to each rider's level and problem. The specific message led to much better marketing performance and let the brand scale massively with one of those audiences.
  • Fishing was named as a niche with strong results in the US and Canada. Treat such claims as a hypothesis (an idea to test, not a proven fact). Test popular hobby markets with similar products.
  • Fishing accessories such as fishing rods were an underrated, profitable dropshipping niche with strong seasonal sales in summer, cited for 2018 and 2019. Seasonal means sales rise and fall with the calendar. Other small-community niches, like canoe accessories or niche extreme sports, follow the same logic.
  • Greenhouses appear under both Hobbies and Outdoors, so count them once when comparing categories.
  • Treadmills, anti-gravity treadmills and aquatic treadmills overlap. Group them into one sub-niche.

Fashion and clothing

1 min
  • The most popular clothing styles to consider as a niche are streetwear, casual wear, athleisure (sporty clothes worn casually) and punk.
  • Women's clothing and fashion for Australia or Europe is a suggested starting niche, especially during cold weather. Choose items that need little explanation so simple image ads can do the selling.

Health, beauty and wellness

2 min
  • A health product was reviewed with these facts. It sold over 745 units, but its ads were heavily bottom-funnel (aimed at people who already want to buy) and lacked a mix of content (different kinds of posts, such as education and stories). It was skipped. Its revenue chart was slowly rising, which was noted as a mild concern about whether the product was stable.
  • A pain-relief supplement brand had no clear audience at first and little traffic (website visitors). Its team reverse engineered (studied and copied the approach of) similar supplements. They found competitors advertising on diabetic websites, survival websites and natural health websites. The team had not known these audiences would respond. After targeting these new groups, the product scaled from about 20,000 USD per month to over 500,000 USD per month in two months.
  • Essential oils have traditionally been marketed to women, so a line aimed at men fills an underserved gap (a need few sellers meet). Marketing, website design and imagery must be built for that audience, not copied from mainstream essential oil brands (the big, well-known names).
  • Two brands in the same hair-care niche sold nearly identical products. KYIR sold a Fuller Active Hair Serum in 50 ml with 30, 90 and 150-day supplies, with 20 percent off the 90-day tier and a 90-day money-back guarantee. Divi sold a Daily Growth Care System with a 30 ml Scalp Serum and hair growth vitamins. It offered a monthly subscription (regular automatic payments) at about 86.36 euros (save 10 percent) versus about 96 euros one-time. Its 100 ml Scalp Serum sold for 41.95 euros one-time. Two brands selling nearly identical products signals a validated direction (proven by checks) worth researching further.
  • A skincare brand, Lumera, sold a Microcrystal Serum PM Duo for 39.99 USD on its ad card, with a 4.7 rating over 2,847 reviews and a 90-day guarantee. Its ad with a problem-first hook (an opening line that names the problem) about acne scars and microcrystal technology had run since about March 2026. The Meta Ad Library (Meta's public list of Facebook and Instagram ads) showed 130 results. Winning Hunter (a tool that counts running ads) counted 124 active ads. The brand was added on May 6, 2026, and data was still filling in. The brand has validated ads worth recreating. Its weak point is that it sends paid traffic straight to a product page. This leaves room for an advertorial (an ad written like an article) that agitates the problem further.
  • A hair-removal shaver was sourced from AliExpress (an online marketplace) at about 10 USD or less. It was sold at 29.99 USD to match competitors, with a 60% profit margin. This case is listed under Failed and stopped products below.

Spirituality, gifts and family

2 min
  • Spirituality, astrology and healing stone products were reported as fast-growing sellers during a fulfillment partner call. A fulfillment partner is a company that stores and ships orders for you. Treat such reports as hypotheses to test in your own market.
  • A store selling healing-stone-style products found its best audience was women aged 45 and over. The seller combined that niche with a second interest into an ebook (a digital book), offered it at 19 USD, and ran ads before the book existed. The early click-through rate (the share of viewers who click the ad) was described as quite good. Sales were still unknown at the time of the call.
  • A customized message gift priced at $60, aimed at fathers buying for their sons, showed about 120% progression (growth over time). The same personalized structure can be reused for other gift relationships with a different copy angle (a different way of wording the message). Do this without copying the competitor's page word for word.
  • The first winner was a kids LED skirt sold in Q4 2020 in the gifting niche. It earned about 10,000 euros in one month. Ads targeted grandparents and parents with a retirement-related angle. The founders realised the product was seasonal to Christmas and would fade afterwards. (Older source: Q4 2020.)
  • The second product was a problem-solving item with viral potential (a chance to spread fast online). It was chosen after about three months of saving and new research. It brought in about 16,000 euros in its first month at a 26% profit margin. It later moved to custom labeling (putting your own brand name on the product) and inventory (stock you hold), in addition to dropshipping.
  • A draw-following train toy, where a toy train follows a line drawn on paper by a child, previously sold very successfully during seasonal testing.
  • A teach-your-child-to-read book, Teach Your Child to Read in 100 Easy Lessons by Siegfried Engelmann, was first published in 1983. It was built on the DISTAR method (a scripted teaching method). The speaker reported over one million copies sold. Its product page for the last 30 days showed the figures listed in the Market signals section below.
  • A Halloween slides product was priced at $25 with $5 shipping. The seller who scaled it first uploaded a video organically (free, unpaid reach), then used spark ads (TikTok ads that boost an existing post). The videos reached about 17 million and 18.4 million views. The store was a single product page with no homepage, only one product and no downsells (cheaper backup offers shown after a "no"). Its sizes were in US format. It reported about $134,000 in sales from September 15 to September 26, roughly 11 days, with order number 6368 and about 5,367 orders. The store had a 60 percent off banner and urgency text (words like "ends soon" that push buyers to act now). It was built before Halloween.

Pets

1 min
  • Pet products are considered a strong category because owners spend heavily on their animals.
  • A pet product with 640 active ads, running only as a single image ad, was marked as a strong candidate.
  • An educational kids' toy with 273 active ads was running in only one European country. This signaled room to test other markets, such as the US or the rest of Europe. Even weak reviews and an average website did not stop it from being a valid candidate.
  • A human version of a viral pet grooming comb was found on TikTok. Its top video was uploaded on July 17, had about 4 million views, and the account was still posting. A sample purchase showed brand sales of about $1,800 to $1,900. That works out to roughly $500 in sales per million views. The product was judged promising but not proven. The $500-per-million-views figure was used as the benchmark for deciding whether to test it.

Vehicles and off-road

1 min
  • Hardtop gazebos (garden shelters with a solid roof) are an example of a high-ticket product. They retail for about $3,000 and have sold in very large volumes, with over $1,000,000 in revenue in a single month reported.
  • Searching 4x4 accessories (parts for four-wheel-drive off-road vehicles) in Australia on Google Shopping (Google's product results page), then browsing several retailer sites, showed products from about $800 to over $3,000. These included rear cargo drawers (pull-out storage at the back of a vehicle) around $1,000, lift kits (parts that raise a vehicle) around $800 to $2,200, and canopies (roof covers for a truck bed) at about $3,000. Based on the range seen across sites, the working average price was estimated at $1,000.
  • Caravans and RVs (recreational vehicles, such as motorhomes): average price 900 USD, products suitable for shipping, and an enthusiast market (people who love the hobby) because RVs are bought for recreation rather than need. The niche had 10 direct competitors, which was acceptable for Australia. It had plenty of suppliers, a range of product types sold to the same customer for recurring revenue (money that keeps coming in), and 175,000 combined monthly searches. It was approved (green).

Home services and bulky goods

1 min
  • Pool tables need slate (a stone slab) installed, leveling (making the table flat) and felt (cloth covering) attached. Most sellers left customers to handle this. A seller who dominated this market dropshipped the table. It posted local ads for installers in the customer's city and hired freelance contractors. These contractors met the curbside delivery (left at the curb, not inside) and installed the table in the home. The seller could charge a premium and made many sales, because the extra steps were the differentiator (the thing that set it apart). (Older source: Source A, describing a magazine article about a decade earlier.)
  • Adding product types can broaden a niche. Stand-up paddle boards alone gave eight suppliers. Adding kayaks gave about 12, and adding kite boarding brought the total to 25 suppliers, which passes the minimum.
  • A print-on-demand store (where each item is printed only after an order) with eight years in business was reported at about £6,000 profit per month. It had a 44% profit margin and annual revenue around £188,000. Verify such figures on the store listing before relying on them.

Validation: worked niche cases

4 min

It is a very large online retailer that dropships most of its products, about 99% by the estimate used there. One competitor met the traffic rank condition (site popularity rank) under 100,000. The niche therefore passed validation. The next step was choosing which niche to pursue first.

The top text ads and organic results were opened in new tabs, and known chains with stores were skipped. Google Shopping's seller list was then opened. Its sellers were checked one by one through the header (top of the website), footer, About us, returns page, Google Maps and brand filters.

Validation: worked niche cases

5 min

A niche is one narrow product market. Validation means testing a niche with simple checks before you build a shop for it.

  • Generator niche validation (US, 2021 setting):
    • The owner searched Google Shopping (Google's product price search) for generators.
    • Many listings were above 1,000 USD. The price test passed, with an average of 1,000 USD or more.

    • Competitor counts per product were mostly 5 or fewer, or borderline at 10 plus. Competitors are other sellers of the same product. Competition passed.
    • Recurring potential (other products the same buyer may buy again) existed through log splitters (machines that split wood), snowblowers and pressure washers.
    • Search volume (how many times people search a term each month) was high across the related keywords. Traffic passed.
    • The enthusiast question (are buyers hobby fans, not just people who need it?) was rated maybe. Some buyers need a generator and others want one. That gives 5 yes answers out of 6.

    • Average price was 800 USD.
    • The products suit shipping.
    • The buyers want the product, but do not need it. So it is an enthusiast market.
    • There were 18 suppliers and 8 direct competitors.
    • A range of product types had healthy search volume.

  • The top 5 niches in the example:
    • All have a minimum profit of $854 per sale.
    • All pass the price point, demographic, brand loyalty and seasonality tests.
    • All have demand above 10,000 monthly searches.
  • CNC machines:
    • Average price $11,000.
    • Average profit about $2,700 per sale.
    • Most were rated A+ by the research tool.
    • They pass the tests named here.
    • The high price usually means pre-selling is needed. Pre-selling means taking orders by phone call before the sale.
    • So this niche only suits someone willing to take phone calls.
  • The CNC starting product in the validation example was priced at about $1,900. The average price benchmark here is $11,000.
  • Both figures come from different data sets. Apply each one to the price range it describes.
  • Massage chairs:
    • Average price $3,400.
    • Average profit $854 per sale.
    • 110,000 monthly searches.
    • Some buyers may still call before ordering, because the price is above $2,000.
    • The niche looks strong.
  • Rooftop tents (tents that sit on a car roof):
    • Price about $2,000.
    • Profit about $500 per sale.
    • 33,000 monthly searches.
    • Buyers at this price usually purchase online without calls.
    • This fits a hands-off business (one that needs little personal contact).
  • Budget dining table:
    • A dining table sells for $500 with free shipping.
    • A big, heavy table of this kind costs about $250 to ship on average.
    • COGS (cost of goods sold, what you pay the supplier for the product) is also $250.
    • Before customer acquisition (the cost of getting a buyer), card fees (payment processing fees), the seller's time and traffic costs, net profit is $0.
    • Net profit is the money left after all costs.
    • Once those costs are added, every sale loses money.
    • The conclusion is not that dining tables are a bad niche.
    • The conclusion is that this price point is too low for the item's size and weight.
  • Premium dining table:
    • A solid wood dining table sells for $1,200 with free shipping.
    • Because it is heavier, average shipping cost is $300.
    • COGS is $600, which is 50% of the selling price.
    • Total costs are $900, so net profit is $300 per order before other expenses.
    • The $300 shipping figure is a high-end assumption. It applies to roughly the top 2 to 3 percent of orders, since most orders ship for less.
    • If supplier and customer are both in California, shipping drops to $150. COGS and price stay the same.
    • That raises net profit to $450 per order.
    • Even a cross-country shipment at $300 still nets $300 per order.
  • Gun safes:
    • A large, heavy safe priced at $200 cannot be shipped profitably with free shipping.
    • Such safes are workable (possible to sell for profit) only in the roughly $800 to $1,000-plus price range.
    • In that range, the margin covers COGS and shipping.
  • Liver detox supplement example:
    • Judged promising with these figures.
    • Revenue of 46,000 dollars in the last 30 days.
    • 1,008 to 1,100 units sold.
    • Product price 34.18 dollars.
    • Shipping fee 5.00 dollars.
    • Commission rate of 30 percent. The commission rate is the cut paid to the creator (a person who promotes the product) who sells it.
    • Its revenue graph went up and down.
    • Its ads were mostly bottom-funnel, meaning aimed at people ready to buy.
    • The ads had strong editing (how videos are cut) and a varied set of avatars (the types of customer the ads speak to).
  • The liver detox example was checked further over the last 90 days.
    • The revenue chart peaked at about 3k, then started to slow down.
    • Before committing, the trend was reviewed again.
    • The decision was to customize the ads rather than copy them.

Viral and challenge tests

1 min
  • A TikTok challenge account (an account that posts challenge videos) for an instant ice cream product. The team was given the product, not allowed to choose it. The test was late in the summer season.
    • About six videos were drafted.
    • In the first two days, the checkered red-and-white background looked messy and results were weak.
    • A blue juice video using a corn trend reached only about 7,000 views.
    • Comments kept mentioning Breaking Bad (a famous TV series).
    • The team posted a video reply to a top comment. The reply was built on that Breaking Bad reference.
    • That reply reached about 1.1 million views and went viral (spread very widely).
  • On day three, the team changed the background.
    • They switched the whole account to Breaking Bad themed videos. These used dialogue, theme songs and on-screen text.
    • Four videos passed 1 million views.
    • The account reached about 8 million views in two weeks. Later figures put the total at nearly 10 million.
    • It had around 400,000 likes.
    • Only about 1% converted to followers (became followers). The expected figure was about 40,000 followers.
    • The Instagram version reached about 500 followers after posting late. It did not convert sales.
  • Uploads were 3 per day, and 4 on the viral day. Posts went out around 12:00, around 16:00 and between 18:00 and 19:00.
    • Sales did not reach the team's target of sales per million views.
    • So the challenge was treated as a views exercise.
    • Uploads stopped when it ended.
    • Ad spend (money spent on ads) was zero.
    • The only costs were the product, which was returned for a refund, and food.

Failed and stopped products

1 min
  • A cheap hair-removal shaver, sourced from AliExpress at about 10 USD or less.
    • It was sold at 29.99 USD to match competitors. The profit margin (the share of the price kept as profit) was 60%.
    • Its own team tested it for two weeks with consistent uploads.
    • It got about 8 million views, with one video at about 5 million views.
    • Sales reached about 5,000 USD. About 3,000 USD was kept after costs.
    • Views and sales did not match. 5,000 USD on 8 million views is about 625 USD per million views. That is below the 1,000 USD per million target.
    • Quality problems led to exposure, and the team stopped the product:
      • The product worked only for 3 to 5 uses.
      • It caused skin redness and burns.
      • It was awkward to use.
    • Competitors had used one consistent female model. They reused the same intros (opening lines or clips at the start of videos) until their views dropped.
  • The paintball niche was rejected. Its guns cannot be advertised on Google, and its average price fell below the minimum.
  • A health product that sold over 745 units was skipped. Its ads were heavily bottom-funnel.
  • The liver detox example was not committed to until its 90-day revenue slowdown was reviewed.

Seasonal and short-term products

1 min
  • Trampolines and motorcycle helmets both showed strong seasonality. They failed the evergreen test (a product that sells all year).
  • So both were moved to red (rejected).
  • Halloween and gifting products (items bought as presents) with a holiday angle are seasonal.
  • They will fade after the event.
  • Check a seasonal product against its sales window (the months when it sells) before you commit to a store around it.

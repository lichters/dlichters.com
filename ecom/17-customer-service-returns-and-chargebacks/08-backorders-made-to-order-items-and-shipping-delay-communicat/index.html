Playbook › 17. Customer service, returns and chargebacks

Backorders, made-to-order items and shipping-delay communication

Section 17.8 · 8 of 22 in this chapter · 17 min read

In short

  • Tell customers about any wait before they pay, and give the real date.
  • Confirm the wait by email after each order, before the order goes to the supplier.
  • If something is late, write to customers first. Call them when needed. Silence causes refunds and chargebacks.
  • Do not sell products needed by a fixed date, like Christmas, unless delivery is sure to arrive in time.
  • Avoid shipping routes of 3 to 5 weeks and launches during holidays.

Setting expectations before the sale

2 min
  • Keep taking orders for an out-of-stock item (an item you cannot ship right now) when restock is a few weeks away.
  • Add a note at the top of the product description with the estimated restock date.
  • Put information about delayed shipping prominently in the product description. Do not put it only at checkout (the payment page).
  • State up front: assembly requirements, shipping duration, and who is responsible for installation.
  • Tell customers the exact date an out-of-stock product will be back. Do this on the product page and right after the order.
  • Customers accept a wait when they know what is happening. They are far less likely to email later asking where the product is.
  • A customer whose item is delayed should be told on the website and by email that the item is backordered. A backorder means the customer orders now, and the item ships later, when stock arrives. Give the expected ship date. **
  • When a product is backordered, show the expected date on the product page.
  • Also offer a deposit option, so customers can order now. A deposit is a small part of the price paid first. **
  • Make the lead time visible before checkout. Lead time is the time from order to delivery.
  • Confirm the lead time by email after the order.
  • The risk of a chargeback rises when a customer does not know a product is built to order. A chargeback means the customer asks their bank to take the money back, and the store loses it.
  • Be open with customers and suppliers about production time (how long making the product takes) and delivery time.
  • Customers often expect instant delivery, and production cannot give that. Give realistic times from the start.
  • Customers who order unique products they cannot find locally usually accept a shipping delay of up to about two weeks.
  • Their patience is much lower for products needed for a fixed occasion.
  • Products must be delivered before the promised date. Otherwise, you risk chargebacks.
  • Avoid selling products tied to fixed dates unless delivery is guaranteed in time.
  • Avoid supplier routes with three to five weeks of shipping. Long shipping times caused many refunds (money the store gives back to the customer).
  • Avoid launching during holidays that delay shipping. Late orders lead to complaints and payment disputes. Those can put a Stripe account (the payment company that takes card payments) and a Facebook account (the one you use for ads) at risk.

Confirmation emails and made-to-order follow-up

2 min

Made to order means the product is only made after the customer buys it. Follow this procedure for made-to-order sales:

  1. After the order is placed, contact the customer by phone or email to thank them.
  2. Confirm that the product is made to order.
  3. State the estimated manufacturing and delivery time.
  4. Check that the customer understands and accepts the lead time.
  • Email the customer after the sale to confirm the lead time before the order goes to the supplier. The customer is then not surprised by a delay.

Confirmation email for made-to-order items. Fill in the ETA (estimated time of arrival, the expected delivery date) from the supplier's lead time. Send it after each sale, before the order goes to the supplier:

Thank you for your order. You should have received your order confirmation already. We just want to confirm that this product is made to order, and the estimated delivery is [ETA]. Please reply to confirm you are aware of this.

Template for the first confirmation, with a lead-time placeholder. A placeholder is a blank in square brackets that you fill in:

Hi [customer name], thank you for your order with [store name]. We have received it. I just want to make sure you saw that [product name] is made to order, and the estimated manufacturing and delivery time is [four to six weeks]. If you have any questions, reply to this email or call us. Thank you!

Made-to-order follow-up sent immediately after purchase, by email or phone. The lead time in the example is 16 weeks. **

Thank you for your order for [product name]. Please confirm that you understand this product is made to order, with a current estimated time of [lead time, e.g., 16 weeks] for production and delivery. We will keep you updated throughout the process.

Backorder confirmation. It asks the customer to confirm they saw the note on the product page:

Hi [customer name], thank you for your order of [product name]. We want to confirm that you saw the note on the product page that this item is on backorder with an estimated ship date of [estimated ship date]. Please reply to confirm that you are okay with waiting until that date, and if you have any questions, just let us know. Thank you again for your order, [store name].
  • Answer complaints about delayed shipping with the lead time.
  • If a supplier delays the order, tell the customer before the shipment date. Do not wait for them to ask.
  • Keep customers informed about their order at all times.
  • Shopify's automated emails (sent by the store software you build the shop on) cover much of this. You must still contact the customer directly in many cases.

Backorders: first contact, escalation and revised dates

6 min

Escalation procedure (the steps to take when a backordered product causes problems):

  1. Put a notice above the Add to Cart button (the button that puts an item in the shopping cart). The notice says the item is backordered and gives the expected date.
  2. Send a confirmation email right after each purchase. Ask the customer to confirm that they are willing to wait.
  3. Stop paid ads (paid online adverts) for backordered products.
  4. Restructure the store so the most profitable in-stock products appear first.
  5. Run only a small, tight Google Shopping campaign (paid ads that show products in Google search) with the best-margin in-stock products. Margin is the profit per sale. Keep it running until stock (inventory) is no longer a problem.
  6. Keep looking for and following up on new supplier approvals in the meantime.
  7. When stock returns, remove the notices and follow-up emails. Restore ad spend on the other products, and rebuild the best sellers.

Procedure when the supplier's date for a backorder slips (moves later):

  1. Call each customer who placed the order first.
  2. If the customer does not answer, leave a voicemail and say an email will follow.
  3. Explain the new ship date and the reason. Offer to cancel the order or keep it.
  4. If the customer keeps the order, process it when stock arrives. That customer is first on the list.
  5. Send the follow-up email to everyone, even those reached by phone.
  6. Do not cancel all orders without first trying to keep them.

Phone script and voicemail for customers whose backordered order has a new ship date. A call script is the words you say. A voicemail is the message you leave on their phone. Replace the placeholders in square brackets before use.

Call script: Hi [FIRST NAME], this is [YOUR NAME] from [STORE NAME]. We know you placed order [ORDER NUMBER]. Unfortunately, due to [REASON], the product will not ship until [NEW DATE]. If you would like to cancel your order, we can cancel it for you right away. If you would like to keep it, we will process it when it arrives, and you will be one of the first to receive it because you are already at the top of the list.

Voicemail: Hi [FIRST NAME], this is [YOUR NAME] from [STORE NAME] about your order [ORDER NUMBER]. The product now ships on [NEW DATE] due to [REASON]. Please call me back, and I am also sending you an email with the details.

Procedure for a backorder with a supplier delay. **

  1. Keep the payment pre-authorized at checkout. Pre-authorized means the money is held on the card but not yet taken. Do not capture it (take the held money) until the item can ship.
  2. When a supplier reports a backorder, contact the customer with the expected ship date.
  3. Ask whether the customer is willing to wait for the exact product. You may optionally offer a small discount (for example 5 percent) for waiting.
  4. If the customer will not wait, offer the closest in-stock alternative that can ship within 48 hours.
  5. If the customer declines both options, refund the payment.

Backorder email for customers whose order is delayed. Pick the bracketed refund sentence that matches whether the money has been captured (taken). **

Hi [Customer name],

Thank you for your order of [product name] on [order date]. We have just learned that this item is backordered at our supplier's warehouses, and the earliest expected ship date is [ship date].

If you really want this exact product, we can ship it to you as soon as it is back in stock, as one of the first shipments out. Would you be willing to wait until [ship date]? [Optional: As a thank you for waiting, we can give you [discount, for example 5%] off.]

If you would rather not wait, [if payment is not yet captured: you have not been charged yet, so there is nothing to refund; if payment is already captured: we will refund your full payment right away]. We also have [alternative product name], which is extremely similar: [key benefit 1], [key benefit 2], [key benefit 3]. It can ship within 48 hours.

Please reply to this email and tell us which option you would like: wait for [product name], switch to [alternative product name], or receive a refund.

Thank you for your patience,
[Your name]
[Store name]

Phone script for a backorder with a new earliest ship date. It offers the same choice at the later date. **

Hi [CUSTOMER NAME], this is [YOUR NAME] from [STORE NAME]. We see you just placed an order for [PRODUCT NAME]. This product is currently backordered, and I'm sorry about that. It's going to be back in stock on [ETA DATE]. If it's about [TIME UNTIL STOCK], we can ship it to you first out the door when the shipment comes in. Would you be willing to wait? If the wait is longer, the earliest date it can ship is [SHIP DATE], and we would make you the same offer then. Please know this ETA is the same no matter where you order this product, because it applies to every seller. If you need it sooner, we have a similar product, [ALTERNATIVE PRODUCT NAME], that can be shipped within 24 to 48 hours with a [PARTIAL REFUND AMOUNT] partial refund. If you'd rather keep your order, we'll make sure you get updates and ship it as soon as possible. Which option would you like?
  • For long backorders, you can leave the card uncharged (not yet charged) until the product is ready. Ask whether the customer wants to wait. Customers generally accept this when they are informed.
  • If the payment is only pre-authorized, there is nothing to refund when the customer declines.
  • If the payment is already captured, refund the full amount right away.
  • Customer communication is key. It is what separates a decent store from a great one.
  • Keep customers informed about delays, replacements, ship dates and arrival dates. **
  • When delivery is delayed, keep the customer updated with each revised estimated arrival date.
  • You can add a helpful extra in a postscript (P.S., a note at the end of an email), such as an installation video or a related article. **
  • For shipping delays, be proactive. That means contacting the customer before they ask. Give regular status updates.
  • Explain that the expected date is the same for everyone. If possible, offer a similar in-stock product as a replacement.
  • A free gift is a nice extra. It does not replace clear communication. **
  • Selling without stock and leaving customers unaware leads to angry follow-up messages after two weeks.
  • Tell customers about the delay automatically at the time of ordering.
  • When a customer will not wait for an out-of-stock product, offer a different design from the slow-selling stock (items that sell slowly) as a gift.
  • Most customers accepted this and waited for their full order.
  • If customers ask about delayed delivery, reply promptly and explain the situation. Examples are high demand after a viral video (one that spreads very fast online) or shipping slowdowns.
  • Customers who get a timely explanation are often understanding.
  • Send proactive updates about delays before customers complain. For example, send a short email saying the order is still late and you are monitoring it.
  • Proactive communication prevents most anger.
  • Keep the customer informed at every step when delays happen. Address concerns before the customer gets angry.
  • Frequent, honest communication reduces complaints, even when the delay cannot be avoided.

Quick update email for an order that the supplier is taking longer to process:

Subject: Quick update on your order {order_number}

Hi {customer_name},

I wanted to give you a quick update on your order for {product_name}. Our supplier is taking longer than expected to process it, and I am actively monitoring the shipment. I will send you your next update by {next_update_date}.

Thank you for your patience and understanding. If you have any questions in the meantime, just reply to this email.

Out-of-stock and delay notices

3 min

Handling out-of-stock items or long shipping times:

  1. Send a confirmation email as soon as the problem is known. Include a clear apology.
  2. State the realistic shipping time. In the example, it was two to three weeks.
  3. Offer an immediate refund if the customer does not want to wait.
  4. Answer all customer messages promptly. Keep the tone respectful and honest.

In the seller's German market, telling customers about a long shipping time up front led to almost no refunds. Staying silent while customers expected fast shipping led to angry emails and refund requests. In the end, the store was shut down.

Out-of-stock update email. The example gives a delivery window (the time range for delivery) of 2 to 3 weeks:

Subject: Update on your order [Order number]

Hi [First name],

Thank you for your order of [Product name]. We just found out that this item is currently out of stock, and we are sorry for the inconvenience. Your order is expected to arrive within 2 to 3 weeks. If you would prefer not to wait, reply to this email and we will refund your payment right away.

Warm regards,
[Store name] Customer Care

Delay email that offers two choices, a full refund or a wait of about 15 days:

Subject: Your order is delayed

Thank you very much for your order. Due to huge demand we are currently out of stock, and we are very sorry for the delay. You have two options: you can request a full refund, or you can wait about 15 days for your order to arrive. Simply reply to this email with your choice, and we will take care of it immediately. Thank you for your patience and for choosing [BRAND NAME].

Note on the wait windows: the 15-day window in the email above and the 2 to 3 weeks in the other out-of-stock notices come from different stores. Use the realistic date from your supplier in every case.

Email for a digital product (a file, such as a guide, sent by email instead of shipped) when the fulfilment system (the system that processes orders) is briefly delayed:

Subject: Thank you for your order

Hi [FIRST NAME],

Thank you very much for your order of [PRODUCT NAME]. We are currently experiencing a short delay in our fulfilment system, and we apologise for the wait. Your digital guide will be sent to [CUSTOMER EMAIL] within two to three days, and sooner if possible. Thank you for your patience, we hope you enjoy it.

Kind regards,
[SHOP NAME] Team
  • Last year, a seller grew a mini-winner (a small product that sold very well). The supplier could not ship on time.
  • A flammable lighter item (one that catches fire easily) was held in customs (the border check). Nearly 200 orders were not shipped. The orders were sent again until the third try.
  • The seller emailed every customer. The email said the delivery was late, not a scam.
  • Delivery took nearly six weeks. Only two chargebacks happened.
  • Delays from slow suppliers such as AliExpress (a large online supplier site) can be handled in the early stage. Talk to customers often.
  • Regular emails back and forth keep most buyers patient.
  • The same long routes were also linked to many refunds. So this works for early-stage stores (new stores) that message actively.
  • It does not work for routes with three to five weeks of shipping.

Made-to-order lead-time changes and compensation

1 min

Handling a made-to-order product whose lead time grows (for example, from 4 to 6 weeks to 10 to 12 weeks), and customers cancel. **

  1. Show the current lead time on the product page.
  2. Tell affected customers the delay comes from the supplier's warehouse inventory. So cancelling and ordering elsewhere gives the same wait.
  3. Offer a gift that costs under 100 dollars but has high perceived value to the customer (it feels worth a lot to them), to keep the order.
  4. If there is no suitable gift, offer a partial refund (money back for part of the price) of about 100 dollars to the original card.

Handling a made-to-order collection on the store (a group of products shown together). **

  1. Create a separate collection called Made to Order, or custom made, for these products.
  2. Keep the products also in the main collections.
  3. Show the lead time next to Add to Cart, and send the follow-up confirmation after purchase.

Compensation options by situation:

Situation Option Source
Customer declines a backorder but payment is still uncaptured Nothing to refund; offer a 48-hour alternative
Customer waits for an exact product Optional discount, for example 5 percent
Customer will not wait for an out-of-stock design Gift from the slow-selling stock
Lead time grows and customer cancels Gift under 100 dollars, or partial refund of about 100 dollars if no suitable gift
Late Q4 order 50 percent refund or another gift
Alternative product can ship sooner Partial refund amount to the customer

Split shipments and multiple sources

1 min
  • When one order ships from two warehouses (storage places), send an automated email through Klaviyo (an email tool) after the order is placed.
  • The email should say that one item is in stock closer to the customer. Include tracking (a code to follow the parcel) for the item that ships first.
  • A split order from two suppliers ships as two parcels. That raises shipping cost compared with one supplier shipping the same items together.
  • Accept this cost, or consolidate the catalogue (product list) to one fulfilment source.

Carriers and payment delays

1 min
  • For a delayed freight delivery (a large shipment), check the shipping company's online reviews before deciding whether to keep using it.
  • Note expected delivery times on the shipping policy page. A one-off delay is not a reason to change carrier (shipping company). (2023.)
  • For an order paid by PayPal e-check (a bank transfer through PayPal), the payment may take two to three weeks to clear. It may not clear at all.
  • Tell the customer the order will ship when the e-check clears. (2023.)

Supplier slowdowns, seasonal peaks and Q4 cutoffs

1 min

Q4 means the last three months of the year (October to December). A cutoff is the last day to order and still arrive in time.

  • During a supplier slowdown such as Chinese New Year (a holiday when many Chinese factories slow down), some organic orders (orders that come in without paid ads) still arrive.
  • Tell customers about expected delays in advance. This prevents refund requests caused by silence about shipping times.

Procedure for a product that goes viral a few days before Christmas:

  1. If a product goes viral a few days before Christmas and customers place orders unlikely to arrive by the holiday, accept the orders.
  2. Send follow-up emails to every customer confirming their order and shipping timeline.
  3. Update them throughout fulfilment (packing and shipping) with tracking.
  4. Even if products arrive after December 25, consistent communication reduces chargebacks and improves customer satisfaction.
  • In Q4, customers who buy about 5 days before Christmas, hoping for delivery in time, often file chargebacks when the product arrives late.
  • This is a major Q4 risk for dropshipping stores (stores where the supplier ships each order directly).

Procedure for setting a Q4 cutoff and handling late orders:

  1. Work out the last order date that still delivers before Christmas, based on supplier and shipping times.
  2. State the delivery risk clearly on the store and in emails.
  3. If an order will arrive late, email the customer early and explain the plan.
  4. Offer a compensation option such as a 50% refund or another gift.
  5. Use this plan to avoid chargebacks.

The two Q4 procedures above differ. The viral procedure accepts late orders and relies on messages after December 25. The cutoff procedure sets a last order date, based on supplier and shipping times.

  • Use the viral procedure for sudden demand (a spike, a sudden jump) close to the holiday.
  • Use the cutoff procedure for Christmas sales you plan ahead.

Long backorders and cash

1 min
  • A seven-figure store (one earning more than one million dollars a year) had about 3 million dollars in annual sales. It handled 300 to 500 orders per month.
  • COVID (the pandemic) pushed orders to about 1,000 per month. Many orders went on backorder.
  • The store kept about 500 to 700 of those customers by asking them to wait.
  • But the backorders stretched for months, while normal costs and the owner's pay continued.
  • Customers grew tired of waiting and asked for refunds.
  • The owner realized the account might not cover both supplier bills and refunds. So the owner asked for help in a panic. **

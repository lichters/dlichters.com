Playbook › 17. Customer service, returns and chargebacks

Other details

Section 17.22 · 22 of 22 in this chapter · 18 min read

In short

  • Unpaid invoice after shipment: call the customer first, then text, then check the delivery proof, then message on LinkedIn or Facebook.
  • A chargeback (money taken back from you by the customer's bank or PayPal) carries risk. Do not make it your default.
  • Charge for shipping only for upgraded services. Otherwise, offer free standard delivery.
  • Answer every customer with the same frame: greeting, facts, timeframe, closing, sign-off.
  • Talk to real buyers. They reveal the bottleneck (the one thing slowing you down).

Payment, collections and chargebacks

3 min

Unpaid invoice after shipment. An invoice is a bill. If a customer does not pay an invoice after shipment, follow these steps:

  1. Call the customer.
  2. Text the customer.
  3. Check the tracking record (the shipping company's log of where the package went) to prove delivery.
  4. Message the customer on LinkedIn or Facebook if they still do not respond.

Pending PayPal payment. A pending payment is one that is not finished yet. For a customer whose PayPal payment is pending, check the notification for a reason first. If none is shown, contact PayPal support.

Chargebacks and refunds in the sales plan. A refund is money given back to the customer. A chargeback is money the customer's bank or PayPal takes back from you. Refunds, chargebacks and lifetime value (the total money one customer brings you over time) often get too little attention during the front-end sale (the first sale to a customer). Include them in every sales plan and report them with each offer.

Chargeback risk from test orders. A dummy test order is a fake purchase made only to study a rival store. When placing one on a competitor's store, contact their support first. Say the order was placed by mistake. Request cancellation or a refund. Expect refusal, often with a claim that the order has already shipped. Some testers then consider a chargeback. This carries risk. It should not be the default.

Benchmark from a store with low disputes. A benchmark is a reference point to compare yourself against. A founder (the owner who started the business) working from Thailand reported more than 1,000 orders with two chargebacks and fewer than one refund per month. The details are in the case study below.

Collection letters: persuasion and threat in turn. Collection letters are letters asking for unpaid money. A collection series is a set of these letters sent over time. Alternate persuasion with the threat of loss in a collection series. A strong threat frightens some debtors (people who owe money) into paying, but it angers others. So follow a threat with a persuasive letter that restores goodwill (good feeling toward you) before the next threat.

Collection letters: appeal to honesty. Formula: remind the debtor of an honest trait he is proud of, such as paying one's debts. Then say that his account (his balance with you) reflects that trait. Why it works: a debtor who sees himself as honest is more likely to pay than one who feels accused.

  • Example one: Most of our customers pay their accounts promptly because they value their word, and we are confident you are one of them.
  • Example two: Our records show your history of paying on time, and this one invoice is simply waiting for your confirmation to close.

Collection letters: amount, date, consequence and benefit. Formula: state the exact amount owed, the specific date by which payment must arrive, the consequence if it does not, and the benefit of paying now. Why it works: a precise deadline and consequence are more likely to prompt action than a general reminder.

  • Example one: Your balance of 25.00 dollars is due by the 17th. If payment is not received by then, your account will be recorded as overdue in our credit file (the record of how someone pays their debts).
  • Example two: Your invoice of 340 dollars is due by the 30th. Paying by then keeps your account in good standing (in good shape, with no unpaid debt) and lets you order next month without a hold (a block that stops new orders).

Collection letters: specific threats. Threats in collection letters must be specific. State the amount, the deadline date and the exact next step, such as a transfer to the legal department. A vague warning is easy to ignore.

Collection letters: before and after record. In a payment reminder, show the debtor the exact current entry on his account. Show also the entry it will become if payment does not arrive by a set date. A visible before-and-after record makes the consequence concrete. Keep the consequence limited to actions you are legally entitled to take.

Shipping, delivery and checkout costs

2 min

Charging for shipping. Do not charge customers for shipping unless it is for an upgraded service, such as expedited shipping (faster delivery) or white glove delivery (the carrier brings the item inside and may unpack it). If free shipping margins (the money left after costs) are insufficient for specific suppliers, stop running ads to those products. Rely only on organic traffic (free visitors who find you without ads).

Shipping cost as an expense. Shipping costs are an expense that comes out of gross profit (money left after product costs). They are not reduced by the store's own margin.

Default offer: free standard delivery. Offer free standard delivery by default. Let customers upgrade to white glove delivery or white glove with assembly. This model has worked well compared with charging for every delivery option.

Checkout transparency. A checkout is the payment page. Use a checkout that shows shipping costs and fees clearly. This avoids shock at checkout. Customers who do not expect extra costs may abandon the order (leave without buying) or return the item.

Residential or commercial address.

  • Customers may be asked whether an address is residential (a home) or commercial (a business). Use a Shopify app (an add-on for the software your store runs on) or a native option (built in) if available. If none exists, contact Shopify support to check.
  • For delivery questions about residential versus commercial addresses, check Shopify support for an app or native option before making changes to checkout.

Customer accounts. Customer account login is usually enabled. Most stores see little use because purchases are one-off (a single purchase) or infrequent.

Installation.

  • When a customer needs installation for a product, avoid taking on installation liability (legal responsibility for faults or damage) directly. Refer customers to local installers. Build a network of local installers in major cities.
  • For a product that is basic furniture or not regularly installed by professionals, the freight-to-third-party-receiver model (freight is sent to a third-party receiver, a company that takes the delivery) is not the norm. Ask the supplier about their standard delivery process first.

Supplier quality and supplier relations

3 min

Invoices and wholesale prices in packages. Suppliers (the companies that make or sell the products to you) do not include invoices or receipts with wholesale prices (the price your store pays) in packages. The product box will show the brand name (normal, expected). It will never show the internal wholesale cost.

Supplier approval before selling their products. Suppliers request approval before allowing you to sell their products. Explain that your store is not yet live. Demo products (sample products on the site that show suppliers what their product pages will look like) are there for this purpose. You will launch on [specific date]. This is standard practice and fully disclosed (openly told to them).

Reporting MAP pricing violations. MAP (minimum advertised price) is the lowest price a seller may show for a product. A violation is when someone shows it lower.

  1. Find a competing store selling the supplier's product below the MAP price.
  2. Use Loom (a screen recording tool) to record a 20-second video stating the competitor's name, product, price, and that they are violating MAP policy.
  3. Email the Loom video to your supplier contact at customer service.
  4. The supplier will typically correct the violation quickly if they enforce MAP policy.

Supplier mismatch case study. The founder of a store with a printed fashion item ordered the product after a video call showed the item. The founder asked the supplier for a factory photo or video. The supplier said photos would slow production. No sample was ever sent, even though one had been promised. The supplier then shipped a lower-quality product. The printed item was sent in place of a woven product (fabric made by weaving threads together).

  • Within one day the store received six PayPal disputes (customer complaints filed with PayPal), and more followed the next day.
  • The founder spent many hours on customer service. Someone else opened a Trustpilot profile (an online review page) with negative reviews, and the store was labelled as a scam.
  • The supplier threatened to close the account if the founder opened a dispute and did not apologise.
  • The founder decided to stop working with this supplier. The founder postponed the relaunch (the planned reopening) from the 15th to around the 19th while checking the price with new factories. The founder also planned to redesign the store and hire a virtual assistant (a remote helper).
  • Takeaway: the founder should have requested a photo or video from the factory before the first order. The founder should not have ordered in bulk (a large quantity), and should not have trusted a supplier that refused to show the product.

Early product example. A first product ran for about one month. The first ad launched on 7 January. The product produced about 11,000 EUR in revenue (total money in from sales). This shows that a single well-chosen product can carry an early store while other markets are tested.

Customer service staff and virtual assistants.

  • A customer support virtual assistant who handles chat, phone calls, email, and Zendesk tickets (requests tracked in Zendesk, a support software) reduces seller dependence (the business needing you personally). If staff are willing to stay with the new owner, tell the buyer, because buyers highly value continuity (keeping the same team).
  • A founder working from Thailand built a dropshipping business (you sell products that suppliers ship straight to buyers) with over 500,000 dollars in sales in about 2.5 years. This was reportedly done without answering calls or emails in real time. At the start he worked late nights and early mornings, calling suppliers and answering emails himself.
  • He then added a visible phone number, an FAQ (a list of common questions and answers), personal product notes, a clear shipping page and detailed shipping-time emails. These reduced contact volume (how many messages came in) significantly.
  • He hired a native English-speaking (English is their first language) virtual assistant to answer calls for a flat 150 dollars per month (a fixed price). He also uses a shipping-tracking app and a Gmail scheduling and reminder tool.
  • Over more than 1,000 orders, he had two chargebacks and fewer than one refund per month.

Fulfilment model comparison. Fulfilment means how orders are stored, packed and shipped. On FBA (Fulfilled by Amazon, where Amazon does the storing and shipping), Amazon handles most returns, refunds and customer calls. The seller only answers occasional questions by email. Customer service load is much lower than running a dropshipping store.

Customer communication

4 min

Message templates. A ready-made PDF (a document file) of customer service message templates (pre-written messages) covers the common situations. Adjust the store name, product name and customer service agent name in each template before sending it to customers.

  • The PDF is grouped into four scenario types: tracking inquiries, returns and refunds, order modification or cancellation, and refunds for lost orders.

Reply structure. Every customer reply follows the same frame (the same structure).

  • Greeting: thank the customer and offer help.
  • Order status: give the specific facts.
  • Timeframe or action: give the expected timeframe or the action taken.
  • Closing: ask if they have further questions.
  • Sign-off: give an agent name, store name and role.
  • Pattern formula: Greeting + Status fact + Timeframe or action + Closing + Signature.
  • Example for a delayed order: Hi Sam, thanks for reaching out, happy to help. Your order left our supplier on March 3 and is now with the carrier (the shipping company). Expect delivery within 7 to 10 business days. Let me know if anything else comes up. Best, Emma, Customer Care at [store name].

Response window for student or community messages. Answer student or community messages within a fixed window (a set time slot) after the morning business checks. Members in other time zones (different clock times in other countries) may start their day while the operator is still in the middle of the workday. Schedule this as its own short block rather than answering whenever a message arrives.

Opening a support ticket. A support ticket is a request you send to support. When you open one, include your name, your goals and whether you are new or experienced. This lets the right helper be assigned and help you faster.

Customer service as a differentiator. A differentiator is what makes you stand apart from others. Customer service can be a differentiating part of the offer (what you sell and promise). Buyers expect service close to what they would get in a physical store. A distinctive service level, such as extended phone hours, can win sales when competitors do not offer it.

Customers referred from another marketplace. A marketplace is a large shopping site, such as Amazon. Referred means sent to you by that site.

  1. Reply by email, acknowledge that Amazon referred them, and offer to help.
  2. Offer a recorded video call, using FaceTime, Skype or a similar tool, to answer their question. An audio-only recording is acceptable if they are not comfortable on camera.
  3. At the start of the call, ask how they found your store.
  4. Answer their question and give practical advice.
  5. End the recording with a call to action (a clear request, such as visiting your store) that points to your store.
  6. Embed the recording on your About Us page and on the product pages for those products.
  7. Run a remarketing ad (an ad shown again to people who already visited your site) to visitors of your site, so they remember you before buying elsewhere.

Template: email reply to a customer referred by another marketplace.

Hi [Name], thanks for reaching out about [product]. Amazon sent you to us for support, and I would be glad to help. Would you be open to a short recorded video call so I can walk you through it? If you would rather not be on camera, an audio call works too. Before we start, could you tell me how you found us?

Business mindset and service quality.

  • Poor customer service is a sign of low business confidence. It appears alongside doing business only for money and running a business on plans that never get executed. Fixing the owner's confidence and self-alignment (acting in line with your own goals and values) is part of improving service quality.
  • A competitive business should turn setbacks (problems or failures) into advantages, so each problem becomes a chance to improve. Track each setback and record what it reveals about the customer experience or the supply chain (the path products take from supplier to buyer).
  • Speaking directly with real people who have bought the product is the most important way to make progress. These conversations reveal the bottleneck so it can be identified and solved.
  • Good customer service and product quality can make shoppers buy from a dropshipping store even when many stores sell the same product. Satisfied customers also promote (recommend) the store to others.
  • Scaling (growing a product's sales) a product with poor quality or service makes unhappy existing customers see the ads and share complaints. Since iOS (Apple's phone system) limits tracking (following people across apps), you cannot fully hide ads from past buyers, even when they are excluded.

Customer targeting

1 min

Aim for the right buyers. Aim to attract the right buyers rather than the most buyers. Customers who do not fit the product create a poor business, complaints, and extra costs.

Pain-point research. A pain point is a problem the customer feels. Find out what the product solves, what customers say in reviews, and what problems they face in daily life. Use this to build a precise customer avatar (a detailed picture of your ideal buyer).

Customer conversations before private label. Private label means selling a product under your own brand. Before private labeling a main product, the store should already have a substantial sales history (a record of many past sales), many customer service interactions and ongoing contact with customers. These provide the data (facts and numbers) needed to find differentiation.

Platform support, accounts and policy compliance

4 min

A platform is the site or app you sell on, such as Shopify, Google or Facebook. Policy means its rules. Compliance means following them.

Support channels for a training program. Support is available through the private, members-only Facebook group. There, questions are answered by the instructor and experienced high-ticket dropshippers (sellers of expensive products). Support is also available by email to support@dropshipbreakthrough.com. The link is in the page header. Before posting, search the group for the question, as it may already be answered.

Shopify theme and app support. A theme is the design template of the store. Apps are add-ons.

  • Shopify Support for theme edits: contact Shopify directly for a theme question, such as where to place a Klaviyo embed code (a snippet of code from the email tool Klaviyo) in the Dawn theme (Shopify's standard free design). Shopify built the theme and can give the exact location.
  • Store and product issue support: email the support address with the theme version used. The developers can locate code added elsewhere, including custom code (code written for your store), that changed a setting.
  • Send technical Shopify questions to the support address of the training provider, which passes them to the developer team. This is not the coach's area of expertise.
  • When business email support is needed, contact the store's support address and describe the page and the problem. The team then passes the request to the right designer or developer.
  • For a support problem with Google Search Console (Google's tool that shows how Google sees your site) or other tools, email the support team with the exact error message. The team may already have a fix from earlier cases.

Unplanned Shopify theme code error. Use this when red error lines appear in the theme editor (the screen where you edit the store's design) after removing a third-party app's JavaScript. JavaScript is the code that makes a page act. Third-party means made by someone outside Shopify.

  1. Find the extra or missing bracket or line that was removed.
  2. If unsure, post a job on Upwork (a site for hiring freelancers) for a Shopify developer, describing the exact red error.
  3. A five-minute fix is typical. A budget of about $20 is usually enough.

Bulk price changes. Shopify bulk edit (changing many products at once) and product export (downloading a product list as a file) are the approach for price changes in bulk. No repricing app (an app that changes prices automatically) is used by the team.

Digital content protection. When content is copied, first message the copier once to remove it. If there is no response, file a DMCA takedown (a formal copyright removal request) with the host (the company that runs the website), for example Shopify for a Shopify store.

Store name collisions. A name collision means two sellers use the same name. If another store uses the exact same name and sells the same products, change the store name and buy a new domain (your web address) to avoid consumer confusion.

Advertising account restrictions. An ad account is the account you use to run ads. Restrictions are limits placed on it. Ad accounts and business assets (the items your business uses to run ads) can be restricted. This happens when the landing page (the page people reach from an ad) or creatives (the ad images and videos) break platform policy. Examples are before and after claims or weight loss claims. Audit the landing page and creatives (check them carefully) against the policy before launching or reusing creatives.

Google Merchant Center. Google Merchant Center is Google's tool for product listings.

  • Google Merchant Center policy violations caused by automated bot scrapes (bots that copy your site automatically) can be contested (challenged). Request a manual review (a human check). Then ask for the domain to be whitelisted (added to an approved list), so it is reviewed manually before suspension (the account being shut down).
  • Merchant accounts can be suspended if the account is set up from abroad while listing a US address. If this happens, appeal and provide the correct details.

Facebook account bans due to ID issues. A ban is a block placed on an account. Submit the ID review (a check of your identity) in Meta Business settings (the settings for Facebook's business tools). It usually takes 24 to 48 hours. If it does not work, contact the account seller or a support provider.

Pinterest. Pinterest's support team can help with account bans, restrictions and ad insights (data on how your ads perform). The phone number on the business account is used for support, not verification (proving you are who you say). Do not follow Pinterest's general marketing advice for campaign decisions, because it is not aimed at direct response selling (selling that asks for an immediate purchase).

Ads and click quality

1 min

Click-through rate threshold. Click-through rate (CTR) is the share of people who see an ad and click it. A threshold is a limit. Do not scale ad spend (increase the money put into ads) when CTR falls below 2%. A low CTR indicates poor product-market fit (the product does not match what buyers want). It will exhaust budget (use up your money) without converting traffic (turning visitors into buyers).

Click fraud. Click fraud means fake clicks that waste your ad money. A third-party click fraud service (a company sold to block fake clicks) was tested at the time and was not worth the cost. The author cancelled after 30 days and does not recommend it. Use Google's Invalid Click analysis (Google's report of clicks it marked as fake) and an account audit (a full check of your account) first.

Marketplace delivery commitments (Google Shopping Actions)

1 min

Google Shopping Actions is the program that lists your products in Google's shopping results.

Older advice, Source B 2021-22.

Joining Shopping Actions. Joining requires the merchant (seller) to state specific delivery time frames for orders and to keep them. Other marketplaces use the same kind of delivery commitment.

Delivery reliability before joining. Before joining, check that you can deliver within the stated time frame at least 90% of the time. Example: if you promise delivery in 7 days, at least 90% of orders must arrive within 7 days. If you cannot, do not join, because suspension (being removed from the program) will follow.

Seller score. The seller score is driven by buyer feedback on delivery times, return rates (how many items come back) and cancellation rates (how many orders are cancelled). Each has thresholds (set limits). A poor seller score is hard to recover from.

Retailer Standards score. The Retailer Standards score is calculated from order defects (problems with orders), such as late deliveries and high cancellation rates. A merchant must deliver 20 items to receive a score the following month. The score is checked on the first day of each month.

Defect trends. Defect trends are patterns of problems over time. Late deliveries and a high order cancellation rate both lower the Retailer Standards score. Check the defect trends in Merchant Center regularly, so that problems are corrected before the monthly score is set.

Daily order check. The Shopping Actions Today's Snapshot in Merchant Center (the daily overview screen in Google's seller tool) flags orders as due today. An order is flagged when it is pending shipment (not yet shipped), missing tracking information or has wrong tracking information. Clear these flags daily, because late or untracked orders reduce the Retailer Standards score. That score determines Shopping Actions visibility (how often your products appear).

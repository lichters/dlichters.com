Playbook › 17. Customer service, returns and chargebacks

Damaged, defective, wrong or missing items and sizing problems

Section 17.11 · 11 of 22 in this chapter · 12 min read

In short

  • Check each delivery when it arrives, and take photos of any damage before you accept it.
  • Damaged or broken items are the supplier's job to replace or refund. Ask your suppliers to confirm this in writing.
  • Shipping insurance covers parcels that are lost or damaged on the way.
  • Size problems cause many returns, so give every clothing and shoe product a clear, detailed size guide.
  • Answer customers fast and keep them updated until the problem is solved.

Packaging check and photo evidence

2 min
  • Ask the customer to check the packaging when the items arrive.
  • Ask them to note any visible damage while they sign for delivery (confirm receipt with the delivery driver).
  • For damaged goods, they email photos to the returns address.
Examine packaging when items arrive and document any visible damage while signing for delivery. For damaged goods, email photos to returns@YOURWEBSITE.com and we'll file an insurance claim on your behalf.
  • For a non-working product, ask for evidence, such as a short video showing the defect, before you accept the return.
  • This filters out claims that are not genuine (false claims).
  • Many customers will not complete the extra step, which lowers refunds.
  • The policy stays clear and fair.
  • Seller errors (mistakes made by your store, such as a wrong item, a damaged item or an incorrect product photo) are handled under the competitor's seller-error rules (the rules of a competing store).
  • Record these separately from change-of-mind terms (the rules for when a customer simply changes their mind).
  • In these cases, the customer is not charged.
  • One competitor allowed 30 days from delivery to report a damaged item.
  • This is a separate window from its change-of-mind period.
  • Record each reporting window as its own value in the risk reversal column (the column in your sheet for your money-back promise).

Photo request to the customer (damage in transit)

Damage in transit means damage that happens while the parcel is on its way.

  1. Apologize to the customer and ask for photos of the damage.
  2. Ask the customer to take a photo right away, with a smartphone or camera, that clearly shows the damage to the product or the packaging.
  3. Ask the customer to email the photos to the company email address.

Supplier photo request

A supplier is the company that makes or ships your products.

  • Ask the customer for a photo of the damage, then send the photo to the supplier and ask for a free replacement.
  • The supplier decides whether to ship a replacement part, a whole replacement item or a reshipment (sending the item again).

Refusing delivery

2 min

Refusing delivery means saying no to a parcel at the door, so it goes back to the sender.

  • Add a clear instruction to the delivery notification (the message that tells the buyer a parcel is coming), the delivery page and the shipment page on your website. The instruction says the customer must refuse delivery if any damage is visible.
  • If the customer refuses the shipment and notes the damage on the packing slip (the paper list inside the box), the carrier (the shipping company) returns the item to the shipper (the sender).
  • Refusal and acceptance are two different paths.
  • If the customer accepted the shipment, the supplier may ask for photos as proof, and there is usually a short window (a limited time) for such claims.
  • Your documentation and refusal instructions must match.
  • When damage is visible, both steps can be used: signing for delivery (documenting the damage and accepting the parcel), or refusing it (sending the parcel back to the shipper).
  • The customer chooses which step to use.

Procedure: damaged item where the customer refused delivery

  1. Add a clear instruction to the delivery notification, delivery page and shipment page on your website that the customer must refuse delivery if any damage is visible.
  2. If the customer refuses the shipment and notes the damage on the packing slip, the carrier returns the item to the shipper.
  3. Email the supplier right away with the order details and the damage, and request a replacement.
  4. If the customer accepted the shipment instead, the supplier may ask for photos as proof, and there is usually a short window for such claims.
  5. Reply to the customer politely and apologetically, without blaming them, and tell them you are handling it.
  6. Act as soon as it happens, because fast action shortens the replacement lead time (the wait until the new item is sent). In one case, the issue was resolved within a day.

Customer email template for a refused damaged delivery:

Hi {customer_name},

I am very sorry that your {product_name} arrived damaged, and I understand how frustrating that is. Thank you for refusing the delivery and noting the damage, which was exactly the right step. I have already contacted the manufacturer and am requesting a replacement as quickly as possible. I will keep you updated at every step.

Thank you for your patience and understanding.

{your_name}
{store_name}
  • Damage in transit is never the store owner's fault, but the owner must still resolve it and replace the item quickly.
  • Customers who are told they can refuse delivery, and who are handled politely, usually accept the outcome.

Supplier replacements and refunds

2 min

Who bears the cost

Defective means the product does not work properly.

  • Defective products should be refunded or replaced by the supplier, not by the store owner.
  • In over 10 years of operation, no supplier has refused such a request on thousands of orders.
  • You should still ask your suppliers.
  • Defective or damaged goods are the supplier's responsibility.
  • The supplier ships the replacement, not the dropship store. A dropship store is a store that sells products the supplier ships directly to the buyer.
  • When a working product confuses the buyer during setup, the customer contacts the retailer (the store that sells to the buyer), not the supplier.
  • So the store must budget for that support (set aside money and time for it).
  • Only defective or damaged items go back to the supplier, which ships a replacement.
  • Even when a supplier accepts no returns, confirm in writing that damaged or defective items qualify for a refund or replacement.
  • Then state the no-return rule clearly on each affected product page, so the customer knows before buying.

Procedure: product differs from the description

  1. Identify the problem: for example, the product differed from the description on the site.
  2. Contact the supplier and ask whether it will replace the item, ship the correct product or refund.
  3. Decide case by case whether to offer a refund, replacement or the correct product.
  4. Document the outcome in the supplier notes.

Procedure: cross-border returns and refunds

Cross-border means the order ships across a country border.

  1. For damaged products, refund the customer and let them keep the item.
  2. For size complaints, point them to the size guide on the store.
  3. Make sure the store has a detailed size guide.
  4. If the mistake was the seller's own, refund the customer.
  • Note: step 1 (refund and keep the item) differs from the supplier-replacement rules above.
  • Use it where the cross-border policy applies.
  • Elsewhere, the supplier replacement applies.

Procedure: damaged or non-working items (supplier refund)

  1. Ask the customer for a photo or proof of the fault.
  2. Refund the customer if the fault is confirmed.
  3. Request the refund from the supplier, for example CJ (a dropshipping supplier), because the product did not meet the promise.
  • This procedure refunds the customer, while the supplier-replacement procedures ship a replacement.
  • Choose the path that matches the customer's preference and the supplier's answer.

Procedure: AliExpress shortcut

  1. For a damaged item, ask the customer for a photo of the damage.
  2. Send the photo to the supplier and ask for a free replacement.
  3. For AliExpress (a large online marketplace for suppliers), email them with a screenshot (a picture of the screen) of the customer message. They usually refund automatically with free returns.
  4. If needed, offer the customer a discounted second item as the replacement.

Disputed removal of the damaged unit

Use this procedure for a damaged-delivery dispute where the supplier shipped a replacement but did not remove the damaged unit, and the customer wants it removed.

  1. Ask the supplier to share the removal cost (the cost of taking the old item away).
  2. Offer the customer a partial refund of $50 to $100 so they can have the old unit hauled away (taken away by a removal service).
  3. Keep the relationship with the supplier intact.

Carrier insurance and claims

3 min

Insurance rules

A claim is a formal request for money after a loss.

  • Confirm that the supplier insures shipments. Insurance is what lets you file a claim for an item damaged in transit.
  • After the claim pays out, the value can be recovered.
  • Shipments should be insured.
  • For a damaged item, a customer claim supported by the insurance can allow the item to be disposed of (thrown away) rather than shipped back.
  • Every shipper used should insure its packages.
  • So rely on shipping insurance for transit damage.
  • For a damaged item, the shipping insurance covers the loss, whether the supplier shipped on their account or yours.
  • For a change-of-mind return, the customer's return is governed by your return policy.
  • Shipping damage that happens in transit counts as a shipping issue, not a customer return.
  • Whether the customer pays for return shipping, and whether there is a restocking fee (a charge for taking an item back into stock), are policy decisions.
  • These decisions are set by the competitor benchmark (what a competing store does).
  • Lost or damaged parcels are covered by carrier insurance.

Package protection

Package protection is an optional paid add-on for the buyer.

  • Package protection covers lost, damaged and stolen parcels during delivery, as well as a free return.
  • This coverage helps keep the customer relationship intact after a delivery problem.
  • US merchants offer and use package protection more widely than Australian merchants.
  • Australian merchants trust their national carrier highly.
  • Australian brands are still raising awareness of package protection.

Customer claim message

If your order arrives damaged, please contact us at [support email] within [number] days of delivery with photos of the damaged box and the product. We will file a claim with the shipping carrier on your behalf. Once the claim is approved, we will ship you a replacement item.

Full procedure: damaged or defective order in shipping or at the factory

  1. Ask the customer to take a photo right away, with a smartphone or camera, that clearly shows the damage to the product or the packaging.
  2. Ask the customer to email the photos to the company email address.
  3. If the customer has opened the packaging, ask them to put back everything they removed and reseal it (close it again).
  4. If the packaging is damaged:
    • Ask the customer to photograph the damage, then open it carefully and inspect the product.
    • If the product is fine, the process ends here.
    • If the product is damaged, ask them to photograph the damage to the item and then reseal the packaging.
  5. Contact the relevant supplier by phone or email to report the damage, and email the photos to the supplier.
  6. If the supplier booked the shipping, ask the supplier to arrange a replacement and to collect the damaged product if it wants to.
    • The supplier may ask the customer to dispose of the damaged item, which is acceptable.
  7. If your business booked the shipping, first ask the supplier to replace the item, with you booking the shipping again.
    • If the supplier refuses because you booked the shipping, ask the supplier to prepare a replacement for dispatch (sending), then arrange shipping as normal.
  8. File an insurance claim with the shipping carrier, following its instructions.
  9. Keep the customer updated throughout the whole process.
  • Keep the customer informed at every stage of a damaged-in-shipping claim.
  • Do this from the first photo request until the replacement or insurance outcome is known.
  • If a customer reports damage after using the product for several weeks, there is no fixed rule.
  • So decide case by case.

Warranty eligibility

1 min

A warranty is a promise to repair or replace a faulty item for a set time.

  • All products carry a 2-year warranty.
  • Faulty items within that period can be checked for a warranty claim.

Procedure: is a faulty product eligible?

  1. Ask the customer how they have been using the product and what the fault is. Note down their answers.
  2. First, contact the relevant supplier to confirm the warranty claim process.
  3. If the supplier agrees the circumstances qualify, ask who honours the warranty (covers it): the supplier, or the original manufacturer if these are separate companies.
  4. If the supplier is responsible, ask the supplier to replace the product.
    • If the supplier wants to collect the faulty product, contact the customer and ask them to get it ready for pickup.
  5. If the original manufacturer is responsible, the customer may need to lodge the warranty claim (submit the request) directly with the manufacturer.
    • Get the manufacturer's contact details and give them to the customer.
  6. If either side disputes the process or the status of a warranty claim, inform the responsible person and ask for guidance on the next step.
  • Warranty disputes about the process or the status of a claim must be escalated (passed up) to the person responsible for guidance.
  • Do this before any further action.

Missing parts and late shipments

1 min
  • For lost or damaged items, the carrier insurance applies.
  • For missing parts, the ideal outcome is that the customer accepts a replacement shipment.
  • The alternative is a partial refund proportional to (matching the share of) the missing items.
  • For late shipments, use the replacement or refund approach.
  • In all scenarios, respond immediately.
  • Customers accept problems when they hear from you quickly.

Heavy items

1 min
  • For very heavy items, around 150 pounds for some appliances, returning the product is impractical for the buyer.
  • The better fix is to have the supplier pick up the damaged unit and ship a replacement as soon as possible.
  • This is better than asking the buyer to dispose of it.
  • Note the conflict: for a damaged unit, the supplier may allow the customer to dispose of it, which is acceptable for standard shipments.
  • For very heavy items, use the supplier pickup and replacement from instead.

Sizing and fashion returns

1 min

Decision rule

  • For a size problem, refund the customer and let them keep the item, instead of paying for a return.
  • The refund is cheaper than the return shipping.

Procedure: fashion sizing returns

  1. Ask the customer to send proof that the item is too big or too small.
  2. Once the proof is received, decide based on the situation whether to refund the money or send a replacement in the correct size.
  3. Make the choice that makes more sense for the store at that moment.

Footwear and other sized products

  • Sizing issues cause a significant share of refunds in footwear.
  • Communicate sizing clearly on the product page and with the supplier to reduce returns.
  • For wrong-size shoes, check the customer's centimeter measurement and the size shipped.
  • If the supplier sent the wrong size, refund the customer.
  • For clothing and other sized products, expect refunds from sizing issues, and plan for them.
  • Treat sized products as having a higher refund rate (the share of items given back) than standard items.
  • Stretch fabrics (fabrics that give a little) can reduce the impact of small fit differences.
  • Sizing remains the most important factor for returns.

Choosing products

  • Placing the size chart in three places on the product listing still did not stop buyers from choosing the wrong size.
  • Clear size guidance alone is not enough.
  • So pick products that need little or no size selection.

Size guides

1 min
  • Make the size guide accurate, and communicate sizing clearly on apparel (clothing) products.
  • This keeps wrong-size orders from becoming the store's fault.
  • For clothing and other sized products, provide a detailed size chart with all measurements.
  • Agree the numbers with the supplier.
  • Check the sizes used in the target country (the country you sell to).
  • A no-refunds policy can be added to the landing page (the first page a visitor sees).
  • A good size guide on the store is essential.
  • A missing size guide is a strong reason for customers to return items.
  • For size complaints, point the customer to the size guide on the store.
  • Make sure the store has a detailed size guide.

Playbook › 17. Customer service, returns and chargebacks

Reply templates and scripts for pre-purchase questions

Section 17.5 · 5 of 22 in this chapter · 8 min read

In short

  • Answer customers fast. A quick first answer often decides the sale.
  • Keep short, clear answers ready for common questions.
  • If you do not know an answer, say so, ask your supplier, then reply to the customer.
  • Add each answer to your product page, so future customers see it.
  • Never use fear to sell. Show the product's real benefits instead.
This section has 9 parts. Tap one to open it.

Speed and first response before purchase

1 min
  • Being the first store to answer a pre-purchase question (a question asked before buying) often decides the sale.
  • Fast answers are a direct sales tool.
  • Speed and quality of service can beat competitors who sell the same product.
  • Replying within minutes can raise sales.
  • A quick answer to a product question can turn a visitor into a buyer.
  • Check messages from your phone, so no question is left waiting.
  • Shoppers who call before buying to check stock (whether an item is available) often choose the store that responds.
  • Answering the phone can win sales that other stores lose.
  • Phone calls from prospective customers (people who may buy), such as asking whether an item is in stock, are normal when selling higher-priced products.
  • Answer or return them as soon as possible.
  • Pre-purchase questions from website visitors usually come by phone, live chat (a chat box on the website) or email.
  • They are a daily task. Automate or delegate them. Automate means a tool handles them. Delegate means you hand them to someone else.
  • Post-purchase questions (questions asked after buying) come through the same channels and need the same handling.
  • Shoppers fear buying online and then getting no answer from customer service.
  • Only promise fast customer service if you can actually reply quickly.

Answering known questions in chat

1 min
  • When you know the answer, give it right away in the chat to close the sale (get the buyer to buy).
  • Shoppers who ask pre-purchase questions should get a fast reply without extra effort from the store owner.
  • Example: a shopper asks how fast stickers ship. The store replies: "They ship within two to three days."
  • Quick, specific shipping times answer buying doubts directly.
  • Most customer service questions are simple. **
  • They concern delivery time, shipping time or discounts. **
  • Prepare clear answers to these first. **
  • Store pre-written answers in the live chat app.
  • Customers then get an instant reply about a specific product, including shipping time and usage.
  • Pre-written answers help when you expect the same questions about one product.
  • Reply to a chat question you cannot answer yet with the following text. Then send the answer as a follow-up (a later message with the answer) once it is ready:
When a chat question comes in and you don't know the answer yet, respond right away by saying it's a good question, that you're looking into it, and that you'll reply as soon as possible. Once the answer is ready, send it as a follow-up.

Handling a question you cannot answer yet

2 min

Standard procedure (phone or chat)

  1. Receive a customer question you cannot answer right away.
  2. Respond: "That's a great question. I want to make sure you get an accurate answer. May I have your name, email, and phone number?"
  3. Contact your supplier (the company that makes or sends your products) with the customer's exact question.
  4. Collect the supplier's answer.
  5. Reply to the customer with the accurate response.
  6. Update your product page (the page that sells one item) with this information for future customers.

Supplier-verified variant

  1. Tell the customer you want to confirm the answer and get the correct information.
  2. Collect their name, email address and phone number if the question came by phone.
  3. Ask your supplier rep (the supplier's sales contact) the question directly, stating the customer's needs and quantities.
  4. Send the verified answer to the customer.
  5. If the question is likely to come up again, add the answer to the product page as an FAQ (a list of frequently asked questions) or in the description.

Use this reply when a customer asks a product question you cannot answer yet. "Manufacturer" means the company that makes the product:

That's a great question, and I want to make sure I give you the correct answer. Could you please send me your name, email address and phone number? I will check directly with the manufacturer and get right back to you.

Product question not answerable from the product page

  1. Tell the customer you do not have the answer right now. Take their name, phone number and email.
  2. Contact the supplier rep (or email the supplier) with the product model number (the code that identifies the exact item) and the customer's question.
  3. Call the customer back or reply with the answer.
  4. Add the answer to the product page so future customers see it.

Older procedure (2020-2021)

Treat these as older advice. The steps still work, but the wording is dated.

Procedure, 2020 **:

  1. Tell the customer that it is a great question and that you want to make sure you give the right answer.
  2. Collect the customer's name, phone number and email address.
  3. Contact the supplier to get the answer.
  4. Add the answer to your website, so future customers can find it.
  5. Reply to the customer with the correct information and try to close the sale.

Procedure, 2021 **:

  1. Respond promptly: "That's a great question. Let me get you the answer."
  2. Contact the supplier for the answer.
  3. Provide the supplier's answer to the customer.
  4. Capture that information and add it to the product page so future shoppers see the answer without asking.

Principles for supplier questions

  • Expect customer questions about products you did not know before.
  • Ask the supplier for the answer. Then add frequent questions to the product page.
  • Each store owner needs to build product knowledge in the niche (the small market your store focuses on).
  • Answer customer questions by checking with the supplier. Then publish the answer on the product page.
  • Phone and support answers for product questions should follow a set script (fixed wording).
  • A sales phone call should never end without a follow-up. The customer should be called back with an answer, not left waiting.

Responding to pre-purchase hesitation

1 min
  • Using fear, threats or crime statistics to push a purchase repels most buyers.
  • Instead, let the buyer recognize the need.
  • Then show the product points they care about most, such as reliability and ease of installation.

FAQ building and product-page content

1 min

Building the FAQ

  1. Answer every customer question by email or phone. Then add that question and answer to the FAQ.
  2. Keep building the FAQ over the first months of the store.
  3. Make new customer service staff read and memorize the FAQ during onboarding (their first training), because these are the questions they will receive.
  4. Link customers to the FAQ so they can find answers without calling or emailing.
  5. Where possible, add the answer to the product description or to a staff note so the question does not come up again.

What the FAQ page should cover

  • The FAQ page should collect the questions customers ask again and again.
  • Cover price guarantee (your promise about price), product guarantee (your promise about the product), shipping policy (your shipping rules), returns (sending items back) and support.
  • If one visitor has a question, many others probably do too.

Preparing support for a team

These two sources describe the same procedure and are merged here.

  1. List the questions customers are likely to ask, both before and after purchase.
  2. Write a clear, polished answer for each question.
  3. Compile the questions and answers into a PDF document (a file type that looks the same on any device).
  4. Let the support team copy and paste the answers to customers.

Turning questions into product-page content

  • Turn customer questions into product page updates.
  • Future visitors then find the answers without calling.
  • Product pages should give customers all information they need before buying.
  • This reduces customer inquiries over time.
  • Write detailed FAQs on each product page for every recurring customer question.
  • Then the answer is there before the customer has to ask.
  • Write content that answers questions a buyer has before buying, such as whether to offer a phone number.
  • Link those questions to your products. This keeps the blog (your written articles on the website) focused on buying decisions, not general interest.

B2B and wholesale discount requests

1 min
  • Standard reply for a B2B (business-to-business, selling to other businesses) or wholesale (buying in bulk to resell) buyer who wants a discount: create a multi-use discount code (a code many times over) named after the business.
  • Give the code only to that buyer.
  • Set the percent off (the percent taken from the price) as the B2B price.

Reply template:

Thank you for your question. That's a great question, and I want to make sure I give you the correct answer. Could you please send me your name, email address and phone number? I will look into this and get back to you with the answer as soon as I have it.

MAP discount requests

1 min

Procedure for customers who request a discount on products with strict MAP policies. MAP (minimum advertised price) is the lowest price a brand allows you to show.

  1. Acknowledge their request professionally.
  2. Explain that the price is set by the manufacturer to protect brand value and that this is industry-wide (the same across the whole industry).
  3. Offer alternative value: same-day shipping if possible, expedited (faster) service, or reference other benefits.
  4. If you cannot offer discounts or special terms, maintain confidence in the price and emphasize service quality and expertise.

Price-match answers

1 min

A price-match guarantee is a promise to match a lower price. The rules below apply together. Use the row that matches the situation.

Situation Answer Source
Customer finds a lower price from an unauthorized seller (a seller the brand has not approved) A price-match guarantee only works against the MAP. Explain that the seller violates the supplier's policy and cannot be trusted to ship the product. A discount that matches what a coupon code (a discount code) already gives can still be offered. (2023)
Working with a non-MAP supplier (a supplier with no minimum price rule) Do not offer a price-match guarantee. **
You sell above MAP (at a higher price than the minimum) Do not have a price-match policy. **
A supplier sells below MAP on another platform (for example Wayfair, a large online store) and refuses to act, and that supplier makes up most of the business Remove the price-match policy. **

Expired promotions

1 min
  • Honor (keep the offer for) an expired promotion (an offer that has ended) when the customer has a real reason for missing it. **
  • Decline expired codes requested from old archives or without a reason. **

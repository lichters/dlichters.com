Playbook › 17. Customer service, returns and chargebacks

Hiring, briefing and managing support staff and virtual assistants

Section 17.3 · 3 of 22 in this chapter · 25 min read

In short

  • Hand off customer service early, once you have seen how many calls and messages come in.
  • Do not outsource phone support until the business profit can pay for it. Until then, use voicemail and return calls the next day.
  • A virtual assistant (VA, a remote helper) costs about $3 to $5 per hour and can take over email work.
  • Give helpers access only to the tools and data they need. Keep your personal inbox private.
  • Prepare extra help before big sales days such as Black Friday.
More key points
  • Reply to emails within 24 hours. If there is no reply, follow up.
  • If you cannot answer, pass the question on: ask the owner, then check the supplier's webpage, then call the supplier.
  • Do not rush. A delay of about 10 minutes is fine if you give the correct answer.
  • Agents are the customer's first contact, so train them with ready-made replies and a shared guide.

When to outsource

2 min
  • Outsource customer service first. Outsourcing means hiring outside helpers to do the work. A constant flow of negative messages can drain the owner. Even big brands get negative feedback.
  • Outsource customer support early. Answering questions takes a lot of time, especially with many orders.
  • Some days bring hundreds of emails. These can overwhelm the owner.
  • Answering customer emails can take three to four hours per day. This blocks growth.
  • A virtual assistant (VA, a remote helper who works online) at $3 to $5 per hour can take over this work. This gives those hours back to the owner.
  • Typical daily tasks in an online store are answering live chat, phone and email questions. Live chat is a chat box on the website where visitors type questions in real time.
  • Other daily tasks are handling follow-up questions after a purchase, and processing new orders with suppliers (the companies that send you the products) until they ship.
  • These tasks need a human. They are usually the first to hand off once you understand them.
  • Outsource customer service only after you have seen call volumes (how many calls come in), call times, and how many calls turn into sales.
  • A new hire in another time zone (the local clock time where a person lives) must be fully briefed before taking over.
  • Do not outsource phone support until business profit can pay for it.
  • Until then, use voicemail (recorded messages callers leave) with a clear message. Return calls and emails the next day.
  • Per-call phone answering services (companies that charge for each call they answer) are not recommended at the start.
  • The vendor example is only a starting point to see what is possible.
  • Early on, answer live chat from the owner's phone when possible.
  • Consider a paid 24/7 answering service (covering all day and night, every day) only once the business is profitable and reinvesting profits.
  • Hire your own employees for customer service and other tasks that need care toward customers. These are called direct hires.
  • Third-party agents (outside companies) care less about your customers.
  • They often cost about the same as a direct hire.
  • Where sources disagree on how to set up the team, use direct hires for work that needs care toward customers.
  • Use a virtual team (remote helpers) for hours and time-zone coverage.
  • Support hours depend on three factors:
    • Product price. A higher price means fewer sales and fewer support contacts.
    • Customer demographic (the age and type of customer). Older customers make more phone calls.
    • Budget.
  • Product knowledge questions can go to general support staff.
  • Pre-purchase questions (questions asked before buying) need live chat and email coverage.
  • This may give you an edge if competitors avoid it.
  • Prepare customer service before a sales peak such as Black Friday. Black Friday is the big shopping day after US Thanksgiving.
  • In one example, a team of three VAs was not enough when orders surged. Customer messages (tickets) fell four to five days behind.
  • Train extra helpers in advance. These can be family, or a VA at around 3 USD per hour.

Where to find virtual assistants

1 min

  • Hiring steps to plan for: job posting, qualifying (checking candidates), hiring and training.
  • Staff customer support with VAs in regions with reverse working hours (working hours opposite to yours), such as the Philippines.
  • This covers different time zones. Hire them through job platforms such as onlinejobs.ph. (2021.)
  • Outsourcing customer support to a virtual team in the Philippines is a major growth step.
  • Support covers live chat, phone and email.

Hiring and vetting

1 min

Vetting means checking people before you hire them.

  • When hiring customer service staff, look for similar past experience.
  • The person should be able to give a reference (someone who vouches for them).
  • Treat the job as a real position, even though it is virtual (done online). (2023.)
  • For a store selling to US customers, the person answering calls should be a native speaker (someone who grew up speaking the language) with an American or Canadian accent.
  • Customers then assume the business is a normal local company.
  • Accents from some outsourcing regions can lower trust with this audience.

Onboarding a new hire

1 min

Onboarding means getting a new hire ready to work.

Set up the assistant in this order:

  1. Give access to the support mailbox, sales and orders.
  2. Write standard operating procedures (SOPs, step-by-step written rules) and reply templates (pre-written answers).
  3. Hold a weekly call to review work.
  4. Update the instructions in one batch, not with small changes sent continuously.
  • When hiring for fulfillment (packing and shipping orders) or design, give clear, complete instructions.
  • Vague briefs lead to unwanted results.
  • Schedule calls regularly. Do not wait for the assistant to start the conversation.

Onboarding a customer service or fulfillment hire:

  1. Document every step of customer service and fulfillment for each supplier and each scenario.
  2. Set up an open channel with the new person, such as Slack (a team chat app) or Facebook Messenger.
  3. Expect questions for the first few weeks, possibly up to a month. The new hire will not know every answer yet.
  4. Keep answering questions until the process runs smoothly.

Scope of the role

1 min
  • A VA for customer service handles three areas:
    • Visitor questions before purchase.
    • Customer updates after purchase.
    • Order processing.
  • Order processing means contacting the supplier to place the order after a customer orders.
  • It does not mean taking orders by email from customers.
  • VAs can handle order processing, tracking (checking where a parcel is) and routine questions without expert skills.
  • Many customers mainly want to hear a real person.
  • Phone support is often the first role to hand off.

Phone coverage and time zones

1 min
  • Answering customer questions instantly raises conversion. Conversion rate is the share of visitors who buy.
  • An owner in a distant time zone should outsource real-time answers.
  • Do not rely on a night shift. Working through the night is unhealthy over the long term.
  • When you work from a distant time zone, staff phone support first, then email support.
  • Staff them with people who work during your customers' business hours (US business hours in the example).
  • Do not answer calls yourself at night.
  • Answering support calls yourself at 3 or 4 a.m. because of a time-zone gap is not sustainable.
  • Plan staffing before sales volume grows.
  • Split phone support and chat or email support into separate people only when volume grows beyond what one person can handle.
  • Use your target reply times (how fast you want to answer) to judge this.

Setting up a first phone helper (small business)

1 min
  1. Forward the business phone line to the helper's personal mobile.
  2. Pay per answered call instead of a fixed salary.
  3. Greet callers with "Hi, can I help you?"
  4. Take a message and ask the caller's questions.
  5. Tell the caller that a product specialist will call back.

Access rights and permissions

1 min
  • Give VAs access only to the support mailbox and the sales and order data they need.
  • Do not give them the entire personal inbox.
  • Keep the owner's personal inbox separate from customer complaints.
  • A VA can approve discounts up to 10% on their own.
  • Any discount above 10%, or any other customer credit (money or value given back to a customer), needs owner approval.

Inbox labels and email triage

1 min

Triage means sorting emails by urgency. A label is a coloured tag that sorts emails.

  • Colour-code Gmail labels by priority. This tells the assistant which emails to handle first.
  • Customer issues get a red label, because they are the most urgent.
  • An inbox overnight may hold around 20 emails.
  • An urgent $20,000 order could be buried among them.
  • Priority labels make sure the assistant handles that order before routine messages.
  • Keep customer issues and sales inquiries in their own Gmail labels, not in the general inbox.
  • This lets you find them later.
  • Customer issues get a red label so they are handled first.

Order boards

1 min

An order board is a visual task board where each order is a card.

Order Processing board (one card per order)

2 min
  1. Send the order. Record the product name and shipping address, then send the order to the manufacturer (the maker of the product).
  2. Once the manufacturer confirms the item is in stock (available to ship), move the card forward.
  3. If the item is on backorder (sold out at the supplier, shipping later), notify the customer first. Only then move the card to Backorder with a due date.
  4. Move the card to Shipped once fulfilled, and record the tracking number.
  5. Move the card to Claims and Damages when a claim (a complaint asking for a refund or replacement) or damage notice arrives. Add notes about its progress.
  • Keep cancelled orders on the board instead of deleting them.
  • The same customer may call back, and the quote information may still be needed.
  • Put a due date on backorders so they are not forgotten.
  • Move cancelled orders to a cancelled list, not deleted.
  • If a replacement item is out of stock, tell the customer a new item is coming but is temporarily unavailable.
  • Keep the card in the Claims and Damages list with a due date. The claim stays visible until the replacement ships.
  • Add status notes to the card with each update.
  • Shopify (the software that runs the online store) order notes are hard to track once there are many backorders.
  • Staff must scroll through each order and cannot see due dates.
  • Track order status on a visual board with due dates on each card.
  • This matters once volume reaches about 30 to 50 orders per month.
  • Keeping important customer details only in single Shopify orders makes them hard to find as volume grows.
  • The owner cannot see all pending items together.
  • A separate order-tracking board solves this.
  • Without a shared board, backorders and customer updates get lost.
  • One customer waited since July, and the follow-up depended on memory.
  • Lost notes from calls and chats also cause missed quotes.
  • Record every customer request on a board that everyone can see.

Asana order board (automated order flow)

1 min

Asana is an online task board app. Automated means it runs by itself.

  1. A new order arrives and is added automatically to the first column of the Asana order board. The help desk (the tool that collects customer messages) sends a notification.
  2. The VA waits for ClearSale (an outside service that checks orders for fraud, meaning fake or stolen payments) to approve the order.
  3. After approval, forward the order to the right supplier, or place it on the supplier's website. Then wait for confirmation.
  4. When the supplier confirms stock, charge the customer's card in Shopify. The card was only authorised at checkout, meaning approved but not yet charged.
  5. Move the card to the confirmed column in Asana.
  6. When the order ships, add the tracking number in Shopify. Then update the status in Asana.
  7. Mark the order complete in Asana with a check mark.
  8. If a shipped order later has a problem, remove the check mark. Move the card back to the column that matches the new issue.

Backorders on the Asana board

1 min
  1. When a supplier reports a backorder, get the backorder date from the supplier.
  2. Record that date in Asana.
  3. Move the order card to the backorder column.
  4. Inform the customer about the backorder using a ready reply template (a pre-written message) in the help desk.

Damaged items and returns on the Asana board

1 min
  1. For a damaged item or a return, ask the supplier for their damage or return procedure.
  2. Explain that procedure to the customer.
  3. Update the order status in Asana.

Support workflow with a VA

1 min
  1. Create reply templates for the most common messages, such as: where is my package, when will it arrive, and where is my tracking number.
  2. Translate messages with DeepL (a free online translation tool) when the assistant does not speak the customer's language.
  3. Review the assistant's drafts before sending them, especially for general messages.
  4. Keep separate templates for each target market (the group of customers you sell to).
  • Routine questions get a direct reply from the assistant.
  • For unusual cases, the assistant sends the email screenshot to the founder.
  • The founder dictates the response.

  • The owner monitors these accounts when staff are absent.

Escalation when the answer is unknown

1 min

Escalation means passing a question up to someone who can answer it.

  • When you cannot answer a customer's question by phone, chat or email, pass it on. Do one or more of these steps, as they fit, in this order:
    1. Contact the business owner through the agreed contact methods. Ask the owner. If the owner is available, they will either answer the question or ask you to forward the customer to them.
    2. If the owner is not available right away, tell the customer you will get back to them shortly. Take their contact details. Then reply as soon as you can.
    3. If the owner is not available and you need product information, first check the supplier's webpage. A supplier is the company that makes or ships the product. If the information is not there, call the supplier and ask.
    4. Do not rush. Reply once you have the correct information. A short delay of around 10 minutes is acceptable.
  • To find product information for a customer, the VA (a virtual assistant, a helper who works remotely) follows this order:
    1. Search Google for the answer.
    2. If the answer is not found, take the customer's question and contact details. Pass them to a sales rep contact number or email. A sales rep is a person on the sales team.
    3. The sales rep then calls the customer back once the needed information is available.

Agent training and knowledge base

1 min
  • Customer service agents are usually the customer's first contact with the company. So improving agent training is typically the first and most powerful step to raise overall satisfaction. Satisfaction means how happy customers are.
  • Build an internal knowledge base or wiki for agents. A knowledge base is a shared library of answers. It should contain:
    • a guide to the brand voice (the way the brand sounds in writing)
    • canned responses for common scenarios, such as angry customers and refund requests (canned responses are ready-made replies)
    • upselling scripts (upselling means offering a bigger or extra product)
  • Support agents hear the most direct customer complaints. So ask the support team what they think causes low satisfaction. Their input on customer needs is one of the most useful sources for two things: changing how support is delivered, and improving the wider purchase experience.
  • Check customer questions and contact requests by reviewing chat and phone activity. Do this during the first months of a new hire's (new employee's) training. Send corrections privately as needed. Watching the chat app is the simplest way to see the answers given to customers. This helps catch misspellings and rude replies before customers notice.

Quality rubric

1 min
  • A quality rubric is a scoring checklist for support replies. It defines the key elements a high-quality reply must have. It gives an objective framework for scoring response quality. Typical criteria are:
    • response time (how long a customer waits for a reply)
    • alignment with company policy
    • brand tone and voice
    • any other elements the business considers essential
  • Judge the rubric against the actual customer outcome too. A reply can score well on tone (for example, playful humor) yet still fail to produce a clear, satisfying interaction.

Dropshipping means selling products you do not stock. The supplier ships them to the customer.

  • The sources give two email targets. Use 24 hours as the baseline rule for every support email. It is also the trigger for a follow-up. Use the 30-minute window where a dedicated person covers the inbox during staffed hours (set hours when a person is on duty).
  • Track customer support response times. Set a target time for every channel, such as tickets (written support requests), phone calls and live chat.
  • Set KPIs for each support channel: chat response time, email response time and phone answer rate (the share of calls you pick up). Where phone support is part of the job, track the percentage of calls converted to sales. Conversion means the share of calls that end in a sale.

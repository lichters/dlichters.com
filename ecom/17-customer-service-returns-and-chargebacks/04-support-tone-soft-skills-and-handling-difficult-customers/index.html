Playbook › 17. Customer service, returns and chargebacks

Support tone, soft skills and handling difficult customers

Section 17.4 · 4 of 22 in this chapter · 7 min read

In short

  • Be quick, truthful and friendly with every customer. Good service brings repeat buyers and good reviews.
  • Never guess. If you do not know an answer, say you will check, then call back with the confirmed answer.
  • Answer your phone when you can, because missed calls lose sales.
  • Stay calm with angry customers, and write down each step you take.
  • Match each country's style. In Switzerland, write ss instead of ß (a special German letter).

Support mindset and tone

  • Customer service drives both repeat purchases (customers who buy again) and five-star reviews (top ratings from buyers).
  • Reviews are a trust factor (a sign that makes people believe you). Trust raises your website conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy).
  • So service quality directly affects sales.
  • Always be quick, truthful and friendly, on phone, email, chat (live written messages on the website) and social media.
  • Empathy (feeling what the customer feels) is the most important skill in customer service.
  • Understand the customer's situation from their side. Listen fully.
  • Reflect back what you heard before you act.
  • Stay friendly and patient, even with rude customers.
  • Basic courtesy (good manners) matters more than any technique (a trick or method). For example, do not talk over the customer, and do not ignore their message.
  • Greet every customer warmly. Ask what problem you can solve for them.
  • Build a genuine personal connection (a real, human bond).
  • Customers who feel cared for are more understanding when problems appear during the order.
  • Remember details from earlier talks, such as names and past topics. Use them in later contacts.
  • This matters most for repeat buyers.
  • Keep a consistently positive tone (how your words sound) with every customer.
  • A competitor (another seller) selling the same product is often only one click away.
  • When product and price are similar, service is what sets your store apart.
  • Be empathetic. Imagine you are the customer waiting four weeks for a late product, with no clear date.
  • Frustration is natural in that situation, so acknowledge it in every reply.
  • Even if a delay comes from a supplier (the company that sends you the product), a carrier (the parcel company) or processing time outside your control, you are still responsible for solving it.
  • Respond with empathy and focus on the solution.
  • Customers often accept a long wait or a problem if you communicate regularly and stay polite.
  • Rudeness, not the problem itself, is the main reason customers leave.
  • Service that does not lead to a sale still has value.
  • It builds goodwill (a good feeling that builds trust for later) and a human connection.
  • Listening to a customer who needs to talk can keep them loyal for future purchases.
  • Do not sell products you do not carry (products you do not have in your range).
  • Still listen and help where you can.
  • Being a supportive point of contact (the person they can turn to) can keep the customer loyal even when no sale happens.
  • Treat your customers with respect and aim to improve their lives, since the best dropshipping businesses (stores where a supplier ships each order for you) excel at service.
  • Customer relationships are where you can separate yourself from competitors.

Chat and proactive contact

Proactive contact means you reach out first.

  • Staff may start chats with website visitors when they have time, but this is optional.
  • If a visitor does not reply to the first message, do not send a follow-up, so customers do not feel pestered.

Regional communication styles

  • Match each market's communication style (how people usually talk and write there).
  • Use separate reply templates (ready-made texts you fill in) for each market, even within the same language.

Germany Often more direct, harder to satisfy on refunds (money given back) Expect firm refund expectations and answer directly.
Switzerland Very polite Write German-language replies without the letter ß (a special German letter); use ss instead.

Never guess, and admit what you don't know

  • Never guess or make up answers to customer questions.
  • Give information only once the owner (the person who runs the store) or the supplier has confirmed it.
  • Never invent an answer to a product or technical question.
  • If the customer gets wrong information and finds out after delivery, you will have to pay to fix the problem.
  • If you do not know a product detail, do not guess.
  • Tell the customer you will check with the manufacturer (the company that makes the product) and call back with accurate information.
  • Then use the answer to update your product page (the page where customers buy).
  • Admitting you do not know an answer, then following up with the correct information, builds trust.
  • Customers appreciate accurate information.
  • Over time, the owner gains a stronger knowledge base (a collection of facts you can look up) in the niche (your product area).

Phone reply when you do not know the answer:

Good question, [customer name]. I don't know the answer at the moment, so I'll consult our team and return your call as quickly as possible. May I have your phone number and email address?
Use this reply on the phone when you do not know the answer to a customer's question. Replace each bracketed placeholder and keep the order of the steps:

That is a great question. I do not have the exact answer right now, but I would like to reach out to our partner at [brand or partner name] so I can get you the correct information. Can I get your name, phone number and email address? I will follow up with you as soon as I have the answer, because I do not want to give you incorrect information.

Handling a question you cannot answer

  1. Tell the customer you need to check the answer. Do not guess.
  2. Offer a callback (you call them back) within a stated time, such as five or ten minutes. Or put them on hold (a short pause, often with music) if the phone system allows it.
  3. Check the answer with your supplier, by phone or on the supplier's website.
  4. Call the customer back and give them the confirmed answer.

Holding reply (a short answer that buys you time):

Thanks for asking. I want to confirm that exactly before I answer, so you get accurate information. Could I call you back in five minutes?

Voicemail and callbacks

  • Sending callers to voicemail (a recorded message box for missed calls) loses sales, because callers simply phone the next seller on their list.
  • Customers have said they bought because someone answered the phone. So answered calls are a cheap sales safeguard (a low-cost way to protect sales).
  • Voicemail greeting:
Hello, you've reached [business name]. We are busy right now and cannot take your call immediately, and we don't want you to have to wait on hold. To use our callback service, leave your name and your contact details after the tone, and we will call you back as soon as we can. Thank you for your patience.

Handling a voicemail callback

  1. Receive the SMS (text message) or email notification that a message has been left.
  2. Listen to the message and note the caller's name and what they need.
  3. Call the customer back as soon as you are able to.

When to call customers

  • Avoid calling customers early in the morning.
  • In most countries, keep calls before about 8 p.m.. Use evening and night hours instead.
  • Evening callbacks work because customers are usually at home at that time and can actually talk.
  • Calling after a day of work also fits a side-business owner's schedule.

Difficult and angry customers

  • Do not return an angry caller's hostility (hostile, unfriendly behavior) with hostility.
  • Do not block customers in anger. A single heated reaction can turn a fixable problem into a lost customer and a public complaint.
  • Angry customers often vent (let out feelings) about problems in their own life, so their anger is rarely about the store itself.
  • Stay calm and keep your composure (staying calm under pressure).
  • Still follow your own process.
  • Some customers stay unreasonable no matter how well you respond, and that is not your fault.
  • As long as you complete your own steps correctly, their reaction does not change the correct response.
  • Do not take personal offense (do not feel personally hurt) at customers who react badly to a sensible response.
  • Document each step so you can show you handled the situation correctly.

Handling objections that products ship from China

  • Some customers object that products come from China. This is rare.
  • It can usually be handled by giving the tracking number (a code to follow the parcel) and finding a solution with the customer.
  1. When a customer objects that the product ships from China, first send the tracking number, since customers often ask for it.
  2. If they remain upset, point out that many everyday consumer electronics, such as iPhones, are also made or shipped from China.
  3. If the problem is serious, offer a solution.

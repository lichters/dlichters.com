Playbook › 17. Customer service, returns and chargebacks

Writing shipping, returns, refund and warranty policies

Section 17.9 · 9 of 22 in this chapter · 42 min read

In short

  • Your return rules must match each supplier's return rules, because the supplier decides what you can offer.
  • Every return window needs an end date, such as 30 days, and it must be written on your site.
  • Put shipping and return details where buyers see them before they pay.
  • Products from suppliers that take no returns must be marked "not eligible for returns" on every page.
  • Before you publish, check your Contact Us page and policy pages with Google Gemini (Google's AI tool) and fix what it flags.
More key points
  • Your return policy must say where to send items, and that the return address comes only after your return is approved.
  • Never publish AI text that still has empty square brackets or example values you did not check. Match every value to how your store really works.
  • For a new store, keep the default settings. Change them later, when you know each supplier's real rules.

Before you start: supplier terms and policy basis

2 min
  • A supplier is the company that holds the product and ships it to your buyer. Dropshipping means you sell this way and never keep the stock yourself.
  • Base your store's return policy (the rules for sending items back and getting money back) on each supplier's return policy.
  • If a supplier allows 30-day free returns, your store can offer the same.
  • If a supplier allows no returns, mark those products on the site as not eligible for returns.
  • Read the signed supplier approval documents, the papers where the supplier agrees to work with you.
  • These papers state each supplier's return terms. Your store's return policy is set around them.
  • Match your return window (the number of days a buyer has to ask for a return) to what the supplier allows.
  • Read the supplier's agreement documents before you set the policy.
  • Show any longer supplier window on the product pages.
  • Return responsibility for heavy items depends on each supplier's policy. You learn it when the supplier approves you.
  • If suppliers charge return shipping (the cost of sending the item back) or restocking fees (a fee kept from the refund to cover handling the returned item), state those fees in your shipping and returns policy.
  • Base that policy on the supplier's terms.
  • Return policies differ between brands.
  • Your store's returns policy should summarise all supplier policies.
  • Each supplier's policy should be noted in the retailer paperwork.
  • Before you list a brand, check its return process (how returns are handled from start to finish).
  • Brands with supplier-level support and clear policies reduce return and warranty problems. A warranty is the maker's promise to repair or replace faulty goods.
  • Expect higher return rates in some niches (narrow product markets).
  • Avoid those niches when you choose products.
  • Compare your return policy with the top two or three competitors (other stores selling the same kind of product) in your niche.
  • A policy with refunds within 30 days without a reason, a restocking fee of 15% to 20%, and customer-paid return shipping is acceptable if competitors do the same.
  • You can set the fee lower to stand out.
  • To set return shipping fees, look at the return policies of your top future competitors.
  • Model yours on them.
  • Choose whether to charge the actual cost or a fixed amount for return shipping.

Shipping policy content

2 min
  • The shipping policy must be clear enough for a reviewer to understand within one minute.
  • It must state when orders ship after purchase. "Ship" here means "send out", also called dispatch. A range of days is acceptable.
  • It must state the average transit time (how many days the parcel takes to arrive).
  • It must state the shipping cost, including whether shipping is free.
  • Vague shipping policies are a known cause of compliance problems (breaking ad or platform rules, which can get a store blocked).
  • Avoid any wording that could be read as misleading.
  • Use this template for the shipping policy text:
Once your purchase goes through, you'll get an order confirmation email. We dispatch every order within [X] days of placement and send you an email with the tracking number as soon as it leaves us. Across the United States, every order ships free through [carrier(s) you typically use]. Choose free shipping and transit takes [X] to [Y] business days. If the store offers faster options, add this line: customers who need quicker delivery can pick [upgraded delivery option] at checkout, and those expedited rates are calculated live at checkout. If the store ships internationally at no charge, add this line: every international order ships free, and with free international shipping transit runs [X] to [Y] weeks.
  • Reference figures from the US example in the sample text: dispatch within 3 days.
  • US transit is 3 to 5 business days. A business day is a working day, Monday to Friday.
  • International transit is 1 to 3 weeks.
  • The carrier is USPS (US Postal Service, a US delivery company).
  • If you do not yet know your real average shipping times, check competitors' shipping pages to estimate them.
  • The policy can be updated later. It only needs to exist and be accurate from the start.
  • A high-ticket dropshipping store (one that sells expensive products) usually does not ship internationally.
  • Publish the international shipping paragraph only if the store actually ships abroad.
  • A store that ships worldwide should state the international transit time in weeks.
  • The shipping page should list the expected shipping time for each supplier or brand, because suppliers ship at different speeds.
  • Update these times as they become clear, for example after a few weeks of order data.
  • Shipping time must be stated in the store's policies, along with contact methods, the return policy and product descriptions.
  • Missing details are a common reason for a store review (a check by an ad platform or payment provider). (2023)
  • Keep store policies short and clear, not long legal texts.
  • Sample policies are only a few sentences each.
  • Many shoppers do not know a new store.
  • Write the shipping policy (cost, delivery time, carrier) and the returns policy in plain English.
  • Make them better than competitors' policies.
  • The contact page must offer at least two ways to reach the business, such as a phone number and a business email.
  • A contact form may be added, but it does not replace the two methods.
  • Until dedicated support mailboxes exist, use support@ followed by your domain (for example support@yourdomain.com) in the policy templates.
  • Replace it with the real mailbox once it exists.

Shipping and returns page placement

1 min
  • Shoppers read the shipping policy before they spend a lot of money.
  • Place shipping information where buyers look before ordering, and make it easy to find and complete.
  • The returns and exchange page answers what happens if the buyer is unhappy. Buyers ask this before purchasing.
  • Keep it visible before checkout (the payment step) to reduce hesitation.
  • The shipping page can link to the returns policy.
  • A short shipping statement with a link to the returns page reduces questions.
  • Leaving the return policy off the page leaves shoppers worried about damaged or unwanted items.
  • State easy returns clearly on the page.
  • Most merchandise sold by mail ships subject to return.
  • Return rights are a standard part of direct response selling (selling through ads that push buyers to act now).
  • A clear return policy removes the buyer's risk.
  • Do not open the returns and exchanges policy by default, especially a harsh one.
  • Place it in an expandable section (a box that opens when clicked) for curious buyers.
  • A long multi-step return process can scare off undecided visitors.
  • Pre-footer trust column (the strip just above the bottom of the page): use a clear header such as "Easy returns".
  • Link that header to the returns policy page once that page exists. Until then, leave the link empty.
  • Policy pages such as shipping or terms are needed before they are linked in the pre-footer.
  • In the example, a temporary link to terms of service was used. Replace it with the correct page later.
  • Publish the shipping policy and the returns policy on the store.
  • Make sure everyone handling orders has read both before working on them.

Free shipping, add-ons and accessories

1 min
  • A free standard shipping offer on all products is a common store policy.
  • Expedited shipping (faster shipping for extra money) or white glove delivery (staff carry the item in and set it up) can be offered as paid add-ons.
  • Conditional free shipping (free only when set conditions are met) is set up on the shipping page of Shopify (the online store software).
  • Create separate shipping profiles (saved sets of shipping rules) for each case.
  • Each profile lists the criteria that must be met for it to apply.
  • Accessories with flat-rate shipping (one fixed shipping price) should be sold only as add-ons to a main product.
  • Do not list accessories as standalone products.
  • Do not include accessories in ads and product feeds (product lists sent to ad platforms).
  • When offering free shipping, a customer return means the shipping cost already paid may not be recovered.
  • Design the return policy around each supplier's return rules, not a single store-wide policy.
  • Advertising free shipping while charging customers for return shipping is acceptable.
  • The return costs must be clearly disclosed in the policy.
  • Clarity in advance prevents complaints about shipping.

Return window

1 min
  • Return windows should always have an end date.
  • Unlimited free returns make it impossible to tell when a product is profitable.
  • Match the store's return window to the supplier's policy.
  • Example: 30 days when the supplier allows 30 days.
  • The US average return window for physical products is about 30 days.
  • Supplier windows can be 14, 30, 60 or up to 180 days. (2023)
  • No general US legal return period exists for ordinary products that the store must offer. (2023)
  • This is general information, not legal advice. (2023)
  • A UK retailer may be required to offer a 14-day return policy for consumer goods.
  • Check local rules before setting the policy.
  • The US has no equivalent blanket rule for most products.

Return conditions and eligibility

1 min
  • Returned items must go back in original packaging with all parts.
  • A returned item must be unused.
  • It must be kept in the state it arrived in.
  • It must still be in its original packaging.
  • Any return needs a receipt or other proof of purchase.
  • A change-of-mind return (the buyer changed their mind, the item is not faulty) can be accepted even when special packaging has been opened.
  • This is true as long as the product itself is in original condition.
  • Record this condition, because it decides whether a return is accepted.
  • For change-of-mind returns, the store's policy should copy the supplier's policy for gently used products. (2023)
  • The store cannot send the item back to the supplier under different terms. (2023)
  • Return conditions to state in the policy: [L0539 student input] "New, unused, in original packaging, all accessories included" is the example condition set.
  • Return wording for a dropshipping store reads: "Items must be returned in their original condition."
  • Record the return conditions as one clear rule set per supplier when they differ.

Non-returnable items and excluded products

2 min
  • Typical exceptions to change-of-mind returns include the following.
    • Sanitation products (toilets, bidets and similar bathroom items) that were opened or installed.
    • Electronics, unless in sealed original packaging.
    • Items sold by the metre.
    • Specially ordered items.
    • Installed, modified or used items.
  • Free freight included in the price can be deducted from the refund.
  • Bidets and toilets (personal hygiene products) cannot be returned once they are opened or installed.
  • Food and flowers (perishables, things that spoil fast), gift cards, and health and personal-care products cannot be sent back.
  • This is the exclusion in the Source D sample policy.
  • If a product is an ingestible (anything you eat or swallow), a hygiene item, a pre-owned good (used before), or a final-sale item (cannot be returned or exchanged), the return policy must say so.
  • These categories are often excluded from standard refunds.
  • The exclusion must appear on the policy page. Do not leave it for the buyer to discover.
  • Refunds apply only to full-price items.
  • Discounted goods are not refundable in the sample policy.
  • A supplier with a no-return policy should be matched on your store.
  • Add a note to each product page from that supplier. The note says the item is not eligible for returns unless it arrives defective (faulty) or damaged (broken).
  • Use the supplier's exact wording. (2023)
  • Products the supplier does not take back must be marked as non-returnable on the policy page.
  • Mark them also on each product page. This prevents disputes when a customer later asks for a return.
  • Suppliers without a return policy can still be sold.
  • The product pages and the return policy page must state that those products are not eligible for return.
  • If a supplier refuses returns for a product, you have two options.
    • Option 1: mark the product as not eligible for returns on its page.
    • Option 2: ship the product to a fulfilment centre (a warehouse that packs and ships orders) that you control, if you want to sell it.
  • If a supplier refuses returns for damaged items, do not accept returns for those products either.
  • State this exclusion in the return policy.
  • Also state it on that supplier's product pages. (2025)
  • When some suppliers accept no returns, state that clearly on their product pages, where customers see it before buying.
  • Products from suppliers that do not accept returns should be excluded from the return policy.
  • Add a note on those product pages and in the shipping and returns tab (the section of the product page) that the items are not eligible for returns.
  • Products from suppliers that accept no returns must be named as not eligible for return on the shipping and returns page.
  • Name them also on their product pages.
  • Wording for a no-return product: "This item is not eligible for returns unless it arrives defective or damaged. [Use the supplier's exact return wording]"

Restocking fees

1 min
  • Restocking fees and return shipping costs depend on each supplier's return policy.
  • Customers usually pay return shipping, but this varies by supplier.
  • A restocking fee should match the supplier's fee, not be added on top of it.
  • The purpose is to avoid losing money on returns, not to make profit from them. (2025)
  • Restocking fees and return terms can differ per brand. (2025)
  • Show them on the general return policy page. (2025)
  • Optionally, show them in expandable sections on each product page. (2025)
  • Use this structure for a brand-specific return policy (2025):
Our return policies vary by brand. Please see the list below for the return policy of each brand.

Brand A products can be returned within 30 days. A 10% restocking fee applies, and you are responsible for return shipping costs.

Brand B products can be returned within 60 days. A 20% restocking fee applies, and you are responsible for return shipping costs.

Replace the brand names, return windows and fees with the exact terms from each supplier.

Refunds, timing, cancellations

3 min
  • The refund timeframe should be a fixed number of business days counted from when the item is received and inspected, for example within 7 business days. Stating the start point of the clock prevents customers from expecting refunds on the day the return is shipped.
  • Sample refund rule: approved refunds go back to the original payment method within 5 to 7 business days after the returned item is inspected.
  • Partial refunds may apply to items not in original condition, damaged items, or returns after the window.
  • Refund wording from the lesson: "If you're unhappy with your products for any reason, we refund the full amount minus shipping. To ask for a refund, email [business email] or phone [phone number]. [Add restocking fee, if any.] [Add brands or products that cannot be returned, if any.]"
If you're unhappy with your products for any reason, we refund the full amount minus shipping. To ask for a refund, email [business email] or phone [phone number]. [Add restocking fee, if any.] [Add brands or products that cannot be returned, if any.]
  • The lesson's sample policy refunds the full product price minus the original shipping charges and tells customers to request this by email or phone.
  • The refund policy must state any restocking fee and any brands or product types that cannot be returned, because a reviewer checks for these.
  • The refund and return policy should answer: what the refund policy is, how a customer requests a refund, how returns work for damaged items, and how a customer starts a return.
  • Source B's example refunded the original shipping cost for store-caused returns and did not refund it for remorse returns. The 2025 template in Dropship defaults: store refunds original shipping cost for defective or wrong items.
  • Default refund policy settings for a new dropship store: the customer pays for return shipping, the store pays for defective or wrong items and refunds the original shipping cost, orders canceled before shipping receive a full refund, and exchanges use prepaid shipping labels.
  • Cancellation policy: full refund if canceled before shipping.
  • Original shipping cost policy: the original shipping cost is refunded for store-caused returns.
  • For a steam shower or bathtub that fails local inspection, the return is the purchase price minus the original shipping fee, the return shipping fee and a 15% restocking fee if it is in original packaging. If opened or assembled, the restocking fee rises to 25%.
  • Refund disputes are best prevented with clear product page wording about returns. Keep the wording specific to the supplier and product type. (2020)
  • A refund dispute can happen when a return promise is inconsistent (see Consistency).

Warranty text and guarantees

1 min

  • Warranty blurb for product pages: "We offer a [warranty length] warranty on all [Brand] products. This warranty is limited to [scope, for example parts only] and covers [short description of what it includes]." Place it directly under the warranty statement on each product page of that brand.

Blanket policy versus supplier exceptions

4 min

Sources disagree on whether to match each supplier individually or use one blanket policy with exceptions. Each applies in the case stated:

  • Match each supplier individually: "Don't create uniform policy; match supplier policies individually."
  • Use one blanket policy with exceptions only for suppliers that do not accept returns. (2021 onwards)
  • Use one general policy for most suppliers and give unique policies to suppliers whose terms differ. (2023)
  • Use one standard 30-day return policy for the store and add exceptions only for extreme outliers. This keeps the policy simple. (2023)
  • Use a blanket return policy built from the average of your suppliers' return policies.
  • Set your return and shipping policy as an average of the supplier policies. Do not copy each supplier's policy into the store.
  • For a store with several brands, use one general return policy that works for all suppliers, or add a separate return policy tab on each product page for a brand with different terms. (2023)

When each applies:

  • Use the blanket policy when most suppliers share similar terms, and show exceptions separately.

  • Use an individual supplier policy when supplier terms differ widely, such as a supplier with no returns or a supplier with a 14-day window.

  • Supplier return terms differ in windows, restocking fees and return shipping. Because of this, a blanket policy can sometimes cost around $100 on a single return.

  • Main shipping and return policy covers most products. Any supplier with stricter terms gets its own policy on the product page, and those exclusions are also listed in the main policy.

  • If a supplier refuses returns for a product, mark the product as not eligible for returns on its page, or ship it to a fulfilment centre you control.

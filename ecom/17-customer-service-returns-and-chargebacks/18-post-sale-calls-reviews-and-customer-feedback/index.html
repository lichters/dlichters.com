Post-sale calls, reviews and customer feedback

Section 17.18 · 18 of 22 in this chapter · 15 min read

In short

  • Ask every new customer for a review after delivery. Do not wait for reviews to come on their own.
  • Call or message customers at the time their order should have arrived.
  • Ask for feedback on purpose. Customers rarely give it unless you ask.
  • Never make up reviews or copy them from other stores.
  • Keep surveys short, with about five questions.
This section has 19 parts. Tap one to open it.

Why post-sale contact matters

2 min
  • Most online shops (e-commerce stores) see only about 1%, 5% or 10% of customers leave a review.
  • A planned set of follow-up calls after the purchase (a structured post-sale call program) can raise the review rate (the share of customers who leave a review) to roughly 50% or more.
  • Post-sale calls serve two goals at once. They make the customer feel looked after by checking that their needs are met and their questions answered. They also bring in reviews and feedback for the business.
  • Post-sale calls reveal what customers expect, want, or would like improved. This can be information the business did not have before.
  • A customer who gets an unexpected, helpful follow-up call is more likely to talk about the business and to buy again. Sending upsell messages (offers for a bigger or extra product) before or just after the call makes this effect stronger.
  • Reviews will not come on their own. The business has to ask for them actively, most effectively by phone. Hoping customers will write one by themselves is not an effective approach.
  • Asking customers for feedback on the business shows where to improve. It is often a core part of conversion rate optimization (CRO, changing the store so more visitors buy). It also makes customers feel they have a say. This raises their mental investment (how much they care about the business) and the chance they buy again.
  • Customer feedback will not arrive on its own. Ask for it proactively (on your own initiative) every time you want to learn something about the business.
  • Feedback can point to website usability problems (the site is hard to use), content gaps (information customers want but cannot find), weak customer service, or problems with how products are delivered. Targeted customer answers show where the business can grow.
  • Track customer feedback and queries (questions) to find weak points that do not show up in analytics (the reports that show visitors and sales). Be proactive about shipping delays or site issues.
  • After-sale service (refunds, returns, damages and defects) shapes the feedback score (the overall rating from customers) and loyal customers. Be proactive about shipping delays. Monitor customer feedback for product or page issues.
  • Request reviews from happy customers consistently. Track the number of reviews compared with sales volume (how many sales you made). **

Timing the contact

1 min

Sources give different timing rules. Choose the rule that matches your own delivery times.

Rule Source Applies when
Call 7 days after purchase by default, adjusted to typical shipping duration (how long shipping usually takes). If items normally ship in 14 days, call on day 14 so the customer has received the order. ** Default; adjust to your shipping duration
Call on day 10 after purchase if deliveries normally take 7 days. The exact timing depends on the store's own shipping times. ** Standard delivery of about 7 days
Schedule the follow-up call 3-5 days after delivery confirmation (proof from the carrier that the parcel arrived). Delivery is confirmed
  • Call each customer once the product should have arrived.
  • When delivery status is available, base the review call on confirmed delivery. If it is not available, use the store's typical delivery time. For example, call 10 days after purchase when orders usually arrive within 7 days. **

Who makes the calls and how the routine runs

1 min
  • Assign the calls to yourself, using calendar reminders. Or assign them to a customer service virtual assistant (VA, a helper who works remotely). The VA makes the calls in the gaps between inbound support calls (calls customers make to you). **
  • Making the calls yourself gives you a much better understanding of your market and customers. The insights can improve other marketing, content ideas, and emails that answer common questions. **
  • Run the post-sale review call as a routine for every new customer. Do not pick only customers who seem likely to review. The consistent routine raises the review rate. A selected sample does not. **
  • Do not place the review call to customers known to be unhappy. About 95% or more of customers are usually not upset, so the call is safe to make to most of the customer base. **

Setting up the review multiplier call system (a routine that multiplies the number of reviews) **:

  1. Decide whether you or your customer service team makes the calls.
  2. Share the call script with the team.
  3. Have the team schedule calls to customers at the right time after purchase, to collect reviews.

Review call process

1 min

Review collection process, in order **:

  1. Develop a call script first, based on a template and adapted to your own situation.
  2. Offer the customer several ways to leave a review.
  3. Ask customers who are very positive for a video review.
  4. Have your team manage the process.
  5. Track which reviews you gained. Follow up with customers who promised a review but have not left one.

Delivered-order review follow-up, Trello workflow:

Trello is an online board made of cards and lists. A "comment" is a note added to a card.

  1. Find delivered orders not yet reviewed in Trello's "Delivered/Pending Review" list.
  2. Call the customer and ask about product experience and satisfaction.
  3. Request specific content: text review, product photo, or video.
  4. If the customer hesitates, offer an incentive (a reward such as a discount code, gift card, or item).
  5. Collect the content and confirm the incentive via follow-up email.
  6. Move the card to "Reviewed" and document in a Trello comment the content type and date.

Scheduled follow-up call, review and testimonial (a testimonial is a customer's written or video praise of the product):

  1. Schedule a follow-up call 3-5 days after delivery confirmation.
  2. Call the customer and ask about product experience, quality, and satisfaction.
  3. Request specific content: text review, product photo, video, or testimonial.
  4. If the customer declines, offer an incentive (discount code, gift card, or physical item).
  5. Collect the content via email or directly document the response.
  6. Send an incentive confirmation and thank-you message.
  7. Add a Trello comment with the completion date and content type received.

Review call script

1 min

Use the script as a guide to content only. Speak in your own natural language (your everyday words). Fill the [brackets] before the call. **

1. Greeting: Hi [customer first name], this is [agent name] from [store name]. I am calling to thank you for your order of [product name].
2. Check-in: I just wanted to make sure it arrived in good condition and that it is working the way you expected. Are you happy with it?
3a. If the customer is happy: That is great to hear. Feedback from customers really helps us serve you and others better. Would you be willing to leave us a quick two-minute review? Then send the text review link [review link]. If you like, you can also leave a short video or send a photo with the product. Our video review tool [VideoPeel link] makes that a few clicks on your phone.
3b. If the customer is not happy: Thank you for telling me, I am sorry about [issue]. Let me fix that now: [resolution options]. Once it is sorted, would you be open to sharing your experience in a review?
4. Close: Thank you for your time and for being a [store name] customer. You will get a short follow-up email with the review link [and reward details, if any].

Follow-up email: send it within [number of hours] of the call. Restate the thank-you, include the text review link and the video or photo link, and state any reward terms [e.g. 10% off a future order or store credit for a text review; a larger reward for a video or photo review].
  • Use the call to mention other retention programs (ways to keep customers coming back), such as loyalty programs (rewards for repeat buying) and referral programs (rewards for bringing in friends). **

Review options and rewards

1 min
  • Offer four review options during the call: dictating the review over the phone, emailing the review text to the business, following a review link from the review app (the app that collects reviews) to post it online, or recording a video review. **
  • Ask customers who are very positive for a video review. **
  • For a post-purchase customer testimonial, send an email within 24 hours of purchase. Offer a refund in exchange for a video testimonial submitted through a form. The form must collect first and last name, the video upload, and a checkbox giving permission to use the content in ads.
  • Offer an incentive (a reward) when a customer hesitates or declines, as in the procedures above. Do not offer incentives in a way that makes the review inauthentic (not genuine). See the integrity rules below.

Review request emails and messages

2 min

Review request, short delivery check. The note in this template says to keep the message to one or two sentences, to include no link to a review app, and not to ask for a photo or video:

Subject: [store name] order #[order number]

Hi [customer name],

I see your order arrived on [delivery date]. I would really appreciate any feedback on the product and your experience ordering from [store name].

Thank you,
[your name]

Notes: Keep this message to one or two sentences. Do not include a link to a review app, and do not ask for a photo or video.

Review request, follow-up after delivery:

Hi [customer first name], I saw that your order was delivered, and I wanted to make sure it arrived in good condition and matches what you expected. Please reply and let me know how your experience was, since I am the owner of [store name] and your feedback reaches me directly. If you are happy with it, would you mind leaving a review here: [review link]? If you know anyone who might like our products, we would be glad to take care of them as well. Thank you, [your name]

Review request, delivery check with photo request:

Hi [Customer name], I just saw that your order was delivered, and I wanted to check in. I hope you are loving it. Is everything okay with your order? If you are happy with it, would you be willing to leave a short review, and send photos or a video if you have them?

Review request, after-delivery conversation:

Hi [customer first name], I wanted to follow up on the [product name] you received a few days ago. I'm curious-how are you enjoying it? How was your experience with us? [Listen to response] That's wonderful to hear! Since we're building our reputation, I'd really appreciate if you could leave us a review on our site. Would you also be willing to send us a photo with the product? Thank you so much for your business.

Personal review request email procedure:

  1. Send a personal email to each customer after delivery, not an automated one.
  2. Ask whether the product matches what they expected and whether their experience was good.
  3. Ask for a review by linking to the review page.
  4. If the customer replies with a positive experience but leaves no review after a set period, you may use that email as a review on the store, with permission.
  • The sources give two different approaches to the review link in the request message. The 2024 Source A template says to include no link. The 2024 Source A template includes the review link. Both are recorded here. The sources do not state which applies in which situation.

Review request channels and volume

1 min
  • Review requests can reach customers through post-purchase email automation (emails sent automatically after buying), phone calls, text messages, or photo submission requests. Phone calls create the highest engagement (customer replies and activity) and personal connection. This is especially valuable early on, when you are building social proof (other buyers' approval that makes new visitors trust the store). Email automation handles volume. Calls handle relationship-building.

Review counts and content

1 min
  • Minimum review count to launch: 10 reviews in total, with 6 to 7 of them image-based (with photos). Image reviews convert significantly better than text reviews, meaning more of them lead to a sale.
  • Only use reviews that belong to the store, such as existing reviews from the supplier with permission. Never copy reviews from competitor sites (rival stores).

Increasing review count with CSV import (not recommended) (a CSV is a simple spreadsheet file; AliExpress and Amazon are online marketplaces; Riview and Loox are review apps):

  1. Import initial reviews from AliExpress or Amazon via the Riview or Loox app.
  2. Download the CSV file of imported reviews.
  3. Copy individual reviews and duplicate them 10x within the CSV.
  4. For example, take 10 reviews and duplicate each 10 times to create a list of 100 reviews.
  5. Re-import the expanded CSV into the app.
  6. Customers see the higher review count and trust the product, even though they rarely check beyond the first 10-20 reviews.
  • This procedure is documented here as the source gives it. But it produces copied, fabricated (made-up) reviews. It conflicts with the honest-review rules and must not be used.

Authentic reviews and review integrity

1 min
  • Build product reviews honestly, without fabrication (making them up). When a customer makes a purchase, follow up relentlessly to request a review. Authentic customer reviews build market credibility faster than hiding "no reviews yet" or creating fake reviews. **
  • Changing reviewer names, genders, or avatars (profile pictures) on borrowed or template reviews to make them look real turns them into made-up testimonials. Use only real reviews with the names or initials the customer actually gave.
  • Look at the reviews on the product page for negative or suspicious entries. Identify reviews from people who did not buy the product. These are often posted by competitors or haters (people who dislike the brand). Remove or do not approve those reviews, and keep genuine feedback visible.

Review dates

1 min
  • Review dates can be omitted if you are concerned that older reviews make the product look outdated. Alternatively, keep dates visible, because they show product longevity (that the product has sold for a long time) and an established sales history. The sources give both options. Choose by the product's situation.

Ratings and platform risk

1 min
  • Meta (the company behind Facebook and Instagram, where ads run) may ask buyers about their experience about a week after purchase and collect a rating. If the store's ratings fall below a threshold (a minimum line), the ad account (the account used to run ads) can be banned. This risk is mostly seen with very high-volume stores (stores with many sales) that have many poor ratings. The source does not give the threshold.

Post-sale feedback surveys

1 min
  • A post-purchase survey should contain about five questions. One to three questions also work. Five balances getting people to engage against making the survey too long to finish. **
  • Survey questions must be relevant to customers, answerable by them, and produce meaningful feedback on what the business actually wants to learn. Rotate the questions (change them over time) as your open questions (things you still want to learn) change. **
  • Ask only a few questions per call so the caller is not burdened.

Set up the post-purchase feedback system, in this order **:

  1. Create a post-purchase survey using a tool such as Google Forms, SurveyMonkey, GoSurvey, or Typeform. Choose about five questions from a template list or write them yourself.
  2. Set up an automated email that sends the survey to customers at your chosen point after purchase.
  3. Define the reward for responses and set up the thank-you message, either a template email or a video.

Thanking respondents

1 min
  • Thank every customer who responds to the survey, ideally with a personal message. Even a short email reply saying the response was seen and naming the reward is enough. **

Thank-you follow-up process **:

Bonjoro is a tool for sending recorded personal messages.

  1. Receive the survey response, which includes the respondent's name and email.
  2. Trigger the thank-you message for that person, either a pre-recorded Bonjoro message or a reply email.
  3. Mention that the reward is available and where to claim it.

Call-based customer questions

1 min

Customer service survey during calls **:

A Q&A tracking sheet is a spreadsheet of questions and answers.

  1. Let the caller first get their problem solved or receive the information they called for.
  2. Then say you have a couple of quick questions that are asked of everyone who calls.
  3. Ask whether they noticed anything not working on the site. If yes, ask what it was.
  4. Ask whether they looked at other sites while researching the product. If yes, ask which ones.
  5. Ask which of those they liked better and why, and what they liked about them.
  6. Record the answers in the Q&A tracking sheet.
  • Asking questions during a pre-purchase call (a call before buying) is especially valuable because the buying decision is still fresh in the caller's mind. Ask only a few questions per call so the caller is not burdened. **

Tracking reviews and feedback

1 min
  • Record every customer who was called, who had a conversation, and who promised a review. Also record whether the review was actually posted. Follow up with customers who promised but have not yet left the review. **
  • Track the number of reviews compared with sales volume. Request reviews from happy customers consistently. **
  • Use a Q&A tracking sheet (Google Sheets, or any tool such as Trello or Asana, a task-management app) to record customer service answers. This matters most when virtual assistants handle service. It must be built into the customer service SOPs (standard operating procedures, the written step-by-step rules for a task), and access must be given to the right people. **
  • Document each completed review in Trello with a comment that records the content type and date.

CSAT: measurement and analysis

1 min

CSAT (customer satisfaction score) is a rating customers give after a contact. Gorgias is a tool for handling customer messages. A ticket is one customer request in that tool.

  • CSAT data is in Gorgias under the Statistics section, in the Satisfaction view. It shows a chronological list (in time order) of tickets, including ratings of 4 stars or lower. The Satisfaction dashboard has a CSV download button to export all CSAT scores.
  • CSAT ratings alone have limited value. The written customer comments, especially negative ones, explain what to change in customer service.
  • CSAT is shaped by what happens before and after purchase, not only by support. Clear product descriptions set expectations. Good product photography, faster and more reliable shipping, free shipping for eligible customers, and a helpful post-purchase experience all raise satisfaction.
  • When a customer gives a low CSAT score without an explanation, reach out proactively and ask for written feedback. This reveals why the rating was given, what the customer expected, and how to prevent lower scores in the future.

Review negative CSAT responses in this order:

  1. Read and audit the low-rated responses, not just the positive ones.
  2. Identify recurring themes, such as long wait times, confusing information, or unresolved product issues.
  3. Tally (count) how many tickets mention each recurring issue.
  4. Rank issues by tally count. The more tallies an issue has, the higher its impact on satisfaction.
  5. Fix the highest-impact issues first, for example through agent training or automation for faster replies.

Acting on feedback

1 min
  • Post-sale calls reveal improvement points that may not have been known before. Use them to adjust customer service, product information, and delivery communication.
  • Insights from calling customers yourself can improve other marketing, content ideas, and emails that answer common questions. **
  • Feedback can point to usability problems, missing content, weak customer service, or delivery issues. Act on the answers that recur across customers.

Processing returns, refunds and exchanges

Section 17.10 · 10 of 22 in this chapter · 17 min read

In short

  • Write a clear return policy that says where returns go and who pays the shipping.
  • Your supplier agreement, the contract you made when the supplier was approved, decides who handles returns.
  • A change-of-mind return must be claimed within 7 days of delivery, the customer pays shipping, and the reply is store credit only.
  • Do not refund, or let store credit be used, until the supplier confirms the item arrived in good condition.
  • Refunds usually reach the card in 5 to 7 business days, so say this in every refund reply.
More key points
  • Most customers are honest. Treat them well.
  • Use strict rules only when abuse starts to cost real money.
  • Refund abuse is mainly a risk for cheap items, under about $50.
  • For a return outside the policy, explain the policy politely first. Then consider accepting the return.
  • Set up your recall and unopened-return process before you need it.

Return policy, ownership and who pays

1 min
  • Publish a clear return policy. It states a return address (where items are sent back to) and who pays return shipping. Then refuse fraudulent claims (false or invented return requests). A clear policy makes refusal defensible.
  • Set your return policy at least as competitive as those of your competitors.
  • Who handles a return is set by the supplier agreement, not by who ships the order. Check that agreement. If the return process is not stated, ask the supplier.
  • If you ship on your own account and a customer returns an item, create the return label through your carrier (the shipping company, such as FedEx or USPS). Send the label directly to the customer. Whether the item goes back to the supplier warehouse depends on the supplier's return policy. **
  • Many suppliers do not accept returns. So set up return addresses on your side for customers who return items, and record these addresses in the accounts sheet.
  • Do not set up a process that requires shipping returned items to yourself and reselling them. It is messy. Match the supplier's policy, and keep exclusions (items not accepted for return) in your general return policy. **
  • Do not use a personal home address as the return address when using Buy with Google (a Google checkout that shows your address to shoppers). The address is shown to the customer, and returns can go there if they do not contact you first. Consider carefully whether to use Buy with Google at all. **
  • Keep unsold returned items on a list. An assistant can offer them to customers at a lower price when the product is in stock again.

Change-of-mind returns and category rules

1 min

  • Returns are accepted in three categories:

    • Change of mind (the customer simply does not want the item).
    • Damaged in shipping.
    • Faulty or under warranty. If a return claim fits none of these, inform the person responsible for returns before deciding.

  • A change-of-mind return is only possible when all of these apply:

    • The claim is made within 7 days of delivery.
    • The customer pays and arranges the return shipping.
    • The return is for store credit only (no refunds are offered).
    • The goods are in their original packaging. Requests outside these conditions go to the person responsible.

  • A change-of-mind claim must be submitted within 7 days of the order being delivered.

  • Processing an approved change-of-mind return:

    1. Give the customer the return shipping address once the claim is approved. That address is the original supplier.
    2. Notify the supplier that a return is coming.
    3. Do not let the customer use or receive the store credit until the supplier confirms the goods arrived in good order (in good condition).
    4. After the supplier confirms safe delivery, process any re-order from the customer in the normal way.

  • Store credit (a balance the customer can spend only in your store) from a change-of-mind return cannot be approved or used until the supplier has confirmed that the returned goods were received in good order.

Supplier return workflows (RMA)

3 min
  • A return needs an RMA number (return merchandise authorisation: a return code). The customer requests it from the store before sending the item back. Questions about a product's return policy are answered within 24 hours. Refunds go to the original credit card used for the order. **
  • Benchmark (a target to measure yourself against): a competitor's return process typically issues a return authorisation within 2 business days. Where the item qualifies for a free return, it also sends reply-paid postage details (postage the customer does not pay for). Record this response time as a benchmark for your own process. **

Standard supplier flow, version A (procedure with a 30-day label window):

  1. The customer emails the store requesting a return.
  2. Contact the supplier with the order number and the return request.
  3. The supplier issues a return shipping label, usually within a return window (the number of days allowed to send back), such as 30 days, stated in the supplier agreement.
  4. Attach the label to the package. The carrier picks it up and returns it to the supplier warehouse.
  5. Refund the customer or exchange the product after the supplier receives the item. **

Supplier flow, version B (customer pays return shipping by default):

  1. The customer starts a return request with your company.
  2. Contact the supplier with the customer order number and return reason.
  3. Request a return shipping label from the supplier by email.
  4. Provide the return shipping label to the customer.
  5. The customer ships the product back to the supplier. The customer typically pays return shipping, unless the supplier policy specifies otherwise.
  6. The supplier refunds your company.
  7. You refund the customer or offer store credit, based on customer preference. (2023)

Supplier flow, version C (supplier label, refund after receipt):

  1. The customer emails the store with a return request at the address listed on the returns policy page.
  2. The store acknowledges receipt of the request.
  3. The store asks the supplier for a return shipping label for that order number.
  4. The supplier sends the label, and the store emails it to the customer.
  5. The customer attaches the label. The package is picked up and shipped back to the supplier.
  6. The supplier refunds the store, and the store then refunds the customer. (2025)

Supplier flow, version D (drop-shipped item, broken; drop-shipped means the supplier ships straight to the customer):

  1. The customer emails the store to report a broken item and asks for a refund or return.
  2. The store emails the supplier and asks for a return shipping label.
  3. The supplier sends the return label to the store.
  4. The store sends the label to the customer, who ships the item back.
  5. The item returns to the supplier's warehouse.
  6. The supplier refunds the store, and the store refunds the customer. **

Supplier flow, version E (customer requests, brand label, direct to warehouse):

  1. The customer requests a return.
  2. Contact the brand and request a return shipping label.
  3. The brand sends the label to the customer.
  4. The customer ships the item to the supplier's warehouse.
  5. The customer is refunded. **

Standard supplier flow, version F (item goes directly to warehouse):

  1. The customer asks for a return.
  2. The retailer contacts the supplier with the order number.
  3. The supplier sends a return shipping label to the retailer.
  4. The retailer sends the label to the customer.
  5. The customer ships the item back. It goes directly to the supplier's warehouse.
  6. The supplier credits the retailer, and the retailer refunds the customer. (2023)

Generic flow (summary of the seven-step pattern):

  1. The customer requests a return.
  2. Notify the supplier.
  3. The supplier sends a return shipping label.
  4. The customer ships the item back.
  5. You refund the customer, and the supplier refunds you. **
  • Supplier return request email (use for each return; replace the bracketed fields):
Subject: Return authorization request, order [order number]

Hi [supplier rep name],

A customer from order [order number] would like to return [product name]. Could you please issue a return authorization number for this return and send the return shipping label directly to the customer?

Thank you,
[your name]
[store name]

**

  • For suppliers with different return policies, keep one catch-all (general) shipping and returns template for suppliers that share a policy. Create a separate template for a supplier with its own policy, and show it in the shipping and returns section of that supplier's product pages. **

Supplier-side problems: proof, reshipping and missing labels

1 min

Handling refunds and returns with a supplier (procedure):

  1. Collect proof that something went wrong, such as the product is broken, incorrect or not delivered.
  2. Ask the supplier for a refund or a free reship (a replacement sent at no charge) when proof exists. Good suppliers will do this.
  3. If the product is returned, confirm the return address and contact details before reshipping.
  4. Reship costs are lower than shipping a new parcel from China, so reshipping is often the better option.
  5. Contact the supplier at the same time as you answer the customer, without making a definite promise to the customer yet.
  6. Ask the supplier once for their refund policy, and publish a link on your store so customers can see the terms.
  • When a supplier cannot deliver a return label, or the logistics (the delivery system) are not working, use this wording. Present it as a planned process, not as a free giveaway, so customers do not learn that they can skip returns, and word does not spread through the same postcode:
Sure, absolutely, we will refund you now. At the moment our logistics are not working for us, so we will refund you in this instance and send your return label once the process is working again.
  • If a customer says they have no return label and asks what to do with the item, suggest they gift it to someone who could benefit from it, or donate it to a local charity or shelter. Customers usually respond well to this. It turns the refund into a positive story for the brand.

Refund timing and confirmation

1 min

  • Refunds are confirmed to the customer by email. The money is credited to the original credit card within 5 to 7 business days of the refund being processed. Tell the customer this timeframe in every refund reply.

  • Timing conflict (two sources disagree): the Source C-sourced material uses 5 to 7 business days for card refunds. The brands in the Source D 2026 examples promised 5 to 10 business days, depending on the bank's processing time.

    • Use 5 to 7 business days as the standard in replies when your card processor (the company that handles card payments) and bank data support it.
    • Otherwise, use the 5 to 10 business day window.
    • Put the same refund window in your refund policy and in replies.

  • Refund-timing wording used by the Source C examples: "5-7 business days, depending on your bank or card issuer." (The card issuer is the bank that issued the customer's card.)

  • Refund process after a supplier return: the supplier refunds the store, and the store then refunds the customer. Refund the customer only once the supplier has received the item or confirmed the goods.

  • Structure for a reply to a delivered order with unhappy product quality (structure only; write the text in your own words):

    • Thank them, and say you are happy to help.
    • Apologise that the product did not meet expectations.
    • Say the store aims for a great experience but sometimes falls short.
    • State that the store stands by its refund policy and wants to make it right.
    • Confirm a full refund has been processed, and that an email confirmation will arrive shortly.
    • Say the charge should return to the credit card within 5-7 business days, depending on the bank.
    • Tell the customer they may keep the product.
    • Invite further questions.
    • Sign off as the store customer support representative.

  • Structure for a reply to a delivered order with unhappy shipping time (same structure as above, with the complaint about shipping time):

    • Thank them, and say you are happy to help.
    • Apologise that the shipping time was not satisfactory.
    • Say the store aims for a great experience but sometimes falls short.
    • State that the store stands by its refund policy and wants to make it right.
    • Confirm a full refund has been processed, and that an email confirmation will arrive shortly.
    • Say the charge should return to the credit card within 5-7 business days, depending on the bank.
    • Tell the customer they may keep the product.
    • Invite further questions.
    • Sign off as the store customer support representative.

  • If an order has been delivered and the customer is unhappy with it, you can issue a full refund and let the customer keep the product. No return is required. Tell the customer the refund has been processed, and that the card credit can take 5 to 7 business days.

Partial refunds and keep-the-item deals

2 min

Partial refunds in Shopify (Shopify is the online store software; procedure; older: 2019):

  1. Go to the order details in Shopify.
  2. Locate the refund section.
  3. Click "Partial refund" (not "Full refund").
  4. Enter the refund amount (for example, $100 off the full product price).
  5. Confirm the refund.

Do not accidentally select the full refund option.

  • When a customer asks for a return, offer a deal to keep the item first, since that is easier than sending it back. **

  • For a refund request without a defect, charge the customer for return shipping. Alternatively, offer a partial refund (a refund of only part of the price) of around 30 to 50 percent so the customer keeps the item.

  • When negotiating a refund where the customer keeps the item, aim for break-even (the point where the seller does not lose money on the order). A 50 percent refund was used in practice.

  • Return shipping for a US customer typically costs about 15 to 20 USD. That often exceeds the value of a cheap product. Quote this real cost to the customer when explaining why a return is not worth it.

  • Handling a return request when return shipping is high:

    1. Apologise and explain that shipping the item back to the US is expensive, quoting a realistic rate of about 15 to 20 USD.
    2. If the mistake was yours, send a free replacement.
    3. If the customer is not at fault, offer a replacement in which the customer pays only shipping, for example about 7 to 8 USD, while you cover the product cost.
    4. If the customer insists on a refund and does not want a replacement, offer a partial refund that is lower than the sale price by the return shipping cost. Let the customer keep the product.

  • Example: for a product sold at 50 USD where return shipping would cost the customer 20 USD, offer a partial refund of 30 USD and let the customer keep the item. This avoids the trip to the post office and the wait for the return. The seller stated this may not be the best method, and customers accepted it with mixed reactions.

  • For a low-cost product where return shipping costs 15 to 20 USD, a replacement with the customer paying about 7 to 8 USD for shipping was accepted by most customers. Partial refunds drew more complaints. Lead with the paid-shipping replacement option.

  • When you handle return and replacement emails yourself, read the customer's first message to judge whether they will accept paying for shipping. Offer a paid-shipping replacement to those who seem willing. Offer a free replacement or a refund to those who do not.

  • Landed cost (the total cost to get the item to the customer) of a cheap item shipped to the US: supplier price (about 2 to 3 USD) + shipping to the US (about 5 USD) = about 7 to 8 USD. If the customer pays about 7 to 8 USD for shipping on a free replacement, the seller's cost is covered.

Store credit and exchange-first returns

1 min
  • When customers request refunds, offer store credit instead. Store credit keeps revenue on your books. It keeps the customer within your own range of products and brands. It lets you fulfil (deliver) their purchase need with an alternative product or brand you control, maintaining the sale.
  • Return credit options in Loop (a return app for online stores):
    • Store credit delivers a gift card code by email once the return is set up.
    • A refund to the original payment method is issued minus applicable fees (charges that apply).
    • Refund timing varies.
  • Choose the model that fits the return type:
    • Change-of-mind returns under the 7-day rule are store credit only.
    • Loop's exchange-first model (offering an exchange before a refund) offers refund or exchange, with bonus credit on refunds.

Exchange-first logic in Loop (Checkout Plus)

1 min

Customer return flow with Checkout Plus (a Loop feature for free returns):

  1. The customer enters the order number and zip code (postcode).
  2. Loop detects that the customer is covered for a free return.
  3. The customer selects the return reason and the item to return.
  4. The summary page removes the handling fee (a charge for processing the return) for a customer who opted in.
  5. The customer submits the return and chooses store credit or a refund to the original payment method.
  6. The customer buys a label from the preferred carrier. The label is billed to the brand's FedEx or USPS account.
  7. The customer attaches the label and ships the item back.

Exchange-first return logic in Loop:

  1. The shopper selects the item to return.
  2. The shopper gives a reason, such as too big, too small or did not like it.
  3. The shopper chooses refund or exchange.
  4. If the shopper wants a refund, Loop offers bonus credit to shop again now.
  • A bonus credit of about $5 or $10 for shopping again, added on top of the refund value, can turn a refund into an exchange. The credit works across the full catalogue (product list).
  • Shoppers who want an item in a different size are more likely to exchange than to refund. This is why the return reason question comes before the refund-or-exchange choice.
  • Shoppers who pay for free returns in Loop's model are not charged the handling fee at return. Those who did not opt in pay the handling fee on return.

Cross-border returns

1 min
  • Returns from a customer in another country are usually sent to a local return centre, and the customer pays return shipping. This applies in the UK, the Netherlands and elsewhere.
  • Global return solutions are lacking in dropshipping. Returning a product to a facility where it can be handled matters most for fashion, because fashion return ratios (the share of orders sent back) are higher.
  • Returns from overseas dropshipping suppliers are very hard to process. Some stores absorb the cost of a return instead of chasing the supplier (following up until the problem is solved). This is especially true when the return is requested after the order has already shipped.
  • Refund process for a customer who requests a refund:
    1. Check whether the customer is in the same country as the store's target market (the country you sell to).
    2. If yes, ask the customer to send the product back so it can be resold.
    3. If no, which is the usual case, tell the customer to keep the product.
    4. Refund the customer if the reason for the request is valid.

Refund abuse and requests outside policy

1 min

  • Refund abuse (asking for money back in bad faith) is rare. Most customers do not do it.

    • The few who do are a small group.
    • Generous service often pays off in brand goodwill (good feelings toward your brand that keep people coming back).
    • Design the service policy around the honest majority.
    • Use stricter rules for the few abusers only when they become a real cost.

  • Refund abuse without returning the item is mainly a risk for low-priced items, such as those under about $50. For expensive products it was not seen in practice. (2023)

  • Responding to a return request outside the policy (a request the rules do not cover):

    1. Send a polite first email that explains the policy.
    2. Include a screenshot of the return policy page and a link to it.
    3. If the customer stays unreasonable, consider accepting the return.

    **

  • Customers ask for returns of unopened product (sealed, never used) during a recall (the maker pulls products back from sale, often for safety or quality reasons). They get frustrated when refused.

    • Define a clear recall and unopened-product return process before it is needed.

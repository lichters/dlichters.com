Order status, tracking and delivery inquiries

Section 17.6 · 6 of 22 in this chapter · 18 min read

In short

  • Check the order status before you answer any tracking message.
  • Processing (preparing the order) and transit (the trip to the customer) are two different times. Keep them apart.
  • Use a ready reply for each case. Pick the one that matches the order's status.
  • Offer a free reship (a new parcel at no cost) first. Offer a full refund when the customer asks for one or when the parcel is lost.
  • Promise a delivery time you can really keep, because late parcels bring complaints and refund demands.
More key points
  • Tell customers where their order is before they ask, because shipping from suppliers often takes 5 to 14 days.
  • Hide the shipping origin on tracking and emails, so customers do not feel tricked later.

Why tracking and order status matter

1 min
  • An order tracking system (a way to follow where a parcel is) stops orders from disappearing.
  • Orders can disappear because of supplier delays (the company that ships the product is slow), emails lost in the inbox, or mistakes by staff.
  • The risk grows as order volume (the number of orders) grows.
  • The risk is highest with new suppliers whose reliability (how dependable they are) is not yet proven.
  • Tracking questions are the most frequent support request.
  • Prepare standard replies for four common cases: order not shipped yet, order shipped, shipped but not received, and shipped with an angry customer.
  • Most customer messages are about legitimacy (whether the store is real), order status, and stock (whether items are available).
  • Detailed product pages, an FAQ (a page of common questions and answers) and automated tracking emails remove most of these questions.
  • An order tracking tool (an app that follows parcels) can send tracking information to customers automatically after shipment.
  • This reduces "where is my order" contacts (messages sent to support).
  • Automatic shipment updates sent through a tracking app reduce customer inquiries about order status.

Order status check before every reply

1 min
  1. Look up the order status in the store admin (the back office where the store is managed) and the fulfillment system (the system that packs and ships orders) before replying to any tracking question.
  2. If the order is not shipped yet, send the not-shipped reply. It says the order is being processed (being prepared to leave), a tracking email follows once it ships, and orders take about 10-20 days after shipping.
  3. If the order is shipped and the customer asks about tracking, send the shipped reply. Include the shipping date, the 3-5 business day delay for tracking (a business day is a working day, Monday to Friday), the tracking link (a web address that shows the parcel's status), and a screenshot (a picture) of the current status.
  4. If the order is shipped and the customer says they got no confirmation emails, send the junk/spam version (the version for this case) of the shipped reply. Ask them to check junk and spam first.
  5. If the order is shipped but the customer is angry, send the angry reply with the current status. Take responsibility for delivery and promise a free reship or a full refund if the parcel is lost in transit (on the way).
  6. If the parcel is delivered and the customer wants a refund, offer a free reship first. Skip this only if they ask for a refund on first contact. In that case, process the refund without pushing a reship.
  7. If the tracking cannot be found at all and the parcel appears lost, send the lost-order refund reply. Confirm the refund is already started.

Delivery times: processing and transit

Two words to know:

  • Calendar days count every day, weekends included.
  • Business days count only working days.

  • Use one basis for transit time in every template, preferably business days, so customers do not get conflicting estimates.
  • Present delivery timelines as estimates (not promises).
  • Keep supplier processing time separate from carrier transit (the time the delivery company takes).
  • Do not promise a deadline that cannot be met. When processing takes about 3 days before a shipment from Illinois to Philadelphia, a 5-business-day deadline cannot be guaranteed. Say that upfront.
  • Set expectations with the customer before they become frustrated.
  • Give customers a rough delivery window once a sale is made, such as one to two weeks. Base it on where the parcel is in the shipping process, for example after customs (the border check) and on its way. Update it when possible.

Confirmation emails and spam checks

1 min
  • Confirmation emails for order, shipping and delivery can land in the junk or spam folder.
  • When a customer says they received nothing, ask them to check those folders first.
  • Automated emails sent by a store or its platform can land in the spam folder.
  • Before reporting a missing confirmation or account email, search both the main inbox and the spam folder for the sender's name. (2025)
  • If support emails seem missing, check the spam folder first. Replies may land there.
  • If you are in the mentor program, also post in the member Slack channel (a group chat room) to get attention faster.

Guarantees and refund policy

1 min
  • State a guarantee (a promise) covering every package to customers.
  • If a parcel is lost in transit, the customer may choose a full refund or a free reship.
  • The store can add a store credit (money to spend in the store) on top of the reship.
  • The default refund policy (the standard rule) is that a customer can get either a free reship or a full refund.
  • The preferred first offer is a free reship.
  • If the customer asks for a refund on first contact, give the refund without pushing a reship.

Lost parcels and refunds

1 min
  • For lost shipments, send the tracking information that shows the item was lost.
  • Then keep the customer updated on replacements, when they ship and when they arrive.
  • Most customers only panic when they are not kept informed.
  • If the fulfillment platform can no longer track an order and the package is considered lost, issue a full refund.
  • Tell the customer the refund has already been initiated (started).
  • Do not wait for the customer to ask for a replacement before refunding.

Late delivery and delay responses

1 min
  • When a customer complains about a late delivery, first find the cause before choosing a remedy (a fix).
  • Possible causes are outdated product page delivery times, slow order submission, or a supplier that took longer than usual.
  • Each cause has a different fix.
  • If a supplier never ships a paid order and the customer becomes upset, a partial refund such as 5% combined with a firm delivery date can settle the situation.
  • The exact remedy depends on what went wrong.
  • For delivery questions, promise only what the supplier and carrier can deliver.
  • Contact the store team when a customer needs a quote instead of guessing a price.

Delivery promises and Christmas

1 min
  • Setting delivery times that are too short is over-promising. (Over-promising means promising more than you can deliver.)
  • Short delivery times may raise conversion. (Conversion means the share of visitors who buy.)
  • But customers who get their parcel later than promised lead to complaints and chargebacks. (A chargeback is when a customer asks their bank to take the money back.)
  • Set the estimate to what the fulfillment center can reliably meet. (A fulfillment center is the warehouse that packs and ships your orders.)
  • Improve the estimate as your setup matures.

Origin and trust after purchase

1 min
  • Some customers do not know their parcel ships from China. (Origin means where the parcel comes from.)
  • If they find out later, they become more anxious.
  • So hide the origin on tracking and emails.
  • This protects trust after the purchase. (Trust means the customer feels safe buying from you.)

Tracking from day one

1 min
  • For dropshipping with physical products, shipping can take 5 to 14 days. (Dropshipping means your supplier ships the product straight to the customer. Physical products are goods you can hold, not digital files.)
  • So customers will often ask where their order is.
  • Tracking is the code and page that show where the parcel is.
  • Plan customer service for these tracking questions from the start. (Customer service means your team that answers customer questions.)

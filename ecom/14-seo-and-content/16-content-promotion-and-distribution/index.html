Content promotion and distribution

Section 14.16 · 16 of 20 in this chapter · 18 min read

In short

  • Plan how to promote each article before you write it. Keep promoting it for at least two weeks, up to 30 days.
  • Pick only three to four promotion methods per article. Start with the easy ones.
  • On launch day, post the article on social media and email it to your list.
  • Promotion speeds up Google ranking, but it does not replace it. Articles that are only published are rarely seen.
  • Use a scheduling tool to queue your social posts ahead of time, so you don't have to remember each one.
More key points
  • One article can become several social posts. Use 5 to 10 short snippets from it.
  • Reuse one image from a blog post in emails, Pinterest and other sites.
  • Creator content can go on your website, in your emails, on social media and in ads.
  • Post each video on every platform where it fits. Results can differ a lot from one platform to another.

Promotion Planning

2 min
  • Set the promotion plan when the title is planned, not after publishing.
  • Typical channels (places to share) are social media, a YouTube channel, a Facebook page, backlink outreach (asking other websites to link to you), search text ads (written Google ads), Google Display Network ads (banner ads on partner websites), and email to your list.
  • Publishing content is only the first half of content marketing.
  • Without active promotion, new pages rarely get seen.
  • Search engines and algorithms (automatic ranking rules) look for signals of quality and relevance.
  • Those signals have to be created by promoting the content on purpose.
  • Early traffic (the first visitors) gets the snowball rolling, so that organic discovery (people finding it on their own) can follow later.
  • Promote every content piece for at least 2 weeks, ideally up to 30 days, before moving on.
  • Each piece needs active promotion for a minimum of two weeks, and for up to 30 days after publishing.
  • The promotion strategy should be planned during content research, before the piece is written.
  • Content promotion pursues five goals:
    • generating initial traffic (visitors) and sales,
    • stimulating social sharing,
    • earning backlinks,
    • building an audience,
    • filling retargeting audiences (people who already visited, shown ads again) and the top of the funnel (the first step, where strangers learn about you).
  • For each piece, plan three to four promotion methods from the available list, not all of them.
  • Choose based on the content type and resources, and start with the ones that are easy to carry out.
  • Content must be actively promoted.
  • Promotion helps real people discover it.
  • It also signals to search engines that the content is important enough to rank.
  • Promotion is optional, but it speeds up the organic ranking process (free ranking in Google results) that would happen anyway.
  • Focus on the few methods with the highest effect, rather than trying every channel.
  • The long-term goal is organic Google ranking. New content can rank from the start.
  • Each share and visit adds to the ranking signals over time.
  • So promotion mainly speeds up a process that keeps going without it.

The Twelve Promotion Methods

1 min

Use these twelve methods, in the order presented, when choosing the three to four methods for a piece.

  1. Outreach to other websites for sharing or linking (link building: getting other sites to link to you).
  2. Organic posting to social media (Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest, YouTube). Organic means free, without paid ads.
  3. Paid ads on Facebook, Google Display Network, Google Search and YouTube.
  4. Email to the existing audience (your current subscribers).
  5. Sharing in forums, Facebook groups and online communities in the niche (a small, focused market).
  6. Linking to new content from older content that already gets traffic.
  7. Promotion on content networks (Taboola, Outbrain, Quuu Promote), which show your content on other sites.
  8. Expert roundup posts (articles that collect answers from several experts).
  9. Producing a video and posting it to YouTube.
  10. Converting the content into an infographic (a picture that shows facts) and sharing it.
  11. Submitting the content to online content communities (sites where people post and vote on content).
  12. Outreach to people or sites that were featured in a best-of or list-style article.

Launch Sequence for New Content

1 min
  • Launch each article by:
    1. Posting it to your business social media channels.
    2. Emailing your existing subscriber base (everyone on your email list) with a link.
  • This speeds up organic traction (early momentum) beyond waiting for search engine rankings to build naturally.
  • Promotion means actively driving readers to newly published pages and blog posts from day one, without paid traffic.
  • The aim is to send Google early engagement signals (signs that people interact with the page) that the content is being seen.
  • That raises the chance of a higher organic ranking.
  • A large number of visitors on a new page on launch day tells Google the page is being checked out.
  • If visitors stay on the page for a while, this is a further positive signal of authority (trust and expertise).

Social Sharing

1 min
  • A single organic social post reaches only a small share of followers.
  • On Facebook it is commonly estimated that fewer than 10% of followers see an organic post.
  • Posting once and considering the job done wastes most of the reach potential.
  • To maximize one piece of content on social media:
    1. Over about one month (two weeks as a minimum), post 5 to 10 times about the same content.
    2. Split the content into 5 to 10 small snippets (short pieces) of interesting facts, questions and comments related to it.
    3. Post each snippet as its own social post with a link to the full content piece.
    4. Use a different image on each post, such as images from the article.
    5. Make each post's copy give a reason to click by telling readers what they will learn.
    6. Schedule all posts in advance with a tool such as Buffer or Hootsuite (tools that publish posts at set times for you).
  • A handful of recent social media posts, about five, is enough to show activity.
  • What counts is a page with recent content, not the number of posts. (2024)
  • Free traffic can come from answering questions in forums and YouTube comments and linking back to your site.
  • It takes more manual effort (hand work) but costs nothing. That makes it good for testing an offer before spending on ads.

1 min

Posting plan for a brand-led content strategy (a plan where your brand leads the content):

Platform Early phase Target
Facebook Every Monday and Wednesday Daily by early next year
Instagram Posts on the same early schedule Posts every day
Twitter Same early schedule Daily
Pinterest Once a week Once a week
Blog (WordPress) Once a week Once a week
  • Friday is used for Q&A sessions (live question-and-answer sessions) with the audience.
  • YouTube and the blog host unique content, and social posts link to them.

Automation with Zapier

1 min
  • Zapier is a tool that connects apps and runs tasks automatically.
  • Use it to publish new blog posts to Facebook and LinkedIn as soon as they are created.
  • The goals are to maximize reach, keep an active social presence, and eliminate manual posting.
  • Automate blog post sharing only to Facebook and LinkedIn through Zapier.
  • Avoid automating to Twitter, Instagram, or Pinterest.
  • These platforms need custom image sizes and text formatting that native scheduling (the app's built-in scheduler) doesn't handle.
  • Without that, posts look bot-generated.
  • New blog posts published on Shopify or WordPress (website platforms) trigger Zapier to post immediately to Facebook.
  • Then it repeats at 7-day, 30-day, and 60-day intervals, using Zapier delays (set waiting times).
  • This extends content lifespan (how long it stays useful) across different audience segments (groups of people).
  • When new podcast or audio episodes publish to an RSS feed (a feed that lists new episodes so apps can read them), Zapier automatically creates an unpublished draft blog post (a saved post that is not public yet).
  • It also sends a Slack notification (an alert in the team chat app Slack).
  • This provides a content starting point without manual transcription uploads (uploading a written copy by hand).
  • Example: The Ecommerce Lifestyle Podcast publishes at least one episode per week.
  • When the podcast owner set up Zapier automation to post new episodes to LinkedIn, their personal Facebook page, and a business Facebook page, the initial setup took about 10 minutes.
  • Over the next 20+ episodes, every new podcast posted automatically, without any manual team effort.
  • Where sources differ: The Zapier rule above limits automation to Facebook and LinkedIn.
  • The older scheduling method (2021-22) queues a different image and copy for each snippet post in Buffer or Hootsuite, including on Instagram and Pinterest.
  • Use the scheduling method when each post is written and imaged separately.
  • Use Zapier only for the Facebook and LinkedIn posts.

Email to Your List

1 min
  • Five channels for promoting published content start with your subscriber list: email your subscriber list and ask them to share.
  • Email your existing subscriber base with a link when each article launches.
  • Email to the existing audience is one of the twelve methods.

Contributor and Expert Outreach

1 min
  • Five channels for promoting published content also include contacting people who contributed to the content and asking them to share it.
  • When a post with contributor input goes live:
    1. Email every contributor on the same day.
    2. Thank them, include the link and say you would appreciate it if they shared the post.
    3. Ask them to share it through whatever channels they use, such as a blog, Facebook page or group, or their website. Keep the request casual and do not make it sound mandatory.
  • For expert roundups or quote-based articles, ask the top three contributors to share the post.
  • Their audiences are the most relevant.
  • Their shares carry more weight when the post is published on your site.
  • Process for an expert roundup post:
    1. Use the attention matrix (a research grid for finding your audience's interests) to identify industry or niche experts.
    2. Email them asking for a quote or answer relevant to the content topic (for example, one tip on a specific question).
    3. Include each answer in the content with a link to the expert's website or social profiles.
    4. After publishing, email every contributor with the link and ask them to share it with their list or followers and/or link to it from their website.
  • Five channels for promoting published content also include emailing suppliers or companies mentioned in the content and asking them to share it.
  • Five channels for promoting published content also include contacting relevant influencers (people with large followings) and asking them to share.
  • Each group has access to audiences or relationships that amplify (boost) your content.

Outreach to Publications and Newsletters

1 min
  • Five channels for promoting published content also include reaching out to industry publications and asking them to feature it.
  • When an industry publication or newsletter (a regular email to subscribers) sends a roundup of top posts, email its editor when you publish a strong post.
  • Ask them to include your link in the next roundup, for example in a weekly Monday newsletter.
  • Publications and newsletter curators (the people who pick the content) rarely share content on their own, so proactive outreach (you reaching out first) is required.
  • Without a direct request, your post is unlikely to appear in their roundups.
  • Outreach email template for an industry publication or newsletter curator that sends a regular roundup of top posts:
Subject: Link for next week's roundup

Hi [Editor name],

I read your weekly roundup of the best posts in [Industry or niche]. We just published [Article title]: [URL]

Would you consider including a link to it in next week's newsletter? I am happy to send any details you need.

Best,
[Your name]
  • Outreach to people or sites that were featured in a best-of or list-style article is one of the twelve methods.

Press Releases and Guest Posts

1 min
  • Press releases (official news notices sent to the media) drive traffic and ranking lift (a boost in Google ranking).
  • One operator executed 10 per week with success and planned to scale to 15 per week.
  • Each release was rewritten with a different angle but the same core message.
  • This is an ongoing strategy with measurable impact on product visibility.
  • Press releases are not useful for a new store seeking brand recognition at launch.
  • Guest posts (articles you write for another site) or mentions on industry blogs and authority sites (trusted, well-known sites) within the niche, not direct competitors, work better.
  • Where sources differ: The 10-to-15-per-week press release program is an ongoing strategy with measured results.
  • The launch-stage advice to skip press releases and use guest posts or industry mentions applies to a new store seeking brand recognition.
  • Use the first when the store is running the program and can measure visibility, and the second at launch.

Authority Site Placements

1 min
  • Paid placements on authority sites in your niche, such as blog banner ads or sponsored posts, can bring referral traffic (visitors who click a link on another site) and sometimes backlinks.
  • Google discourages paid links.
  • So choose relevant sites and treat the placement primarily as a traffic source.

Forums, Communities and Free Traffic

1 min
  • Content shared in forums, Facebook groups and niche communities should be genuinely useful, not mainly a sales pitch.
  • Most content is not built to close a sale.
  • Share it where the target market already gathers, and let the value of the piece earn the click.
  • Sharing in forums, Facebook groups and online communities in the niche, and submitting the content to online content communities, are two of the twelve methods.
  • Free traffic can come from answering questions in forums and YouTube comments and linking back to your site.

Infographics

1 min
  • Process for an infographic:
    1. Pick a blog post, ideally a how-to or listicle (a list-style article), which suits infographics well.
    2. Have it converted into an infographic, either by a freelancer on Fiverr (a site for hiring freelancers) or with a tool such as Piktochart (a design tool).
    3. Put the source URL (web address) or the original post URL on the infographic.
    4. Publish it as a standalone page (its own page) on the website or blog and share it on social media.
    5. Submit it to infographic directories such as visual.ly, Pinterest and the website.
    6. Include the URL in the description or comments of every place it is posted.
  • Infographics made from a blog post can be embedded (placed inside a page) on the store's site.
  • They can also be posted on social media as extra content, sent in email, posted on Pinterest, or offered to other websites in exchange for a credit link back to the store.
  • Converting the content into an infographic and sharing it is one of the twelve methods.

Pinterest

1 min
  • Pinterest (a visual idea and search site) can bring steady organic traffic if your niche suits the platform.
  • Create a Pinterest account.
  • Make sure every product is pinnable (can be saved as a pin with a picture). (2024)
  • About 80% of Pinterest interactions are repins.
  • A repin means users reshare a pin from inside the platform, rather than a pin taken from a website.
  • Repins are the main reason content can spread from one follower to the next.
  • Create vertical images (taller than they are wide) for blog post content that you pin to Pinterest, not only for product images.
  • Blog pins that link back to the store can generate traffic in the same way product pins do.
  • Pinterest content should also be original, because shared unique content earns more sharing than republished material.
  • Pinterest organic growth approach:
    1. Post 15 to 20 posts at the start.
    2. Build a very specific niche audience, such as working moms, work-from-home entrepreneurs or nail enthusiasts.
    3. Use the Pinterest trends section to find trending keywords and the hashtags attached to each trend.
    4. Post consistently, about 5 to 10 pins per day.
    5. Aim for a target of 100,000 monthly viewers before expecting steady organic traffic.
  • Where sources differ: The platform plan above sets Pinterest at once a week in the early phase and as the target.
  • The growth approach sets 5 to 10 pins per day after the first 15 to 20 posts.
  • Use the weekly cadence (how often you post) for a brand-led plan that also runs a blog and social channels.
  • Use the daily cadence when Pinterest is the main growth channel.

Hashtags

1 min
  • Hashtags are words after # that help people find posts.
  • Broad hashtags such as #explore, #fyp and #foryoupage do nothing for discovery.
  • Nearly everyone uses them, so do not rely on them.
  • Use only 3 to 5 niche-relevant hashtags per post.
  • Choose hashtags that describe your specific topic, rather than general reach tags.

Paid Promotion

1 min

Principles

1 min
  • Paid traffic can amplify other channels such as blog posts, YouTube videos, and social media content.
  • It does this by sending targeted paid views to content you already produced.
  • Unlike organic reach, you control who sees it.
  • You pay for results such as views only when they happen.
  • Informational searches (people looking for answers, not buying) that do not fit a product page, such as beginner-level questions, are better served by a content page and a search text ad than by shopping ads (product ads with a picture and price).
  • The goal is to move these visitors into the email list and retargeting audiences, from where they are later converted (turned into buyers).

Facebook

1 min
  • Paid Facebook promotion of content:
    1. Use Page Post Engagement or Traffic campaign types (ad goal settings), or boost existing posts about the content (pay to show a post to more people).
    2. Use the primary image from the blog post or a video, with less than 20% text in it.
    3. Write ad copy that creates curiosity and shows which problem the content solves.
    4. Target interests identified in customer avatars (made-up profiles of typical buyers), or lookalike audiences (new people who resemble your existing customers) built from the email or customer lists.
  • Facebook content promotion can start with a budget of about $20 to $30 to get people engaging with a post and into retargeting audiences.
  • Typical content ads on the Google Display Network and YouTube are also started on small budgets.
  • The test is run for a short time rather than for months.

Google Display Network

1 min
  • To promote content on the Google Display Network:
    1. Choose keywords that fit the topic of the content (the search phrase the content is optimized for), or audiences that resemble people who have already visited the site.
    2. Run static image ads (still pictures, not moving) in the recommended sizes of 300 x 250, 300 x 600, 728 x 90 and 320 x 100.
    3. Either design the images yourself in Snappa (a design tool) or hand the entire job to an outside provider.

YouTube

1 min
  • YouTube content promotion: target people who watched YouTube channels that appeal to the target audience, identified during attention matrix research, to build audiences around those channels.

Google Search Text Ads

1 min
  • Google Search text ads for content:
    1. Target informational searches linked to the products and the content, such as best charcoal grills 2019.
    2. Use only exact and/or phrase match keywords (settings that decide how closely a search must match your keyword).
    3. Match the ad headline as closely as possible to the headline of the content page.
    4. Use the ad copy to build curiosity and name the problem the content solves.
  • Well-structured content search ads can reach a click-through rate (CTR, the share of people who see an ad and click it) of 20% or more.
  • This is because most advertisers promote product or collection pages rather than content, so content ads stand out in the results.

Headline Pattern for Content Ads

1 min
  • Content ad headline formula: a headline that states the problem and a clear reason to read before buying, such as Don't buy [product] until you've read this guide, works because it reads differently from the sales-style ads around it.
    • Example 1: Don't book a surf trip until you've read this beginner guide.
    • Example 2: Don't choose a running shoe until you've read our fit guide for this year.

Content Networks

1 min
  • Taboola and Outbrain are content advertising networks (services that show your article as ads on other sites).
  • Their ads appear on large media sites such as Forbes, entrepreneur.com and the New York Post.
  • Outbrain is recommended as the starting point because it is simple to run.
  • To run it: apply, open an account, and create an ad consisting of an image and a headline.
  • Targeting is guided by customer avatars and interest targeting.
  • On content networks such as Outbrain, the headline is the key lever (the main thing that drives results).
  • It should state clearly what the reader will get from the content.
  • Testing can start with a small budget of tens of dollars.
  • Quuu Promote is a paid content-sharing service.
  • It pushes content to people who look for material to share on social media or to link to.
  • The campaign is set up by adding an RSS feed (a feed that lists your new content).
  • The service then promotes content automatically.
  • Its catalogue is split into market categories, with some niches covered better than others.
  • Promotion on content networks (Taboola, Outbrain, Quuu Promote) is one of the twelve methods.
  • Other social and native networks (ads that look like normal article content) where cold traffic (visitors who do not know you yet) can be developed include native ad networks like Outbrain and Taboola.
  • They also include both paid and unpaid social platforms relevant to the business.

Tools for Promotion

1 min
  • Buffer and Hootsuite are scheduling tools. A scheduling tool publishes your posts for you at a set time.
  • They queue social posts in advance. To queue means to line them up before they go live.
  • Your posting plan then does not depend on you remembering to publish each post.
  • Snappa is a tool for building ad images. An ad image is a picture used in a paid ad.
  • Fiverr is a website where you hire outside people. You can use it to outsource (hand off to someone outside your team) infographic creation and similar design work.
  • Piktochart is an infographic creation tool. An infographic is a picture that shows facts using charts and short text.

Repurposing Content

1 min
  • Linking to new content from older content is one of the twelve methods. The older content should already get traffic (visitors who reach the page).
  • For a single article, turn 5 to 10 snippets into separate social posts. A snippet is a short piece of text taken from the article.
  • Each social post gets its own image and a link to the full article.
  • Infographics, emails, Pinterest pins (image posts on Pinterest, a site for finding ideas) and outside sites (other websites) can all reuse one blog post's visual. A visual is an image or graphic.

Creator and Influencer Content Repurposing

1 min

A creator is a person who makes content online. An influencer is a person with a large following who promotes products. Repurposing means using the same content again in new places.

Repurposing channels for influencer content:

Channel Typical content
Website Testimonials (customer praise or reviews), images, videos that link to product pages
Email marketing Testimonials, images, videos
Organic social media (free reach, no paid ads) Reposted stories (shared again; stories are short posts that disappear after a while), Reels (short vertical videos), and carousel posts (posts with several images to swipe through)
Paid media (ads you pay for) The same content as organic social, with ad spend (money put into ads) behind it
Cross-promotion (both sides promote the same post) A joint post with the creator, shown on both the brand's and the creator's pages
In-store displays Only relevant for brick-and-mortar brands (businesses with a physical shop)
Audience submissions (fans send in their own content) Creators encourage followers to submit their own videos or try the product on camera

Publishing Across Platforms

1 min
  • Publish each video on all relevant platforms. A platform is an app or website where posts appear. Relevant means it fits your audience.
  • The same video can perform very differently on each platform. To perform means to get views and results.
  • A weak result on one platform does not mean the video will fail on another.

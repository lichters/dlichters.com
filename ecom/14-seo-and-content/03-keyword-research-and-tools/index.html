Keyword research and tools

Section 14.3 · 3 of 20 in this chapter · 17 min read

In short

  • Check the country first. Search numbers change from one country to another, so wrong country means wrong numbers.
  • Start with broad words, then find specific phrases. Buyers often type long, specific phrases, and those are easier to rank for.
  • Keep only words that fit your shop, with at least 100 searches a month. Remove words that do not match what you sell.
  • Use free tools first. Paid SEO tools such as Ahrefs are not needed at the start.
  • Check Google Trends for rising topics. Keyword Planner numbers can lag behind real trends.
More key points
  • Search Console shows which search words bring people to your store.
  • Choose search words that match what you actually sell.
  • The Pages tab shows which page appears for each search word.
  • Make a copy of the keyword sheet before you add anything to it.

Tool stack and when to use each tool

3 min
  • Google Keyword Planner is free with a Google Ads account. Google Ads is Google's paid advertising service.
  • Keyword Planner shows search volume (how many times a word is searched each month), competition level (how many advertisers bid on it) and bid estimates (what advertisers may pay per click).
  • Use Keyword Planner to find keywords with high search volume and low competition for content. Content means blog posts and articles.
  • Without a Google Ads account, Keyword Planner is not needed for SEO (search engine optimisation: making pages rank high in Google's free results).
  • Keyword Planner is most useful for SEO keyword research in a niche (a narrow market). Its data can serve as a fallback if your main research tool is not working.
  • Keyword Planner has a landing page option. Enter the URL of a competitor's or manufacturer's page. It returns keywords relevant to that page.
  • Export those results into a spreadsheet to build a master keyword list (one sheet holding all your keywords).
  • Keyword Planner numbers can lag behind Google Trends, so do not rely only on it for trending topics.
  • Keyword Finder allows 2-3 free keyword searches per day.
  • Paid Keyword Finder access provides keyword suggestions, search volume, competition metrics, and shows how competitors rank for those keywords.
  • Ubersuggest costs about $10 per month and is a cheap option for keyword research.
  • Google and YouTube autosuggest (the suggestions shown while you type) are free. They generate ideas from root terms (the broadest words about your product).
  • Google Ads search data from existing Google Shopping campaigns (Google's product-ad campaigns) is a usable source of keywords.
  • Freelancers on Fiverr (an online marketplace for hired workers) can be hired to pull keywords.
  • Answer the Public is a question-based keyword tool. Use it to find the questions buyers ask.
  • Alexa.com provides website traffic rankings (traffic means visitors to a site). It also shows organic traffic (free search visits) and paid traffic (visits from ads). It shows keyword performance, backlink analysis (links from other sites to a site) and competitor performance metrics.
  • Use Alexa.com to check micro-influencer websites (sites of small social media creators) before outreach (asking a site owner for a link or a partnership). It also shows competitor traffic.
  • SpyFoot.com, SimilarWeb.com and Alexa.com show where competitor traffic comes from. They also show which keywords competitors rank for and their backlink sources.
  • Free versions of these tools offer limited data. Paid versions provide detailed insights.
  • Ahrefs is the SEO research tool used for keyword and competitor research. It has a free trial.
  • Ahrefs is preferred over Keyword Planner for keyword research, because Keyword Planner has hidden more data over time.
  • Ahrefs Competing Domains (a list of sites that compete with yours) and Content Gap (keywords competitors rank for and you do not) show competitor keyword gaps.
  • Ahrefs is expensive and not needed in the early stage of a store. Invest in it once paid traffic is profitable enough to fund content work.
  • Paid suites such as Ahrefs, Moz and SEMrush are more accurate and show more data points. They are not necessary at the early stage.
  • Cheap tools such as Ubersuggest and Google Keyword Planner are sufficient for content keyword research.

Market settings and the data you need

1 min
  • Set the country in every keyword tool to the market where your customers are. For example, use English / United States for a US store, or Australia for an Australian store. Do this before pulling any data.
  • Search volume and difficulty differ by country. Data from the wrong country gives wrong numbers.
  • At the content research stage, you need only two data points per keyword. They are search volume in the country your customers search from, and search difficulty.
  • Search difficulty (how hard it is to reach the top of Google for a word) is usually scored from 0 to 100 in tools such as Ubersuggest and Ahrefs.
  • A higher score means harder to rank.
  • Paid difficulty and average CPC (cost per click, what advertisers pay for one click) are also reported in tools like Ubersuggest. They are useful for paid ads.
  • Extra metrics and backlink-level data are needed only later, when a specific page will be promoted with a link building campaign (getting other sites to link to yours).

Collecting keywords

2 min

Keyword research procedure

  1. Collect broadly all relevant keywords in your market. Cover your products, your niche and the interests you serve.
  2. Group the keywords into themes or topics.
  3. Allocate each keyword to a page type: content, collection page (a page listing several products) or product page (a page for one product). Map them to specific pages as you build content.
  4. Record everything in a living keyword research document. It should be easy to access and update. It is typically a spreadsheet.
  • Decide first which search term a content piece should rank for. This stops it from overlapping with product or collection pages.
  • Keyword research is needed for both content marketing and general SEO.

Starting from root terms

  • Start with root keywords. These are the broadest search terms relevant to your products.
  • Run each root term separately through the keyword tool.
  • Expand from three kinds of root terms. These are the product type (for example surfboards), the activity or use it serves (for example surfing), and each product line (for example wetsuits).
  • Activity and use terms bring entertaining and problem-solving content ideas. Product-type terms alone do not reveal these.
  • Run terms about the use or problem your product solves, such as a sport or activity, to get content ideas.
  • Run every relevant root through the same export process.

Ubersuggest collection workflow

  1. Enter a root keyword in Ubersuggest.
  2. Open Keyword Ideas.
  3. Export the list to CSV (a plain spreadsheet file).
  4. Repeat for every relevant root term and related root term. For a surf shop, this includes surfboards, surfing and wetsuits.
  5. Keep only the terms that match your business.

Master sheet procedure

  1. Paste all exported CSV files one after the other into one spreadsheet.
  2. Sort all rows by search volume, highest first.
  3. Remove terms with less than 100 searches per month.
  4. Remove terms you will never rank for. For example, remove used if you only sell new products.
  5. Move the remaining terms to the Content, Collection or Product Page tab, according to their intent (the goal behind the search).
  • Sort by search volume and judge each term by hand. Ignore terms with zero or very low volume.
  • Create separate tabs or categories for Content, Collection and Product Page. Move each term into the matching one.
  • Exclude terms irrelevant to your business. Examples are used products when you only sell new ones, or drawings when you sell no art.
  • Some terms do not fit your business. For example, drawing for a surfboard shop, or rentals if you do not rent. Ignore them, even if their volume and difficulty look attractive.

Grouping variations

  • Term variations with the same meaning should be treated as one theme. Examples are surfboards for sale and surfboards on sale, singular and plural, or word-order differences.
  • Combine their volumes for a realistic estimate. Ranking for one usually helps with the other. Treat them as one target, not two.
  • Action item for this stage: run the full research for your market and products. Export the raw data to a spreadsheet. Sort and sift it. Build a list of search terms to target with content, such as a video, a blog or another format. Keep the list updated as the program continues.

Finding related keywords on Google

1 min

Related searches procedure

  1. Search the focus keyword on google.com.
  2. Scroll to the bottom of the first page of results.
  3. Read the box labelled Searches related to for the most common variations.
  4. Skip variations that name a competitor, for example a marketplace site.
  5. Copy each relevant related search into the worksheet as a related keyword.
  6. Use the remaining relevant variations on the page. Examples are adjectives such as big, retro, hybrid or cheap. Skip any variation that another page already targets.
  7. Repeat with the next keyword ideas if needed.
  • Related searches are a free way to find keywords and variations.

Keyword groups

  • The main keyword of a group is the keyword with the highest search volume found in Keyword Planner.
  • Add related keywords and variations to that main keyword. Together they form a keyword group.
  • Brand names such as IKEA are not targeted. People searching a brand already know what they want, and the brand is not yours.
  • Variations with the prefix best can be used on collection pages.

Competitor research

3 min

Competitor check on Google

  1. Search the keyword on google.com.
  2. Ignore the sponsored ads (paid ads) at the top. Look only at the organic results (free results).
  3. Open the top results. Note the page type, such as a product page, a collection page or a blog.
  4. Note what each top page includes. Then your own page can offer more value and rank better.
  • Use competitor pages to see what already ranks. Then build a better page with more useful content around the same keywords. Do not copy other sites' content.
  • A competitor's top results show which marketplaces, large retailers and specialist stores compete for the term.

Competitor blog topics

  1. Search your niche keyword followed by the word blog on Google.
  2. Open the top two ranked blogs.
  3. Note the topics in their posts. Include buying tips, news sections and older posts.
  4. Add each relevant topic as a keyword in the worksheet.
  • A competitor blog that looks unmaintained, is not fully working and has not been updated for a long time is usually easier to outrank for its topics. Check its older posts and news section as well as the newest posts. These still show which topics are covered, and they make a list of ideas.

Ahrefs Content Gap procedure

  1. In Ahrefs, open the Competing Domains tool and enter your domain. Look at the list sorted by common keywords. Pick a competitor that is closely related to your store.
  2. Open the Content Gap tool. Set the competitor as the target that ranks for keywords. Set your own site as the one that should not rank for them. This shows keywords the competitor ranks for and you do not.
  3. Enable the checkbox At least one of the targets should rank in the top 10. This shows only keywords the competitor ranks in the top 10 for.
  4. Run the search and export the keyword list.
  5. Vet each keyword with the RAW method (see Vetting keywords).
  • The Content Gap method gives a long list of article ideas that audiences already search for. The keywords come from competitor rankings. Vet every keyword before writing an article for it.
  • Keywords from competitor rankings are often irrelevant to your audience. Examples are brand names or product-specific terms of a competitor. Use judgement to remove keywords your buyers would not care about.

Ahrefs Keywords Explorer

  • Research starts in Keywords Explorer, Ahrefs' keyword research tool. Its keyword idea tabs are: All keyword ideas, Phrase match, Having same terms, Also rank for, Search suggestions, Newly discovered and Questions.
  • Filters include KD (keyword difficulty), Volume, Word count, SERP features (extra boxes on the results page), Include, Exclude and More filters.
  • The Questions tab is an Ahrefs feature. Other tools do not offer it in the same way.
  • The SERP overview (the list of top-ranking pages for a search) shows Backlinks, Domains and Traffic columns. Clicking a page's backlink count opens its backlink report. That report shows the linking sites to contact for outreach.
  • The Parent topic field shows the broader topic a keyword belongs to. It shows that topic's own search volume and the #1 ranking page for it. Use it to check whether a niche term is broad enough to attract links. Then compare it with narrower variations.

Vetting keywords

3 min

RAW vetting procedure

  1. Relevance: Does your audience care about this keyword? Remove keywords specific to a competitor, or keywords that do not match what your buyers want. Keep keywords that answer a real question buyers have about your category.
  2. Achievability: In Ahrefs, use Site Explorer (the Ahrefs tool that shows a site's keywords and links) to find the keywords your site already ranks for. Add filters, export, and paste the list into Keyword Explorer to see keyword difficulty. Use that difficulty as a benchmark for what you can rank for.
  3. Worth it: Keep keywords with a cost per click of at least $1 and a monthly search volume that matches your site size.

  • A keyword difficulty of about 45 was the example for surfboards for beginners. It is not easy, but not extremely hard to rank. Page one still requires backlinks. So it is a good candidate for an ultimate guide content page.
  • Very low difficulty with some volume, such as surfboard volume calculator, is a good target for a content page.
  • Work on low-competition keywords first. Pick ones with good search volume and a high CPC. They are the easiest to rank for. High-competition keywords are still worth content, but later.

Cost per click

  • Choose keywords with a cost per click of at least $1 in Ahrefs. A higher CPC means advertisers consider the keyword profitable. So the traffic may be worth more.
  • The $1 CPC rule is the worth-it check in the RAW method.
  • Average CPC is also available in Ubersuggest. It is useful for paid ads.

  • One thousand monthly searches of qualified traffic (visitors who are likely buyers) can yield significant sales over time.

Validating a category keyword

  1. Enter a candidate keyword, such as non-toxic running shorts, into Ahrefs or SEMrush.
  2. Check how many people search for it per month.
  3. Create the category if monthly searches exist. Skip it if they do not.
  4. Repeat the check monthly for hybrid keywords, since their volume can change.

Long-tail versus broad terms

1 min
  • Long-tail keywords (longer, more specific phrases) reportedly make up over 70% of online searches, according to a Moz figure.
  • Longer queries, such as a product with size, colour and price, show a later buying stage than short broad queries.
  • Broad, generic niche keywords name the whole niche. They bring a lot of traffic but few sales. Ranking for them takes a lot of time and money. So a dropshipping store (a shop that sells products without holding stock) should not aim for them first.
  • Long-tail keywords are the better organic target. They are easier to rank for, and their searchers have buying intent. Broad keywords attract people who are only curious.
  • Long-tail keywords include collection names (store subcategories), brand names the store sells, and combinations of product names, brand names and SKU numbers (a product's stock code).
  • A new store can start ranking sooner for search terms that name specific supplier brands or exact SKU numbers. Those searches are narrow and specific.
  • For a single-brand store, most search volume goes to the brand name. Product-name and niche keywords often have little volume. Long-tail questions and categories are where extra traffic can come from.
  • Term variations and word-order differences may carry the same intent. Group them rather than treating each as a separate target.

Search intent

1 min
  • Choose informational long-tail queries (searches to learn something). Examples are how-to questions, setup questions and questions about choosing a product. Each one can become an article. For a podcast gear store, examples are how to start a podcast, how to get a show listed on Apple Podcasts and which hosting service to choose.
  • Comparison keywords such as X versus Y, and definition keywords such as what does X mean, passed the relevance and value checks for a dropshipping store. These queries match buyers who are comparing options or learning category terms.
  • Keywords for brands are not targeted. Keywords with the prefix best can be used on collection pages.

Question-based research

1 min
  • Research the questions buyers ask before they buy. Use a question-based keyword tool such as Answer the Public.
  • Write content that answers each question. Cover each stage of the buying journey, including material, colour and feature variants.
  • The Questions tab in Ahrefs Keywords Explorer provides question keywords.
  • Informational questions are the content targets for how-to, setup and product-choice pages.

Year-stamped and trend keywords

1 min
  • Search queries that include a year usually lag the current year by about one year. People may still type the previous year's version of a term. Before writing a headline or ad for a year-stamped topic, check which year the query uses, and match it.

Google Trends procedure

  1. Go to trends.google.com.
  2. Enter the niche keyword and click Explore.
  3. Set the country to the country where you do business.
  4. Set the time filter to Past 90 days to see what is relevant now.
  5. Scroll down to see the trending searches. Note any breakout terms that are doing better than ever before.
  • Breakout terms show a rise before Keyword Planner volumes catch up. Keyword Planner numbers for these terms can lag. So content can pay off early, even when the planner shows low volume.
  • Do not rely only on Keyword Planner for trending topics. Missing a breakout term while waiting for planner numbers means arriving late to the topic.

Verifying a breakout term in Keyword Planner

  1. Copy the trending keyword from Google Trends.
  2. Open Keyword Planner and go back to keyword ideas.
  3. Search the copied keyword. Note its average monthly searches, competition rank and high-range top-of-page bid (the high end of what advertisers pay for the top ad spot).
  4. Record these values in the worksheet, even when search volume is low. Keyword Planner numbers lag behind Google Trends.

Search Console query data

1 min

Finding keywords to optimise

1 min
  1. Open Performance in Search Console. Search Console is Google's free tool that shows how your store appears in Google search. Performance is the section with the search numbers. Scroll down to the Queries report. The Queries report is the list of search phrases, called queries, that people typed.
  2. Sort queries by impressions to see which searches show your store most often. Impressions means how many times your store appeared in search results.
  3. Click a relevant query, for example a product name your store actually sells. This filters the graph to that query over the last three months. A filter shows only the data you choose. The graph is the chart of the numbers.
  4. Open the Pages tab to see which page ranked for that query. Ranked means the page showed up in the results for that search. Compare its impressions, average position (where it usually appears in the results, where 1 is the top) and clicks (how many people pressed it).
  • Search Console shows which queries get clicks. Google Analytics hides them as not provided. Google Analytics is Google's tool that counts visitors to your site. not provided is the label it shows instead of the search word.
  • Connect Search Console with the same Google account used for Analytics.
  • High-impression queries that do not match what the store sells bring impressions but almost no clicks. Examples are generic phrases about dropshipping (selling products you do not keep in stock yourself) or a category.
  • Optimise pages for queries that match the actual product, not for broad industry terms. Optimise means improving a page so it fits a search better.

Keyword worksheet

1 min
  • The keyword research worksheet exists as a Google Sheets file. Google Sheets is an online spreadsheet. A worksheet is a file for recording research in rows and columns.
  • You can copy the Google Sheets file into your own Google Drive. Google Drive is Google's online file storage.
  • The worksheet is also available as an Excel download, for Excel, Apple Numbers or other spreadsheet software. Spreadsheet software is any program for working with tables of numbers.
  • Make your own copy before adding keywords, so the original template stays unchanged. A template is a ready-made file you fill in.
  • Organic keyword research finds topics that people in your niche already search for on Google or Bing. Organic means results that are free, not paid ads. Your niche is your market or topic. Bing is another search engine.
  • You can create content that can rank for those terms. Content means the pages and text on your site. Ranking means showing up in search results for a term.
  • Keywords are stored in a tracking sheet, which is a spreadsheet where you record them. That sheet becomes the basis for content planning, meaning deciding what to write next.

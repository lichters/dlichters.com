Playbook › 14. SEO and content

Monitoring, reporting and ongoing maintenance

Section 14.19 · 19 of 20 in this chapter · 8 min read

In short

  • Check Google Search Console (Google's free site-health tool) once a week, or every two weeks on a Shopify store.
  • Fix the errors that stop Google from showing your pages first.
  • Judge your visitors by the right countries and search words before you change the store.
  • Expect smaller real gains from blog rewrites, not big multiples.

Search Console Review Cadence

2 min

Google Search Console is Google's free tool that shows how your site appears in Google search. Check it on a regular rhythm. Sources give different intervals. Each row keeps its own wording.

Source Interval Scope IDs
Source A approximately once per week General check for errors or warnings (problems Google reports), fixing issues that could affect rankings (where your pages appear in Google results), and monitoring which keywords drive free organic traffic (visitors who arrive from unpaid Google results)
Source A at least once a week Errors and indexing problems (pages Google cannot add to its search list), and tracking which keywords the store ranks for organically
Source A 2023 every couple of weeks Errors and issues that affect organic traffic
Source A every two weeks; more often for high-traffic stores, less often for low-traffic stores; better scheduled in a calendar Full routine for a Shopify store (Shopify is the platform that runs the online store) (see below)

The general rules say weekly. The 2023 rule and the Shopify routine say every couple of weeks (every two weeks). The Shopify routine is the only source that changes the interval based on traffic (how many visitors come).

Weekly check (general procedure)

  1. Check Google Search Console about once per week.
  2. Look for errors or warnings in your site's data.
  3. Fix any issues that could hurt your rankings.
  4. Watch which keywords (the search words people type) bring free organic traffic to your store.

Fortnightly Shopify check (every two weeks)

  1. Open Google Search Console > Sitemaps (the list of your pages that you send to Google) and make sure the sitemap is still submitted (sent to Google).
  2. Open the Review snippets report (the star and review details Google shows under results) and make sure all pages with reviews are valid and green.
  3. Open the Core Web Vitals / Page experience report (Google's speed and visitor-comfort scores) and check for errors or critical issues and that the site is secure (uses an encrypted connection).
  4. Open Performance and review impressions (times your page showed in results), average position (your average spot in results) and CTR (click-through rate: the share of people who saw your result and clicked it), then note which queries (search words) and pages changed.

Triage of Search Console Issues

1 min

Triage means sorting problems by how urgent they are.

  • Errors that stop Google from indexing (adding your pages to its search list) or ranking pages must be addressed.
  • Issues such as soft 404 (a page that looks empty or missing but still loads normally) or missing fields (missing product details) are not urgent unless pages are de-indexed (removed from Google's list) or missing from Google.
  • Address non-urgent issues after ad performance is stable, or post screenshots (pictures of the report) to get help.
  • The Smart SEO audit score (a rating from an SEO checking tool; SEO means search engine optimization, making pages easier to find in search) is a guide. Fix errors that can be resolved quickly, but do not chase a perfect score.

Search Console Reports

1 min

Performance report

  • The report shows impressions (times your page appeared in results), clicks, and the search queries (keywords) that led to clicks and impressions, along with the pages involved. It is used to judge free organic traffic and which keywords rank (appear in results).
  • It shows total clicks, total impressions, average CTR and average position for queries, pages, countries, devices and search appearance over a chosen date range (for example, last 3 months).
  • Default settings: Search type "Web" and date range "Last 3 months". Tabs: Queries, Pages, Countries, Devices, Search appearance. It can be filtered by position and exported with the EXPORT button.
  • Data lags (arrives late). The "Last updated" label shows the delay, for example 5 hours ago, or several hours.
  • Limits: the report has limited filtering (sorting options) and detail and works mainly as a quick overview. For deeper keyword and ranking analysis, add Ahrefs or SEMrush (paid third-party SEO tools), which give more usable data.

Indexing and user-experience report

  • The report shows how many site pages are indexed, meaning stored in Google's search list so they can appear in free Google results. It also flags user-experience problems (things that make visiting worse) such as slow loading pages.

Tool Choice

1 min
  • Google Search Console is the main tool for tracking SEO results, including rankings for product searches versus competitors.
  • Ahrefs (a paid SEO tool) is an optional advanced tool and is not required.
  • Sources differ on third-party tools (tools made by other companies). The 2021 rule calls Ahrefs optional, while the older advice says dedicated tools give more usable data for deeper analysis. Both apply: start with Search Console, and add a third-party tool such as Ahrefs or SEMrush when deeper keyword and ranking analysis is needed.

Checking Search Results Accurately

1 min
  • To see how a product page appears in organic Google results (unpaid listings), including price, stock, review stars and delivery details, search in an incognito window (a private window that does not use your account) or a logged-out window.
  • A logged-in search for a product term can return only Shopping results (paid product boxes) and hide the organic listings.

Diagnosing Traffic Quality

1 min

Checking where organic traffic really comes from

  1. Open Google Search Console and review which keywords produce impressions and clicks, since Google Analytics (Google's free visitor-measuring tool) gives poor search term data.
  2. Use Google Analytics to see which countries the visitors come from.
  3. Judge whether the traffic is qualified (right countries, right keywords) before optimizing the site for conversion (getting visitors to buy).

Common mistake

  • A low sales count with many visitors can mean the traffic comes from unrelated search terms or from countries where the store does not sell, not from a weak store.

Tracking Keywords and Organic Rankings

1 min
  • Mapping keywords to pages (giving each page its own search words) gives SEO work a clear focus and replaces scattered effort.
  • Once the top keywords are tracked, ranking progress and organic traffic show whether the SEO strategy works.
  • Keep tracking which keywords the store ranks for organically during each Search Console check.

Tracking TikTok Traffic with UTM Parameters

1 min

UTM parameters are small tags added to a link. They tell you which visitors came from TikTok.

Procedure

  1. Search "TikTok utm parameters builder" and use the TikTok Ads Manager link (TikTok's ad tool).
  2. Enter your product URL (the web address of the product), and the builder generates a UTM-tagged link (a link with tracking tags).
  3. Use this link in TikTok campaigns (groups of ads or posts run toward one goal).
  4. View results in Shopify Reports (Shopify's built-in report pages) > Sessions by Marketing (visits sorted by source) and select UTM term and UTM content filters (choose the tag types to split results).

Quick-Win Sequence for Top Blog Posts

2 min

A blog post is an article on your site. A quick win is a fast improvement.

Procedure for the top five posts by traffic

  1. Run a diagnostic pass (a checking round) for about 30 to 45 minutes with no edits. Pull query-level Search Console data (results broken down by search word) for the top five posts, identify queries with high impressions but few clicks, and check the brand name search results and the storefront setup for localized URLs (web addresses set up for each country or language), using canonicals (tags that name the main version of a page) and hreflang (tags that tell Google which language or country version to show).
  2. Rewrite the title tag (the headline Google shows), H1 (the main heading on the page), meta description (the short summary under the title in results) and first intro paragraph of those posts around the high-impression queries the diagnostic found.
  3. In the same editing pass, add an on-topic call-to-action block (a button or line telling the reader what to do next) and an email capture (a sign-up form for emails) to each post.
  4. In the same pass, add contextual internal links (links from one page to another page on your own site, placed where they fit the text) from those posts to the relevant collections (product category pages) and to the About or mission page, using descriptive anchor text (clickable words that describe the target page).
  5. Fix only the technical issues the diagnostic actually found, not a blanket clean-up (fixing everything at once).

Scope of bulk rewrites

  • In a bulk blog rewrite (changing many posts at once), every blog article can get a row in the CSV (a spreadsheet file with one line per item). But full title, meta and intro rewrites should go only to the articles that actually carry search demand (people really search for them). In one case that was seven articles, and the rest did not need the same effort.

Expected results

  • Average position in Search Console blends every query a page ranks for (it averages all search words together). Part of a low click-through rate can therefore come from weaker, lower-ranked queries.
  • Do not predict a big multiple of today's clicks from a target CTR (a goal click rate). Present the result as a range and expect real but smaller gains.

Case: content-led Shopify store (a store that grows mainly through articles)

  • Starting point: top blog posts had high search impressions but low click-through rates and no next step for readers.
  • The diagnostic pass checked query-level data, the brand search result and the localized storefront setup. The team chose five posts for changes.
  • The plan: rewrite titles and intros on those posts around high-impression queries, add an on-topic CTA (call-to-action) and email capture, add internal links to collections and the mission page, and fix only the technical issues found in the diagnostic.
  • Projection for one top post: about 33,858 impressions, 90 clicks, average position about 8, CTR about 0.27 percent. Reaching about 1 percent would mean about 340 clicks.
  • Takeaway: the case shows the plan and the projected CTR math, but no measured result from the rewrites.

Content Refreshes

1 min
  • Update existing blog posts once a year by revising the title to show the new year (for example, change to "updated 2021").
  • Keeping content current helps maintain top search rankings.
  • After the publishing pace is established (you post new articles at a steady rate), shift effort from creating new content to optimizing (improving) pages and posts that already perform well.
  • Improve existing winners rather than constantly producing new content.

Keeping SEO Work Continuous

1 min
  • Run the Search Console routine on a fixed schedule, every two weeks for a Shopify store and adjusted for traffic, and place it in a calendar.
  • Track keyword rankings and organic traffic sources each time, so that ranking progress shows whether the strategy works.
  • Once publishing is established, direct effort to improving pages and posts that already perform well.

Search listings: titles, Meta descriptions and snippets

Section 14.8 · 8 of 20 in this chapter · 24 min read

In short

  • Write a unique title and description for every product, collection and page. Google shows these to people who search.
  • Keep the title under 70 characters and the description under 320. Longer text gets cut off.
  • Rewrite the automatic description. Shopify fills it in, and it is rarely good enough.
  • End every description with a call to action, a short line that tells people what to do next.
  • Check every claim and offer. AI tools can invent details, so compare the text with the real product before you save.
More key points
  • Low Smart SEO scores show which products to fix first. A score of 100 does not mean nothing is left to improve.
  • Check every AI-written title and description against the real product facts before you save.
  • Keep the new title on the same subject as the page. A different subject can make the page lose its current rankings.
  • If people see a page often but rarely click it, rewrite the title and first paragraph to answer their exact question.

Where the listing is set

2 min
  • The search engine listing decides what Google shows for a page. A search engine is a site like Google or Bing that finds web pages.
  • The listing has three fields. A field is a box you type into.
    • Page title: the headline Google displays.
    • Meta description: the short text shown under the title in results. It is a summary written for search engines.
    • URL handle: the short name at the end of your web address, after your domain (your shop's main address). It is also called the slug.
  • These fields are optional for demo products.
  • On a product page (the page for one item), the Search engine listing section shows the product's URL. The URL is the web address.
  • You can edit how the product appears in search results in that section.
  • The section is at the bottom of the product edit page.
  • Its name is Search engine listing preview. A preview shows how something will look.
  • The section holds the Page title, Description, and URL and handle fields.
  • The preview shows the title, URL and description as they look in Google and Bing.
  • If no meta description is set, Shopify (the software your online shop runs on) uses the product description. The product description is the text you wrote about the product.
  • A collection is a group of products in your shop. Collections have a Search engine listing section.
  • The page title and meta description show what Google displays for the collection's keywords. Keywords are the words people type into Google to search.
  • You can leave them empty at first. Complete them later as part of SEO work. SEO (search engine optimisation) means making your pages easier to find in Google.
  • Pages, such as About or Contact, use the Edit website SEO section.
  • That section holds a search engine listing preview and these fields: Page title, Description, and URL and handle.
  • Shopify shows a character counter under Page title and Description. A character is one letter, number, space or symbol. The counter shows how many you have typed.
    • The limit is the most you may type. For Page title it is 70 characters.
    • For Description it is 320 characters.
  • Page title and Description are what customers see in Google.
  • Make them accurate at launch (the day your shop goes live). Improve them later.
  • Once the business is running, these fields need proper SEO work.

Homepage and social sharing settings

1 min
  • Set the homepage title and meta description in Shopify under Online Store > Preferences > Title and meta description. The ">" sign means "then": click one menu, then the next.
  • The same place can be reached through Sales channels (the places you sell from) > Online Store > Preferences.
  • (2021-22) The homepage title allows up to 70 characters.
  • (2021-22) The homepage meta description allows up to 320 characters.
  • (2021-22) The homepage meta description appears under the page title in Google. It describes what the store sells and why to buy from it. It must have real content, not filler (empty words).
  • (2021-22) The homepage title is the clickable headline in Google results. It is also the text in the browser tab (the small label at the top of your browser window).
  • (2021-22) The homepage meta description is the snippet beneath it. A snippet is the short block of text Google shows for a page.
  • Set both for the root domain (your main web address). Describe what you sell and why people should buy there. It is a writing box, not a technical setting.
  • (2021-22) The title and meta description define how the store appears in search results.
  • (2021-22) A social sharing image is the picture shown when someone shares a link to your store on social media.
  • Set it under Online Store > Preferences > Social sharing image, at 1200 x 628 px (px means pixels, the size unit for screens).
  • Social platforms show it when someone shares a link to the store. It is also used if no other relevant image can be found.

Character limits and truncation

1 min
  • Content longer than the limit is truncated (cut off short) in Google results. Important information then gets hidden.
  • Shopify shows a character counter in the preview.
  • Keep the meta title (the same thing as the Page title) at or below the limit shown. The example shows 53 of 70 characters.
  • Keep the description at or below 320 characters.
  • Longer text is cut off with an ellipsis (three dots:...).

Auto-filled values and common mistakes

1 min
  • (2021-22) With no custom meta data (your own text), Shopify copies the product title and product description into the meta title and meta description.
  • The copied description is often cut off mid-sentence. It reads like a product spec dump (a plain list of technical details). Always customise it.
  • (2021-22) The auto-filled meta title is often acceptable, especially for product pages, because it usually matches what shoppers search for.
  • (2021-22) The auto-filled meta description is rarely usable and should always be rewritten.
  • (2021-22) On platforms that do not fill in meta data, Google chooses the title and description from any text on the page. This can give an unattractive snippet, so set meta data by hand.
  • (2022) Changing meta tags (hidden details that describe a page to Google) does not affect Google Ads product data, as long as product names and descriptions stay the same. Google Ads is Google's paid advertising tool.
  • Changing meta descriptions or page names does not break links as long as the URL stays the same.
  • If they are in the wrong format, correct them.
  • (2021-22) Shopify shows a preview of the product title in the search title field.
  • Check that the search title matches the product title and that the URL is clean. Clean means simple and readable.
  • Then replace the auto-filled description with a short text.

Writing page titles

3 min

Product titles

  • Product page titles include the brand name, product name and SKU (stock code: a code that identifies one exact product).
  • Remove the business name from product pages. Keep it in the homepage meta title instead.
  • If the title is too long, remove extras first, such as the lowest price or free shipping.
  • ** The page SEO title should include the product name, the SKU and the brand name. This is what is added in most cases.
  • Brand-led formula: brand name, then the full product name, then a short click trigger (a reason to click), such as free shipping, insurance (cover if the parcel is lost or damaged), or a real offer. Keep it under 70 characters.
    • Example for a bamboo cutting board set: Northlane Bamboo Cutting Board Set | Free Shipping and Insurance
    • Example for an oak bookshelf: Hearth and Co. Oak Bookshelf | Order Today, Free Shipping
  • Benefit-led formula: [Target keyword] + [unique, verifiable claim such as largest, fastest, or most affordable] + [brand or short descriptor], kept within the character limit.
    • Target keyword means the words you want this page to rank for. A verifiable claim is one you can prove.
    • It works because the keyword matches the search query (the words a person typed into Google). It appears in bold in the snippet.
    • The claim gives a reason to click over competitors (other shops selling the same thing).
    • Example for a standing desk: Standing Desk for Small Offices | Quietest Electric Motor Under $400
  • Purchase-intent words, such as buy and online, in product titles help those pages rank for searches by people ready to buy. These people convert (turn from visitors into buyers) better than people running broad searches for information.
  • Find high-converting keywords in Google Ads and add them to product meta titles and descriptions. Then the organic listing (the free result, not an ad) matches what buyers already search for.
  • Long-tail phrases: a long-tail phrase is a longer, more specific search phrase. If your best revenue (money from sales) comes from one, such as handmade German chef knife, add that exact phrase with its qualifier (the extra words that narrow it down) to the meta title and meta description.
  • This matches search intent (what the searcher wants) more closely. It can improve both clicks and conversion for that query.

Matching the product title versus rewriting it

  • (2021-22) Make sure the meta title matches the product title.
  • The meta title does not need to match the product page title word for word.
  • It can be rewritten to name a specific benefit or unique selling point (what makes your product different). For example, it can say the product is the largest of its type. Keep the target search term in it.
  • Use the matching rule when the product title already names the product clearly.
  • Use the rewrite when a verifiable claim gives a reason to click.

Keywords and headlines

  • Include the focus keyword in the meta title, meta description and URL. The focus keyword is the main keyword you want the page to rank for.
  • (2021-22) Headlines can include keywords for organic ranking without losing their function.
  • A headline should also state a benefit that follows from the main promise.
  • (2021-22) Each meta title and meta description should be unique for the page (different from every other page) and contain the focus keyword.
  • Write them using the copywriting principles for meta descriptions. Copywriting is writing that persuades people to act.
  • The page title and meta description are read together in search results, so they work as one unit.
  • The title names the product with the target keyword.
  • The description gives a reason to click, such as a shipping detail or a real offer.

Case example

  • (2021-22) This is a before-and-after case for an RV (recreational vehicle, such as a camper) fridge product on Shopify.
    • Before: the unedited snippet used the auto-pulled product description. It was cut off mid-sentence at the character limit.
    • After: the edited version used a benefit-led title with the target keyword RV fridge. Its description was copy-style and ends with a call to action.
    • Result: it displays in full, with no truncation.

Writing meta descriptions

4 min

Principles

  • Write for the click, not only for the ranking. A ranking is the spot your page holds in results.
  • Treat the description as a short sales letter.
  • (2021-22) A search snippet is a sales message only in the sense of getting the click.
  • The actual selling happens on the product page.
  • Treat the meta description like ad copy whose only goal is the click.
  • (2021-22) Write the meta description as short copy addressed directly to the searcher.
  • Use a question or a direct statement, not a list of specifications.
  • End with a clear call to action, for example Shop online today. People do not reliably decide to act on their own.
  • Use the brand name, product name and SKU number in the meta description.
  • The business name is optional and can be left out to keep the title short.

Formulas and patterns

  • Capital-letter formula: capitalise a few key words, such as FREE, NEW or LIMITED, so the snippet stands out among 10 to 15 competing results.
    • Remove repeated brand names.
    • Shorten words where you can. For example, replace "and" with "&" or "+" if the platform allows it.
    • Example: Handmade Oak Desks | FREE Delivery in 2 Days, 10-Year WARRANTY, Shop Solid Wood Now.
    • Example: Organic Cold-Pressed Olive Oil, EXTRA Virgin, Bottled in Italy | FREE Shipping on Orders Over $50.
  • Sensory or benefit-led hook: these beat plain product-fact lists. Sensory means words that appeal to the senses, such as taste or smell.
    • Hooks can use fancy-sounding wording, ingredient origin, FREE in capitals, or same-day dispatch (sending the order out the same day).
    • Generic phrases such as order online today add nothing.
    • Example: Ceremonial Grade Matcha from Uji, Japan | 30g Tin, FREE Delivery, Same-Day Dispatch Before 2PM.
    • Example: Hand-Roasted Single-Origin Coffee Beans | FRESH Roasted Weekly, FREE Shipping Over $40, Order Today.
  • Copy-style formula: [direct question or address to the searcher] + [one specific product benefit] + [short proof or feature] + [call to action].
    • It works because it speaks to one searcher intent (one reason the person is searching). It ends with an action.
    • Example for a pet water fountain: Want a quieter, cleaner water bowl for your cat? Our 2-litre filtered fountain runs whisper-quiet and needs cleaning only once a month. Order today and get free shipping.
  • Short description pattern: one sentence naming the product's main benefit, followed by one short call to action. The call to action tells the shopper what to do now.
    • Formula: [main benefit sentence]. [action verb] [product] online today.
    • It gives the result a reason to click and a clear next step.
    • Own examples: Quiet cordless vacuum with 60 minutes of runtime (how long it runs before it needs recharging) and a washable filter. Shop online today.
    • Own examples: Hand-stitched leather wallet in three colours. Browse the full range today.
  • Benefit-and-trust formula: call to action, product name, brand name, one real trust or shipping detail, and an optional real offer, written like a short ad.
    • A trust detail is something that makes people feel safe buying.
    • Example for a bamboo cutting board set: Shop the Northlane Bamboo Cutting Board Set today and get free shipping and insurance on your order.
    • Example for an oak bookshelf: Order the Hearth and Co. Oak Bookshelf today with free shipping and a 30-day exchange window (time to swap the item).
  • Keyword-first formula: start with the target keyword (for example Order the [keyword]). Then add the brand name, one short benefit, and a real shipping or store offer such as free shipping. Close with the official store.
    • The keyword at the front helps the page rank and gives searchers a faster match. This can raise CTR (click-through rate: how often people click your result after seeing it).
    • Example 1: Shop the [keyword] from the official [brand] store. Soft, durable and in stock, with free shipping on all orders.
    • Example 2: Order the [keyword] online from [brand]. Fast delivery and easy returns, with free shipping over [amount].

Template for product descriptions

Order the [PRODUCT NAME] today from [BRAND NAME]. [BRAND NAME] offers free shipping and insurance on all orders. [ONLY IF THIS PRODUCT HAS A REAL BONUS: Order today and receive a free [BONUS NAME].] Delete the bracketed bonus sentence if this product has no bonus.

Benefit-focused template

To improve your search result click-through rate, construct Meta descriptions that identify the page topic and your brand, then emphasize convertible benefits like free shipping, insurance protection, and a complimentary bonus item. This benefit-focused approach performs better than standard product descriptions.

Calls to action and checks

  • (2021-22) End the meta description with a call to action (a short line telling the reader what to do next).
  • (2021-22) The meta description must not be cut off.
  • It should end with a call to action such as Shop Online Now.
  • The meta title, URL and product title should all match.
  • (2021-22) Check each product in this order:
    1. Make sure the meta title matches the product title.
    2. Make sure the URL reflects the product title (updated title).
    3. Make sure the meta description is not truncated.
    4. Add a call to action such as Shop Online Now.

Phone numbers

  • Show a phone number in the meta description only where it makes sense.
  • Higher-priced products with a long sales cycle (the time from first interest to purchase) benefit from call-us messaging. Buyers want to speak to someone first.
  • A phone number is a differentiator (something that sets you apart) when competitors do not show one.

Snippet elements and what drives clicks

2 min
  • Search results show the page URL, meta title, meta description and structured data.
  • Structured data is hidden code that gives Google facts, such as reviews, price and stock status.
  • Search results can also show sitelinks, which are extra links under a result that Google makes on its own.
  • Each of these is a place where a searcher decides whether to click.
  • (2021-22) Google bolds search terms that match the query exactly in the snippet. Bold means thicker letters.
  • Use the focus keyword in three places: meta title, meta description and URL.
  • Each keyword occurrence adds a bolded word. It draws the eye and signals relevance to the searcher.
  • Meta data (the title and description that describe a page) is not a direct Google ranking signal. A ranking signal is a clue Google uses to order results.
  • It does strongly affect organic click-through rate (CTR), which is itself a ranking signal.
  • Optimize meta data mainly to earn clicks from search results.
  • The meta description has not been a direct ranking factor since 2003.
  • Its value is mainly the CTR it earns. Spending much time on it for low-traffic pages (pages with few visitors) is usually wasteful.
  • CTR is the share of people who see a listing in organic search results and click it.
  • A 100% CTR is not realistic.
  • But a higher CTR signals to Google that the listing matches the search. This can help rankings over time.
  • ** Page titles and meta descriptions should be written for CTR, since more organic clicks lead to higher organic rankings.
  • (2021-22) Misleading or incomplete snippets lead to immediate bounces.
  • One example is showing a product as available when it is out of stock.
  • A bounce is when a visitor leaves at once.
  • High bounce rates hurt SEO, because user interaction (how people use the page) is also a ranking signal.
  • Accurate snippets filter for visitors who really want the product.
  • (2021-22) On Shopify, Google generates organic search sitelinks completely on its own.
  • Merchants (store owners) cannot set them in the product or page editor.
  • You can only prepare pages for sitelinks where possible. Google has said it may add manual control (you pick them yourself) later.
  • Optimized product page titles and keywords help both organic search traffic and Google Shopping results. Google Shopping shows products with pictures and prices.
  • Updating them is low priority when product pages already follow the standard structure.
  • It is unlikely to hurt.

Claims, bonuses and accuracy

1 min
  • Mention bonuses or promotions in the meta description only if they really apply to that product.
  • A promotion such as $10 off orders over $50 should not appear on a single product priced under $50. It cannot be claimed there.
  • Do not keep claims for bonuses or offers the store does not have.
  • Remove any promotion that is not active before saving the page.
  • AI tools (computer programs that write text), including Smart SEO and others, can invent product details.
  • Always check generated meta text against the actual product before saving.

Blog posts and pages

1 min

  • Set the URL and listing fields for a Shopify blog post or page:

    1. Save the post first.
    2. In the search engine listing preview, check the URL and handle field.
    3. Replace the handle with the target keyword in lowercase, words separated by hyphens (-).
    4. Set the page title. Keep the product name, or use a clearer phrase such as A guide to customizing your standing desk or The ultimate guide to customizing your standing desk.
    5. Delete the description that Shopify pulls automatically from the content.
    6. Enter the meta description (see the meta description steps) and save.

  • Ask ChatGPT (an AI chat tool) for a meta description with this prompt:

Please write a meta description for a blog post called '[post title]'. The post targets the keyword '[target keyword]'. Keep it accurate to the actual post content, include the keyword naturally, and keep it under 160 characters.
  • Add a meta description to a blog post:
    1. Ask ChatGPT for a meta description using the post title and target keyword (prompt above).
    2. Read the result and check that it matches the actual post content.
    3. In the search engine listing description field in Shopify, delete the automatically pulled text.
    4. Paste the checked meta description and save.

Prioritising pages and testing CTR

1 min

  • Audit the top 10 pages by page views in Google Analytics (Google's free tool that counts visitors). Page views are how many times a page was opened.

  • This follows the 80/20 rule: a small part of the work often gives most of the result.

  • Improve their meta descriptions and titles before the rest.

  • Test and improve each page's listing:

    1. Write compelling, keyword-relevant titles (50-60 characters).
    2. Write descriptive meta descriptions (150-160 characters) that encourage clicks.
    3. Ensure both are unique per page.
    4. Test for click-through rate in Google Webmaster Tools (now called Search Console, Google's free tool that shows how your pages do in search). Improve if CTR is below the niche average (the average for your type of shop, or your market).

  • Start with title and meta rewrites on pages that already get many impressions (how often your result was shown).

  • Target pages at positions just outside the top results, roughly positions 8 to 11. A position is where your page shows in results.

  • A CTR lift of 1 to 2 percentage points on 30,000 or more impressions is meaningful traffic without new content. Percentage points are steps on the percent scale, such as 1% to 2%.

  • CTR check example: 90 clicks from 33,858 impressions, at an average position of about 8, gives a CTR of about 0.27%.

  • A page near the bottom of page one (the first page of results) commonly earns 1 to 3%.

  • Reaching just 1% on those impressions would mean about 340 clicks.

  • Brand search check: if your own brand ranks lower than third-party aggregators in Search Console, act on it. Aggregators are other sites that collect and list information from many sources.

  • For example, your brand may rank around position 10 with hundreds of impressions.

  • Make the homepage title lead with the brand name.

  • Set up Organization schema (hidden code that tells Google facts about your business) and sitelinks.

  • Confirm the brand's own pages are the ones shown.

Smart SEO and AI-assisted drafts

2 min

  • Smart SEO (a Shopify app that scores pages for search engines) gives each product an SEO score from 0 to 100. SEO means search engine optimization: making a page easy for Google to find and understand. The score checks the title and meta description. Meta title and meta description are the short texts that Google shows in search results. Use low scores to find products to improve first. Do not treat a 100 as proof that nothing is left to improve.

  • Case: a product named only 100K Club had a Smart SEO score around 50. The meta title was changed to include the brand name and the full product type, Brand 100K Club shirt. The score rose to about 75. The change was made by editing the meta title field in the product's Smart SEO record (the settings box for that product in the app).

  • Generate meta title and description with Smart SEO AI (the app's built-in AI text writer):

    1. Open the product in Smart SEO and click edit on the meta title and description.
    2. Delete any description text you do not want to keep.
    3. Enter keywords for the generation (the words people type into Google), such as the brand name, product type, and product name.
    4. Choose a tone of voice (the style of the writing) that fits the brand: expert for a serious brand, playful for a fun one.
    5. Click Generate.
    6. Choose the titles and descriptions you like. You can keep both options.
    7. Check every generated claim against the real product details.
    8. Click Save.

  • Generate meta tags for blog posts (articles on your store): Smart SEO > Meta tags > Articles > Edit > Generate. Pages and special pages can be handled the same way in their own sections.

  • Generate and choose meta tags in Smart SEO:

    1. Open Smart SEO > Meta tags > Articles and click Edit on the post.
    2. Click Generate.
    3. Enter the target keyword(s) (the main search words you want the page found for) in the keyword field, for example customizing your standing desk plus ultimate guide.
    4. Select a different tone and click Generate again to change the style. Each run gives a new meta title option and a new meta description option.
    5. Compare the generated options with the ChatGPT meta description. Keep the stronger one.
    6. Enter the chosen meta title and meta description into the post's search engine listing (the box in Shopify that sets how the page looks in Google), then save.

  • The Smart SEO meta tag view may still show old values right after a post has changed. Reload the view, or check the saved values in Shopify, before you rely on them.

Bulk rewrites from Search Console data

4 min
  • Google Search Console is Google's free tool that shows how your pages appear in search. Bulk SEO update workflow with an AI tool (a chatbot that writes text):
    1. Pull query-level data (data for each exact search phrase a person typed) for the chosen pages over about 90 days. Include impressions (how many times your page was shown in results), position (where your page ranks, where 1 is the top) and clicks for each query.
    2. Scrape (copy automatically) the current title, meta title, H1 (the main heading on the page) and intro (the first paragraph) of each page, so the AI can compare them with the new targets.
    3. Ask the AI to write rewrites for each page, built around its highest-opportunity query (the search phrase with the best chance to bring more visitors), and to output a CSV (a plain spreadsheet file with rows and commas) with one row per page.
    4. Export the CSV to a spreadsheet, edit it, and check it critically for topic changes, duplicated text and weak targets.
    5. Verify the CSV before import. Every row should have the same number of columns, and quoting (quotation marks around text that contains commas) should be correct.
    6. Apply the approved changes to Shopify in bulk, either through a bulk import app or a Shopify MCP connection (a direct link that lets an AI tool read and change your store).
    7. Run the internal link rules (links from one page of your store to another) through the same process: review outside Shopify first, then apply.
  • Bulk rewrite prompt:
You are working on an online store. For each page URL in the list below, use its Search Console query data from the last 90 days. Pick the single highest-opportunity query for each page, preferring queries with many impressions and positions just outside the top results. Then write a new H1, a new meta title, a new meta description and a new first paragraph that stay on the page's existing subject, lead with the target query, and capture the secondary queries naturally in the intro. Do not change the subject of a page, and do not optimize a page around just one item from a set of similar topics. Output a CSV with the columns URL, section, clicks (90d), primary target, impressions, average position, current H1, new H1, current meta title, new meta title, new meta description and new intro. Flag any page where the best target query does not fit the page's topic.
  • The same CSV rewrite pipeline used for blog posts can be extended to product meta titles, product intros, collection meta titles, collection intros and collection H1s. A collection is a group of products on one page. Build each group from its own query data. Rewrite product or collection pages only where search data shows real demand.
  • Rewriting the title, meta title and first paragraph of a ranking page is among the fastest ways to improve it. Target the highest-opportunity query, or the striking-distance query (a search phrase where your page sits just outside the top results). Capture secondary query variants (close variations of the same phrase) in the intro.
  • When a query has many impressions but zero clicks, the snippet (the short preview text under the title in results) probably does not answer what the searcher wants. Rewrite the title and intro to answer that exact question directly, for example by stating the name of the thing being asked about.
  • Check that an AI rewrite keeps the same subject as the existing page. If the new H1 and meta title describe a different entity (a different thing, person or product) than the one ranking now, the page can lose its current rankings.
  • Click-prompting title framings, such as myth-busting (correcting a common false belief), can raise CTR (click-through rate: the share of people who see your result and click it). Use them only when the content is accurate and delivers on the promise of the title.
  • Example: a fear-of-sharks article had about 617 impressions for phobia of sharks and about 604 for fear of sharks, both near position 8, yet zero clicks. The snippet never named the medical term for the fear that searchers were looking for. Putting that term into the title and opening lines lets the result answer the question directly.
  • Example: a great-white-shark facts article earned 80 clicks in the last 90 days for the query facts about great white sharks, at about position 10.5 with about 814 impressions. The rewrite kept the page on its broad subject and captured secondary queries, such as 10 fun facts about great white sharks and interesting facts about great white sharks, in the intro. A myth-busting meta title was proposed to raise clicks, but only if the content delivers on that promise.

Pre-publish checks

1 min

  • Check the listing before publishing. Confirm that the search title matches the product title, the URL is clean (short and readable, with no extra code), and the description is a short text, not the main description pulled in automatically.

  • Run this checklist for every new page. Go back and do it for existing pages that lack custom meta data:

    1. Pick the focus keyword (the main search phrase the page should rank for) for the page.
    2. Put the focus keyword in the meta title.
    3. Put the focus keyword in the meta description.
    4. Put the focus keyword in the URL.
    5. End the meta description with a call to action (a short line that tells the reader what to do next).
    6. Make sure structured data (code that tells Google what the page is about) is installed and up to date.
    7. Optimize the page for sitelinks (the extra links Google sometimes shows under a result) where possible.
    8. Repeat this checklist for every new page, and go back and do it for existing pages that lack custom meta data.

  • The Page title and Description in Edit website SEO (the Shopify section where you set how a page appears in search) must be accurate at launch.

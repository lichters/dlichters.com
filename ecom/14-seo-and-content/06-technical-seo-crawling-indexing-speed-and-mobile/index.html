Technical SEO: crawling, indexing, speed and mobile

Section 14.6 · 6 of 20 in this chapter · 20 min read

In short

  • Google's crawler must be able to reach your pages, or they never appear in search.
  • Fix access problems first. Writing better text only helps after that.
  • Big stores get limited crawl time, so remove or hide pages that add nothing.
  • Speed matters. Aim for a page that is usable within about four seconds, and test each page type.
  • Check mobile first. Phone results are usually weaker on Shopify.
More key points
  • Test your store on real phones and tablets, then check speed, buttons and text size.
  • After deleting a product, Google drops it once it re-scans your sitemap (your list of pages). You can resubmit the list to speed this up.
  • Use redirects only where they are needed, and fix every 404 error page.

Technical SEO: Role in the Overall Process

1 min
  • Technical SEO is the behind-the-scenes setup that lets search engines reach, understand and index your website. SEO means making your site easy to find on Google. Older advice (2021-22).
  • A crawler is a Google program that visits and reads pages. Indexing means storing a page in Google's list of pages it can show in results.
  • If a crawler cannot properly access a site, the pages will never appear in search results.
  • In that case, the quality of your on-page content (the text and parts you write on each page itself, called on-page SEO) does not matter. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Technical SEO is the first layer to fix, before on-page SEO and content work. Indexing comes first, because it is a condition for ranking (showing up in a good position). Older advice (2021-22).
  • Crawlers also decide which pages belong in the index. So some pages should be kept out of it on purpose. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Fixing access and crawl problems is part of crawl optimization (making crawling work better). It is not just a separate technical issue.
  • If the crawler has trouble accessing pages, it may cover only part of a site on one visit. Some pages stay uncrawled until a later pass. Older advice (2021-22).

Crawl Budget

1 min
  • Search engines give each site a limited amount of crawl time and a limited number of pages per visit. This limit is called the crawl budget.
  • Because of this limit, they rarely crawl an entire large site in one pass. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Pages can be crawled incompletely or skipped. This delays indexing of new pages and updates. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Crawl budget only matters for larger sites, such as online stores with hundreds, thousands or tens of thousands of pages. Older advice (2021-22).
  • A site with about five pages is crawled completely on every visit. It needs no crawl budget work. Older advice (2021-22).
  • If a large site has many low-value pages (pages that add little), the crawler may spend its budget on them. It may then miss your optimized, priority pages. Older advice (2021-22).
  • The crawler does not follow your priorities by default. So the goal is to make its time as efficient as possible. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Low-value pages that take up crawl time can push well-optimized pages out of a crawl. Older advice (2021-22).
  • The same pattern tends to repeat on each later visit. So priority pages may be indexed late or missed for a long time. Older advice (2021-22).
  • You cannot increase, reduce or see the exact value of a site's crawl budget. The only lever is removing obstacles so the crawler works more efficiently. Older advice (2021-22).
  • The overall aim is to send crawl time to pages that matter for SEO. The site should also be easy for crawlers to navigate. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Pages that add no value should not use crawl time that better pages need. Older advice (2021-22).

Factors That Make Crawling More Efficient

1 min
  • Internal links are links from one page of your site to another. They give crawlers paths and help them understand your site structure. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Few internal links leave some pages hard to reach, so they are less likely to be found. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Flat structure: a flat site structure has important pages reachable within few clicks from the main navigation (the main menu). Older advice (2021-22).
  • A flat structure makes crawling considerably easier. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Site architecture (how pages are arranged and grouped) and menu structure both influence how effectively a site is crawled. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Page speed: slow pages increase the time a crawler needs to get through a site. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Faster pages let more of the site be crawled in the same budget. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Slow loading and slow-to-process pages both lengthen each crawl pass. So a site with heavy pages gets fewer pages crawled in the same time. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Redirect batches: large batches of redirects (sending old web addresses to new ones) slow down crawling. Older advice (2021-22).
  • These batches often come from mass URL changes (changing many web addresses at once) that were not strictly necessary. Older advice (2021-22).

Site Audits and Indexation Checks

1 min

An audit is a check of your site for problems. Indexation means how many of your pages are in Google's index.

Free Crawlers

1 min
  • Screaming Frog SEO Spider is a free website crawler (a tool that scans your site the way Google does). It works for up to 500 URLs (web addresses) and is useful for on-page SEO audits.
  • Xenu's Link Sleuth is another free, similar crawler.

Checking Indexation With a Site Search

1 min
  1. Search site:yourdomain.com in Google.
  2. Check the number of indexed pages:
    • The count should roughly match the number of pages on your site.
    • Too few suggests an indexation issue, such as a robots.txt or robots meta tag problem. (robots.txt is a text file that tells crawlers which parts of a site they may visit. A robots meta tag is a tag in a page's code that gives the same kind of rules for that page.)
    • Too many suggests a canonical problem. A canonical tag tells Google which copy of a page is the main one. This is common on large CMSs (content management systems, the software that runs a site) such as Magento or self-hosted platforms. It is less common on Shopify.
  3. Check that title tags (the page title shown in results and browser tabs) are not missing, duplicated or unprofessional.
  4. Check that product titles and descriptions are optimized.

Ahrefs Site Audit

2 min
  • Ahrefs is an SEO tool. Its Site Audit report menu includes: Internal pages (pages on your own site), Indexability (whether pages can be indexed), Links, Redirects, On page, Social tags, Duplicate content (the same text on several pages), Localization, Performance, Images, JavaScript (code that makes pages interactive), CSS (style code) and External pages (pages on other sites). Older advice (2021-22).
  • For finding thin pages (pages with very little content) and duplicate pages, use the Indexability and Duplicate content reports. Older advice (2021-22).
  • The audit overview shows Health Score: the share of your internal URLs (pages on your own site) that have no errors. Older advice (2021-22).
  • The overview also shows Issues distribution: how many errors, warnings and notices (serious problems, smaller problems and small remarks) you have. Older advice (2021-22).
  • The overview also shows Top issues. These include:
    • Orphan page (a page with no incoming internal links, meaning no other page links to it)
    • 404 page
    • 4XX page
    • Meta description tag missing or empty (the short summary text shown under a result)
    • Page has links to broken page
    • Page has no outgoing links
    • Title tag missing or empty
    • Duplicate pages without canonical
    • Redirected page has no incoming internal links Older advice (2021-22).
  • To see the full list, click View all issues, then View affected URLs to export the list. Older advice (2021-22).

Procedure for working through an audit:

  1. Open the Ahrefs site audit overview and read the Health Score, Issues distribution and Top issues.
  2. Click View all issues.
  3. Open the issue you need, for example Orphan page (has no incoming internal links).
  4. Click View affected URLs.
  5. Export the list of affected URLs and their values.
  6. Paste the export into the audit spreadsheet, then check each URL for internal links or content and fix it. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Example audit of a drop-shipping store (a store that ships products directly from suppliers) with about 5,337 internal URLs crawled. Older advice (2021-22).

  • Another example audit of an e-commerce store (an online shop) with 5,337 internal URLs had 24,702 issues in total. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Those issues were: 1,517 errors, 13,058 warnings and 10,127 notices. Older advice (2021-22).

  • Automated audit tools are not always fully accurate. Relying only on them can miss issues. Older advice (2021-22).
  • A manual review (checking by hand) is recommended to be certain nothing is missed. Older advice (2021-22).

Crawl Control: robots.txt, noindex and Redirects

1 min

robots.txt

1 min
  • robots.txt is the text file that tells crawlers which parts of a site they may visit.
  • Only robots.txt rules block crawling. Older advice (2021-22).
  • A noindex tag does not stop crawling. (A noindex tag tells Google not to list a page.) Older advice (2021-22).

Editing robots.txt on Shopify:

  • Current (2024): robots.txt can be edited by adding a theme template named robots.txt.liquid. (Liquid is Shopify's template language. A template is a design file in the store's theme.)
  • Go to Online Store > Themes > Edit code, then add the template under Templates.
  • Check the changes in Google Search Console after saving.
  • robots.txt cannot be edited by the merchant (the store owner) on Shopify. So noindex is the practical tool there. Older advice (2021-22).
  • This older statement reflects older platform behaviour. It conflicts with the 2024 method above.

Procedure for a 'Submitted URL blocked by robots.txt' error in Google Search Console (Google's free tool that shows how Google sees your site):

  1. Click the page in the Examples table to open the side panel, then click 'Test robots.txt blocking'.
  2. The robots.txt tester highlights the rule that blocks the URL.
  3. Click 'See live robots.txt' in the tester to see where the file is located.
  4. Edit the rule only if the block is unwanted. If you lack permission to edit the file, contact your hosting provider (the company that stores your site) or check its documentation.

noindex

1 min
  • Place this tag in the head (the top, hidden part of a page's code) of a page to keep it out of the index:
<meta name="robots" content="noindex">
  • Google treats noindex as a suggestion that keeps the page out of the index. The page can still be crawled. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Adding noindex by editing theme code (the store's design files) can accidentally noindex the whole site. Older advice (2021-22).
  • One such error went unnoticed for about two weeks. Recovery requires re-indexing from scratch (asking Google to list the site again, starting over). Older advice (2021-22).

Redirects

1 min
  • Use redirects only where they are needed. For example, when a page is deleted and should point to a replacement. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Large batches of unnecessary redirects slow down crawling. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Minimize redirects as part of Shopify speed work. Older advice (2021-22).

Handling 404 Errors

1 min
  • A 404 error means the URL points to a page that does not exist.
  • Every 404 found on the store should be resolved. Usually this means updating the link or setting up a redirect.
  • The Smart SEO app reports these errors so they can be fixed.
  • In Search Console, 404 errors mean the page no longer exists.
  • They often remain because the old URL is still listed in the sitemap (a file listing all your page addresses for Google).
  • Submitting a fresh sitemap usually removes them.

Use Google Webmaster Tools (called Google Search Console in 2022-2025) to find indexing errors:

  1. Check the Coverage section (Google's report of which pages are in the index and which are not) for 404 errors, server errors and excluded pages.
  2. Review the Performance section to see which queries (the phrases people type into Google) drive clicks and impressions (how often your result is shown).
  3. Monitor for manual penalties (punishments Google applies by hand for breaking its rules) or security issues.
  4. Submit sitemaps to ensure all pages are crawlable.

Getting Pages Indexed

1 min

Submitting New Pages

1 min
  • Submit new product pages, blog posts and optimized URLs directly to Google for faster indexing.
  • Without this, Google may take weeks or months to discover and index new pages.

Request indexing in Google Search Console:

  1. Go to search.google.com/search-console.
  2. Log in with your Google account.
  3. Select your property (your site as added to the tool).
  4. Paste the new page URL.
  5. Click 'Request Indexing'.
  6. Google will crawl and index it immediately or very quickly. Older advice (2022).

Request indexing with URL Inspection (2025), a tool that checks one page at a time:

  1. Go to Google Search Console.
  2. Open URL Inspection from the left sidebar.
  3. Paste the product page URL in the search field.
  4. Click 'Request Indexing' to add the page to Google's crawl queue (the line of pages waiting to be visited).
  5. Google will recrawl the page faster than waiting for random indexing.

Pages Crawled but Not Indexed

1 min
  • Some pages are marked as crawled but currently not indexed. Google visited them but has not listed them.
  • First confirm that they are real pages you want indexed, and not duplicates (copies of other pages).
  • Only then look for a fix.

Sitemaps

1 min
  • Submit sitemaps to ensure all pages are crawlable.
  • A fresh sitemap usually clears old 404 entries from Search Console.

Page Speed: Ranking, Conversion and Crawling

1 min

Ranking means your position in search results. A ranking factor is something Google uses to order results.

Speed as a Ranking Factor

1 min

The sources disagree on how strongly speed ranks. Both views are given below, with the conditions under which each applies.

  • Strong view: Site speed directly affects Google organic search rankings (the unpaid results). Faster pages rank higher. Older advice (2020).
  • This is independent of Shopify's hosting. It depends on theme lightness (a light theme has less heavy code), app efficiency and image optimization. Older advice (2020).
  • Strong view: Google uses page loading speed as a signal when deciding how high to rank a product page.
  • So a slow page can lose organic position to faster competitors.
  • Speed fixes are among the quickest SEO gains, because their effect can show within days.
  • Strong view: Website speed is a ranking factor, and slow pages lose conversions (visitors who buy or take action).
  • Weak view: Page speed is a Google ranking factor, but a weak one.
  • Relevant, high-quality content and search intent (what the searcher wants) match outweigh speed.
  • A slow page can still rank first if its content is better than competitors'.
  • When each applies:
    • Speed matters most when competing product pages have similar content. Then experience becomes the tiebreaker (the factor that decides between equals). Older advice (2021-22).
    • The 2018 mobile speed update affects only the slowest pages and a small share of queries. Older advice (2021-22).
    • It applies the same standard whatever platform the site is built on. Google has tracked page performance signals since about 2010. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Site speed must be good enough that a mobile visitor on a weak connection still gets the page.
  • Google treats a blank page after a click as a negative ranking signal.
  • Page speed on Shopify pages and blog posts depends first on the theme (the store's design template) and the installed apps.
  • Image optimization is an additional lever on top of a suitable theme and a lean app setup (few apps).

How Much Speed Is Enough

1 min
  • A store does not need the fastest page online or a perfect score in speed tools. Older advice (2021-22).
  • It only needs to load faster than the sites it competes with in the search results for the same queries. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Judge speed relative to those competitors before deciding it is a problem. Older advice (2021-22).

Benchmark against competitors (measure them to compare):

  1. Enter each main competitor's domain into Google PageSpeed Insights (Google's free speed test).
  2. Compare FCP (First Contentful Paint, the time until the first content appears), LCP and CLS (both explained in the Core Web Vitals section below) with your own results.
  3. Only the gap to the sites you rank against matters for SEO. Older advice (2021-22).

Speed and Conversion

1 min
  • Page speed matters for conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy) separately from SEO.
  • A visitor who can see and use the page after about three to four seconds is usually fine. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Conversion may start to drop once the usable point goes beyond roughly four seconds. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Fully loaded time (often 7 seconds or more, until everything on the page has finished loading) is less relevant.
  • Customers can use the page before everything has finished loading. Older advice (2021-22).

Speed Case Study

1 min
  • Before any changes, a store had a GT Metrics (GTmetrix) performance score of 99, a structure score of 95 and an LCP under one second.
  • After compressing images (making files smaller) with Smart SEO and adding descriptive alt text (a short written description of an image) to the product images, the scores rose.
  • The new scores were a performance of 100, a structure of 96 and an LCP of 678 ms (milliseconds, thousandths of a second).
  • These changes were only small.

Measuring Page Speed

1 min

Tools

1 min
  • Test page speed with at least three tools, because each reports different data.
    • Google PageSpeed Insights: Google's own tool, and the one that matters most for search. It is free at developers.google.com/speed.
    • It analyzes page load time and gives recommendations for optimization.
    • Use it to find performance bottlenecks (the slowest parts) and track improvements.
    • GTmetrix: technical waterfall (a chart of each file loading) and Yahoo-developed YSlow scores (a rating system Yahoo created).
    • WebPageTest.org: real browser runs from chosen locations and devices, e.g. Android or iPhone, with three runs per test.
    • Pingdom is similar to GTmetrix and optional.

Test Order and Scope

1 min
  1. Test the homepage.
  2. Test a random sample of three or four product pages to confirm results are consistent.
  3. Test collection pages (pages listing several products).
  4. Identify which problems to fix based on the results. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Always test each page type separately.
  • A fast homepage does not mean fast product or collection pages.
  • A page-speed test of the homepage URL only tests the homepage. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Always look at both mobile and desktop results in PageSpeed Insights. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Mobile scores are usually lower on Shopify.
  • Mobile matters more because most visitors arrive on mobile.

Reading Scores

1 min

  • Scores in red are the priority. Older advice (2021-22).
  • A low score alone is not a reason to panic if competitors score similarly. Older advice (2021-22).

Waterfall Charts

1 min
  • Each row in a waterfall chart (a bar chart showing files loading in order) is one asset loaded. An asset is a file such as HTML (page code), CSS (style code), JS (JavaScript), an image or a font.
  • Long bars and blocking segments show what delays the page.
  • Beige or sandy colour in GTmetrix means waiting for the asset to load.
  • Orange in WebPageTest shows a connection problem.
  • Filter by HTML, CSS, JS or image to isolate the cause. Older advice (2021-22).

Core Web Vitals

1 min

Metrics and Thresholds

1 min
  • Core Web Vitals are Google's main speed and stability measurements. The three are LCP, CLS and FID.
  • LCP (Largest Contentful Paint, the time until the biggest visible item appears) should be under 2.5 seconds for good. This is Google's threshold (the limit that counts as good). Older advice (2021-22).
  • Practical LCP target on Shopify: under 4 seconds.
  • Shopify limits control over some parts of the stack (the layers of technology the store runs on). So the practical goal is 4 seconds, even though Google's good threshold is 2.5 seconds. Older advice (2021-22).
  • CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift, how much things jump around while the page loads) above 0.25 on mobile is flagged as a problem.
  • FID (First Input Delay, the wait before the page responds when someone taps or clicks) is the third metric. It is measured as input responsiveness.
  • Field data means measurements from real users. This is the Chrome User Experience collection over 28 days.
  • Lab data means a simulated test run in a controlled setup.
  • Field data and lab data can differ for the same page. Read both.
  • Treat field data as what Google measures for ranking. Older advice (2021-22).

Core Web Vitals in Search Console

1 min
  • The Core Web Vitals report in Google Search Console groups URLs as Poor, Need improvement or Good.
  • It is based on Chrome UX Report field data (real-user data from Chrome browsers).
  • It names the failing issue per group, such as LCP longer than 4s on mobile or CLS above 0.25 on mobile.
  • It offers a validation step after fixes, which asks Google to check that your fix worked. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Core Web Vitals and Page experience data only appear in Search Console about one week after the store is set up.
  • A brand-new store will show no data at first.
  • Once data appears, check for errors and resolve any critical issues.
  • After you submit a sitemap, Core Web Vitals data takes time to appear.
  • If it is still empty after a while, resubmit the sitemap.

Status of Core Web Vitals as a ranking signal:

  • In the 2020 to 2021 guidance, Core Web Vitals were reported in Search Console and PageSpeed Insights. They were not yet a confirmed ranking signal. Older advice (2020-21).
  • Google usually gives reporting 6 to 12 months before a metric becomes a ranking signal. Older advice (2020-21).
  • Core Web Vitals were framed as mobile-focused user experience metrics. Older advice (2021-22).

Fixing Page Speed on Shopify

1 min

Fix Order

1 min

Work through these steps in order:

  1. Compress images as far as possible in a lossy way (making files smaller by losing a little quality), up to the point where quality starts to suffer.
    • Do this offline (on your computer, before uploading) using a bulk offline tool (one that handles many files at once), such as ImageOptim.
    • Do not rely on compression apps instead. They may run JavaScript or compress less.
  2. Load videos in a thin (lazy) way, meaning only when needed, so their JavaScript only loads when the user clicks play.
    • Use the provided code snippet or a lightweight app. Code is the preferred option.
  3. Implement lazy loading for images, so they load only when the user scrolls near them.
  4. Remove unused apps and their leftover code from the theme.
  5. Use a theme with minimal, clean code.
  6. Minimize redirects.
  7. Minimize JavaScript, e.g. do not use homepage sliders or quick view on collection pages. Quick view is a pop-up that shows product details without opening the product page. Older advice (2021-22).

Images and Media

1 min
  • Remove slideshows, carousels (rotating image boxes) and heavy media elements from product pages if they slow page load time.
  • Website speed is a ranking factor, and slow pages lose conversions.
  • Videos placed in the product description should use a thin (lazy) load, so they do not noticeably slow down the product page. Older advice (2021-22).
  • The default Shopify video embed (the standard way a video is placed on a page) adds at least 1 MB (megabyte, a unit of file size) to the page size.
  • It also adds a large amount of JavaScript. This significantly hurts page speed and therefore SEO. Older advice (2021-22).

Apps and Code

1 min
  • Installing unnecessary apps or plugins (add-on tools) on your Shopify store increases load time, because each app adds to it.
  • Only keep apps that are essential to store function.

Mobile Optimization

1 min
  • Mobile visitors and mobile results matter most. Review mobile first, because mobile scores are usually lower on Shopify (a store-building platform). Older advice (2021-22).
  • Mobile optimization steps:
    1. Test your store on mobile devices (phones, tablets).
    2. Ensure images, banners, titles and CTA buttons (buttons that ask visitors to act, such as "Buy now") display correctly on small screens.
    3. Check mobile page speed using Google PageSpeed Insights (a free Google tool that tests how fast a page loads).
    4. Optimize font sizes and touch targets (the areas a finger taps, such as buttons) for mobile users.
  • Check mobile Core Web Vitals (Google's scores for a page's speed and stability) in Search Console (Google's free tool that shows how your site appears in search). The report names failing mobile issues such as LCP longer than 4s on mobile (LCP, Largest Contentful Paint, is how long the main content takes to appear) or CLS above 0.25 on mobile (CLS, Cumulative Layout Shift, is how much the page jumps around while loading). Older advice (2021-22).
  • A site must load well enough on mobile that a visitor on a weak connection still gets the page. A blank page after a click counts as a negative ranking signal (a clue Google uses to rank pages).

Removing Deleted Pages and Old URLs From Google

1 min

After a Site Rebuild

Older procedure (2021):

  1. Submit the site map (your list of all pages, sent to Google) to Google.
  2. Request a recrawl (ask Google's robot to visit your store again) so Google re-examines the store.
  3. Google then removes the old pages from its index (its list of pages it can show in search) and adds the new ones. Older advice (2021).

After Deleting Products

  1. Delete the product from your store. The sitemap updates automatically at yourstore.com/sitemap.xml.
  2. Wait for Google to re-crawl the sitemap (Google's robot visits the list again). The deleted page then drops out of it and no longer shows as an error in Search Console.
  3. Optional: open Google Search Console, click Sitemaps and resubmit yourstore.com/sitemap.xml to request an immediate re-scan.
  4. Google Merchant Center (Google's tool for product listings in shopping results) also stops receiving deleted products. The feed (the product list sent to Google) resubmits on its schedule and skips missing pages.

Redirects for Deleted Pages

  • Use redirects (forwards from an old address to a new one) where a deleted page should point to a replacement. Do not redirect in large batches that are not needed. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Every 404 (the error page for a missing address) found on the store should be resolved, usually by updating the link or setting up a redirect.

More rules worth knowing

1 min
  • If the crawler (Google's robot that visits and reads pages) has trouble accessing pages, it may cover only part of a site on a given visit. Pages it has not reached yet stay uncrawled until a later pass. Fixing access and crawl problems is therefore part of crawl optimization (making it easy for the robot to visit pages), not just a separate technical issue.

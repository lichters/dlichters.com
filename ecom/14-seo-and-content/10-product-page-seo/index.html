Product Page SEO

Section 14.10 · 10 of 20 in this chapter · 20 min read

In short

  • After launch, change product names and web addresses to the words people type into Google.
  • Check Google Search Console after two weeks to see if results improved.
  • Use AI tools for ideas only. Rewrite the text yourself and check that every claim is true.
  • Never copy the supplier's text word for word. Copied text does not rank well in Google.
  • Use your main search term 3 to 5 times on your best product pages.
This section has 22 parts. Tap one to open it.

Timing and Priority

2 min
  • Product pages are the pages that convert into sales, so SEO effort goes there before other content.
  • Apply product page SEO to every product page, not just one or two, and use the same keyword and product information standards across the whole catalog so every product matches its relevant searches.
  • Product page SEO makes products appear for the exact product names shoppers search for and for closely related niche terms. Relevance between the search and the product page decides whether a product shows up at all.
  • Keyword research for product descriptions is done after launch, once ads are live and traffic is coming in. It is not required before uploading products.
  • Launch with supplier-provided descriptions. After two to four weeks, check which products already receive impressions, clicks and sales, then rewrite and enhance those descriptions first.
  • Start with the top 20% of products by traffic. You do not need to rewrite every description at once.
  • For a large catalog, writing unique descriptions for every product is too much work for too little return. Focus the effort on pages that already receive traffic.
  • Products you want to rank for need unique, longer descriptions. Give your writing time to those products first and decide which descriptions get the most effort.

Finding the Highest-Traffic Pages

Procedure

  1. Pull a monthly report in Google Analytics.
  2. Open the Content section.
  3. Sort pages by traffic.
  4. Add unique copy to the highest-traffic pages first, then work down the list.

Procedure

  1. Upload all products with the supplier's specifications as a starting description.
  2. Run Google Shopping ads to drive traffic.
  3. In Google Analytics, open Behavior > Site Content to see which product URLs visitors view most.
  4. Write unique product descriptions for the top 20 percent of those pages, following the 80-20 rule.
  • Unique descriptions on the most visited product pages raise conversion, because shoppers get more information. The same pages also tend to rank better organically.
  • For a monthly view of traffic, use the Content report. For a view of which product URLs visitors view most, use Behavior > Site Content.
  • Rewrite the pages that receive the most traffic in Google Analytics first. (2023.)

Supplier Descriptions: Launch Versus Rewrite

1 min

Sources agree that the target is a unique description. They differ on whether copied text is acceptable at launch. Both positions are listed below, with the conditions for each.

Target state

  • Write unique product descriptions for every product. Copied manufacturer or competitor descriptions do not rank in search results.
  • Product descriptions must be at least 300 words long and must be unique, not copied from other sources.
  • Use reverse image search and competitor product pages to find more information for descriptions. Write descriptions that differ from the supplier's copy to avoid duplicate content.
  • Use an SEO-based method for product pages rather than generic AI-generated text. (2025.)

Acceptable at launch, rewritten later

  • Copied supplier descriptions do not cause a blacklist, but original descriptions tend to rank better over time, so rewrite them as traffic grows.
  • Supplier product descriptions copied at launch are generally acceptable, because duplicate manufacturer copy is common across many sites. Later, find the highest-traffic product pages and rewrite them to be unique and more useful. (2023.)
  • When a new supplier is approved, its default catalog descriptions can be uploaded on day one and rewritten later. Leaving them unchanged at launch does not hurt the product page.
  • Supplier stock descriptions are used by many stores, and duplicate content is not a reason to avoid them, because product content can still rank well. Rewriting them gives better results, so do it when time allows.
  • Stock supplier descriptions are the base of product descriptions, then built out in the SEO lesson of module six.
  • In the early launch phase, supplier descriptions and specifications may be copied and pasted into the store, because duplicate content matters little for paid ads and speed to the first sale matters more. Rewrite them later during the growth phase.

Older position

  • Copying a competitor's product descriptions or supplier default text weakens your chances of ranking for those products, because search engines see duplicate content.

Product Titles

3 min

Principles

  • The product title is the most reliable keyword target because it is the H1 heading and the most relevant term for that page.
  • Broad product type terms are better left to collection or content pages, unless the site is small and the product is the only one of its type.
  • Use a unique custom title instead of the generic supplier listing name. Unique names usually face less competition on Google when shoppers search for them.
  • Titles and descriptions should target the brand name, the supplier's name and product identifiers, rather than many extra keywords.
  • Match product titles and URL slugs to how top future competitors write them. Include SKU or variant numbers only if those competitors include them. (2024.)
  • Titles should match what buyers actually search for, such as brand, color and product type, rather than the long technical name from the supplier. Full technical details and variations belong in the description.

Title order

Sources give different orders. Each is listed with its conditions.

Order Conditions Source
Brand name, product name, SKU number Standard order. Variants may occasionally be included. (2023.)
Brand name, product name, SKU number Format in Smart SEO. (2025.)
Brand, model name, product type, optional descriptor Pattern for brands with sibling products. Separates the item from similar products of the same brand.
Brand, product name, SKU, supplier identifier Built into the product name field for long-tail targeting.
Search term in the exact order people type it Used when optimizing from search data.

Formula

Structure each product title as:

Brand > Model name > Product type > Optional descriptive words that separate the item from similar products of the same brand

Example pattern: Brand Model Product type in Colour

Search engines use the title as a primary signal to match products to shoppers' searches, so the product type (for example electric skateboard) should be included even if the supplier omits it.

Examples in the same pattern:

  • Trailride Lumen 2 carbon road helmet in matte white
  • Harbor Loft 4 ceramic pour-over coffee dripper in sand

It works because shoppers search by product type and search engines treat the title as a main signal for matching a product to a query.

Common mistake

  • Supplier product titles often lack the brand name or the product type, and that gap hurts search matching. Edit titles when uploading. A title with only a brand and model number should have the product type and colour added.

Search-term order

Example: change 'Source A zip up hoodie' to 'Source A hoodie zip up' when shoppers type the second form.

Keyword choice

  • On a product page, the focus keyword should be the product name, brand name and SKU or model number, not the broader niche keyword. Keyword cannibalization becomes a real problem mainly when many blog posts cover related topics, or when a collection page targets the same keyword as the product pages. (2024.)
  • For the target keyword in a rewrite, use the short product name that starts with the product type, not the brand name, size or finish. Shorter names match what shoppers actually search for.

Sources differ on the focus keyword. uses the product name with brand and SKU or model. uses the short product name starting with the product type. Use for the page's focus keyword and when choosing the target keyword for a description rewrite.

Keyword in the title

  • The focus keyword should be placed as early as possible in the page title, since the title is the H1 of the product page.
  • Place the focus keyword at the front of the page title.
  • Smart SEO rule: do not put targeted keywords in the URL or the title. (2025.)

Sources differ: forbids keywords in the title, while and place them at the front. applies to the Smart SEO workflow. Outside it, place the focus keyword at the front of the title.

Feed title

  • Use the SEO-optimized product title as the Shopify page title.

URLs and Product Handles

1 min

Decision: whether to include a SKU in the URL

  1. Search for the product on Google without the SKU.
  2. Look at the top organic results, not the ads.
  3. If the top results show one product page with all variants selectable, leave the SKU out of the URL and keep all variants on one page.
  4. If the top results are split by SKU, include the SKU and create a separate page for each SKU.

Handle changes

  • Do not change the product handles, and do not put targeted keywords in the URL. (Smart SEO, 2025.)
  • Change the URL handle to the short keyword form (for example Source A-hoodie) and let Shopify create the automatic redirect from the old URL.
  • Use a short page URL containing the focus keyword.
  • When optimizing a product, keep the URL the same. Avoid changing the URL unless needed, and use redirects when URLs must change. (2023.)

Sources differ. says do not change handles. changes the handle when optimizing from search data, and Shopify creates the redirect. says keep the URL unless a change is needed, and use redirects for any change. Use under Smart SEO. Otherwise change a handle only when the search data justifies it, and create a redirect as and describe.

Variants Versus Separate Listings

1 min

Decision procedure

  1. Search Google for the product name without any color or variant word.
  2. Check how the top results list their variants.
  3. If the top results list variants together on one page, do the same.
  4. If they break variants apart, break them apart too.
  5. Test the choice over time, because it varies by supplier and product.

Competitor check (2025)

  • When choosing product variants vs separate product listings, check how the top 2-3 competitors handle the same products, and replicate their structure.

Competitor check

  • Check the top two to three competitors in the niche. If they use separate product pages for variants, use separate pages. If they use product options, use options.

Shopify handling

  • Shopify product variant handling should follow what the top search results do for the same product name, because variant pages affect search and Google Shopping results.
  • Use the niche keyword in the store name and in product variant pages. Breaking variants into separate pages usually creates more direct traffic for specific keywords, but test it.

Sources differ in emphasis. notes that separate variant pages usually bring more direct traffic for specific keywords. and say to follow the top results. Use the competitor check by default, and test separate pages where you expect direct traffic for specific keywords.

Size and color tables

  • Unique product content such as size tables, color tables and variant charts helps both SEO and the customer's buying decision. Presenting your own version of this information also makes your listings different from competitors.

Keyword Placement

2 min

Where keywords belong

  • Place keywords throughout the product page: in the title, bullet points, description, and the Seller Central keyword field. Write naturally for human readers, not search bots, but ensure keywords appear organically.
  • A week-long oversight omitting keywords from Seller Central significantly reduced visibility and conversions until corrected.
  • Use the niche keyword in the store name.

Long-tail keyword procedure

  1. Build the keyword from the brand (supplier name), the product name, the SKU and any unique supplier identifier.
  2. Put the full string in the product name field.
  3. Repeat it in the product description and the SEO description section of the product page.
  4. Do not stuff the same phrase repeatedly.

Focus keyword and synonyms

  • Choose a focus keyword plus relevant synonyms, based on the keyword research already done for that product.
  • Keywords from the product's own search data are used first. Keyword research for product descriptions is done after launch.

Revenue-based keywords (Google Ads)

Procedure:

  1. Identify the high-converting Google Ads keywords by revenue.
  2. Place them in the product title.
  3. Place them in the product description.
  4. Place them in the meta title and meta description.

This improves organic conversions and helps your ads show for those keywords more often.

Keyword counts

Sources give different counts. Each applies to a different measure.

Measure Value When it applies Source
Occurrences in the product description 3 to 5 times Effective optimization without keyword-stuffing penalties
Occurrences on top-visited pages 3 to 5 times The product name counts as one occurrence, so two mentions in the rewritten description plus the product name on the page gives three
Occurrences across the whole page 5 to 10 times Typical placements that reach 5: page title, one H2 heading, the first 100 words of body copy, one product image file name, one product image alt text
Number of distinct keywords on the page 3 to 5 keywords Include the brand name, product name, and product ID such as a SKU or GTIN when one exists (2023)
  • Placing the target keyword in nearly every line of the description causes keyword stuffing, which reads awkwardly and hurts the page. Use the keyword naturally.
  • Include the focus keyword at least once in the first 100 words of plain text, most often the product description.

Google and the feed

  • Google reads the product description first and the features second when it reads the Merchant Center feed.

Writing the Description

1 min

Procedure

  1. Write at least 300 words that are unique to the product.
  2. Describe the important features of the product.
  3. Explain how each feature benefits the customer.
  4. End with a call to action that encourages the customer to buy the product.
  5. Add a list of 4 dot points that highlight the main features.
  6. Add a list of technical details such as dimensions, weight and similar specifications.

Length and readability

  • Product descriptions should contain at least 300 unique words. Below this, Google may treat the page as thin content, which is why the threshold matters for indexing.
  • Product descriptions should be set in at least 18pt font and use short sentences and short paragraphs to keep the text readable.
  • Long, technical or expensive products should be chunked with subheadings, each describing the few paragraphs beneath it. Short product descriptions do not need this.
  • Putting too much information on a product page is said to hurt SEO. Keep product page tabs to basic information at launch and expand them later.

Content formula

Describe what the product is, who it is for, and what problem it solves, with specifics about use and fit. Such content works because it answers the questions a buyer types into search, which helps the product rank and convert.

Example 1: Solid oak dining table that seats six, with a finish that resists scratches, for families who host weekly dinners.

Example 2: Compact standing desk with a bamboo top and a 60 to 120 cm height range, for home offices with limited space.

Headings

1 min
  • Each product page should have exactly one H1 tag, which is the page or product title. No other H1 tags should appear on the product page.
  • Headings in the description area should be H2 tags. No heading tagged H1 to H6 should ever contain a link.
  • Links inside heading tags are a negative signal for Google, so they should never be used on any page type.
  • The focus keyword should appear in at least one H2 heading, preferably the first one.
  • Tag every heading in the description area as H2, with no link attached.

Images and Video

1 min

Feed-optimized page procedure

  1. Use the SEO-optimized product title as the Shopify page title.
  2. Give images descriptive file names.
  3. Write a unique alt tag for each image.
  4. Write a solid product description.

Image rules

  • Make product images unique where possible, each with a unique descriptive file name. At least one image, ideally the first, carries the focus keyword in its file name.
  • Compress product images as far as possible without visible quality loss.
  • Give images in the description area unique file names and alt tags.
  • Typical placements for the focus keyword include one product image file name and one product image alt text.

Video rules

  • Load videos in the description area with a thin (lazy) load.

Question and Answer Sections

1 min
  • Add a question and answer section to product pages. It serves shoppers and supports SEO.
  • Add a short question-and-answer section near the end of each product description, using an H2 or H3 heading for each question. Only include questions and answers that do not already appear on the FAQ page or in other content on the site.
  • Product-page Q&A sections can improve conversion rates by answering buyer questions on the spot. They also add unique content to product pages and give search engines extra question-and-answer material to display.

Meta Title and Meta Description

1 min
  • Give the meta title and meta description a unique text that includes the focus keyword.
  • Set the page title to the keyword, followed by the brand name. Rewrite the meta description with the keyword at the front.
  • Meta titles and descriptions can be improved with keywords when the URL stays the same.
  • Place the revenue-based keywords in the meta title and meta description.
  • Check the meta description on each listing as part of the checklist.

Shopping Feed and Search Listing

1 min
  • Customize product page copy for SEO. Google Merchant Center pulls information from product pages, so better-optimized pages can rank higher in free listings.
  • Aim to rank in the top 1 to 3 organic Google results for your brand name plus product name by improving your product pages. This gives you two spots on the first page when you also run Google Shopping ads. (2023.)

Search engine listing check

  1. Open the Search engine listing section in the product.
  2. Read the title and description and make sure they are readable and make sense.
  3. Shopify pulls them automatically from the product title and description, so fix any gibberish before saving.

Using Search Data After Launch

1 min

Optimization procedure

1 min
  1. Open the product page on the live store (the public store that customers see). See how it currently looks.
  2. Open the same product in the Shopify admin (the back office where you edit your store) and edit it.
  3. Change the product title (the name shown on the page) so it contains the search term (also called keyword, the words people type into Google) in the exact order people type it. Example: 'Source A hoodie zip up' instead of 'Source A zip up hoodie'.
  4. In the SEO section (the part of the editor for search engines, where you make pages easier to find), set the page title (the title shown in Google's results) to the keyword, followed by the brand name.
  5. Rewrite the meta description (the short text under the title in Google's results) with the keyword at the front.
  6. Change the URL handle (the last part of the page web address) to the short keyword form (e.g. 'Source A-hoodie'). Let Shopify create the automatic redirect (a forwarding from the old address to the new one) from the old URL.
  7. Save the page.
  8. Check Google Search Console (Google's free tool that shows how your pages appear in search) again after two weeks.
    • Look for more impressions (how often your page is shown to searchers).
    • Look for a better average position (how high your page ranks on average).
    • Look for a higher CTR (click-through rate, the share of people who click on it).

AI-Assisted Writing

1 min

Principles

1 min
  • Use ChatGPT, an AI chat tool (AI means artificial intelligence, a program that writes text), for outlines (rough plans) and bullet ideas for product or collection content (pages that group several products).
  • Then expand and rewrite the text in a human voice (in a natural, personal style).
  • Fully AI-generated text is easy to spot and may be penalized (pushed lower in search results). (2024.)
  • AI writing tools such as Copy.ai (another program that writes text) can make ideas and drafts for product descriptions (text that describes an item for sale).
  • They do not replace human copywriting (sales text written by a person).
  • Use them to break writer's block (being stuck and unable to start) or to make variations (other versions), then edit the result.
  • The free version of ChatGPT is enough to rewrite product descriptions.
  • A paid plan is not required.
  • Manual rewriting (rewriting by hand, without AI) is also possible if you prefer not to use AI.
  • AI-written product descriptions can include details that are not true for the product.
  • Compare every claim (every statement about the product) with the real product specifications (the official facts about the product). Remove anything unverifiable (anything you cannot check) before publishing (before it goes live).

Rewrite procedure with ChatGPT

1 min
  1. Open the top-visited product page (the page with the most visitors) in Shopify (the software that runs your store). Copy the supplier-provided description (the text the supplier, the company that sends you products, wrote).
  2. Open ChatGPT and paste the prompt from the pattern below. A prompt is the instruction text you send to the AI. Replace the placeholder (the marked spot to fill in) with the supplier description in quotes.
  3. Choose the target keyword (the main search term for this product). Use the short product name that starts with the product type, not the brand name, size, or finish (the surface look).
  4. Paste the product name into the prompt in place of the placeholder and send it.
  5. Review the output options (the answers the AI gives) and take the parts you like. You do not have to use everything.
  6. Check that the text matches the actual product and contains no claims that are not true.
  7. Paste the approved text into the Shopify product description, replacing the old text.
  8. Proofread it (read it closely for mistakes), verify the data (check that the facts and numbers are right), and edit anything you want to change.

Prompts

1 min

Direct response (writing that makes the reader act right away) structure for product pages (2024 prompt, full text):

Direct response copywriting for product pages uses a proven structure: open with a compelling headline, provide 2-4 lines describing the offering, itemize distinctive features in bullet format, then explain the customer benefits of each feature in bullets. Incorporate your primary keyword at least twice throughout the copy.

Product description prompt (2024, full text):

Take on the role of a direct-response copywriter. Draft a product description for [product name and/or URL] in this layout: a headline, one to three sentences of description, a features-and-benefits section, and a group of bullet points. Draw only on facts found in the product data you were given, make up no specifications, and before the text goes live, recast the finished copy in a natural, human tone.

Rewrite prompt (full text):

Please act as a direct response copywriter. Rewrite this product description: '[PASTE SUPPLIER DESCRIPTION]'. Use this format: Headline, a two to four sentence description, features and benefits, and bullet points. Please include the target keyword, which is the product name [PRODUCT NAME], at least two times. Use only facts that appear in the original description and do not add any claim that could be untrue.

Quality and rewrite targets

1 min
  • Use an SEO-based method for product pages rather than generic AI-generated text (general text not made for your product). (2025.)
  • Aim for the target keyword 3 to 5 times on top-visited product pages. The product name counts as one use.

On-Page SEO Checklist

1 min

Listing checklist for each new product (2021)

1 min
  1. Write a unique product description rather than copying the manufacturer's (the maker's) or a competitor's text. Copied text does not rank well in Google results.
  2. Add product tags (short labels that help sort and find products).
  3. Add a meta description (the short text under the title in Google's results).

A listing missing any of these items means the listing is not finished.

On-page checklist

2 min

Run the on-page SEO checklist (the list of fixes made on the page itself) in this order:

  1. Choose a focus keyword (the one main search term for the page) plus relevant synonyms (words with the same meaning). Base this on the keyword research (the search term study you already did for that product).
  2. Make sure visible breadcrumb navigation (a small trail of links that shows where the page sits in the store) is present on the page.
  3. Tag the page title (usually the product title) as H1 (the main heading level of the page). Place the focus keyword at the front of it.
  4. Make sure product images are unique where possible, each with a unique descriptive file name (a name that describes the picture). At least one image, ideally the first, should carry the focus keyword in its file name.
  5. Compress product images (make the files smaller) as far as possible without visible quality loss (without the picture looking worse).
  6. Make sure the product image and the title are both visible above the fold (on screen before scrolling) on mobile. Ideally, the price and the add-to-cart button (the button that adds the item to the basket) should be visible too.
  7. Tag every heading in the description area as H2 (a sub-heading level), with no link attached.
  8. Place the focus keyword in at least one H2 heading, preferably the first.
  9. Place the focus keyword at least once in the first 100 words of plain text (normal written words, not code).
  10. Use the focus keyword 5 to 10 times on the whole page.
  11. Write the product description in at least 18pt font (a font size of 18 points, a large text), with short sentences and short paragraphs.
  12. Write at least 300 unique words in the product description (words that are not copied).
  13. Give images in the description area unique file names and alt tags (short text that describes a picture for screen readers, which are tools for blind users, and for search engines).
  14. Load videos in the description area with a thin (lazy) load (the video loads only when needed, so the page opens faster).
  15. Minimise boilerplate content (the same standard text repeated on many pages) across the page.
  16. Load the review widget title (the title of the box that shows customer reviews) dynamically (filled in automatically) with the product title included.
  17. Use a short page URL (the web address of the page) containing the focus keyword.
  18. Give the meta title (the page title that search engines show) and the meta description a unique text that includes the focus keyword.

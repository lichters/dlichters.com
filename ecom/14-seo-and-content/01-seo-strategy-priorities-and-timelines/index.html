Playbook › 14. SEO and content

SEO strategy, priorities and timelines

Section 14.1 · 1 of 20 in this chapter · 21 min read

In short

  • SEO means getting your pages into the free search results. Paid ads are the opposite.
  • Only page one of Google matters much, because most clicks go to the top few results.
  • Build product pages first. Write blog posts later.
  • Expect SEO to take months, often six months to a year, before it pays off.
  • Google ranks pages mainly on quality content, backlinks (links from other websites to yours) and work on your own pages.
More key points
  • Blog posts, guides and product pages bring visitors from Google over time. This is a long-term plan.
  • Start blog content only after your paid ads make a profit.
  • Publish at least one new content piece per month.
  • Never stop SEO work, or competitors will catch up.
  • Do keyword research first, and choose the search terms yourself.

What SEO (free ranking in search results) and content do for a store

3 min
  • SEO (search engine optimization, work that helps your pages rank in search for free) means getting your store's pages into the unpaid results of search engines.
  • These unpaid results are called organic results.
  • Paid search ads are the opposite. The advertiser pays for each placement.
  • SEO targets Google and Bing. Bing is the second major target after Google.
  • Organic Google search means people find your store through unpaid text results.
  • These results usually appear on page one (the first page of results).
  • It costs nothing per click.
  • But it depends on your store's ranking (where your page sits in the results).
  • Organic search traffic (visitors from unpaid results) comes when people search for a brand name, a product name or a SKU number (a product's code).
  • They click through an unpaid result. It costs nothing per click, but it is not directly paid for.
  • Organic traffic means any visitors who do not come from paid ads.
  • It includes search results, organic social posts (free posts on social media) and free shopping listings.
  • SEO produces free organic traffic from Google and Bing search results.
  • It is the free counterpart to paid search ads.
  • SEO is one of the core traffic sources (the main ways visitors reach you).
  • It brings in visitors without paying for each click.
  • It counts as free traffic, but it takes work to build.
  • Content marketing (creating articles, guides and other helpful pages) sits between publishing and advertising.
  • Instead of letting third parties earn from that audience, you build a platform you own and advertise your own products on it.
  • Owning the channel gives you control over how customers are influenced and served.
  • Blog posts (articles on your site) keep bringing visitors for years after a one-time cost to write them.
  • Pay-per-click (PPC, where you pay for every click on an ad) charges you for every extra visit.
  • So content has a long-lasting return on investment (ROI, what you earn back from money spent).
  • Ranking for keywords (the search words people type) that matter to your business is like owning the best shop locations in your industry online.
  • If you hold those search results, competitors are pushed out of them.
  • Organic search traffic is one of the traffic sources in the funnel (the path from a first visit to a purchase).
  • It brings visitors without paid ads.
  • It adds to Google Shopping traffic (visitors from product listings with pictures and prices on Google).
  • It works alongside email and retargeting (showing ads again to people who already visited).
  • A single piece of content that ranks on the first page of Google can bring steady monthly organic traffic for years.
  • Over time, that can add up to tens of thousands of visits.
  • So judge content cost by this long-term return, not by the short-term price.
  • Content marketing is a medium- to long-term traffic strategy.
  • Early on, it brings only small amounts of traffic through promotion.
  • It can lift conversion on the site (the share of visitors who buy) when the content solves a buyer's problem.
  • Content and SEO build awareness at the top of the funnel (the early stage, when people first discover you).
  • So a shopper who later searches a high-value term already knows the brand.
  • This raises the conversion chance (the chance a visitor buys) on those high-value searches.
  • Once SEO is fully built, you can drive large volumes of top-of-funnel traffic.
  • These people have no loyalty to a brand. They want the best product. Guide them to yours.
  • Low-value generic searches (broad searches with little buying intent) should be targeted through SEO and content marketing.
  • Do not bid on Google Shopping for them (bidding means paying to win an ad spot). These channels reach that audience more profitably.
  • Organic search traffic from keywords that problem-aware people type (people who know they have a problem but not yet the solution) costs much less than paid ads for the same keywords.
  • This makes it affordable to target the problem-aware stage at scale.

How the search results page is laid out

1 min
  • On a Google results page for a niche search (a narrow topic), the top product boxes are Google Shopping product listing ads.
  • The sections marked "Sponsored" are paid search text ads.
  • Further down come product feed results (listings built from your product list sent to Google) and organic listings.
  • Only the organic listings are the target of SEO.

Ranking factors

3 min
  • Website search rankings depend on three primary factors.
  • They are: quality backlinks (links from other websites to yours), quality content on the site, and onsite optimization (improving your own pages).
  • Google's own description names three ranking factors above the rest.
  • They are quality content, backlinks from other websites, and optimizing the site for RankBrain (Google's AI ranking system).
  • Other SEO work can help a bit more. But without these three, a site will struggle to rank.
  • The two sources phrase the third factor differently. Source A calls it onsite optimization. Source B, citing Google, calls it optimization for RankBrain.
  • Both describe the same area of work.
  • Neglecting any one of the three factors limits search rankings, however well the other work is done.
  • SEO work falls into three main categories: on-page SEO (work on each page), technical SEO (work on the site's behind-the-scenes setup), and off-page SEO.
  • Off-page SEO is mainly link building, which means getting other websites to link to yours.
  • On-page and technical SEO alone rarely produce strong organic search traffic.
  • They multiply the results of content marketing and link building, so all three should run together.
  • Backlinks are a core ranking factor.
  • Deep content (long, detailed pages) earns more links.
  • So building deep content is a prerequisite (a must-do first step) for reaching the top of Google's organic results.
  • Link building is a separate task that must also be done to generate significant organic traffic.
  • Backlinks matter most for very competitive keywords in crowded industries, not for niche e-commerce stores.
  • Read together, the backlink statements apply as follows.
  • The core-factor view applies to competitive keywords.
  • The 2022 crowded-industry view applies to niche stores.
  • Ranking signals (clues Google uses) include user behavior: bounce rate (the share of visitors who leave straight away), time on page and return visits.
  • A good store experience can improve Google Shopping and organic performance.
  • Google evaluates aggregated, anonymized user interaction data (combined, unnamed visitor behavior) to judge whether a result is relevant to a query (the search).
  • Visitors who click an organic result and leave within seconds signal a poor match.
  • So keeping visitors on the page and getting them to interact further is a ranking lever (something that moves rankings).
  • Google states that it uses aggregated, anonymized interaction data to judge relevance.
  • But it does not publish how much bounce rate or time on page weighs in ranking.
  • Treat both as indicators of relevance and page quality, not as confirmed direct ranking factors.
  • Organic traffic depends on many combined factors, so a quick list of tips is not enough.
  • The full SEO material should be studied in depth.
  • Established stores rank better in organic search mainly because they have existed longer, built more links and done SEO work for longer.
  • A newer store can overtake them by applying the same SEO practices consistently over time.
  • Domain authority (a score estimating how strong a site is) alone should not decide whether to enter a niche.
  • Checking competitor scores only shows which keyword competition level is realistic for your store.
  • Organic clicks concentrate on the first few Google results. Deeper result pages are rarely visited.
  • So product pages should aim for the top organic positions, not just to be indexed (saved by Google so they can appear in results).
  • Clicks concentrate at the top. About three quarters of traffic goes to the top three organic results.
  • The number one spot takes roughly two thirds of that.
  • Reaching position one matters more than reaching position three.

On-page SEO

1 min
  • On-page SEO means optimizing each individual web page.
  • The goal is to help search engines understand what the page is about, what context it provides, and which searcher it serves.
  • The goal is to make the page match the search terms it should rank for.
  • On-page SEO works through many small adjustments to a page's content and structure.
  • These adjustments tell search engines which search terms the page should match.
  • It is the first SEO work most sites do after technical SEO is in place.
  • It should be reviewed even if some of it was already done.
  • Product pages need SEO work for the search engine listing (what shows in results) and the product description.
  • Collection pages (pages grouping products by category), standard pages and blogs also need SEO work.
  • Use the newest SEO guides. (2024.)
  • Apply the SEO optimizations from day one, as part of setting up product pages and the store.
  • They are not a later project.
  • For a new store, plan page titles (the headline shown in results), URLs (web addresses) and categories before launch.
  • It is much harder to add SEO structure to a finished site later.
  • Do not delay paid ads on a new Shopify store (an online store platform) because most URLs show as not indexed.
  • Only the key pages need to be indexed.
  • Indexing duplicate URL variants (copies of the same page under different addresses) would hurt search results rather than help them.
  • Outsourcing alt text (a short image description for search engines) and meta descriptions (the short summary under a result) to freelancers on Upwork (a freelance marketplace) is acceptable.
  • The writing does not require expert skill.
  • AI tools (artificial intelligence programs) can speed up the work where you can use them. (2024.)

Target the products you sell

1 min
  • SEO should target the products, brands and SKU numbers you sell, because product pages bring organic buyers.
  • Ranking for a broad niche keyword does not produce the same sales.
  • A narrow customer focus simplifies content and SEO planning.
  • One buyer's interests answer the question of what to publish first.
  • Broad stores with many product types and buyer types often stall because they cannot decide where to start.
  • For a nationwide high-ticket dropshipping store (expensive products that a supplier ships for you, without you holding stock), geo-targeted local SEO (SEO aimed at one place) is usually not worth it.
  • Customers can be anywhere in the country.
  • Local SEO normally covers a location within one state.
  • Once the area spans several states, it is no longer local SEO.
  • Low-margin brand products (products that earn little profit per sale) can still rank organically if the site is set up correctly.
  • That can produce some sales without paid traffic.
  • A new store without traffic data can find its likely best sellers by checking the top product placements in the category pages of established competitor stores.
  • Optimize those product pages first, because top placement usually reflects strong sellers.
  • A store can earn its first sale without any paid ads by ranking organically in Google for long-tail searches (longer, very specific search phrases) such as brand name, product name and SKU number.
  • These rankings can be captured before Google Shopping product listings are set up. The sale came at zero ad cost.

  • Paid ads can be funded from the profit those visitors generate. (2024.)
  • Start organic SEO early, even while doing other work, because traffic takes a long time to build. (2025.)
  • Free Google product listings can drive traffic without ad spend (money spent on ads), so list products there early.
  • Relaunching a stalled store (a store that has stopped growing) also benefits from these free listings.
  • Do not prioritize getting content written immediately.
  • Start with niche validation (checking that a narrow market has buyers) and supplier approval.
  • Build content gradually as the store proves profitable.
  • A blog is not needed to get suppliers to approve a new store.
  • So a tight budget should go to product pages and supplier outreach (contacting suppliers) first.
  • Blog content can be added later to build organic traffic over time.
  • SEO and content marketing are an advanced, later-stage layer.
  • Reaching initial sales does not depend on them.
  • Improve store pages and collection pages for SEO as the store grows, since organic traffic builds slowly.
  • Free Google product listings and paid Google Shopping ads give data much faster than SEO.

What to prioritize first

1 min
  • A new store should prioritize product pages over blog content.
  • Blogs and content pages should come only after product pages are optimized.
  • They should come only once the site is being crawled (read by Google's bots) through Search Console (Google's free tool for checking how your site performs in search).
  • For a store that has not done SEO yet, improve the collection pages and product pages first.
  • Those are the pages you want people to land on.
  • Build blog content around them afterward.
  • Start SEO work with the top 20% of products, which bring in about 80% of website visits. (2024.)
  • Find those products in Shopify analytics (Shopify's own statistics) or Google Analytics (Google's free visitor-tracking tool), and optimize them first.
  • Sources agree on the order of the work: product pages come first and blog posts come after.
  • The first step differs by source.

Sequence A, a Shopify store SEO order (undated):

  1. Install the recommended SEO app.
  2. Do organic keyword research.
  3. Optimize product pages.
  4. Optimize collection pages.
  5. Optimize standard pages.
  6. Optimize blog posts.
  7. Promote the content to get early traffic.
  8. Finally, optimize the search results once everything is set up.

Sequence B, a 2020 checklist:

  1. Increase site speed (measure with Google Page Speed Test and fix identified errors).
  2. Mobile optimize (automatically handled by Source A theme).
  3. Customize top 20% of products by traffic with more photos, embedded videos, and Q&A.
  4. Create 3-5 anchor blog posts (massive value pieces) with contributions from niche authorities.
  5. Publish guest posts (articles you write for other websites) on high-ranking competitor sites (free or paid).
  6. Appear as guest on niche YouTube channels or podcasts, or start your own interviewing suppliers and customers.

Sequence C, a 2021 priority order:

  1. Optimize product pages so they rank for buying keywords such as product names, brand names and SKU numbers.
  2. Create buyer's guides directly in Shopify.
  3. Add blog posts later, since they target lower-converting search terms and make up a smaller share of traffic.

  • The affiliate program (a program where partners earn a commission on sales) was kept but no longer relied on.
  • Organic content became the primary growth engine.

Content strategy and cadence

2 min
  • Build content over time to become the authority (the trusted expert people look to) in your niche (your specific product market). Typical content includes buying guides, tutorials and detailed product pages. This is a long-term plan, not a day-one task. (2024.)
  • Start blog content only after your paid advertising campaigns (ads you pay for) are profitable (they earn more than they cost). Prioritize paid traffic (visitors from ads) first. Build organic traffic (free visitors from search engines) in the background. (2023.)
  • Begin with one piece of content per month minimum, ideally weekly. (2023.)
  • Content works best when it runs alongside ongoing SEO (search engine optimization, making your pages easy to find on Google) rather than as a separate effort. Operators (store owners) who combined both reached a point where organic traffic exceeded their paid traffic.
  • Stopping or slowing content and SEO work lets competitors catch up and take the top market position. Never pause organic traffic efforts, even after success.
  • Do keyword research (finding the search terms customers type) before writing content, since without it you do not know what the content should target. Content also performs better when backlinks (links from other websites pointing to yours) point to it.
  • A realistic SEO cadence (pace) is one quality content piece per month, each followed by backlink building in the months after it is published, rather than many pieces in one month. Each piece gets some traffic on its own, but sustaining organic traffic takes a steady stream of pieces.
  • Build blog content around product pages and collection pages (pages that group several products). Create buyer's guides directly in Shopify (the online store platform) before adding blog posts.

Outsourcing, agencies and ownership

2 min
  • Before hiring SEO help from freelancers (independent workers) or agencies (companies that do the work for you), learn basic on-page SEO (improving the content on each page itself) first. It takes about two and a half hours.
  • For these store types, simple on-page techniques are usually enough. Outsource only the specific tasks you still want done. Avoid broad e-commerce SEO services, which often cost a lot for few results.
  • Keyword research can be outsourced in raw form (a plain list of terms), but the owner must still decide which search terms to target and what content to produce.
  • Raw keyword research can be bought from freelancers on Fiverr (a marketplace for freelance work). The store owner must still decide which search terms to target and what each piece of content should cover, because a freelancer cannot make that decision.
  • Content writing is commonly outsourced because most people write poorly for this purpose.
  • Backlink building is worth trying once yourself first. After that, it can be outsourced to an agency or to a virtual assistant (a remote helper) trained on the process, which is cheaper.
  • Be even more cautious when hiring an SEO agency than when hiring a Google Ads agency (a company that runs paid ads on Google for you).
  • Be cautious about SEO agencies that charge large monthly retainers (fixed monthly fees) for slow results.
  • SEO work on an existing store often comes down to a few tweaks to product pages. Hiring an expensive SEO agency rarely returns its cost. (2023.)
  • Done For You Content Marketing (a service where others create your content for you) is listed as an outsourced content marketing option in the December 2020 coaching call. The call names it without describing its scope or cost.
  • SEO work can be split into on-page and off-page SEO (work done away from your own site, such as backlinks) and assigned to a dedicated SEO person. Content such as blog posts can be written by a separate content writer who reports to the marketing manager.
  • The grow stage has its own deep dive on SEO, content marketing and backlink building, and a separate deep dive on Google Ads. An owner strong in one area but weak in SEO should start with the SEO deep dive first.

From action list to work schedule

1 min
  • SEO works only through continuous work. Occasional or cram sessions (working in short, intense bursts) produce little effect. The three biggest SEO levers (the factors that matter most) work together and should run at the same time: keyword research, content production and backlinks.
  • SEO, content marketing and link building each deliver value alone, but they work much better together. Plan them as one connected effort over time instead of choosing one over the others.
  • A full SEO action item list (a to-do list of SEO tasks) is often too long to work through in one go. Before starting, decide which items come first and which approach you will use for each one, so your time is not wasted.
  • Keep one action item spreadsheet that lists all SEO tasks for the product pages, collection pages and blog pages. Use it to track which tasks are open, in progress or done.
  • A 90-day rock (a short goal you finish in 90 days) for a six-month SEO priority can be completing six pieces of content, each with promotion. This turns the six-month target into a smaller quarterly milestone that can be measured.

Turning the action list into a work schedule:

  1. Go through every SEO action item and note what each one requires.
  2. Check the site and mark items that are already done so they are not repeated.
  3. Decide which of the remaining items you will implement.
  4. Sort the chosen items into higher-priority and lower-priority groups.
  5. Place the higher-priority items first in your work schedule, then add the lower-priority items after them.

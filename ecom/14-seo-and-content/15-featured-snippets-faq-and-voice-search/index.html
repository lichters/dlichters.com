Featured snippets, FAQ and voice search

Section 14.15 · 15 of 20 in this chapter · 10 min read

In short

  • AI answers in Google mainly hurt question-style research searches, not product, brand, or SKU searches.
  • Write product and blog text that answers the questions people ask, not just text full of keywords.
  • A page must already rank in the top four results before it can win a featured snippet.
  • Put a clear answer block at the top of each page, and build an FAQ page that links to your products.

Scope: Which Searches AI Answers Affect

1 min
  • AI-generated overviews (short AI summaries shown at the top of Google results) affect rankings for research-based informational queries (searches where people want to learn something). They do not affect e-commerce product page rankings. Product names, brand names, and SKU searches (SKU is a store's own product code) are not affected by AI. Continue applying standard SEO best practices (search engine optimization, the habits that help pages rank) to product pages unchanged, because they remain effective for e-commerce.
  • Most e-commerce traffic (visitors who arrive from search to a store) comes from buying-intent searches (searches by people ready to buy) such as product names, brand names, and SKU numbers. AI answer features mainly affect question-style searches, so this traffic is largely not reduced by them.

Finding the Questions Customers Ask

1 min

  • Product descriptions and blog content (articles on your site) should answer the questions people ask AI tools about a product or niche (a narrow market segment), not only include keywords (the words people type into search). Questions collected from forums, communities, and search results are the raw material for this content.

  • ** People usually type shortened search phrases, but they speak complete questions when using voice search (searching by talking to a device). Keyword research for voice should therefore target full natural-language questions (questions written the way people talk).

  • ** One short typed keyword, such as "best smart speaker", usually stands for several full spoken questions. Expand each target term (the word or phrase you want to rank for) into question variants (different ways of asking the same question), then cover those variants in the FAQ (frequently asked questions page) or page content.

    Pattern:

    • Target term: "best smart speaker"
    • Expand to: "what is the best...", "where can I find the best...", "where can I buy the best..."
    • Example for a different product: "best running shoe for flat feet" expands to "what is the best running shoe for flat feet?" and "where can I buy running shoes for flat feet?"

  • ** Spoken searches often ask for nearby options in the first person (using "me" or "I"), such as "what's the closest restaurant to me?". Businesses with a local presence should therefore include question-and-answer content that names their location or service area in the question form.

Validating a Question Before You Target It

1 min
  • ** Before using a question as an H2 heading (a second-level section title on a page) to target question-and-answer featured snippets (the short answer box Google shows above normal results), validate that it is an actual search query. Type the potential question into Google to see whether it produces results, or check your keyword research data to confirm that people search for that question. This ensures your featured snippet strategy targets real user searches.

Winning Featured Snippets: Eligibility and Frequency

1 min
  • ** Featured snippets appear for only a share of search queries (roughly 12 percent, as estimated at recording time). When one exists and you win it, the click-through rate (the share of people who click a result after seeing it) is much higher than that of a normal top result.
  • ** A page can only be pulled into a featured snippet if it already ranks in the top four organic positions (the first four normal results, not ads) for that search term. A page ranking at position 10 has no chance, so ranking in the top four must come first.
  • ** Only pages already ranking at positions 1 to 4 should be optimized for featured snippets, because pages at lower positions are too far from the top to benefit yet. Focus first on raising rankings.
  • ** Featured snippets are mostly triggered by informational queries such as "what is" or "how to" questions, and they mainly show content pages such as blog posts. Product searches rarely show them.
  • ** Even when product pages rank in positions 1 to 4, check their keywords for featured snippets, but expect the realistic wins to come from content pages.

Winning Featured Snippets: How Google Chooses the Snippet

1 min
  • ** Google chooses the snippet by content structure, not by ranking alone. A page in position 1 is more likely to be used than one in position 4, but if the top result lacks a clean answer block (a short, clear answer section), Google moves down to the next result and pulls from there.
  • ** Google pulls only the short answer or list from the top of a page, not the full article. A long piece must therefore still show its concise answer block at the beginning to be eligible.
  • ** Leapfrogging (a lower-ranked page jumping over higher ones): a page ranking at position 3 can win the featured snippet and appear above the position 1 result, even if it cannot outrank the position 1 page directly. This works only when the sites above lack a good snippet-ready answer, and not for every keyword.
  • ** Google may choose a short, clear definition from a third-party blog (a site that is not the official source) over its own documentation for a featured snippet. A precise definition placed at the top of your own page can therefore win the snippet even against authoritative sources (trusted official or expert sites).
  • ** A featured snippet sits above the organic results and pushes them further down. Google often shows a set of related question-and-answer boxes beneath it. This placement is why its click-through rate is much higher than that of a normal first-place result.

Winning Featured Snippets: Snippet Types

1 min
  • ** The two common snippet types are definition (a direct answer to "what is X") and list (a numbered list, such as "top 10 ways"). Match your content to the type shown in the search results.

Winning Featured Snippets: Placing Content for a Snippet

1 min

  • ** Add a clear definition at the very start of the post, as the first element of the page, to capture definition-type featured snippets, provided the page already ranks in the top 4 search results.

  • ** Procedure for placing content for a featured snippet:

    1. Add a clear definition or a numbered list to the page that already ranks for the keyword.
    2. Put the definition or list at the very start of the post, above the detailed content.
    3. For a list post, give each list item its own section with an H2 heading, then add the detailed explanation below the list.

  • ** Listicle structure (a list-style article) for list snippets: a numbered list of short answers sits at the top of the post, with each item linked to an H2 section containing the full details below. Google extracts only the short numbered list with its headings, not the whole article.

    Pattern: A "Top 7 ways to reduce cart abandonment" post opens with a numbered list of seven short items, then gives each item an H2 section with an explanation.

  • ** In a list-type post, each list item's H2 section can include its own picture. The short numbered summary at the top stays clean for Google, while the detailed sections below add visual depth for readers.

Winning Featured Snippets: Reviewing Opportunities

1 min
  • ** Review the list of featured snippet opportunities every few months, roughly every six months, because new keywords will enter the top four positions as SEO work progresses.

Building Question-and-Answer Content on Blog and Content Pages

1 min
  • ** In blog posts, use common customer questions on the topic as H2 headings, with the answer as a paragraph directly below each heading. Search engines give H2 headings more weight (treat them as more important), so this makes the answer easier to find.
  • ** A page that poses a question and answers it directly on the page has a better chance of being returned as the spoken answer to that question. The same question-and-answer format can also help a page win featured snippets.
  • ** An FAQ page can match a specific question-style informational keyword, though a content page is the usual default for informational queries. Collection and product pages should not be used to answer those questions. A collection page is a store page that groups several products.

Building the FAQ Page: Setup and Structure

1 min
  • ** Build a standalone FAQ page (a separate page of its own) and link it in the main site navigation (the menu visitors use to move around the site). Also link to it from other relevant content wherever possible.
  • ** The page title of an FAQ page is formatted as the H1 heading (the main title of a page), and every individual question on the page is formatted as an H2 heading. Questions should never be left as plain body text.
  • ** Put a keyword that is not yet targeted elsewhere in the FAQ page's H1 title, next to the words "frequently asked questions" or "FAQ".

Building the FAQ Page: Authority

1 min
  • ** Voice search results favor high-authority pages (pages that search engines trust most), just as image search does. Gaining links from other content on the site strengthens the authority (how much search engines trust a page) of the FAQ page.
  • ** If pages that attract many backlinks (links from other websites) are linked to the FAQ page, some link value flows into it. This helps when the FAQ page cannot be linked to directly from external sites.

Building the FAQ Page: Internal Links and Purchase Path

1 min
  • ** At the end of each FAQ answer, add an internal link (a link to another page on your own site) to the most relevant next page, such as a collection page or product page, if one exists. Answer the question first, then guide the visitor to the logical next step.
  • ** An FAQ page on its own does not move visitors toward a purchase because it contains no products. Internal links from answers are what carry visitors deeper into the store.

Building the FAQ Page: Build Procedure

1 min
  • ** Procedure:
    1. If the store has no FAQ page, create one, start filling it with questions, and add it to the site navigation.
    2. Add questions and answers to blog posts where customers commonly ask about the topic, using question H2 headings.
    3. Add a question-and-answer section with H2 or H3 headings (H3 is a smaller sub-heading under H2) to relevant product pages, making sure the content is unique.
    4. Add an internal link at the end of each answer to the logical next page.
    5. Keep adding new questions as new products and categories are added to the store.

Building the FAQ Page: Maintenance

1 min
  • ** Update the FAQ page at least once a year, and add the word "updated" to the end of the title only after the content has actually been revised. Updates can happen more often whenever new customer questions appear.
  • ** An FAQ page is a living document (a page that keeps changing), because the questions customers ask are discovered only over time. New products and categories regularly bring new questions that should be added.

Product Pages, Content Pages and AI Recommendations

1 min

  • Product and content pages can be tuned so that LLMs recommend the store (LLMs are large language models, the AI systems behind chatbots) for detailed buying queries, such as "the best [niche] to buy". A search for the brand name already returns the store.

  • As of mid-2025, there was no proven page change to get recommended by ChatGPT shopping (ChatGPT's shopping feature). The approach was to watch trends and test only once a method shows results.

  • ** Once category collection pages (pages that group products of one type) and best-of pages are in place, questions that customers ask about the niche become a source of extra pages. Each of these pages can naturally feature your product where it answers the question.

  • ** Have writers draft FAQ content from the questions they have when reading a product description. Send those questions to the manufacturer and upload the answers. This creates useful product content and builds knowledge for the team. Use the same method for FAQ pages and product pages.

    Example: The writer's questions become the FAQ entries, and the manufacturer's answers become the published content.

Voice Search

1 min
  • ** Voice search is a fast-growing search method, driven by voice assistants (talking AI helpers) on phones and smart speakers such as Google Home. Its share is expected to keep rising.
  • ** Voice search targets full questions. Structure pages so that each question is posed and answered directly on the page to improve the chance of being returned as the spoken answer.
  • ** Voice search results favor high-authority pages. Build authority for the FAQ page through internal links from other content on the site.

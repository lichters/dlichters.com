Site structure, navigation and internal linking

Section 14.5 · 5 of 20 in this chapter · 15 min read

In short

  • Keep your site flat: no page should be more than four links from the home page.
  • Every page needs at least one link pointing to it, or search engines may never find it.
  • Send the most links to your best sellers and main collections, because those pages make the most money.
  • Check every AI link suggestion by hand before it goes live.
This section has 9 parts. Tap one to open it.

Site structure and link depth

2 min
  • Site structure means how your pages are arranged and linked to each other.
  • Use a flat structure, not a deep one. Flat means few steps from the home page to any product. Deep means many steps, like a maze.
  • Your structure should have no orphan pages. An orphan page is a page that no internal link points to. An internal link is a link from one page of your own site to another page of your site.
  • In a flat structure, no page should be more than four internal links away from the home page.
  • Treat the home page as the hub, meaning the central point of the site. Aim for three, or even two, links to key pages. But four links is the maximum, not the goal.
  • Count links, not clicks. Google follows links. It does not click menus. A mega menu (a large drop-down menu that shows many subcategories at once) still counts each step as a link.
  • A mega menu can make a deep structure look shallow, because all subcategories show up without a click. Collections nested inside collections inside a mega menu add links that search engines follow. A collection is a page that groups related products together. Check the link count, not the number of visible clicks.
  • The right depth is not a fixed number. It depends on your products and how customers expect them to be organized. A flat structure must still make sense from the shopper's point of view.
  • Lay out your site so that visitors and search engine crawlers can both move through it easily. A crawler is a search engine robot that reads your pages.
  • Link value is the ranking strength that passes from one page to another through links. A backlink is a link from another website to yours. When a page gets a backlink, its link value passes to the pages it links to. So one backlink can help the pages underneath it. Your site structure decides how far that value reaches.
  • Internal site structure matters for SEO (search engine optimization, the work of getting found in search results) for three reasons. First, it helps crawlers move through the site and find new pages. Second, it lets link value from backlinked pages flow to other pages. Third, it helps shoppers find products. Happy shoppers engage more, and that indirectly supports SEO.

Audit procedure to flatten the structure

  1. Go through the product pages. They usually sit at the bottom of the structure.
  2. For each product page, count the internal links needed to reach it from the home page. Include links inside mega menus.
  3. Flag every product page that needs more than four links.
  4. Restructure menus and collections so each flagged page is reached in four links or fewer. Try for three or two where the product organization still makes sense for customers.

Orphan pages

2 min
  • Orphan pages have no internal links pointing to them. That makes them hard for crawlers to find, so they are less likely to be crawled. Crawled means read and added to the search index. Avoid orphan pages in general. There are exceptions where an orphan page is intentional. (older, 2021-22 for Source B; the exception wording is from Source B 2021-22)
  • A page that gets no internal links, and cannot be reached from the main menu or home page, is an orphan page. Search engines understand orphan pages poorly. So every blog or collection needs at least one internal link from the site structure.
  • Orphan pages often exist because a page was made for a single ad or search term. Here is what to do with each one:
    • If the page is still needed, add at least one internal link so Google can crawl it.
    • If the page is not needed, delete it and redirect it to the nearest relevant collection page. A redirect automatically sends visitors from an old web address to a new one.
    • If the page should stay out of search, noindex it. Noindex is a tag that keeps a page out of search results.
  • Crawlers can only find an orphan page through the sitemap (a file that lists all your pages) or through external backlinks. Visitors cannot reach it from any other page on the site. So each orphan page needs either an internal link or a deletion with a redirect.
  • On e-commerce sites (online stores) with many products, orphan pages are usually collection or category pages. They can also be product pages, opt-in landing pages for a lead magnet, or admin pages. A lead magnet is a free gift, such as a guide, given in exchange for an email address. Orphan pages mess up site structure and crawling. They are also harder for shoppers and search engines to find. So avoid them.
  • Making collection pages only as landing pages for paid ads creates orphan pages. Paid ads are ads you pay for, such as Google search text ads or Facebook ads. If you leave these pages out of all navigation, they become orphans. Even a landing page needs at least one internal link from another page on the site.

Fix options for an orphan page

  1. Add the page to the main menu.
  2. Add it as a sub-collection inside an existing collection.
  3. Add at least one link to it from another page on the site, such as a content page.
  • An SEO tool (paid software that checks your site) such as Ahrefs or Raven Tools finds orphan pages automatically. A page audit is that check. Fixing orphan pages is a medium-priority task. It is not urgent, but it is worth doing.

Navigation and main menu

1 min
  • Every product must belong to at least one collection. It must also be reachable from the main navigation. Otherwise, customers browsing the site would never find it. Linking products into the navigation also helps search engines reach every page.
  • Put the most important pages in the main menu. This gives them internal links from the home page. Link them from other places too.
  • A well-planned main menu creates many internal links from the home page. Over time, the home page usually collects the most backlinks. People who discover a business often link to its home page. Its link value then flows out through the main menu.
  • Feature a high-value collection page in the main menu. Sub-collections (collections inside a bigger collection) can also link back up to their parent collection. That adds internal links.
  • Do not label main menu items with generic terms such as Shop or Catalog. Use the product types and keywords that customers search for. A keyword is a word people type into search. Use the specific product names of your store. Generic labels lead to long link dumps, which help neither customers nor SEO.
  • Blog pages are usually added after a store starts growing, because they serve informational purposes. Create the blog post first. Then add its link to the navigation, like any other page.
  • Keep the home link in the navigation. Most sites link to their homepage, and it does not hurt search rankings. Users click the logo, so a separate home link is optional.

Breadcrumbs

1 min
  • Breadcrumbs are the trail at the top of a page, like Home > Shoes > Boots. They show where the page sits. On Shopify, an online store platform, breadcrumb navigation is a site-wide setting. You turn it on or off in the theme (the design template of your store). On other platforms, you may need to add it page by page.
  • Breadcrumb navigation helps customers find their way. It also builds internal links quickly, which search engines value.
  • Breadcrumbs can be added to a Shopify store, because product categories already exist in the store. Their SEO impact was uncertain at the time of the call. So test them before relying on them.
  • Where the sources differ: Source A (2023) calls the SEO effect uncertain and recommends testing. Source B (2021-22) treats breadcrumbs as a quick way to build internal links. Both agree on the customer benefit. Turn them on, then measure before counting on an SEO gain.

Internal link types, anchor text and placement

2 min
  • Internal links come in two types. Navigational links come from menus and site navigation. Contextual links sit inside the body text of pages, such as blog posts. A site needs both types to get the best SEO results.
  • Contextual links help search engines discover new content. They also show which pages are related. If several articles on one sub-topic link to each other, search engines see the site as more authoritative (trusted as an expert) on that topic.
  • Internal links between your store's own pages help Google judge the site's context and relevance. This is a separate lever from external backlinks. Both need planning. Strong external links do not replace a sound internal link structure.
  • Link to related blog posts, product pages and other pages. This keeps readers on the site. Suggested-post blocks at the bottom of articles are one way to do this. They are added with an app (a plug-in that adds features to your store).
  • Search engines treat pages with many internal links pointing to them as more important than pages with few. Use this to signal which pages matter most.
  • The more internal links a page receives, the more important search engines think it is. They also get more context about it. So revenue-driving product and collection pages should get the most internal links. Blogs should not be treated as equally important.
  • Link value also flows through contextual links to other pages. Link from content pages to the commercially important pages. These include best-selling products, popular collections, and products with better margins (the profit left after costs). Do not link mainly to pages that add little business value.
  • Do not point many internal links at low-value pages, such as admin pages linked from every page. Search engines may treat them as important. If such a link must exist, make it a nofollow link. Nofollow is a tag that tells Google not to pass value through the link.
  • Do not put internal links to low-value pages. These include admin pages and products that rarely sell and do not convert. A conversion is a shopper completing a purchase. Instead, decide which pages matter most. Then build a strategy to link to them more than to other pages.
  • Place the focus keyword in one or two internal links per page. Do not stuff keywords into every anchor text. Anchor text is the clickable words of a link. Search engines read the text around a link as well as the anchor text itself.
  • Over-optimized anchor text looks artificial. An example is a keyword in every internal link on a page. It can hurt rankings instead of helping them.
  • Internal links between related product pages are acceptable where they make sense. Place them near the bottom of the product description, not early in the copy.
  • Where the sources differ on product-to-product links: Source E (2025-26) sets the default rule that products must not link to other products. This is part of the automation rules (see below). Source B allows related product links placed near the bottom of the description. Apply the stricter Source E rule to automated linking. Treat Source B's placement advice as the reference for manual links written into a product description.

Links by funnel level and cluster building

2 min
  • A funnel is the path a shopper takes from first interest to buying. It has three levels: top, middle and bottom.
  • Do not link from product or collection pages to top-of-funnel blog articles that only answer general questions. That moves shoppers away from checkout. Links that answer a purchase question on the page itself keep shoppers closer to buying. An example is a sizing guide on a product page.
  • Internal link priority by funnel level:
Funnel level Typical pages Internal link share
Top of funnel Informational blog articles Smallest
Middle of funnel Guides and category-style blog articles, middle-funnel collections Medium
Bottom of funnel Product pages and collection pages Largest share, clearly more than every other level

Links should flow from top to bottom of the funnel, not in reverse.

  • Use 3 to 5 internal links on each collection page. Place them as a mini navigation, a small menu of links. On each blog post, build a couple of internal links to collection pages or other blog posts. This forms clusters, which are groups of linked pages on one topic.
  • Plan your URLs (web addresses) before writing. List the planned collection URLs, for example /collections/men's-running-shorts. Also list the blog URLs. Include pages that do not exist yet. Give that list to the writing prompt. Then every new page links to the planned pages, and clusters build as content is produced.
  • Linking from an older page that already gets daily traffic to a new, related page gives the new page two sources of visitors. The first source is link equity, the ranking value from the older page's backlinks. The second is readers who click through from the existing link. So linking from high-traffic old posts is a practical way to kick-start a new page.
  • Internal linking is the first priority in an SEO clean-up. It shows search engines which pages matter and how they relate to each other. Pages without solid internal links are poorly understood and rarely rank.
  • Both zero internal linking and excessive, random internal linking hurt SEO. Zero linking leaves pages disconnected. Linking everywhere dilutes the signal of which pages are important.

Cornerstone content

4 min
  • Cornerstone content, also called pillar content, is a small set of pages at the heart of the business. They are the most valuable either financially or as the entry point to a competitive, high-volume search term. These pages are built to rank at the top of page one.
  • The cornerstone strategy is an advanced SEO method for competitive keywords against established sites. Most sites skip it. So it can help a newer site overtake them.

Cornerstone strategy steps

  1. Identify the content pages that sit at the heart of what the business is about.
  2. Include those pages in the main navigation, not only in a blog or resources section.
  3. Feature those pages on the home page.
  4. Send as many internal links as possible from other content and collection pages to the cornerstone content.
  5. Build four or five smaller content pieces on related topics. Link each of them to the cornerstone content. Build external backlinks to each smaller piece.
  6. Build external backlinks directly to the cornerstone content as well.
  • Example: a surfboard shop could make an ultimate guide to surfboards its cornerstone page. It would target a broad keyword that gets tens or even hundreds of thousands of searches a month. The page is large, detailed and comprehensive. It sends traffic that can later be retargeted (shown ads again to people who visited before) or added to an email list.
  • Link flow in a cornerstone structure runs upward. Supporting pages link up to the cornerstone page. Do not link the cornerstone page back down to them. That way it keeps more internal links than most other pages on the site.
  • Supporting pages on related angles let you build many more backlinks in total than one page can receive. Their link value flows into the cornerstone page. This raises its page authority or page rank far beyond what direct backlinks alone would achieve. Page authority and page rank are scores that show how strong a page seems to search engines.
  • Example: a cornerstone guide on a high-ticket product (an expensive item) can link to sub-guides split by budget. These could be the best options under $10,000 and under $5,000. This lets one hub page cover several related search intents at different price points. Search intent is what the searcher wants.
  • Cornerstone pages in the main menu also serve shoppers who browse the navigation rather than search for a specific product. Those shoppers are usually in the middle of the funnel. So these pages can support conversions as well as SEO.

Plan cornerstone content from the content plan

  1. Review the content plan. Identify which pages will be the cornerstone content. There can be more than one.
  2. Decide which other content pages will be created and receive backlinks. Link them into the cornerstone content.
  3. Decide which collection pages should link into the cornerstone content.
  • A collection page can serve as cornerstone content for a high-volume, competitive keyword. Backlinks to collection pages are hard to build. So link to them heavily from content pages that do receive backlinks.

Case: Rain or Shine Golf, cornerstone structure in 2020

Rain or Shine Golf is a store that sells golf simulators (indoor systems that let you practice golf at home) and accessories. In 2020 it used a cornerstone structure. Its main menu showed its main product types, brands and resource pages. Product drop-downs led to collections, which is the usual setup.

Several individual high-priority products were also placed at the top level of the menu. These were the SkyTrak Practice Golf Simulator Package at $3,399.00, the SkyTrak SwingBay Golf Simulator at $7,299.00 (compare-at price $7,799.00), and the Uneekor QED SwingBay Golf Simulator at $11,299.00. A compare-at price is the old, crossed-out price shown next to the sale price.

The store also placed several guides in the main menu. These included Best Golf Simulators of 2020, Best Golf Launch Monitors, and Best Golf Hitting Mats. It also had a Best Golf Simulator Screens and Enclosures guide. These guides target keywords with high search volume that shoppers use early in their buying journey.

A Resources page collected dozens of content pages. Many of these pages linked into the few guides in the main menu. The guides also linked to collection and product pages. This gave the store a pyramid of internal links, with many links at the bottom flowing up to a few key pages at the top.

The guide page opens by saying a home simulator once cost a $20,000 minimum. Now it can be had for well under $10,000, or even $5,000. It then lists links to sub-guides, such as Best Indoor Home Golf Simulator Under $10,000 and Best Golf Simulators Under $5,000.

The coach says the approach gets good traffic and sales results for the store. The cornerstone pages also serve shoppers in the middle of the funnel. Those shoppers browse the menu rather than searching for a product.

Internal link automation and review

1 min
  • Automation means rules run by a machine, like AI. Before any automation, define explicit internal linking rules. Blog URLs may link to other blog URLs or to collections. Collection URLs may link to other collections. Products must not link to other products. Four or five such rules are enough to control a store's link structure.
  • In one audit of 300 AI-suggested internal links, the links came from an export of 300 blog articles. An export is a downloaded list. About 100 suggestions were usable as they were. About 200 were invalid. In those cases, the URL existed but the referenced content did not, or the suggested link was poor. This shows AI link suggestions need a manual check before anything goes live.

Safer workflow for internal link suggestions

  1. Crawl or export the relevant pages with their URLs and content. To crawl means to let a robot scan every page.
  2. Let the AI propose candidate internal links from existing content. Base them on your linking rules.
  3. Review every candidate manually. Mark each one as green light, needs edit, or delete.
  4. Apply only the approved links, and only after the review is complete.
  • Small teams and solo operators can accept more automation for internal links. But the AI should still never publish link changes unreviewed. Even with a small site, check suggested links for hallucinated content before applying them. Hallucinated content is text the AI invented that does not exist.

More rules worth knowing

1 min
  • Internal links show search engines how the site is structured. They also show which pages cover which subjects. They are a way to move ranking value between pages.
  • Backlinks to a content page pass part of their value through its internal links to other pages. So a content page with many backlinks that links to a collection page can lift that collection page. This works even if the collection page has no backlinks itself.

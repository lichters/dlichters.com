Playbook › 14. SEO and content

Content strategy, formats and planning

Section 14.14 · 14 of 20 in this chapter · 56 min read

In short

  • Content brings free visitors from search results. Paid ads alone do not have to carry the whole store.
  • Plan each piece for one clear job, such as answering a customer question or earning a link.
  • Start regular content once the store has visitors, so you know which topics they want.
  • Pick a topic and start writing. Waiting for the perfect order costs more than a good first choice.
  • Copy the shape of pages that already show high on Google, never their words.
More key points
  • Keep one sheet for your content plan, with one row per piece.
  • Read every similar page first. Your page must be better than all of them.
  • Start with two good pieces per month. Do not go higher until you have experience.
  • Publish about two high-quality content pieces per month, every month, with no end date.
  • Quality beats quantity. More weak posts add no value.
  • Promote each piece for at least two weeks and link to it from your store.
  • Google results build slowly, often over several months.
  • Start with how-to guides and listicles (lists with a number in the title). They are the easiest to make.

Role of Content in the Funnel

4 min
  • The funnel is the path a buyer takes, from first noticing a problem to buying a product.
  • Without content, a store depends on paid channels (ads you pay for) to get traffic (visitors). Paid channels include Google ads, paid social (ads on social media) and affiliate marketing (other people promote your store for a cut of sales).
  • Content adds organic traffic, meaning free visitors who arrive from search results. This traffic does not depend on ad spend alone.
  • Search engine optimisation (SEO) means making pages easy for search engines to find and rank.
  • SEO cannot be done well without content, because product pages give search engines too little text to rank for informational searches (searches to learn something, not to buy).
  • Content has four jobs.
  • Job one: answer customer questions and solve problems.
  • Job two: gain attention and engagement (people looking at and interacting with the content).
  • Job three: bring traffic from search engines and YouTube.
  • Job four: build backlinks (links from other websites to yours).
  • Plan each piece for at least one of these jobs.
  • Product pages are commercial, meaning they sell.
  • Content is educational and entertaining, meaning it teaches and entertains.
  • Product pages fit buyers who already know the product (product-aware) or who already know the brands and how they differ (ninja-aware).
  • Buyers who know their problem but not the fix (problem-aware), or who know fixes exist but not which product to pick (solution-aware), need educational content.
  • An example of educational content is a guide that explains what to look for and recommends specific products.
  • A store made only of collection pages (lists of products in one category) and product pages can answer questions only at the product-aware and ninja-aware stages.
  • Content lets a store serve the whole awareness pyramid (every stage of how much a buyer knows) more easily than any other marketing method.
  • Competitors who reach people in the early awareness stages first gain attention, engagement and trust.
  • Those competitors also lock those buyers into their own sales funnel.
  • A brand that appears only at the product-aware stage must win attention away from sources the customer already trusts.
  • To be present everywhere in the buyer's journey, a brand must engage customers with both content and products.
  • Product pages belong where the buyer already knows what they want, but they should not be the entire journey.
  • Educational content builds know, like and trust (the buyer knows you, likes you and trusts you).
  • Educational content also helps buyers decide intelligently.
  • Show both the positives and the negatives of a product.
  • Show both sides because not every visitor is a potential customer.
  • Pain-point content (articles about a problem your buyer has) can rank in search engines and bring traffic.
  • Pain-point content also guides people through their customer journey.
  • This supports conversion (a visitor becoming a buyer) and authority (being seen as the expert).
  • So pain-point content is a strong starting point.
  • A buyer's guide (an article that helps people choose the right product) raises the authority of a store.
  • A buyer's guide also educates customers and links to products that fit what the reader wants.
  • A buyer's guide can be offered as a downloadable PDF (a file to save or print) with a clear format and relevant product links.
  • Buyer's guides, content on the store, and content made together with suppliers help organic traffic and build the store's content base.
  • Publish unique long-form content (long, original pieces) as video on YouTube or as articles on a blog.
  • Use social platforms to link people to that content.
  • These platforms are consumption-focused, meaning built for watching and reading.
  • So viewers who stay are highly qualified (likely to buy).
  • Written blog content is worth doing on its own, because readers like to read and search engines favour written content.
  • Written blog content can also be combined with video.
  • Content is one part of a connected marketing system, not a standalone task.
  • The same content pieces feed email flows (automatic email series), SEO, link building and retargeting (showing ads again to people who already visited).
  • Automated email sequences can use site content in their first emails.
  • Content published on the store's own website is later reused for link building and SEO.
  • Plan each piece with a place on the site, not only for social channels.
  • Produce content from a researched, documented plan made in advance.
  • Do not produce content from topics picked at random.
  • Random content usually performs poorly, and the failure is then wrongly blamed on content marketing.
  • Content delivers its value when it is produced and maintained on an ongoing basis (regularly, over time).
  • Treating content as a one-off project limits the results it can bring.
  • Most e-commerce stores have no regular content production system when they start.
  • A store without one should build it from the beginning.
  • A store already publishing content should first check whether the content fits the market, then promote it.

When to Start Content

2 min
  • Start blog content marketing (publishing articles to attract visitors) once the store has traffic.
  • Starting with traffic lets you know which topics attract visitors.
  • Content made for the blog can also be reused on social media.
  • Buyer's guides and content are most useful after the store has traffic.
  • The reason is that the data then shows which products visitors actually want.
  • Start a done-for-you content package (a service where someone else writes content for you) only after the store is complete and has traffic data.
  • Traffic data tells you which products visitors view.
  • Spending on content before there is data is wasted.
  • Done-for-you content packages add long-term value through unique content and organic traffic.
  • But done-for-you content packages do not produce early sales.
  • If a store still has open site bugs or unprofitable ads, fix the site and make the paid traffic profitable first.
  • On a new affiliate site (a site that earns commission by sending buyers to other stores' products), publish three to five blog posts before buying traffic.
  • The posts show visitors that you know the topic.
  • The posts also give you something to link to when you share the site elsewhere.
  • Sending traffic to a new affiliate site with no posts leaves you nothing to link to.
  • This matters when you use free methods such as forum replies or YouTube comments.
  • Publish a few posts before you send people there.
  • If there are no competitor blogs in your exact niche (a small, specific part of the market), use the closest related content as a starting reference for ideas.
  • Closest related content includes blog posts from related products or from the buyer's own interests.
  • Do not spend time deciding the perfect order of content ideas.
  • Pick any idea and start producing, because the cost of waiting is higher than the cost of a suboptimal (not perfect) first choice.
  • Research can start immediately, and the plan can be adjusted later.
  • Trying to fully prioritise content ideas (ranking them in order of importance) before starting wastes time.
  • That time could be spent producing content.

Content Types and Formats

1 min

Choosing the format

1 min
  • Use one of three content formats: written only, video only, or a combination of both.
  • Pick the format before planning or producing anything.
  • Suitable formats for keyword topics include buyer's guides, how-to articles, entertainment or list posts, industry announcements and new product type alerts.
  • Choose the format to match the search intent (what the searcher wants: to learn, compare or buy) of the keyword.
  • A keyword (the words people type into a search engine) does not depend on the format of the page that targets it.
  • The same keyword can be targeted with a video, a blog post or another written format.
  • So the format choice does not change the keyword assignment.
  • Mix the content themes in your plan.
  • Problem-solving pieces include how-tos, listicles (articles written as numbered lists), ultimate guides and product comparisons.
  • Entertaining pieces include best-of lists, breaking news and controversial takes.
  • Mixing text, images and video suits readers who prefer different formats.
  • Some readers want the reasons in text.
  • Some want photos of real customers with the products.
  • Some want a video that repeats the text.
  • Every blog post should include three elements: text, images and a video.
  • Place the images and video within the text.
  • Videos should come from the suppliers or the store owner.
  • Extensive content libraries such as comparisons, how-tos and buying guides are a strong SEO strategy.
  • Such libraries can drive a large part of a competitor's traffic.
  • Note the content types and count them.

Type reference

1 min

Recommended length and purpose by content type:

Content Type Recommended Length Purpose
Product release (new item announcement with photos) ~500 words Announce inventory and drive immediate interest
How-to guides, repairs, tutorials, tips, user guides 1,000-2,000 words Build authority and rank for informational keywords
Competitor comparisons 1,000+ words Capture 'best of' searches
Product reviews 1,000+ words Rank for specific product queries
News and announcements 500+ words Show fresh inventory and promotions
Testimonials/customer stories 500-1,000 words Build trust and provide social proof

  • A listicle can work as top-of-funnel content (content for people who are not ready to buy) for a shop that sells related products.

  • Example: a surf shop publishing a top 10 list of local surf spots attracts searchers who are not ready to buy.

  • Product pages remain the commercial targets.

  • The listicle serves to attract the audience.

  • Best-of pages and listicles place your own product first.

  • So they only work if the product is genuinely the best option in its category.

  • Build the product quality first, then the pages.

  • Brand versus brand comparison pages can compare your lesser-known brand with a well-known brand.

  • Or they can compare two well-known brands and recommend yours at the bottom of the page.

  • News-jacking (writing about a news story to get attention) uses a source article, such as a medical article. Follow these steps:

    1. Find the article.
    2. Quote the relevant parts.
    3. Link to the original source and mention it.
    4. Add your business commentary.

  • Do not use content behind a paywall (content you must pay to read).

Length and Depth

1 min
  • Use the type-based lengths in the table above as the starting point for each piece.
  • Blog post length is 300 to 1,000 words, depending on the topic.
  • Do not inflate with filler (padding that adds no value).
  • Write what is necessary to explain the topic fully.
  • Blog posting every other week is effective.
  • Aim for quality over quantity in each article.
  • Quality is what currently ranks.
  • Google increasingly favours authoritative, long pages that cover several related keywords.
  • Google favours these over thin pages (short pages) built around one keyword.
  • To rank for a competitive search term (a term many sites fight for), cover the subject in more depth than the pages already on page one (the first page of results).
  • Google tends to favour longer-form content.
  • So a thin article is unlikely to outrank existing pages.
  • When judging a competitor's content, check its volume and quality separately.
  • Long, in-depth pages with images and links to products suggest higher quality.
  • Short pages that answer simple questions suggest lower quality.
  • Quality gaps reveal opportunities.

Idea Sourcing

1 min

Customers and their questions

1 min
  • To find content ideas, work through four questions in this order:
    1. Which questions, problems and frustrations do my buyers run into that connect to what I sell?
    2. Which content subjects related to my products are already being shared on social platforms and through email?
    3. Which content already ranks in search engines for the keywords I am targeting?
    4. Which product-related content topics are other websites already linking to?
  • Customer service records and customer surveys show real pain points (problems that hurt customers).
  • They also show the questions customers ask before buying.
  • They show what customers struggle with or wish they knew more about.
  • Ask existing customers directly and treat their answers as sources for early content topics.
  • Choose which content to create based on keyword research (finding the words people search for).
  • Base the choice on the questions real customers ask in the niche.
  • Keyword-based content should match the questions customers ask.
  • So the question itself can become the post's target keyword.

Communities and social signals

3 min

  • Content research starts with what already performs in your market.

  • Check what popular creators publish and which topics their audiences watch.

  • Use this to model and adapt topics, rather than copying content.

  • Content gaps are topics that large audiences in your market do not cover well.

  • Finding them gives you content opportunities where you can serve buyers with less competition.

  • Shareable content can be found by looking for existing topic pieces with high social engagement or shares.

  • Create something similar but more in-depth, so it has a good chance of getting the same engagement.

  • BuzzSumo (a tool that shows popular articles) shows which topics in your niche are trending.

  • BuzzSumo displays Facebook engagement counts, Twitter mentions, share frequency and related articles.

  • Use this research to align content strategy with audience interest.

  • Topics with many upvotes (likes on Reddit), likes or comments show what the niche audience engages with.

  • Such topics make good content candidates.

  • Reddit threads with question lists can also provide related keywords for a blog post.

  • Content idea research on Reddit (a discussion website):

    1. Search your niche keyword plus Reddit on Google.
    2. Open the niche subreddit (a topic forum on Reddit), for example the standing desks community with about 27,000 members.
    3. Look at the recent posts sorted by the number of upvotes, and note topics that could become a blog post.
    4. Click the top sort option and change the time filter to all time, to see the most upvoted posts that can serve as evergreen topics (topics that stay useful for years).

  • Example: a Reddit question about whether paint on the legs of a standing desk wears off led to a blog topic on customizing a standing desk, including which paint lasts.

  • The question idea was added to the worksheet as a blog post keyword.

  • Content idea research on Facebook groups:

    1. Go to Facebook and type your niche into the search bar.
    2. Click Groups.
    3. Join relevant groups and look for posts with many likes and comments.
    4. Add those topics and keywords to the worksheet.

  • Example: question-style titles from competitor blogs can be used directly as blog topics in a niche such as standing desks.

  • Examples are how long should you stand at a standing desk and tips for buying an adjustable standing desk converter.

  • Add each one to the keyword worksheet as a blog post keyword.

  • Log content ideas from communities:

    1. Join Facebook groups in your niche and note the questions people ask.
    2. Join relevant Reddit communities and collect the discussion topics that come up repeatedly.
    3. Search Google for niche topics to see what people are asking about.
    4. Log each content idea in a Google Sheet, with one column for the idea and one column for the link to the source it came from.

  • Example of an AI-assisted idea chain (steps where AI tools do the work automatically), applied to a food brand:

    1. Every Monday, scrape (automatically copy) a relevant Reddit forum for the niche, for example r/cooking for a food brand.
    2. Use Chat2T to summarise all new posts and extract key insights.
    3. Send those insights to Claude to generate new marketing angles the brand has not yet tested.
    4. Send each angle back to Claude to write a complete UGC video script (a script for a short video made in the style of user-created content).
    5. Add all generated scripts to ClickUp (a task-management tool) for the production team.

  • This chain turns raw community conversations into ready-to-produce creative briefs automatically.

Brainstorming the first ideas

1 min
  • Plan content topics six months ahead by collecting 12 content ideas as the starting point.
  • Include any ideas already in your head.
  • Brainstorm the rest across different content types for your market or niche.

Keyword and SERP Research

1 min
  • SERP means search engine results page, the list of results Google shows after a search.

Keyword discovery

2 min

  • Keyword and traffic tools such as Ubersuggest, Ahrefs, Semrush and Moz are paid research tools.

  • For a search term, these tools show what content already exists.

  • They also show its social share counts, its organic traffic and its backlink counts.

  • Use them to judge what already exists for a topic.

  • Keyword research for the content plan can be done yourself or outsourced (paid to someone else).

  • If it has not been done yet, do it before entering the first content ideas.

  • Keyword pass output (the result of a first round of keyword research) is a list of terms to target with content.

  • Build a keyword list from informational queries before writing.

  • Style-based keyword research for homewares uses a style name rather than a product term. Follow these steps:

    1. Search for style-based keywords related to your products, such as an interior style name. Note the monthly volume (how many searches per month) and the keyword difficulty (a score for how hard it is to rank).
    2. Choose styles with usable volume and low difficulty, and estimate the number of backlinks needed to rank.
    3. Write a style guide that explains the defining characteristics of the look and how to recreate it at home.
    4. Create product-type guides for the style, such as beds, dining tables or wardrobes.
    5. Link from the guides to lists of your top products for a themed room, so the content leads down to products.

  • Example of a style-based entry point: a keyword such as Hamptons furniture had 2,000 to 3,000 monthly searches.

  • The same example had keyword difficulty of 20.

  • It also needed an estimated about 20 backlinks to rank.

  • Content aimed at style searches sits higher in the funnel.

  • It suits homewares, because buyers start with a look or style rather than a specific product.

  • The content can then lead down to product lists.

  • Keyword research workflow step 1 uses the Ahrefs Content Gap method (a feature that shows topics competitors cover and you do not) to choose keywords.

  • RAW, a second check named in the source, is used to vet (check) the keywords.

  • Evergreen keywords (searches that stay steady all year) are optimised once on existing pages such as collections, product pages or permanent pages.

  • Trending keywords can justify one or two new blog posts per month.

  • Plan the two groups separately.

  • Holiday and promotion keywords are covered in the holiday section below.

Checking what ranks

2 min
  • To check what already ranks (appears high in results) for a target keyword, follow these steps:
    1. Search the target keyword in Google.
    2. Note the URLs (web addresses) of the content pages in the top five organic positions on page one.
    3. Record those URLs in the content research document.
    4. Have the person producing the content review those pages before writing, and identify where they can go further with a better piece.
  • If no content pages rank on page one for a keyword, do not drop the keyword.
  • That means ranking should be easier with reasonable effort.
  • If three or four content pages already rank, they are the competition to beat.
  • Finding no similar online content is not a bad sign.
  • It does not mean the content will fail.
  • It only means the topic may be new ground with less existing competition.
  • Research does not have to be finished for every planned idea.
  • But some research should always be done in advance, so part of the planned content is already researched.
  • Content research for a niche keyword, following these steps:
    1. Search Google for your main niche keyword, for example ring light.
    2. Analyse all organic results on the first page and ignore paid ads.
    3. Note the format Google returns for the query, such as top 10 lists, best-of pages or buying guides.
    4. Build your guides, videos and reviews to match those formats and topics, instead of producing large volumes of content blindly.
    5. Over a few months, this content that mirrors what already ranks lifts a new site against established competitors.
  • Publishing more content on a fixed weekly schedule does not automatically improve rankings.
  • SEO gains for a new site usually build slowly, often over several months.
  • A niche that is new in a country can have high-volume keywords with little competition.
  • So ranking can be possible with content alone.
  • Content should target those keywords before adding backlinks.
  • Case: the seller found keywords with high search volume and low competition in the adult coloring book niche, which is new in the US.
  • The seller wrote seven blog articles of about 500 words each, targeting 10 keywords.
  • The seller used no backlinks, press releases or other link building.
  • The result was page 2 rankings for nine of the 10 keywords and a page 3 ranking for one.
  • The seller saw this as the basis for future free organic traffic.

Competitor Research

2 min

  • Check competitor content for every new content piece. Ask: have competitors covered this topic?

  • If they have, note it and aim to do it better.

  • If no competitor does content marketing in the niche, this part of the research can be skipped.

  • The competitor research document made early in the programme asks whether each competitor does content marketing.

  • Check that answer before searching for competitor content.

  • Competitors with no content marketing can be skipped in content research.

  • Those with content get noted and must be beaten.

  • Do not reinvent the wheel.

  • In markets with existing content, study what works and do everything better.

  • In markets with little content, you are breaking new ground, and organic competition is usually low.

  • The attention matrix lists the people, pages and organisations the audience already follows.

  • Use it to find out whether those sources have already produced similar content.

  • It can be shared with any writer or producer.

  • The attention matrix is a document that maps where a store's audience pays attention.

  • It can be delegated to a virtual assistant (a remote helper).

  • BuzzSumo and Social Animal work like a search engine for content that was shared on social networks.

  • Enter a topic to see shared pieces and how others approached it.

  • Use this for inspiration rather than copying.

  • BuzzSumo has a free sign-up that lists the most popular pages and articles for a niche keyword.

  • Results are sorted by social shares and engagement on Facebook and Twitter.

  • This shows what the audience cares about.

  • Steps to pick content topics from a competitor's blog:

    1. Use the free version of BuzzSumo to search your niche keywords.
    2. Find which of the competitor's posts have the most shares or comments.
    3. Identify the theme of the best-performing content.
    4. Create your own content on a similar but different angle, rather than rewording theirs.

  • Competitor collection pages, step by step:

    1. Search the competitor category on Google, for example L-shaped standing desks.
    2. Note the content types ranking (product pages, collection pages, top 10 lists).
    3. If lists rank, create your own top 10 article on the collection page.
    4. Review the top results and determine the features and topics they cover.
    5. Write your own content covering the same topics, but better.
    6. Add it below the products on the collection page.

  • Research procedure for a product you sell, following these steps:

    1. Select a product you are selling.
    2. Search Google for articles on that product.
    3. Read the top three to five ranking articles on the first page of results.
    4. Note the repetitive patterns, topics and information covered across those articles.
    5. Combine the best information from all of them.
    6. Write a new article in your own words covering those same topics (you can use AI to assist).
    7. Post it to your blog to acquire Google SEO ranking.

Pattern Matching

1 min

Formats that already work

2 min
  • A research process that avoids copying competitors (other shops that sell to the same buyers):
    1. Find the most popular content of competitors. Examples: blog posts with the most comments, or product pages with strong descriptions.
    2. Describe the intro (opening), the body (main middle part) and the conclusion (ending) of that piece in one sentence each.
    3. Have the writer start from scratch with that framework (the plan of parts), using their own words.
  • Look at the format Google shows for your keyword. A keyword is the search phrase buyers type.
  • Build guides, videos and reviews that mirror (copy closely) the top formats and topics.
  • Pattern for content aimed at buyers who know they need a solution (solution-aware) or who know their problem (problem-aware):
    • Write a broad guide, titled as the ultimate guide to a product category for a specific buyer type.
    • It explains the buyer's needs.
    • It lists the characteristics (features) to look for.
    • It recommends a short set of specific products that meet them.
    • Two new examples: The Complete Guide to Choosing a First Road Bike for Commuters and The Ultimate Guide to Picking a Beginner Espresso Machine for Small Kitchens.
  • Page types to build on the store for SEO (search engine optimisation, getting found in Google), in this order:
    1. Category collection page with buying-intent keywords (words that show the buyer is ready to buy), turned into a landing page (a page built to get a sale or sign-up) with your product at the top.
    2. Best-of page for the category with the current year in the title.
    3. Listicle (an article written as a numbered list), for example a top seven list for the current year.
    4. Generic category page.
    5. For-sale page.
    6. Top seven page.
    7. Brand versus brand comparison pages.
  • Brand versus brand comparison pages are covered under Content Types and Formats above.
  • Style guides (guides to a look or design) come first.
  • The top products for a themed room sit after the guide.

The Content Planner

1 min

Planner structure

3 min

  • The BSGR Content Planner is a spreadsheet (a table in Excel or Google Sheets).
  • It has one row per content piece and one column per planning field (one box for each planning detail).
  • Keep the full multi-month list in this single sheet, so the status and owner of every piece is visible in one place.

  • Example of one filled planner row for a home espresso equipment shop (a store that sells coffee machines for home use):

    • Working title: How to choose a grinder as a first-time espresso buyer. Espresso is strong coffee. A grinder crushes coffee beans.
    • Overview: the features the reader needs to judge.

  • Example of a filled content planner row for a fictional fishing gear shop (an invented store, used as a sample):

    • Title: Best Starter Fishing Rods Guide.
    • Overview: help first-time anglers (people who fish for the first time) choose a rod and understand key specs (product details, such as length) before buying.

Filling in the planner

2 min
  • A content title can start as a broad topic. Refine it later.
  • The overview states:
    • who the content is for,
    • what it should teach or achieve,
    • The goal of the piece.
  • Target keyword and similar online content are recorded in this order:
    1. Take the primary target keyword from keyword research and record it.
    2. Add the similar terms, close variations and synonyms (words with the same meaning) of that keyword. Plan to include them in the content.
    3. Search the keyword in Google and record the URLs of ranking content pieces.
    4. Check social share data (how often a topic is shared online) with BuzzSumo or a similar tool, and record strong pieces.
    5. Check competitor websites for content aimed at the same audience, and record it.
  • Research phase output, in order:
    1. Content topic and title.
    2. Overview with the content's purpose and goal.

    4. Similar online content that already exists for those terms.
  • Anyone who produces content must review all recorded similar online content before writing, recording or planning it.

Keyword-led weekly system

1 min

    1. Define the target market and write the customer profile (a short description of your typical buyer).

  • The email then links directly to that article page, not the homepage (the front page).

  • Keyword-based content is produced in this order:

    1. Research the keywords yourself.
    2. Create the content. Most beginners should write it themselves. Later, collaborate with niche influencers who can contribute pieces, or hire an expert.
    3. Make basic graphics yourself in Canva (an online design tool), or outsource images on Fiverr.

Article workflow and quality control

1 min

  • Content production workflow from start to finish:

    1. Choose keywords with the Ahrefs Content Gap method (a way to find keywords that competitors use and you do not). Ahrefs is an SEO tool. Then vet them (check them) with RAW.
    2. Create the article in the Google Docs template (a ready-made document layout). Brief the writer with the brand guideline (the rule book for your brand's voice).

    5. Write the headline and meta description (the short summary shown under your link on Google), often with a sales copywriter (a writer of text meant to sell).

    7. Upload the article and check its SEO with a plugin (an add-on tool) such as Yoast (an SEO add-on).
    8. Publish the article.
    9. Track the article's email signups each month. Use the best performers to find similar keywords.

Who can write

1 min
  • Professional writing ability, marketing expertise or native English fluency are not requirements for content creation. Fluency means speaking a language easily.
  • These skills can be learned over time, or outsourced (handed to outside people) to freelancers.
  • This removes a psychological barrier (a mental block) that stops many people from starting a content strategy.
  • Keep research and planning in-house (done by you or your team).
  • Decide which article titles, About Us format (the page that tells who you are) and product page elements the site needs.
  • Outsource only the writing, because the owner must control the content direction.
  • Start the content writer search on Upwork (a freelance hiring website) and specialised platforms.
  • Recognise that great general writing does not produce great dropshipping content. Dropshipping means selling products that a supplier ships straight to the buyer.
  • Pivot (switch) to Facebook groups for niche enthusiasts who understand your category.
  • They write with insider passion and knowledge that generic writers lack.
  • When outsourcing content writing, find niche enthusiasts in Facebook groups rather than professional writers.
  • Niche passion produces better content for dropshipping stores than generic writing skill.
  • If the writer has no niche knowledge, find an experienced writer in niche communities such as Reddit (a discussion website) or Facebook groups.
  • Then post a paid gig request there. (2023)
  • ProBlogger has a jobs board (a list of open jobs) where content writers can be hired for blog and written content.
  • Its community is mostly full-time freelance bloggers, usually native English speakers. This gives better applicants than general freelance platforms.

Finding writers step by step

1 min
  • Finding writers for a broad, non-technical niche:
    1. Post a content writer job on Upwork with the outline of the pieces you want.
    2. Send the same piece brief to at least three writers.
    3. Compare the three samples and pick the writer whose voice fits best.
    4. Send the remaining work to that writer.
  • Finding writers for a technical or specialised niche:
    1. Search the niche term on Facebook and open the Groups results.
    2. Join all relevant groups.
    3. Identify members who post often and whose posts get many likes and comments.
    4. Send them a private message with an offer to write articles for your site.

Hi [Name], my name is [Your name] from [your store domain]. I saw your posts in the [Facebook group name] group, and the feedback they get from the members is great. We are creating new content for our website, and I would like to know whether you would be interested in writing a few articles for us or [other content you need]. If you are interested, I can send you the outline and the list of article titles we need, and we can take it from there.

Standards for outsourced writing

2 min
  • Do not use PLR content (Private Label Rights, which let you buy content, edit it and sell it as your own).

  • A writer brand guideline (the rule book for writers) can set key factors per article.
  • Examples: a minimum of 1,000 words, three links to external sites, and the target keyword used a fixed number of times, for example three.
  • Editors use the same list to check each draft.
  • Have the writer complete the article, then have a native English editor edit it.
  • Have the person producing the content review all similar online content before writing, and make their version better.
  • Share the planner with them.
  • A virtual assistant can build the attention matrix and receive the planner.
  • Hire a content producer, such as a writer or video editor, by posting a job.
  • Give candidates a test, hire one, and produce two quality pieces of content per month.
  • For written content, find a content writer. For video, find an editor and, if someone else records, a person to do the recording.
  • Make sure a producer is ready before production starts.
  • Beginners should write their own content first. Later, collaborate with niche influencers who can contribute pieces, or hire an expert.
  • Example of a designer partner: a Fiverr designer makes the graphic for the freelance writer's piece.

  • Without that experience, do not overreach.

Cadence and Volume

2 min
  • Content marketing (writing articles and guides to attract buyers) only builds results when you publish it consistently. Irregular output loses momentum. Steady output adds up over time.
  • Produce two high-quality content pieces per month as an ongoing target, not as a one-off launch goal.
  • Target a minimum of two high-quality content pieces per month as an ongoing baseline (the lowest amount you keep doing), not as a campaign with an end date.
  • Publish about two high-quality content pieces per month. Daily publishing is not needed for a high-ticket dropshipping business (selling expensive products that a supplier ships directly to the customer). Posting more low-quality content adds no value. Quality matters more than quantity.
  • A content marketing plan of two high-quality pieces per month is a workable publishing rhythm (a steady schedule you can keep).
  • Start publishing blog posts once every two to three weeks. Publish 2-4 articles per week consistently, rather than bulk publishing (posting many at once, then nothing).
  • A blog needs about one quality post per week or every other week that is valuable to the reader. More posts do not help if the quality is low. (2025)
  • There is no fixed minimum or maximum for content articles per month. One high-quality piece per month can be a workable start, provided it is better than the existing results for its target search terms (the words people type into Google that you want to rank for). (2023)
  • Blog posting frequency: every other week is effective.
  • Publishing more content on a fixed weekly schedule does not automatically improve rankings (where your page sits in Google results). SEO (search engine optimisation, getting found in Google) gains for a new site usually build slowly, often over several months.
  • Evergreen keywords (search terms that stay popular all year) are optimised once on existing pages. Trending keywords (terms rising in searches right now) can justify one or two new blog posts per month.
  • Move from two pieces per month to more after 12 months of consistent work. For example, add one more per month, or hire another person.

Distribution and Promotion

1 min
  • Promote each content piece for at least two weeks, ideally up to 30 days. Build backlinks (links from other websites to yours) to the content pieces where it is relevant.
  • For each content piece, choose three to four promotion channels (places you share it, such as social media or email) and log them in the planner (your schedule sheet).
  • Content is one part of a connected marketing system. The same pieces feed email, SEO, link building (getting other sites to link to you) and retargeting (showing ads again to people who already visited). So promote each piece through those channels.
  • Give each piece a place on the store's website. This lets you reuse it later for link building and SEO.
  • Publish three to five blog posts on a new affiliate site (a site that earns a commission when it sends buyers to a shop) before buying traffic (paying for visitors). Then you have something to link to when you share the site elsewhere.
  • Content on social media can come from the blog. The blog provides material for social posts.

Holiday and Seasonal Content

1 min
  • Start publishing blog content for the promotion periods now. Target keywords in the form niche black friday deals and niche cyber monday deals. Put your niche (your product area) in place of the word niche. This content should be live (published and visible) before the sale dates. (2025)
  • Publish keyword-targeted blog posts (posts built around words people search for) for each holiday offer in the coming month. Use the pattern [niche] + holiday deal, for example Black Friday deals or Cyber Monday deals. Publish early so Google can index (add to its list) and rank them before the holiday searches peak.
  • Each holiday blog post should explain how to get the best deals for that holiday. It should also list which offers are available, which products they apply to, and how to take advantage of them.
  • A page built for a time-limited event, such as a homebrewing trade show (a fair where sellers meet buyers), can rank for the event name in search. After the promotion ends, keep the page as evergreen content (stays useful all year). Consider an affiliate arrangement with the organiser (the event company) for ticket sales. Or add a note that the store attends each year.

Role of Content in the Funnel

1 min

A funnel is the path from a stranger to a buyer. The wider the top, the more people enter.

Content Compared with Sales Copy

1 min
  • Educational writing that only informs, such as a general guide or blog post, is not sales copy (words written to make someone buy). It does not move a visitor toward a purchase. It can have a place on an e-commerce site (an online shop). But product, collection and sales copy should each be written to earn one defined action.
  • Authoritative, educational content in text, image, video or social form raises conversion (the share of visitors who buy). This is especially true for high-priced or technical products. It engages buyers earlier on the customer awareness path (the steps from not knowing about a problem to wanting a product) than product pages alone. You do not need hundreds of blog posts. You need more useful content than your competitors.
  • Content is a long-term investment that builds organic traffic (free visitors from search) and rankings over time. Unique product descriptions, collection page copy and blog posts should include the correct keywords and be formatted properly.

What Makes Content Worth Producing

1 min
  • Great content must be deep, relevant to what is happening now, shareable, well researched and worth linking to. Content that meets these criteria attracts attention, builds trust, shows relevance and authority, gives search engines what they reward, and makes a business look unique.
  • Original content is a competitive advantage. No competitor can copy it, which makes the business feel more unique to visitors.
  • Create unique general content such as blog posts, guest posts (articles written for other sites), and downloadable supplier catalogs, sales sheets and spec sheets (technical product data sheets). Unpublished text from supplier materials gives a large SEO advantage once you publish it on your store.

Depth Before Width

1 min
  • Shallow content (thin, surface-level pages) is common online. So producing deep content is the way to stand out in search and social channels. Search engines and consumers can see large numbers of thin pages. Depth is the differentiator (the thing that sets you apart).
  • There are over one trillion individual web pages online. Most of them are shallow, with very little substance. Because thin pages are so common, deep and comprehensive pages stand out to both search engines and consumers.
  • Content should be deep before it is wide (covering many topics). Only a small share of web content is deep, so depth is a differentiator that compounds over time. A practical test: does the piece give visitors more value than any competitor's version on the same topic?

Mixing Content Types by Funnel Stage

1 min

  • Problem-solving content is often the best starting point for a business that has no content yet. The team already knows the customer problems from running the business. It ranks well in search and YouTube. It also improves conversion by helping buyers move through the customer journey.

  • Entertainment content is the most widely engaging content. It is the main tool for filling the top of the funnel, since it does not require the visitor to be problem aware (knowing they have a problem). It should still be relevant to the target market. It should appeal mainly to that audience, not to everyone.

  • Process for using entertainment content to fill a sales funnel:

    1. Publish interest-based or best-of content that attracts relevant visitors to the website.
    2. Offer an opt-in (a sign-up form) on the page to add visitors to the email audience.
    3. Place a cookie (a small tracking file that lets ads recognise a visitor) for retargeting, so visitors can be shown ads later.
    4. Serve further content to move them through the funnel toward the desired awareness stage.

  • Example: a beard-care brand's blog mixes content types on one landing page (a single page built for one goal). It has a product-related review of nose hair trimmers (problem solving), a controversial opinion piece against neckties (entertainment), a personal-story piece about facial hair (entertainment), and a men's hat style guide (a mix of both). This combination of types is presented as a good model for a mixed blog strategy.

Priority Order

1 min

Source: Source B.

  • These six content types are the most shared and the most linked to (other sites link to them most). They lend themselves to depth. That makes them the strongest choice for SEO and engagement results. Backlinks from other sites are a key reason to favor them.
  • Focus first on how-to, listicles and ultimate guides. They are the easiest to produce and the best starting point. Best of the interwebs and expert roundups come after these.

Problem-Solving Content

1 min

Source: Source B.

  • Problem-solving content is usually the easiest type to produce. Most problems have known answers, and the product itself is typically the answer. It gives immediate value. It can engage people at the problem-aware, solution-aware and product-aware stages of the customer awareness pyramid (the stages a buyer goes through, from knowing a problem to knowing a product).

  • Types of problem-solving content:

    Type Purpose
    How-to guides Step-by-step instructions to solve a specific problem
    Ultimate guides Most comprehensive resource on a topic, helping choose products and answer purchase questions
    Product comparisons Pros and cons of specific products for middle-of-funnel buyers deciding between options
    Listicles (solution lists) Numbered lists of solutions or products to a problem, such as 'top 10 ways' or 'five ways to avoid'
    Explanatory content Simple explanations of technical terms, industry lingo or product technology to help customers understand
    FAQ content Answers to common questions, optionally combined with a listicle format, such as 'top 5 questions when shopping for X'

How-To Guides

1 min

Source: Source B.

  • How-to content, built step by step:

    1. Pick a concrete problem or pain point (something that hurts or annoys the reader). Make the piece a direct answer to it.
    2. Break the solution into logical steps, one segment per step, presented in order (step one, step two, step three and so on).
    3. Explain each step as clearly as possible in jargon-free language (plain words, no special terms), written for the reader with the most basic knowledge.
    4. Use as many images as possible. In written content, insert images. In video, show each step either with an edited static image or with a hands-on recording of the action.

  • Breaking a how-to into small, simple steps makes it feel easy for a beginner. This is what drives the best result for this content type.

Listicles

1 min

Source: Source B.

  • For listicles (articles built as numbered lists), the title contains a number and the list covers different items. They suit both problem solving and entertainment. They are among the most shared and linked content types.

  • Listicles have a low barrier to entry (they are easy to start). Most of the information already exists online or offline. The work is mainly collecting, organizing and rewriting it in a unique way, instead of copying.

  • Listicles often work well with:

    • a short introduction,
    • a table of contents with anchor links (links that jump to a section of the same page) to each section,
    • practical info such as required gear,
    • a short description per item covering location, who it suits and the conditions.

  • Listicle research before publishing:

    1. Search for the topic and see how many existing listicles already cover it.
    2. If a competitor has published a top N list (N stands for any number), publish a longer or differently sized list instead, for example top 17, top 20 or top 21.

  • Listicle numbers that are not round (for example 7, 17, 23 or 33) tend to get higher engagement (more reading and reactions) and more clicks. Round numbers such as 5, 10, 15 or 20 do worse.

  • Title pattern and examples are in Pattern Matching below.

Ultimate Guides

1 min

Source: Source B.

  • Ultimate guides are the most valuable content. They are highly linkable (other sites like to link to them). That makes them strong for problem solving and link building. Their title must be earned. If a piece is called the ultimate guide, it must be the deepest and best resource on that topic.
  • Ultimate guides can be very long, from roughly 5,000 to 10,000 words. Even when little competing content exists, write the piece as if someone could publish a better one soon.
  • Ultimate guides suit high-volume, more competitive search terms. These are words many people search for, and many sites already rank for them. They are broader and harder to rank for. These pieces are easier to build backlinks to. Such terms generally need more backlinks to reach top positions on Google.
  • An ultimate guide can combine how-to and listicle styles. For example, an ultimate guide to choosing a product works as a how-to. Or it can be an unlimited-count list where each item is covered in detail.
  • Not every piece should be an ultimate guide, because it takes more work. But some should be included in the mix.

Best of the Interwebs

1 min

Source: Source B.

  • Best of the interwebs pieces collect existing online content, which makes them quick to produce. Images can be taken from the original sources, so custom visuals are usually not needed.
  • Each item in a best of the interwebs piece needs commentary. Explain why it is good, what it does particularly well or what you like about it. A simple link with no insight is not enough. Personal opinion is acceptable.
  • Entertainment form: curated lists (lists chosen by hand) of the best blogs, YouTube channels or resources in the target niche. These bring relevant visitors to the site.

Expert Roundups

1 min

Source: Source B.

  • Expert roundup production:

    1. Choose a popular question or statement in your market.
    2. Identify experts with large audiences using the attention matrix. This means people regarded as authorities who already have audiences through websites, social media or YouTube.
    3. Find their contact details on their website or social media. Then email each one with the simple question.
    4. Contact more experts than you need, because not all will respond.
    5. Keep the question simple so that a few-line answer is enough. For example, ask for one favorite item.
    6. Build the piece around each answer. Add a short explanation and a relevant image sourced from the internet.
    7. When published, email the participants with a thank-you, a link and a request to share the piece.

  • Expert roundups use experts' names and trust to attract attention to your piece. This is useful when your own name is not yet trusted in the market.

  • An expert roundup should state in its headline that it is based on expert feedback. Leaving that out weakens the piece.

  • Expert roundups can take a few days to a couple of weeks, because the work depends on experts replying.

  • Roundup content gains reach through reciprocity (returning a favour). After publishing, send the contributing experts the link and ask them to share it with their audience. This lets the business use their audience to gain exposure.

  • Roundup content in the entertainment table: expert quotes gathered by emailing experts for a short comment. The published roundup is shared with the experts, who often share it with their audience.

Controversial Content

1 min

Source: Source B.

  • Controversial content takes a position that goes against commonly held beliefs. Use it sparingly, because it is highly engaging but polarizing (it splits people into strongly for and strongly against).
  • A controversial position must be explained in depth, with supporting evidence and a real attempt to convince the reader. A fake position cannot be defended in depth, so do not use it.
  • Controversial content in the entertainment table: taking an opposing position on a commonly held belief in the market to attract attention.

Entertainment Content Formats

1 min

Source: Source B.

  • Types of entertainment content:

    Type Purpose
    Interest listicles Lists about a topic of interest to the audience, such as top surf breaks or top destinations, without selling products
    Best-of-the-internet lists Curated lists of the best blogs, YouTube channels or resources in the target niche, bringing relevant visitors to the site
    Roundup content Expert quotes gathered by emailing experts for a short comment; the published roundup is shared with the experts, who often share it with their audience
    Breaking news content Coverage of new developments in the market; high engagement at the time but not evergreen, so weak for long-term ranking

Idea Sourcing

1 min

Keyword Research as the Source

Source: Source A (no year given).

  • Take topic ideas for pages and blog posts from the keyword research worksheet created during organic keyword research, not from guesses.

Community and Audience Questions

Source: Source A (no year given).

  • Blog post ideas can come from the most popular threads on community sites such as Reddit within a niche. Note which questions get the most engagement and turn them into content candidates.

Source: Source C.

  • A public container such as a blog or website listing content in reverse chronological order keeps you producing because the newest item is what visitors see first. Use it also as a place to develop ideas that are not finished yet.

Platform Analytics and Search Questions

Source: Source E.

  • Rising volume of AI-generated, recycled and syndicated content causes audience fatigue, and attention is split between creators, streaming services and daily life. Content needs a clear strategy to stop the scroll and hold viewers.
  • Idea-sourcing procedure:
    1. Open the analytics of the main platform, TikTok being the most detailed for audience insight, and Instagram analytics for audience behaviour.
    2. Read the search terms your audience uses and the videos they interact with.
    3. Note the questions behind those searches.
    4. Build videos that answer these questions before the viewer asks them.

Problem-Based Ideas

Source: Source B.

  • Before producing any piece of content, follow these steps:
    1. Research what already exists on the topic by searching for it and reviewing who has covered it.
    2. Identify which aspects the existing top content has not covered.
    3. Plan how to go deeper and provide more value than the existing pieces.
    4. Produce the content so it aims to be the most comprehensive resource online on that topic.

Competitor and SERP Research

1 min

Reviewing Existing Coverage

Source: Source B.

  • Listicle research: search for the topic, count how many existing listicles cover it, and if a competitor has published a top N list, publish a longer or differently sized list instead (for example top 17, top 20 or top 21).
  • Ultimate guides target high-volume, competitive terms that need more backlinks to reach top positions on Google.
  • Shallow content is common, so a depth-first approach is the way to stand out against what already ranks.
  • Gap test before production: find what the top content misses, go deeper than it, and aim to be the most comprehensive resource.

Competitor and Keyword Data

Source: Source A.

  • Six-month done-for-you content plan process:
    1. Research the niche, the site, the competitors and the keywords.
    2. Build a content marketing plan from that research.
    3. Write blog posts and source images.
    4. Publish the content on a schedule over six months to build organic traffic from Google.

Pattern Matching

1 min

Listicle Titles

Source: Source B.

  • Listicle title pattern: [Number] + [best/top] + [item] + [location or qualifier] + [audience or experience level qualifier]. The qualifier at the end widens the appeal to a segment, such as beginners, advanced riders or first-time visitors, while the base title still captures the broad search.
    • Travel niche example: "11 Best Hiking Trails in Colorado for Every Fitness Level"
    • Finance niche example: "19 Best Budget Apps for Freelancers Who Are Just Starting Out"
  • Use non-round numbers such as 7, 17, 23 or 33, which tend to earn higher engagement and more clicks than 5, 10, 15 or 20.
  • Listicle structure: short introduction, table of contents with anchor links, practical info such as required gear, and a short description per item covering location, who it suits and the conditions.

Trend Patterns

Source: Source E.

  • Trend search example: a skincare brand checks the search terms in TikTok analytics and finds a viral technique, such as slugging, being searched for often. The brand then builds videos that answer the questions viewers are about to ask, before they search for them.

Content Planner

1 min

Starting Plan Structure

Source: Source A.

  • The six-month plan runs research, a content marketing plan, writing and sourcing images, then scheduled publishing.

Source: Source B.

  • Content planning method: for the first 12 ideas, think of a few different versions of each content type and include them in the plan.

Content Pillars

Source: Source E.

  • Each content pillar should align with the brand's expertise, its business goals and what the audience cares about. Pillars keep content balanced, prevent random posting, simplify monthly planning and show which formats to scale.
  • Content pillars are assigned to fixed weekdays. Two pillars per week is the minimum, five pillars fit daily posting Monday to Friday, and seven pillars are possible but usually too demanding.
  • Test a set of content pillars for two months, then review performance data. In the next month, increase the posting frequency of the pillars that perform well and reduce the frequency of those that do not.
  • Posting without pillars leads to disorganized content and makes batch production much harder. Without a system, the creator also loses motivation more easily.

Flexibility in the Plan

Source: Source E.

  • Keep some flexibility in the schedule, for example one fixed weekday for trend-based videos. Trend slots are harder to batch but allow a fast reaction to trends while the rest of the plan stays fixed.

Batching and Production Rhythm

Source: Source E.

  • Batching means filming a whole series in one session. For example, two hours of filming can produce ten videos for one weekday slot, covering the next ten weeks.

Writer Throughput

Source: Source B.

  • Ask writers to focus on one product category per week, for example twelve collection pages for similar products, so they keep the same subject and write faster. A writer needing about 1.5 hours per 500-word page can usually finish about two pages per day. Plan about twelve content items per week per writer as a starting point.
  • Writing a 500-word collection page took a writer about 1.5 hours, so a typical day allows two or three pages. Set weekly targets based on this measured rate, then adjust after one week of real results. Do not expect a 500-word article in half an hour.

Writers and Outsourcing

1 min

Weekly Assignment Cards

Source: Source B.

  • Assign weekly content work to a writer using this structure:

    1. Create a weekly project card, for example for a specific week in October.
    2. Add the target keywords in the card description.
    3. Set a length, such as 300 to 500 words for collection or product page content.
    4. Attach the link to each collection or product page to be written.
    5. Move each card to the done list when the writer finishes, so progress is visible.

  • Create blog or buying guide tasks using this structure:

    1. Write the blog name or topic.
    2. Add reference sources, such as manufacturer pages, reviews or related blogs.
    3. List the product links that must appear.
    4. Set the target keywords and the desired length.
    5. Move the card to done when published.

Writer Capacity

Source: Source B.

  • Plan about twelve content items per week per writer as a starting point, one product category per week, adjusted after one week of measured results.

1 min

Example titles are [niche] Black Friday deals and [niche] Cyber Monday deals, each explaining the offer, when it runs, which products it covers and how to use it.

Access control and account security for helpers

Section 20.15 · 15 of 26 in this chapter · 9 min read

In short

  • Hire a helper only when you can pay them and doing the work yourself no longer makes sense.
  • Give a new helper small access first. Add more only after they prove reliable.
  • Give each outside person a limited role, not full admin. Do not share one login between people working at the same time.
  • Limit helpers' access to money and customer data.
  • Do not give freelancers direct store access. Use them only for separate jobs that need no store access.
This section has 24 parts. Tap one to open it.

When to add a team member

1 min
  • A new store starts with one user: the owner.
  • Hire staff only when the business can afford to pay them. Also hire only when doing the work yourself is no longer the better option.
  • A virtual assistant (VA, a remote helper) may steal or copy your work. This risk is real. [L245.055]
  • The risk should not stop you from hiring. [L245.055]
  • Reduce the risk by vetting thoroughly, starting small and building trust gradually. Do not avoid delegating (handing off work) altogether. [L245.055]

Vetting and phasing in a new VA

1 min
  1. Learn about the person through interviews and reference checks (asking people who worked with them before). [L245.054]
  2. Start with a small, low-risk project to test reliability (doing what they promise) and character (honesty). [L245.054]
  3. Gradually increase access to business systems (the tools you run the business on) and information over time. [L245.054]
  4. Do not hand over all sensitive information at once to someone you do not know. [L245.054]

First access for a new VA

1 min
  • Begin with limited access: sales, orders and the support mailbox (the inbox that receives customer questions) only. This tests how the working relationship goes.
  • Extend access to other tools only after the assistant has delivered reliable work over a set period. The sources do not state how long that period is.

Account types and permission levels

1 min

A permission is one thing a person is allowed to do, such as editing products.

Shopify staff accounts

1 min
  • Under Settings > Users, staff accounts give team members, such as marketers or designers, access to the store.
  • Shopify Staff Accounts (Shopify's feature for separate team logins) lets the owner invite team members with their own logins and permissions.
  • Use it to give helpers access without sharing the main owner account.
  • A staff account added through the invite form has full permissions, so it can change the whole store.
  • Only add people you trust with full access.

Adding a staff account (2021-22 plan, older)

1 min
  1. Open Settings and go to Plan and permissions, which is the Account page in the Shopify admin (the back office where you run your store).
  2. In the Staff section, click Add staff. Check the staff count, which on this plan is limited to 2 (shown as Staff (0 of 2)).
  3. Enter the First Name and Last Name of the staff member and the email address from their welcome email.
  4. This staff account will have full permissions.
  5. Click Send invite.
  6. The staff member accepts the invitation and works on the store through their own Shopify Partners account (Shopify's free developer account).

Two paths conflict:

  • Older guidance (2021-22) uses Settings > Plan and permissions.
  • Newer guidance uses Settings > Users.
  • Use whichever path matches your admin screen.

Shopify collaborator accounts

1 min
  • Collaborator accounts (limited logins for outside helpers) let a developer, designer or marketer enter your Shopify admin without taking one of your staff account slots.
  • Collaborators do not count toward the staff limit.
  • Use collaborator accounts for freelancers who upload products.
  • Shopify's collaborator code feature grants limited upload access only. It protects payment methods, store settings and account security.
  • Never share your Shopify login with freelancers who upload products.

Giving an outside party access (supplier, 3PL (a company that stores and ships your orders), agency or support staff)

1 min
  1. Go to Settings > Users and open the organization account security page (the page that holds security settings for your whole account).
  2. Click Generate new code.
  3. Share the code together with the store's domain (your web address) or store name identifier with the person who needs access.
  4. The outside party sends a collaborator request using that code.
  5. Review the request in your Shopify admin and approve it.
  6. Assign the appropriate role (a ready-made set of permissions) after approval.

Two options conflict on request type:

  • Older guidance (2021-22) lists two options: let any person send a collaborator request, or accept requests only from people who hold a collaborator request code.
  • The current procedure uses the code.
  • Use the code option.

Shared logins

1 min
  • Do not share one login across several people working at the same time.
  • A second login on the same account logs the first user out, with a message that another user has signed in from a different device.
  • For tools with no financial or customer data, a shared login can be used. Example: a shared Canva account (a design tool) with extra seats (paid spots, one per user) for multiple users.
  • Individual seats are the safer option when more people need access.

Tool access for VAs: conflicting guidance

1 min

Sources differ on how far VA access should go. Each rule fits a different situation.

Situation Guidance Source Date
VA needs the tools for daily work Give access to all tools they need, including system logins (logins to business tools). Accepting this risk is necessary for the work to function. Undated
VA must place orders or pay suppliers Give system logins plus the relevant bank account details and credit cards. Without these they cannot complete ordering or payments. Misuse is considered uncommon in practice. Undated
VA places orders and decides on supplier purchases Give access to supplier pricing and ordering so they can decide and place orders on your behalf. Trust and information access are needed for them to work well. 2018
Shopify store, financial data and customer data Use a staff or collaborator account with limited, view-only access (they can look but not change) to the sections they need. Do not give admin login. Restrict access to financial and customer data. 2024
Supplier orders VA gets passwords, supplier price lists, contracts and credit card details needed to place supplier orders. Undated
Owner's bank or personal financial accounts Not given to the VA. Undated
Profit margin data (what is left after costs) Not given to the VA, since it is not needed for daily tasks. Undated

How the sources fit together

1 min

The undated broad-access rules and the 2024 restriction meet at Shopify and financial or customer data. The 2024 restriction applies there. The broad rules apply to the other tools the job needs. The 2018 rule is older, and it matches the undated ordering rules.

Discount and decision limits for VAs

1 min
  • The VA may grant discounts of up to 10% without asking the owner, since all suppliers accept that margin.
  • Any larger discount or other decision needs the owner's approval.

Protecting finances and sensitive data

1 min
  • Restrict access to financial and customer data for every helper.
  • Billing history, meaning past payments to Shopify, is shown under Settings > Billing.
  • If several team members use the P&L (profit and loss sheet, a report of money in and money out), or it holds sensitive internal data, create a separate restricted-access sheet.
  • Share only the read-only output (results people can view but not edit) with the team.
  • Keep transaction-level data (single payment or sale records) isolated.

Maximum security for sensitive financial data used with AI tools

1 min
  1. Use an old cleared laptop (one wiped of old data) with a fresh OS (operating system, the core software, freshly installed).
  2. Create a brand new Google Drive account (online file storage) on that device.
  3. Create a separate Google Drive for this project only.
  4. Grant sheet access only to this new email address.
  5. Store this laptop offline (not connected to the internet) except during AI sessions.

Reviewing who has access

1 min
  • In Business settings, the Users list shows every person with access to the business portfolio (the business account that holds your ads and pages).
  • The Last active column shows who has been working in the portfolio.
  • The Invite people button adds new users, and the Export button downloads the list.
  • Use this list to review access before adding or removing people.

Storing login details

1 min
  • Keep the login details for every tool the team uses, such as the phone system Mighty Call (a business phone app) and Shopify, on one card in the corporate information board (a shared page for company information).
  • Staff can then find access details without asking the owner. Forgotten passwords stop blocking work.
  • Limit who can view that board, because it holds credentials (login details).

Freelancers and outside parties

1 min
  • Giving freelancers direct access to the store is dangerous. One wrong click can damage the entire store.
  • A Fiverr freelancer (from Fiverr, an online marketplace for freelance work) was found to have copied a logo instead of creating one.
  • Limit store access to trusted staff.
  • Use freelancers only for isolated tasks (separate jobs) that do not need store access.

Fixing access problems

1 min

When a team member makes themselves a domain owner

1 min
  1. Remove the meta tag (a hidden code line in a web page's header) they used to claim ownership.
  2. Message them calmly and ask them to remove themselves as an owner as soon as possible.
  3. Grant only the access they need, and ask them to request any future access in advance.

Message template

1 min 
Hi [name], I just got an email from Google saying you were added as an owner of [store domain]. If you need extra access, let me know and I will grant what is needed. Please remove yourself as an owner of the domain as soon as possible. In future, if you need more access to any tool, ask me in advance.

Keeping the response calm

1 min
  • Most access problems from team members come from carelessness rather than intent.
  • Keep the response calm and fix the access, so the working relationship stays intact.

Further details

1 min
  1. When hiring a virtual assistant, learn about the person through interviews and reference checks.
  2. Start with a small, low-risk project to assess reliability and character.
  3. Gradually increase access to different business systems and information over time.
  4. Do not hand over all sensitive information immediately to someone you do not know.
  • The risk of a VA stealing or copying is real, but it should not prevent hiring. Reduce it by vetting thoroughly, starting small, and building trust gradually. Do not avoid delegating entirely.

Managing the team: communication, performance and offboarding

Section 20.9 · 9 of 26 in this chapter · 18 min read

In short

  • Managing people is its own skill. Learn it before you grow the team.
  • Stay reachable with a short daily check-in, and answer questions at set times instead of all day.
  • Judge staff by results, not hours. Check whether the work is done.
  • Give criticism calmly and factually. Never make it personal.
  • Sick or planned days off: the owner covers the work. The VA only reports the absence.
More key points
  • Cheating, faking time or lying: the person is fired at once. There are no second chances.
  • Missed deadlines: send a written warning first. If it happens again, end the work.
  • Late work: check your own instructions before blaming the person.

Management framework and the manager's role

1 min
  • Managing staff is a separate skill from building a store. Plan to learn it before you scale the team. Older advice (2022).
  • Outsourcing (handing work to outside helpers) is not a magic fix.
  • After you hire someone, your job changes. You now have to manage that person.
  • Budget time for management. Do not expect the helper to run the business alone.
  • Outsourcing does not mean being absent.
  • Stay reachable for questions.
  • A short daily check-in is enough.
  • Communication runs through chat apps such as Skype and Slack (messaging tools).
  • Hold a video call or phone call to catch up about every two weeks.
  • Give criticism in a clear, factual and professional way, with few emotions.
  • Give it the same way you would to an employee.
  • Personal criticism can make spouses and partners feel attacked. This reduces cooperation.
  • Hire A players (top performers).
  • Remove C and D players (weak performers) from the team.
  • Many owners who think they have B players (average performers) actually have C and D performers. Fixing this is the key to making the team work. Older advice (2021-22).

Four management stages

  • Manage each new hire in four stages: onboarding (the first setup and training), communication, performance review and rewards.
  • Set clear expectations for each stage. Older advice (2021-22).
  • The four stages only need to meet basic principles.
  • You do not have to follow one exact method.
  • Meeting those basic needs at each stage is enough for successful outsourcing. Older advice (2021-22).

Working hours and availability

1 min
  • Set fixed schedules for all team members.
  • This way, everyone knows when colleagues are available to work together.
  • Example: 8 AM Eastern to 12 PM, 2 PM to 6 PM Pacific. (Eastern and Pacific are US time zones, meaning different clock times.)
  • Say clearly which hours each person works.
  • Then the team can rely on each other and work together in Asana (a task tool) and Slack.
  • Flexible working hours are fine as long as the assigned tasks get done.
  • Example: starting early and leaving later around an appointment.
  • Watch whether the task list is completed, not how many hours are logged.
  • Set business hours for phone and messages.
  • The VA (virtual assistant, a remote helper) answers calls Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 17:00.
  • Messages that arrive outside those hours are handled the next business day.
  • This stops the owner from working around the clock.
  • Time zones (clock differences between places) matter for Pakistan-based assistants.
  • Switzerland is about 3 hours ahead of Pakistan.
  • The UK is about 4 to 8 hours ahead.
  • Plan overlapping working hours accordingly.
  • How the hour rules fit together:
  • Fixed schedules define when colleagues can collaborate.
  • Flexibility applies to the rest of the time, as long as the tasks are done.
  • The sources do not describe a conflict between them.

Reply windows and contact rules

2 min

Why reply windows matter **

  • A reply window is a set time when you answer questions.
  • Letting staff call whenever they want interrupts your focus, the same way unmanaged customer calls do.
  • Swapping one constant interruption for another gains nothing.
  • Answering questions the moment they arrive breaks your workflow and lowers productivity (the amount of work you get done).
  • Set the reply habit from day one.
  • A habit of constant availability is hard to reverse later.
  • Most questions from VAs do not need an instant answer.
  • About 80% can wait for your scheduled reply times.

Emergency exception

  • Allow immediate contact only in real emergencies.
  • Example: a family emergency that stops a key staff member, such as the main customer service contact, from working.
  • For everything else, most questions from assistants can wait for your scheduled reply times. Older (2021-22).
  • Only a real emergency justifies immediate contact.
  • Example: a key staff member being unable to work. Older (2021-22).

Channels and group chat

  • Communicate mainly through written messages in tools such as Skype or Slack.
  • Keep WhatsApp as a backup.
  • Make sure the whole team can reach each other. Older (2021-22).
  • Day-to-day communication with a VA or freelancer should be about 80% written messages.
  • Not phone or video calls.
  • Reserve calls for rare cases. Calls interrupt work and take more time. Older (2021-22).
  • Create one group chat for the whole team in Skype or Slack.
  • Staff, especially in the Philippines, value feeling part of a team.
  • The group chat builds connection between people who work for you. Older (2021-22).

Reply template **

The following message tells the team when you will reply. Use it unchanged, or adapt the time to your own reply window.

Good morning everyone, I hope you are all doing well and have a great start to the day. I will check messages again around 3 pm today, so please send anything urgent before then. Have a good day.

Team culture and communication between members

1 min
  • Encourage team members to talk to each other, not only to the owner.
  • This makes the group feel like a team, instead of a set of people who each receive a few tasks per week.
  • Build a culture where members propose unconventional ideas (ideas outside the usual approach) every day.
  • Do not say no automatically.
  • Judge each idea by its risk and its possible downside.
  • If the risk is low, test the idea and measure its impact on the brand.
  • This leadership behavior keeps the team ahead of trends and enables real innovation.
  • When staff or contractors suggest a way to make a repeated task more efficient, take the suggestion seriously.
  • Do not shut it down.
  • Ignoring such input keeps inefficiencies in place and discourages people from improving the work.
  • Tell the team that mistakes are acceptable if they are reported honestly and owned (taken responsibility for).
  • Respond to errors by simplifying the process, so the same mistake is less likely.
  • Do not respond by getting angry.
  • Including freelancers in team meetings gives them a sense of belonging.
  • It makes them feel part of a business worth being proud of.
  • This loyalty helps the business in return.

Daily routine

1 min

Daily VA routine

  1. The VA signs in each morning and posts a good-morning message stating availability (when they can work) and the day's plan.
  2. The owner reviews the plan and gives direction.
  3. Both sides keep a back-and-forth conversation during the day. If no reply comes, the owner asks promptly what the VA is working on.
  4. The owner asks about the VA's day to build the relationship and keep them engaged.

Daily communication and trust

  • Communicate with virtual assistants every day, for example through Slack.
  • Once trust is established, give them authority (permission) to decide and fix problems without asking first.
  • Review their decisions at the end of the month.

The sources do not tie the meeting frequencies to one team size. Use the twice-weekly and two-calls-per-week rhythms for a working team of assistants. Add the monthly team meeting and monthly Kaizen once the team grows and includes freelancers.

Team meeting procedure **

  1. Hold a team meeting twice a week, on Monday and Thursday mornings.
  2. Appoint one assistant as team lead who attends every meeting.
  3. Check in with each assistant by call several times a week. Review the current state of the business with them.
  4. Treat each assistant as a regular member of the company, so they feel part of the team.

Monthly Kaizen procedure

  1. Hold the meeting once a month with the owner, all full-time assistants and all freelancers, including accounting and graphics contractors.
  2. Ask each person what is working, what is not working and what should be done to fix it.
  3. Turn the answers into actions for the business.

Reporting structures

1 min
  • Require daily reporting from direct reports (your direct employees).
  • Use Asana, a doc file, or written bullet points.
  • Any system is fine, but check it consistently.
  • If you don't check reports, people stop trying.
  • Review, verify and give feedback on progress, so people stay accountable and improve.
  • Create different reporting structures by role.
  • Each role needs metrics (numbers that show performance) that drive behavior and visibility.
Role Reporting Metrics
Sales team Daily performance reports, incentive-based compensation (pay linked to results) Daily performance
Product listing Monthly or quarterly metrics Products listed, product options improved
Operations Order processing metrics, task completion in Asana Order processing, task completion

Asana order reporting procedure (Asana Reports, paid version, $10 per user)

  1. Create a task for every order. A task is a single to-do item. Task title = order number + customer name/email.
  2. A single team member owns each order from placement through delivery.
  3. Others collaborate via task comments (short notes on a task), such as shipping updates or supplier issues.
  4. The Reports tab automatically shows completed orders (last 4 weeks, 7 days, yesterday) and open tasks per person. It can be filtered by individual.
  5. Show team members their completed tasks and open task count to keep them motivated and aware.
  6. Show the operations manager only the Upcoming tasks next 8 weeks view, hidden from the team, to see who is overloaded and needs help.

KPIs, OKRs and targets

1 min
  • Each team member needs a clear mission, company goals and a personal KPI (key performance indicator, a number that tracks success).
  • Review progress in a weekly meeting covering both company and individual targets. Older advice (2020).
  • Set OKRs (Objectives and Key Results, a goal plus measurable results) for 6 to 12 month periods.
  • Conduct check-ins and reshape them every 6 months based on progress.
  • OKRs align your team to the business vision and leadership goals.
  • They also create transparency across the organization about priorities.
  • Plan staff tasks three to six months ahead.
  • Set aside a full working day for the planning session.
  • Load each person's weekly targets through the end of the year, so they know their work well in advance.
  • Planning this far out reduces daily decisions about what staff should do next. Older advice (2021-22).

Measuring performance

2 min

Measure results, not hours

  • Monitor virtual assistant performance by output (all customer emails answered, all tasks completed) rather than hours worked.
  • Measure the VA by results rather than hours.
  • As long as all emails, messages and calls are handled, the exact working hours and breaks are not tracked.
  • Downtime can be filled with assigned tasks in Asana.
  • Manage remote team members by results.
  • Check whether the task is done, not screenshots (pictures of the screen) or time tracking (logging hours).
  • If output is repeatedly late, first check whether the training or instructions were unclear.
  • Only then conclude the person is not right for the role.
  • Heavy time-tracking software such as Time Doctor (a tool that logs work time) did not work well with part-time staff.
  • Part-time staff often stopped using it or left.
  • Daily reports and detailed tracking created friction without improving results.
  • Measure weekly output instead of tracking hours. Older (2021-22).
  • Measure staff by weekly results, not by the process they use.
  • If the agreed number of content items or uploads is finished by the end of the week, the method does not matter.
  • Only follow up when weekly targets are missed. Older (2021-22).

Judging speed and effort

  • Before judging a remote helper's speed, estimate how long the task takes you to do well, including photos and details.
  • If a task that takes you about one hour is still unfinished after half a day, the cause is usually unclear training or a misunderstanding.
  • It is not necessarily laziness.
  • Check the instructions and the training material first, then decide.
  • A staff member reported ten or twelve hours for a task that produced only a few items in a week. This showed poor time estimates.
  • Ask for an explanation of time spent.
  • Consider replacing staff whose output does not match their reported effort.
  • Clear expectations at the start prevent this. Older (2021-22).

Detail orientation

  • Repeatedly missing details, such as missing tags (keywords on a product page) or meta descriptions (short summary text that search engines show), is a sign of low detail orientation.
  • After several repeated misses despite clear instructions, replace the team member.

Reviews and performance improvement plans

2 min

Review cadence

  • Hold regular review catch-ups with each team member.
  • In them, review their work, raise improvement points and recognize good work.
  • Also cover changes to systems or processes, and let them suggest improvements. Older (2021-22).
  • Start with a review about every two weeks.
  • Then move to monthly.
  • Once a person is long-term, you can reduce it to quarterly (every three months).
  • A half-hour meeting is usually enough. Older (2021-22).
  • Set regular review dates with each team member about once per month, as the default cadence. Older (2021-22).

Reviewing work procedure **

  1. Set regular review dates with each team member individually, about once per month. Put them in your calendar and theirs.
  2. If you have a larger team, also set a time for team meetings. These are more general and cover reviewing work and seeking feedback on processes and opportunities.
  3. Stay positive and focus on developing people, not punishing them.
  4. Where performance is below standard, ask why. Ask what the individual needs to improve, then give them time to improve.
  5. If improvement is not visible, end the arrangement.

Individual review meeting procedure **

  • First catch up on the person and their life, such as their family, studies or other commitments.
  • Then go through a prepared agenda of points to discuss.
  • If they are doing well, say so clearly and ask for any further improvement suggestions.

Performance improvement procedure **

  1. At the end-of-month performance meeting, identify one aspect of the work to improve.
  2. Agree on a plan together by asking what they need to make the improvement happen.
  3. Set a target, such as improving that area by the end of the next month. Confirm they understand and accept it.
  4. Give them the time to improve. For a high-impact task where poor performance cannot last, use a shorter period.
  5. If improvement is not visible, end the arrangement.

  • Staff who answer calls should be monitored closely at the start, by recording calls where legal.

  • For flexible hours, watch whether the task list is completed rather than how many hours are logged.

Written working hours

1 min
  • Give each VA written working hours and duties. If they are not present as expected, end the work and hire a replacement, with a firm zero-tolerance policy (no second chances). Older (2021).

Warnings, termination and hiring

1 min

Termination for culture violations

1 min
  • Automatic termination (ending the job at once, without waiting) for a business culture violation (breaking the rules of honest work): cheating (faking time tracking or falsifying reports), fraud, or dishonesty. No second chances. When trust is broken at this level, the person cannot stay. This is an automatic line in the sand (a rule that is never crossed). Say it clearly during onboarding (the first days of setting someone up).

Yellow card procedure

1 min
  1. Note missed deadlines or quality problems. Refer to what was agreed.
  2. Send a written warning, called a yellow card (a written warning with a name), that states the expectation.
  3. If the problem repeats, end the work relationship.
  4. Dismiss someone only with stated reasons and prior warnings. Never for reasons such as lack of money.

Early termination and hiring

1 min
  • Expect to let go 30-35 people on the way to building a 35-person team. Most early terminations (ending the job early) come from poor onboarding, unclear SOPs (standard operating procedures, which are written step-by-step instructions), or poor standards. As you improve systems, processes and expectations, fewer people fail. The mistakes are yours. Learn from them and improve your hiring and onboarding.
  • If a VA does not work out despite careful hiring, let them go early, not late. A quick replacement costs less than a long poor fit. Hire slowly, dismiss quickly. Older (2019).
  • Repeatedly missing details despite clear instructions leads to replacement.

Offboarding

1 min

Ongoing staff offboarding procedure

1 min

Offboarding means the steps to take when someone leaves.

  1. Before telling them, remove their access to everything (the right to log in), ideally overnight. This includes Shopify (online store software), Google Drive (file storage) and any other accounts.
  2. Remove access to shared payment cards. If the person had card details, order a new credit card.
  3. Remove access to the phone system, if they had it.
  4. Remove them from all team group chats.
  5. Send a short written message: the job is ending because the required improvement did not happen. Refer to the earlier talk.
  6. Pay out any remaining pay. Do not hold a call to explain the decision.

Project-based freelancers

1 min
  • For project-based freelancers (people paid per job, not in an ongoing role), you can simply stop giving them work, with no conversation or message. Older (2021-22).

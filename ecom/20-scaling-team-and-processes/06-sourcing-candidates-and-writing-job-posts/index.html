Sourcing candidates and writing job posts

Section 20.6 · 6 of 26 in this chapter · 25 min read

In short

  • Pick the right site: OnlineJobs.ph for long-term helpers, Upwork for short projects.
  • Write a clear job post with tasks, pay, hours and tools.
  • Filter applicants with a simple question and a test before you hire.
  • Never hire the cheapest helper. Poor work can seriously harm the business.
  • Shortlist first. Check the four points: followed instructions, real experience, attention to detail, and good language.
More key points
  • Interview only 3 to 5 people.
  • Give a small paid trial task before any long-term deal.
  • Check reviews and references before you hire.

Writing the job post

1 min

Principles

1 min
  • A job post should be simple and direct.
  • State only what is needed, with no fluff or hype.
  • Clear posts make it easier for candidates to understand the role.
  • Clear posts also reduce unsuitable applications.
  • State expectations, pay and weekly hours clearly.
  • Clear terms attract good candidates and filter out weak ones.
  • Write a moderate job description.
  • Prefer candidates with less experience. You can then train them to your own processes instead of hiring someone with fixed habits.
  • State in the post that an editor will help the writer improve.
  • Good writers value feedback, and this attracts stronger applicants.
  • Writers choose clients who signal they will develop their skills.

Required contents (version A, Source B)

1 min

Include the following in every job posting:

  1. Job title or position name.
  2. Position description.
  3. Proposed pay rate, which can be a starting point rather than the final agreed rate.
  4. Location preference, if one is required.
  5. Hours per week and any specific working hours, such as business hours in your country.
  6. Whether weekend work is required.
  7. Required software or tools and the existing skills needed, for example Photoshop (image editing software) for image editing or existing Shopify knowledge.

Required contents (version B, Source A)

1 min

A detailed job post should cover:

  1. Responsibilities, such as phone answering, email, live chat (real-time text chat with customers), and whether the assistant works with other assistants.
  2. Required skills, such as sales ability, product knowledge, and whether the owner will train a less experienced person at a lower rate.
  3. Working hours and the time zone (the clock region) they must work in.
  4. Pay rate.
  5. Work environment, such as a quiet space without background noise, for phone roles.
  6. Hardware requirements, such as a working computer.
  7. Availability and any fixed commitments the candidate has.
  8. A link to the store, asking whether the candidate can see themselves selling these products and talking about them.
  9. An instruction to reply on the first line that they have read the full job posting. This filters out applicants who skim.

A detailed post on Online Jobs PH or Upwork can be used the same way.

Upwork job post setup

1 min

Create the Upwork job posting in this order:

  1. Select the category Customer Service.
  2. Select the subcategory Customer Service.
  3. Enter a job title and describe the work, for example handling live chat and emails.
  4. List the responsibilities the assistant will carry, such as email and live chat handling.
  5. Select the skills for email and live chat.
  6. Enter the required working hours and the time zone.
  7. Choose the payment type: hourly when first testing someone, fixed price once a good candidate is found.
  8. Choose Entry-level as the experience level.
  9. Answer the additional questions on expected duration (for example more than six months) and hours per week.
  10. Set the freelancer preferences and screening filters described in the following rules.
  11. Add the candidate questions described in the screening procedure.
  12. Publish the posting.

Upwork settings for an email, live chat and order processing role:

  • Experience level: Entry-level is the correct choice for a VA who answers emails, handles live chat and processes orders, because this is an entry-level role.
  • Freelancer type: Independent.
  • Job success score (Upwork's rating of how well a freelancer finished past jobs): set the filter to 90% or higher and reject any candidate below 90%.
  • Freelancers who take on too much work tend to miss deadlines and see their score drop.
  • Minimum experience on Upwork: 100 hours, which shows the candidate has already done some work on the platform.
  • Location: does not matter for an email, live chat and order processing role.
  • English level: set to Fluent.
  • Do not require Native or Bilingual, since the Fluent filter already includes these candidates.
  • Expect applications within the first 30 minutes after posting, and many of them.
  • Plan time to review them quickly.

Keyword and phrase instructions in the post

1 min
  • Include a keyword filter (a required phrase that shows the applicant read the post) in the job title or description.
  • Example wording: "To apply successfully, include [specific phrase] in your application" (for example, "include your best color").
  • A keyword filter filters out low-effort applicants automatically.
  • Ask applicants to put a specific phrase, for example "customer service is my favorite", in the subject line of their application.
  • Replace keyword filtering with an Asana form (an online form that feeds Asana, a task tool), which is the modern approach.
  • Direct applicants to complete the Asana form to apply successfully.
  • Form responses become Asana tasks with the candidate's email as the task name.
  • Customize 6 key screening questions in the form.

Location and time zone

1 min
  • Location only needs to be stated on platforms that are not location-limited.
  • On OnlineJobs.ph all candidates are based in the Philippines.
  • On platforms such as FreeUp or Upwork, a required location (for example, the US) must be written into the post.
  • Time zone matters, but it is not decisive.
  • A location close to the target market's time zone makes working hours easier to share.
  • For example, the Philippines is three to four hours from Australia, depending on daylight saving.
  • Factor a VA's time zone and location into the hire.

  • Regions with a similar time zone can reduce friction for managers.

Application filters and screening tricks

1 min

Filters that run before any interview

1 min
  • Simple instructions for how to submit the application and what it must contain are the first screening layer.
  • They show whether a candidate reads and follows directions.
  • Candidates who ignore the submission format or required content should not be interviewed.
  • Ignoring simple instructions before hiring predicts ignoring SOPs after hiring.
  • Ask a screening question in the Upwork application, such as a simple arithmetic question like 2+2.
  • Applicants who do not answer it have likely not read the job description and can be ignored.
  • A correct answer to a screening question shows that an applicant read the full job description and is serious about the work.
  • Skipping the screening question is a sign of random, mass applications.
  • A simple screening question, such as a basic arithmetic question, shows whether an applicant read the full job description.
  • Applicants who skip screening questions are usually applying at random and can be ignored.
  • Filter out everyone who did not answer the screening questions.
  • Hidden secret word: add a random secret word, for example "tuna fish", to the post with a request to include it in the reply email.
  • Discard applicants who omit the word, since they most likely did not read the post.
  • Secret-word filter result: it reduced hundreds of VA applications to three qualified candidates.
  • The remaining candidates were built into a long-term working relationship.
  • Easter egg (a hidden instruction that tests attention): ask applicants to add a small picture of a purple giraffe at the top of their application, and reject those who miss it.
  • Most applicants miss this instruction, so it separates careful candidates from others.
  • Current employment question: "Do you currently hold a similar position in another company? If yes, what type of clients/products are you responsible for?"
  • This is the most selective question.
  • Candidates answering "yes" should not advance.
  • You need people fully committed to your company, not part-timers juggling multiple employers.

Screening tests after the first filter

1 min

Criteria every applicant must meet, checked against each applicant:

  1. Internet speed screenshot.
  2. Audio recording of the applicant's voice sample.
  3. English grammar test.

Screening steps in order:

  1. Require a passing score of 80 percent or higher on an English grammar test.
  2. Ask for a screenshot of an internet speed test showing upload and download speeds (how fast data is sent and received).
  3. Request an audio recording of the candidate reading a short text aloud, to check that their English is good enough for customer calls.
  4. Include an easter egg in the job posting (see the purple giraffe filter above).

Video and audio answers

1 min
  • Ask a few questions that require a short video or audio answer, for example "why do you want this job".
  • Discard written answers that you would not be willing to send to a customer.
  • Keep those that show the person can talk to customers well.
  • For ongoing hires, interview the candidate by video using prepared questions, as you would for an employee.

Experience requirements

1 min

Sources differ on how much prior experience to require. Each applies in a different situation.

  • Experience solving problems matters more than Shopify experience for US roles, because small details can be learned on the job.
  • Hire experienced freelancers for US roles through Upwork, and for Philippines-based roles through OnlineJobs.ph.
  • For most Shopify high-ticket dropshipping tasks (selling expensive products without holding stock), experienced people already exist.
  • So requiring proof of prior experience in the application is realistic.
  • Candidates without that experience can be filtered out without losing good options.
  • Hiring someone overqualified, with good English and years of customer service experience, saves time.
  • They need fewer explanations, make their own decisions, and are less likely to leave for a better salary.
  • For a VA who answers emails, handles live chat and processes orders, Upwork's entry-level setting is the correct experience level.
  • This is a separate setting from how overqualified you are willing to hire.
  • Write a moderate job description and prefer less experienced candidates when you will train them to your own processes.

Language

1 min
  • The working language must be the language the business and its customers use, whatever that language is.
  • A candidate whose written language is weak can be rejected, even if the weakness comes from a non-native background.
  • Reason: a writing standard cannot be taught on the job.
  • Frequent misspellings or poor grammar are a reason to remove a candidate.
  • This cannot be taught later.

Shortlisting rules

2 min

To shortlist means to pick the few best applicants to look at closely.

A funnel is the path from many applicants down to one hire. A post is a job ad.

Other figures:

  • Expect hundreds of applicants. So shortlist, test several candidates at the same time, and compare results before choosing.
  • Expect hundreds of applications to a job post. Judge candidates by past work samples.
  • Filtering early saves interview time.

Shortlisting order

Shortlist applicants in this order:

  1. Check whether the application followed the posted instructions. If not, delete it without reply.

  2. Check whether the candidate has experience with the task. If the posting asked for proof of experience and it is missing, remove the candidate. People who have never done the task are generally filtered out.

  3. Check attention to detail. Look at:

    • effort in the application
    • full written answers versus one-liners
    • quality of any CV (a written summary of work history) or resume
    • spelling and sentence structure

    Drop candidates who did the bare minimum.

  4. Check command of the working language. This means how well they read and write it. It must be good enough for the customer, product and content work involved. Frequent misspellings or poor grammar are a reason to remove the candidate, since this cannot be taught later.

Working the shortlist

  • Shortlist applicants in a spreadsheet (a table file) such as Google Sheets (Google's free online spreadsheet tool) before sending follow-up questions.
  • Follow-up questions are extra questions sent after the application. They can repeat the job post questions or be new ones.
  • Shortlist the applications you like in a spreadsheet. Then email follow-up questions to the shortlisted candidates.
  • Reply only to the candidates you shortlist. Replying to hundreds of applicants is too time consuming.
  • If you have an executive assistant (a personal helper who handles admin work), that person can handle the replies to the others.
  • If more candidates remain after the four checks than the interview limit allows, rank the remaining candidates. Keep the top five.
  • If the candidates still cannot be separated, choose randomly and move on without overthinking.

Follow-up questions

  • Review the follow-up answers for responsiveness (how fast and well they reply). Also check spelling and grammar, and whether the answers look scripted (copied or pre-written, not personal).
  • Drop candidates who take days to reply.
  • Send the remaining applicants a follow-up with specific questions about their knowledge and experience for the job.

Interviews, trials and hiring

5 min

Interview and trial

  • Run a five-minute interview test on a call with the best candidates.
  • Offer a half-day paid trial to the last three or four. A paid trial is a small test job that you pay for.
  • Test with scenario questions (what-would-you-do questions).
  • Also give a small paid trial batch, such as three or four product descriptions (short texts that sell a product) with minimal instructions. This shows how they take initiative, meaning act without being told.
  • Give a paid trial task and measure how fast it is done.
  • Give a trial task before a long-term commitment.
  • Before offering a paid trial task, check each content applicant against all three requirements. A content applicant is someone who writes or makes content.

Checks before hiring

  • Check reviews on the platform, especially recurring themes (the same problem mentioned again and again) that need discussion.
  • Check portfolios (collections of past work).
  • Ask previous clients for references (people who can vouch for the candidate).
  • Before hiring a freelancer (a self-employed worker you hire for one job) on Fiverr (a website that sells small services), check the number of reviews and the feedback left by previous clients.
  • Reviews serve as social proof, meaning proof that others trust them.
  • Adding a line at the end of an Upwork (a website where clients and freelancers meet) job post that points to your positive client reviews can help attract more applicants.
  • This works only if you already have good client feedback on the platform.
  • A Klaviyo (an email tool for online shops) specialist can be found on Upwork or similar sites.
  • Ask for screenshots or a recording of past flows (automatic email sequences) before hiring.
  • To find a Shopify bookkeeper (a person who keeps the accounts; Shopify is software for running an online shop), search Upwork for Shopify bookkeeping. Review the profiles with direct e-commerce experience.
  • Define a list of criteria that every applicant must meet. Then check each applicant against it.

Five-step VA hiring process (version A)

A VA (virtual assistant) is a helper who works remotely, from another place.

  1. Write a very detailed job post.
  2. Shortlist the applications you like in a spreadsheet. Then email follow-up questions to the shortlisted candidates.
  3. Review the follow-up answers for responsiveness, spelling and grammar, and whether the answers look scripted. Drop candidates who take days to reply.
  4. Run a five-minute interview test on a call with the best candidates. Offer a half-day paid trial to the last three or four.
  5. Sign an agreement letter (a written contract). Then train the new hire gradually, starting with email and moving to phone calls and live chat (chatting with customers in real time).

Six-step freelancer and VA sequence (version B)

  1. Write a clear, attractive job posting. It states the job, expectations, pay, and weekly hours. This attracts good candidates and filters out weak ones.
  2. Check reviews on the platform, especially recurring themes that need discussion.
  3. Ask your business community or forums (online groups where business owners discuss) for recommendations.
  4. For ongoing hires (people you keep long-term), interview the candidate by video call using prepared questions, as you would for an employee.
  5. Test with scenario questions and a small paid trial batch, such as three or four product descriptions with minimal instructions, to see how they show initiative.
  6. Check portfolios and ask previous clients for references.

Five-step paid trial approach (version C)

  1. Post a job with a clear list of required skills and tasks.
  2. Ignore applicants who do not read the requirements, such as those who send only a generic message or a copied resume.
  3. Send the remaining applicants a follow-up with specific questions about their knowledge and experience for the job.
  4. Give a paid trial task and measure how fast it is done.
  5. Keep the people who deliver quality. Turn the best part-timers (people who work fewer hours) into full-time staff.

Small first task

Procedure for a first VA hire:

  1. Post a job ad for a small, clearly defined task, such as uploading a batch (a group) of products with descriptions, images and prices.
  2. Review the candidates' profiles (their online pages with past work). Give a trial task before a long-term commitment.
  3. Keep the first task simple so a bad result is easy to correct by deleting and rehiring.

Fiverr procedures

To find a VA on Fiverr:

  1. Type "virtual assistant" into the Fiverr search bar.
  2. Compare the sellers on offer (people selling their services). Check how many reviews each one has.
  3. Contact the sellers you consider directly.
  4. Give them a task and see whether they can deliver it.

To find a VA on Fiverr for small tasks:

  1. Type "virtual assistant" into the Fiverr search bar.
  2. Look for sellers who have a good number of reviews.
  3. Contact the sellers directly. Give each one a concrete small task to see whether they can deliver.
  4. Pay only after you accept the finished job, so the seller is paid only if you are satisfied.

Fiverr suits small tasks and small store projects. Use it to test how a VA works before committing to a larger role.

Upwork procedure

Hiring a VA on Upwork:

  1. Post a job with the exact tasks you want to delegate (hand over to someone else).
  2. Write a clear job description that states those tasks.
  3. Add screening questions (questions that filter out people who do not fit) to the job application.
  4. Filter out everyone who did not answer the screening questions.

Agency intake (Monazar VAs)

An agency is a company that finds and supplies VAs for you. To request a VA through Monazar VAs:

  1. Open the Monazar VAs website.
  2. Scroll down to the contact form (a box where you type your details).
  3. Enter your name, your email address, and either your Skype ID (a username for Skype, a video call app) or your WhatsApp number (WhatsApp is a messaging app).
  4. Write a brief explanation of what you need and how a VA should help your business.
  5. Submit the form. Then wait for the team to contact you and set up a consultation call to discuss your needs.

Typical intake steps with an agency-based VA service:

  1. Open the provider's website and fill in the contact form with your name, email address, and either your Skype ID or WhatsApp number.
  2. Briefly describe what you need and how a VA could help you.
  3. Expect a call from the provider to run a needs analysis (a call to check your needs). They confirm your requirements and whether they can help.

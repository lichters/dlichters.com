Pay, bonuses, retention and contracts

Section 20.10 · 10 of 26 in this chapter · 17 min read

In short

  • Pay per finished job, not per hour, for most outside work.
  • Pay a bit above the usual rate to keep better people longer.
  • Full-time remote helpers in the Philippines usually cost 300 to 500 USD per month.
  • Pay on time, every time, or good people leave.
  • Plan a first raise. Sources give 30 days, 6 to 8 months, or one year as options.
More key points
  • Sign a written contract before a remote helper (virtual assistant, VA) starts work.
  • Hiring locally can make workers employees, which costs you more. Start with overseas workers.
  • Keep good staff as long as you can. Each person who leaves wastes the training you paid for.
  • Pay fairly and on time. Happy, steady staff stay longer.

Pay levels by role

1 min
  • Full-time offshore VA (a remote helper who works from another country) in the Philippines: usually 400 to 500 USD per month.
  • Pay slightly above the common rate. This attracts people with office experience. It also lowers turnover (the number of people who quit).
  • Full-time virtual staff from online-jobs.ph: about 300 USD per month to start. This site hires remote staff in the Philippines.
  • Experienced staff rise to about 350, 400 or 500 USD per month.
  • Full-time VA after proving themselves (no year given): about 200 USD per week. This comes from one author's experience with Philippine VAs.
  • This figure is per week. It cannot be compared directly with the monthly figures above.
  • Product lister (a person who adds products to an online store), starting salary (no year given): about 500 USD per month.
  • This is lower than customer service. Product listers do not talk to customers, and their workload changes less from month to month.
  • Routine VA work at about 10 USD per hour (no year given): tends to give lower quality.
  • These team members also tend to leave.
  • Where the sources differ: the 2019 range is 400 to 500 USD and describes a full-time offshore VA with office experience.
  • The 2020 range starts at 300 USD and rises with experience.
  • Both apply to full-time hires in the Philippines. They fit different career stages and different candidate types.
  • Target level (no year given): pay VAs close to US minimum wage levels (the lowest legal hourly pay in the US).
  • Treat them well and build a team culture (the shared way a team works and treats each other).

Pay above the market

1 min
  • Pay above market rates (what others usually pay) to attract people with strong English and past experience.
  • Higher pay lowers the risk of poor work.
  • Pay above the cheapest rate. This improves quality and consistency.
  • It also helps you keep people for years.
  • Pay a good VA somewhat above the typical Philippine rate. This secures quality and loyalty.
  • Strong, engaged VAs then take on extra tasks for other businesses, even after the original store is sold.
  • Pay above the average rate for the role.
  • A second hire at average pay produced weak candidates. Raising the offer by about 100 USD per month brought much better applicants.

Pay models: per task, per product, per article and hourly

1 min

General rules

1 min
  • Pay per task, meaning per finished piece of work. Examples: one product description, one word, or one batch of 500 images.
  • Hourly pay gives no reason to work fast.
  • Exception: ongoing work such as social media management.
  • Offer a bonus for finishing a per-task job within a set time.
  • This motivates the freelancer to work efficiently.
  • Decide your budget first. Then pay more for tasks that deliver high value.
  • Set budgets by what you can afford, not by a fixed rule.
  • Judge price by quality, tested through the trial process (a short test job before you hire).
  • Higher spending often gives better quality. But some good writers charge less.

Procedure: working out pay for a freelancer or VA (no year given)

1 min
  1. Set the budget first: decide what you can realistically afford.
  2. Assess the value: check whether the task produces a big jump in profit. Pay more for high-value tasks.
  3. Research the market on platforms like Upwork (a freelance job site). See what freelancers ask and what the average pay is, for example for a VA in the Philippines.
  4. Negotiate with freelancers. They are often willing to split work or adjust scope (the amount of work included) to fit your budget.

Written content and video (older: 2021 to 2022 for Source B; 2019 for Source A where marked)

1 min
  • Pay per finished project, never per hour.
  • Hourly pay gives no reason to finish quickly. It can also encourage dishonest freelancers to slow down.
  • Per-word pay for written content is an accepted alternative.
  • Treat each content piece as piece work (paid per finished item).
  • Agree the price before starting. Negotiate it first.
  • Use the same logic for video editing. Agree a price per video based on its length and requirements.
  • Pay writers a fixed price per article, not an hourly rate.
  • Hourly pay makes costs hard to predict when some writers are slow.
  • Quality matters more than volume.
  • Outsourcing a 500-word article to a non-native English writer (someone whose first language is not English) on Odesk (the old name of Upwork) cost about 5 USD per 500 words at the time described.
  • This is a rough rate. Check current freelancer rates before you budget. (older: rate from an earlier period, no year given)

Product uploads

1 min
  • Pay upload staff per completed product (adding products to the store), not a fixed salary, when output changes.
  • Some part-time upload staff earned more than a full-time hire in busy months. Pay followed the work done.
  • This keeps costs tied to output.
  • One VA was paid per product uploaded, not hourly.
  • In a busy period with many new suppliers, this VA earned roughly 800 to 900 USD per month.
  • In slower months the same VA earned roughly 300 to 400 USD per month.
  • Calculate the rate per product before setting pay.
  • Use this formula: rate per product = total payment ÷ number of products.
  • Multiply the number of products by the rate to get the payout.
  • Worked example: a 200 USD test task of 179 products gives a rate of about 1.12 USD per product (200 ÷ 179).
  • The earlier figure of 1.16 USD is wrong. Recalculate the rate before you set pay.

Hourly pay: where it fits

1 min
  • For reformatting a supplier CSV (a spreadsheet file with values separated by commas) into Shopify's import format (the file layout Shopify, an online store platform, reads), a freelancer who already knows CSV files can be paid about 20 USD per hour.
  • The work takes roughly 20 minutes to one hour, however many products the file holds.
  • So an hourly rate is cheaper than a fixed price per product.
  • Check whether the data is ready to upload before you price a VA task.
  • A CSV that only needs reformatting takes about 20 minutes.
  • Scattered data entered one by one can take dozens of hours.
  • Hire only people with many reviews (customer ratings) for the exact workflow.

Commissions and profit bonuses

1 min

Principles

1 min
  • Do not pay only a percentage of sales (commission) and expect staff to drive results alone.
  • Use clear KPIs (key performance indicators, the goals you measure).
  • Add point-based incentives. Points can be redeemed for money, time off or products.
  • Performance-based bonuses motivate higher performance, especially for sales teams.
  • Set clear rules on acceptable behaviour, such as collaboration rather than toxic competition (harmful rivalry).
  • This prevents conflict and keeps the drive for results.
  • Use a profit-share (pay tied to profit) or incentive model when hiring VAs with a documented process.
  • This lets a small team test products faster.

Commission bases in use

1 min
  • A common commission for a VA is 1% of the total sales they are responsible for.
  • Once you hire the right assistant, a small extra bonus in the paycheck helps keep them motivated.
  • Reward a sales-focused VA with 1% of the sales they generate.
  • Track the commission by sales channel (where the sale came from), so it is easy to see who generated each sale.
  • Pay 1% of the total sale price only on customer exchanges that need a quote or multi-unit requests (orders of many units at once).
  • Also pay on exchanges that run longer than two back-and-forth emails.
  • Simple questions about stock, colour or shipping are ordinary customer service. No commission applies. (merged: duplicate rules)
  • Use net profit (money left after all costs) instead of sales volume where discounting is a risk.
  • A bonus based on sales volume made VAs discount heavily to hit the target. This hurt profit.
  • Monthly VA bonus = 1% of net profit, paid to each VA.
  • Example: a store with 10,000 USD net profit in a month pays each VA 100 USD as a bonus.
  • For large B2B orders (sales between businesses), the VA can receive a bonus of 1% of the order value.
  • This is a bonus, not a commission on sales.

Phone answering

1 min
  • Commission for phone support lines up the phone worker's pay with sales results.
  • This works well for businesses with low call volume, 2 to 5 calls per day. Hourly wages would be inefficient here.
  • Early on, one VA was paid 5 USD for each day the assistant answered the phone.
  • Later the fee became a flat 150 USD per month for answering calls. That is roughly 5 USD per weekday.
  • Flat-fee cost per weekday = monthly fee ÷ number of weekdays in the month.
  • The 150 USD per month is about 5 USD per calendar day.
  • With about 22 weekdays per month, the cost is about 6.80 USD per weekday (150 ÷ 22 = 6.82, which the source rounds).
  • A phone-answering fee is worth paying. Each sale-related call can be worth 50 to 300 USD.
  • The assistant may already be at a computer for other work.
  • Use a flat monthly rate after the first months, not hourly or per-call pay.

Sales roles

1 min
  • Assign a designated representative (one named person) for B2B deals.
  • Scale the commission with deal size.
  • Large corporate deals may take months to close. The commission should reflect that effort.
  • Reward strong deals with an extra bonus in the paycheck, in addition to commission.
  • Good pay and recognition make team members work to close better deals.
  • An early mail-sales hire started with a 25 USD weekly drawing account (an advance paid against future commission) plus a 5% commission on mail sales.
  • Once commission earned far more than the base, the company replaced the arrangement with a fixed salary.
  • Use a drawing account with commission while sales are uncertain.
  • Switch to salary once the commission level is clear.

Raises and career growth

1 min

Timing of the first raise

1 min
  • Plan the first salary raise after 6 to 8 months.
  • Tell the hire this during hiring, so they know growth is planned.
  • This gives motivation and prevents a feeling of being stuck at a flat rate.
  • Give an annual raise after the first year, plus occasional bonuses.
  • In the 30-day procedure, raise pay if the work is good after 30 days (see the hiring section).
  • The sources give different first-raise points: 30 days, 6 to 8 months, or after the first year.
  • None says which to choose. They are presented here as alternatives.

After the first raise

1 min
  • Tie future raises to performance, such as finishing new projects or taking on responsibility, not to time.
  • Show each team member where they can grow, such as eventually managing other VAs.
  • Share company goals with them.
  • The author set a goal of about 10 employees for those who grow with the company.
  • Build long-term relationships with the same staff.
  • Do not keep training new people for the same task again and again.
  • Do not start with salaries you cannot afford.

Procedure: salary progression (no year given)

1 min
  1. Set a starting salary that was negotiated with the hire.
  2. Agree on salary goals with long-term staff. Review them every month or year.
  3. Raise pay when the stores grow and the team member takes on more responsibility.
  4. If a bad month occurs, a loyal team may accept a short-term salary cut.
  5. Only pay raises the business can afford, based on calculated monthly profit.

Example of progression (no year given)

1 min
  • A team member started at 100 USD per month two to three years earlier.
  • They set goals with the owner each year.
  • Pay reached 800 USD, then 1,500 USD per month, as the stores grew.

Bonuses, rewards and recognition

1 min

Bonus types

1 min
  • Pay a small bonus after a strong month.
  • Other low-cost rewards: extra paid days off, funded relevant training, and more responsibility.
  • These cost little compared with the value the team creates.
  • Reward large milestones (clear achievement points) with bonuses. For example, an extra payment after 1,000 products uploaded keeps staff motivated.
  • Milestone bonuses increased loyalty without extra management effort.
  • Plan these rewards in advance.
  • Paying bonuses is fine.
  • Paid time off is also a good way to recognise people.

Equipment and workstations

1 min
  • Hardware programme: find tools that slow the team down.
  • Example: single screens for an operations team processing 100+ orders per month.
  • Buy hardware such as screens, laptops and chairs, and give them to the team.
  • Result: a significant morale boost (better team mood) and measurable productivity improvement.
  • This is worth the investment for core teams (the people the business depends on most).

Workstation programme:

  1. Offer 50% company reimbursement (the company pays back part of the cost), max 1,000 USD total purchase per employee.
  2. The employee pays 50%.
  3. The employee applies through the manager and provides the purchase link and price.
  4. The owner approves each purchase.
  5. The employee buys a laptop, chair, mouse or headset.
  6. The company reimburses 50% after purchase.

This builds loyalty and gives the team proper equipment while sharing the cost.

  • Reward examples include buying a staff member new equipment, such as a laptop.
  • Another example is paying for higher-level training.
  • That training builds skills they can use later in other jobs. It also strengthens their resume (a summary of work history).

Recognition

1 min
  • Thank and praise a partner or team member when a task is completed.
  • Recognition motivates them to take the next task.
  • Pair praise with a clear request for the next step.
  • Recognise and reward team members by giving feedback and listening to concerns.
  • Regular recognition of good work encourages people to do their best and builds loyalty.
  • Tell them directly when they do a good job.
  • Build a personal relationship with your team.
  • For example, ask about their safety and well-being during a crisis.
  • Staff who feel valued go beyond their job and stay longer.
  • Remote team members were flown to meet the owner in person for two nights during a trip abroad.
  • The owner said this moved the working relationship to a higher level and made the team feel more like a unit.
  • This is a low-cost way to strengthen a remote VA team.

Rewarding good work

1 min
  1. Always pay people on time.
  2. Where work is provided above your normal standard, find ways to reward it.
  3. Paying bonuses is fine.
  4. If you work with Filipino staff, pay the 13th month (an extra month's pay required in the Philippines).
  5. Paid time off is also a good way to recognise people.

Warning on large rewards

1 min
  • Overly large rewards set expectations you may not keep.
  • Use rewards sparingly, so the person does not come to expect the same big reward every time.

Paying on time and cash flow

1 min
  • Pay team members twice per month, at the beginning and middle of the month.
  • This gives steady cash flow (money coming in and going out over time) every 2 weeks, and it is predictable.
  • Use one system for all payments, from spreadsheet to payment processor (the service that sends the money), to keep a standard.
  • Always pay on time.
  • For per-project work, pay as soon as the project is submitted, unless you agreed a different arrangement.
  • For weekly pay, pay on the same day each week, at as close to the same time as possible.
  • Staff with families depend on predictable income.
  • Late or irregular pay makes it unlikely they will stay long term.
  • Your agreement to pay is part of the deal, so you must meet it.
  • Do not pay a freelancer in full before any work is delivered.
  • This is especially risky on a platform without buyer protection (a rule where the site holds payment until you accept the work).
  • Otherwise you can be left with an unfinished task and no recourse (no way to get your money back).
  • Choose platforms that hold payment until you accept the finished work, or pay in stages tied to deliverables (finished parts of the job).
  • Structure hiring so that payment depends on delivered work.
  • Paying freelancers upfront, or assigning only simple tasks before payment, can lose money when work is never delivered.

Statutory obligations

1 min
  • In the Philippines, the 13th month is a local requirement, similar to year-end holiday pay.
  • Pay it to staff who have worked for you there for any period and are still with you at year-end.
  • If you work with Filipino staff, pay the 13th month.

Hiring terms and trial periods

1 min
  • Hire VAs on full-time contracts with exclusivity (they work only for you), instead of 3 to 4 hours a day.
  • Part-time staff tend to look for other full-time work and become distracted.
  • State at hiring that they work only for your business.

Hiring and retaining virtual staff procedure

1 min
  1. Hire the first person on a 30-day contract.
  2. Monitor their work and give proactive feedback (early feedback, before problems grow) to help them improve.
  3. If the work is good after 30 days, raise their pay, for example from 300 to 350 USD per month.
  4. Set shared goals, such as a bonus when monthly sales pass a set amount.
  • Virtual staff for full-time roles can be found on online-jobs.ph for about 300 USD per month.
  • Pay rises to about 350, 400 or 500 USD per month for experienced staff.
  • OnlineJobs.ph is used to hire VAs, including staff in the Philippines.
  • Many hires in the team stayed for five or more years.
  • Prefer a long-term assistant who knows the store and processes.
  • The author's team had worked together for four years.
  • Once trained, the team can run ad campaigns (paid advertising runs). The founder only reviews campaigns and makes decisions.
  • Starting with an assistant you pay out of revenue (money the store takes in) can work when cash is short.
  • Move to a monthly fixed salary once revenue exists.
  • Calling outsourced helpers "team members" or "employees" instead of "virtual assistants" supports a culture where people stay long term.
  • The framing matters most when the owner is hiring for ongoing roles rather than short tasks.

Contracts

1 min

  • Sign a formal written contract before the virtual assistant (VA, a remote helper who works online) starts work.

  • Before a VA starts training, sign a written contract. A contract is a signed agreement that sets the rules.

  • You can find a generic VA contract online and adapt it to your situation.

  • The contract should state:

    • pay
    • weekly work hours
    • holidays observed
    • vacation notice (how early the worker must ask for time off)
    • notice period (how long before quitting the worker must say so)
    • training obligations

  • Use simple contracts for team members in the Philippines, Bangladesh and South America.

  • Standard templates (ready-made forms, such as from LegalZoom, an online legal-form service) are enough.

  • Contracts are not always needed from day one. Some team members ask for one later, for a loan (credit) or a visa.

  • This is not standard practice, but it helps when someone asks.

  • A formal contract is easier to set up when you use a hiring platform (a website that finds and manages remote workers).

  • A private arrangement should rest on trust and a clear revenue split (the agreed share of income each side keeps).

  • Test products with a VA using a documented process (written steps) and an incentive model (pay tied to results).

  • The VA checks products against your criteria list (a checklist of rules). This frees your time for decisions.

Employment classification

1 min
  • Hiring ongoing staff in the country where your business is registered can lead the government to treat them as employees instead of contractors.
  • Employees are independent in name only: they bring duties that small businesses often cannot manage.
  • That is a reason to start with overseas workers.
  • This applies in the US, Australia and the UK.
  • Use contract forms and terms that match the worker's status.
  • See Contracts above: simple contracts for Philippine, Bangladeshi and South American team members.

Keeping staff long-term

1 min
  • Keep good team members for as long as possible.
  • Turnover (staff leaving and being replaced) wastes the time spent on training and on learning the suppliers, market and customers.
  • Keep expectations stable. Change them only in a conversation.
  • Build long-term relationships with the same staff.
  • Do not start with salaries you cannot afford.
  • Staff who feel valued are more willing to go beyond their job and stay longer.
  • Pay VAs close to US minimum wage levels (the lowest hourly pay the law allows in the US).
  • Treat them well and build a team culture (a shared way of working and feeling part of something).
  • Good pay and treatment keep turnover low and produce much more committed work.
  • Paying a good VA somewhat above the typical Philippine rate secures quality and loyalty.
  • Predictable pay and timing make staff more likely to stay long term.
  • A loyal team may accept a short-term salary cut in a bad month.
  • Show growth paths (clear steps for a career) and share company goals, so staff can see a future with the business.

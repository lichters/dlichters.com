Playbook › 20. Scaling, team and processes

Readiness to scale and scaling priorities

Section 20.1 · 1 of 26 in this chapter · 13 min read

In short

  • Build a store that already sells and makes real profit before you scale it.
  • Grow ad spend slowly: no more than 2x your daily spend until 2-3 weeks of profit are proven.
  • Fix customer service, shipping and refunds before you sell more.
  • Keep adding more suppliers and products. Add advanced tactics only after the basics are maxed out.
  • Do not outsource or automate until the business is profitable.

Gate 1: Working, Profitable Live Store

2 min
  • Begin scaling only after the system works. The store must be live (open to real visitors), attracting traffic (visitors), generating sales and producing real income.
  • Before scaling, check that the business is still tied to a diagnosis (a clear reason for each result). Revenue that swings from month to month without a clear cause, and sales that stall around $1 million per year, are typical signs of inconsistent and disconnected marketing. These symptoms point to a missing system rather than a problem with the market alone.
  • Use this procedure to set up the business at its current stage:
    1. Set up the business for success at its current stage (grow phase).
    2. Read your numbers regularly and understand what they show.
    3. Use those numbers to decide how to scale the business.
  • Scaling (growing sales by spending more and doing more) has three focus areas: increasing order volume, improving efficiency and maximizing what already works.
  • Scaling does not mean restarting operations. Layer new strategies on top of your existing traffic generation (ways of getting visitors), customer communication, order processing and supplier relationships.
  • Consistency matters more than aggressive scaling. Do not scale daily spend beyond 2x (double) until 2-3 weeks of proven profitability are observed, even if day one shows profit. Aggressive scaling requires supporting systems (payment gateways, which process card payments; fulfillment, which means packing and shipping orders; and supplier capacity) that may not be in place.
  • Scale slowly rather than pushing volume hard on the front end (the visible part of the business, such as ads and the store). A weak backend (the hidden part: payments, shipping, account problems) can collapse a business that is scaling fast. Check the backend before each scaling step.
  • Keep improving a store that makes only a few thousand dollars net profit (what you keep after all costs) per month before starting another store. A struggling store can be made profitable by repeating proven steps and looking for missed items.
  • Gross sales are not profit. Gross sales means all money from sales before costs. Tens of thousands of dollars in monthly sales means gross sales, not net profit. Stores at $5,000 to $15,000 a month should focus on the main growth goals rather than on other channels.

Gate 2: Core Setup Running at Full Potential

1 min
  • Pursue advanced scaling tactics only after all of the following are true:
    1. Core strategies are fully implemented.
    2. The store is live with consistent traffic and sales.
    3. Performance limits have been reached with current channels: supplier expansion, Google Shopping Ads (product ads in Google) and remarketing (showing ads again to people who already visited).
  • Begin refining, tweaking and expanding strategies only once suppliers and traffic are truly maxed out. Advanced tactics belong in later stages, after the fundamentals are exhausted.
  • Add advanced levers only after the core setup runs in sequence at its full potential and results are already where you want them. Extra levers squeeze out the last gains; they do not fix a weak base.
  • Keep the core scaling levers maxed out before adding advanced strategies. Layering on extra tactics too early tends to overwhelm the operation without adding necessary results.
  • Advanced techniques are not part of the basic program. Discovery and Demand Gen (Google ad types, formerly Discovery for lead generation), and search and video ads, are added once a store has suppliers and steady sales.

Gate 3: Operations Hold at Current Volume

1 min
  • Problems scale with sales. If customer service, shipping, back orders (orders waiting for stock), refunds, chargebacks (a customer reversing a card payment through their bank) or cash flow already struggle at current volume, multiplying sales will multiply those problems too.
  • Fix existing operational problems before pushing for more sales. Scaling marketing and sales alone is not enough, because operations, team and finances must grow with it.
  • Scaling only marketing and sales while leaving operations, team and finances unchanged can make customers angry and eventually end the business. Success itself can become the cause of failure.
  • Scaling multiplies problems as well as sales. Ten times the revenue brings ten times the cash flow strain, customer service load and chargebacks. Chargeback volume that grows too high can get a merchant account (the account that lets you accept card payments) cancelled.
  • Maintenance comes before growth. Maintenance work keeps current sales and fulfillment running: customer service, website upkeep and product management. Growth work increases sales through marketing and team building, and it should only be added once maintenance runs reliably.
  • Answer every pre-launch checklist item with Yes or No. Any No means that item is unfinished, so fix it before switching on paid traffic. The store is ready for ads and real visitors only when every item is a Yes.

Supplier Choice Risk

1 min
  • A battery-operated product was bought from a supplier in China for 46 US dollars plus 30 US dollars shipping, bringing the total to 76 US dollars. The buyer then received an import bill of about 38 euros or pounds from FedEx, which made the product too expensive to sell. The example shows why Chinese dropshipping (a supplier ships the product straight to the customer, so you never hold stock) leads to surprise costs, delays and unhappy customers.

Team and Outsourcing Timing

1 min
  • Do not outsource or automate business processes until the business is profitable. Outsourcing means paying other people to do work. Automation means software or systems doing tasks. Both add cost and complexity that an unproven store cannot carry.
  • Outsourcing and automation suit stores that already get traffic and sales and want to hand off as much manual work as possible. Stores that are not yet running should not prioritize them.
  • Growth requires new skills in stages: first product research, copywriting (writing words that sell), video editing and Facebook ads, then hiring and managing people. Plan for team building as part of scaling.
  • Revenue growth and team expansion reinforce each other: more revenue enables hiring, and a larger team drives more revenue. This cycle cannot reverse except through deliberate downsizing.
  • Once revenue grows past six figures (100,000 dollars or more per month), the tactics that worked before stop working. The business needs a team and automation to reach seven figures. Processes must be repeatable so the business runs whether a given person is present or not.
  • Scaling requires always tackling a bigger problem than the last one, since bigger problems bring bigger rewards when solved. Each level of growth needs new tools and new help.

Lever 1: Suppliers and Products

1 min
  • Focus on two levers first: securing as many suppliers as possible in your niche (a narrow market), and scaling your traffic. Together these are the main drivers of revenue growth at this stage.
  • These two levers alone are sufficient to reach five-figure monthly revenue (10,000 to 99,999 dollars), and then to scale toward six figures and beyond.
  • Supplier minimum to launch: 2 suppliers with at least 20 products between them. Continue getting approvals (a brand's permission to sell its products) in the background while running early ads.
  • Keep approving more brands in the background, even after launch, while the store runs on its first suppliers.
  • Growth comes from continuously adding new deals and working with more suppliers. Store maintenance is ongoing, while adding suppliers and products is the main growth engine.
  • Once a store runs, keep adding products as new suppliers are signed. This is ongoing work, not a one-time setup.
  • Once the store has real products and live traffic, re-contact suppliers who declined earlier. Their previous "not right now" answer may have changed now that you can show results.
  • Running a store that already sells well without changing it usually caps its growth. Keep testing new suppliers, products, offers and traffic sources, because adding a few strong suppliers can sharply raise sales.
  • Methods for finding suppliers outside standard criteria should only be used after the niche verification (checking the market is worth it) and supplier research steps of the plan are complete, typically after the fourth step or later. Use them when you need more suppliers, more products or less competitive products within a niche that is already working.

Lever 2: Traffic

2 min
  • Early-phase focus order:
    1. Decide which products to upload first.
    2. Upload the top 20 percent of best sellers.
    3. Finish the product page optimizations (improving pages so they sell more).
    4. Then start the paid campaigns.
  • Spend most time on paid traffic early, around 70 to 75 percent, because it gives fast traffic and sales turnaround. SEO (search engine optimization, getting found in Google for free) is a longer-term investment.
  • Product SEO comes first. Product SEO means making product pages rank in Google. It has the easiest early wins and should be done before blog or backlink work. Ranking product pages does not require backlinks (links from other websites to yours), though relevant backlinks help.
  • Growth marketing covers these areas as a continuous marketing system after launch:
    1. Search engine optimization (SEO).
    2. Content marketing (articles and videos that attract buyers).
    3. Backlink building.
    4. Email marketing.
    5. Retargeting (showing ads again to people who already visited).
    6. Pay-per-click advertising (paying for each click) on Google Ads and Bing Ads.
    7. Copywriting, including product copy that sells.
    8. Affiliates (partners who earn a commission per sale) and the Dream 100 outreach method (contacting 100 key influencers or partners in your niche).
    9. Customer multiplier funnels (follow-up offers after a purchase that earn more from the same customer).
    10. Financial management and knowing your numbers.
  • The growth phase starts at launch and continues for as long as the business runs. It is a continuous marketing system, not a one-time task.
  • A dropshipping business does not need to complete every growth marketing area before it can earn sales. These can be worked through at the owner's own pace, and adding new tactics as the business grows helps prevent early growth plateaus (times when sales stop rising).
  • Set the first ad budget by what you are comfortable testing, not by a click target. Typical launch budgets are $10 to $30 per day. About 10% of total ad spend goes to remarketing. Example: $20 per day gives a $600 monthly budget, with $60 going to remarketing.
  • Scaling ads means increasing spend gradually once a store is profitable. Fear of spending more on ads is the main thing that holds back growth from about 100,000 USD to a million USD per month.
  • A $1M annual revenue goal at 10x ROAS (return on ad spend: 10 dollars earned for each dollar spent on ads) needs about $100,000 total ad spend in the worst case. That spend is built up gradually over 12 months, not paid upfront.

Lever 3: Efficiency and Order Value

1 min
  • Grow revenue without losing margin (the profit you keep per sale after costs) in three steps:
    1. Add profitable products or suppliers.
    2. Expand into additional traffic sources or streams.
    3. Raise average order value (the typical amount per order) or customer lifetime value (the total a customer spends over time).
  • Build the offer so that ROAS can fall and the campaign still stays profitable. The margin cushion (the profit room you have) decides whether a drop in performance is survivable. Products with a healthy margin can ride out seasonal dips, while thin margins turn the same dip into a loss.
  • Growing from 10K to 50K monthly revenue at a $1,000 average order value means lifting sales from 10 to 50 orders per month.
  • Choose the lever by diagnosis, not by habit.
Diagnosis Lever
Few suppliers, but traffic already optimized Add suppliers
Many products, but weak ad performance or unclear ad profitability Optimize ads and product pages while raising budget
  • Scaling ads and efficiency work comes after the core levers are maxed out (see Gate 2).

Revenue Milestone Ladder

1 min
  • Progressive revenue targets: five figures per month as the first milestone, six figures per month as the second, with membership in the 100K Club (the group of stores making six figures a month) as the next achievement level.
  • Common revenue milestones for dropshipping businesses are five figures per month, then six figures per month, then beyond. Reaching six figures per month is the point at which a business is commonly considered to have entered the 100K club.
Step Monthly revenue Next requirement
1 Five figures (10,000 to 99,999 dollars) Maxed-out suppliers and traffic before advanced tactics
2 Six figures (100K Club) Team and repeatable processes
3 Beyond six figures Team and automation to reach seven figures
  • Coaching and product development show that a store needs a strong early product base before scaling.

Milestone Ladder: Step-by-Step Growth

2 min
  • Scale in steps: first earn around 1 USD a day, then 100 USD a day, then 1,000 USD a day, before scaling up further. Jumping straight to around 10,000 USD in a single step is risky because the operator has not yet learned how to manage that money and those processes.
  • Getting from zero to roughly $500 to $1,000 in daily revenue is the hardest phase of scaling. After the first roughly $1,000 day, growth mostly comes from raising the ad budget and adding more ads to proven products.
  • Focus by revenue stage:
Stage Main focus
Beginner (no sales yet) Foundation building, product research, store setup
First sale achieved Consistency, fixing metrics, understanding data
$500/day Scaling, creatives (ad images and videos), A/B testing (comparing two versions to see which works better), angles (the main message of an ad)
$10k/month and up Operations, hiring, systems, LTV, building, brand
$100k+/month Pure scaling strategy, backend systems, exit potential
  • Advice and strategy should match the revenue stage a store is at. A beginner needs foundation work, while a store doing several hundred dollars a day needs scaling and testing. Mixing stage-specific tactics across levels slows progress.
  • Phase 6, Optimization & Scaling (Days 75-90), targets consistent monthly revenue between $5,000 and $20,000 from a single proven product.
  • To scale a winning product in Phase 6 (Days 75-90), execute these steps in order:
    1. Identify and fix funnel bottlenecks (the funnel is the path from ad to purchase; a bottleneck is the step that slows everything).
    2. Produce new creative variations every week to maintain ad freshness.
    3. Double down on the winning advertising angle by allocating more budget to it.
    4. Launch retargeting ads to recover abandoned carts (people who added items but did not buy) and engage repeat visitors.
    5. Add upsells (an extra or pricier item offered at checkout) and bundles (items sold together as one package) to increase average order value.
    6. Introduce subscription or recurring revenue products (MOO) (products that bill customers again and again) to build predictable monthly income.
  • Week-by-week roadmap for a new branded dropshipping store:
    1. Weeks 1-2: foundations and store setup.
    2. Weeks 3-4: product research and validation.
    3. Weeks 5-8: ads and testing frameworks.
    4. Weeks 7-8: scaling with systems (MRR, monthly recurring revenue, and retention, meaning customers who come back).
    5. Week 9 onward: advanced strategies, automation and long-term growth.

Milestone Ladder: Brand Targets

1 min
  • The revenue goal for the brand this year is 100,000 in revenue, with growth in following years through better brand deals (agreements with brands for better prices and terms) and supplier terms.
  • One expert names 30,000 to 40,000 USD of sales per day as the scale that successful dropshippers aim for. At that level, doing the work alone is very difficult.

Launch Preconditions Before Paid Traffic

1 min
  • Procedure to prepare a store for launch:
    1. Set a launch date and communicate it to all suppliers.
    2. Email each supplier to request product information, pricing and product images before the launch date.
    3. Upload products for all approved suppliers, and keep adding products after launch.
    4. Start ads once the first two suppliers' products are uploaded and the site is ready.
  • Early days of store launch may take 30 minutes. Later days with more complex tasks may take 2-3 hours. Following the 21-day launch plan targets a store live with traffic within 21 days.
  • A Done-for-You store build (a service that builds the store for you) takes between 2 and 3 weeks. Speed may vary with the queue. Assume 2-3 weeks, but builds may complete faster if there is no backlog.
  • Pre-launch checklist: see Gate 3.

When Not to Add Advanced Tactics

1 min
  • The self-contained program is designed to build a profitable business without additional programs. Advanced strategies are optional rather than necessary.
  • Do not add advanced strategies if the store is still in build or stabilization phase, is currently scaling profitably with existing methods, or is already delivering the target results.
  • Do not rush into advanced strategies too early or overwhelm yourself with tactics you do not yet need. Based on extensive field experience, this premature escalation is a recurring error.
  • Do not add advanced tactics before the core is maxed out (see Gate 2).
  • Do not outsource or automate before the business is profitable (see Team and Outsourcing Timing).
  • Where sources disagree on timing:
    • Source D times advanced strategies by calendar, starting at week 9 onward, and includes subscription products in Phase 6 (Days 75-90). The calendar is a reference point; check the conditions in Gate 2 before starting.
    • Source A and Source C time advanced tactics by conditions: a live store with consistent sales, and maxed-out suppliers and traffic. The conditions decide, not the calendar.
  • Growth marketing advice from 2021-22 says to keep adding new marketing tactics as the business grows, to prevent plateaus. This is older advice and applies to growth marketing areas, not to advanced tactics before the core is maxed out.

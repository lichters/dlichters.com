Business partners, mentors and community support

Section 20.23 · 23 of 26 in this chapter · 14 min read

In short

  • Do not start a business with friends or family as partners. The only exception is paid, project-based specialist work.
  • Write a contract before any work starts. It must say who does what and how a partner can be removed.
  • Build your first store alone before you bring in partners or hire people for daily work.
  • Pick one method at a time, and ask questions early, so problems get solved.
This section has 17 parts. Tap one to open it.

Deciding whether to take a business partner

1 min
  • Avoid business partnerships with friends or family members.
  • The exception is a family member who does paid, project-based specialist work, such as graphic design or web design. That is acceptable.
  • Partnerships are hard to keep working.
  • Write a contract before any work starts. It must define each partner's responsibilities. Include clauses (written rules inside the contract) for buyout (one partner buys out the other) or removal if a partner does not deliver. Older (2021).
  • Avoid managing partners and equity partnerships early. A managing partner runs the business day to day. An equity partnership means a partner owns a share of the company.
  • Roles and effort often become unbalanced once a store takes off, and partners can fall out.
  • Build the store from zero to one (from nothing to a first working store) with your own effort first. Then outsource (pay others to do) the day-to-day work. Older (2021).
  • The partnership model can be repeated several times with different people. This creates multiple extra income streams (extra sources of money).
  • Overseeing 5 to 10 or more stores this way avoids working 8 to 12 hours a day running each one yourself.
  • Sources differ in emphasis. The caution against early equity partnerships applies to your first store. The repeatable partnership model applies when you expand beyond one store.
  • Partnering to run several stores at once is a higher-risk expansion than building one store alone.
  • It offers a larger payoff if it succeeds, but the outcome is uncertain.
  • Two people working as a team who find synergy (good teamwork that adds up to more than each person alone) tend to perform very well. They move faster than solo operators.
  • Choose business partners, suppliers and collaborators as carefully as you would choose close friends.
  • They shape the quality of your daily work and the time you have left for it.

Choosing partners

1 min
  • When choosing partners to run stores, prioritize people who have completed proper training, have already made a first sale, and are fully committed.
  • If the program requires it, also check that they are willing to relocate (move) to the operating location.
  • Surround yourself with people who are more talented than you in their areas. Your output tends to match the quality of the people and inputs around you.
  • If you are the most talented person in your group, look for a group where you can learn more.

Defining roles and decision rights

1 min
  • Define each partner's role in writing, so that responsibilities do not overlap or fall through the gaps.
  • Define roles with this procedure:
    1. List what each partner is good at and what each is not good at.
    2. Assign ownership (who is responsible for what) accordingly.
    3. Use personality type, such as introvert (prefers quiet, focused work) or extrovert (prefers talking and meeting people). Use it to decide who handles outreach (contacting people) and who handles ads and back-end work (behind-the-scenes tasks customers do not see).
  • Give each partner their own area and the freedom to decide within it, so they do not need to approve every small choice.
  • Escalate (pass up to the group) only decisions that affect the shared plan or money.
  • Partners do not need to report every routine action, such as a refund (money returned to a customer), to each other.
  • Escalate only significant decisions and changes.
  • Partners in a small business should be willing to take on tasks outside their formal role when needed.
  • Share equally the workload and the stress of the business between partners. Do not let one person carry it.
  • Schedule important business conversations at a set time on a workday, not in social or family settings.
  • Before the conversation, prepare the proposed solution, the reasons for it, and its pros and cons (advantages and disadvantages). Then a decision can be made quickly.

Working with family members

1 min
  • Give family members clear rules and a written job description before they start working, the same as any other hire.
  • Family work benefits from explicit expectations, meaning expectations that are clearly stated.
  • Working with family can work well. Set the rules first and treat the arrangement as a business relationship.
  • Avoid vague arrangements that make it hard to ask for results.
  • The exception for contracted specialized services is covered under "Deciding whether to take a business partner."

Coaching and mentor programs

1 min
  • Growth in dropshipping (selling products that a supplier ships straight to the customer, so you hold no stock) depends on three resources. These are a coach, virtual assistants (remote helpers), and continuous education (ongoing learning).
  • Plan for all three as the business scales (grows).
  • An outside coach (an expert who is not on your team) who audits (checks) the whole store and gives you a prioritized task list (tasks ranked by importance) can produce a quick sales lift (a fast rise in sales).
  • In one case, nearly $6,000 more in monthly sales came after the first coaching call.
  • A coach's advice is that learning is best when you focus on one approach and avoid switching between too many tools.
  • Once a process works, keep it.

Source C mentoring

1 min
  • Mentors usually answer student questions within one hour, or within 48 hours when they are traveling.
  • Send questions in advance through a form before group calls so the mentor can prepare answers.
  • One call per week is the standard during the build phase (the early stage of setting up the store).
  • Calls can be spaced further apart depending on progress.
  • Several calls per week are only useful in the scaling phase (the growth stage). This is when ads are live, sales are coming in, and decisions about Facebook ad campaigns (groups of ads run together) are needed.

Source D support structure

2 min
  • Effective program support combines three layers: a personal pod (a small private group with a coach and a Client Success Manager), a shared community channel for general questions, and scheduled live Q&A calls.
  • Together, they make sure a participant always has a clear route to get help.
  • In a personal pod, each participant gets a dedicated coach with real operating experience at six-figure revenue ($100,000 or more in revenue).
  • Each participant also gets a Client Success Manager (CSM) whose job is to keep the participant on track with their plan.
  • The CSM role is about follow-through (finishing what was planned) and accountability, not only answering questions.
  • A weekly live group call is a working session. Students bring their numbers, ads, product pages and stuck points, and the coach reviews them in real time (as they happen).
  • The purpose is to unblock students (remove what is stopping them) each week, not to sell.
  • Combining one-on-one guidance, group knowledge and live call support gives you personalized answers for your business. It also gives you the benefit of collective experience.
  • This combination shows you the next concrete step to take.
  • The Source D community has revenue-based tiers (membership levels based on monthly sales).
  • Members move up when they hit the next milestone. Each move assigns them a coach who specializes in that revenue stage.

Peer networks and masterminds

1 min
  • Work with your community instead of alone.
  • Talk to others in the same position, learn from their mistakes, and lift each other up together.
  • Solo work is significantly harder and less effective.
  • From the start, talk with peers about campaigns and goals. Use Google Ads support (Google's help for its paid ads), Quora (a question-and-answer website), Facebook groups, private forums and masterminds.
  • Get advice from other ecommerce entrepreneurs (people who sell online) without needing to share your store.
  • Peer networks help you learn the same tools and logic, and can lead to partnerships.
  • A mastermind group (a small group that meets to share ideas and solve problems together) should be a small private group of 2 to 5 people.
  • Ideally, its members are owners who each spend less than $1,000 per month on Google Ads.
  • Members share information and work together on scaling profitable campaigns.
  • Online marketing is competitive.
  • A supportive group of peers makes the workload feel easier. It also helps keep momentum (forward speed) during scaling.
  • Joining a community of established store owners is a practical way to get a second set of eyes on a store.
  • For example, the community can give a peer review of the homepage (the front page of the website) and the policy pages (rules pages such as returns and shipping).
  • Brand ambassadors are fans who promote your brand. Build their community through a private Facebook group, with reasons to join such as brand appeal, a social cause, and rewards.
  • Aim to grow it to about 20,000 members. About 3,000 had been reached at the time of writing.

Discord and live calls

1 min

  • Source A live group coaching calls are held on the first Wednesday of each month at 2 PM EST (Eastern Standard Time, a US time zone).
  • The calls are hosted on Discord. Attendance is included in the membership as a bonus.
  • All live coaching calls are recorded.
  • Replays (recordings to watch later) of the previous three months are available. Missed calls can be watched within that window.
  • Source D live Q&A calls (question-and-answer sessions) take place every Thursday at 8 p.m. Singapore Time (SGT) in the Voice channel of the Discord community.
  • Weekly Source D Q&A calls cover common roadblocks, advanced strategies to scale faster, and live reviews of stores, ads and funnels (the step-by-step paths customers follow to buy).
  • You can ask anything in real time and receive direct feedback from the mentors.
  • Sources differ on schedule because they cover different programs. Source A's live calls run monthly on the first Wednesday. Source D Q&A runs weekly on Thursdays. Follow the schedule of the program you belong to.

Onboarding

1 min

New member onboarding in an online ecommerce community:

  1. Create your Shopify store first, using the trial link provided in the community.
  2. Join the Discord server and open a private support ticket. This is where support, coaching and progress tracking happen.
  3. In the ticket, introduce yourself with your name, your goals, and whether you are completely new or already experienced. This makes sure the right helper is assigned.
  4. Send a short greeting in the welcome channel to unlock the other channels.
  5. Open the course and start with the first part, then work through the parts in order.

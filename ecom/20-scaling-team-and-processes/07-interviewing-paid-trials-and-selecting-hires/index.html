Interviewing, paid trials and selecting hires

Section 20.7 · 7 of 26 in this chapter · 15 min read

In short

  • Give every applicant written instructions. Anyone who ignores them is out.
  • Hire for problem-solving, not the lowest cost. A cheap hire who cannot solve problems costs you more later.
  • Use a paid trial with real work before you hire. It shows more than an interview.
  • Keep one spreadsheet of all candidates, so you can compare them later.

Screening the applicant pool

1 min

Filtering on application answers and written instructions

1 min
  1. Ask each applicant if they have done a similar customer service job before. (Customer service means helping customers with their questions and problems.)
    • If yes, ask for a link to a company they worked for.
    • Ask for examples of typical emails they wrote. This shows how they communicate.
    • Decide if they are the right hire based on these answers.
  2. Read their follow-up answers for spelling and grammar.
    • Check whether the reply looks like a copied script.
    • Someone who will handle customer emails must write accurately and professionally.
  3. Treat slow replies to follow-up questions as a sign of low interest.
    • A candidate who takes a week to answer is likely applying to many jobs.
  4. Remove unsuitable candidates with specific written instructions.
    • Also send a test task that copies the real work. A test task is a small job that shows real skill.
  5. Add a simple test question to the job listing (the job advert), such as "What's 2+2?"
    • This checks whether applicants actually read the listing.
  6. Set the pass mark (the minimum score to pass) from your own data.
    • Score a few hundred applicants from each country you hire from.
    • Then choose the cutoff based on those averages.
    • A test you write yourself can be faked. So treat a candidate who skips it as the strongest reason to reject them.

Full screening sequence from one source

1 min

The source lists these steps in order. About 50 percent of applicants removed themselves at step 1. About another 30 percent were removed at step 2.

  1. Write a job post with specific application instructions, such as how to apply.
    • Remove applicants who do not follow them.
    • About 50 percent of applicants eliminated themselves this way.
  2. Ask screening questions about English level.
    • This removed roughly another 30 percent of applicants.
  3. Send the remaining candidates a test task with three written questions:
    • A reply to a very angry customer.
    • A reply to a bad review.
    • An action plan for an unshipped order that was the company's fault.
  4. Choose the best candidates. Send them a scheduling link for a 15-minute video call via Skype.
    • Skype is a free app for video calls and chat.
    • The scheduling link comes from Calendly, a free online booking tool.
  5. Spread the interviews over two or three days.
    • The total time is about two to three hours.
  6. Record every candidate's results in a Google spreadsheet (an online spreadsheet, like Excel) right after each call.
    • This lets you compare the best candidates later.
  7. Sign the contract before training starts. A contract is a signed agreement.

Setting the right candidate profile

2 min
  • Hire for talent and problem-solving ability, not for the lowest hourly cost.
    • A cheap hire who does not know the products forces the owner to answer unusual customer questions personally.
  • Hire for qualifications rather than the lowest cost.
    • Weak candidates need more training than they are worth.
    • A junior hire who needed too much training was dropped after the first month.
  • Hire overqualified people or people who already run their own business.
    • Overqualified means they have more skill or experience than the job needs.
    • They add skills and can later run other team members.
    • Being replaced by a strong hire is less of a threat than having weak support.
  • Expect freight problems. Freight means large shipments.
    • A store that ships freight will have these problems again and again.
    • Logistics means moving goods from supplier to customer.
    • You need a team member who can call freight companies, vendors (suppliers), and customers, and solve the issue.
    • Look for this problem-solving skill first.
    • Product knowledge and Shopify experience can be taught. (Shopify is software for building an online store.)
  • Check personality fit, since owners and assistants with very different styles can clash.
    • Have both parties take the free 16 Personalities test, an online personality test.
    • Compare the results. They can show strengths and weaknesses to manage.
  • Forbid other online jobs. Team members must not work for other online companies while working for you.
    • Screen for this during hiring.
    • Enforce it every time. This protects your knowledge, systems, and competitive advantage.
  • When screening virtual assistants (VAs), a VA is a remote helper who works online.
    • Give work history the most weight.
    • Favour candidates who worked for large, established companies, often in the US or Australia.
    • Find out why they left. A job at a small online store is a weaker signal. **
  • Screen VAs as carefully as a local employee, with a longer process and more checks.
    • In one year using this approach, about 8 assistants were hired and only 1 was let go. **

Using the Source C customer success hiring procedure

1 min

This is a reference procedure from the source. Interview rounds and pay differ between units. Both values are shown in the interview formats section below.

  1. Start with a Google Form screening. A Google Form is a free online survey tool.
    • Ask about dropshipping (selling products that a supplier ships to the customer for you), e-commerce (selling online), and tools such as Zoho, Zendesk, or email tools.
    • Zoho and Zendesk are customer support software tools.
  2. Run a multi-round interview. The source uses six rounds.
  3. Hire over Zoom, a video meeting app. Offer a salary of about $250 per month.
  4. Onboard the new hire with Loom recordings about the brand and systems.
    • Onboarding means the first training and setup.
    • Loom is a tool that records your screen as a video.
  5. Spend week one on shadow testing. This means practising the work on the job before taking on full duties.
    • Then take over all tasks from week two.

Interview formats

1 min

Choosing a format: typed chat or video call

1 min

Sources disagree on the medium. Use the format that fits the role.

Format Source When to use it
Typed chat on Skype or Slack, one question at a time Source B Hiring a VA or freelancer for mostly written, message-based work; you want a searchable written record
15-minute video call via Skype, after a written test Source A Adding a voice check to a written screening; the short call assesses fluency and fit
  • Interview by message, not by phone or video.
    • Use standardized template questions (the same set of prepared questions).
    • This method is more efficient. **
  • Type your interview instead of using voice or video.
    • Written chat shows how quickly someone replies and how well they write.
    • This matters because most daily work with a VA or freelancer happens by message.
    • A freelancer is a self-employed person who takes separate jobs. **
  • Use typed chat rather than recorded voice or video.
    • A written record is quick to search.
    • Finding one answer in an audio or video recording is much slower. **
  • Schedule video interviews via Skype within 2-3 days of application.
    • Keep each interview to 15 minutes.
    • Use Calendly to schedule.

Question set and interview opening

2 min
  • Prepare up to 15 interview questions.
    • Use the same core set for every candidate.
    • Change only small details to suit the role.
    • This keeps candidates comparable. **
  • Ask open questions during onboarding and interviews. Open questions need more than yes or no.
    • A strong VA asks many questions.
    • One who asks few will get stuck later without saying so.
    • Asking questions during onboarding is a good sign of a future good hire.

Shortlist invitation, ready to paste:

Hello [Candidate name],

Thank you for applying. You have been successful and shortlisted for an interview.

Can you attend an interview at [proposed time, adjusted to the candidate's time zone]? If this does not suit you, please tell me a time that works for you.

The interview will be held by typed message on [Skype or Slack]. Please send me your [Skype ID or email address for the Slack invite] so I can set it up.

Kind regards,
[Your name]

Interview opening message, ready to paste:

Hi [Candidate name], welcome to the interview. I will send you one question at a time. Please answer each question fully before I send the next one. There is no need to rush, but please reply in detail.

Run the typed interview in this order. A time zone is the local clock where someone lives.

  1. Send a success notice with an interview time that works in both time zones.
  2. State that the interview will be typed on Skype or Slack. Slack is a team chat app.
    • Ask for their Skype ID, or the email address for the Slack invite.
  3. Once the candidate has joined, post the introduction in the chat and send it.
  4. Send the first question and wait for the full typed answer.
  5. Send the next question only after the answer arrives. Repeat until all questions are asked.
  6. Thank the candidate and say when you will reply. This is typically within three days.

Keep the chat as a written record for later comparison. **

Stage-based video interviews

1 min

Interview stage 1 (Short Call):

  1. Duration: 10-15 minutes.
  2. You have already reviewed their written application answers and recording.
  3. Reintroduce the company. Explain what you do and what you need.
  4. Ask the candidate to introduce themselves.
  5. Assess English fluency (how easily they speak English), communication, and fit.
  6. Tell them this is one of many short calls. Only the best advance.
  7. Follow up by email to selected finalists for stage 2.

Interview stage 2 (Final Call):

  1. Duration: 45-60 minutes.
  2. Prepare case study materials. A case study is a real example the candidate works through.
    • Include a link to the website, SOPs (standard operating procedures, which are written step-by-step work guides), FAQs (frequently asked questions), and the product catalog (the list of all products).
  3. Send the materials before the call so they can prepare.
  4. During the call, ask realistic scenarios, such as "Does this product have [feature]?" or "What is the estimated delivery time?"
  5. The answer must come from the materials you sent. The candidate has not seen the products before.
  6. This tests problem-solving, product learning ability, and communication under pressure.
  7. Select the top 5-6 candidates for final interviews from approximately 400 applicants.
  • Conduct the final call yourself, even as you grow.
    • This call is when you decide who joins the team.
    • You need to know the person. They need to know you. You both commit to working together.
    • This builds accountability and culture from the hire's first day.

Multi-round interviews

1 min
  • Run six to seven interview rounds before hiring anyone. This tests the candidate's eagerness to learn.
    • Starting pay for candidates who passed was often 200 to 300 USD per month.
  • Hiring a VA frees up your time, so you can keep growing or scaling the business.
    • Choosing the right person matters more than hiring fast.
    • A poor fit costs money and progress.

Note: the Source C procedure above states six rounds and about $250 per month as salary. states six to seven rounds and 200 to 300 USD per month as starting pay. Use the range that matches your budget and the number of rounds you can staff.

Practical tests

1 min

Phone and site-navigation test

1 min

Interview line to add phone duties to a virtual assistant role:

By the way, we also get about one or two calls a day. Would you be able to answer those as they come in, in addition to the email work?

Testing a phone-answering VA candidate:

  1. Ask the candidate for a phone number. Say you will call later in the week.
  2. Call at random times, Monday to Friday, between 8 a.m. and 5 p.m. Do not announce a test.
  3. Count the rings before the candidate picks up.
  4. Assess how professional they sound and whether they are a native English speaker.
  5. Casually ask them to log into the store and open the homepage. Time how long it takes.
  6. Ask them to describe the homepage and read back the phone number shown on it.
  7. Ask them to find a specific product through the search bar or the menu.
  8. Ask them to read the note from the seller on that product.
  9. Ask them to find a second product and tell you how long it takes to ship. Time the search.

The test shows availability, professionalism, computer readiness, and store knowledge in one short call.

  • Testing by phone and site navigation reveals more than a CV (a résumé) or an interview.
    • It shows real availability and skills.
    • These are the same skills customers will need on a real call.

Product uploader sample

1 min
  1. Post a job or search for candidates with relevant experience.
  2. Review the work samples they have sent before.
  3. Give a small sample project of 10 to 30 products.
  4. Triple-check the sample work for correctness.
  5. Only then give the full project.

Test projects for freelancers

1 min
  • Start every new freelancer with a test project, such as writing part of an article.
    • Do this before committing to multiple pieces.
    • Use test projects to confirm quality and turnaround time (how fast the work comes back).
    • Use them to choose between several candidates by comparing their results. **
  • Test whether a VA can work alone before hiring them for a task.
    • Check if they can finish it without constant checking.
    • If you cannot trust them to work independently, they are not the right fit.

Paid trials

1 min

Trial offer and pay

1 min
  • Offer a paid half-day trial to the last three or four candidates.
    • Walk them through the processes as if they were already hired.
    • The trial reveals how they actually work. A phone call cannot show that.
  • An owner had two strong candidates on paper and could not choose between them after interviews.
    • A paid half-day trial made one candidate's superior performance clear.
    • That candidate was hired.
  • A paid half-day trial carries a small risk. A candidate who sees the business processes could start a competing store.
    • The owner judged this risk highly unlikely and accepted it.
    • Real work on the trial shows how a candidate performs better than an interview.
  • Do not hire the successful candidate outright.
    • Tell them they are successful and offer a paid trial first.
    • Pay the trial at the full rate for the position, because they do the real work. **

Trial steps

1 min
  1. Tell the successful candidate they are selected and offer a trial.
  2. Provide the SOP for the task so they can do it properly.
  3. Walk them through an onboarding session to confirm they understand the task.
  4. Define the trial parameters, such as its length or deliverable (what they must hand in). These depend on the task.
  5. Review the result at the end of the trial and decide whether to continue. **

Trial length and deliverable

1 min
Role Trial length or deliverable Source
Customer service One-week trial, with a review at the end Source B
Content production (e.g., blog writing) First piece of work, for example one blog post Source B
Any role, owner-led Paid half-day trial, walking through processes as if already hired Source A

Trial and delivery length depend on the role.

  • The half-day trial is the approach for the final three or four candidates in the Source A source.
  • The one-week trial is the Source B approach for customer service.

Judging the trial

1 min
  • A trial does not need to be perfect, since everyone makes mistakes in a new role.
    • Small issues that you can fix are acceptable. You can develop the person.
    • A large quality gap means the trial is failed. **

Trial failure message, ready to paste:

Hi [Candidate name],

Thank you for your work on the trial. Here is your payment for the trial.

Unfortunately you were not successful in the trial, so we will not be offering you ongoing work. Thank you for your time, and good luck for the future.

[Your name]

**

Evaluating candidates

1 min

Checks after each interview

1 min

Evaluate each interview using these checks, in order:

  1. Did the candidate clearly agree to the pay, the hours, and any specific hours?
    • If not, it is a red flag (a warning sign). The candidate is not hired.
  2. How many other clients does the candidate have?
    • A large number is a red flag for part-time or full-time roles.
    • They will not be able to give the hours they promise.
  3. Did the candidate answer in detail?
    • Short answers of a few words are a red flag.
    • Multi-line answers are a positive sign.
  4. How quickly did the candidate reply?
    • A long delay for a simple question suggests divided attention.
    • Slow understanding of the question is also a red flag.
  5. Did the answers feel honest and genuine, rather than like fluff written to please you?
    • Trust your gut feeling (your instinct) here.
  6. Does the candidate fit your company culture? Culture fit means how well they match your way of working.

Any red flag on these checks means the candidate is removed. **

  • The interview does not test the task itself, since most people learn the task quickly.
    • It is used to assess speed, attention to detail, culture fit, and red flags.
    • The job application already gives a good indication of experience. **
  • Attention to detail is required for any VA or freelancer role.
    • Those roles follow SOPs and cannot be run loosely.
    • High-level thinking is the owner's job. It does not replace attention to detail in these roles. **
  • A candidate who takes about 10 minutes to start replying to one interview question is probably dividing attention with other work.
    • Slow replies during the interview are a warning sign for future responsiveness. **
  • If a part-time or full-time candidate already works with around 10 other clients, they are unlikely to deliver the hours they promise, even if they say otherwise.
    • Treat a high number of other clients as a red flag for hours. **

Reference checks and verification

1 min
  • On Upwork (a freelance job website), recent hires have sometimes claimed experience they did not have.
    • Verify work samples and experience before hiring. **
  • Ask for a link to a company the candidate has worked for in a similar role.
    • Also ask for examples of typical email correspondence they handled.
  • Find out why the candidate left previous large, established companies.
    • This shows more than the CV alone. **
  • Compare the sample work sent by the candidate with a small sample project.
    • Do this before the full project is given.

Selecting the hire

1 min

Deciding between finalists

1 min
  • If two or three candidates pass all checks, choose the one with:
    • The strongest gut feeling,
    • The strongest relevant experience, or
    • The best fit with your culture.
    • Give candidates a decision date, typically around three days after the interview. **
  • A negative gut feeling during the interview stage rarely improves later.
    • You will work with the person often, sometimes daily.
    • Move on to another applicant when the gut feeling is negative. **
  • The first 3-6 months reveal whether a win-win exists. A win-win means good for both sides.
    • For you: business value.
    • For the team: culture fit.
    • For the employee: fair pay, growth, and respect.
    • Set this expectation during hiring. Both sides should know early termination is possible if it is not working.
    • This removes surprises and keeps the focus on actual fit.

Rejecting candidates and avoiding rushed hiring

1 min
  • Thank unsuccessful applicants and keep good relations with them.
    • They can be called on for a second assistant hire later.
  • Rushing is costly. Rushing the hiring process, skipping steps, or skipping paid trials leads to the wrong hires.
    • You then pay for work that does not return value.
    • You either pay someone to redo it or spend your own time fixing it. **
  • Hiring a VA frees up your time, so you can keep growing or scaling the business.
    • Choosing the right person matters more than hiring quickly.
    • A poor fit costs money and progress.
  • Remove any candidate who fails a check in the evaluation sequence.
    • Send the failure message to those who completed a paid trial without success.

Further details

1 min
  • Keep a candidate tracking spreadsheet for all applicants, interview notes, and hiring decisions.

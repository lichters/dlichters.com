Team structure, roles and work tools

Section 20.11 · 11 of 26 in this chapter · 13 min read

In short

  • Give every task one clear owner. One person does the work, and one person answers for the result.
  • Start with a virtual team (remote helpers). You do not need an office until monthly sales near $500,000.
  • Add a project manager when monthly sales reach about $20,000 to $100,000.
  • Split the work by role. Do not give one helper everything.
This section has 9 parts. Tap one to open it.

Ownership and accountability

1 min
  • The RACI matrix (a chart that shows who does what) gives four role types to every task and function:
Role Meaning
Responsible The person who does the work
Accountable The person accountable for the outcome
Consulted People whose input and ideas are sought
Informed People kept in the loop

Map every deliverable (a finished piece of work) across your team to these roles. Simplify and review whenever you add a channel (a place where people talk or send work, such as a chat group), make a new hire, or change a process.

  • A RACI matrix prevents confusion and competing priorities. It does this by creating clear ownership and structured handoff sequences (set orders for passing work from one person to the next). Without it, several team members pursue conflicting directions for the same task.
  • Each new hire takes over the backup role (covering for someone else when they are away) and tasks of the previous hire. This frees the owner and gives the team a clear chain of responsibility. Continue handing out tasks until the owner is only doing sales and high-level work.
  • Shared tools make the business transparent. The owner can see order status, check whether emails are answered, and read reports in Asana (a task-tracking app) and the help desk (the tool that holds customer messages). The owner can also reach the VA (virtual assistant, a remote helper) directly on Slack (a team chat app) when something is open.

Team structure by revenue phase

3 min
  • Phase guidance, with both thresholds (the cut-off numbers) from the sources and when each applies:
Monthly sales What applies Ids
Up to roughly $500,000 A virtual team is fine for drop shipping. An office and an in-house team are not needed.
$20,000 to $100,000 The business moves toward a real brand and marketing gains weight. A project manager takes over leading the team toward performance goals. ,
Roughly $500,000 and above The source gives the $500,000 point as the threshold for an office and an in-house team. It gives no separate rule above that level.

  • Both thresholds apply together. A virtual team with a project manager is the expected setup at $20,000 to $100,000 per month. The office threshold of about $500,000 comes later.

  • At $20,000 to $100,000 per month, a project manager (the person who leads the team toward goals) monitors the customer service and influencer outreach staff (people who contact online content creators to promote products) against performance goals. The project manager can also help hire new people. Expect to pay about $8 to $12 per hour on Monazar or Upwork (online hiring sites).

  • Virtual teams stay connected by adding everyone to the same Slack channel (a chat room inside Slack). This includes virtual assistants doing different tasks, such as product research (finding products to sell) and product image sourcing (finding product photos). This shows them that they are part of one company.

  • Define which team roles the business will need, such as ads managers (people who run paid ads), customer service reps (people who answer customers), graphic designers and videographers. Decide whether these staff are based overseas or local in an office. **

  • An operations manager (the person who runs the daily work) can run the business day to day, so the owner only checks in weekly. In a larger setup, one operations manager can oversee a network of businesses while the owner sits at the top. **

  • A typical team structure, **:

    • The owner handles research and supplier connections (links with companies that make or ship products) plus ad management, 1 to 5 hours per week.
    • A business or operations manager oversees support and sales leads (sales lead and support lead).
    • Design and copywriting teams (people who write sales text) report directly to the business manager.

  • Keep one team across all stores rather than separate teams per store. Operations and marketing teams can serve every store. **

  • 10 virtual assistants and freelancers (independent workers hired per job) can cover supplier sourcing (finding suppliers) for high-ticket dropshipping (selling expensive items that the supplier ships directly to the customer), website design, product uploads, product optimization (improving product pages), product descriptions and graphic design. They can also handle occasional smaller tasks. **

  • A content-led brand (a brand that grows mainly through videos and posts) can run with a small team. The owner films the videos, and a video editor handles editing. A part-time social media person posts for about three hours per day, and a full-time designer handles visuals. Divide the work so the owner focuses on creating content.

  • When scaling (growing sales fast) a dropshipping business, split responsibilities across distinct roles:

    • Product research and scaling strategy
    • Branding and creative testing (trying different ads to see what works)
    • Paid ads and funnel optimization (improving the path from ad click to purchase)
    • Operations, fulfillment and backend systems (packing, shipping and behind-the-scenes tools)

    Each role covers a different part of the business, so the whole operation gets full coverage.

  • Operations-focused people who handle fulfillment and backend systems are also well placed to negotiate with suppliers and designers. They can also keep stores streamlined (simple and easy to run) for sustainable growth. Assigning supplier negotiation to the person who runs operations keeps the fulfillment side and the supplier side aligned.

  • One company grew to about 15 people, split between the US and the Philippines. Roles covered web design, operations, social media and support. Support was divided into live chat, phone and email. **

  • As the business grows, managers handle daily tasks. The owner checks the business at a high level by tracking the main drivers of revenue (the things that bring in money). These include ad spend (money paid for ads), return on ad spend (ROAS, sales made for each dollar spent on ads), support response times and reviews. **

Accountability chart and org structure

3 min

**

  • An accountability chart is an org chart (a diagram of who is in charge of whom). It shows each role's responsibilities and who holds each person accountable. Draw it bigger than the current team so it shows the structure the business is growing into. Treat it as a living document (one you keep updating).

  • In a Traction-style accountability chart (based on the Traction business system), the top roles are called:

    • Visionary, who owns ideas, R&D (research and development, testing new ideas), culture and market fit (whether people want the product).
    • Integrator, who owns systems, the business plan and targets.

    These labels replace CEO (chief executive, the top boss) and COO (chief operating officer) titles.

  • Every business has three components: sales and marketing, operations, and finance and admin. Each has a director. The top leader should have only these three direct reports (people who report straight to them).

  • Split a combined role into two hires when the workload becomes too big for one person:

    • Customer communication goes to customer service.
    • Supplier work, order management and order processing go to order processing.

    A later split can separate sales from customer service when the business gets busy.

  • Once several marketing specialists such as SEO, PPC and content writers are in place, hire a marketing manager to coordinate them. This takes the owner out of implementing training material and day-to-day marketing work.
  • Add the expected team size to the business description for each horizon (time frame), because it is a concrete marker of growth. A 1-year picture might show a few employees, while a 3-year picture might show around 10 staff. Keep the description to a short overview.

Dividing roles and delegating

1 min

  • Assign each team member the roles that match their strengths. One VA handled support and operations. Another VA, with graphics and Shopify back-end skills (Shopify is an online store builder; back-end means the behind-the-scenes setup), built additional stores. (Older advice: source year not stated)

  • Split the workload across assistants rather than giving one person everything. For example, two assistants can each manage the same two stores, so no individual is overloaded.

  • Virtual assistants for live chat, phone calls and order shipping should be separated from social media and content roles. One person handling all of them quickly breaks down.

  • Do not expect one virtual assistant to handle every task well. Group related tasks, for example email plus live chat plus phone. Define clear goals and tracking for each role. **

  • Split influencer work across dedicated roles:

    • One person for micro-influencers (creators with small followings)
    • One for mid-level and high-level deals (bigger creators and bigger contracts)
    • One for content curation (choosing and organizing content)

    Three people covered the influencer function at one brand.

  • A B2B (business-to-business, selling to companies) sales VA with real estate experience was close to closing an order worth about 18,000 for a government buyer. The owner had planned to offer an extra 200 discount. The VA reduced the discount and still closed the deal. Give experienced sales staff authority to set final pricing within agreed limits.

Hiring with managers

1 min
  • Involve team managers and leaders in the hiring process from the beginning:
    1. Have them help create screening questions (questions that filter candidates), read candidate answers, and push people through the hiring funnel (the steps from applicant to hire) in Asana.
    2. Have them conduct the first 10 to 15 minute interview call (short call).
    3. Keep the final decision for yourself.
  • This gives leaders ownership of their team, involvement in culture (the team's shared way of working), and investment in new hires. It is more effective than hiring a manager for an existing team.
  • A manager who has learned a documented hiring process (written steps everyone follows) can run a hire on their own. The owner supplies the content. Expect to coach the manager closely on the first hire. Expect the second hire to be run without the owner. **

Capacity measurement

1 min
  • Measure operating capacity (how much work the business can handle) by asking the team how busy they are. Ask whether orders, customer questions or supplier orders are slipping through. Late orders and forgotten supplier orders are signs that the current capacity limit has been reached. **
  • Realistic capacity can turn out well above the first estimate once a strong team is in place. Reassess capacity as the team grows instead of relying on the initial number. **
  • Measuring what a virtual assistant actually does reveals how much capacity they have. It is common to underestimate it and overload them. Track their workload with time data before assigning more tasks. Review the results when hiring additional assistants.
  • Time tracking tools such as Hubstaff (software that records work hours) and the Upwork time tracker log and monitor the working hours of remote team members.

Workflow boards and task management

2 min
  • Create separate boards (visual task lists) for workflows (step-by-step ways work moves) with different timelines. Examples are regular quotes, white glove installation quotes (a premium service where a crew delivers and sets up the product), product upload and content. White glove installation requests can come in at three or four per day. They need separate follow-up with both the manufacturer and the customer. Keep a separate board for each workflow to avoid mixing them. **
  • Monday.com (alternatives: Asana or Trello, both task apps) is used for task management (tracking who does what). It stores social media planners, works-in-progress (work that has started but not finished), OKRs (a goal-tracking method built on objectives and key results) and daily deliverables in one accessible location.
  • Source A uses Asana for the hiring funnel and for team reports. The weakest part of the system is the manual updating of statuses, notes and due dates. That depends on the VA remembering each update. Where possible, choose tools that update statuses, notes and due dates automatically.
  • Trello is a kanban-style project board (a board of columns and cards that move from stage to stage). It is used to track tasks, assign owners and manage recurring processes when a team or virtual assistants are involved.
  • Basecamp is a project management tool for team tasks. Its small plans are affordable for a startup team. It can track monthly promotions with assigned roles and responsibilities. **
  • Google Drive stores day-to-day files and shared documents. **
  • Grammarly is a free grammar and formatting tool for Gmail and other writing platforms. It catches repetitions, run-on sentences (long sentences that run together without a proper break) and spelling errors. It does this in professional email communication with suppliers. **
  • Working roadmap prompt (a ready-made instruction you give an AI tool):
Turn the 30-day roadmap into a shorter working version. Give one line per action, grouped by week one, week two, week three and week four. Separate fixes to do now from testing ideas, and keep only the metric each action should improve. Then assign every action to a named team member with their responsibility.

Communication

1 min
  • Slack is used for informal team communication and rapid information sharing.
  • Slack keeps distributed team members (people working in different places) connected. Add everyone to the same channel, including virtual assistants doing different tasks. This shows them that they see themselves as part of one company.

ClickUp for personal and team task management

2 min

  • Set up ClickUp (a task app with a free plan) for personal task management:

    1. Create separate Spaces (top-level folders in ClickUp) for each area, such as life management, the e-commerce brand, and mentoring. Keep the brands in separate folders.
    2. In each list (a group of tasks), add tasks with an assignee (the person who does it), a priority, a due date, and a description.
    3. Break tasks into subtasks (small tasks inside a bigger task) and sub-steps.
    4. Use the List view as the default for simple to-do/done lists.
    5. Use the Board view for status-based pipelines (lines of work with stages) such as creatives (ads and visuals) (Ideas, Scripting, Editing, Done).
    6. Use the Calendar view to see all tasks with due dates across the workspace.
    7. Filter the Everything view (a view showing all tasks) by priority to see the daily top tasks.
    8. Save a priority view and work from it when time is free.

  • ClickUp is a free task management tool with list, board, calendar and whiteboard views (different ways to look at the same tasks). It also has a Docs feature for dynamic documents (documents that can change into tasks). Docs can turn text into tasks. It also has custom fields (extra labels you add to tasks), threaded comments (replies grouped under one comment), and a Gmail extension (a small add-on that works inside Gmail) for turning emails into tasks.

  • ClickUp custom fields: add a custom field of type Drop Down (a list of options to pick from) with options such as strategy call, PDF, case study and video library. This way each deliverable shows its type. Add fields via the list's column settings or from the field library.

  • ClickUp views for different processes:

View Use for
List Simple to-do lists
Board Statuses (to do, done, or pipeline stages)
Calendar Deadlines across lists
Whiteboard Brainstorming creative hooks (opening lines or images that grab attention) and angles
Docs Processes and reference material
  • When a team starts producing creatives or deliverables, use a status pipeline (a line of stages each item moves through). Use it to track each item from idea to shortlist, scripting, storyboarding (drawing scenes before filming), editing and final. Move items across stages as they progress.
  • ClickUp Docs can turn a text item into a task. Select the text, create a task from it, add it to a board such as brand creatives, then set priority and due date.
  • Organize work by separating areas (brand, mentoring, life management). When a calendar block (a set time slot on your calendar) is set for one area, only tasks of that area appear. This avoids distraction from other work.

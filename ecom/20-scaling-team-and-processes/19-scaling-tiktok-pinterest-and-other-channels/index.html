Scaling TikTok, Pinterest and other channels

Section 20.19 · 19 of 26 in this chapter · 25 min read

In short

  • Keep Google Ads as your main source of visitors until sales reach about $30,000 to $50,000 a month.
  • Fix your payment setup and money flow before you scale hard.
  • Test each new TikTok product with a small, simple setup. Stop any part that spends its break-even amount without a sale.
  • Post 3 to 4 videos a day on TikTok, and make more of any video that goes viral.
  • Use separate phones and emails for each TikTok account.
More key points
  • Cut any ad that spent its break-even cost per sale without a sale. Break-even cost per sale is the most you can pay for one sale and still not lose money.
  • Only raise spending (scaling) on what already sells.
  • Test new Pinterest products small, at $5 per day.
  • Copy a product that sells every day into a second account, and protect it with your own content.

Deciding When to Add a Second Channel

2 min
  • A channel is a place where you show ads or get visitors, such as Google, Facebook or TikTok.
  • Keep Google Ads (Google's paid ad system) as your main traffic source until the store makes about $30,000 to $50,000 a month in sales. Then consider adding other channels. This is older advice from 2019.
  • At monthly sales of about $5,000 to $20,000, do not take time away from Google Ads to build other channels. This is true even if Google seems not to grow. This is older advice from 2019.
  • Channels like Pinterest (a site of pictures and ideas) work, but they take time to learn.
  • Adding them too early pulls your focus away from the main Google setup. This is older advice from 2019.
  • Move to other channels when Google growth stalls. Growth stalls when bigger budgets no longer add sales. This is older advice from 2019.
  • Then test Pinterest properly. Also try Facebook and Instagram ads aimed at cold audiences (people who do not know your brand yet). This is older advice from 2019.
  • When you hit the profitable limit of Google Shopping (product ads shown in Google search), more traffic must come from new sources.
  • These can be organic search (free search results, not paid) and social media.
  • Pushing harder inside Google Shopping does not fix this. This is advice from Source B to 2022.
  • Once Google Shopping cannot grow further at a 10X return (ten dollars back for each dollar spent on ads), it is time to consider other ad platforms.
  • Scale a winning product on Facebook until its audience runs out. Then test TikTok ads for it.
  • Most products in this approach performed best on Facebook.
  • If a store is profitable but not growing, open another channel such as Google. This turns the business into an ecosystem (a group of channels that work together). More channels give more touch points (moments when a customer sees your brand).
  • Build an ecosystem of channels (Facebook, TikTok, Google). Each channel covers gaps the others leave. This matters especially as Q4 (the last three months of the year) approaches.
  • Growth is limited by your payment setup and cash flow (how money comes in and goes out), not by the number of TikTok accounts.
  • Fix the setup, systems and rules before you scale hard.
  • Direct-response TV and radio (ads that ask for an immediate reaction) are an option to consider once social ads work.
  • They need large spending and carry financial risk if they fail. Test before you commit big budgets.

Fallback When a Main Ad Account Is Suspended

1 min
  • An ad account is the account that runs your paid ads. A suspended account is one the platform has blocked.
  • While a main Facebook ad account is suspended, keep testing products on other channels. These can be Snapchat, Pinterest, TikTok and Twitter.
  • Then move to Google Ads. Return to Facebook once it is restored.
  • Running several channels at the same time lowers your risk if one account gets blocked.

Small Budgets and the Five-Month Checklist

1 min
  • On a small budget, growth can come from a unique angle or a viral post (one that spreads fast). Growth does not have to come from outbidding competitors on ads.
  • A post that gets comments, shares and reactions can be boosted for about $5. You can choose who sees it with targeting (picking the people an ad reaches). This is cheaper than a bidding war (competing on price for the same ad space).
  • Five-month marketing checklist when Google Ads alone is not enough:
    1. Run Bing Shopping (Microsoft's product ads).
    2. Use influencer marketing (paying creators with followers to promote you).
    3. Use blog banner advertising (ads shown on blog pages).
    4. Make SEO (search engine optimization, getting pages to rank higher in search for free) your highest priority.
    5. Run paid social traffic campaigns (paid ads on social media that send visitors to your store).
    6. Use referral marketing (rewarding customers who bring in new customers).
    7. Run email marketing (messages sent to people who signed up).

TikTok Organic: Posting and Momentum

1 min
  • Organic means free posts, with no ad spend. Momentum means a video's growing reach and attention.
  • The standard is 3 videos a day, and 4 on a day when content goes viral.
  • For accounts with the time to make them, aim for 4 to 6 videos a day.
  • After a video goes viral, post more. Do not hesitate to make similar videos.
  • Repeat a proven format with small changes. Change the sound, wording, color or movement. This can keep the momentum going.
  • Many creators stop posting after one viral hit. They fear it will damage the account.
  • This wastes the momentum. A viral moment should be used by making more output.
  • Uploading inconsistently, such as one upload a day with long gaps, lets momentum fade.
  • Keep uploading regularly to get the most from any breakout video.
  • Once organic videos prove they sell, fund paid ads from the profit those videos already made. Do not use your own money.
  • Keep this order: organic content first, then paid ads to maximize sales, then brand building once the product is proven. Brand building means making people know and trust your name.

TikTok Accounts: Number and Setup

1 min
  • You can run 2 to 3 or more TikTok accounts on the same phone.
  • You do not need to buy extra devices. Log in and out as needed.
  • Use a separate email address for each account.
  • Give each account a purpose: testing and research, product promotion, or personal use.
  • Sources differ on the limit per phone.
  • One source says no more than three accounts per phone, ideally two. It mentions about seven as a maximum.
  • A ban on one account can hurt the others on the same device.
  • Use separate phones for separate products. Keep successful accounts apart from experimental ones.
  • This protects your working account when new tests are added.
  • Do not log into one TikTok account from two phones at the same time.
  • Share finished video files through a cloud folder (online storage). Let one person upload to the account.
  • Split team roles. One person builds the store while the other creates content.
  • This lets the content creator test products faster.

TikTok Account Structure and Prospecting Setup

1 min
  • A prospecting campaign shows ads to people who do not know you yet.
  • A campaign is the top level of an ad setup. Inside it are ad sets (groups of ads with one audience and one budget).
  • TikTok ad account structure should be simple and broad.
  • You do not need dozens of campaigns or many ad sets per campaign. The platform performs best with broad targeting (a wide audience with no narrow filters).
  • Start with no more than five ad groups per campaign. Three is a good starting number.
  • A typical start includes broad and recommended interest ad sets. It also includes one hashtag ad set. Hashtag ad sets often underperform over time.
  • Set up a prospecting campaign with three ad sets:
    1. Ad set 1 is broad targeting. Include both genders and all ages except 13 to 17. Add no interests.
    2. Ad set 2 uses TikTok's Recommended Interests (interest groups TikTok suggests). Click the use these button.
    3. Ad set 3 uses hashtag targeting (showing ads to people who watch videos with chosen hashtags). Use one ad set per hashtag.
  • Start a new TikTok prospecting campaign with Lower Cost (maximized conversions, meaning the platform tries to get the most purchases for your budget). Do not use Value Optimization yet.
  • Value Optimization aims at buyers who spend more. It needs customer data to work. Without data, performance is poor.
  • TikTok Recommended Interests is the best interest-targeting option.
  • In the speaker's tests, it beat manually entered interests in 9.5 out of 10 tests.
  • Use the recommended interests as they are, even if some look odd.
  • For hashtag targeting, use about three hashtags. Put each in its own ad set, so you can see which one gets results.
  • Hashtag targeting is not yet available in every ad account. Hashtags tied to a brand or a specific product may not show as options.

TikTok Budget Structure: ABO and CBO

1 min
  • ABO (ad set budget optimization) means you set the budget for each ad set.
  • CBO (campaign budget optimization) means you set one budget for the whole campaign. The algorithm (the platform's automatic decision system) splits it across ad sets.
  • For testing, split the budget by ad set (ABO), not by campaign (CBO).
  • CBO can send most of the budget to one ad set. That makes the test unfair.
  • The speaker prefers ABO at all stages. He notes CBO may improve as TikTok's algorithm matures, as Facebook's did.
  • Example of ABO: set $10 each for three ad sets.
  • Example of CBO: set a total of $30 for the campaign. The algorithm spreads it across ad sets.
  • Under CBO, ad sets can train each other. Data from a strong ad set can help a broad ad set find buyers.
  • With ABO, ad sets optimize separately. They do not share data.
  • Example of the problem with CBO: with a $30 campaign budget, the algorithm can give about $25 a day to one ad set. The other two get only about $2.50 a day each.
  • This is why ABO gives each ad set an equal budget. It makes a split test fair.
  • Source disagreement: in the operator's experience, CBO performed poorly on TikTok. It had also done so on Facebook when it was introduced. So budgets were set manually per campaign.
  • Source disagreement: later notes use CBO campaigns for scaling and for the staged budget ladder (a step-by-step budget increase). This is used once a creative and audience are proven. See the scaling sections below.

TikTok Scaling: Horizontal and Vertical

2 min
  • Scaling means growing your budget and sales.
  • Horizontal scaling means copying a winning ad set. Vertical scaling means raising the budget of the same ad set step by step.
  • Scaling procedure for TikTok ads:
    1. Run one campaign for scaling and one for testing.
    2. Judge performance by comparing Google Analytics (a tool that tracks visitors) with TikTok ad results. Use UTMs (tags added to links that show where visitors came from).
    3. If a campaign performs well, duplicate it three times.
    4. Double the budget on the duplicates.
    5. Run a separate testing campaign for new creatives (ad videos or images) all the time. Move creatives that beat the benchmark (the target result) into the scaling campaign.
  • Vertical versus horizontal scaling on TikTok:
    1. Horizontal scaling: duplicate a winning ad set without changing anything. For example, $25 a day becomes $50 across two copies.
    2. Vertical scaling: raise the budget of the winning ad set step by step. For example: $25, $50, $75, $100.
    3. Use horizontal scaling at lower budgets, around $25 a day. The percentage jump from vertical scaling is large there. A large jump can trigger re-optimization (the platform restarting its learning).
    4. Use vertical scaling at higher budgets, around $500 a day. A rise from 500 to 700 is a small percentage change.
    5. Split test both methods (compare two versions fairly). Keep the one that works best.
  • A simple account with broad targeting and few campaigns is reported to have scaled smoothly to about $5,000 a day. It is claimed it can scale to about $10,000 a day.
  • Scaling happens by raising the budget of ad sets with a positive ROAS (return on ad spend, meaning sales divided by ad cost).
  • Duplicating an ad set that already has enough data can move the copy automatically toward value optimization.
  • Using value optimization only makes sense after data exists. Early value optimization without data can perform very badly.
  • Value optimization transition on TikTok:
    1. After the ad set has enough data, duplicate the best-performing ad set.
    2. Switch the copy from Lower Cost to Value Optimization.
    3. Scale that copy by raising its budget.
    4. Do not switch to Value Optimization early, when there is no data. Results can go badly wrong.

TikTok Staged Budget Ladder (CBO)

1 min
  • Staged scaling procedure for TikTok CBO campaigns:
    1. Confirm that CPC (cost per click) is below $1, CPM (cost per 1,000 views) is below $10, CTR (click-through rate, the share of viewers who click) is above 1.5%, and the store conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy) is good.
    2. Start from the initial CBO with a daily budget of $50.
    3. Double the budget to $100 and monitor results for three hours.
    4. If results stay consistent or improve, raise the budget to $150 after three hours.
    5. If results still hold, raise it to $200 after another three hours.
    6. If results still hold, raise it to $250 after another three hours.
    7. At the end of the day, reset the budget to $50, so the next day starts from scratch. Then repeat steps 3 to 6.
  • Each increase waits three hours.
  • The budget reaches $250 nine hours after the first move to $100. The method then ends with the end-of-day reset to $50.

  • If results get worse after a budget increase, reset the budget to $50. Retry the scaling sequence the next day.
  • Higher budgets bring more reach, more traffic to the store, and more sales.
  • So budget increases should be staged and checked for consistency. Do not make them all at once.
  • When several CBOs perform well, increase the budget on all of them. Use the same staged scaling method.

TikTok Creative Testing and Break-Even

2 min
  • Break-even means the point where sales cover your costs. Here, it is the product profit.
  • Break-even test per creative: let each creative spend up to the product profit. For example, $20 profit allows $20 spend per creative.
  • Keep a creative running only if it produces a purchase. Otherwise, kill it (turn it off).
  • TikTok ads work like a casino in their first two days, when they deliver the best traffic.
  • A creative with no sales in those two days is unlikely to start selling later. Stop it.
  • Starting a testing phase: begin with one ad group per creative.
  • With 10 creatives, that means 10 ad groups at $20 a day each, with no interest targeting.
  • Vertical scaling on TikTok with ad groups:
    1. Find more winning creatives first. With only three winners, scaling to large numbers is unrealistic.
    2. Create variants of the best creatives. Change small elements such as the music, the first two seconds, or the hook (the opening that grabs attention).
    3. When several creatives perform, raise each ad group budget from $20 to $100 a day.
    4. Stack the top three best creatives into one ad group, instead of one creative per ad group.
    5. Each day at about 11:30 to 11:45 pm (before midnight), increase the budget of these ad groups by $20. For example: $100 to $120, then $140, and so on.
  • Judge creatives by CTR and CPC, and cut ad groups that reached the break-even cost without sales.
  • In the testing stage, the goal is to learn which ad group, audience and creative works. It is not to get immediate sales from every test.
  • If the landing page (the page visitors arrive on) is good, and add-to-cart and checkout rates are healthy, you can turn off an ad group. Do this if it spent the break-even cost without a sale.
  • The purpose of the testing stage is not an immediate sale. It is finding which audience, creative and ad group works. Judge this by CPC and CTR.
  • When sales are unclear, judge the creative by CTR.
  • An ad with a CTR of 1 percent or more across ad groups was the strongest candidate in the test.
  • A high CPM on some TikTok ad accounts can distort results. So a poor result is not always caused by the product or creative.
  • Check the account before you blame the test.

TikTok Manual Bidding Scaling

1 min
  • Manual bidding means you set the highest price you will pay for each result yourself.
  • Manual bidding scaling on TikTok:
    1. Create a new manual bidding campaign once you have several winning creatives and winning ad groups.
    2. Set the daily budget per ad group between $100 and $300, depending on your total budget.
    3. Create five ad groups with different target cost-per-purchase values. Start from the average cost per purchase. For example: average $12, then $11.50, $11, $10.50 and $10.
    4. Lower-target ad groups may perform well. They can buy purchases at a lower cost.
    5. Do not raise budgets in a straight line. Duplicate a well-performing ad group and double its budget. For example: $100 to $200, then $400, or a $300 to $400 range, as used.
    6. Watch the ad groups closely. Manual bidding can overspend suddenly.
  • Manual bidding on TikTok can suddenly overspend. For example, it can spend $100 to $200 a day without matching purchases. This can happen even when the budget is set to $300 and earlier spend was low.
  • Monitor these ad groups daily.
  • Scaling a manual bidding ad group on TikTok: do not raise its budget in a straight line.
  • Duplicate the ad group that performed well. Set the duplicate's budget to double the original. For example: $100 to $200, then $300 to $400 with the next duplicate.
  • Keep checking each duplicate. Overspend can happen suddenly.

TikTok Ad Group Scaling and Replacement

1 min
  • Scale by duplicating winning ad groups.
  • Consider switching to cost cap bidding (a limit on your average cost per purchase) once a campaign has about 50 or more purchases.
  • Replace underperforming ad groups with new creatives. Do not let them run unchanged.
  • If a test campaign is in its last four to five hours of the day, do not change it mid-day.
  • Instead, launch new ad groups the next day with fresh creatives.
  • Compare the next day's results across creatives. Identify which part of the funnel (the steps from ad to sale) fails.

TikTok Test Campaign Structures

2 min
  • Test structure: one campaign with five ad groups. These are the original plus four duplicates. Each holds two to four video ads.
  • Let the test campaign run for two days before you judge results.
  • Testing and scaling in order, with one campaign of three to four ad groups:
    1. Launch one campaign with three to four ad groups. For example: open audience, stacked interest (several interests combined), hashtags, and another open or stacked interest audience.
    2. Set each ad group budget to about $25 to $30. Let it run the first day.
    3. Put in the three best creatives per ad group. Use one ad copy (the written text) with an offer line (a sentence with a deal).
    4. Judge creatives by CTR and CPC. Cut ad groups that reached the break-even cost without sales.
    5. Once one creative and audience are winning, build CBO campaigns. See the aggressive scaling procedure below.
  • Testing structure for a new product on TikTok:
    1. Create one campaign with three to four ad groups. Each has a different audience. For example: open targeting, a stacked interest audience, a hashtag audience, and one more open or stacked audience.
    2. Set each ad group budget to $25 to $30. A $20 ad group budget caused spending problems on new accounts.
    3. Add the three best creatives to each ad group. Name them add 1, add 2, add 3. Use the same ad copy in each group.
    4. Use one ad copy with an offer line, such as "70% off", "get yours today" or "ending soon".
    5. Schedule the ads to launch at 6 am Eastern time (or 8 am). Spend then begins around 9 am, after the 30 to 40 minute ramp (the warm-up time before delivery speeds up).
    6. Let the test run for the first day. Then judge by CPC, CTR and sales.
    7. Turn off ad groups that have spent the break-even amount without a sale, when the landing page is performing well.
  • A winning product run through the TikTok scaling structure typically shows about 2 to 5 sales on day one and about 10 to 15 sales on day two.
  • Results far below this suggest the test has not yet found a winner.

TikTok Aggressive CBO Scaling

2 min
  • Aggressive CBO scaling on TikTok, in order:
    1. Once a winning creative and audience are known, launch five CBO campaigns. Each gets $150 and has four ad groups.
    2. Use the same single winning creative and ad copy in each ad group. Use open targeting: US, age 18+, all genders, no interests.
    3. Use manual payment and add no more than $50 at a time.
    4. After each campaign has spent about $50 total, check results. Cut campaigns without a sale if any other campaign has one.
    5. Cut any campaign whose CPC is above $1. Keep the one with the best CPC.
    6. Scale the winning campaign after three profitable sales. First raise it to $300, then to $500 or more a day, in steps.
  • Scaling structure for a winning product on TikTok:
    1. Take the winning creative (for example, add 2), the winning ad copy and the winning audience. For example: open targeting, US, 18+, all genders, no interest filters.
    2. Launch five CBO campaigns at $150 each. Each has four ad groups using the same audience and only the winning creative and ad copy.
    3. Use manual payment and add no more than $50 at a time.
    4. Let each campaign run until about $10 is spent. That is about $50 in total for the test.
    5. Cut every campaign without a sale. If no campaign has a sale, cut any campaign with a CPC above $1. Keep the one with a CPC around $0.5 to $0.6.
    6. When one campaign gives profitable sales, scale it by raising its budget step by step. Profitable sales means about three sales at 2x ROAS. Break-even ROAS is around 1.4x to 1.5x.
    7. Check campaigns several times a day. Cutting and scaling are needed all the time.
  • Source disagreement on the spend before the first check: one procedure says about $50 per campaign. The other says about $10 per campaign, which is about $50 in total for the test. Use the first check point that fits your campaign size.
  • Scale aggressively only when a campaign has shown several profitable sales. For example, three sales at about 2x ROAS.
  • Raise the budget in steps, such as $150 to $300. TikTok tends to reward campaigns that bid more with better audiences.
  • Once a campaign has produced profitable sales, raise its budget in steps of $50. Wait between steps. Do not jump straight to a large amount.
  • Move from the starting $150 toward about $300 a day. Later, move toward about $500 a day, but only as results hold.
  • TikTok scaling needs frequent hands-on checks during the day. Campaigns must be cut or raised soon after results show.
  • Unlike Facebook, where an end-of-day review is often enough, plan to stay at the computer during scaling periods.
  • After a budget increase on a TikTok campaign with a strong creative, the platform tends to direct the campaign to better-bidding audiences. So performance can improve after a raise.
  • Judge the result after some time, not in the first minutes after the change.

Cutting Rules

1 min
  • Stop any ad group or campaign that has spent its break-even CPA without a sale. An ad group is a set of ads with the same targeting. A campaign holds one or more ad groups. CPA (cost per acquisition) means the cost of one sale. Break-even CPA is the most you can pay for one sale and still break even. Scaling (spending more to grow sales) requires this strict cutting rule.
  • A good campaign keeps making sales at each break-even CPA spend step. If it fails to sell over two break-even steps, the chance it will sell later is very low.

Protecting a Winning Product

1 min
  • After three to five days of consistent sales (sales every day, not one spike), move to custom content, meaning your own unique content, to protect the product from copycats. This typically starts around day three.
  • Start with small, low-key influencer tests (creators who promote your product). Grow the number of creators as sales come in. Small creators can help. When many of them post the same product, larger creators may reach out to you.

Spark Ads and Coins

1 min
  • Use Spark Ads (TikTok ads that boost one of your own posts) for bigger budgets, roughly $100 to $500, and for multiple ad groups. You can create them by copying an ad group up to 20 times.
  • Use coins (TikTok's prepaid credit for boosting posts) for smaller budgets, such as $20 to $30, since coins count as one ad group.
  • Test organically (for free, with unpaid posts) first. Use coins only for a new product you are highly confident about, or for a second account of a proven product. Move to Spark Ads once you have money to scale.

Duplicating Accounts for Proven Products

1 min
  • After an account reaches about 10,000 followers and brings in sales, a second account can promote the same product. Someone else or another phone can run it. Reuse the clips that already performed on the first account.
  • Two accounts, each with about 10,000 followers and each getting about 1 million views per day, produce about 2 million views per day in total. Adding a link to each account multiplies the sales potential.
  • Duplicate accounts only for products that already perform. A good sign is strong sales per million views. If a product does not perform, copying its content is not worth the effort.
  • Assume competitors will copy winning clips and launch the same product. Competing against yourself with a second account captures more of the market before others saturate it (fill it up).
  • Competitors can download posted clips and repost them without the watermark (the logo overlay). Disabling downloads only partly limits copying. Plan for copies and stay ahead.
  • If a second account brings in thousands of dollars in sales, a third account can make sense if you can run and maintain it yourself. There is no fixed limit on accounts beyond your capacity to manage them.
  • A product account that grew slowly can still produce a big result after a later viral clip (one that spreads fast). The example estimated that six million additional views could bring about 30,000 in sales.

Pinterest: Test Setup

1 min
  • Test each new product at $5 per day, because Pinterest needs time to learn the audience (who clicks and buys). Higher early budgets mostly waste money.
  • In the tests described here, products profitable at $5 per day stayed profitable. A $100 per day budget did not improve results.
  • Start with a maximum of $5 to $10 per day for the first test. Run three to five products in parallel (at the same time) if the budget allows, since one of them is likely to win.
  • Pinterest has no ad set budgets (ad set is Pinterest's word for ad group), only a campaign budget (CBO). Set the daily budget at campaign level.
  • Build the first test campaign:
    1. Choose the Conversions objective (the goal is sales).
    2. Name the campaign with the product name and the launch date.
    3. Select the target country. US is the largest market; Germany and the Netherlands also work.
    4. Set the campaign status to Active (switched on).
    5. Set the daily budget to $5.
    6. Select run on specific days and choose the next day as the start date.
    7. Set the launch time to 12:00 AM UTC (midnight, world clock time).
  • Pinterest uses only UTC for scheduling, with no local time option. Launch campaigns at midnight UTC.
  • To set up the next test, duplicate a campaign that already works. Change only the product name, launch date and targeting. Keeping the budget, country and schedule unchanged makes the new test easier to compare with the previous one.

Pinterest: Targeting and Settings

1 min
  • Ad group level: name the ad group "stacked interest". Then, under Targeting details, choose the interest keywords option and select interests from the dropdown (menu).
  • Select 5 to 20 interests, ideally 7 to 10 closely related to the product. Choose interests that describe the target audience, not only the product itself.
  • Keep the total audience size between 5 and 10 million (the sweet spot), and never above 30 million.
  • Select sub-interests (narrow topics inside a category) one by one, not whole parent categories. The audience shrinks when you add sub-interests. Single picks let you see which interest generates sales later.
  • Demographics and audience: select only female if the product is for women. Set the age range from 21 and up, or 25 and up if that fits the product. Location: US or specific countries. Leave language and devices unrestricted. Keep the default budget and schedule.
  • Optimization and delivery: set Optimization to Checkout (the ads aim for purchases) and Delivery to Automatic. Then keep the ad group active.

Pinterest: Scaling

2 min
  • Scaling a winning Pinterest campaign (spending more on it):
    1. If results are highly profitable after day 3, double the budget.
    2. Find more interests and keywords and put them in a separate campaign.
    3. Launch the new campaign at higher budgets.
    4. Move to CPA-based scaling (raising spend based on cost per sale).
  • Two procedures disagree on the trigger. One doubles the budget when results are highly profitable after day 3. The other requires the campaign to stay above break-even for two days in a row. Use the day 3 check for highly profitable results. Use the two-day check for a stable campaign.
  • Scale a campaign only when its ROAS (return on ad spend: revenue divided by ad cost) stays above break-even for two days in a row. Example: break-even ROAS of 1.5 and actual ROAS of 2.5. Then the daily budget doubles from $5 to $10.
  • Raise budgets at the start of the day, around 12 AM UTC, or up to 4 to 5 AM UTC. Do not raise them at 6 PM or 10 PM, because a late increase spends within one to two hours.
  • Scale a winning product:
    1. Confirm the campaign has stayed above breakeven ROAS for two days in a row.
    2. Double the daily budget at the start of the UTC day, for example from $5 to $10.
    3. Open the targeting breakdown and select interest to see which interests produce sales.
    4. Identify the interests that generate profitable sales, such as skincare or makeup.
    5. Create new campaigns that target the winning interests and keywords, rather than only increasing the existing budget.
  • Horizontal scaling (building more campaigns, not only raising one budget) means building more campaigns with profitable interests or keywords.
  • For keyword campaigns, add 50 to 80 relevant keywords. Use the add-all-results option to check audience size, and keep the audience between 10 and 30 million.
  • Advanced CPA scaling is considered only after 10 to 15 days of consistent results. It sets a maximum cost per acquisition, for example under $20. It requires detailed setup beyond the basic test.
  • Example: if a Pinterest campaign shows profitable sales broken down by interest, such as skincare and makeup at a ROAS of about 4 to 5, launch a second campaign. Target only the strongest interest, such as skincare alone.

Platform Updates: TikTok Inbox and YouTube Shorts

1 min
  • TikTok's inbox (direct messages) added games you can play inside messages, and labels to sort conversations. Commentary on the update notes that Instagram messages are still seen faster than TikTok DMs. So Instagram is the stronger channel for reaching creators you work with.
  • On YouTube Shorts (short vertical clips), the dislike button was removed. It was replaced with "Not interested" and "Don't recommend channel" options. The heart icon also replaced the thumbs-up as the like signal. Do not expect dislike feedback on Shorts.

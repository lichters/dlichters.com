Scaling Meta ads

Section 20.18 · 18 of 26 in this chapter · 32 min read

In short

  • Wait for about 6 to 7 sales that keep coming in over several days before you scale an audience up.
  • Raise budgets slowly, about 20 percent at a time, and watch the results for a few days.
  • Every ad account behaves differently, so test a setup on a small budget before you trust it.
  • Turn off the ads and ad sets that lose money, and keep the ones that make money.
  • Start with one CBO campaign (one budget that Meta shares across its ad sets). Triple it only after about two to three months, once the product works.
More key points
  • Set an automatic rule that stops any campaign that spends its break-even cost per purchase (the most you can pay for one sale before you lose money) without making a sale.
  • Put about 20% of the daily ad budget into testing new ads and audiences.
  • When something wins, raise spend in steps. When an account is weak, add new ads before you cut spend.
  • Use self-scaling (automatic budget increases) only during big sale events like BFCM or Halloween.

Scaling directions and principles

2 min

Scaling means spending more on ads that already make money. There are two directions.

  • Horizontal scaling means finding more audiences. An audience is the group of people your ads are shown to. While a product stays profitable, launch 2 to 3 new interests every day. An interest is a topic a person follows, such as a hobby, that you target. Keep testing for as many working audiences as possible. Reuse them with new creatives (the ad images, videos and text people see). Turn off ad sets that do badly. An ad set is a group inside a campaign that holds one audience and its ads.
  • Vertical scaling means raising the budget (the money spent per day) of existing campaigns or ad sets. A campaign is the top-level container that holds ad sets. You can also copy (duplicate) them. Use either method, or both, to scale aggressively.
  • Test whether raising budgets or duplicating works better in your own account, because the best method depends on the account.
  • Budget increase methods differ per ad account. An ad account is your advertising account on Meta, the company behind Facebook and Instagram ads. Test doubling the budget, a 25 percent increase, or duplicating the ad set on one campaign first. Roll a method out to other campaigns only if it works there.
  • Every ad account behaves differently. Test everything instead of copying what worked for another account. Test a structure with a small budget before committing to it.
  • There is no single strategy that works for every product or ad account. Keep testing broad and interest approaches, and scale whichever one proves profitable. A broad approach targets a wide group of people. An interest approach targets people by topic.
  • No single formula fits every account. Choose the structure based on what is profitable. Keep your cost per acquisition (CPA: what one sale costs you in ads) below your break-even. Break-even is the point where your ads pay for themselves, with no profit and no loss.
  • Keep scaling campaigns running for the best audiences and creatives. Use CBO where it works. CBO (campaign budget optimization) means you set one budget for the whole campaign, and Meta splits it across the ad sets. If CBO does not work in the account, use ABO campaigns that only scale the best audiences and creatives. ABO (ad set budget optimization) means you set the budget on each ad set yourself.
  • Always keep scaling campaigns running. Use CBO to scale the best audiences with the best creatives. If CBO does not work for your account, switch to an ABO campaign dedicated to scaling.
  • The testing campaign is a starting structure. Once purchases show which interests and creatives win, move to CBO or other campaign types to scale.
  • Scale a winning product only once the pixel shows real purchase data. The pixel is a small tracking code on your shop that reports sales back to Meta. Until then, keep testing.

Readiness to scale: proof thresholds

2 min

ROAS (return on ad spend) is the money earned back for each dollar spent on ads. A ROAS of 2 means 2 dollars back for every dollar spent. BE ROAS is the ROAS at break-even.

Sources disagree on the sales threshold, meaning the number of sales you need before scaling. Use the values below according to the situation.

Source value Threshold Context
Interest or ad set in ABO testing At least 3 purchases with ROAS above break-even Starting a CBO scaling campaign with a $100 budget
Ad set with consistent sales At least 4 purchases Raising the budget by 20 percent every 24 hours
Interest proven profitable At least 6 profitable sales Choosing a scaling method
Audience or ad set About 6 to 7 sales; 5 is too few Most common rule, applied before scaling
Winning interest About 7 sales Raising its budget or starting a new $50 CBO without interests
  • Before scaling a Meta ad set, wait for roughly six to seven profitable sales in a consistent pattern. Four or five sales is too little data. A single unusually strong day can distort the picture, so look for steady sales across several days.
  • Consider scaling an audience only after it has about 6 to 7 sales. Five sales are too few to be confident. Early on, use a small budget rather than a high one.
  • Do not scale a Facebook ad set until it delivers more than six or seven sales. Before that point, keep collecting data at the current budget.
  • Once a winning interest reaches about seven sales, you can raise its budget, or start a new $50 CBO without interests to test further. Both options should follow the same ROAS and sales thresholds.
  • A campaign needs a minimum daily spend of about 10 or 50 pounds per day to be easily scalable. Below that level, scaling is considered difficult.
  • Once the pixel has 1,000 or more purchases, you can also target a broad audience with no interests.
  • Do not scale an audience with a ROAS below 1.7 when the break-even ROAS is 1.5. A higher ROAS gives more confidence, and 1.7 is the minimum for scaling.
  • Advertisers disagree on how far ROAS should sit above break-even before scaling. Some use break-even plus 0.3 to 0.5. A more conservative rule, with a break-even ROAS of 1.5, requires a minimum of 1.7, about 0.2 above. Use the margin your own account supports.
  • Set your own benchmark ROAS and adjust the formula to it, because a 20 percent margin is not every seller's target.
  • Test any new scaling method on only one winning campaign first. Do not test on your sole profitable campaign.
  • At low budgets, around $10 per asset, daily sales are very unstable. An asset is one ad or one creative element. Even ad sets at about $300 can swing from a ROAS of 2 to under 1, though higher budgets tend to stabilize results somewhat.
  • Scaling is not a day-one activity. Segment and scale by profit only after the account has data and standard optimizations are done. Segmenting means splitting results into groups so you can compare them. Standard optimizations are the routine fixes to settings.

Testing structure and creative flow

2 min
  • Testing campaign structure:
    1. Interest-testing campaign: take winning creatives and test new interests.
    2. Creative-testing campaign: take the winning interest and test new creative variations.
    3. Once winners are identified, combine all winning creatives into one audience targeting for scaling. Targeting means choosing who sees the ad.
  • Start each new product with $5 per ad set across five to six ad sets, about $25 to $30 per day. Once a test ad set shows a good CTR (click-through rate: the share of people who see the ad and click it) and consistent sales, choose how to scale. You can raise the budget on the winning ad sets, or add spend to test ad sets so the winning audience is found faster.
  • Testing procedure for a new audience:
    1. Test the audience for two to three days.
    2. Remove creatives that perform badly in a consistent way.
    3. Wait until the audience has 6 to 7 sales.
    4. Confirm a ROAS of at least 1.7 when break-even ROAS is 1.5.
    5. Start with a CBO budget of about $50.
    6. Once it is proven, scale using the three-campaign structure below.
  • A CBO campaign with a daily budget of about $50 is an acceptable starting budget for testing a scaling candidate. A scaling candidate is an audience that might be ready to grow.
  • Do not double a budget abruptly. A product that converted above 3 percent in testing (conversion rate is the share of visitors who buy) dropped to zero add-to-carts the day after the budget was doubled. An add-to-cart means a visitor put the product in their shopping cart.
  • Move the winning creative into its own campaign, then duplicate the same creative into multiple ad sets to scale it:
    1. Find the winning creative among the test ad sets.
    2. Move that creative into its own campaign.
    3. Duplicate the same creative into multiple ad sets to scale it.
  • Relying on a single ad to scale is risky, even if that ad has good metrics. Metrics are the numbers that measure results. Build more ad variants (different versions of the same ad) so the campaign does not depend on one creative.

CBO and ABO

2 min
  • ABO is used for testing interests, and CBO for scaling, in many accounts. CBO does not work for every account, and ABO can perform better. The right choice depends on testing your setup.
  • CBO scaling setup: put a proven interest into a CBO campaign with a budget of 100 to 200 USD. Include 4 to 5 ad sets with the same audience, with about four creatives in each.
  • Sources disagree on how many creatives go into each CBO ad set. Use about four creatives per ad set in a proven CBO scaling campaign. Use about three creatives per ad set when a campaign starts with 4 to 5 ad sets. Use three creatives per ad set in the three-campaign structure. Use two to three creatives rather than one when building the first scaling CBO.
  • In a CBO, Meta assigns the budget to the ad sets that reach the best pockets of the audience. Pockets are the groups of people who respond best. Meta then searches for similar people to grow those pockets. This is why CBO works for scaling once there is a budget of about 100 to 200 USD.
  • A campaign-level budget (CBO) moves money toward the best-performing ad sets. It can sometimes send spend to a weaker one based on early engagement, such as early likes and clicks. Review the allocation regularly. Turn off the weak ad sets after enough data has built up.
  • A high-budget CBO does not spend evenly across creatives. On one day, one creative gets most of the budget. On another day, a different one does. A scaling campaign therefore needs several working creatives, not one.
  • Before building a scaling CBO, keep ads that work in other test ad sets, even if they fail in one ad set. Remove an ad from the CBO only if it performs badly everywhere, and keep looking for more good creatives.
  • A CBO scaling campaign starts with 4 to 5 ad sets. Each ad set contains about 3 creatives.
  • If CBO does not work in an account, scale with ABO instead. Duplicate the winning ad set three times at 100 USD each in one ABO campaign. Or create three separate ABO scaling campaigns with 100 USD each.
  • CBO campaigns with several ads do not work for every account. In the case described, they gave zero sales and a rising CPA compared with testing. Testing CPA was about $10 to $50, and CBO CPA was about $20. Switching off underperforming ads inside a CBO can disrupt the optimization (Meta's automatic learning and adjusting). So in this case, ABO was preferred for scaling.
  • Move a winning product from an ABO test into its own CBO:
    1. After the ABO test, shortlist the products whose ad sets show strong results.
    2. For each winning product, create a new CBO campaign with three creatives.
    3. Wait until the new CBO is live and running.
    4. Only then turn off the individual ABO ad set for that product.
  • Once the best cost cap is found, move it into its own individual CBO campaign. Use that cap across other winning audiences and broad campaigns as you scale.
  • Create three CBO campaigns using the winning interest. Then increase budgets there when the data supports it.

Horizontal scaling procedures

1 min
  • Horizontal scaling procedure for a winning ad set:
    1. Move the winning ad set into its own scaling campaign.
    2. Duplicate it, for example 10 times, as ABO campaigns with about $20 in each.
    3. After a short spend, switch off the copies that have no sales. For example, kill (turn off) an ad after a day without sales.
    4. Keep the copies that perform well and scale them further.
  • Horizontal scaling means duplicating a winning ad set into a new campaign, often about 10 copies. Then keep the best performer and turn off the rest. It is used when the ROAS stays profitable but no longer supports a large budget jump.
  • Budget testing with a small ad set: run three copies at 10, 15 and 20 units of budget to find the best budget for the ad. Then scale the winner horizontally by duplicating it again.
  • You can duplicate your top 3 to 5 winning audiences or interests into a CBO campaign with a $150 to $200 budget. Use this when a single ad set's budget increases stop working.
  • Once a campaign is unprofitable, check it at ad set and ad level first. A single audience or creative can drag down the whole campaign. So kill that bad ad set or creative.
  • Keeping underperforming ad sets running alongside the winners drains budget. So turn them off consistently.
  • If an interest or ad set stops working across several days, switch it off and try something else. An interest that stopped can sometimes work again after days or weeks. A stop does not mean it is dead for good.

Vertical scaling: budget steps and rules

6 min

Last 3 days + today ROAS is about equal to BE ROAS Reduce budget by 20% to stabilize.

  • Scaling an ad set with a 20 percent step: when an ad set consistently produces sales and has at least 4 purchases, raise its budget by 20 percent of the initial budget every 24 hours. Do this as long as it performs 20 percent above BE ROAS. Check whether budget increases or duplication work better for your account.
  • Scaling by a 20 percent step on a moderate ad: when an ad has only a moderate result but is still profitable, duplicate it at a 20 percent budget step. Run it a few days. If it holds, raise the budget again by 20 percent. This avoids risking large budgets on an ad whose ROAS may drop.
  • Scaling a profitable Advantage+ campaign (Meta's automated campaign type):
    1. Start with a budget such as $100.
    2. Raise the budget by 20% every two to three days while the campaign stays profitable.
    3. If results drop after a few days, return to the previous budget level.
    4. If the campaign is not profitable after about a week, close it and scale a winning interest instead.
  • Scaling a winning Facebook ad with a slow, step-by-step increase:
    1. Keep the winning ad in an ABO campaign with its own budget.
    2. Raise the budget by about $20 on the same ad. It already has engagement, and Facebook has learned from it.
    3. Run it for a few days.
    4. If the ad holds, add another $20 after about three more days.
    5. Repeat and raise the budget slowly while the return stays acceptable.
    6. Once the ad is strong at a higher budget (for example, $100), test it against a more aggressive budget. Then move to $200 and $400 if it holds.
  • Scaling an ad that has proven itself:
    1. Keep the original ad set running. Do not touch it.
    2. Duplicate the ad set three times.
    3. Put more budget into each copy, for example three ad sets at about $40 per day.
    4. Test under the bigger budgets and see which one holds.
  • Scale a CBO campaign by raising the budget by 25 percent. If profit margin falls after a 25 percent increase in budget, reduce the budget again. This is called descaling.
  • Scaling by a fixed step on a CBO, with a 100-dollar starting point:
    1. Find an interest in ABO testing that gives at least 3 purchases with a ROAS above break-even.
    2. Launch a CBO campaign with a $100 budget.
    3. Create three duplicate ad sets, each with the same winning interest.
    4. Add the winning ads (creatives) to the ad sets, using two to three creatives rather than one.
    5. If the campaign runs for about 12 hours with a ROAS of 3 or more and at least 3 purchases (2 purchases can be enough), raise the budget by $100. For example, go from $100 to $200.
    6. Do not raise the budget again on the same day. Check the next day and repeat the +$100 step (to $300, then $400) only while the ROAS holds.
    7. If the CBO is not profitable on day one, cut it. If it is profitable on day one but not on day two, let it run to day three. Cut it if day three is also unprofitable.
    8. Keep testing new interests with 15 to 20 percent of the budget so the next winners can be moved into CBO.
  • Aggressive scaling method: double the CBO budget repeatedly until it reaches 500 dollars. After that, increase the budget by only 20% per adjustment.
  • Budget scaling rule: increase daily ad spend by a maximum of 2x (double). For example, go from $50 per day to $100-150 per day. Do this only after observing 2 to 3 weeks of consistent profitability. Aggressive scaling without proven consistency leads to losses.
  • When results are strong, scale aggressively by nearly doubling the budget. For example, go from 50 to 80 pounds per day. Keep it if performance holds for a few days before the next step. Sustained performance at the higher level then justifies moving to the next budget step, such as 100 pounds per day.
  • Return on ad spend typically drops sharply after the first aggressive budget increase. Then it falls by smaller amounts with each further increase. The largest drop comes from the initial jump, so a strategy must allow for that first decline.
  • Aggressive scaling is safe only if the ROAS after the jump stays about 0.8 above break-even. If it does, the budget can be doubled again, and the ad set can move into its own scaling campaign.
  • A Meta campaign that performs well can be scaled in steps. For example, go from about $100 per day to $500 per day, and later to thousands or tens of thousands per day. Each step should follow only a stable result with a strong audience and targeting. Jumping directly to high budgets is a common way to trigger restrictions, which are Meta limits or blocks on your account.
  • Scaling a test from 5 dollars per day to 100 dollars per day in one jump led to an ad account block. Scale in gradual steps after the product shows profit.
  • Do not double a budget on one day. Do not copy a 300 dollar CBO to a new 300 dollar campaign. Neither carries a guarantee, and either can break a working setup. Increase in steps across days, and watch the ad account's response before duplicating.
  • Do not change every campaign at once when scaling. A bad budget change can hurt all campaigns in the account. Apply a change to one CBO campaign, watch the results, and then extend it to others.
  • Scale a winning campaign by duplicating it at a higher budget only when ROAS is well above break-even. If results are only acceptable, raise the budget of the existing campaign instead.
  • Example: a $50 CBO campaign with a strong ROAS can be duplicated at $100 while the original stays at $50. Total daily spend becomes $150. If the $100 copy fails, retry the duplicate at $50, or increase the original budget by 20 percent.
  • On Facebook, smaller daily ad set budgets of roughly £10 to £30 often deliver better and cheaper impressions than large budgets. An impression is one showing of an ad. Scale across several small ad sets first. Raise budgets in steps rather than doubling a single ad set, because big jumps make delivery search harder. Delivery is how Meta finds people to show the ad to.
  • Short event campaigns, such as Black Friday or Christmas, may be scaled up and down quickly. Evergreen campaigns (campaigns that sell all year) need a steady 7-day window before any budget change.
  • Facebook budget steps for a campaign started at $50 per day:
    1. Start the Facebook campaign at $50 per day, and make sure the creatives and page are solid.
    2. Let it run for 7 days.
    3. If all target metrics are hitting, raise the budget by 15%.
    4. Let it settle for about 3 days.
    5. If it still performs well, raise the budget another 15%. A 15-20% step stays safe for the learning phase, which is Meta's start-up period while it learns who to show the ad to.
    6. If traffic arrives but there are no sales after 7 days, turn the ads off and improve the website before spending again.

Duplication methods

4 min
  • Triple duplication for a CBO (campaign budget optimization, where Meta shares one campaign budget across its ad sets) with a $300 total start:
    1. Launch three campaigns with the same ad set (the group of settings that decides who sees the ads) at the same budget each.
    2. Start with a total of about $300, for example $100 per campaign. Check that the setup is correct.
    3. Optionally start at $200 per scaling campaign. (Scaling means raising spend on what already works.)
    4. Add an automatic rule (a setting that acts without you) that turns off a campaign when it spends its break-even cost per purchase without producing a sale.
    5. If it fails, relaunch after a couple of weeks at a different budget.
  • Triple duplication with high budgets, using 200 USD per CBO as the example:
    1. Launch three identical CBO campaigns with the same ad sets and creatives (the ads themselves: images, videos and text), each on a high budget. The example used 200 USD per CBO.
    2. Let all three run until about 10 to 20 USD of spend.
    3. Turn off any CBO with very high CPCs (cost per click, what one click costs) and no sales.
    4. If one CBO has a sale in this phase, keep it running and turn off the other two.
    5. If no CBO has a sale after about 15 to 20 USD of spend (the break-even, the point where sales cover costs), turn off the weakest one. Let the other two spend another 15 USD.
    6. If one of the two still has no sale after that, turn off both.
  • Use triple duplication only after roughly two to three months.
  • It is only for when the product works and the budget is high.
  • For the start, a single CBO campaign is enough.
  • Audience overlap (how many of the same people two ad sets reach) above 15 to 20 percent is poor.
  • More than three duplicated CBOs on the same audiences is likely to compete with yourself.
  • That can raise CPM (cost per 1,000 views) and waste spend.
  • Treat this as an untested risk.
  • Use this if the budget increase steps stop working.
  • Duplicate the ad set five times into a CBO campaign, creating three CBOs.
  • Keep the one that performs, close the others (turn them off), then scale the budget of the winning CBO.
  • Three-campaign scaling structure (scaling means raising spend on what works):
    1. For each winning interest (a topic your buyers care about, used in Meta's targeting), create one CBO campaign with four ad sets.
    2. Put three creatives in each ad set.
    3. Duplicate that campaign so you have three campaigns.
    4. Launch all three together.
    5. Set an automatic rule that turns off campaigns with no sale after roughly eight to twelve hours, or after spend equal to the break-even cost per purchase.
    6. Keep the best-performing campaign and turn off the other two.
  • Duplicating with one variable changed, used when budget changes and duplication do not work:
    1. Duplicate the ad set 3 to 4 times at the same budget.
    2. Change one thing per ad set, keeping the interest the same. For example, use men only or women only (depending on the product).
    3. Narrow the ad set to the best country.
    4. Narrow the ad set by age.
    5. Narrow the ad set to the best placement (where the ad shows, such as feed or stories) and platform (the app, such as Facebook or Instagram).
  • Ad set variants for the one-variable method:
    1. Ad set 1: men only or women only (depends on the product).
    2. Ad set 2: narrow down to the best country.
    3. Ad set 3: narrow down the age range.
    4. Ad set 4: narrow down to the best placement and platform.
  • Duplicating an ad set in Ads Manager (Meta's tool for running ads):
    1. Open the campaign, ad set or ad that should be reused.
    2. Press Duplicate, then Duplicate again, to create a copy inside the same level.
    3. For a new ad, rename the copy named "copy" and swap in the new creative.
    4. For a new ad set, duplicate the ad set, then change the name and the ads.
    5. For a new campaign, duplicate the campaign. It carries over all ad sets and ads inside it.
  • Copying ads between ad sets:
    1. Select the ads to copy. Click the first one, then Shift-click (hold Shift and click) the last one.
    2. Press Command C on a Mac to copy them.
    3. Open the target ad set and press Command V to paste the ads.
    4. While the pasted ads are still selected, remove the word "copy" at the end of their names.
    5. Use Edit > Find and replace. Type "dash copy" in the find field, leave the replace field empty, press Replace, then save the draft (a version saved but not yet live).
  • Budget testing and duplication: a case where the first sales were about 1,700 in revenue (currency not stated).
  • The budget was raised three times within 10 days. Each time, Facebook cut it back the next day.
  • Support staff could not explain the cap (a limit on spend).
  • Horizontal scaling (adding separate ad sets, each on its own interest, instead of raising one budget) was used as a workaround.

Automated rules and loss control

1 min
  • For campaigns with a $100,000+ budget, set up automated rules to prevent losses at each scaling stage.
  • Set an automatic rule that switches off a scaling campaign once its spend reaches the break-even cost per purchase without a single sale.
  • Automatic rules matter because campaigns may launch overnight and run unattended.
  • Set an automatic rule that turns off campaigns with no sale after roughly eight to twelve hours, or after spend equal to the break-even cost per purchase.
  • Losses on individual days are normal under about $1,000 daily spend.
  • So a profitable day can absorb one or two losing days.
  • Consistent results usually appear once a campaign has spent above $500.
  • A bigger budget can show inconsistent CBO days. The other campaigns offset them.
  • Review the allocation (how the budget is split) of a campaign regularly.
  • Turn off the weak ad sets after enough data has built up.
  • Scaling a winning interest: first confirm the interest has at least 6 profitable sales. Then choose a scaling method:
    1. Raise the ad set budget (example: from $20 to $40 per day), or duplicate the winning interest into a CBO campaign.
    2. For a CBO duplication, use a $100 campaign budget with the winning interest copied 4 times. Each copy reaches a different pocket (a separate slice of the audience).
    3. Test both methods. One may work better than the other in a given account.
    4. Check which ad sets receive budget. Cut (turn off) underperformers once you have enough data.

Self-scaling, events and refresh

1 min
  • Use self-scaling (automated budget increases every 2 to 4 hours) only during specific high-intent events. High intent means buyers are ready to buy.
  • Events: Halloween, BFCM (the Black Friday and Cyber Monday period), Cyber Monday, or very aggressive offers (60% off vs. typical 40%).
  • Self-scaling works during events because people have high purchase intent and expect sales.
  • During normal days, frequent budget increases hurt performance.
  • Winning Facebook campaigns often fade after about one to two months.
  • Plan a refresh before results drop. A refresh means changing the creative, the hook (the first seconds that catch attention) or the audience.
  • Or duplicate into a new campaign.

Creative testing allocation and budget split

1 min
  • Allocate 20% of the daily ad budget to creative testing.
  • Example: if the daily budget is 500 dollars, dedicate 100 dollars to testing.
  • Keep spending 15 to 20 percent of the budget on testing new audiences and creatives.
  • Do this even for products that have worked for months.
  • Keep a separate audience testing sandbox and creative testing sandbox (separate test areas) to do this.
  • Split a daily ad budget into 20% for testing, 10% for retargeting (showing ads again to people who already visited), and 70% for existing winning audiences and scaling campaigns.
  • Example: on $100 per day, allocate $20 to testing, $10 to retargeting and $70 to scaling.
  • Keep testing new interests with 15 to 20 percent of the budget.
  • This way, the next winners can be moved into CBO.
  • Testing across formats maximizes the chance that something works.
  • Formats are types of ad, such as BOF (bottom-of-funnel, ads for people close to buying), UGC (user-generated content, real-customer-style material), natives (ads that look like normal posts) and whitelist pages (ads run under a partner's page).
  • A format that fails still gives information.
  • A winner in one format is still a win.
  • Aggressive scaling to very high monthly revenue needs four things in place:
    • winning audiences
    • winning creatives
    • strong email and SMS (text message) marketing
    • Google Ads
  • Without these, scaling spend usually causes losses.

Advantage+ and campaign build

2 min
  • Advantage+ campaign setup under the Sales objective. Advantage+ is Meta's automated setup.
    1. Click Create and choose the Sales objective.
    2. Keep Buying type on Auction (the normal way to buy ads) and click Continue.
    3. Enter the campaign name with product, budget type, creative type and date or year.
    4. Under Budget, choose Advantage+ campaign budget (CBO) and enter the daily budget.
    5. Set Campaign bid strategy to Highest volume or value.
    6. Name the ad set after its creative.
    7. Under Conversion, set Conversion location to Website, Performance goal to Maximize number of conversions, Dataset to the default pixel (the tracking code on your store), and Conversion event to Purchase (a completed sale).
    8. Under Show more settings, open Attribution setting (how Meta credits a sale to an ad). Choose Standard attribution. Set the selections to 7-day click only, with engaged-view and view-through (credit for ad views, not clicks) set to None.
    9. Check the start date, end date and time zone.
    10. Set ad set spending limits only if needed.
    11. Under Audience, keep Broad (a wide audience), 18 to 65+ and all genders. Keep Advantage+ audience on.
    12. Keep Placements on Automatic with Advantage+ placements on.
    13. Name the ad.
    14. Under Identity, select the Facebook Page and check the Instagram account.
    15. Under Ad setup, choose Create ad, Manual upload and Single image or video.
    16. Enter the destination URL (the link people land on) to a collection or product page. Keep Use shop default off.
    17. Under Creative, leave Source URL empty. Turn off the catalog (your product list) if it is not optimized. Crop the vertical 9:16 version (a tall format for phone screens).
    18. Enter primary text, a headline if wanted, and choose Shop Now.
    19. Skip Meta AI options. Turn off Enhancements, except Show products when the catalog is optimized.
    20. Set URL parameters (tags added to links for tracking), check the preview, then press Publish.
  • There is no single strategy that works for every product or ad account (the ad account is the account that pays for ads).
  • Keep testing broad and interest approaches.
  • Scale whichever one proves profitable.

Bottom-of-funnel scaling

2 min
  • BOF (bottom-of-funnel, ads for people close to buying) has a hard scaling ceiling. Its warm audience (people who already know you) is small.
  • For solution-aware products (people who know a fix exists), BOF stays profitable indefinitely.
  • But it cannot grow past the fixed daily impression supply (the number of ad views available each day).
  • BOF gathers warm sales. It does not create the volume needed for $1M+ monthly revenue.
  • Trying to scale BOF to large monthly revenue targets is a mistake.
  • The warm audience pool is small by nature. That sets a strict ceiling on impressions and spend.
  • BOF delivers high ROAS (return on ad spend, money back for each dollar spent) on the inventory (ad space) it has.
  • Still, its limited scale makes it unsuitable as a main growth driver.
  • Introduce new BOF concepts weekly. Examples: new testimonials, new benefit angles and new social proof (other people's positive reviews).
  • Rotate fatigued ad sets out (fatigued means they stopped working).
  • Do not refresh constantly if your video campaign works well.
  • The inflating (growing) warm audience keeps ROAS high, even on existing ads.
  • Scale BOF budget only until ROAS drops to your comfort threshold (the lowest return you accept).
  • If your breakeven ROAS (the return level where you stop losing money) is 1.5X, scale until you reach 2.5X ROAS. Then hold budget steady.
  • Do not overscale. Monitor ROAS and reduce it if it drops below your target.
  • Set BOF budget at roughly 30% of what your video ads are spending.
  • This is a guideline, not a fixed rule. Adjust based on ROAS and available inventory.
  • If your video campaign is still strong, the warm pool keeps inflating. You can hold BOF budget steady without constant increases.
  • Adding new ads is better than reducing budget.
  • If an account underperforms, introduce new ad concepts and variations. This gives the algorithm (Meta's delivery system) more material to work with.
  • Do not cut spend first. Reducing spend only shrinks the business.
  • Growing requires adding new creative constantly.

Targeting, demographics and ceilings

1 min
  • Choose a single winning gender and age from the breakdown (the report split by age and gender) rather than mixing all demographics.
  • For a product aimed at both genders, check whether men or women perform better.
  • Focus budget on the stronger group.
  • Facebook groups audience targeting at the ad account level.
  • So ads in different campaigns can still reach the same people.
  • Separate campaigns per format are still worth it. They control budget and let each format scale on its own.
  • Every ad has a scaling ceiling, the point where spend stops being profitable.
  • Example: one ad can take about $2,000 a day, another stops working at about $100 a day, images may cap near $200 a day, and strong videos can scale to about $3,000 a day.
  • Separate formats let each one run at its own ceiling.
  • Facebook's CBO shifts budget by percentage.
  • It can push about 95 percent of spend into one favored ad, whatever the total budget.
  • Putting different formats into one campaign lets a favored video starve the other ads (take their budget away).
  • If an ad in a campaign gets no spend, move it to its own separate campaign for a fair test.
  • The point of a new campaign is to isolate the variable (test one thing at a time).
  • The copy-analysis method (checking which ad wording wins) is most useful once you are scaling.
  • Apply it when you are moving beyond testing.
  • Scale a CBO campaign with a low-volume test before extending it.
  • A budget step only follows a stable result with a strong audience and targeting.

Team, time and staffing

1 min
  • After switching to the Power Five approach (a named method in this text), a whole team needs about 5 hours per week for Facebook ads.
  • That is down from more than 50 hours per week with three people.
  • If lead generation (collecting names and emails from interested people) is not running yet, focus first on these:
    • hot dynamic
    • hot static
    • customers goodwill
    • promotions
  • "Hot" means people who recently visited your store.
  • Lead generation comes after these are working.
  • Quick wins come first:
    • Set up dynamic remarketing (ads showing the products people viewed), with varied ads.
    • Add a short video ad for hot traffic.
    • Run a goodwill video (a video that builds trust, not a sales push).
    • Promote to warm audiences.
    • Match email promotions.
  • Lead magnets (free offers that collect emails), opt-in pages (signup pages) and designed assets can wait.
  • At the business-owner stage, a trained virtual assistant (an online helper who works remotely) can set up and manage Facebook ad campaigns.
  • The founder reviews them, analyzes results, and makes the final spending and offer decisions.
  • Hire an ad manager only if they are trained on your account or show a strong CV with past client results.

Segmentation by profit margin

1 min
  • Segment alpha-beta campaigns (campaigns grouped as alpha and beta) by profit margin (what you keep from each sale after costs) only after campaigns have generated enough history.
  • History shows which products drive the most sales with the highest margins.
  • This requires finishing all standard optimizations, such as negative keywords (words that stop ads from showing) and site conversion optimization (improving the store so visitors buy).
  • It is not a day-one task for new stores.

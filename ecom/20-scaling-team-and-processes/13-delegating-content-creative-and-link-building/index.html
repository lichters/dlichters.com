Delegating content, creative and link building

Section 20.13 · 13 of 26 in this chapter · 24 min read

In short

  • Write your first 10 to 15 articles yourself before you pay any writer.
  • Pay per project, never per hour (per hour means paying for each hour worked).
  • Test every new helper with a small paid job before you give them more work.
  • Use one tracker (a shared spreadsheet that shows progress) to see where work is stuck.
  • Hand off one task at a time, and write a step-by-step guide (an SOP) for each task first.
More key points
  • Repetitive link building work can go to a low-cost helper, once you have written step-by-step instructions.
  • Do each task yourself a few times first, then write the instructions, then hand the task over.
  • Post on social media only when you have content to fill it, and check your brand's look before you post.
  • Buying old social media accounts to send messages carries a real risk of bans, so check the platform rules first.

Deciding What to Delegate

2 min

To delegate means to hand a task to someone else.

  • Write the first 10 to 15 articles yourself. This confirms that content marketing (publishing helpful articles that bring in buyers) works. Invest in a writing team only after the channel has shown results.
  • Extra writing is not needed at the start. A few authoritative articles beat many generic ones. Authoritative means trusted and expert. Do not outsource blog writing to Fiverr or iWriter. (Fiverr and iWriter are freelance marketplaces, websites where you hire people for single jobs.)
  • Write product descriptions and SEO content yourself, or learn them from free training, before paying someone on Fiverr. SEO means making your pages easy for search engines like Google to find. The training covers SEO in detail.
  • Grow the business beyond your own hands. Hire people to take over your daily tasks one step at a time. Start with writing, the easiest role to delegate. Then add email, design, editing and data entry.
  • Outsource tasks you cannot do well yourself, such as detailed mock-ups (rough sample designs) and graphics, once cash flow (money coming in and going out) allows. Start with rough mock-ups yourself. Bring in specialists to reduce errors as the business grows.
  • Decide between doing an edit yourself and paying a freelancer. Compare the hours a first attempt takes with the freelance price. Learning a new editing app can take days. So outsourcing simple tasks can cost less than the time they take you.
  • Outsourcing ad creative (the images and videos used in ads) to freelancers does not guarantee strong results. Learning video editing from scratch takes a long time. Plan for both before relying on either.
  • Dropshipping (selling products that a supplier ships straight to the buyer) needs fast content and product testing (checking whether a product sells). Do it yourself or with someone who can produce content quickly. Or pay a freelancer to deliver within 24 hours.
  • Keep the creative team separate from routine operations. Copywriters (people who write sales text) need freedom to incubate (let ideas grow). Their output drops when they are held to the same visible schedule as operational staff.
  • Creative and offer quality drive results more than campaign structure (how ads are set up). A winning creative makes the strategy matter much less. Test many creatives and offers continuously. Creative testing is one of the largest parts of the job, whether done in-house or outsourced.
  • Use VAs (virtual assistants, remote helpers) for repetitive tasks such as social media, email, customer service and content curation (choosing and sharing content). Hire them through platforms (online job sites) such as AppWork, or alternatives such as OnlineJobsPH. Focus your own time on strategy.
  • Create a clear role split for every task. The owner keeps strategy and final approval. The owner delegates the rest.

Hiring and Vetting

3 min

Vetting means checking people carefully before you hire them.

Hiring the first content producer

  1. Post a job for a freelancer, such as a writer or video editor.
  2. Give the candidate a test piece of work. A candidate is a person applying for the job.
  3. Hire the best candidate.
  4. Start producing the first content piece with them.
  • Hire quickly and imperfectly rather than after a long search. The first producer can be replaced later, once output (what gets made) starts.

Hiring freelance writers on Upwork (a website where freelancers offer their work)

  1. Write a job post that sets clear expectations: one article per week, a keyword (the search term you want the article to rank for) with guidelines, and finishing the article with an editor.
  2. State the price: $50 to $100 for a 1,000-word article.
  3. Say you need to hire three freelancers, which attracts more applicants.
  4. Say that new Upwork freelancers without ratings are welcome, because you will vet them by reading their work.
  5. Expect roughly 20 to 40 applicants per post.
  6. Have an editor shortlist the top five applicants.
  7. Check each shortlisted candidate for a style match with your brand. This means checking that their writing fits your brand's voice.
  8. Hire three to five writers and pay for a one-article trial.
  9. Hire the best trial writers for 10 more articles and keep only the best.
  • Freelancers abroad can disappear, get sick, or deliver work that does not match their samples. Always test a writer with a paid trial article before committing to more work.
  • Upwork gives access to writers and editors worldwide. Lower overhead (fixed business costs) can mean lower rates. Low rates do not automatically mean low quality. A clear vetting process is needed to find strong candidates.
  • Writers from lower-cost markets can produce good results at the start. Ranking for bigger keywords (appearing high in search results) requires native-level writing (writing as well as a native speaker) and higher pay. Pay more when competition for the keyword is higher.
  • Use a clear-writing guide, such as Paula LaRocque's book on writing, as the standard for judging editor candidates. Use it to recognise clear, natural prose (ordinary written sentences) before you hire an editor to screen writers. Use a clear-writing guide.

Finding writers through industry communities

  1. Join Facebook groups for your industry.
  2. Post a request for a paid article on a topic that matches the audience.
  3. Ask members with experience to message you privately.
  4. Hire a writer who already knows the product and the community.

Hiring a native English editor

  • Hire a native English editor you respect to review articles and emails written by non-native writers. Judge the editor by reading their own writing for clarity, conciseness (using few words) and persuasion (ability to convince).
  • Savings only happen when the editor works quickly.
  • A native editor needs about 15 minutes for a 500 to 600 word article. Then they do a second pass (a second review). If an editor spends more than an hour on an article that short, something in the process is wrong.

Hiring and vetting a content team member

  1. Post the role on freelance or job boards (websites that list jobs), such as Upwork, Fiverr or a Philippines-based virtual assistant job board.
  2. Interview candidates. For a video editor, consider a chat over WhatsApp with sample files.
  3. Have the new hire produce test TikToks (short vertical videos).
  4. Audit those test videos, meaning check them carefully, for brand fit, message and compliance risk (the chance of breaking platform or legal rules).
  5. If the work is good, give the hire posting access, or have a VA handle uploads.

Hiring the right role for the job

  • Written content needs a content writer. Video content needs an editor. If someone else is doing the recording, you also need a separate person to record.
  • Higher-quality writers are less likely to do manual publishing tasks (uploading and posting by hand). So expect to hand posting to a separate virtual assistant.
  • Name the producer for each piece, whether a writer, freelancer, VA or yourself. If several people work on one piece, record each person. Examples are a writer plus an infographic designer (someone who makes visual charts), or a video editor hired on Fiverr. This keeps responsibilities clear.

Pay, Test Projects and Briefing

3 min

General rules for outsourcing content production

  1. Always pay per project, never per hour.
  2. Always start with a test project.
  3. Use a quality job posting, with a template (a ready-made layout you fill in).
  4. Provide clear instructions to freelancers on what a piece of content should include.
  • Give each freelancer written guidelines for every piece. The guideline document works as an onboarding guide (a welcome guide for new people) and an SOP (standard operating procedure, a written step-by-step method). It tells the freelancer what to deliver.

Briefing a research or content writer

  • When briefing (giving instructions to) a freelance content writer for research, give them access to your attention matrix document (a research sheet on what grabs your buyers' attention). For an ongoing content writer, also give them access to your content planning document. This lets them see what you fill in there. Both are part of the instructions you provide.
  • Hand the finished customer profile (a written picture of your ideal buyer) to anyone who writes or markets for you. This includes in-house staff, new hires, outsourced copywriters, ad agencies (companies that run ads), email agencies and customer service. It helps them learn about your customers quickly.

Writer checklist, given to every new writer and editor

  1. Know the customer from the customer profile.
  2. Write as if speaking to the customer face to face, in a casual tone.
  3. Use short words, short sentences and short paragraphs.
  4. Use bullets and lists where they fit.
  5. Give concrete examples.
  6. Follow the rule for how often to use each keyword (keyword density).
  7. Follow the rule for how often to use subheadings (small titles inside an article).
  8. Check the readability score (a number showing how easy the text is to read) before handing in the article.

Pricing for specific tasks

  • Do not spend time creating or editing images yourself. Custom images, such as blog hero images (the large picture at the top of a blog post), YouTube thumbnails (preview pictures for videos), in-post images and infographics, can be outsourced on Fiverr. A professional custom image costs around 10 USD.
  • A custom-designed image, such as a blog hero image or a YouTube thumbnail, costs around 10 USD on Fiverr.
  • When outsourcing video editing per project, state the video's running time (for example, a 30-minute video). List exactly what the editor must do. The editor then quotes one price for that project, and you can negotiate it.
  • A freelancer charged 5 USD per video to translate captions (on-screen subtitles) into French. The freelancer also added a French voiceover (spoken audio track) to short TikTok-style clips.
  • Hiring a freelancer on Upwork to build a simple calculator page was estimated at $50 to $100. The job could be completed in one day. This was used as the cost benchmark (a reference price to compare against) for building a tool page for a keyword.
  • A product-description team of three freelancers working on a project can produce about 350 unique 200-word product descriptions per week. So a complete store can be ready in about one week.
  • Fiverr can be used to outsource images for blog posts and content pages. Canva (an online design tool) can be used to make basic graphics yourself. Both keep content production affordable.
  • Do not pay for social media management that offers only a few posts per month. For example, 1,500 dollars for four posts. At that level it does not generate meaningful returns. In the example, the owner had already spent over 4,500 dollars.

Writer rates: sources differ

  • Upwork writers at $50 to $100 per 1,000-word article were the benchmark for a managed article team.
  • A freelance writer at about $30 per article was too expensive to fill 60 to 80 empty collection pages (pages that list a group of products). Hiring in-house or on a part-time contract was more practical for ongoing content. Estimate the total cost of the content backlog (everything still waiting to be written) before choosing a freelance rate.
  • Use the Upwork rate for article production. Use a part-time or in-house contract when the backlog is large and ongoing. Pay more for keywords with higher competition.

Production Roles and Team Structure

2 min

Roles when systemising content marketing (setting up repeatable steps)

  • Writer: drafts articles from keywords.
  • Email copywriter: writes emails and is paid more than the article writer, because the email list (the people who signed up for your emails) is the most valuable audience.
  • Editor and project manager: reviews articles and emails, and coordinates everyone. This can be a native US English speaker.
  • Designer or illustrator: creates keyword images.
  • Data entry: uploads articles to the blog and enters emails into the email provider (the software that sends your emails). This is a lower-level role. It is estimated at three to four hours of freelancer time.
  • Quality assurance (checking that work is right): the owner's final review.

Reference team size

  • A content team that produced about 7 articles per week had 7 writers, 3 editors, 2 graphic designers and 1 data entry assistant. The data entry assistant prepares search result research for writers.

Team size by stage

  • Once consistent content has produced results for about 12 months, you can grow in three ways. Publish more than 2 pieces per month. Increase promotion of existing pieces. Or hire an additional person.
  • Output and promotion should keep rising. Do not hold them flat once results are visible.
  • Once consistent content has produced results for about 12 months, raise output beyond 2 pieces per month, or hire an additional person to keep growing.
  • Use a fixed two-month review to keep the content process running. Spend at most about 30 minutes per review adding research and ideas to the planner.
  • Spread one content writer across several stores (separate online shops). Work one store at a time, for about one to two months per store, until its collection pages are complete. A backlog of 60 to 80 empty collection pages was expected to take about six to eight months at that pace. Estimate the backlog size before assigning the writer to a store.

Outsourcing the pipeline (the full chain of steps from idea to publishing)

  • Outsourcing keyword research (finding the search terms people type) and the attention matrix to a virtual assistant is acceptable and cheap. The owner does not need to do all content tasks personally.
  • Techniques from Amazon Kindle eBook publishing (selling digital books on Amazon) can be adapted to dropship store content creation. In that model, creators build systems around templates (ready-made formats) and questionnaires to manage third-party writers (outside writers). This lets you scale without expanding the core team.
  • A VA can take a finished Word document or draft, insert images, videos and other assets (files such as pictures and clips), and publish it to the website. An assistant already handling Shopify product uploads (Shopify is a store-building platform) can usually learn blog posting from short instructions.
  • Outsource one task at a time. Perform each task yourself until you understand it. Write an SOP (step-by-step method). Then delegate.

Article Production Workflow and Trackers

2 min

Production hub

  • Use one shared Google Doc per keyword (a shared online document) as the production hub, the single place where all work for a keyword lives. It holds the keyword, the article section where the writer drafts and the editor edits, the image, the article URL (the web address of the article), the email subject line (the email's title) and the email body (the text of the email).

Per-keyword article workflow

  1. Change to the next keyword on the list.
  2. Writer drafts the article and hands it to the editor.
  3. Editor edits the draft and returns it to the writer.
  4. Writer rewrites the article and hands it to the editor.
  5. Editor finalises the article and hands it to the email writer.
  6. Designer delivers the initial sketch of the keyword image.
  7. Owner gives feedback on the sketch.
  8. Designer delivers the final coloured image.
  9. Email writer adds the article link, then writes the subject line and the email body.
  10. Data entry uploads the article to the blog and loads the email into the email provider.
  11. Quality check: confirm every step is complete, then send.

Tracker setup

  • Track every keyword in a spreadsheet, with one row per keyword and one column per task. Put the team member's name in each column header. Mark each completed handoff with an X.
  • Use the grid to find bottlenecks (places where work piles up). For example, ten finished drafts may be waiting for the editor.
  • Track each article in a shared spreadsheet, with one column per production step. Add a checkmark when each step is done. This shows who is responsible for each step and where work is piling up, such as when an editor has a backlog.
  • Outsourcing without a tracker hides where work is stuck. For example, drafts may wait for editing, or articles may never get uploaded to the blog. Without a per-keyword overview, you only notice the problem when the output is already missing.

Operating principle

  • Set up systems and check results regularly. Then revise processes (change the steps) when results fall short. Process, people and product are the core elements of any business. Content is mostly a process and people challenge.

Video, Creative and File Management

4 min

Creative strategy stages

  • Creative strategy (the plan for ad visuals and messages) runs through four stages: conception, planning, production and management. An ad has to pass through all four to be produced and tested properly.
  • Conception: develop the ad concept through research before anything is made. This is where the idea and style are chosen based on evidence.
  • Planning: define how the ad will be put together and which assets must be produced. A plan is needed before any filming, creator briefing (instructions for the people making content) or design work starts.
  • Production: the actual creation of content, such as filming, getting material from creators or designing assets. A plan alone produces no ads.
  • Management, in order:
    1. Upload the ads and confirm they are live.
    2. Check that the media buying is set up correctly. Media buying means buying ad space and setting up paid ads.
    3. Assess the performance metrics (the numbers that show how well each ad works) of each ad.
    4. Use the learnings to feed back into the system. Learnings means what you learned from the results.
    5. Repeat the cycle, improving the ads that worked and stopping the ones that did not.
  • Most brands do only conception and production. Planning and management are ignored or done poorly. Their ideas often appear at random times. They are launched after scraping material together (gathering scraps in a rush). This makes it impossible to test the angles (the different ways of pitching) in an orderly way.

Closing the creative loop

  • Measure what is launched and how it performs. Use the analysis to suggest the next creatives. Then brief them and contact influencers when needed. Each step should feed the next.
  • Build new creatives by combining hooks (the first words or seconds that grab attention) and testimonials (customer reviews shown in ads) from a large research list. This lets you scale testing without filming each ad from scratch. Keep the research list growing so new combinations keep coming.
  • When one concept proves profitable, make new scripts around it, with small hook or detail changes. Keep investing in that concept. Once a fresh creative set is tested weekly or bi-weekly (every two weeks), the winning concept becomes the one to push.
  • Creating resized static ad variants (still-image ads in different sizes) can be delegated to a VA once the process is documented. The resizing step is repetitive and does not need the owner.
  • Brainstorm sessions should run weekly or biweekly (every two weeks). This lets creatives come up with ideas, test new ideas and identify emerging trends (new patterns just starting) to stay ahead of the curve.
  • Once one editorial page (an article-style sales page) converts (turns visitors into buyers), reuse its structure for other products. Change only the character, the problem, the expert and the product facts. Keep the AIDA order (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action, the order of a sales message) and the 70/30 split fixed. Each new page then follows a tested pattern.
  • Once a sales letter or funnel (the path a buyer walks through) has proven a closing structure (the way it ends with a purchase), reuse that structure for new offers. Only the opening and the body need fresh creative work.
  • Use the awareness pyramid (a model of how aware buyers are of the problem and your product) as a recurring check. Use it across traffic-based work such as content marketing, pay-per-click advertising (paying for each click on an ad), retargeting (showing ads to people who visited before) and email marketing. Do not use it only for conversion rate optimisation (improving the share of visitors who buy).

Planning before building

  • Before building any new email sequence (a series of emails sent in order), retargeting campaign or content-based ad, write the plan first. The plan covers what the message should say and when it should be sent. Repeat this step each time a new email idea, new product or new content piece is considered. The plan makes the build faster and easier to hand to outsourced staff.
  • The target is to raise email volume to two, three or four emails per week. A founder cannot produce that volume by hand. So modular building blocks (reusable parts you assemble) are needed to make it workable in daily operation.
  • Email marketing example: an email marketer was hired from onlinejobs.ph (a freelance job site). She was trained on your products and Klaviyo (an email software tool). She then created campaigns she had never made before. She became an expert in your business through hands-on work. On-the-job training is often more effective than hiring an "experienced" expert who will not adapt to your specific needs.

Video file management

  1. Create one main project folder with three subfolders: video, audio and assets.
  2. Store raw footage (unedited video) and product footage in the video folder. Include stock footage (ready-made clips you buy or download).
  3. Store music in the audio folder. Split it into sound effects and songs.
  4. Store graphics and photos in the assets folder, in an imagery subfolder.
  5. Keep the final project files in a cloud folder such as Google Drive (online storage). Keep a local synced copy (a copy on your computer that updates automatically). Do not keep them only in Downloads.
  • Moving or deleting source files after importing them (bringing them into the editing program) breaks the links in the project. Footage can then disappear when you reopen it. Store all media in a fixed location, such as a cloud folder synced locally. Never rely on the Downloads folder.
  • Keep raw editing project files backed up in the cloud, so a project can be recovered if the local computer or drive fails. Store source assets in organised folders alongside the project file.

Link Building Delegation

1 min

What can be delegated

2 min
  • Delegate backlink building work, such as skyscraper outreach, to a VA (virtual assistant, a remote helper) or a freelancer (a self-employed worker you hire for a job). A backlink is a link from another website to yours. Skyscraper outreach means finding popular content, making a better version, and asking sites that link to the old version to link to yours.
  • Written SOPs (standard operating procedures, which are step-by-step written instructions) make it possible to keep building backlinks to the site on an ongoing basis.
  • Resource page link building (getting links from pages that list useful sites) and broken link building (replacing dead links on other sites with your page) can also be handed to a VA, assistant or freelancer.
  • These processes suit delegation once the niche opportunities are identified. A niche is a narrow topic or market.
  • Link building tasks such as spreadsheet work (data in rows and columns), email sending and follow-ups are simple.
  • They need no advanced English or specialist skills. They can be outsourced at low cost, unlike coding, development, content writing or creative work.
  • The outreach work (contacting other sites to ask for a link) is repetitive and manual.
  • It needs little specialist skill, so a general VA can usually perform it.
  • There is no need to pay a high-cost freelancer. The tasks can be added to an existing VA's workload.
  • Compared with classic one-by-one skyscraper link building, this system is highly automated and scalable (it can grow without needing much more work).
  • After setup, the ongoing time commitment stays small.
  • Most of the work can be outsourced.
  • Broken link checking can be outsourced to a VA at about 5 USD per hour.
  • The assistant runs the checker (a tool that finds dead links) on every content page (a page with written text) of a niche site.
  • The assistant lists the broken links inside the content, with the URL (web address) where each was found.
  • You review the list to decide which links to pursue and which of your pages to offer.
  • Data pulling (exporting data from tools), merging (combining files) and cleaning (fixing errors and duplicates) of the likely-linker list are repetitive manual tasks. A likely-linker list is a list of sites that may link to you.
  • A VA can complete these tasks once the topics are chosen.
  • Sort the backlinks column (the column that counts links to a site) or the referring domains column (the list of different websites that link to you) from highest to lowest.
  • Both sort keys give a useful ordering for choosing which domains (website addresses, like example.com) to process first.
  • The per-domain backlink exports (one file per website) can be merged into one master sheet by copy-and-paste or by an automated method.
  • The automated method depends on whether you use Mac or Windows and which spreadsheet app you use. Follow instructions that match your setup.

Resource page outreach procedure

1 min
  1. Give the virtual assistant your outreach templates (ready-made message texts) and written instructions on the search and filling-in steps.
  2. The assistant searches for resource pages using the approved search terms and builds a list of candidate sites.
  3. Review the list, approve or reject entries, and double-check the quality of each site.
  4. Once the list is approved, the assistant sends the outreach emails and manages the follow-up emails.
  5. Repeat the search periodically to find new resource pages.

Ownership and priority

1 min
  • If the owner keeps doing repetitive link building tasks personally, they drop down the priority list.
  • Tasks done by the owner are rarely completed.
  • Consistent outsourcing prevents this slide.
  • Outsource link building in this order:
    1. Perform each link building task yourself a few times until you understand it.
    2. Once the work is affordable, write or record an SOP for the task.
    3. Delegate the task to a VA or freelancer using that SOP.
  • Existing VAs with spare capacity (unused working time) are a good first choice for link building tasks.
  • They can take on part of the work immediately.
  • This avoids hiring for a new role before the workload justifies it.
  • For a serious link building goal in a growing business, hire a dedicated link building team member, full time or at least part time.
  • Once content production runs steadily, more link opportunities appear.
  • A dedicated person speeds up progress.
  • Extensive outreach also builds industry relationships.
  • These relationships can lead to business partnerships, co-promotions (promoting each other's products), guest posting (writing articles for other sites), featured mentions and promotions to their audience.
  • In a long-term business, these relationships can pay off well beyond the link itself.

Social Media and Influencer Outreach

1 min

Organic social posting

1 min
  • Organic social posting (unpaid posts) shows that the brand is active and real when customers search for it.
  • It is not a direct sales channel, so it does not bring many sales on its own.
  • Paid social (paid ads on social media) for high-ticket products (expensive items) often has minimal return on ad spend (money earned per dollar spent on ads).
  • Paid social also has a long payback (time needed to earn back the cost). Outsourcing it is not recommended.
  • A social media VA received a brand kit (logo, colors, fonts and style rules) from the owner. She had access to the website images.
  • She made slight changes to each image so every post looked unique.
  • She published across all accounts from Hootsuite (a tool that schedules posts).
  • A VA wrote a blog post every two weeks.
  • Each post was proofread (checked for errors) before publishing.
  • The posts were then shared on social media.
  • Delegate social media engagement (interaction such as replies and follows):
    1. Hire a VA or use automation tools (software that runs tasks by itself).
    2. Have them engage on Pinterest (a picture-sharing site): pinning, inviting followers, following accounts.
    3. Manage Instagram, Facebook and Twitter interactions.
    4. Post blog content links to social channels.
    5. Monitor engagement metrics (numbers that show interaction, such as likes) and adjust frequency based on results.

Posting cadence: sources differ

1 min
  • Cadence means how often you post. The expected Instagram cadence was two to three posts per day, with Instagram as the primary platform.
  • Creator-made content (content made by content creators) can supply much of this volume.
  • Use this cadence only when you have creator content to fill it.
  • In the first phase, post occasionally, such as when a new supplier approval comes in. Do not post several times a week.
  • SEO (search engine optimization, getting found in search results) and blogs are a longer-term play than paid traffic (visitors from ads).
  • Use this cadence for a new store.
  • Recurring weekly content formats posted on fixed weekdays build loyalty.
  • Followers learn when to come back.
  • Choose a format simple enough to sustain for the long term. Consistency matters more than complexity.
  • Examples of recurring series: a Monday myth-busting post (correcting common false beliefs).
  • Examples of recurring series: a Wednesday behind-the-scenes post (a look inside your business).
  • Examples of recurring series: a Friday weekly recap (a summary of the week).
  • Adjust the day and format to the brand's rhythm (its usual pattern).

Influencer and outreach

1 min
  • Use experienced VAs to research influencer lists (lists of people with online followers who promote products).
  • The manual work is cheap compared with the value of the list.
  • A list of a few hundred influencers typically pays back the cost of the work.
  • After a campaign, record which influencer collaboration formats and messages worked best with your audience.
  • Reuse these proven approaches in future partnerships to improve results over time.
  • For a technical niche where you are not an expert, find people who already have authority in niche Facebook groups or Reddit communities (online forums).
  • Offer them a paid freelance content gig (a paid one-off writing job).
  • Influencers can also be asked to contribute to your content.
  • Outreach can be scaled by hiring a VA who sends messages from several social accounts.
  • This is funded from about 500 dollars per month of cash flow (money coming in and going out).
  • The seller used purchased older Instagram accounts for this.
  • That carries account-ban and platform-policy risk (the risk of being banned or breaking the platform's rules). Check this before doing the same.

Outsourcing vs. owner-led

1 min
  • Outsource posting and engagement once the brand's tone (the way the brand sounds) is set.
  • The owner should still check the brand kit and approve the first posts.

Further details

1 min
  • Build an attention matrix (the research list this section describes). Aim for at least 50 entries, with no upper limit.
  • Keep adding entries, remove those that disappear, and review the whole list about once a year.
  • Building the attention matrix is a simple, repeatable research task.
  • A VA can do it from written instructions.
  • It is a good first task for a new team member. A founder can do it personally before hiring.
  • Build the attention matrix in short, regular daily time blocks, not one long session.
  • Keep working on it until you have a solid number of entries.
  • A VA can take on this task from written instructions. It is a good first onboarding task (the first task a new hire does).
  • Backlinks for product pages are not required to rank (appear high in search results). Start with product page SEO.
  • Competitive niche keywords (search terms many sellers chase) need more backlinks and time.
  • Long-tail product pages (very specific pages with less competition) can rank without them.

Scaling Google and bing ad budgets

Section 20.17 · 17 of 26 in this chapter · 11 min read

In short

  • Raise the daily budget (the most you spend per day) only when ROAS (return on ad spend: revenue earned per dollar spent on ads) stays at 10X or higher.
  • Never raise budgets or bids below 10X.
  • Each review, do the steps in order: check search terms first, then negative keywords, then bids, then budget.
  • Launch one ad channel at a time, and make it profitable before starting the next.
  • Ignore Google's suggestion to raise budgets. Decide from your own numbers.

Core principles of scaling Google ad budgets

2 min
  • Scaling works in three steps, one after another: add negative keywords (search words that stop your ad from showing), adjust cost-per-click bids (CPC: the price you pay each time someone clicks your ad), and raise daily budgets. Work through each step in order to keep profit and control.
  • The three steps are negative keywords, max CPC per product (your highest allowed click price for each product), and daily budgets. Repeat the negative keyword and CPC work before each budget increase.
  • Start with a small budget so Google can learn, and so searches that bring clicks but no sales (non-converting search terms) are filtered out sooner. Scale spend only once campaigns are profitable enough to reinvest (put profit back into ads).
  • Scale a campaign only while it stays profitable, with positive ROAS. Growth comes from adding profitable spend, not from spending as much as possible at once.
  • Grow budgets in steps. The priority is moving from a profitable $500 per month to $1,000, then $1,500, and so on. The goal is to be profitable at twice the current size.
  • Total profit in dollars rises even as ROAS falls toward 10X. Keep a sustainable ROAS (around 15X) and grow revenue through larger budgets. Do not try to keep the highest percentage return.
  • Plan for ROAS to drop when a product scales. Keep enough margin (the money left from each sale after costs) so the product stays profitable at the lower return.
  • Do not launch all Google Ads channels at once. A channel is one way of getting traffic, such as Google Shopping, Bing, or Facebook. Build and make one channel profitable before starting the next.
  • Do not raise budgets because Google recommends it. Google will always suggest a higher budget, so decide changes only from your own ROAS data.
  • Before raising the ad budget, fully optimize negative keywords and campaign segmentation (splitting campaigns into groups, such as by brand or product). Check impression share (how often your ad shows for the searches it could show for). Improve store conversion (the share of visitors who buy) so more visitors buy. **

  • The 10X floor is the stop line for any increase. The 15X figure is the level to aim for while scaling. Both apply at the same time.
  • Check that ROAS stays consistently above 10X for two weeks to a month before any raise.
  • Scale a channel only as far as its ROAS stays at 10X or above. When that limit is reached, put the remaining budget into the next channel.
  • A ROAS drop at a larger budget is normal. Example: 15X at $3K, an initial drop to about 10X at $5K, then optimize back to 15X at $5K.

The first month: learning phase

1 min
  • Expect about 30 days of steady optimization (regular checking and adjusting) before most successful operators (the people who run the store) see real momentum in Google Shopping campaigns.
  • The first month is spent filtering bad traffic with negative keywords, calibrating CPC bids (setting click prices correctly), and training Google to find the campaign's most profitable keywords.
  • Growing from a profitable $500 per month to a profitable $1,000 per month often takes real time, even on large campaigns. This should be the top priority.
  • Time needed to manage paid search (ads you pay for on search engines) rises with spend. A store at $1,000 per month needs far fewer management hours than one at $10,000 or $50,000 per month.

Negative keywords and CPC levers

1 min
  • Add negative keywords from the search terms report (the list of what shoppers actually typed) in every review. This keeps budget away from traffic that does not convert.
  • Raise max CPC bids in small steps, such as five cents at a time, to find the ROAS sweet spot (the best balance of click price and return).
  • Large CPC jumps can cause spending at an excessive rate (spending too fast). If an ad is unprofitable and overspending, cut it quickly.
  • Pausing underperforming products (products that sell poorly) and reducing bids on weak performers moves spend toward the best-converting products. Successful stores know which products drive profit and bid aggressively on them.
  • Pausing non-performers frees budget and inventory slots (space for products in your catalogue) to test new products, so the catalogue improves over time.

Review routine

1 min
  • Set a recurring reminder, weekly or bi-weekly (every two weeks), for scaling optimization. Make it a fixed owner task in the calendar, not a one-time setup.
  • Each review, in this order:
    1. Review search terms.
    2. Add negative keywords.
    3. Adjust CPC bids.
    4. Increase the daily budget if ROAS is at least 10X.
  • Once you know the routine, a full review of product-level max CPC changes in a Shopping campaign takes about 15 minutes. The first one or two reviews take longer.
  • Track these numbers regularly: quality score (Google's rating of how relevant your ad is), CTR (click-through rate: the share of people who see your ad and click it), CPC, conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy), and ROAS.

Budget progressions

2 min

Sources give different ladders (step-by-step budget plans). Use the ladder that matches the account's stage and ROAS. Never skip a step while ROAS is weak.

  • Google Shopping ladder, 2024 and 2025 sources:

    • Example from one source: $10, $15, $20, $30, $50 per day, potentially reaching $500 to $2,000 per day if profitability is maintained.
    • Alternative: a new account starts with a shared daily budget (one budget used by several campaigns) of $10. Steps follow $10, $15, $20, $30, $40, $50, each only after the previous level has held the target return.
    • Reaching $500 to $2,000 per day can take about a year of scaling, if the return holds.
    • At a $2,000 daily budget with a 10X return, daily revenue must be at least $20,000.
    • Stop increasing once the next level cannot be reached while staying at 10X. That level is the breaking point (the level where growth stops being profitable).

  • Later Google Shopping steps (2025): move from $30 to $40 or $50. Never jump from $30 to $120, because large jumps make Google relearn the campaign and waste spend.

  • Shopping ladder with pauses (2024): $50 to $70 per day, wait about two weeks and check ROAS, then $90 or $100.

  • Large-budget ladder (2023): $100 to $120 per day, let it settle for a few days, then $140.

  • Monthly search budget ladder (general): start with a budget that delivers at least 10X, for example $1,000 per month. Raise in steps, such as $1,000 to $2,000, then $3,000 to $4,000. Keep ROAS at or above 10X at each step before going further.

  • Make larger jumps only once the business has a stronger bankroll (money reserves) and accepts more risk.

Raising a daily budget step by step

1 min

Procedure A, 2024 source:

  1. Click Shopping campaigns at the top, then Campaigns, to see the daily budgets. Example: both campaigns at $15 per day as a shared budget.
  2. Confirm that ROAS has held at 10X or higher for about two weeks, and that negative keywords and product CPCs were adjusted in that period.
  3. Click the edit icon next to the budget and change the value. Example: $15 to $20.
  4. Let the new budget run for one to two weeks.
  5. Then add negative keywords from the search terms, adjust CPCs based on results, and check ROAS again.
  6. If ROAS is still at least 10X, increase the budget again and repeat the loop.
  • Example: after two weeks at a 15X return, with negative keywords and CPCs already adjusted, a $15 daily budget can be raised to $20 per day.

Procedure B, 2025 source:

  1. Check ROAS over two weeks to a month.
  2. Confirm ROAS is consistently above 10X.
  3. Raise the daily budget modestly, such as from $30 to $40 or $50.
  4. Watch ROAS for the next period before raising again.
  5. If you raised the budget too far, lower it back right away.

Scaling a large catalogue on a limited budget

1 min

Procedure (2023): the two sources below describe the same method, so they are merged here.

  1. Use historical sales data (sales records from the past), such as about three years, to identify the top 1 to 500 products out of 5,000.
  2. Place those products into a Google Shopping campaign.
  3. Optimize their product pages first (the web page for each single product).
  4. Keep the remaining products on free organic traffic (free visitors who find you through search) only.
  5. When the winners take off, optimize the ads further, raise spend, and add more products gradually.

Product-level focus and Shopping actions

1 min
  • Shopping action items:
    1. Place customer search terms at the front of Shopping product titles.
    2. Run Facebook dynamic product ads (ads that automatically show visitors the products they looked at) for visitors who viewed products.
    3. Add an incentive opt-in on the site (such as a discount offered in exchange for an email address) to capture emails and earn the email revenue lift (extra sales from email).
    4. Review the numbers regularly: quality score, CTR, CPC, conversion rate, and ROAS.
  • A strong brand can stay in one campaign while the other brands get dedicated budgets. Separating the strong brand's ads from the rest protects its performance. **

Ceiling of Google Shopping

1 min
  • Profitable Google Shopping traffic is limited. The ceiling (the most you can earn) varies with niche (the market segment you sell to), product, brand, and market. In the examples, it ranged from tens of thousands to hundreds of thousands of dollars in sales per month. **
  • Narrow niches have smaller search pools (fewer people searching) and a lower ceiling. A seller of beginner-only products hits the limit much sooner than one covering all skill levels. **

Adding other channels

2 min

When to start a channel

  • Start Google ads only after the first two suppliers are approved and have their products uploaded. Keep recruiting suppliers and uploading products while the ads run.
  • Build and profit from one channel before starting the next.
  • Add other channels, such as SEO (free ranking work in search results), social media, content, and email, once paid search runs profitably. They tend to perform better on top of that base.
  • A profitable Google Shopping campaign can work as a retargeting channel (showing ads again to people who already visited). Run it for a while before judging, since it can pick up the slack in profit.

Bing (a second search engine)

  • Early-phase split (2024): focus most of the budget on Google, for example $500 of $1,000. Move $100 to $150 into Bing and reserve about $100 for Meta (Facebook and Instagram), growing Bing later.
  • Earlier rule (2019 **): Bing Shopping ads should start only after Google Shopping product listing ads are profitable and tuned. Starting Bing early will rarely double income.
  • Apply both: the 2024 split works for a small early budget, while the 2019 rule governs when Bing shopping should start. Bing is never a substitute for a tuned Google Shopping campaign.

Six-month sequence (2024)

  1. Before October is out, confirm the store's numbers and the exact minimum ROAS.
  2. In months two and three, roll out product listing ads (PLA: ads that show product pictures and prices), search text ads on PLA terms (plain text ads on the search words that bring product ads), and search retargeting (the three-pack), plus retargeting for product listing ads.
  3. In month four, launch dynamic product ads on Facebook.
  4. In month five, start cold lookalike audiences (new people similar to your past buyers, who have not visited yet) that feed Facebook pre-sales engagement content (content that builds interest before a sale). This takes time to build.
  5. Roll out each piece in sequence, not everything at once.

Management time and handover

1 min
  • Plan the owner's or manager's time to grow with the budget. The workload is lightest during the initial setup and learning phase.
  • Grow a paid search channel in this order:
    1. While spend is small, invest your own hours to set up campaigns, learn the mechanics, and understand the account.
    2. Prove that the campaigns are viable and profitable.
    3. Once viability is proven, hand day-to-day management to a professional, or bring in a consultant for targeted review.
    4. With paid search running profitably, add other channels such as SEO, social, content, and email.
  • A campaign profitable at $500 per month can realistically be scaled to $3,000 per month profitably with ongoing work.

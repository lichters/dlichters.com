Automation and AI workflows

Section 20.4 · 4 of 26 in this chapter · 24 min read

In short

  • Automate a task once it repeats, even if it is not yet perfect.
  • Do each task by hand once first, so you understand it before you automate it.
  • Automation saves time but does not replace human judgment for exceptions.
  • Start with AI Workflows (AI doing fixed steps in order). Skip AI Agents for now, because they are unreliable.
  • Keep your daily manual work to 30 minutes or less.
More key points
  • AI can do many written tasks, but a person must check every AI output before it goes live.
  • Start with simple, fixed steps. AI agents that choose their own steps are still unreliable.
  • Test a small batch first, then scale up only when the results look right.
  • Decide the mix of people and AI for each brand before you pick tools or team size.

Readiness: when a task is ready to automate

1 min
  • A task that repeats regularly is ready for automation. Repetition is the test, not perfection.
  • Automation fits tasks that happen day after day.
  • Automate order capture only once suppliers reliably have stock. Until then, capture orders by hand and have a team member confirm stock on each order. **
  • Fix the cause of a problem before you automate the answer to it. A problem that never happens needs no handling.
  • Automate only after you have fixed the root causes (the real reasons) of calls, emails and refunds.
  • Automate repeated customer-facing steps once volume grows (more orders), because doing them by hand becomes overwhelming.
  • Example: quiz replies were moved to a team member for a personal touch. The team went back to automation after about one week, because the automated version was better.
  • Find repetitive manual tasks written down in Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs: step-by-step written rules for a job).
  • Any task that repeats consistently can become a Zapier automation. Zapier is a tool that connects apps and runs steps for you. Doing this reduces human error and frees team capacity for high-value work.
  • Any task that staff repeat the same way several times is a strong candidate for automation.
  • It saves money and frees people for higher-value work (work that earns more or needs human thinking).
  • Automation handles rule-based, repetitive tasks. A rule looks like "trigger X, perform Y": when X happens, do Y.
  • Automation does not replace human judgment for exceptions, negotiation or creative decisions. Use the time it frees for those tasks.
  • A dropshipping store is a shop where suppliers ship orders straight to customers. The aim of an automated one is to keep daily manual work to 30 minutes or less, even when the store is very profitable.
  • Judge every tool and automation by how much recurring manual work it removes.

Manual first, then automate

1 min
  • Do each process once by hand to understand it. Then move it to automation for ongoing work.
  • Early on, focus on manual work that cannot grow by itself, such as personal email follow-ups and one-by-one outreach to abandoned checkouts (customers who left before paying).
  • This manual work gets the most sales from the traffic (visitors) you already have.
  • As you grow, these tasks can be automated or handed to others.
  • Manual work also gives customer feedback that shows what to improve.
  • For a team, automate the order email step only after several orders have gone through smoothly by hand.
  • Add automation once the manual process is verified (checked to work).
  • Supplier order emails and the Trello card move are done by hand at first. Trello is an online board of cards, one card per task.
  • Once several orders go through without errors, add a Zapier automation. It moves the card to "sent to supplier" when the order email goes out.
  • Add a note on the card showing it was moved automatically.
  • Add an extra note to an automated card when an automation moves it. This way staff can tell it was not handled manually.
  • Study automations only after the store gets paid traffic (visitors from paid ads) and runs day to day.
  • Automations help most once you understand the normal workflow (the usual order of work). **
  • Learning automation pays off over time.
  • After you understand one automation, you start seeing similar chances across the business. Examples: inventory updates (stock counts), customer emails, competitor monitoring (watching rivals) and report generation.

Time saved and where it goes

1 min
  • Automation is meant to free time.
  • Campaign management, bid optimization (adjusting how much you pay per ad click), and Google and Facebook ad bid optimization together typically take about 5 hours each.
  • Use the saved hours for growth instead of manual adjustments.
  • Move time saved by automation to higher-value work. Examples: product titles, product page conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy on that page), cross-channel feed distribution (sending your product list to many sales channels), branding and cold social funnels (ways to reach people who do not know you yet).
  • For example, five hours of bid tweaking can go to product titles or product pages instead.
  • Automate in steps, not everything at once.
  • As paid traffic becomes more consistent, shift attention to branding, conversion rate optimization (CRO: getting more buyers from the same visitors), SEO (search engine optimization: showing up in Google results), email marketing, social media, content marketing and influencer outreach (asking creators with followers to promote you).
  • Automation and outsourcing let order volume, sales and customer count grow without a matching increase in work, manpower or hours.
  • This is what makes the business ready to scale (grow big).
  • Automation templates (ready-made setups) can be used to automate most store operations.
  • Ready-made templates can be set up directly in the system.

Learning the stack

2 min

The stack is the set of tools your business uses.

  1. Use each tool in the stack yourself at least once to learn how it works: Shopify (software to run an online shop), Facebook page, Google Sheets (a free online spreadsheet), Trello, Slack (a team chat app) and Zapier.
  2. Only after you understand each tool, set up the automations that connect them.
  3. Keep the free versions of Slack, Trello and Google Sheets unless a real limit is reached.
  4. Pay only for Zapier's $20 per month plan, and only when you need the Shopify integration (the link between Zapier and Shopify).
  • Automations work like a virtual employee (software that does repeat work like a staff member).
  • A virtual employee handles repeatable tasks such as order tracking, posting and sending notifications.
  • Connect each automation through Zapier and have it report results to Slack or a shared sheet.
  • Automation platforms cost approximately $20 per month and reliably execute tasks.
  • They avoid the operational costs and risks of employees: no sick days, errors, salary increases or staff turnover (people leaving).
  • Process automation costs $20 per month.
  • It runs 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year, including holidays.
  • Once set up, automation runs without errors.
  • It does not stop for illness or other human factors, and it never asks for a raise.
  • It keeps business secrets safe and keeps improving its abilities.
  • This frees the owner for work that needs human judgment and strategic thinking (long-term planning).
  • Automations have trade-offs.
  • Automated email sequences (a series of emails sent in order) sometimes arrive at the wrong time for some customers. Examples: during backorders (an item is out of stock and ordered for later) or shipping delays.
  • This timing mismatch is an acceptable trade-off for operational scalability (growing without more staff) and simple systems.
  • Overall it causes minimal harm. **
  • Updating order notes in Asana (an online task manager) by hand is the weakest point in daily work.
  • Automation or a redesigned workflow would reduce this burden.

Zapier and Trello order flows

2 min

A Zap is one automation built in Zapier.

Trello order flow:

  1. Customer places order: Zap 1 automatically creates a card in paid orders with complete order details (name, email, product, SKU (the product's code), shipping address).
  2. When the supplier ships, mark fulfillment (packing and sending the order) as shipped in Shopify. Zap 2 automatically moves the card to shipped orders.
  3. When tracking (the shipment's tracking number) shows delivery, manually move the card to delivered and pending review.
  4. Contact the customer by phone, email or text using the follow-up script to request a review and feedback.
  5. Once the review is received, move the card to the reviewed column.
  6. Goal: ensure no order is forgotten and every customer receives follow-up within days of delivery.
  • When an order is marked as shipped in Shopify, Zapier moves its Trello card from the "Sent to Supplier" list to the "Ship Orders" list.
  • No one has to move the card by hand.
  • Two ways to set the delivered move are given.
  • By hand: when you check tracking yourself, move the card on delivery (step 3 above).
  • Automatic: set the delay between "fulfilled" and "delivered" in Trello to match the usual delivery time, for example 7 days after fulfillment.
  • Supplier order automation: after all setup is complete, toggle (flip) the switch labeled "off by default" to "on".
  • The Zap then immediately processes all new paid orders according to the rules you set.
  • After turning on the Zap, watch it for the first few orders.
  • Check the Shopify admin (the back office of your shop) and the BCC emails (hidden copies of sent emails) in your inbox. Confirm emails are being submitted to suppliers correctly.
  • Checking the first orders proves the setup works before you rely on it for all orders.
  • The Trello board is a management dashboard (one screen that shows the status of all work). It needs no daily involvement.
  • If the paid orders list holds several cards with old dates, that signals slow supplier processing or internal fulfillment gaps. You can then intervene quickly.
  • Order tracking and review follow-up can be self-managed or handed to a virtual assistant (a remote helper).
  • The system scales from low to high volume without manual intervention.
  • As volume grows, systematic tracking stops orders slipping through with new or untested suppliers.
  • It also keeps customer contact consistent for review collection.

1 min

  • B2B bulk orders handled through quote forms typically involve 50 or more units per request.

Build the custom order automation in Zapier as follows:

  1. Set WuFu (as written in the source) or Typeform (an online form tool) as the trigger (the event that starts the automation). Select the "Form Completed" event.
  2. Add an action: send a Slack private message (a message only one person sees) containing the customer name, email, phone number, location and inquiry details.
  3. Add a second action: insert a new row into the Google Sheets tracking sheet with all lead information (a potential customer's details).
  4. Both actions run automatically in sequence when the form is submitted on the website.

Automation versus outsourcing

1 min
  • Outsourcing means hiring humans to perform business tasks.
  • Automation means using tools, apps and bots (programs that act on their own) to execute processes automatically.
  • Outsourcing means buying a service from a third party in place of an in-house resource (someone on your own team).
  • Outsourcing adds capabilities the owner lacks.
  • It is not the same as automation, which removes the human step entirely.
  • The two can be combined, but they solve different problems.
  • Example: setting up automated email sequences that nurture leads (warm up potential buyers) and close sales is automation.
  • The repeated manual work of writing and sending disappears.
  • Paying someone to write and send those emails by hand would be outsourcing, which is a different thing.
  • Compare the two on cost and reliability. Automation runs for about $20 per month with no sick days or turnover.
  • Staff bring the costs and risks listed under readiness and cost above.

AI workflows, AI agents and their levels

1 min

AI means artificial intelligence, such as ChatGPT or Claude.

Automations progress through three levels. Start with AI Workflows (AI doing fixed steps in a set order).

Level What it does Example Current status Source
Workflows Linear, triggered by time or events, without AI Daily ad reports to Slack; competitor ads to Google Drive Reliable
AI Workflows Chain multiple AI steps in a predetermined order Scrape forum → summarize → generate angles → write scripts → save to spreadsheet Start here
AI Agents The system decides what to do dynamically Email agent that chooses between responding, notifying or scheduling Unreliable, fails frequently
  • AI Agents are AI that picks its own next steps. Current technology is unreliable and fails frequently.
  • Do not prioritize building agents now.
  • Agents often make unpredictable decisions or misinterpret instructions.
  • Most tasks that seem suited to agents work more reliably as AI Workflows. AI Workflows use predetermined steps with AI at each stage.
  • Plan to revisit agents in a few months as the technology matures.
  • Example of an email agent. It can:
    • notify you in Slack about an incoming email;
    • compose a reply using Claude or another AI model;
    • send the reply directly to the sender;
    • read your previous emails for context;
    • check your calendar for availability;
    • create a Google Calendar event for a proposed meeting.
  • It makes these decisions dynamically, without a predetermined sequence.
  • AI org charts: a hierarchy of agents. A CEO agent (the top boss role) delegates to specialized agents such as a CMO (marketing head), COO (operations head) or CFO (finance head) agent.
  • Each specialized agent can manage its own sub-agents.
  • For example, a CMO agent might supervise copywriter, competitor research, data analysis and landing page builder agents.
  • This models how human teams scale and allows automation of complex multi-team workflows.

AI creative workflows: setup

4 min

Source D setup (Steps 0-8 once, then Steps 9-10 for each new ad script):

  • Steps 0-8 build five reference files (documents the AI reads every time) in a Claude or ChatGPT project (a saved workspace where files stay).
  • The five files are: Voice Guide, Structure Library, Banned List, Avatar and Psychology Lens.
  • Once these five files live in the project, Steps 9-10 create a new ad in a fresh chat every time.
  • The project reads the five files automatically, so you supply only the product, winning scripts, research and chosen angle.
  • Set up Steps 0-8 once and upload the five reference files to your project.
  • After that, run only Steps 9-10 for new ads.
  • Every script session is a new chat in the same project, so the five files stay constant and you change only the angle (the main idea of the ad).

Procedure:

  1. Gather 5-10 winning ad scripts from Kalodata (a tool that finds popular products and ads) or the free Meta Ad Library (a public list of ads). Any ad running for months is a proven winner. Copy them into one document and keep it.

  2. Open Reddit Answers and ask six research questions, one at a time. Copy each complete answer into one research document:

    • (1) What's so hard about [SITUATION]?
    • (2) Why do people struggle with [PROBLEM]?
    • (3) What have people tried for [PROBLEM] that didn't work?
    • (4) What finally worked for people with [PROBLEM]?
    • (5) Why do people give up on [PROBLEM]?
    • (6) What do people dislike about [CATEGORY]?

    Copy everything. Don't pick and choose, because the next prompts sort it.

  3. Run the Step 3 prompt to reverse-engineer (take apart to find the ideas behind) the concepts in your winning scripts. It identifies concept families (groups of similar ideas) and gaps.

  4. to 8. Run the Step 4-8 prompts to generate and download five files: Voice Guide, Structure Library, Banned List, Avatar, Psychology Lens. Then upload each into your project knowledge.

  5. Run the Step 9 prompt in a new project chat to get a ranked angle bank (a sorted list of ad ideas).

  6. Run the Step 10 prompt in the same chat, pick one angle and get your script.

Conflict hierarchy (Step 10). A conflict hierarchy is the order of which rule wins when two rules clash. The Source D source names two files differently: "Voice Guide" in the setup and "Style Guide" in the rule. It also names "Banned List" in the setup and "Banned Blacklist" in the rule. Use the names as they appear in your project.

Use the five-file conflict hierarchy exactly as written:

  1. Style Guide governs voice first.
  2. Structure Library governs beats second.
  3. Banned Blacklist supplies hard negative constraints third.
  4. Avatar & Context tells you who to write to fourth.
  5. Psychology Lens informs but never overrides voice, fifth.

Flag any brief element that contradicts a reference file instead of overriding it silently.

Bottom-of-funnel ad production. Bottom-of-funnel ads are aimed at people ready to buy.

  1. Ask Claude: "Make me four [concept type] bottom-of-funnel ad prompts for ChatGPT."
  2. Copy each prompt to ChatGPT and generate image descriptions or ad text.
  3. Upload each generated image to Canva (an online design tool).
  4. Re-download from Canva to remove AI metadata (hidden AI tags inside the file).
  5. Upload all images to Meta.
  6. Create one ad set per concept (a group of ads sharing one budget and audience), e.g., testimonial, free gift, benefit. Put 4-8 ads inside each.
  7. Set the budget to $50 per day (or $15-20 AUD minimum) and test for 2-3 days.

Prompt (Source E-style system brief: a standing set of instructions for an AI; reproduce unchanged):

Set up a streamlined AI system to help our brand produce ads that perform better using data and scale more quickly. Core capabilities: 1. Getting to know customers: go through survey results, persona profiles and behavior data. Work out the real pain points, desires, objections and buying motives. Watch for emerging patterns across our segments. 2. Market and competitor research: look into rivals and the direction of the industry. Surface gaps and openings where our brand can differentiate and strengthen its positioning. 3. Creative planning and ideation: come up with new ad angles, hooks and creative concepts drawn from customer insight. Strengthen current ideas by basing them on data and proven marketing frameworks. 4. Scriptwriting and copywriting: produce persuasive, high-converting ad scripts and marketing copy. Revise copy so it matches the emotions, language and motivations of the audience. 5. Performance feedback loop: examine ROAS, CTR, hook rate, watch time, CPC and comparable metrics. Clarify why particular ads win and others fail. Turn these findings into concrete guidance for the next round of creatives. 6. Knowledge base integration: rely on all uploaded files and research as the main knowledge source. Apply insights and Breakthrough Advertising principles automatically to everything produced. Keep every answer anchored in real audience data. Benefits for the founder: converts customer feedback into clear, actionable insight; recommends creative angles and strategies matched to the audience; helps produce better ads faster from real data; supports ongoing improvement by evaluating ad results and closing the creative feedback loop.

AI creative workflows: the reference-file build

2 min
  • Build the reference files that a brand's custom AI assistant needs. A custom AI assistant is an AI helper set up for one brand.
  • Keep these files inside the assistant's project. A project is the folder the AI tool uses to hold its files, so its research follows the brand's own process.
  • Include these six files:
    1. A brand or product overview.
    2. A strategy knowledge file describing the creative method.
    3. A copy quality rules file.
    4. A team process overview.
    5. A discovery call template, meaning a set of questions for the first talk with a client.
    6. Production documents for static ads (still image ads) and for video ads.
  • Use AI to analyze what customers say.
  • A weekly review scraper can turn customer language into ad ideas. A scraper is a tool that automatically copies text from web pages, here customer reviews.
  • Many people see AI only as an idea generator, a script writer or a visual generator.
  • In practice, almost any task that can be written down can be completed by AI, automated, or assisted by AI.
  • Tools, templates and software built with AI can assist current team members.
  • They can also replace some roles entirely.
  • This reduces slow manual work in creative production, which means making ads and content.
  • A coded workflow connects AI tools and software. It is a set of automatic steps where each step passes its result to the next.

    1. The custom AI assistant creates a prompt-based output. A prompt is the written instruction you give the AI.
    2. The workflow sends it to a platform such as Arcads, a tool that creates ads with AI.
    3. The result is saved to a spreadsheet.
    4. A human is notified on Slack, a team chat app, to review it.

Creative team roles and the human-AI balance

2 min
  • A traditional creative team has fixed roles: strategist, copywriter, editor and designer.
  • A strategist plans what to say and to whom. A copywriter writes the words.
  • Those roles can be replaced by a breakdown into processes and tasks.
  • Each task can be done by a person or by an AI agent. An AI agent is an AI that works through a task on its own.
  • This works only as long as the process is predictable, meaning it always runs the same way.
  • The process must also be written as step-by-step instructions.
  • Break every team role into processes.
  • Then break each process into individual tasks, from start to finish.
  • Check which tasks can be automated or handed to AI.
  • Any task that can be described as step-by-step instructions can, in theory, be completed by AI.
  • Any task a person can be trained to do can, in theory, be trained into an AI agent.
  • The AI can run the same task up to 100 times faster than a human.
  • Projected capability means a forward-looking estimate, not a measured result.
  • Once the software and workflow are set up, AI could return creative test projects within about 10 seconds. A creative test projects several ad versions so you can compare them.
  • A full creative test cycle could be completed in about 20 minutes.
  • Measured experience: the first AI-produced videos took about one hour each while the creator was learning the tools.
  • Once the workflow is familiar, the same production time drops to about 20 to 30 minutes per video.
  • Some brands can run with one creative team member. That person oversees a group of AI agents.
  • This works only with a well-defined, reliable workflow.
  • It also needs a human who reviews the output before it goes live.
  • The right balance of humans and AI is a decision for each brand.
  • The balance depends on its budget, its niche (the specific market or customer group it sells to), its existing team and its required approvals.
  • Work out this balance before choosing tools or team size.
  • A creative strategist's typical tasks are:
    • Analyzing what customers say.
    • Identifying likely buyers and their motivations, meaning why they want to buy.
    • Extracting core ideas for ads, meaning the main message of each ad.
    • Finding ideas and styles not yet tested.
    • Running product, competitor and market analysis.
    • Briefing concepts for ad assets, meaning writing clear instructions for ad images and videos.
    • Reviewing the team's work.
  • Creative systems can be organized into stages.
  • The stages cover conception (coming up with the idea), planning, production and management of ads.
  • Each stage can use its own AI tools.
  • Each stage can also use its own written SOPs, which are standard operating procedures: written step-by-step rules.
  • One AI prompt can set creative KPIs. KPI means key performance indicator, a number that shows whether work is on target.
  • The same prompt can check each team member's launch output per product.
  • This is a simple way to confirm the creative team is delivering against its targets.

Limits of AI workflows and creative automation

1 min
  • Keep a human review step in any AI creative workflow.
  • AI still makes mistakes, such as errors in content or visuals.
  • The review checks the output before ads are published.
  • Have a human edit and fact-check every AI output before it goes live.
  • Do this even when the prompt is well tuned.
  • The first draft can contain wrong product claims, meaning false statements about what the product does, or other inaccuracies.
  • Identify the tasks AI cannot yet do well.
  • Examples include tasks where no tool exists for a combination of tasks.
  • Other examples include tasks that are hard to explain in writing.
  • For those tasks, find a workaround, meaning another way around the problem, or keep a human.
  • Use this list to cut costs.
  • The practical limits of an AI creative workflow are:
    • The number of separate software tools involved;
    • The connections needed between platforms;
    • whether the workflow is set up reliably enough to give predictable output.
  • Design the workflow so these three points are solved before adding more volume.
  • Automation handles rule-based tasks, meaning tasks that follow fixed rules.
  • Automation does not handle exceptions, negotiation or creative decisions.
  • Use the time it frees for exceptions, negotiation and creative decisions.
  • AI agents are unreliable for now.
  • So use AI Workflows with predetermined steps. AI Workflows are AI steps that always run in the same set order.

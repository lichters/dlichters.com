Expanding: second stores, markets, inventory and brand

Section 20.21 · 21 of 26 in this chapter · 16 min read

In short

  • Open a second store only after the first one makes steady profit.
  • A common signal is about $20,000 in sales, but sales are not profit.
  • Keep one niche per store, and keep each store's money separate.
  • Test products with small amounts first. Buy stock in bulk only after sales are proven.
  • Grow in your home market first. Go abroad later.

Readiness before a second store

3 min

  • Build a second store only after the first one is consistently profitable.
    • It must also make steady sales and produce enough profit to support the owner.
    • Launching both at once can copy the first store's mistakes into the second before they are fixed.
  • Grow one niche store until it is maxed out (has grown as far as it can) before opening a second store.
    • Growing an existing store is easier than building a new one.
  • Do not open a second store yet while:
    • The first store has only half its suppliers approved (suppliers are the companies that make or ship your products),
    • supplier product files are not uploaded (these are the item lists your store needs),
    • Facebook ad results are unclear (Facebook ads are paid posts on Facebook).
    • This leads to split focus. The first store must be stable before expanding.
  • Do not open a second store until the first one produces consistent orders, around $20k in sales, and a healthy profit margin.
  • Before opening store two, check the following.
    1. Are you approved with all gold and silver suppliers (the top-ranked suppliers) in your niche?
    2. Are all of their products uploaded to your store?
    3. Are your product pages optimized? (Product pages are the pages that sell one item. Optimized means improved to sell better.)
    4. Is your customer support, by live chat, email and phone, as good as it can be?
    5. Are your traffic sources diversified across Google, Facebook, Bing and SEO? (SEO means free visits from search engines. Diversified means spread across many sources.)
    6. Have you scaled your ad spend? (Ad spend is the money you put into ads. Scaled means increased step by step.)
    7. Do you have email marketing flows for different scenarios? (Flows are automatic email series sent after a trigger, such as a new sign-up.)
    8. Do you run monthly promotions to your social audiences and email list? (Social audience means your followers on social media.)
    9. Do you create content and have a loyal following?
    10. Are you objectively better than your competitors, with a clearer unique selling proposition (the one clear reason to buy from you) and a better buying experience?
  • Do not open multiple stores at the full Shopify fee of $39 per month without being profitable. Shopify is the software that runs an online store.
    • Keep a proven niche with a winning product (a product that sells well).
    • Open a second store to test a new product once the first store has enough data and a working pixel. A pixel is a small tracking code that tells ad tools who bought.

Case: launching two products in new stores

1 min
  • Starting point: earlier tests on a Denmark market and a TikTok product cost about $2,000 in ads (money spent on ads).
    • They brought in $2,700 to $3,000 in sales. That was a small loss.
    • A TikTok product stopped after about $100 spend.
  • Two products were then found with the method.
    • Each was launched in a new store with a new Shopify store, a new Meta ad account (an ad account on Facebook and Instagram) and a new pixel.
    • These were created about two weeks before the sales were reported.
  • One store did about $1,790 in a single day. The other did about $1,500.
  • One product was scaled (grown) within three to four days to over $1,000 per day.
  • Spend was about $500 to $600 per day on one product and about $400 to $500 on the other.
    • Return was about 3x to 4x ROAS (return on ad spend, meaning sales made for each dollar spent on ads).
    • Combined sales were about $3,000 to $5,000 per day.

Store structure: one niche per store and when to separate

1 min
  • Prefer one store per niche.
    • Do not spread the same niche across multiple stores unless you are niching up (aiming at a broader category).
  • Keep a niche store focused.
    • If its niche passed all tests, do not expand it with unrelated products.
    • Sell other product types in related stores instead.
  • Set up each new store as a separate business when its niche is unrelated.
    • Separate stores keep finances, revenue and future sale options clean.
  • Build an additional store for a related product only if buyers would not buy both products.
    • Combine them into one store if one product is a replacement for the other.
  • A niche store with one shared pixel lets you reuse the pixel and its data across several tested products over time.
    • A single-product store can waste a pixel. A niche store keeps the audience data useful for later products.
    • For example, four products at about $300 each means roughly $1,200 of testing spend that still feeds the same pixel.
  • Scale (grow) a store by building more stores and growing the best ones together.
    • Keep each store's finances and bank accounts separate. Then a sale of one network (your group of stores) does not affect the others.
  • Once a store is consistently profitable, do not close it.
    • Keep it running and build the next store on top of the same business.
  • A first store should be evergreen (it sells all year round), not seasonal.
    • Build a second store as a clone of the first, with the same suppliers, a new name, logo and colour scheme.
    • Do this once the first store works.
  • Cloning an existing store with a new niche can be done after the first store is stable.
    • Do not clone before it has consistent traffic and sales.
    • Suppliers already approved can be reused for the new store.

Growing within a store

1 min
  • Expand the product line gradually. Launch one or two new products per month in the same niche, not many at once.
    • Each product can then be tested with its own ads and page.
  • Once a store has repeat customers, promote new launches to them first.
    • This raises average order value (the average amount spent per order) and improves return rates (how often customers come back).
    • Keep existing customers served well while new products are added.
  • Niching sideways means adding product variations or a related category when a niche has too few profitable products.
    • Do this only after return on ad spend is strong in the existing range.
  • After a product proves itself in a native-language store (a store in the buyers' own language), scale it across the market.
    • Keep creatives fresh. Creatives are ad images and videos.
    • They wear out over time (fatigue, meaning people stop responding). So copy and visuals need refreshing when sales drop.
  • Reducing product features can lower cost without hurting sales when customers do not value them.
    • For example, testing 30 designs per book instead of 40 keeps costs similar if buyers do not notice the difference.

Domestic saturation and expansion sequence

1 min
  • When year-on-year metrics (comparisons with the same period last year) show repeating revenue declines at similar percentages, your domestic market is saturating. Saturating means the market is nearly full.
  • Diversify in this order:
    1. First: product line extensions and new customer segments.
    2. Second: additional marketing channels.
    3. Third: international expansion.
  • Reserve international expansion for later-stage growth.
    • It requires VAT compliance (VAT is value added tax, and compliance means following its rules).
    • It also needs import-export coordination (moving goods across borders) and multi-region tax navigation (handling taxes in several regions).
    • These add significant operational complexity.
  • Expand in stages.
    • First succeed in the home market.
    • Then add neighbouring German-speaking markets such as Germany and Austria.
    • The long-term goal is a seven-figure brand, meaning one with one million dollars or more in yearly sales.

Multi-country setup

1 min
  • Use one store for North America (US and Canada).
    • Use a separate domain (web address) or store for the UK or other continents.
  • Procedure for multi-country expansion (2025):
    1. Create separate campaigns by country (a campaign is a set of ads) (Alpha US, Beta US, Alpha UK, Beta UK).
    2. Include the same products in each campaign pair.
    3. Use geo-targeting (showing ads only in chosen countries) so each campaign shows only in its target country.
    4. Separate all retargeting ads (ads aimed at people who already visited) by country (RT 30 days US, RT 30 days UK).
    5. This allows tracking spend, profitability and ROI (return on investment, meaning profit compared to money spent) per country and per product.
  • When a new product is launched, test it across the five largest target markets for about one week.
    • Then concentrate budget on the countries that produce the most sales.
    • In one reported case the US and Australia led. Scandinavian countries (Norway, Sweden, Denmark) were added after they also produced sales.
  • Facebook ads in a second language or market require a store in that language.
    • One store cannot cover all markets. So local-language stores can capture other European markets.
  • Build a localized supplier presence in each target region. Localized means adapted to the local language and market.
    • Have a visible team in the countries where you sell.
    • Every country is at a different stage and needs a localized approach. Examples are Northern Europe, the UK, Germany, Southern Europe, Israel and Thailand.
  • Order processing (handling and preparing orders) and customer contact depend on the country being sold to.
    • A process that works in one market may need adjusting in another.

Amazon FBA

1 min
  • FBA (Fulfilled by Amazon, where Amazon stores, packs and ships your products) scales without operational overhead (extra daily work).
    • Amazon handles fulfilment automatically, whether you get 10 or 800 sales per day.
    • You do not need to manage supplier emails, pack orders or arrange shipping.
    • So the model lets you focus on marketing and inventory planning (deciding how much stock to keep).
  • The Amazon Pro seller account (Amazon's paid selling plan) costs $40 per month.
    • Do not subscribe until the product is ready to list (ready to sell on Amazon).
    • Activate it only when you are prepared to begin inventory and marketing.
  • Maintain inventory levels carefully. Stockouts (running out of stock) cause lost rankings and sales momentum.
    • Use supplier lead time (the days or weeks from order to arrival) to forecast reorder points (the stock level at which you must order again).
    • For example, a 6-week lead time requires ordering when inventory reaches 6 weeks of expected sales.
  • Do not expand to other sales channels such as Amazon or eBay until the main store is consistently doing tens of thousands of dollars a month.
    • Expand only when forced by limits on the main channel.

Building an email list and product ecosystem from Amazon customers

1 min
  • Create a customer email list from all Amazon sales, using a gift card or bonus incentive (a reward for signing up).
    • Email this list to promote your subscription box (a box sent to customers on a schedule), new products or complementary offers (products that go well with yours).
  • Examples from the same model:
    • Parents buying baby products can be offered a discount on a zero-to-two-year-old subscription box.
    • Women can be offered a weight loss product discount code multiple times: immediately after purchase, at 6 months and at 12 months.
  • Design your FBA product ecosystem (the set of linked products and offers) to feed a subscription box business or affiliate revenue stream (income from partner sales).
  • Worked example: resistance bands (stretchy exercise bands) gift customers a $50 weight loss product gift card.
    • The card costs $4 in bulk.
    • If they redeem it for a $50-$100 purchase, the affiliate margin (what you keep after the gift card cost) is $46-$96.
    • If they buy the highest-tier $350 item, affiliate revenue (money earned from partner sales) is $346.
    • This creates additional profit per customer beyond the initial product sale.

International scaling from Amazon

1 min
  • Scale a successful Amazon product into international markets such as the UK, Japan and Germany.
    • Lower competition makes them easier to enter.
    • A translator can localize (adapt to the local language) the product pages.
  • Projection (a forecast of future results) for international expansion:
    • 30 sales per day in the UK plus 30 sales per day in Japan (10 sales per day of each of three sets).
    • This would produce roughly $500 profit per day, or about $15,000 extra net profit per month.

Private labelling, 3PL and bulk inventory

2 min
  • Hybrid model (a mix of two methods):
    • Test products via dropshipping with minimal upfront cost. Dropshipping means the supplier ships each order, and you keep no stock.
    • Once a product proves profitable, manufacture it in bulk (large batches) with a supplier, for example on Alibaba (a large online marketplace for factories).
    • Store inventory at a 3PL warehouse (a warehouse run by a shipping company) close to your target audience, for example in the United States.
    • Sell from stock while continuing to dropship other products to find new winners.
  • Stocking inventory at a 3PL warehouse gives better profit margins than dropshipping.
    • It also enables faster shipping.
    • Faster shipping increases customer loyalty and repeat purchases.
    • Meanwhile, keep dropshipping other products to find additional winners.
  • Case: a wood products seller tested wooden items (cups, tables, chairs) through dropshipping.
    • Once some products proved profitable, he made them in bulk through an Alibaba supplier.
    • He shipped stock to a 3PL warehouse and sold from stock.
    • Products held in inventory brought significantly higher sales and profit margins than dropshipped products.
    • He kept dropshipping other wooden products to find new bestsellers for future manufacturing.
  • Do not attempt private labelling (selling a product under your own brand) without first proving consistent sales.
    • Starting with 3PL or private labelling on untested products wastes capital (money you have invested).
  • If you run a multi-product store, gradually private label all products, not just one.
    • Suppliers often negotiate free or discounted private labelling on some products if the total order is large.
  • When private labelling, account for storage fees. These are what fulfilment centres (warehouses that pack and ship orders) charge to hold your stock.
    • Facilities may charge retainer fees (a fixed monthly fee), per-unit fees (a fee for each item) or no fee, depending on order volume.
    • A poor sales month can trigger unexpected storage charges.
  • When scaling from dropshipping to bulk inventory, maintain original shipping speeds.
    • Do not sacrifice delivery speed for lower per-unit costs.
    • Slower fulfilment (packing and shipping) causes customer complaints, chargebacks (customers getting money back through their bank) and trust erosion.
  • When a supplier requires a full inventory commitment for a new collection, offer a trial with a few weeks of drop shipping first.
    • Stock inventory only if sales prove the demand and the opportunity is large.
  • Pre-buying inventory from a supplier is only worth doing when you sell enough units that stock might run out.
    • Otherwise it locks up cash for a discount.

First local stock order benchmarks

1 min

A benchmark is a target number to measure against.

  • A lower benchmark for a first local stock order (stock kept in the buyer's country) is about 10 orders per day sustained for a month.
  • A higher benchmark is 50 to 100 orders per day for 10 consecutive days (one day after another) before bulk stocking.
  • Starting with a small stock of around 10 pieces keeps the risk limited.

Wholesale and retail

2 min

Wholesale means selling large amounts to businesses. Retail means selling to end customers.

  • When retailers (shops that sell to customers) start asking for sales or scan data (sales data from barcode scans), hire a senior manager for wholesale before the process becomes overloaded.
    • In the example, the wholesale hire used existing relationships to win a chain of about 160 stores.
    • That chain then reordered four additional products within a day.
  • Retail partnerships let you sell large quantities to one buyer at once.
    • Example: a chain buys tens of thousands of sets for its stores.
    • This raises total revenue (all sales money) and supports a higher revenue multiple (a number used to price a business from its yearly sales) when you sell the business. Selling the business is called an exit.
  • Use airports and specialty bookstores (shops that sell only certain kinds of books) as retail channels for small, light and portable products.
    • These suit buyers who travel or need last-minute gifts.
  • Large retailers choose suppliers on a quarterly buying cycle (new supplier decisions every three months).
    • If an existing supplier underperforms in a holiday quarter, a new supplier can take its place the next quarter.
  • Have stock, packaging and paperwork ready before the buying window opens. The buying window is the time when a retailer chooses new suppliers.
    • Missing it by one quarter pushes the entry back.
  • A retail chain may first run a test in its stores in one country.
    • It expands overseas only if early sales look decent.
    • Treat a domestic (home country) retail launch as a test that can unlock international distribution.
  • Once the store has settled, check whether the team can handle B2B sales (business-to-business, selling to companies).
    • Do this through a trade page (a page for business buyers) and special pricing.
    • B2B marketing through publications and trade ad space works best as a later scaling step, since conversion rates (the share of visitors who buy) are lower. (2023.)
  • Evaluate ClickBank (an online marketplace for selling products) as a sales channel, especially for supplements (health products such as vitamins).
    • The source cites a supplement company that reported growth from about $4 million to about $10 million per year within a week of joining.
    • Treat that figure as an unverified anecdote (one story, not proof), not a benchmark.

Brand building

1 min
  • The shift from short-lived dropshipping stores to brands means building a brand that lasts.
    • Test many products over time, then scale the winners with branding and support.
  • Physical presence helps buyers see and try the product.
    • A pop-up store (a temporary physical shop) was run in London one summer.
    • Pop-ups in the UK and abroad were planned. Wholesale stockists (shops that sell your products) created physical points of contact in cities.
  • A clothing brand that works on a cycle can release a new collection every two to three months.
    • Each collection has its own campaign and theme.
    • This keeps the brand fresh, even when some products are similar to earlier ones in different colourways (colour versions).
  • Fund clinical trials (scientific tests on people) for the main product when a patent (legal protection for an invention) exists.
    • For patented supplements, clinically proven products are rare.
    • The brand funded three trials and expected data about a year later.

Team and processes across several stores

1 min
  • Running two or more stores at once requires a team.
    • Order fulfilment (packing and shipping) and customer support need people.
    • One person's capacity fills up quickly.
    • Keeping the store count low and focusing on one or two stores is easier to manage alone.
  • Keep order processing and supplier relationships separate for each store.
    • Mixing them across stores quickly becomes messy.
    • Separating them protects each store's supplier contacts and workflow (the steps of daily work).
  • Creative, marketing, development and technical roles can be shared across several stores when the person has spare capacity (free time to work).
    • Capacity and available time decide how far a role can be shared.
  • When running several stores, reuse the processes and systems built in one store for the others.
    • This avoids redoing work.
    • Building them once and documenting them (writing them down) makes each new store faster to launch.
  • Each new store built on the knowledge of an earlier one is a repeatable process (the same steps every time).
    • The operator applies the same playbook (a set of proven steps) to new sites.
    • Reusing proven marketing and store setup work reduces the learning curve (the time to learn) for every new project.

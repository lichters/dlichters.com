Domain, business email and phone setup

Section 5.2 · 2 of 22 in this chapter · 28 min read

In short

  • Buy a domain (your web address) and check that it is free before you pay.
  • Sign up for Google Workspace (paid business email) on the $7 per month plan first, through the pricing page link.
  • Pick the annual plan only if you will keep the account for a full year. Otherwise pick the flexible plan.
  • Use one main address plus free aliases (extra addresses that go to the same inbox) instead of paying for extra users.
  • Prices change, so check the live pricing page before you sign up.
More key points
  • Use a business email on your own web address. Google Workspace with the Business Starter plan is the usual start.
  • Turn on two-step verification, so a new device needs a code sent to your phone.
  • For one business number, Quo's Starter plan at $15 per user per month is enough, and it includes a toll-free number in the USA and Canada.
  • Google Voice costs $10 per month but gives only local numbers.
  • Send a test email before you use the address with suppliers or customers.

Domain: Choosing and Buying the Domain

1 min
  • Use a ChatGPT thinking model (a mode where the AI thinks longer before it answers) with deep research turned on (a mode where the AI searches the live internet). Deep research checks which domains (web addresses) are actually free. Without it, most suggested.com domains turn out to be taken.
  • Namecheap is the domain registrar (the company that sells and manages domain names) used in this approach. It handles the purchase, domain privacy (hides your personal details in public records), and DNS records (the settings that tell the internet where your domain points). Any other domain provider can be used instead.
  • buy the domain through Shopify (the online store software) if you want everything linked from the start. It costs a few dollars more than an outside registrar such as Namecheap, which is cheaper.
  • If the domain still does not link after the DNS records are updated, take a screenshot of the DNS records together with the exact error message. Paste both into a ChatGPT thinking model to get troubleshooting help.

Google Workspace: Free Trial, Billing and Seats

2 min
  • Google Workspace starts with a free trial of at least 14 days.
  • Billing (adding a payment method) can be set up during the trial or after it. No charge is made before the trial ends. Google sends a notice before the trial expires, so you can leave billing until then.
  • Billing begins 14 days after signup on both the annual and flexible plans.
  • The annual plan is still billed monthly. The monthly charge stays at $7. It is not paid up front.
  • Users can be added or removed as needed. Each user is a paid mailbox, also called a seat or license.
  • If billing is never activated, the mailbox stops receiving mail when the trial ends. Existing messages cannot be retrieved afterward.
  • Billing setup asks for the billing address and a credit card on file (a card saved in the account).
  • Create a payment profile with the address and add a credit card on file. Click Agree and continue.
  • Each additional paid user license costs $7 per month.
  • Aliases are not paid users. They do not add to the monthly price.
  • A Google Workspace account includes up to 30 alternate email addresses (aliases) at no extra cost.
  • You still sign in only with the primary address (the main address of the account). Aliases are used to send and receive mail.
  • Do not click "buy more licenses" in the admin panel (the control page for the account) when you only need extra email addresses. Every added license costs $7 per month.
  • Aliases on the existing account cover multiple addresses without this cost. Add paid users only when an employee needs their own separate inbox.
  • When only the first account is needed during setup, click Cancel on the prompt to buy more licenses.
  • Creating extra user accounts is not needed during initial setup and can be skipped. Additional team members can be added later from the admin console (the control panel for the account).

Google Workspace: Before You Sign Up

1 min
  • Sign up for the $7 per month plan first, before creating any other Google Workspace account for the same domain.
  • If a Google Workspace account was created for the domain before you signed up through the $7 per month link, cancel that account and sign up again with the $7 plan.
  • Otherwise, Google will report that the domain is already in use.
  • Create the business email address before signing up for Quo (a business phone app). Quo asks for a business email and uses it to create the account.
  • The same address is then used to log in to the app.
  • Google changes the signup steps often. Follow the current signup screens, not older guides.
  • Existing Google Workspace business email accounts keep working. They do not need to be changed after such an update.
  • Signing up with a domain you already own leaves that domain and any existing website unchanged.
  • Only the mail routing of the domain (where its email is delivered) is connected to Google.

Google Workspace: Signup Procedures

5 min

Signup via the pricing link:

  1. Open the Google Workspace pricing page through the course link, not the general signup page.
  2. Under Business Starter ($7 per month), click Start a trial.
  3. Enter the business name, choose 1 for number of employees, and select the region where the business operates. Then click Next.
  4. Enter the contact's first and last name and a personal email address as a backup login. Then click Next.
  5. Answer yes to having a domain name. Enter the domain without https:// or www (for example yourbrand.com). Confirm it and click Next.
  6. Set the sign-in address to sales@yourdomain.com, choose a password, and click Agree and continue.
  7. Select the annual plan (or flexible if you want no commitment). Then click Try free for 14 days.
  8. Create a payment profile by entering the address and adding a credit card on file. Then click Agree and continue.
  9. Click Get started.
  10. At the domain host question (the company where your domain is managed), keep Shopify if the domain was bought through Shopify. Then click Continue.

Signup for a business email domain (Source A, shorter version):

  1. Open the pricing page through the link. Choose Business Starter ($7 per user per month) and click Start a trial.
  2. Enter the business name, the number of employees (select just you for a one-person account), and the region where the business operates. Then click Next.
  3. Enter the name of the person setting up the account and a personal email address as a backup for account recovery. Then click Next.
  4. When asked whether the business has a domain name, answer yes. Enter the domain without https:// or www (for example domain.com). Confirm it and click Next.
  5. Set the primary sign-in address to sales@yourdomain.com, choose a password, and click Agree and continue.
  6. Select the Annual plan ($7 per month, 12-month commitment) or the Flexible plan ($8.40 per month). Then click Try free for 14 days.
  7. Create a payment profile with the billing address and add a credit card on file. Then click Agree and continue.
  8. Click Get started to begin the domain verification steps (the checks that prove you own the domain).

Signup with the domain question and admin steps:

  1. Open workspace.google.com (or search for Google Workspace) and click Start free trial.
  2. Enter the business name as you want it to appear. Spaces are allowed.
  3. Select 'Just you' as the number of employees if you work alone.
  4. Select the country where the business will operate, not necessarily where you live. The country cannot be changed after signup. A warning that your IP address (the number that identifies your internet connection) points to another country can be ignored if the selected country is correct.
  5. Enter your own first and last name. You become the account admin (the person in charge of the account).
  6. Enter a current personal email address. Google sends the welcome message there and uses it for account recovery.
  7. When asked whether the business has a domain, choose 'I have a domain' and click Next.
  8. Type the domain name without https:// or slashes (for example example.com). Confirm it is the domain to use, and click Next.
  9. Answer the optional Google marketing questions as you prefer, and continue.
  10. Choose the username prefix (support, sales, hello or info). The full address becomes prefix@yourdomain.
  11. Create a password of at least 8 characters and store it somewhere secure. Complete the robot check (a test that proves you are a person) and click Agree and continue.
  12. Click the blue button to continue into setup. Then sign in again with the new address and password.
  13. If Google asks you to verify the account, enter your country and phone number. Choose Text message (SMS) or Automated voice call, click Get code, enter the code, and click Verify.
  14. Accept the terms of service and privacy policy.
  15. In the Google Admin console (admin.google.com, the control panel for the account), click ACTIVATE under Activate Gmail. This step can take up to 60 minutes.
  16. Click 'Sign in to activate'. Google detects GoDaddy (a domain host) as your domain host. Sign in to GoDaddy if asked, then click Connect.
  17. Wait while Google verifies the domain and updates the mail settings. Near the end, the screen shows 'Less than a minute remaining'.
  18. If you see 'There was a problem updating settings in GoDaddy.com', click 'Sign in to activate' again. If it fails repeatedly, click 'Switch verification method' and try another method.
  19. When Gmail is activated, skip 'Create new users' for now.
  20. Either click 'Set up billing' now or later. Google emails you before the 14-day trial ends. Billing requires your name, address and payment method details.
  21. Click 'Send test email' to confirm that mail works.
  22. Log in to gmail.com with the new business address to access the inbox.

Signup for a Singapore-based store:

  1. Search for Google Workspace and open the sign-up page with the free trial.
  2. Start the free trial and choose the Business Starter plan.
  3. Enter the business name (the store or brand name) and the domain name.
  4. Enter the number of employees (Just you for a solo operator) and the region (Singapore in the example).
  5. Enter the first and last name of the admin. The person who creates the account becomes the Google Workspace account admin.
  6. Finish creating the account. Then use the business email in the Shopify store contact details.

Signup field notes:

  • The sign-up asks for the number of employees. The options are Just you, 2-9, 10-99, 100-299 and 300+. A solo store owner picks Just you. This answer does not block the setup of a single business email address.
  • Entering https:// or www in the domain field creates a wrong domain setup. Enter only the bare domain, such as domain.com.
  • The domain step appears as 'Does your business have a domain?'. Select 'I have a domain' if you already own a domain.
  • Select the buy-a-domain or free page-domain options only if you have no domain yet.

Google Workspace: Connecting the Domain's Mail (Activation)

2 min

Activation means connecting your domain's email to Gmail (Google's email service).

  • Activation routes the domain's email to Gmail. In the Google Admin console, go to Getting started, then click ACTIVATE under 'Activate Gmail for [yourdomain]'.
  • The step is estimated at 5 minutes, with a possible wait of up to 60 minutes.
  • If your domain is registered with GoDaddy, Google detects it automatically during Gmail activation and offers an automated connection.
  • For a larger domain provider, the automated option usually works too.
  • Smaller providers may not be detected. They need an alternative verification method.
  • The connection with GoDaddy is confirmed automatically. Google updates the domain's mail settings and then verifies they are in place.
  • The check can take a few minutes, so wait on the screen rather than closing it.
  • The error 'There was a problem updating settings in GoDaddy.com' is common. It usually means the two systems did not sync.
  • It is not a sign of a setup mistake.
  • Retry 'Sign in to activate' before changing the verification method.
  • Google may ask for phone verification when it detects unusual activity on a new account.
  • The code can be sent by text message (SMS) or automated voice call.
  • The phone number is used only for security.
  • The MX record (the setting that tells the internet where a domain's mail is delivered) for your domain should point to Google: smtp.google.com with priority 1 (the first choice to try).
  • Once that is set, all incoming mail for the domain is delivered to Gmail.
  • Any older mailbox or forwarding setup for the same domain stops receiving new mail.
  • Check what is still in use before switching the record.
  • Send a test email in the Google Admin console after verification.
  • This confirms that the domain's mail routes correctly. Do this before you use the address with suppliers and customers.

Post-activation checklist (Google Admin console, Getting started page, in this order):

  1. Set up billing (5 min)
  2. Send test email (after Gmail is activated and the domain is verified)
  3. Create new users (5 min)
  4. Try out Gmail and other apps (1 min)
  5. Set up your mobile devices (10 min)
  6. Migrate your email and documents, importing existing email, contacts and data (1 hr)
  7. Enhance your mail security, adding protection against spam (junk email) and phishing (fake emails that try to steal logins) (5 min)

Email Addresses and Aliases

4 min
  • Use professional addresses on your own store domain instead of a personal inbox. Use them when contacting suppliers, and keep using them for customers.
  • A simple setup uses three addresses on one account: sales@, returns@, and firstname@ on the store domain.
  • An alias is an extra address that delivers to one inbox.
  • You select which address to send from for each message. You do not need to monitor separate accounts.
  • Aliases cannot be used to sign in to Google Workspace.
  • Always sign in with the primary address (for example sales@yourdomain.com) and its password.
  • Aliases receive and send mail through that primary account.
  • Use the same Google Workspace email account for Google Search Console (a free tool showing how your site appears in search), Google Analytics (a tool that counts site visitors), Google Merchant Center (a tool that holds your product list for Google shopping), and Google Ads (paid advertising).
  • This lets all Google tools connect and share data cleanly.

Add alias addresses (Source A, first version):

  1. In the Google Workspace admin panel, click the blue underlined name of the sales@ user.
  2. In the left navigation, click Add alternate emails.
  3. Enter returns as the first alias. It becomes returns@yourdomain.com.
  4. Enter a personal alias using your first name. Adding your last name is also recommended, for example firstname@yourdomain.com.
  5. Click Save and check that all addresses are listed as alternate emails under the main account.

Add alias addresses (Source A, second version):

  1. After the welcome screen for the new account, click I understand. Then click Cancel on the prompt asking to buy more licenses.
  2. Open the Google Workspace admin panel and click the name of the primary account.
  3. In the left navigation, click Add alternate emails.
  4. Add the alternate emails returns and firstname (for example, the first name of the operator), each created on the store domain. Include the last name in the alias name if it is used for supplier contact.
  5. Check that the list shows sales@, returns@, and firstname@ on the store domain. Then click Save.

Add alias addresses (Source A, older 2020 version, same steps):

  1. Open Google Workspace Admin.
  2. Click your primary user (sales@yourdomain.com).
  3. Select Add alternate emails.
  4. Add support@yourdomain.com or returns@yourdomain.com.
  5. Add yourfirstname@yourdomain.com.
  6. Click Save changes.
  7. Open Gmail and log in as sales@yourdomain.com.
  8. Click the gear icon (the settings button) and select See all settings.
  9. Go to the Accounts tab.
  10. Edit the display name to your store name.
  11. Add additional "Send mail as" addresses for support or returns email.
  12. Add your personal email as another "Send mail as" option.
  13. Set replies to send from the same address the original email was sent to.

Set up sending from the aliases in Gmail (Source A, first version):

"Send mail as" is the Gmail setting that lets you send from an alias.

  1. Click the gear icon in Gmail, then click See all settings.
  2. Open the Accounts tab. Under Send mail as, click Edit next to the sales address.
  3. Change the name from the generic account name to the brand name (for example, the store name). Then click Save changes.
  4. Click Add another email address, enter the name and the alias returns@yourdomain.com, then click Next step.
  5. Under When replying to a message, change the default from "Always reply from the default address" to "Reply from the same address the message was sent to".
  6. Repeat steps 4 and 5 for the personal alias firstname@yourdomain.com.
  7. Click Compose (the new email button), then choose the From address (sales, returns or personal) to send from it.

Set up send-as aliases in Gmail (Source A, second version):

  1. In Gmail, click the gear icon, then click See all settings.
  2. Open the Accounts tab.
  3. Under Send mail as, click edit on the primary address. Change the name to the store name (the name customers should see). Then click Save changes.
  4. Click Add another email address. Enter the name (for example, Returns - Store Name) and the returns@ address. Then click Next step.
  5. In the settings for returns@, set When replying to a message to "Reply from the same address the message was sent to". Then click Next step.
  6. Add the firstname@ address with the first and last name. Then click Next step.
  7. Open Compose and select the From address (sales@, returns@, or firstname@) before sending.
  8. Leave "Check mail from other accounts" empty. This is the Gmail feature that pulls mail in from other inboxes. The aliases were already added in Google Workspace, so you do not need it.

Reply and sending mistakes:

  • Gmail's default is to reply from the primary sales address. This happens even when a message arrived at the returns or personal alias.
  • Change the reply setting to reply from the same address the message was sent to. Then customers see a consistent sender.
  • Do not add anything under Check mail from other accounts. The aliases already receive mail through Google Workspace.

Use the account on a phone:

  • To use all addresses on a phone, add the account to the phone's email app. Use the primary sales@yourdomain.com address and its password.
  • Once added, the app can send and receive from all aliases on the account. No separate setup is needed for each alias on the phone.
  • In any email app on the phone, add an account using the primary address (sales@yourdomain.com) and its password.
  • Send and receive mail from all three addresses, because the aliases are linked to the same account.
  • After activation, the business address can be used at gmail.com.
  • It also works in desktop and mobile mail apps such as Apple Mail or Outlook, by adding the Gmail account to the app.
  • This lets you send and receive on a phone or computer.

Email Tools: Filters and Scheduling

1 min
  • Gmail filters (automatic sorting rules) can apply labels (tags) and sort incoming emails automatically. The assistant can then act on messages without you sorting them by hand. Combine these rules with colour-coded priority labels so urgent messages show up first.
  • Boomerang for Gmail (an add-on for your web browser) can schedule emails to send later. It can also send you a reminder if a recipient has not replied within a set number of days. Use the reminder to track supplier actions that would otherwise be forgotten.
  • Boomerang's scheduled sending is optional for business email. In the store described, suppliers and customers did not object to emails sent late at night or early in the morning. The reminder feature is the more useful part for tracking supplier follow-ups.
  • With Boomerang for Gmail, you can write emails at night and schedule delivery for a set time in the recipient's time zone (their local clock), such as 9 a.m. US time. This shows suppliers that your email arrives during their business hours, for example by scheduling for 9:03 a.m.

Security and Gemini in Google Workspace

1 min
  • Enable two-step verification (a second check, such as a code, on top of the password) for all Google Workspace users. It sends a code by text message when someone logs in on a new device. This protects the account from anyone who obtains the password.
  • The other optional items during setup, such as the Gemini sidebar and workspace app downloads, can be skipped or enabled later.
  • The Gemini AI sidebar in Gmail (Gemini is Google's AI helper) can summarize emails, draft emails and auto replies, and find information in the inbox. It is included with the Google Workspace account and can be minimized or closed. It is optional and not required for the email setup.
  • Gemini in Gmail is included in the Starter plan (Singapore list).

Google Voice: Setup and Use

1 min
  • Google Voice is added to an existing Google Workspace account that was set up earlier with the business email. It costs $10 per month, is managed in the Google Voice app on phone and computer, and offers only local numbers chosen by area code. Toll-free numbers are not offered.
  • Google Voice works well in the US but not while traveling abroad.
  • Google Voice serves as the business phone line, answered by the VA (virtual assistant, a hired helper who works remotely) during business hours. Other inbound messages go into the help desk (the support inbox).
  • On Google Voice, a call recording starts only after the agent (the person answering) presses a key, and a recording notice plays to the caller. Staff often skip this step, so do not rely on Google Voice for consistent call recording.
  • Check the consent rules (laws on whether callers must agree to being recorded) for recording calls in the countries where the store and its callers are located.

Google Voice setup for a US business number:

  3. When prompted, select the location and area code you want. The number issued is a local number.
  4. Install the Google Voice app on your phone and computer to take inbound calls and place outbound calls.

Mighty Call

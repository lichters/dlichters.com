Playbook › 5. Apps and tools

Product feeds, Google merchant center and shopping feed apps

Section 5.16 · 16 of 22 in this chapter · 38 min read

In short

  • Before you connect to Google, check your store pages and remove all dummy text. Gemini can help find the problems.
  • Use a separate feed app to send your products to Google. Paid apps usually cost a few dollars a month.
  • Check the app's default settings before the first send. Missing product details are filled in from them.
  • Check the developer name before you install an app. Many apps look almost the same.
  • Google needs your product list (the feed) to update itself. An app that sends it about once a day is the safe choice.
More key points
  • Do not rely on manual Excel exports. A price that is not updated in the feed causes mismatches.
  • Some store fields do not update on their own. Re-sync them by hand, check the app's values first, and keep the window open.
  • Google Merchant Center holds your product list. Refresh its error list before you act on it, because old errors may be gone.
  • Google's review of a new product usually takes 1 to 3 days.

Before Connecting Anything: Store Readiness and Policy Checks

2 min

  • Google Gemini is Google's AI assistant. It can check your store and its policy pages before you send them to Google Merchant Center (Google's product list for shopping ads) and Google Ads (Google's ad tool).

  • Policy pages are the pages that state your rules for returns, shipping, privacy and terms.

  • Gemini follows Google's own requirements.

  • Gemini can flag placeholders (dummy text left in by mistake), missing information and unclear policy wording.

  • Google's crawlers (bots that read your website) would catch the same problems.

  • Use Gemini, not ChatGPT, to check a Shopify store (the online store software you build on) against Google Ads and Merchant Center policies. [L808.026]

  • Give Gemini the store's home page URL (the web address). [L808.026]

  • Ask it to check every policy page. [L808.026]

  • Ask whether the store is fully compliant, meaning it meets all the rules. [L808.026]

  • An AI assistant such as Gemini can compare your website's shipping and return policy text with the answers you entered in Merchant Center. [L729.017]

  • Use this as a cross-check, which means a second check. [L729.017]

  • Then verify the match yourself. [L729.017]

Policy-by-policy check with Gemini:

  1. Start with the Returns/Refunds policy and paste the full text into Gemini.
  2. Apply Gemini's recommended changes in Shopify.
  3. Repeat the same process for the Shipping, Privacy, Terms and Contact pages.
  4. After all updates, ask Gemini to re-check and confirm that the pages are clean.

Prompt for the policy review:

Hi, I run YOURDOMAIN.com and am getting ready to send the website to Google Merchant Center and Google Ads for review. Please go through the site and the policy pages I share and point out anything that breaks the rules or looks doubtful. For each issue, state what it is and what update you recommend. Pay particular attention to stand-in text, text in square brackets used as filler, made-up or placeholder contact information (such as dummy phone numbers), vague return or delivery terms, missing or unclear shipping charge details, and absent quick-glance summary information. Where you can, supply the exact replacement wording. Once I have made the changes, I'll paste the revised text back to you, so please tell me whether the page is then fully compliant.

Compliance checklist, in this order, before setting up any Google accounts:

  1. Remove every sample product under Shopify Admin > Products. Shopify Admin is the back office of your store.
  2. Click through every header, homepage, collection and footer link. A collection is a group of products. Repair any 404 errors, which are broken links.
  3. Take out placeholder pictures and incomplete sections so the storefront looks finished.
  4. Go through all Shopify policy pages under Settings > Policies and strip out any placeholder text.
  5. Put real email, phone and opening hours into the contact details on the policy pages.
  6. Pass each policy page through Google Gemini and implement every fix it recommends.
  7. Revise the Contact Us page so it shows phone, email and hours in addition to the form.
  8. Recheck with Gemini until it reports everything as compliant. Only then create the Google accounts.

Choosing a Feed App

5 min

Principles

  • A product feed is the data file or stream that carries your product details to Google. A feed app is the tool that sends it for you. [L722.010]
  • Send product feeds to Google Merchant Center with a separate, dedicated app. [L722.010]
  • A dedicated app gives more control than the Merchant Center section of the Google & YouTube app. [L722.010]
  • A paid feed app gives more control over product data than a free one.
  • Better product data generally leads to better results in Google Shopping (product listings in Google search).
  • The monthly fee is usually worth it as sales grow.
  • Feed management apps for Merchant Center typically cost a few dollars per month. [L732.014]
  • The fee is considered worth it for reliable, automatically updated feeds. [L732.014]
  • Software prices for product feed tools can often be negotiated. Ask the vendor before you accept the list price, which is the posted price. [L454.044]
  • Compare tools on pricing model and features, not only on reputation. [L454.044, L454.026]
  • Feed apps for Google, Bing and Facebook are sold separately. [L762.010]
  • Each platform has slightly different feed requirements. [L762.010]
  • A feed management app that talks to Merchant Center can find more errors than Google's own diagnostics (error reports) alone. [L732.022]
  • Such an app tells you exactly which field to fix. [L732.022]
  • Using such an app speeds up error correction. [L732.022]
  • It also improves product visibility in Google Shopping. [L732.022]

Native Shopify Google channel versus dedicated app (sources differ)

  • The native Shopify Google channel is Shopify's built-in link to Google. A dedicated app is a separate tool. [L272.016]
  • Google is taking over the native channel. [L272.016]
  • a feed app provides better control. [L272.016]
  • Shopify's native Google channel misses some useful feed data points.
  • it is harder to edit when feed changes are needed.
  • switch to a dedicated feed app instead.
  • The free Google Shopping app published by Shopify has few customization options.
  • It has a 3.6-star rating in the Shopify App Store.
  • It only makes sense for a quick start.
  • It is not suited to growing a Google Shopping business.
  • The free Google Merchant Center account connects directly to Shopify through the Google sales channel.
  • The setup process is similar to the Facebook sales channel.
  • Use the feed app's submission path, not the Google sales channel's Merchant Center listing option. [L835.133]
  • Avoid the old native Shopify Google feed plugin. It had poor reviews. [L732.006]
  • It only worked for US currency. [L732.006]
  • It failed for customers in Canada. [L732.006]
  • It submitted the highest shipping cost instead of the lowest. [L732.006]
  • It was poorly maintained. [L732.006]

Feed for Google Shopping (Simprosys): Setup

7 min

What it does

  • Feeds for Google Shopping formats your Shopify product data to Google Merchant Center's required format. [L731.020]
  • It then sends the data automatically. [L731.020]
  • Manual spreadsheet feeds are error-prone and time-consuming. That is why an app is used instead. [L731.020]
  • Feed for Google Shopping connects the Shopify product feed to Google Merchant Center via the API (a direct data link between two programs). [L962.004]
  • It updates stock status, price, title, description, SKU (your own product code), photos and promotions. [L962.004]
  • It does this within about 20 minutes. [L962.004]
  • It is preferred over the Google channel app. ** [L962.004]
  • Set to auto update, changes in Shopify flow through the content API (Google's product data entry point). [L247.082]
  • They reach Merchant Center and Google Ads in near real time, meaning almost instantly. ** [L247.082]
  • It offers more control than the built-in Google channel. ** [L801.109]
  • It handles the Google product feed and provides conversion tracking (measuring sales that came from ads). [L852.074]
  • It is the recommended tool for connecting Shopify to Google. ** [L852.074]
  • It publishes products to Google Merchant Center when the product availability box is checked. [L664.075]
  • Use one Google Shopping app. [L664.075]
  • Avoid mixing duplicates. ** [L664.075]
  • Feed for Google Shopping is a paid app of about 5 USD per month, with a free 21-day trial.
  • It connects directly to Google Merchant Center to send the product feed automatically. **
  • It is the recommended tool for managing your product feed to Google. ** [L272.016]
  • It is more reliable and customizable than Shopify's native Google Shopping channel. [L272.016]

Installing

Install steps [L731.021]:

  1. Open the Shopify App Store from the Apps section of the Shopify menu.
  2. Search for "feeds for google shopping".
  3. Select Feeds For Google Shopping by Simprosys InfoMedia.
  4. Click Install app.

Install step from 2026:

  1. Install Feed for Google Shopping from the Shopify App Store. Search for the name. The app is not made by Shopify. Then open it under Apps.

Connecting to Merchant Center (Simprosys / Source B) [L731.022]

  1. Log in with the Google account used to create the Merchant Center account.
  2. Allow the app access to the account.
  3. Select the Merchant Center ID (your account number). Check that it matches the ID shown in the top right corner.
  4. Confirm the account.
  5. In Merchant Center, go to Growth > Manage programs. Click Get started under Shopping ads. Programs are optional Google features you switch on.
  6. Return to the app and confirm that programs have been enabled.

Connecting to Merchant Center (2026)

  1. Install Feed for Google Shopping from the Shopify App Store. Search for the name. The app is not made by Shopify. Then open it under Apps.
  2. Select the Google Merchant Center account to link. Use the same email address for the app as for the Merchant Center account. That address links everything.
  3. Click confirm your account.
  4. Read the notice inside the app about required Merchant Center programs. In Google Merchant Center, enable every program you plan to use. An example is dynamic remarketing (ads that show visitors the products they viewed).
  5. Back in the app, confirm that the programs are enabled. The app then verifies the stored data.
  6. Check that the connection shows your primary domain (your main web address) and your merchant ID (your Merchant Center account number).
  7. Open Feed settings to configure the feed.

Data Fields, Identifiers and Categories

3 min

Fields sent to Merchant Center

  • A Shopify product feed sends these fields to Google Merchant Center: product title, description, price, image, brand, MPN (SKU, your product code), GTIN barcode (the product's barcode number), unique product ID, variant data and product type.
  • Most of these fields come directly from the Shopify product record (the product's saved details).
  • So complete them when the product is created.
  • Complete product details so Google can match your products with search queries (what people type into Google). ** [L834.056]
  • Matching lets Google generate impressions (views of your product in results). ** [L834.056]
  • A product feed app should fill in as many columns as possible. This helps Google match products to searches. ** [L665.050]
  • Shopping feed identifiers are set in the Google Shopping feed app. The settings cover brand, MPN, SKU and GTIN handling. (2023) [L855.112]
  • Feed tools flag issues that Google Merchant Center does not. [L454.029]
  • Examples are HTML in product descriptions, or special characters like the ampersand (&). These can export as broken characters. [L454.029]
  • Use a feed grade check (an automatic quality score for your feed) to catch these problems automatically. [L454.029]

Editing data

  • Manage Products > select product > EDIT PRODUCT changes details in the Shopify admin.
  • SYNC FROM SHOPIFY pulls Shopify changes into the feed.
  • Fields such as title, description, product type and vendor (brand) come from Shopify.
  • Feed for Google Shopping lets you modify the product data sent to Google and Facebook. ** [L963.048]
  • Use it to send only part of a long description. [L963.048]
  • Or use it to adjust fields instead of editing product pages. [L963.048]
  • Feed apps let you change product data before sending it to Google and Facebook. [L510.031]

Product IDs and offer IDs

  • An offer ID is the unique code Merchant Center gives each product version.
  • It follows the pattern shopify_[country code][Shopify product ID][variant ID].
  • Example: shopify_DE_1001_2002.
  • Use this ID to find the exact variant when checking its status or diagnosing a suspension.

Google Product Category

  • Google Product Category values in the feed must be the full Google taxonomy path. A taxonomy is Google's official category list. [L747.024]
  • Put " > " between each level. Example: Health & Beauty > Personal Care > Hair Care. [L747.024]
  • A category that is only the last word does not match the Google category list. [L747.024]
  • A path from a different taxonomy version does not match either. [L747.024]
  • For the Facebook product feed, the Google product category uses the same taxonomy as Google Merchant Center. **
  • Search for the Google Merchant Center product category list.
  • Choose the category that best fits your product.

Currency and tax

  • The product feed is submitted in the same currency as the Shopify store's default currency. [L402.015, L747.018]
  • To change the feed currency, change the currency in the Shopify store settings. [L402.015, L747.018]
  • Tax is set in Quick Sync Settings. [L731.024]
  • Choose "Yes, prices include tax" if the shown prices include tax. [L731.024]

Images

  • If a Facebook feed rejects a product for exceeding character limits, shorten what is submitted through the feed app. ** [L963.049]
  • Do not delete the content from the product page to make it fit. [L963.049]
  • When a product feed app pulls the wrong image, check its settings for image selection.
  • Many feed apps let you choose which image to submit.
  • In Smart SEO (a Shopify app), enable the additional images option.
  • Then images from the Shopify media section are submitted to the Google feed.
  • For products without variants, this is enough.
  • For products with variants, set the variant name or SKU as the alt text (the hidden description of an image) of each variant image in Shopify.
  • This makes each image map to the correct variant in the feed.

Title and description preference

  • When syncing the Google Shopping feed app, choose the search engine optimized title and search engine optimized description options. **
  • Choose these rather than the standard product title and product description.
  • The difference was not seen as decisive, but the SEO options are used.

Reviews

  • Judge.me is a review app used to pull reviews into Google Shopping star ratings. [L274.028]
  • A minimum of 50 store reviews is needed. [L274.028]
  • It is a free app integration with Shopify. (2023) [L274.028]

Meta fields that do not sync on their own

1 min

Meta fields are extra hidden details on each product, such as the SEO title (the page title search engines show) and the SEO description (the short summary search engines show).

  • Shopify does not notify the app when the SEO title, SEO description, custom labels (your own tags for grouping products), age group, gender or similar fields change. You must re-sync them (send them again) manually in the app. ** [L747.017]
  • Re-syncing meta fields overwrites any meta field changes made inside the app, except custom titles and custom descriptions. Check the app-side values before running the re-sync, because they are replaced. ** [L402.011]
  • Keep the browser window open while the app is syncing meta fields. Closing it can leave the sync incomplete. Keep the window open until the app shows that syncing has finished. ** [L402.012]
  • The re-sync pop-up offers these fields as checkboxes (small boxes you tick), with a Select All option: Age Group, Custom Labels, Google Product Category, Pattern, SEO Description, Size, Size Type, Color, Gender, Material, Product Condition, SEO Title, Size System, Unit Pricing Measure and Unit Pricing Base Measure. Tick Custom Labels for custom label changes. Tick the other fields only if they were also changed in the CSV. ** [L402.013]

Merchant Center: Errors, Reviews and Support

1 min

Google Shopping is the product-picture area of Google search, where people buy directly from listed products.

Account basics

Custom labels are your own tags for grouping products. Google Shopping has five slots for them.

Feed Optimization for CTR and Competitor Monitoring

1 min

CTR (click-through rate) is how often people click after seeing your product. Optimizing means improving the product data to get more clicks.

Optimization process [L510.032]

1 min
  1. Take product data from Shopify.
  2. Modify the fields that influence CTR, such as images, titles, descriptions and review data.
  3. Send the optimized feed to Google Shopping.
  4. Send the same feed to Facebook for dynamic product ads.
  • A product feed tool should be used to optimize product titles, which increases traffic and makes data analysis and optimization faster. Feeds can also be distributed to second and third tier shopping sites to improve return on ad spend (how much you earn for each amount spent on ads).
  • Feed tools can pool data from Google Ads and Bing Ads to show a combined CTR. [L510.031]
  • Feed tools can scan competitor product pages (other stores' pages) and search results to detect MAP pricing violations (MAP: the lowest price a seller may advertise) and alert you. They can also help manage product ratings and seller ratings. The manual alternative is to have an assistant check for violations. [L454.032]
  • Competitor pricing alerts from feed tools notify the seller about possible MAP (minimum advertised price) violations. This helps monitor pricing without manual checks.

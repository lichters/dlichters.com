Keyword and SEO research tools

Section 5.6 · 6 of 22 in this chapter · 39 min read

In short

  • Google Search Console and free keyword tools are enough at the start.
  • For an active SEO plan you need at least one paid SEO tool.
  • Ubersuggest is the cheapest paid choice, at about $10 to $15 per month.
  • Free tools show very little data on links and rival sites. Paid tools show far more.
  • Before every export, change the country setting to your target market, because search numbers differ by country.
More key points
  • Hunter.io: set 10 emails per domain, never 100. Every found email uses a credit (a paid unit).
  • Mailshake: sign up for the Basic plan and start at 20 emails per day.
  • Never send cold emails (to people who did not ask) from your main email tool.
  • Copy the blank template for each project. Never type into the original.
  • Smart SEO: start on the Free plan. Never run Tiny SEO next to it.
  • Schema markup is hidden code that helps Google understand a page. Install a schema app instead of hand-coding it or paying someone to do it.
  • Noindex tells Google to hide a page. Use a Shopify app for it, because one small code mistake can hide your whole site.
  • Ubersuggest, a keyword tool, needs an account for good data. The price was about USD 7 per month in 2021-22.
  • Choose the country before you search. Search numbers change from country to country.

Column letters (A, B, C ...) refer to the research worksheet from section 2.3.

Choosing Tools by Budget

4 min
  • Google Search Console is free and enough for starting out. It is a free Google tool that shows how your site appears in Google search. Use it before you pay for AH Refs or SEM Rush, which are paid SEO tools. SEO means search engine optimization: making your pages rank higher in search results.
  • Free keyword research tools are enough for basic keyword research. Paid versions are not needed for this. A keyword is a word or phrase that people type into a search engine.
  • At least one paid third-party SEO tool is required for an active SEO strategy. A third-party tool is made by another company, not by Google.
  • You do not need to use all of the paid tools.
  • Each tool has a different price. Return on investment (ROI, the money you earn back compared with what you spend) is usually easy to reach if you use the tool regularly.
  • The choice of a paid SEO tool depends on your budget and your preference. But at least one such tool is needed.
  • Premium tools offer more data depth than budget tools. Data depth means how much detail the data has.
  • Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools show very limited data on links and search terms.
  • Third-party SEO tools show far more data on links, search terms and competitor sites. Competitors are rival businesses.
  • Paid SEO and outreach tools are worth their cost. Outreach means contacting other website owners, often to ask for links.
  • Automating research and email collection can save hundreds of hours of manual work, if you use the method regularly.
  • Use free or cheaper options at first. Add paid tiers (higher price levels) once the workload justifies them.
  • Free starting source: if you have run campaigns for some time, your own Google Ads search term data already shows search volume for terms in your niche. Google Ads is Google's paid advertising system. Search volume is how many times a term is searched per month.
  • Google Shopping campaigns are product ads in Google. Their search term data can be used as a free source.
  • Outsourcing keyword research to a freelancer on Fiverr is a cheap alternative to doing it yourself. Fiverr is a marketplace for freelance workers.
  • Give the freelancer your root terms. A root term is the main starting word for your research, such as "surfboards".
  • The freelancer pulls the keyword data and sends it back. You then group the terms and allocate them to pages.
  • Budget tier: Ubersuggest is the budget option among third-party SEO tools.
  • A paid Ubersuggest plan costs roughly $10 to $15 per month. This is the price at the time it was written.
  • Ubersuggest has less data depth than premium tools. But it is enough for many basic tasks.
  • Ubersuggest price points differ by source:
    • About $10 per month.
    • Roughly $10 to $15 per month.
    • About $7 US dollars per month, or about 10 Australian dollars per month.
  • The free Ubersuggest account gives very little usable data. A paid plan is recommended, even on a tight budget.
  • Ahrefs is a premium third-party SEO tool at around $100 USD per month. This is the price at the time it was written.
  • Ahrefs is essential for link building and content research. Link building means getting other sites to link to yours. A backlink is one such link.
  • Ahrefs is used heavily when running an active SEO strategy.
  • Ahrefs, necessity, conflicting views:
    • Ahrefs is considered the best option for keyword research. But it is not strictly necessary.
    • Ubersuggest is a lower-cost alternative. Its reports cover the same information.
    • Ahrefs, Moz and SEMrush are expensive paid tools.
    • They are more accurate and show more data points per term. A data point is one measured fact, such as volume or difficulty.
    • They are not necessary for content keyword research. Content keyword research means finding terms for blog articles.
  • SEMrush is a premium alternative to Ahrefs at a similar price.
  • SEMrush's advantage is paid search features, such as ad research. Ahrefs did not offer these at that time.
  • Raven Tools is a suite (a bundle of tools) that costs somewhat less than Ahrefs and SEMrush.
  • Raven Tools' SEO audit tool analyzes a website. An audit is a check. It lists areas that can be improved.
  • Screaming Frog is an older, thorough SEO tool. It runs as a paid desktop application, which means a program installed on your computer, not online.
  • Screaming Frog can handle most SEO tasks. But analyzing a very large website can slow down the computer and take a long time.
  • For large SEO projects, use an online tool instead of a desktop application.
  • A desktop crawl of a very large website can strain the computer and run for a long time. A crawl means a program reading every page of a site.
  • Most SEO research tools provide the same core reports: keyword, competitor and backlink reports.
  • Domain authority and keyword difficulty scores can differ slightly between tools. Domain authority is a score for how strong a website is. Keyword difficulty is a score for how hard it is to rank for a term.
  • The results should still be usable for the method.
  • Moz domain analysis: the free version of Moz.com's tool shows the domain authority of any website.
  • It allows 3 reports per day. It needs no sign-up or payment.

Free Keyword Research Tools

1 min

Google Keyword Planner

1 min
  • Google Keyword Planner is free for anyone with a Google Ads account. It provides relevant search volume data.
  • It is used to check search volume for niche keywords before choosing a niche. A niche is a narrow market.
  • It is used together with Ubersuggest to check search volume and demand for niche keywords. Demand means how many people want the product.
  • Volume ranges depend on account history:
    • New accounts: very broad ranges, for example 10,000 to 100,000.
    • After the account has spent money on ads: the range narrows to within about 1,000.
  • Sign-up asks for a company name and a website.
  • If you do not have a website yet, enter the domain name you plan to use. A domain name is your web address.
  • Keywords Everywhere can replace Google Keyword Planner when that tool will not let you sign up.
  • Keyword and trend data pulled through third-party APIs can fail now and then. An API is a data connection between two programs. Trend data shows how search interest changes over time.
  • One trend-data source returned results only about 9 percent of the time over a few days.
  • First, retry the search after a few minutes.
  • If failures continue, switch to another data source, such as Google Keyword Planner. It requires a Google Ads account.

Ubersuggest

2 min
  • Ubersuggest has a free version with a limit on searches per period.
  • It gives keyword volume ranges within roughly 1,000 right away.
  • It is useful as a free keyword tool before a Google Ads account has spent money.
  • As of 2021, Ubersuggest offered five free keyword searches per day.
  • This was an alternative for countries not covered by the program's keyword tool.
  • A free account is needed to see the full data.
  • Ubersuggest is used to estimate monthly Google search volume per country.
  • It is used for other marketing research later in the business build.
  • Ubersuggest fills the competitor analysis worksheet. It shows where a competitor's website gets its traffic from. Traffic means visitors.
  • Ubersuggest shows: search volume, SEO difficulty, paid difficulty, average CPC, keyword ideas and CSV export. CPC means cost per click, what one ad click costs. A CSV is a spreadsheet file.
  • The Keyword Discovery page has three checks per keyword.
    • Traffic volume: how many buyers search for it.
    • Trends: how search volume has changed over time.
    • Difficulty: whether a keyword is easy to rank for.
  • Use the three checks together. Do not judge a keyword by volume alone.
  • Market setting: in the language and country selector, choose the market. For a US store, choose English / United States.
  • In the URL, the United States appears as locId=2840. A locId is a location code.
  • Change the country to your target country, such as Australia, before every export.
  • Search volume and difficulty differ by country.
  • Keyword overview shows searcher age ranges, mobile versus desktop volume, and a 12-month volume chart.
  • It also shows the average backlinks and domain score of the top 10 ranking pages.
  • For "surfboards", the top 10 averaged 14 backlinks and a domain score of 54.
  • For "wetsuits", the figures were 33 backlinks and a domain score of 72.
  • Use these benchmarks to estimate how much link building a term will need.
  • Top-ranking URLs: the keyword overview lists the top ranking pages. A URL is a web address.
  • Each page shows estimated visits, backlinks, domain score and social shares.
  • Check what kind of page ranks, such as marketplaces, Wikipedia, or store collection pages.
  • Then decide whether to target the term with content, collection or product pages. A collection page groups products of one type.
  • Feature gap: Ahrefs and Semrush both allow keyword ranking reports with a featured snippet filter. A featured snippet is the answer box at the top of Google results.
  • Ubersuggest does not offer this filter. So it is unsuitable for this step.

Google and YouTube Auto Suggest

1 min
  • Google and YouTube Auto Suggest are free. They show popular searches related to a root term.
  • They are a good source of keyword ideas.
  • YouTube keyword research works through YouTube's own search suggestions.

Keywords Everywhere

1 min
  • Keywords Everywhere is a free Chrome extension. A Chrome extension is a small add-on for the Chrome browser. It is also described as a free browser extension for Chrome or Firefox.
  • It shows keyword data next to Google search results.
  • Use it during niche research and campaign planning.
  • Use it to research search volume and estimated cost per click.
  • Use it before a new Google Ads account gains access to the keyword planning tool.
  • Pricing changed: Keywords Everywhere switched to a paid model.
  • Free keyword tools with similar purposes exist.
  • If the paid option is not worth it, use one of those free alternatives for niche keyword research.
  • Where both views apply: the 2019 notes describe the tool as free. A later note says it moved to a paid model.
  • Check current pricing before relying on it.

Other Free Research Sources

1 min
  • Google Gemini is Google's AI model. It was suggested as a starting point for keyword lists. The idea is that Google's own model may have better access to Google search data.
  • Verify any keyword list it generates before using it in campaigns.

Paid Keyword and SEO Suites

1 min

Ahrefs (Keywords Explorer and Site Explorer)

2 min
  • Keywords Explorer is Ahrefs' keyword research section. Site Explorer is Ahrefs' section for checking any website.
  • Ahrefs trial: Ahrefs offers a 14-day trial.
  • The trial is enough time to run the keyword process for 10 articles.
  • You can cancel after the trial if the results are not useful.
  • Plan limits: Keywords Explorer shows 100 daily reports and 1,000 monthly SERP updates in the plan used. SERP stands for search engine results page, the page of results Google shows.
  • Overview metrics: Clicks, CPS (clicks per search) and RR (return rate) are shown.
  • These appear alongside search volume and CPC.
  • Bulk keyword checks: paste a list separated by commas or new lines.
  • Or upload a CSV or TXT file. TXT is a plain text file.
  • This is useful for checking many candidate topics at once.
  • Keyword lists: you can save candidate keywords into keyword lists.
  • You can have up to 20 lists per account.
  • Use these lists to group topics for each niche or project.
  • Ahrefs and SEMrush both quantify monthly search volume for a candidate category. Do this before a collection page is created.
  • The same tools can check volume for hybrid keywords on a recurring basis. Hybrid keywords combine two kinds of products or topics.
  • Backlinks: Ahrefs shows backlinks to any domain. A domain is a website's main address. It also shows the sources of the links.
  • Use it to see where competitors get their links.
  • Also check whether they publish content on other domains.
  • Ahrefs and the skyscraper method: Ahrefs is a paid SEO tool.
  • It is used for keyword research, competitor analysis and backlink reports for individual sites.
  • It also gives metrics such as domain authority and keyword difficulty.
  • It is one of the more expensive paid tools in this link-building toolkit.
  • The skyscraper method means making content better than the top results, then asking sites to link to it.
  • AH Refs (written as "AH Refs" in the source, most likely Ahrefs):
    • Teams use it to track organic keyword rankings. Organic means free search results, not ads.
    • It also analyzes SEO data.
    • It costs approximately $300 per month for team access. This is the 2025 price.
    • Price conflict: the $100 per month Ahrefs figure from 2021-22 does not match the $300 per month figure from 2025.
    • The 2025 figure applies to team access. The 2021-22 figure applies to a single plan at that time.
  • Bulk exports, procedures (Ahrefs): see the export and cleaning procedures below.

Semrush

1 min
  • SEMrush is a widely used SEO and SEM research tool. SEM means search engine marketing, which includes paid ads.
  • It offers keyword, competitor and backlink reports.
  • Its report layouts differ from Ahrefs, so you may need to look around to find the equivalents.
  • Its advantage over Ahrefs is paid search features such as ad research.
  • Its featured snippet filter for keyword ranking reports is available.
  • It is listed among the expensive paid tools that are not necessary for content keyword research.
  • Ahrefs and Semrush are used for collection volume checks.

Moz

Other tools (Answer The Public, Source A, Research Vault, Source A, BuzzSumo)

1 min
  • Answer The Public (answerthepublic.com) generates question and preposition keyword variations for a topic. A preposition term is one that uses words like "for", "with" or "near".
  • You can export these as a CSV. Use them to plan content for subniches. A subniche is a smaller part of a niche.
  • Source A keyword research tool gives search data by country and keyword.
  • It works for any keyword where data is available.
  • Bugs can be reported to support.
  • Research Vault search volume tool shows how often a keyword is searched.
  • Use it to test whether generic, non-brand terms in a non-English product language are popular in English-speaking markets. Non-brand means the term has no company name in it.
  • Source A keyword tool:
    • Use it to find search terms and niche ideas before building a store.
    • Keyword research is done with it.
    • It should always return results, even with zero search volume.
    • If it does not, contact support.
  • In-house keyword research tool: members get unlimited searches when they log in with their member account.
  • Select the target country too.
  • Logged-out use is limited to a free version. So do keyword checks after logging in.
  • BuzzSumo does for blog content what vidIQ does for YouTube. vidIQ is a tool that shows YouTube stats.
  • BuzzSumo shows trending articles and how they perform.
  • Use it to research topics before writing blog posts.

Keyword Research Workflow in Spreadsheets

1 min
  • Export keyword ideas from Ubersuggest for each root term. A spreadsheet is a table file, such as Excel or Google Sheets.
    1. Enter the root keyword in Ubersuggest and open the keyword ideas view.
    2. Check that the country setting matches your target market.
    3. Click Export to CSV and save the file to your computer.
    4. Repeat for every root term in your business. Include terms for product types and product uses.
  • Keyword ideas view tabs: the results are grouped into five tabs: Suggestions, Related, Questions, Prepositions and Comparisons.
  • Each tab has its own count.
  • The SUGGESTIONS count shows how many similar terms were found. For example, 255 for '4x4 accessories'.
  • Each keyword row shows KEYWORD, VOL and CPC columns.
  • A Filters option sits above the list.
  • An EXPORT TO CSV button exports the full list.
  • The Questions tab is a good source for problem-based content topics.
  • Suggestions and Related: the Suggestions tab is the narrowest list of closest matches.
  • The Related tab can contain many times more terms, most of them off-topic.
  • Scan Related for relevant terms. Do not export it wholesale.
  • Use the Questions tab for question-form terms that suit problem-based content.
  • Master keyword workbook layout
Tab Columns Purpose
Raw Data Keyword, Volume, CPC, SEO difficulty (from CSV export) All exported terms combined, sorted by volume, minimum 100 per month
Content Keyword, Volume, CPC, SEO difficulty Informational, problem-based and product-use terms for content pieces
Collection Keyword, Volume, CPC, SEO difficulty Broad product type, branded and specific product type terms
Product Page Keyword, Volume, CPC, SEO difficulty Branded, model-specific and very specific product terms
  • Summary formulas: summarise the volume and CPC columns in each tab. Use these spreadsheet formulas: SUM (adds up), AVERAGE, MIN (lowest), MAX (highest) and COUNTA (counts filled cells).
  • They show the total search volume per page type.
  • They show the average and range of CPC.
  • They show how many keywords each group contains.

Google Search Console

1 min
  • Google Search Console is free. You find it at search.google.com.
  • Use it to verify domain ownership. This proves that the site is yours.
  • Use it to submit a sitemap. A sitemap is a list of your pages sent to Google.
  • Use it to see indexed pages. Indexed means stored by Google so it can show them in search.
  • It shows free organic impressions and clicks. Impressions are times your page appeared in results.
  • It shows the search keywords that brought people to your site.
  • It shows site errors.
  • Keyword tracking: Google Search Console is a free tool to track keyword rankings and organic traffic. Organic traffic means free visitors from search results.
  • Search terms vs Google Analytics: Google Analytics is a tool that measures website visitors.
  • Google Analytics shows many search terms as "not provided" (2020) or "not set" (2021), because of privacy.
  • Use Google Search Console with the same Google account to see the keywords that bring clicks.
  • Google Search Console shows more search terms with impressions and clicks than Google Analytics.
  • Submitting new content: Google Search Console can submit new content URLs.
  • This helps them get crawled sooner. A crawl is when Google reads your page.
  • Import into Bing: when importing from Google Search Console into Bing, see the Bing setup below.

Bing Webmaster Tools

1 min
  • Bing Webmaster Tools is Bing's free dashboard. You find it at bing.com/webmasters.
  • It shows how Bing crawls and indexes a website.
  • Its main areas are Search Performance, Site Explorer and error monitoring.
  • Search Performance shows how the site appears in Bing search results.
  • Site Explorer lets you inspect the site's pages.
  • Both are used to find pages that have not been indexed and to spot issues that need fixing.
  • Sign-in: Bing Webmaster Tools accepts a Microsoft, Google or Facebook account.
  • Use the same email address you used for your Google accounts. This helps you find and manage all accounts consistently.
  • Same email, what it does and does not do: using the same email does not link the accounts automatically.
  • Its only benefit is consistent login credentials across services. This makes each account easier to remember and access.
  • Setup flow, button and label names as shown:
    • Get started: first screen.
    • Import your sites from GSC or Google Search Console: import option on the Welcome page. GSC means Google Search Console.
    • Allow: sharing approval.
    • Import: domain confirmation.
    • Done: shown after the success message.
    • Update now: in the profile section.
  • Importing from Google Search Console:
    • Bing identifies the Google account by its email address.
    • Choose the account that was used to set up the Search Console property. A property is one website entry in Search Console.
    • If several domains are linked to that account, Bing lets you pick which one to import.
    • After importing, Bing may need up to 48 hours to process the data and reports.
    • Until processing finishes, the dashboard data may be incomplete.
  • Profile section: Update now opens fields for personal information, professional details and company information.
  • The same section contains a delete account option. Do not click it by accident.

Competitor and Backlink Research

2 min

  • Backlink export in Site Explorer. A backlink is a link from another website to yours. Site Explorer is the Ahrefs tool (an SEO software company) for checking those links.

    1. Open Backlinks in Site Explorer.
    2. Set the link filter to Dofollow. A dofollow link passes SEO value (ranking help) to your site.
    3. Set Group similar links to One link per domain.
    4. Click Export. Choose Quick export for the first 1000 rows, or Full export for all rows. Use CSV format. A CSV is a plain spreadsheet file.

  • SERP export. SERP means search engine results page, the list Google shows for a search. In Ahrefs, the export button sits at the top right of the SERP results table.

    • Clicking it downloads a CSV.
    • Choose UTF-16 for Microsoft Excel.
    • Choose UTF-8 for Open Office and Libre. UTF is a text encoding, the setting that tells a file how to store letters.

  • Removing duplicates in Google Sheets. Google Sheets is Google's free online spreadsheet. A duplicate is a row that appears more than once.

    • Install the Remove Duplicates add-on (an extra tool) via Add-ons > Get add-ons.
    • Open Add-ons > Remove Duplicates > Find duplicate or unique rows.
    • Choose Duplicates (exclude first instances).
    • Select the URL column.
    • Tick that the table has headers.

  • Removing duplicates in Excel. Excel is Microsoft's spreadsheet program. It has a built-in Find duplicate rows feature. It works the same way as the Google Sheets add-on. When it is available, it is the simpler choice. Apple Numbers (Apple's spreadsheet app) does not offer an easy remove-duplicates function.

  • Excel duplicate modes. The duplicate tool is in the menu. It offers four modes:

    • Duplicates
    • Duplicates + 1st occurrences
    • Uniques
    • Uniques + 1st occurrences

    Choose Duplicates (exclude first instances). This keeps one copy of each URL (a web address) and removes the later copies.

  • Filtering by Domain Rating. Domain Rating is Ahrefs' score for how strong a website is.

    • In Google Sheets, apply a filter with Data > Create a filter.
    • Use the Domain Rating column.
    • Keep only rows with values from 10 to 75.

  • Example case. This case uses a merged Ahrefs keyword sheet of just over 100 SERP results. Merged means several files joined into one.

    • Delete the duplicate rows outright.
    • Do not copy them to another location.
    • In the example, this removed 19 duplicate rows.

  • Other tools. Any SEO tool (software for search ranking work) can replace the Site Explorer backlink report. The same workflow works with those exports.

  • Competitor benchmarks, Ubersuggest. A competitor is a rival site. A benchmark is a number you compare against. See the overview metrics above.

  • Moz domain analysis, free. Moz is an SEO software company. See the free-tools section above.

Email Finding and Outreach Tools

9 min

Outreach means emailing website owners to ask for a link.

Hunter.io

  • Hunter.io is a tool that searches the web for email addresses. It finds addresses linked to a domain. A domain is a website's main address, like example.com.
    • The single Domain Search page handles one domain at a time.
    • Bulk Domain Search processes whole lists.
  • Hunter.io is the primary email address scraper (a tool that collects data from web pages) for the skyscraper method.
    • The skyscraper method means making a better version of popular content. Then you ask sites that linked to the old version to link to yours.
    • Hunter.io finds email addresses for the websites you want to contact.
    • It works best for small to medium-sized sites and companies, including bulk searches.
  • Free plan. Hunter.io offers a free entry-level plan. It is useful for testing the tool.
    • Paid plans are tiered (sorted into levels) by how many emails you need to collect.
    • You can downgrade to the free plan in months with no outreach.
    • Upgrade for a large outreach project.
  • Pay per result. Hunter.io charges only for successful results. A search of a domain or an email address that returns no result costs nothing. You pay per email address actually found.
  • Plan requirement. Bulk Domain Search in Hunter.io requires the Starter plan or a higher tier.
  • Bulk setup, procedure:
    1. Log into Hunter.io on a Starter plan or higher.
    2. Open Bulks and choose New bulk Domain Search.
    3. Enter a List name. It is internal, so any name works.
    4. Set Type of input to Domain names. Company names can be used as input instead when you need contacts for one specific company.
    5. Paste the copied root domain list into the domain field. A root domain is the main part of a web address, like example.com. Or click Select a file and upload a CSV, Excel, Numbers or TXT file.
    6. Set Maximum email addresses per domain to 10.
    7. Set Email addresses type to Generic and personal.
    8. Leave Departments on All departments. Leave Include the sources in the result off unless you need sources.
    9. Click Upload and wait while the file is prepared.
    10. Compare the estimated deliverability tiers and select one.
      • Deliverability is the chance an email reaches a real inbox.
      • A tier is one level of that estimate.
    11. Download the result as a CSV file.
  • Bulk filter settings, in detail:
    • Maximum email addresses per domain set to 10.
    • Email addresses type set to Generic and personal. Generic means shared addresses like info@. Personal means a named person.
    • Departments set to All departments.
    • Include the sources in the result is optional and was left off.
  • Do not set the maximum to a high number such as 100. Hunter.io deducts one plan credit for every successful email it finds. A credit is one unit of your plan's allowance. Large domains use up credits on addresses you will never use.
  • Do not restrict the search to one email per domain. Companies often have several addresses. The single address returned may not be the best one for outreach.
  • Speed. A bulk search of 78 domains finishes very quickly. Bulk searches with thousands of domains can take up to 10 to 15 minutes.
  • Deliverability tiers from a bulk result for 78 domain names requested

  • Threshold.
    • An estimated deliverability of 85 percent is acceptable for outreach.
    • Anything below 80 percent is too low to use.
  • Trade-off. Choose the tier that gives the most usable domains while staying above the threshold.
    • Higher tiers, such as 95 percent, give fewer addresses.
    • 85 percent gives more addresses.
  • CSV result. The Hunter.io CSV lists every requested domain. It shows all email addresses found for each one, with name, position and confidence score. It is the file used for manual selection, meaning you pick by hand.
  • Confidence score. This is Hunter.io's estimate of how likely an address is to be deliverable. Use it together with the address itself, not on its own.
  • Additional CSV columns. The bulk CSV also lists Department, Position, Type, Number of sources, Pattern, Twitter handle, LinkedIn URL and Phone number.
    • Position and Department help check whether a contact fits a content or link request.
    • Pattern shows the naming format the domain uses for its addresses.
  • Bulks history. Hunter.io keeps every bulk search listed under Bulks. Each entry shows its list name, creation time and number of domains requested. You can delete a completed search from that list. This makes it easy to find an earlier run for the same project or remove test runs.

Voila Norbert

  • Voila Norbert is a secondary email finder. It finds the address of one specific person when you know their name and the website or company they work for.
    • Use it when a large business returns too many generic addresses in Hunter.io.
    • Use it when you want to contact one named editor or employee.
  • It is not required for the skyscraper method. It is only needed in rare cases, when you want to reach a particular individual. Hunter.io should remain the default email finder.
  • List cleaning. Mailshake's List Cleaner, powered by Voila Norbert, checks addresses before sending. Mailshake is an outreach tool. List cleaning means checking an email list before you send to it.
    • Each address is sorted into Ok, Risky (higher chance of bouncing) or Bad (almost certain to bounce). A bounce is an email that cannot be delivered.
    • An Ok result does not guarantee deliverability.
    • The first 500 emails are free.
    • After that, the price is $10 per 5,000 credits, with one credit per email.
  • List cleaning is optional, but it is recommended to protect deliverability.
    • Always remove Bad addresses.
    • Remove Risky addresses according to how much risk you accept.

Mailshake

  • Mailshake is a cold email outreach tool. Cold email means emails to people who have not contacted you before. Mailshake is built for sending bulk emails to them. It also manages the outreach process. It reports on campaign results, where a campaign is one set of emails sent for one goal.
  • Mailshake is a bulk and automated outreach tool.
    • It sends personalised cold emails from your connected mailbox. Personalised means each email uses the recipient's details.
    • It runs automated follow-ups.
    • It is used for sending link-building outreach, with the sending mailbox kept separate from the plan.
  • It is used to send the outreach emails to the matched contacts when pursuing backlinks for skyscraper content.
  • Do not send cold outreach emails through a regular email marketing or automation platform such as Klaviyo or Active Campaign.
    • Cold emails to unknown recipients can cause spam complaints (reports that an email is spam) and dead addresses.
    • These can damage the deliverability and performance of your main email account.

Outreach Spreadsheets

3 min

Outreach Email Filtering template

  • The Outreach Email Filtering template is a Google Sheets file. You can copy it into Google Drive, Google's online file storage. The same layout also works as an Excel file.
    • It strips URLs to root domains. Stripping means removing the extra parts and keeping only the main domain.
    • It holds the Hunter.io output for matching.
  • Keep the outreach filtering template blank. Duplicate it for each new project, so every project starts from a clean sheet.
  • Template layout
    • Column A: labelled 'Enter List of Referring Page URLs Here'. A referring page is the page that links to you.
    • Column B: returns the Referring Page URL Root Domain. "Returns" means the cell shows a calculated value.
    • Column C: labelled 'Enter List of Link URLs Here'.
    • Column D: returns the Link URL Root Domain.
    • Columns E and F: hold Email Address and Name.
    • A second tab named 'Hunter Output' receives the Hunter.io results. A tab is one sheet inside the file.
  • Row alignment. Columns A and C must be filled from the Ahrefs output sheets. The alignment must be exact. Each referring page URL must stay on the same row as its matching link URL.
  • Protected columns. Deleting cells in the protected columns of the template triggers a warning.
    • Only remove rows in the URL input columns.
    • Leave the calculated columns B, D, E and F alone.
  • Root domain formula, reconstructed from a blurry screen. It has the same logic as the sheet's own version, which is already in the file.
    • A formula is a calculation typed into a cell.
    • This one removes the protocol (the http:// or https:// part), a leading www., and everything after the first slash.
    • Only the root domain stays.
=IFERROR(IF(ISERROR(FIND("://www.",A2)),MID(A2,FIND("://",A2)+3,FIND("/",A2&"/",FIND("://",A2)+3)-FIND("://",A2)-3),MID(A2,FIND("://",A2)+7,FIND("/",A2&"/",FIND("://",A2)+7)-FIND("://",A2)-7)),"")
  • Preparing root domains, procedure
    1. Duplicate the blank Outreach Email Filtering template for this project.
    2. Copy the referring page URL column from the Ahrefs output. Paste it into column A under 'Enter List of Referring Page URLs Here'.
    3. Read the root domain for each referring page from column B.
    4. Copy the link URL column from the Ahrefs output. Paste it into column C under 'Enter List of Link URLs Here'.
    5. Read the root domain for each link URL from column D.
    6. Remove rows you do not need from the URL columns. Do not delete anything in columns B, D, E or F.
    7. Copy the complete root domain list from column B. Use it as input for Hunter.io.
  • Maintaining the email filtering spreadsheet, procedure
    1. Paste the SEO tool output into columns A and C. Make sure each row lines up exactly with its source.
    2. Do not delete anything in columns B, D, E and F.
    3. Enter the Hunter output in the second tab, named Hunter Output.
    4. When finished, remove the URLs from columns A and D. The other columns refresh automatically.
  • Moving filtered data into the batch spreadsheet, procedure
    1. Open the email filtering spreadsheet. Filter the rows so that rows with a blank email address are hidden or removed. Rows without an email cannot be contacted.
    2. Select all data rows, excluding the heading rows. Copy them.
    3. In the batch spreadsheet, click the first cell under the Prospects URL column. Not under Your Article.
    4. Use Paste special and choose values only. Formulas do not work after they are moved.

Shopify SEO Apps

Smart SEO

  • Smart SEO is a Shopify SEO app built by a company called Sherpas. Shopify is an online store platform. SEO means search engine optimisation, making pages easier to find on Google.
    • It bundles several SEO functions into one tool.
    • Those functions are: meta tags (text shown in Google results), alt tags (text describing an image), image compression, page speed, broken links (dead links), backlink reporting, and structured data (code that tells Google what a page is about).

  • Smart SEO is a Shopify app used for image optimization, meta tag generation and JSON-LD settings on pages, blog posts and articles. JSON-LD is a code format that tells search engines what a page is about. It handles the on-page technical steps in this workflow. On-page means work done on the page itself.
  • Use Smart SEO for SEO work on product pages, blog posts, and pages, including image compression.
    • Do not run Tiny SEO alongside it.
    • Uninstall Tiny SEO once Smart SEO is installed.
  • Smart SEO is the Shopify SEO app used for setting up JSON-LD structured data in the current SEO guidance. The older JSON-LD app is no longer on the Shopify app store.

Plans and pricing

  • Smart SEO has a Free plan and a Pro plan at $9.99 per month.
    • The JSON-LD structured data feature is only available on the Pro plan.
    • If you are unsure how much SEO work you will do, start with Free and upgrade later.
  • Choosing Free means the JSON-LD section is not available. No structured data gets sent to Google.
    • You can switch to Pro at any time later.

Features

  • Image compression. Smart SEO compresses images. Their file size drops while the visual quality stays acceptable.
    • Smaller images load faster.

  • Meta tags and alt text. Smart SEO can generate meta titles and descriptions with AI. This is optional. You can also write them manually.

  • Backlink report. Smart SEO produces a backlink report. It shows which sites link to your store. External sites are other websites. This becomes useful once content grows and more external sites link to you.
  • Left navigation. After Smart SEO is integrated (connected to your store), its left navigation lists separate sections.

Schema, Noindex and Page Audit

1 min
  • Schema markup is hidden code that tells Google what a page is about, such as a product's price. Your store runs on Shopify, a software platform for online shops.
    • If your Shopify store already runs a working schema app, no action is needed. If it does not, install a schema app instead of relying on hand-written code or a developer (a person who writes code).
    • Hiring a developer to add schema markup creates two problems. First, the store owner cannot easily check that the work was done correctly. Second, every future change needs that developer again. A schema app avoids both, because its developers update the markup automatically.
  • Noindex on Shopify: noindex tells Google not to show a page in search results.
    • Use a low-cost Shopify app to add noindex to selected pages. Do not hand-edit theme.liquid, the main design code file of your store.
    • Manual code edits are risky. A small error can noindex the entire domain (your whole website name), which hides your whole site from Google. Search traffic can disappear overnight. Re-indexing (Google scanning your site again) then takes a long time.
  • Google Sheets page audit tracker: a shared spreadsheet where you check each page. A page audit is a check of each page's quality. Use these columns:
    • Page Title (the headline of the page)
    • Page URL (the page's web address)
    • Duplicate Content? (the same text appears on more than one page)
    • Thin Content? (too little text to be useful)
    • Fix Content?
    • Delete?
    • If delete, redirect to where? (send visitors from the deleted page to another page)
    • No-Index? (hide this page from Google)
    • Keep Page Audit and Orphan Pages (pages that no other page links to) in separate sheets or columns.

Bulk and AI-Assisted Tools

1 min
  • SEO (search engine optimization, making your site easier to find on Google) tool: SEO Gets MCP connects Google Search Console (Google's free report on how people find your site) to Claude. MCP is a connector that lets an AI tool read outside data.
    • It shows numbers for each search phrase. Use them to find striking-distance keywords (keywords ranked just below the top results, close to moving up).
    • It sets the targets for rewritten titles and intros (the opening paragraph of a page).
  • Firecrawl integration is a tool that scrapes (copies) the current titles, meta data (hidden page descriptions for search results) and intro text of blog pages.
    • It compares the current content with the new rewrites. Do this before any change is made.
  • Matrix 5 is a Shopify app for bulk imports (uploading many items at once). It is useful for large catalogs (stores with many products).
    • Meta fields (the hidden SEO text boxes) prepared in a spreadsheet can be imported in one batch instead of editing each page by hand.
  • Shop by Markets is a Shopify app that sets up canonical (the main version of a page, to avoid duplicates) and hreflang (a code that tells Google which language or country version to show). It handles these well for market or language storefronts.
    • Use it only when the store runs localized subfolders (folders in the web address, such as /en-gb or /fr-fr) or markets.
    • A single-market store with a standard domain can skip this step.

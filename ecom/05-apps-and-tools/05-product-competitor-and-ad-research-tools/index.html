Product, competitor and ad research tools

Section 5.5 · 5 of 22 in this chapter · 39 min read

In short

  • Pick one or two research tools. Cancel any tool that finds no useful products in about one month.
  • Every paid tool is a monthly cost you must cover before sales arrive. Budget for it before you have revenue.
  • Free ad libraries (public lists of ads) come first. A paid spy tool is worth it only when you have a specific product or niche in mind.
  • Your own ads decide whether a product works. Tools only help you choose what to test.
  • Spy tools show what competitor stores sell and run. Check which plan includes the features you need before paying.
More key points
  • SimilarWeb estimates how many visitors a store gets. Check the country rank for the country you plan to sell in.
  • Bundles claim big savings. Count only the tools you really use, and cancel any tool with no job.
  • Never keep winning products in a shared account. Export your research and save it yourself.
  • Kalodata is a research tool that shows which TikTok Shop products, videos and creators sell best.
  • Set the country to United States before you read any ranking, because results change by country.
  • Use AI (ChatGPT) only for a rough first look at a niche.
  • Check any AI result against real sales numbers before you trust it.

Choosing tools and limiting spend

1 min
  • Pay for a research tool (a program that finds product ideas) for about one month, then cancel if it does not find useful products in that time.
  • Use a strict rule: invest in one or two tools to find around 10 to 50 products in a month.
  • Start with only one or two research tools that you will use every week.
  • Each subscription (a payment you make every month) is a fixed cost. You must cover it before any sales arrive.
  • An ad spy tool (a program that shows other sellers' ads) is worth its cost only when you have a specific product or niche in mind. A niche is a small, focused market.
  • Example: checking which shoes, slippers or bags perform best in one market before you sell in another.
  • For general product research with no clear goal, an ad spy tool is not worth the expense.
  • Research and spy tools often cost $15 to $99 per month each.
  • Some cost more. Pipiads and PPSpy cost about $263 and $299 per month on the list prices shown.
  • A new store should budget these as fixed monthly costs before it has revenue to cover them.
  • Pick one spy tool and use it consistently for product searches and keyword checks.
  • The source names Dropispy, Peeksta, Adspy and Minea as options.
  • Saturation-checking tools (tools that show how crowded a market is, meaning how many sellers already sell the same product) can help screen competition.
  • They do not replace a small paid test. The Facebook data from your own ads decides whether a product works.
  • Some sellers hide their store from spy tools by paying the tool operators to remove them.
  • Other sellers add code to the store that blocks scraping (automatically copying data from a web page).
  • The mentor reported these methods but has not tested them.

Free ad libraries

2 min

An ad library is a public list of ads that anyone can search.

Meta Ad Library and Facebook Ad Library

  • The Meta Ad Library is a free tool that shows active ads (ads running right now) from advertisers.
  • It shows how many ads a creative runs in. A creative is the picture or video of an ad.
  • Use it to study competitors (other sellers in your niche).
  • Look for creatives that run in many ads. Check which hooks (the first lines or seconds that grab attention) big brands keep using.
  • The Meta Ad Library shows every active ad a brand runs.
  • For each ad it shows the start date, format and copy (the written words of the ad).
  • It is reached from a competitor's page under Page transparency (a Facebook section that shows a page's details).
  • The country can be changed to show regional ads.
  • The Meta Ad Library is a free tool that shows all running ads.
  • Filter by country, search by keyword or brand, and screenshot ads for inspiration.
  • The Meta Ad Library is a free, public Meta database (a large store of data run by Meta, the company behind Facebook).
  • You can search currently running ads by keyword and see which pages and stores run them.
  • Use it to find competitor stores through the ads they run.
  • The Facebook Ad Library lets you search by country, category and keyword or advertiser name.
  • It shows the ads a competitor is running on Facebook.
  • Check competitor ads for the same niche title in the TikTok Ads Library, the Facebook Ads Library and the Meta Ad Library.
  • Bookmark them (save the page link) next to Kalodata (a TikTok sales research tool, see below) for quick access.
  • Use the Ads Library filters to show active ads and sort by active ads. You can also see summary details.
  • Use the link from each ad to check the store and its other ads.
  • AdLib (a tool that works with the Facebook Ad Library) connects to the Facebook Ad Library.
  • Click download in AdLib to save videos and thumbnails (small preview pictures) directly.
  • Or use the Facebook Ad Library link for manual downloads.
  • Winning Hunter (a paid research tool, see below) offers a free Chrome extension (a small add-on for the Chrome web browser).
  • It works with the Meta Ad Library. Install it when you want to pull ads into Winning Hunter's analysis views.

Ad spy tools in general

  • Ad spy tools track competitor ads and creatives. They show which ads are running for big brands.
  • Use them to find the creatives a niche is scaling (putting more money into ads that already sell) before you test similar hooks.
  • Ad spy tools such as Pinterest, Snapchat and TikTok searches can show winning products (products that sell well).
  • The seller runs Facebook and TikTok side by side to see which platform works better for a product.
  • Minea and Dropispy show ads and competitor products across platforms.
  • They are used for product research (finding products to sell).
  • Spy tools show what competitors advertise and which products they sell.
  • Tools such as Foreplay, Adspy and Dropispy are used for this kind of ad and product research.

AfterLib filter setup:

AfterLib steps to reach the competitor's Facebook Ad Library page:

  1. Click the three dots next to the product result.

Shared accounts and their risks

1 min
  • Shared-account services such as ShareTool split the cost of an ad account among several users. An ad account is the account used to run paid ads.
  • For example, a tool priced at about 100 per month can cost about 20 per month when five users share it.
  • Shared accounts are much cheaper.
  • But other users can see anything saved in them.
  • Do not save winning products inside a shared ad account.
  • Keep saved product research in your own browser or elsewhere.
  • Access through shared accounts is not guaranteed. This is true even when a bundle promises 24/7 access and no downtime.
  • A suspended shared account can cut off a whole workflow. A workflow is a set of steps you repeat.
  • So export and save research data locally, meaning on your own computer.
  • Avoid building a single critical process on one shared login.

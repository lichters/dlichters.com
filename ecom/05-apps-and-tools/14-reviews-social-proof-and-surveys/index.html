Reviews, social proof and surveys

Section 5.14 · 14 of 22 in this chapter · 27 min read

In short

  • Show real customer reviews on every product page to build trust with new visitors.
  • Use reviews only from real buyers. Copy supplier reviews only with their written permission.
  • Judge.me's free plan is enough to start collecting and showing reviews.
  • Judge.me asks customers for a review 14 days after purchase by default.
  • Never leave fake or spam reviews visible. Hide them.
More key points
  • Ask happy buyers for video or written reviews, because other shoppers trust them.
  • Show one free banner at the top of the store with your discount code, and hide it on brands whose suppliers forbid codes.
  • Ask buyers a short survey right after they pay, with at most five questions, and use their own words in your ads.
  • Use a referral app so customers can invite friends with their own link or code.
This section has 19 parts. Tap one to open it.

Why reviews and social proof come first

1 min
  • Product reviews build trust with new visitors, which helps them decide to buy. Collect both product reviews (feedback on one item) and store reviews (feedback on the whole shop).
  • Shoppers look for social proof (signs that other buyers trust the shop, such as reviews) before buying. So a review app is treated as a must.
  • Install a product review system on every product page of your Shopify store (online store software), to show social proof. Even a basic one is enough. The author calls this well worth the effort.
  • A review section does not need to be built by hand on a Shopify store. Use an app (a ready-made add-on program) for the reviews. Use a page builder (a tool for arranging page layouts without code) or a theme section (a ready-made block in the store's design template) for the layout around them.
  • Review apps and urgency apps are installed on Shopify to add urgency elements (things like countdown timers that push people to buy now) and product reviews to store pages.
  • A Questions & Answers app shows customer questions and answers on product pages. This creates user-created content (content written by customers) for SEO (search engine optimization, getting found in Google). It also builds social proof to increase conversions (visitors who buy).

Ethics and rules for collecting reviews

1 min
  • Ask only approved suppliers (makers you have agreed to work with) for reviews, and only if they have product reviews available. Request their permission before using any reviews. Do not copy reviews without explicit supplier approval (a clear yes).
  • Supplier reviews can be entered with permission, so they appear on product pages in the same style as future customer reviews.
  • reviews provided by a supplier can be uploaded to Judge.me (a product review app) only with written permission from that supplier.
  • Editing or inventing reviews misleads buyers. It can break consumer-protection rules (laws that protect shoppers from being cheated), for example fake-review rules in the US. Use review tools only for real customer feedback.
  • Conflict on seeding. "Seeding" means putting first reviews on a new store. The seeding notes say that initial reviews can come from suppliers, family or friends to build credibility before paid traffic (visitors you pay to bring in) begins. Review apps such as Ali Reviews or Judge.me can seed initial reviews on a new store. They are to be replaced later with real customer reviews. The ethics notes require real customer feedback and supplier permission.
  • Operator reading: the seeding notes and the ethics notes apply to different situations. A seeded review must not be presented as a real customer review. Supplier reviews need permission before they are shown.
  • Importing (copying in) reviews from supplier listings can bring weak or irrelevant feedback. Screen each review before import, and reject any that do not fit the product. Limit imports to four stars and up with photos to keep the store credible.

Judge.me: profile and plans

2 min
  • Judge.me Product Reviews collects, displays and manages customer product reviews. Find it by searching "judge.me" in the Shopify app store (Shopify's shop of add-on apps).
  • Judge.me is the recommended reviews app for dropshipping (selling products that a supplier ships to the buyer, so you hold no stock) stores. It has been used successfully for 7-8 years. It is the best fit for high-ticket dropshipping (selling expensive items this way).
  • The free plan is enough to collect and display reviews. The paid plan is not required, and no billing setup is needed to start.
  • The paid "awesome plan" is optional and can be added later. Installing the free plan requires no agreement to a billing policy (the payment terms).
  • On the free plan, widget texts such as "Awesome" cannot be edited. A widget is a small box added to a page, such as a star rating box. Editing its text requires the paid plan.
  • Conflict on Google star ratings and the free plan:
    • One note says the Judge.me free plan can automatically email review requests. It also says the free plan shows star ratings in Google and Google Shopping.
    • A 2025 note says the Google Shopping star integration (linking stars to Google's shopping results) costs $15 per month. It says the free plan does not include it.
    • Use the 2025 value, since it is the more recent one. Check the plan in the app before relying on Google Shopping stars.
  • Judge.me was chosen over the previous review app. It displays star ratings in Google and Google Shopping, which few low-cost review apps offer. Showing stars in search and shopping results can improve click-through (the share of people who see a result and click it).
  • Judge.me reviews can appear in Google Shopping ads through its Google Shopping feature. It is worth paying for once a store has a good number of reviews (2024).
  • Judge.me integrates more easily with Google Shopping star ratings than Stamped.io (another review app).
  • The paid Judge.me plan sends star ratings to Google Shopping. Stamped.io is an alternative with a similar function.

Judge.me: Google, search stars and trust signals

1 min

  • Judge.me collects reviews and integrates with Google, so star ratings can appear in Google Shopping results. It is used as the review aggregator (a tool that gathers reviews into one place) in the store setup.

  • Judge.me star reviews add schema markup (hidden code that tells Google what a page contains) to organic results (normal, unpaid search listings). This can make star ratings appear in search and increase click-through rate (2023).

  • Judge.me displays reviews across the site and on a dedicated review page. This serves as the store's trust signal (a sign that shows the shop is safe) in place of paid security seals (badges that claim a site is safe) (2023).

  • Judge.me stars can appear in Google Shopping results. They can also replace SEO Rich Snippets (extra details, like stars, shown under a search result) for reviews on new stores (2023).

  • SEO Rich Snippets and Minifier were removed from the Shopify app store. Replace them as follows:

    1. Open the Shopify app store.
    2. Search for rich snippets and minifier.
    3. Choose a 5-star app (an app with top user ratings) that does the same job.

    Existing stores keep the old apps they already had.

  • Judge.me badges look similar to Trustpilot (a well-known review site) and give a more authoritative look on the homepage. Check that the badge links down to the reviews section on the page. Most review apps that work with PageFly (a page builder) support this. If not, contact the review app or PageFly support.

  • Judge.me needs the license number (a code proving you may use the design) from the theme vendor (the company that sold your store design). Enter it in its setup. Without that license entry, the review block may not work correctly on the product page.

Installing Judge.me

2 min

Install steps in Shopify:

  1. Open the Shopify App Store and click Visit the app store.
  2. Search for Judge.me and open the first result, which shows the free plan.
  3. Click Add app.
  4. Agree to give the app access to the store so it works properly.
  5. Click Install app.
  6. Choose who installs it: yourself via the theme editor (the tool for editing store design), or Judge.me's team.
  7. Click Start installation.
  8. Select where reviews should appear: the reviews widget on the product page, stars on the product page, and stars on the collection page (a page listing many products).
  9. Click Choose theme and install on the current live theme (the design your customers see now). Do this unless another theme is installed but not yet published and should be used.
  10. Click Install judge.me and wait for the installation to finish.
  • Search for Judge.me in the Shopify App Store, open the app, click Add app, grant access to the store, and click Install app. Choose between installing it yourself through the theme editor or having the Judge.me team install it for you.
  • The Judge.me support team installs widgets manually. They send an email within three hours after the request. Installation may take about two hours in practice.
  • The Judge.me widget install (2024): after installing, add the review widget to product pages from the app's top-left box. If the automatic install does not show it, use the app's contact-developer box. The developer can install it.

Widgets (procedure):

  1. Go to Judge.me → Widgets.
  2. Locate the Review Widget.
  3. Click Install.
  4. The widget will be added to your Shopify theme automatically.
  • The product page review widget is found under Settings > Review display > Widgets. The first time, click Install. It adds the widget automatically to the Shopify theme. A separate star ratings badge widget is also available there, and several widgets can be used at once.
  • In Settings, the Other widgets section shows where each widget is switched on or off on the site. Under the preview badge settings you can set badge colors and choose Hide badge texts. Leave it off so both stars and text are shown.
  • Branding: open Settings and scroll to the Branding section at the bottom left. Set the primary color (your main brand color) to one of the colors used in the store's layout and color scheme. This primary color also styles the review request emails, including the stars.

Judge.me: review requests and emails

1 min
  • Judge.me automatically sends a review request email 14 days after purchase. This timing makes sure customers have received the product before being asked to write a review.
  • Judge.me Settings groups requests under Review requests and Other emails. These control the automatic review request emails sent to customers.
  • Customers can send their review by email. They can also leave it through the Shopify Product Review app, which should already be installed on the store.
  • Judge.me is a product review app. It stores reviews for each product and also has a general store review area. Use it to add reviews collected by email (2024).
  • Judge.me publishes product reviews on product pages (2023).

Email templates (procedure):

  1. Go to Judge.me → Email Templates.
  2. Edit subject lines (the email titles) or review request copy (the text asking for a review), if desired.
  3. Leave default settings enabled, which is recommended.

Judge.me: moderation

1 min

  • Judge.me automatically approves and publishes reviews by default. In the dashboard (the main control page of an app), you can view all reviews. You can mark spam (junk posts) or unrelated reviews as hidden, and control what appears on product pages.

  • New reviews appear in the review section and are published by default. Each review can be marked as Fake, Spam or Unrelated to the product, which hides it from the store. When hiding a review, choose the reason for hiding it.

  • Moderation steps (checking reviews before they stay public):

    1. Open Judge.me and go to the review section.
    2. Check the new reviews that appear, which are published by default.
    3. For any spam, fake or unrelated review, mark it as Fake, Spam or Unrelated to the product.
    4. Select the reason for hiding the review. It is then hidden from the store.
    5. Edit any review if needed. You receive a notification each time someone leaves a review.

  • Mistake: leaving spam or fake reviews visible on the store. Marking them as Fake, Spam or Unrelated hides them from the product page. This also keeps unrelated content off the store.

Judge.me: entering reviews, imports and migration

1 min

Entering supplier reviews directly (procedure, with permission only):

  1. Confirm the supplier gives permission to use their reviews.
  2. Enter reviews directly on your product pages.
  3. Submit them the same way a customer would submit a review.
  4. They will publish like normal customer reviews.
  • Supplier reviews can be added one by one, or imported with a spreadsheet upload (a file with rows and columns, like Excel) (2025).
  • Judge.me can import reviews that were originally posted on eBay (an online marketplace). Export the eBay reviews as a file first. Then upload that file in Judge.me. This only works if you already have eBay feedback you want to show on your store.
  • The Judge.me importer (the tool that copies reviews in) is very important. Install it so that existing reviews can be brought into the store.

Migrating from an old review app (moving to a new one, procedure):

  1. In Judge.me, go to the import function and click Import reviews to bring in existing reviews from the old app. Confirm the import succeeded.
  2. Wait until Judge.me confirms by email that the app is installed.
  3. In Shopify, remove the old review app(s), such as product reviews apps, after the import is done.
  4. Also remove the old app Stamped.io once it is no longer needed.
  5. If no reviews are currently being collected, the removal steps can be skipped.
  • Do not remove an old review app before its reviews are imported into the new app. Once the import is complete, saving the old app's settings out is not needed for Judge.me.
  • Moving existing reviews from Stamped.io to Judge.me: open the import function in Judge.me, click Import reviews, and confirm that the import succeeded. Remove Stamped.io from the Shopify store only after the import is confirmed.
  • running two product review apps on the same store can show each review twice on the product page. Keep one review app, remove the other, and check the product pages.

Luke's Reviews

3 min
  • Luke's Reviews collects, imports and shows product reviews and photos. It sends automatic review request emails after fulfillment (when the supplier packs and ships the order). It can also run a referral program (a reward for customers who bring in friends) and a photo-review incentive (a reward for sending a photo).
  • The import-to-looks bookmark is a Chrome bookmark (a saved link in Google's web browser). It copies review data from an AliExpress (online supplier marketplace) product page into Luke's Reviews for import.

Setup (procedure):

  1. Go to Apps, open Luke's product reviews and photos, click Install, and scroll down to install the app.
  2. Click Continue as free.
  3. Follow the setup. In the theme editor, turn on the app embed (a switch that turns an app on inside the store design) and click save.
  4. Add the review widget below the product description and click save.
  5. Add the rating widget below the product title and click save.
  6. Choose the store language and icon color, then add your branding colors.
  7. Set the review request email: choose how many days after fulfillment the email is sent. Pick a number based on your supplier's delivery time (the example used 21 days).
  8. Set the photo and video incentive: a discount reward of 15 for photo reviews and 20 for video reviews, given automatically.
  9. Optionally set up the referral program: the referred friend gets a discount (example: 10 off), and the referrer gets a reward (example: $5). You can set a minimum spend and choose whether to show it on the store.
  10. Click continue through the remaining screens.
  • Short version of the same setup: install Luke's Product Reviews and Photos from the Shopify app store (free plan). Turn on the app embed in the theme editor. Then add the review widget below the description and the rating widget below the product title. Set the review request email to be sent 21 days after fulfillment (adjust for the supplier's shipping time). Offer a discount for reviews of 15 for photos and 20 for videos.
  • Referral program settings: set the discount the referred customer receives (example: 10 off), the reward for the referrer (example: 5 dollars), and the minimum spend required. Choose whether to show this on the store.
  • Email timing across apps: Judge.me sends its review request 14 days after purchase by default. In Luke's Reviews, the request is sent a number of days after fulfillment. That number is set to the supplier's delivery time (21 days in the example). Use each app's own timing setting for each store.

Import from AliExpress (procedure, version A):

  1. In Google Chrome, drag the import to looks button from the Luke's Reviews setup into the bookmarks bar (the strip of saved links under the address bar).
  2. Open the product page on AliExpress.
  3. Click the import to looks bookmark, choose your store, and choose the product that the reviews belong to.
  4. Set the rating filter to four stars and up.
  5. Choose the countries the reviews should come from.
  6. Turn on the option to import only reviews that include photos, if wanted. Set fetch most recent reviews (pull in the newest reviews) to yes.
  7. Click preview and import.
  8. Accept each review you want with import, and reject the others.
  9. Click Finish importing.

Import from AliExpress (procedure, version B):

  1. Open Google Chrome and drag the Import to Looks button to the bookmarks bar.
  2. Open the matching AliExpress product page.
  3. Click the Import to Looks bookmark, choose your store and the product.
  4. Set the rating filter to four stars and up.
  5. Choose the countries the reviews should come from.
  6. Enable only reviews with photos and fetch the most recent reviews.
  7. Click Preview and import.
  8. For each review, click Import to keep it or Reject to skip it.
  9. Click Finish importing.
  • The free Looks plan (the free level of the import tool) can import only 20 reviews per import. More reviews require a higher paid plan.

Ali Reviews, Loox and other review apps

3 min
  • Ali Reviews is a paid review app. It lets you edit review text, names, pictures, product links and target country (the country where reviews are shown). It costs about $19 per month at lower volume and about $39.90 per month at high sales volume. The notes say the high-volume price is more than Shopify's 29 USD monthly plan.
  • Judge.me and Loox (another review app) are other review apps besides Ali Reviews. Ali Reviews allows editing of review text, names, pictures and target country. Compare these tools only on real customer reviews and conversion results.
  • Ali Reviews can add review images. Use it to publish reviews that answer specific buyer doubts, such as fit or size concerns.
  • The same example uses Ali Reviews images to show that a product does not have a concern. Judge.me and Ali Reviews are both review apps for Shopify.
  • Judge.me also adds review images, which can strengthen trust on product pages.
  • Riview imports reviews from AliExpress and Amazon, with more customization options than Loox. Both apps can add review sections to product pages. Riview gives more layout flexibility (freedom to change how things are arranged).
  • Rightview can fetch 600 or more Amazon reviews for a product. Amazon Review Export takes only about 100 at a time. It exports a CSV (a plain spreadsheet file) that you can edit and re-import into a review app. It can also be set to update automatically when new reviews appear. Most Shopify review apps do not allow editing.
  • Rivo was suggested as a cheaper alternative to Loox for early-stage stores. It has a 7 to 14-day free trial (a test period at no cost) and a plan around $6 per month. It offers review editing that other apps lack.
  • Right View is a product review app with review editing. It is presented as a cheaper alternative to Loox. It offers a free trial of 7 to 14 days and a plan around 6 USD, and it suits a store on a tight budget.
  • Naming note: the sources name this tool Riview, Rightview, Right View and Rivo. The exact name is not confirmed by the sources. Check the Shopify App Store before installing.
  • Ali Reviews, Judge.me and similar review apps can seed initial product reviews on a new store. Later, replace them with real customer reviews.
  • Stamped.io is a review platform. Its custom forms, menu and plans are as follows:
    • In the Reviews & UGC platform (UGC means user-generated content, such as reviews and photos from customers), go to Customize. Then choose Add Custom Forms to add extra review questions. Examples are how the fit was, how the quality is, or the customer's age group. These questions collect buyer insight beyond the star rating. The platform recommends adding them once reviews start coming in.
    • The main menu includes Home, Queue, Customers, Products, Analytics and Marketing. Under Moderation it has Reviews, Questions, Net Promoter Score (a score showing how likely customers are to recommend you), Checkout Comments, Visual UGC and Instagram. Loyalty & Rewards is a separate section. The Home page summarises the last 30 days of widget interactions, revenue and advocate traffic (visits from customers who promote the store).
    • The free-forever plan supports only standard product reviews, covering up to 50 orders. Photo reviews and other richer review types require a paid plan, starting with Basic at $23 per month for up to 200 orders.
  • Judge.me and Stamp.io are the most common review apps on Shopify. Ask other review tools, such as Yotpo (another review app), whether they can load the review title with the product name.
  • Stamped.io is an alternative review app with a similar function to Judge.me.

Junip

2 min
  • Junip is a Shopify review app for collecting, managing and displaying product and store reviews. It runs review request flows (automatic sets of messages asking for reviews) and campaigns to start gathering reviews right away.
  • Junip sends customers an email asking them to leave a review. The email links to a mobile-first submission form (a review form designed first for phone screens). Review request flows and campaigns are set up inside Junip.
  • The submission form accepts a star rating and a text review by default. Customers are encouraged to add photos and videos. Custom questions can be added using product traits (details of an item) and customer attributes (details of a buyer).
  • Photo and video reviews add more evidence than text alone. The vendor gives no figure for how much they raise conversion, so test them on your own store.
  • Junip review displays adapt automatically to the look and feel of the Shopify theme. No custom code (special code written by hand) is needed to match the brand.
  • Junip integrates with Klaviyo, OmniSend and PostScript (email and text message tools). It offers API access (a way for other programs to connect to it) for custom needs. These integrations connect review collection with existing email and SMS (text message) tools.
  • Junip offers a free 14-day trial that can be installed directly. Use the trial to test review requests and displays before paying.

Junip incentives (an incentive is a reward or discount for leaving a review):

  • Junip has a two-tier incentive system (two levels of reward). The standard incentive covers all reviews. The immediate incentive covers only reviews that include a photo or video.
  • The standard incentive is given for any submitted review, regardless of its content. The reward does not depend on how good or detailed the review is.
  • The immediate incentive is given only when a review includes a photo or video. Text-only reviews do not receive this second-tier reward.
  • Junip generates a unique, single-use discount code (a code that works one time only) for each eligible review that is submitted. The customer receives this code after submitting the review.
  • Path: Incentives > Add incentive > Add standard incentive. Under Incentive type, pick Free shipping, Percentage discount or Fixed discount. Then set the amount.
  • Under Reward this to, choose "Verified buyers only (Recommended)" or "Anyone who leaves a review, including unverified buyers." Verified buyers are people who actually bought the product. The verified-only option is marked as the recommended choice.

Junip incentive setup (procedure):

  1. Go to Incentives and click Add incentive.
  2. Click Add standard incentive.
  3. Under Incentive type, select Free shipping, Percentage discount or Fixed discount.
  4. Enter the amount for the chosen incentive type.
  5. Under Reward this to, select Verified buyers only (Recommended), or Anyone who leaves a review, including unverified buyers.

Announcement bars

  • The app supports promotional messages such as coupon codes or floating bar alerts (a bar that stays visible as the visitor scrolls). It supports dynamic text that changes based on product collections (groups of products).
  • Quick Announcement Bar by Hextom is an expiring coupon banner (a discount code with an end date) that can be excluded from specific pages or collections. Use it when some suppliers forbid discount codes, so the banner does not appear on their product pages.
  • Conflict on advanced targeting:
    • Quick Announcement Bar is the fallback (backup) when a store's theme (the store's design template) has no built-in announcement bar section. Its free plan covers a sitewide code bar. The paid tier adds advanced page targeting (choosing exactly which pages show it) and custom code options that this setup does not need.
    • in the app, use the Advanced targeting option to exclude specific vendors (brands that supply products) or collections, so the bar does not show on their pages. The app's conditional logic (rules that show something only in certain cases) is free to use.
    • Use the 2021 value only if the free advanced targeting is still available in the app. Otherwise use the paid tier described above.

  6. Set "Is the bar clickable?" to no.
  7. Set the close button to no.

  11. Click Save.
  • The bar's target configuration (the settings that decide which pages show it) is at its default of showing on all pages. Keep it set to every page for a sitewide code, and make sure the bar is shown on both desktop (computer) and mobile (phone).
  • If different suppliers need different coupon codes, exclude the pages of the brands that should not show the current code. Paste their URLs (web addresses) or a keyword from their URLs into the app's exclusion field. This is optional and depends on the store's profit margins (the money left after costs).

Surveys: post-purchase feedback

1 min
  • A post-purchase survey (a short questionnaire shown after buying) asks buyers why they bought, what their pain points (the problems they had) are, and what they expect. It is useful for stores that do not yet have enough own customer data. Re-convert runs such surveys.
  • Post-purchase surveys ask customers questions after the purchase flow (the steps of checkout and payment). Their results show which messages and angles (ways of framing a message) customers respond to.
  • A post-purchase survey app can ask buyers why they bought and how they use the product. The exact app name was not clear in the source.
  • Post-purchase surveys reach people who have already paid, not random visitors. Use them to learn why customers bought, what problem they had, and what result they want.
  • A single on-site survey can run through an online chat widget (a chat box on the site) or Hotjar (a tool for visitor feedback). Visitors answer simple questions on the site to give feedback on their experience.
  • Feedback forms do not need polished design. A simple option such as Google Forms (a free form tool from Google) is enough. Spend the effort on writing specific questions, not on the look of the survey.

Surveys: Zigpoll

2 min
  • Zigpoll is a post-purchase survey tool. It asks customers about their pain points, wants and objections (reasons they hesitate to buy). The responses help decide what to say in ads.
  • Zigpoll is a survey app that asks customers questions after they have bought, so answers come from people who already paid. Responses can be exported as a CSV file (a spreadsheet file) for analysis.
  • Zigpoll connects to third-party tools (other companies' apps) such as Slack (a team chat app), so survey responses can be passed on to other business apps. Check the integration options (ready-made connections) in the app before building a separate export process.
  • A Zigpoll post-purchase survey appears on its own for buyers right after they buy. No separate invitation link is needed. Set it up as a post-purchase survey so responses come from people who have already paid.
  • Zigpoll setup rules:
    • Set at most five questions per survey, each with four to five answer options.
    • Reorder questions with drag and drop (moving items with the mouse) under Slide Management (the screen that orders survey pages), and check the Survey Preview before publishing.
    • Set open-ended questions (questions with a free answer) to the Long Answer question type (a free text box), and keep closed questions as multiple-choice (pick one from a list) with four to five options. Use Long Answer where you want the customer's exact words about their problem, trigger or result.
    • Reorder the slides with Slide Management by dragging and dropping them into place.
  • Rewards: a Zigpoll survey can show a promo code (a discount code) on its completion screen as the reward for finishing. Alternatively, offer a monthly cash prize draw for one respondent, for example $1,000, if a discount is not suitable for your margin.
  • Export: open the survey's responses in the Zigpoll dashboard and use the export button to download a CSV file. If the button is hard to find, Zigpoll support can point it out.
  • Analysis setup: create a Claude project (a saved workspace in Claude for one topic) for the product, add the exported survey CSV to the project's knowledge, and start all analysis chats inside that project so the file stays available.

Analysing responses (procedure):

  1. Wait until you have a usable number of survey responses, around 50, 100, or 200 or more.
  2. Download the responses from Zigpoll.

Referral and loyalty

2 min
  • Smile.io is the recommended loyalty app (an app that rewards repeat buying) for Shopify stores. It is simple to set up, highly customizable, and lets the store owner set the reward rate for their products. Other loyalty apps exist in the Shopify App Store (Shopify's app shop) if Smile.io does not fit.
  • Luke's Reviews includes a referral program (a plan where customers invite friends for a reward), set up as described in the Luke's Reviews section above.
  • a referral app gives each customer who signs up the tools needed to refer others, such as a link or code and ready-made shares (ready-to-send messages). The app tracks every referral made through that link or code.
  • Referral tracking works like affiliate marketing (paying people who send buyers). Each participating customer uses a unique link or code, and the system attributes purchases to that customer (credits the sale to them). This is how a referral is credited to the person who made it.
  • Setup of a referral app is largely automatic. Install it, then configure it (set its options) for the business and the chosen reward model (the kind of reward). Once configured, the app generates the materials customers need to refer others.
  • Refersion is a referral tool that can also run affiliate arrangements. This is useful when working with bloggers or forum owners. It is one of the recommended tools for referral programs.
  • Referral Candy is another recommended referral app. Further referral apps are available in the Shopify app store.
  • Talkable is a referral and rewards app that costs about $2,000 and includes fraud protection (checks against fake referrals). It is useful when referral volume becomes too high to approve each referral manually.
  • Friendbuy is an affordable referral app for running a friend-referral program (customers invite friends) in your own store. It is an alternative to Talkable for a similar purpose.
  • Klaviyo (an email marketing tool) can automate review request emails once manual follow-up volume becomes too high. Use it after the first reviews are collected manually.

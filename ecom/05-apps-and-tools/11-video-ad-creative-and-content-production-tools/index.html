Video, ad creative and content production tools

Section 5.11 · 11 of 22 in this chapter · 27 min read

In short

  • Before you edit, make one folder for each ad's pictures and videos.
  • Never rename or move a file after you import it into an editor. The link to it breaks.
  • Start with CapCut. It is free, quick, and the desktop (computer) version is best for ad work.
  • Good light matters more than a good camera.
  • Use Splice to edit TikTok ads and product videos. Use InShot for videos that go to several platforms.
More key points
  • Check Splice's price in the app store first. Two different prices are stated.
  • Before making long AI voice audio with ElevenLabs, check your plan's character limit (how much text it can convert).
  • Before uploading AI-made images to Meta, re-download them through Canva. This removes hidden data that Meta can read to spot AI content.
  • To make a wide video tall for social ad spots, use a separate online editor.

Planning the Project Folder and Assets

1 min

Organise assets before building any ad

1 min
  1. Create a Google Drive folder (online file storage) for the campaign.
  2. Inside it, create two subfolders: "Image Assets" and "Video Assets".
  3. Gather all images: AliExpress product photos, Instagram posts, user-generated content (UGC, pictures or videos made by ordinary customers or creators), screenshots.
  4. Gather all videos: TikTok product clips, Instagram Reels (short vertical videos on Instagram), YouTube Shorts (short videos on YouTube), self-filmed footage.
  5. Add product images, lifestyle images (photos of the product in real life) and platform-specific clips (clips sized and styled for one app, such as TikTok).
  6. Reference this folder when designing ad creatives (ad images and videos) in Canva (an online design tool) to keep them consistent and fast.

Editing project folder structure

1 min

Set up the folders in this order before importing anything (importing means bringing files into the editor):

  1. Create a separate assets folder for images, emojis and graphics.
  2. Create a separate audio folder for voiceovers, music and sound effects.
  3. Create a separate video folder for stock footage (ready-made clips you can use) and product clips.
  4. Keep these folders stable. Moving or renaming files breaks the links in editing software such as Premiere Pro (a professional video editor), which then reports that the files cannot be found.
  • Renaming or moving a source file (the original file you start from) or its folder after import breaks the link to the clip, so keep raw footage (unedited original video) in fixed locations.
  • Folders inside a Premiere Pro project bin (a folder inside the project window) only organise your work and do not affect links to source files. Use import folders such as assets, audio and video.
  • Google Drive, Dropbox or an external hard drive (a separate disk you plug in) can store project folders. Cloud storage (files kept on the internet) lets you reach the same files from a phone, for example to download clips or upload recorded voiceovers.
  • When importing downloaded clips into CapCut, choose "Stay in original location" in the Imported media pop-up to keep the files in their download folder instead of copying them.

Principles for choosing

1 min
  • CapCut is quick to start on a phone or desktop. Desktop editors such as Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve suit serious production. If you plan to produce ads regularly, pick one desktop editor and learn it properly.
  • Premiere Pro, Final Cut and DaVinci Resolve are heavier (slower and bigger) but offer more functionality. CapCut is cheaper and easier for beginners and already includes graphics, sound effects and auto captions (text the app writes from speech).
  • Splice is practical for beginners making short-form ads, but it cannot place sound effects or cuts at split-second precision (exact timing to a tiny fraction of a second). For frame-level timing (timing down to one single picture in a video), use DaVinci Resolve or Final Cut Pro on Mac. Use Splice for most edits and switch to the desktop editor only when precise timing matters.
  • Test an editor before committing. Principles transfer between tools, so switching later is low-risk.
  • Premiere Pro often requires buying a transition package (a set of extra effects you pay for) before transitions can be used. CapCut ships a large set of transitions (effects that join two clips) such as zoom, wipe and shake that can be added directly. For beginners who need quick transitions without extra purchases, CapCut is the faster starting point.
  • InVideo and Contagion are video editing tools. One of them was used to edit short ads in this example.

CapCut

1 min

CapCut is a free video editor from the company behind TikTok.

Desktop or mobile

1 min
  • For ad creative work, CapCut desktop (the computer version) is preferred over the mobile app because all recorded footage can be imported at once, edited and saved in a project file (one saved file that holds the whole edit). The free basic plan is sufficient for beginners.
  • CapCut on a phone is fast and capable, but it stores everything on the phone and is less efficient for managing video projects than desktop software. Large projects are inefficient on a phone.
  • CapCut Pro costs 10 US dollars per month and adds more transitions, extra effects and a larger library of graphics and sound effects. Beginners do not need it to produce Facebook ad creatives.
  • The free library covers basic transitions, effects and text animations. Text templates, some animations and additional pro transitions require Pro. For a beginner ad workflow, the free items are enough to start. Paid items are worth adding only once the basic edit is working.
  • Some animation options, such as advanced animated captions, may require a Pro account (a paid plan). Check whether a feature is locked before building an ad template around it.

Layout (desktop)

1 min

Layout means how the screen is arranged.

  • The media bin (the panel that holds your files) holds imported footage, sounds and images. The timeline (the bar at the bottom where clips are placed in order) layers clips. The player (the preview screen) in the middle shows the output. The right-side control panel changes the selected asset. The control panel holds volume, speed, animation and other settings for the selection.
  • The timeline is at the bottom and holds all video, audio and graphics tracks (rows that stack layers). The preview window is in the middle. The left panel has the tabs Media, Audio, Text, Stickers, Effects, Filters, Adjustment and Templates. The right panel is the Tools area, which changes with the selected clip and controls position, scale (size) and other properties.
  • Click any imported file in the Media panel to play it in the preview window. Review footage there before dragging it onto the timeline.
  • Scrub footage (drag across the preview to move through it) before placing it on the timeline, to check what each clip contains. Dragging a clip onto the timeline makes it the active track for layering.

Layout (mobile)

1 min
  • The video preview sits at the top, the timeline in the middle and the main menu at the bottom with Edit, Audio, Text and Stickers. Under Edit, the Split option cuts the clip at the playhead (the marker that shows the current spot).

Frame rate and performance

1 min
  • Frame rate is how many pictures a video shows each second, written as fps. Shoot and edit at 30 fps, but check the frame rate your camera or phone actually recorded and match the project to it.
  • Under the CapCut menu, Edit settings offers a proxy option (a small, lighter copy of your footage) that caches media (stores a temporary copy) so a weaker computer runs more smoothly.
  • Recorded footage at 1920 x 1080 pixels is the landscape format (wide screen; pixels are the tiny dots that make up a picture), useful when searching for horizontal footage.

Shortcuts and timeline controls

1 min
  • A: selection tool (the arrow that picks and moves clips). B: blade (cut) tool. Ctrl+B: splits the clip under the cursor without switching tools.
  • Space: plays from the playhead, and pressing it again stops playback.
  • Left and right arrow keys step through frames. Shift plus arrow keys moves faster.
  • A selects and moves clips. B activates the blade to cut.
  • Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes. Ctrl/Cmd plus mouse wheel zooms the timeline.
  • Zoom the timeline by holding Ctrl or Cmd and scrolling. This helps when checking small cuts or the audio waveform (the picture of sound as a wave), and shows where speech starts and stops.
  • Keep Snap switched on as a beginner. Snap makes clips lock to the ends of neighbouring clips and avoids small gaps. Snap is the toggle near the timeline controls and can be turned off when free placement is needed.
  • Clips snap to the edges of neighbouring clips when you drag them. This helps line up B-roll (extra footage laid over the main clip) and transition points without manual measuring.
  • On mobile, hold a timeline item with your thumb and drag. The item vibrates when it nears an edge, which helps it snap into position.

Cutting and trimming

1 min
  • The audio waveform under each clip makes pauses and silent gaps visible. Use it to find cut points for filler words (um, uh, like) before you play the A-roll (the main talking footage) back.
  • Remove filler or a mistake:
    1. Play the footage and find the mistake or filler.
    2. Switch to the blade tool with B and cut at the start and end of the unwanted section.
    3. Switch back to the selection tool with A and click the unwanted piece.
    4. Press Delete or Backspace to remove it. The gap closes automatically, so the remaining clips move together.
    5. Replay the section and repeat until the take is clean.
  • In selection mode, grab the edge of a clip and drag it inward or outward to shorten or lengthen it. This trims (cuts off the ends) without the blade.
  • Deleting a clip with Backspace closes the gap it leaves, so the next clip shifts left.
  • Drag a selection box across several clips on the timeline to move or delete them together.
  • To select several layers, drag a selection box over them or hold Cmd (Mac) or Ctrl (Windows) while clicking each one. You can then move them or change their text style together.

Speed

1 min
  • To change a clip's playback speed, select the clip and use the Speed control in the video section of the right-hand control panel. A 2x playback shortcut (double speed for the whole timeline) like the one in Premiere Pro was not shown here, so speed changes are made on the clip itself.
  • On mobile, open Speed on a clip. Normal changes the whole clip evenly. Curve changes speed over time to create a speed ramp (a speed that changes gradually), for example slow at the start and fast at the end.

Keyframes and motion

1 min
  • A keyframe (a marker that saves a setting at one moment) stores a value such as position or scale at one point in time. The clip animates between two or more keyframes, which creates movement such as following a subject or zooming in on a product.
  • Keyframes can animate more than scale, including opacity (how see-through something is), position, rotation, size and alignment, using one keyframe at the start and one at the end. For example, a fade-in text is made by keyframing opacity from zero to full.
  • Add a moving zoom or pan (a slow zoom or sideways move) in CapCut:
    1. Add a keyframe with the diamond button beside the play button.
    2. Move the playhead.
    3. Change the scale to zoom.
    4. Repeat for each position change to make a moving zoom or pan.
  • The keyframe button sits beside the play button. Tapping it places a red diamond at the current position. To remove a keyframe, hover over the diamond until it turns red and tap the minus option.
  • Motion blur in the adjustment settings adds blur to movement so it looks more like real camera motion. Use a light setting, because heavy blur looks unnatural.
  • Fade out: grab the end of the clip, drag to adjust its length, then choose Fade from the bottom menu and select fade out so the clip ends by fading to black.

Transitions

1 min
  • Split a clip at the playhead, click the small white marker between the two clips and choose a transition from the Movement category, such as Pull out, Pull in, Shake or Zoom. Adjust the transition length after it is applied.
  • On mobile, tap the point between two clips to open the transition presets (ready-made settings), which include zooms, flashes and burns. Choose one to join the clips.

Audio, sound effects and text-to-speech

1 min
  • Add a sound effect to a CapCut ad:
    1. Search YouTube for a sound effect, for example by typing sfx and the effect name.
    2. Copy the video link and download it as an MP3 with 4K Video Downloader.
    3. Import the MP3 into CapCut.
    4. Place the sound on the timeline at the moment it should play.
  • Built-in text-to-speech (the app reads text aloud in a computer voice): tap the text-to-speech button in the bottom menu, type the script and choose a voice preset. CapCut belongs to the company behind TikTok, so it often adds features suited to TikTok content.
  • Add a voiceover from ElevenLabs (an online AI voice tool) in CapCut:
    1. Type the script into ElevenLabs and generate the voiceover.
    2. Download the generated audio file.
    3. In CapCut, use the audio import option to upload the file.
    4. Merge the imported audio with your video track in the timeline.

Building a UGC-style ad

1 min

Recommended order in CapCut:

  1. Record or film the main talking-head footage (A-roll, the person speaking to the camera), either as UGC or as a typical ad, from a finished script.
  2. Cut out filler words, pauses and mistakes from the A-roll and play it through to check the script.
  3. Overlay B-roll product shots (extra shots of the product) on top of the A-roll while the speaker talks.
  4. Add background music and sound effects.
  5. Add auto captions and style them.
  6. Add graphics such as icons, checkmarks and bullet points.
  7. Add transitions, cuts, zooms and keyframed motion.
  • CapCut is used to make video creatives by combining clips, text, descriptions and AI voiceovers. It is a common editor for building several ad variations quickly.
  • CapCut cuts downloaded ad videos down to their hook (the first few seconds that catch attention), combines clips from different ads into one new creative and exports (saves the finished file) the result. The workflow runs entirely on the downloaded files.

Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve

1 min

Premiere Pro

3 min
  • Premiere Pro is a professional video editor used alongside After Effects (a motion graphics program) for motion graphics (moving titles and shapes).
  • Panels (boxes of tools on screen): the Project panel (bottom left) holds imported media and bins. The Timeline (bottom) shows clips in sequence, with visual tracks above and audio tracks below a divider. The Program monitor (center) previews the combined result. The Effect Controls panel (top) edits the selected clip.
  • A new sequence (a timeline with its own size settings) takes the aspect ratio (the shape, such as wide or tall) of the first clip you add. Create a separate sequence for vertical video so the ratio is correct. Check the ratio before editing, because the sequence settings follow the first imported clip.
  • Set up a project:
    1. Create a dated project folder for the product.
    2. Create bins for video, audio and assets inside the project.
    3. Import the downloaded clip and drag it onto the timeline.
    4. Keep visual tracks above the divider and audio tracks below it.
    5. Place extra footage such as B-roll on higher visual tracks.
  • The selection tool grabs and moves clips on the timeline. The razor tool cuts a clip where you click. Its shortcut is C in Premiere Pro and X in DaVinci Resolve.
  • Effect Controls for a clip include position (left and right, up and down), scale, rotation, opacity or visibility, and clip speed. Adjust these per clip in the Effect Controls panel.
  • Scrubbing means dragging the playhead along the timeline to move back and forth through footage. This is how you review and find cut points.
  • Duplicate a text layer:
    1. Select the text layer.
    2. Hold Alt on Windows or Option on Mac.
    3. Drag the layer out to create a copy.
    4. Repeat to build a set of text layers, then edit each one's wording and position.
  • Essential Sound panel (Premiere's audio helper): use it to set each audio clip's type, such as Dialogue, Background music or Sound effects, and apply the matching preset (ready-made setting) for balanced voice levels.
    • Set a voiceover clip to Dialogue and choose the preset "balanced male voice" to clean up and balance the recording. Adjust the volume afterwards if needed.
    • Lower the clip volume first if the audio is too loud.
    • Keep settings basic. Adjust clip volume, and turn reverb (a room echo effect) or echo off if they are not needed.
  • Tick-tok style text overlay: add a text layer with Open Sans Bold (a font, which is a style of letters) and adjust the line height (the space between lines of text) in the text settings. Place the text with the Vector Motion position controls (Premiere's position settings) so it sits clear of the product.
  • A fast zoom transition can be added from the Effects panel using a zoom blur preset, placed between two clips. Third-party presets (add-ons from other makers) such as Film Impact or Premiere Composer provide more zoom transitions.
  • Create a zoom-in keyframe:
    1. Select the clip and open Effect Controls > Transform.
    2. Move the anchor point onto the product or subject you want to zoom towards.
    3. Set a keyframe on Scale at the start of the move.
    4. Move the playhead forward and change the Scale value to zoom in, which creates a second keyframe.
    5. Check the motion in playback and adjust the anchor point if the subject drifts off centre.
  • Right-click a keyframe and choose Ease In or Ease Out to create smooth speed curves (ease means a slow start or slow end). Use the speed graph for custom curves when a preset is not enough.

DaVinci Resolve

1 min
  • DaVinci Resolve by Blackmagic Design is free and includes most of the functions of Premiere Pro, making it a budget option for video editing.
  • It covers most functions of Premiere Pro for basic editing work and is a budget-friendly option for beginners.
  • It is a free desktop editor with a layout similar to Premiere Pro. It has a folder section for organising media and drags clips onto a timeline, but its shortcuts differ.
  • The preset "balanced background music" applies a finished music mix once you place a music track.

Splice, InShot, Edits, iMovie and TikTok's Editor

1 min

Splice

1 min
  • Splice is a phone editing app (it also works on a computer). It is used for cutting, voiceovers (recorded narration), speed changes, slow motion and pulling out sounds. Splice is preferred over TikTok's editor for product videos.
  • Cost: about $12 per month. One statement says $12 per year, so verify the price in the app store.
  • Splice is used to edit TikTok ads. It matches on-screen text (words shown on the video) to speech, and it adds non-copyrighted sounds (sounds you can use without a copyright owner's permission) before upload.
  • Splice is a mobile (phone) and desktop (computer) editing app. The advice was to edit on a phone and send the file to a computer.
  • Splice works better on iPhone because TikTok allows higher-quality uploads from iOS (Apple's phone system). Uploads from Android (Google's phone system) are limited. Android phones are still viable.

InShot

1 min
  • InShot is a mobile (phone) video editor. It is used to edit creator content (videos made by content creators) before uploading it to social platforms.
  • InShot is not tied to any single platform, so its exports (the finished saved files) are easier to reuse elsewhere. Use InShot for content that goes to several platforms.
  • Platform-native text tools (text tools built into each app) in Instagram, TikTok and YouTube Shorts add overlay text (words placed on top of the video) in each platform's own fonts and style. This happens when the video is uploaded.

Edits (Instagram)

1 min
  • Edits is a separate mobile video editing app made by Instagram. It is Instagram's counterpart to CapCut (a popular video editor). It is a standalone download, not a feature inside the regular Instagram app.
  • Edits offers more editing options than Instagram's built-in reel editor (the editor inside Instagram for short vertical videos called reels). These include more fonts and animations. Edits removes many of the built-in editor's limitations. The built-in reel editor is still more restricted.
  • The Edits app has five main tabs (sections you switch between): Ideas, Reels, Edits, Record and Inspiration.
    • Inspiration is opened with the plus icon at the bottom of the screen.
  • The Reels tab shows videos similar to your own, or videos the app recommends you produce.
  • The Edits tab is the project gallery (your list of work). It holds current projects and drafts (unfinished versions).
    • Projects can be sorted by date created or alphabetically.
  • Recording inside the Edits app allows up to 10 minutes per clip.
  • How Edits' export metadata (hidden information saved in a file, such as how it was made) behaves after re-uploading to other platforms is unknown. Use InShot for content that goes to several platforms.

iMovie

1 min
  • iMovie covers basic cuts and transitions (the effects between two clips) for simple video edits.
  • iMovie is enough for basic cuts, cropping and simple transitions on UGC (user-generated content, videos made by ordinary people or customers) or repurposed footage (old footage reused in a new video).
    • Use a separate online editor when a clip must be converted to vertical format (tall, phone-screen shape) for social placements (the spots in social apps where an ad appears).
  • Online editors are used to convert formats, for example making a video vertical for ads.

TikTok's Built-In Editor

1 min
  • TikTok's built-in editor is fast for quick trims (cutting off the start or end) and small fixes. It does not re-render (rebuild) a full project.
  • If TikTok's text-to-speech timing (when the computer voice speaks) jumps around and will not stay in place:
    • Restarting the phone did not fix it.

Voiceover and Text-to-Speech

ElevenLabs

1 min
  • ElevenLabs is an AI voice tool. It converts a script (the written text) into speech in a chosen voice and supports multilingual output (more than one language).
    • Non-English languages need the multilingual option.
    • Several text-to-speech tools (tools that read written text aloud) were compared. ElevenLabs sounded the most natural to the creator.
  • ElevenLabs generates AI voiceovers that can be added to short video creatives (short ads). Use it when you need narration without recording your own voice.
  • ElevenLabs is used to make the voice in ads AI-generated.

    1. Choose a voice.
    2. Optionally change the voice settings.
    3. Check the languages offered under the multilingual option.
    4. Paste the prepared text.

  • Before producing audiobooks or other long audio for a store, check how many characters (letters, spaces and symbols the tool counts) or words the text-to-speech plan includes.
    • Choose the subscription tier (price level of a plan) so the full text can be converted without running out of allowance.

AI Video, Avatar and Script Tools

Scripts and Images

1 min

Avatar Animation

Video Generators

1 min

    • They can create ads from a brand website, analyse ads, or research ads in public ad libraries (public collections where anyone can see ads).
    • Some allow limited customisation (changes).
  • Pre-built platforms can replace whole traditional roles, such as a designer or manual static ad production (making still image ads by hand). They work without custom instructions.
    • Choosing which platform to use, or whether to use one, requires knowing which are best and how to prompt them (how to write the instructions you type in).
  • Pre-built platforms work fairly similarly. Trying one for a month is a reasonable test before committing (paying for a long plan).

Graphics, Canva and Static Assets

    • The thumbnail (a small preview picture), using its thumbnail templates (ready-made designs)

  • Re-download AI-generated ads before uploading to Meta (the company behind Facebook and Instagram):
    • Upload AI-generated ads from ChatGPT or Claude to Canva, and re-download them before uploading to Meta.
    • This removes the metadata (hidden data) that Meta reads to detect AI-generated content. This prevents potential CPM penalties (CPM is the cost per 1,000 ad views, so penalties raise it).
    • It takes 2 minutes and has no downside.
  • Use Photoshop or Illustrator (professional design programs) with an Envato Elements library (a subscription library of ready-made design files). Use this when you need graphics and effects beyond Canva.

Converting Video to GIF with ezgif

1 min
  • ezgif.com, or any search result for "video to GIF converter", converts video clips to GIF format (a short looping animated picture).
    • Upload the video.
    • Select the time range.
    • Adjust quality and size.
    • Download.
  • Procedure:
    1. Go to ezgif.com.
    2. Click Upload Video.
    3. Select start and end time (in seconds or milliseconds).
    4. Click Convert to GIF.
    5. If the file size is too large, click Optimize and reduce quality (for example from quality 100 to 120).
    6. Check that quality is acceptable and not pixelated (blocky).
    7. Click Save or Download to save the GIF file.
  • Typical file reduction: 3.49 MB reduced to 1.27 MB by quality adjustment.

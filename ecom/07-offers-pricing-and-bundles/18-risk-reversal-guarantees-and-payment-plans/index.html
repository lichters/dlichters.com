Risk reversal, guarantees and payment plans

Section 7.18 · 18 of 28 in this chapter · 22 min read

In short

  • Risk reversal means the seller takes on the risk of a purchase, so the buyer feels safer and buys more.
  • A money-back guarantee works best when it is clear, strong, and has no hidden conditions.
  • A price-match guarantee is safe only for products whose suppliers force every seller to use the same price (MAP). Otherwise, do not offer it.
  • Before you set a return fee or a return window, check your supplier's return policy and your own profit left after costs.
  • Check your store's real limits for Shop Pay and other payment options before you show them to buyers.
More key points
  • Show a small monthly payment instead of the full price, and use this carefully.
  • Test a new payment option for one month against the old one before you keep it.
  • Measure how many buyers pick each price level, and test guarantee length against how many visitors buy.

Risk reversal: the principle and the decision rule

2 min
  • Risk reversal means moving the risk of a purchase from the customer to the seller.
  • Buyers are risk-averse, which means they dislike the chance of losing money.
  • If all the risk stays with the buyer, conversion (the share of visitors who buy) goes down.
  • Risk reversal can take the form of a returns policy (rules for sending items back).
  • It can also be a price-match guarantee (a promise to match a lower price elsewhere).
  • It can also be another satisfaction guarantee.
  • It can also be a worry-free installation or pre-assembled delivery for products that need setup.
  • A money-back guarantee is the strongest offer element.
  • The main reason buyers hesitate is perceived risk, meaning the feeling that something could go wrong.
  • A money-back guarantee makes any offer more attractive.
  • This only works if the supplier (the company that ships the product for you) is reliable and product quality is good.
  • Treat risk reversal as a must-have for making a lot of money in high-ticket dropshipping.
  • High-ticket means expensive products. Dropshipping means a supplier ships the product for you.
  • The customer must be protected if something goes wrong.
  • Place the guarantee after the value stack.
  • A value stack is the list of everything the buyer gets, with what each item is worth.
  • First build the value.
  • Then remove the risk with a money-back guarantee or another risk reversal.
  • Example of another risk reversal: a statement that it costs nothing if the buyer is not satisfied.
  • The guarantee then reinforces a strong offer.
  • Make the guarantee the main headline (the biggest line at the top of the page) when the guarantee is strong and the offer is clear.
  • A guarantee boosts response, meaning how many people reply or buy.
  • The better the guarantee, the better the response.
  • Strong guarantees outperform weak ones.
  • Some marketers claim guarantees are outdated.
  • Strong guarantees still outperform weak ones, even so.
  • If you make a big claim, the guarantee must have no caveats (hidden conditions or fine print).
  • A lowest-price guarantee should exclude only things like secondhand products.
  • Every extra rule or state-specific exception (a rule that applies only in some US states) makes the whole offer look weak.
  • A guarantee is more convincing when it comes from someone the buyer knows and trusts.
  • Example: a local dealer (a nearby shop or seller) who signs a written pledge to refund.
  • A guarantee from a distant, unknown company is less persuasive.
  • Guarantees are not limited to products.
  • They can cover service levels, such as lowest fare, maximum downtime (how long a service may stop working), or a fixed meal service time.

Trial periods and free examination

1 min
  • A trial period with a refund, such as three to four days or one week, lowers the buyer's risk.
  • It raises perceived value.
  • Offer it only when the business can afford the refunds.
  • Design the terms to keep the refund rate (the share of orders refunded) as low as possible.
  • Offer a free examination period (time to try the product before paying) of 6 days, one week, 10 days or 30 days.
  • The free period comes before the buyer pays.
  • The seller pays shipping both ways, so returning the product costs nothing.
  • Make clear that the buyer owes nothing unless he keeps the product.
  • A free trial with a return window means the buyer can send the product back within a set time.
  • Example: a full refund within 90 days.
  • This turns the guarantee into a no-risk trial.
  • A 100-day free trial or lifetime warranty can work for furniture-type brands.

  • Avoid it for a stingy customer base (buyers who are unwilling to spend).

  • Do not ask for a purchase up front.

Price match and price guarantees

1 min

The MAP rule

1 min
  • Suppliers that enforce minimum advertised price (MAP, the lowest price a shop may advertise) forbid resellers from selling below a set price.
  • Because of this, a price guarantee is low-risk.
  • Competitors cannot legally undercut you (sell cheaper).
  • They rarely offer a lower price.
  • Most online shoppers do not know that dropship suppliers enforce MAP.
  • MAP requires all authorized retailers (approved sellers) to advertise at the same price.
  • Because customers do not know this, they compare prices across shops, looking for discounts that legally cannot exist.
  • This is why a price guarantee is a powerful conversion tool.
  • It appears to offer pricing assurance, which is genuinely true because of MAP.
  • It creates almost no refund liability (little risk of having to pay money back).
  • Price matching works with MAP products because all sellers keep the same price.
  • With non-MAP products, a competitor can cut prices by a large amount at any time.
  • You would be forced to match a price where no margin is left.
  • A non-MAP competitor can cut its price by as much as 30% within a week.
  • A price-match promise can force you to sell with no margin.
  • Use price-match guarantees only when all sellers are bound by the same MAP.
  • The same MAP keeps prices equal everywhere.
  • A price-match promise only makes sense in MAP markets, where sellers keep one enforced price.
  • Matching costs little there.
  • Outside MAP, the same promise forces you to match prices on products you would sell at a loss.

  • Customers could ask you to match prices on products where you would lose money.
  • So the policy can only hurt you.

  • Keep the installment terms visible at the price point and at checkout (the payment step), so the frame is not a surprise.

Testing and decision steps

1 min

Testing a payment option

Procedure:

  1. Run the Shop Pay vs. Klarna test for one month. Shop Pay is Shopify's fast checkout, which can split payments. Klarna is a separate pay-later service.
  2. Review the last three months of sales and note the Klarna transaction count (the number of paid Klarna orders).
  3. Remove Klarna from the site by disabling the payment method (the way buyers pay).
  4. Enable Shop Pay installments.
  5. Run for exactly one month.
  6. After the month, compare: did Shop Pay transactions exceed, match or fall below the Klarna baseline (the starting number you compare against)?
  7. Decide whether to keep both, switch, or revert (go back to the old setup).

Testing a fee or price level

  • Measure uptake (how many buyers choose each option) at each level.
  • Test guarantee length (how long the money-back promise lasts) against conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy).
  • Guarantee length is an offer element that can be measured.

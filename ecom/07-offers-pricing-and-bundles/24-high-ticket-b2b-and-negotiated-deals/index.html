Playbook › 7. Offers, pricing and bundles

High-ticket, B2B and negotiated deals

Section 7.24 · 24 of 28 in this chapter · 11 min read

In short

  • Judge an expensive product by the profit you keep after all costs, not by its price.
  • Before a big sale, build trust with reviews, a phone number and a real person to talk to.
  • Do not give business buyers a big discount up front. Start from your normal discount and negotiate.
  • Give each business buyer a personal discount code, with a limit so others cannot use it.
  • Test deposits and offers on your store and measure the results instead of guessing.
This section has 21 parts. Tap one to open it.

Deciding whether high-ticket and B2B fits your store

1 min
  • High-ticket means an expensive product. Judge it by its margin, the profit you keep after shipping (freight), returns and damage. A higher price alone does not guarantee profit.
  • Above about 90 to 100 USD in the US market, your store, ads and offer must build trust before the sale. Buyers read reviews and search the brand before they buy at this price.
  • A high-ticket product with healthy margin leaves room for a deeper discount when you need one to close a larger deal. A discount must never cut the sale below a worthwhile profit.
  • B2B means business-to-business, where you sell to companies. Bulk orders from businesses can get a larger discount and be accepted at a lower margin, because total profit still stays high.
  • Lead-generation example (collecting contact details of people who are interested): mobile-only lead generation worked up to about $997 per offer. A $30,000 program converted through mobile leads. The advertiser found no limit on mobile above that point, and a desktop (computer) was not required.

Concierge, VIP and trade access

1 min
  • A concierge (a personal helper) or VIP (special, top-level) service can be your USP (unique selling point, what makes you different).
  • This works especially well in B2B niches (small markets of business buyers). Customers placing very large orders value personal contact and custom quotes (prices made just for them).
  • Such orders can range from $10,000 to $100,000 or more.
  • Pattern: tell visitors on the site that a real person is available to talk.
  • Then give custom quotes and work with them directly on large orders. This fits B2B niches, where single orders can reach $10,000, $50,000 or $100,000.
  • Create a separate trade page (a page for business buyers) on the store. Business buyers often want bulk discounts and negotiation (back-and-forth on price). Be ready to offer custom deals.
  • Show a phone number on the trade page so businesses can call directly. Discounts depend on order volume (how much they buy), and custom quotes are available.

Procedure A: trade request with supplier quote

1 min
  1. Add a trade or business-to-business form page (a page where companies send requests) to your store.
  2. Collect the customer's name, email, phone number, company name, products of interest, quantity, and the date they need the order by.
  3. Request a quote from the supplier (the company that provides your products) for that exact quantity, delivery location and date.
  4. Pass the supplier's quoted price to the customer. Do not promise a trade discount upfront, because it varies from order to order.

Procedure B: trade pricing instead of a fixed coupon

1 min

A coupon is a discount code. Here you price each trade request on its own instead.

  1. Create a page on the store aimed at trade buyers, not at a product.
  2. List known business clients on the page.
  3. Add a contact form asking for name, phone, email, products of interest, units per product and purchase timeline (how soon they will buy).
  4. When a request arrives, ask your supplier for the best wholesale price (the bulk price) on those quantities, then send the buyer a quote.

Example

1 min
  • A trade buyer asks for 10 units of item one and 10 units of item two. Ask the supplier for the best wholesale price on exactly that order, and quote the buyer a price for that order only.

Procedure C: trade page with volume-based discounts

1 min
  1. Create a page for business customers and link it from the footer menu (the menu row at the bottom of every page).
  2. Explain that discounts are based on order volume and that custom quotes are available.
  3. Show a phone number so businesses can call directly.
  4. Embed a contact form (a form placed inside the page) that asks for name, company name, phone number, email, products of interest and quantities.

Quoting prices to prospects

1 min

A prospect is a possible buyer.

  • Discuss price and delivery terms only after you know the prospect wants what you sell and other issues are worked out. Confirm the product fit first, then address price.
  • Do not let price questions replace the discovery process (finding out what the buyer really needs).
  • A budgetary quote (a rough estimate) is based on expected production quantity, order size and other variables that often change before ordering.
  • It can be prepared with little effort for a prospect who needs early pricing. But first evaluate the probability of winning his business (how likely you are to get the sale).
  • If the product meets the prospect's criteria, do not give a precise price for every quantity or option before he has committed to buy.
  • Detailed quotes take the estimating team's time (the staff who calculate prices). They rarely pay off when the prospect has not committed.

Net saving formula for a new packaging or process

1 min
  • Labor savings + storage savings = maximum extra material cost the prospect will accept. Labor means paid work time, and storage means the cost of keeping goods.
  • If the material price stays below that amount, the net (after-all-savings) overall cost is lower and he can commit to an order.

Example: concrete price test

1 min
  • A prospect wants extra advertising value on the outer packaging. The packaging costs about one dollar more per unit.
  • Ask whether he will buy if the net cost stays in line with his current spending.
  • This commitment is needed before any numbers are prepared.

Volume-based business discounts

1 min
  • Business discounts should depend on order volume.
  • Very large orders, such as those over 50,000, can justify deeper incentives than orders around 2,000.
  • Do not give a large discount upfront in B2B deals.
  • Start from your default discount (your normal rate) and negotiate from there. The size depends on margin and order size.
  • Example: one large B2B order of about $66,000 to $67,000 received a 15% discount after negotiation.
  • A few orders of this size per year can add significant revenue (money coming in) and profit.
  • B2B and bulk-buyer discounts can use any agreed percentage, such as 2%, 5%, 10% or 12%, set per negotiated deal.
  • The 12% level was used as the example for a large order.
  • Discounts shown on the standard income line (the normal sales report figures) do not include custom quotes made through draft orders (orders you build by hand before the customer pays).
  • Large stores with bulk buyers may give steep negotiated discounts that never appear in the discount line. So check draft orders separately.

Personalised discount codes for business buyers

1 min
  • A reusable discount code (a code customers type at checkout to get a price cut) named after the business suits an ongoing account.
  • Example: an interior designer who buys for clients. (2025.)
  • A code limited to one use in total suits a unique offer to a single buyer. Nobody else can redeem it once used.
  • This protects the agreed price from being shared or reused.

Procedure: personalised discount for one B2B buyer

1 min
  1. Click Create discount and choose Amount off order.
  2. Name the discount code after the buyer, for example the buyer's first name.
  3. Set the value type to percentage and enter the agreed rate, for example 12%.
  4. Set maximum discount uses to once per customer.
  5. Under customer eligibility (who may use the code), choose specific customers and enter the buyer's email address.
  6. Set the end date to 24 hours from now.
  7. Click save discount and send the code to the buyer.

Template: personal discount email

1 min 
Hi [CUSTOMER NAME],

I have created a personal discount code for you: [DISCOUNT CODE]. You can apply it at checkout on our store to get [DISCOUNT PERCENTAGE]% off your order.

This discount is valid until [END DATE AND TIME, TIME ZONE], so please place your order before then.

If you have any questions, just reply to this message.

Deposits for custom and made-to-order products

1 min

A deposit is a part payment made up front. Made-to-order means the product is built after the customer orders.

  • Taking a deposit on a custom-built or made-to-order product is common practice. It is similar to construction or home building.
  • Deposit size matters.
  • A large deposit, such as 50% or 60%, may discourage buyers.
  • The deposit is non-refundable (they cannot get it back), so buyers fear losing it.
  • A small deposit, such as 20%, lowers the barrier (the hurdle) to committing.
  • But a small deposit raises the chance that customers back out, leaving the store with a built product.
  • State clearly that deposits on custom orders are non-refundable, in the checkout or product wording, before the order starts building.
  • Test the deposit model on the store by offering a few options. Measure which gets more interest, rather than relying on one theory.
  • Options include split payment (paying in parts), pre-order (ordering before the stock exists) and deposit schemes.
  • Apps exist for pre-selling and pre-ordering.

Negotiated offers and make-an-offer promotions

1 min

Make an Offer app on the store

1 min
  • Procedure:
    1. Install the Shopify app (the store software you use) called Make an Offer.
    2. Set the promotion to run for a fixed window, for example three days.
    3. A Make an Offer button appears under Add to Cart (the button that puts an item in the shopping basket).
    4. The shopper chooses an offer price and enters an email address and phone number.
    5. You accept the offer, counter with a higher price, or decline it. You are never forced to accept.
  • Example on a 3,000 dollar product: offers within about 10 percent below the list price (the normal shelf price) were accepted.
  • Offers around 20 percent below were countered (answered with a higher price). Expect offers at both levels when you run this.

Make-offer promotion by email for a few days per month

1 min
  • Procedure:
    1. Email the list (your group of subscribers) and run Facebook, Instagram and Google remarketing ads (ads shown again to people who already visited) to long-standing audience members.
    2. Announce that offers are accepted for a stated number of days.
    3. Customers reply to the email or send a private message on Facebook with offers on specific products.
    4. Accept, counter or decline each offer. No offer has to be accepted.
    5. Shopify App Store apps (apps sold in Shopify's app shop) can add a make-an-offer button. But manual handling (doing it by hand) is recommended for the first attempt.
  • Mistake: the make-offer approach takes a lot of time, because someone must respond to every offer.
  • It can still produce many extra sales. So the team needs capacity to answer offers during the promotion window (the time the offer runs).
  • A make-an-offer or name-your-price email invites subscribers to propose a price. You accept or counter each reply.
  • This tactic runs only through email, not ads. It is risky for large lists.

Make-an-offer email procedure (2025)

1 min
  1. Send an email to the list inviting customers to make an offer or name their price on a product they want.
  2. Ask them which products they are interested in and how much they would pay.
  3. Accept offers that are fair and reply to the rest with a counter price.
  4. Use this for promotions that are not public (not shown to everyone). It carries risk if the list is large.
  • Mistake: a site-wide make-an-offer button (a button on every page of the store) was removed after it caused problems with Google.
  • Keep make-an-offer offers in email instead of a public button on the store.

Dealers, free goods and referral credit

1 min

Dealers are shops or agents who resell your products.

  • Avoid extra discounts or loading offers (offers that push extra stock onto dealers) to dealers.
  • Dealers often shift sales between stores rather than adding total demand.
  • Spend that budget on acquiring new customers instead.
  • Free goods (extra items given at no cost) given to dealers must be sold with advertising.
  • Each free case means the ads must sell proportionally more to earn the same return (money back from ads).
  • Figure the cost of free goods at your selling price before offering them.

Template: referral credit letter for an existing customer

1 min

A referral credit is an account that lets a customer pay later, because someone recommended them.

Dear [CUSTOMER_FIRST_NAME], [REFERRER_NAME] tells us that you are one of her good customers. She says you pay as agreed, so we have opened a credit account for you with us, ready for use at any time. When you want anything from our range, just place your order and pay nothing in advance. We are glad to send it without any further investigation, since you were recommended to us in this way.
  • Keep the recommendation as the reason for the account.
  • Adjust the names and offer to match your product and terms.

Where sources differ

1 min
  • Site-wide make-offer button (a button on every page): an app can place a Make an Offer button under Add to Cart for a fixed window, such as three days.
  • A later source reports that a site-wide button was removed after it caused problems with Google. It says offers should run through email.
  • Use the app only for a short, monitored window (one you watch closely).
  • Use email for the default channel (the normal way to run offers).
  • Discount code use limit: a reusable code named after the business suits an ongoing account.
  • A single-use code suits a unique one-off offer.
  • The personal-code procedure sets the limit to once per customer. That is a per-person limit, not a total limit.
  • Upfront discount size: a large discount should not be given upfront.
  • Depth of discount can rise when margin and order size support it. It can be used to close a larger deal.
  • Trade buyers should not be promised a trade discount before the supplier quote is known.
  • Margin: high-ticket products are judged by the margin left after all costs.
  • Bulk orders may be accepted at a lower margin only when total profit still stays high.
  • Trade request data: Procedure A asks for quantity, delivery location and date. It then requests a supplier quote for that exact order.
  • Procedure B asks for products, units per product and purchase timeline. It then asks the supplier for the best wholesale price on those quantities.
  • Use the fuller data set for large orders where delivery terms affect the supplier price.

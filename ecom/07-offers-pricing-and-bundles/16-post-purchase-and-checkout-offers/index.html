Post-purchase and checkout offers

Section 7.16 · 16 of 28 in this chapter · 19 min read

In short

  • Show an extra offer right after the buyer pays. It adds money without slowing down the first purchase.
  • Set up your offers before you build the funnel. The later steps depend on them.
  • Check every upsell price against your bundle prices. Unclear prices cause refunds and chargebacks.
  • Test the same product at a discount first. It often sells best.
  • Show an extra offer right after the buyer pays, while they are still excited.
More key points
  • Make the offer time-limited ("today only") and say that it disappears when the time ends.
  • For unfinished carts, a discount of about 10% works better than a big discount.
  • Test every offer every day and compare how much money each one earns per view.

Core principles of post-purchase offers

2 min
  • A post-purchase upsell is an extra offer shown after checkout is complete. Because the buyer has already paid, the offer adds no friction to the first purchase. ReBuy presents it as a risk-free way to raise revenue from orders that are already placed.
  • Post-purchase upsells raise average order value (AOV, the average amount each order brings in). This matters most when margin is low, for example around 25 USD per order. Margin is the profit left after costs.
  • Even a 5 USD increase per order adds up over many orders.
  • Customer acquisition cost (CAC, what you pay to win one buyer) is already paid on the first purchase. So it can be removed from the margin calculation for upsells and cross-sells. A cross-sell is a related, different product offered to the buyer.
  • Lead Value Optimization (LVO, raising what each visitor is worth) raises the value of every visitor. It offers related products and services right after a purchase.
  • LVO offers can come from your own catalog. They can also come from other companies. Those are affiliate products (products you promote and earn a commission on).
  • Configure LVO offers first, before you build the post-purchase funnel. The upsell and related-product sequences depend on them.
  • Treat LVO as a prerequisite (a step you must finish first) for the post-purchase layer. It is not an optional add-on.
  • A modern sales funnel (the path a buyer walks from first click to payment) keeps selling while the buyer is still in the cart, before they leave the site.
  • Each accepted upsell can be followed by another upsell. So every purchase builds on the one before.
  • One-time offers (deals shown only once) and limited-time buy-now offers are a support tactic. They are not the main focus of a store's funnel strategy.
  • Always have an upsell ready. Show one or two upsells right after a purchase, on the same page, before the buyer leaves.
  • This is the simplest way to earn extra money from buyers who have already decided to pay.
  • An upsell is a bigger or better offer. A downsell is a cheaper offer shown after the buyer says no. Upsell and downsell products must relate to the main product or belong to the same niche (a narrow market).
  • Then customers see them as natural add-ons to what they just bought.
  • **** For a low-priced product, plan a pipeline (a set path of pages and offers) on your own website. Customers are upsold to higher-priced items there.
  • Plan the upsell path before traffic arrives.

Offer count and structure

1 min
  • Each product should have at least one upsell.
  • Two to three upsells are standard for typical conversion (the share of visitors who buy).
  • Up to four upsells can be added for more aggressive offers.
  • Make the first upsell the same product the customer bought. Offer it at a lower price point (the price level).
  • Show the first upsell right after checkout as a one-click addition (the buyer adds it with one click).
  • Buyers accept congruent add-ons (add-ons that closely match what they bought) more readily. They do this while they are still deciding.
  • Shopify (an online store platform) allows at most two levels of post-purchase upsells on checkout.
  • Conflict on the number of levels: the general structure allows up to four upsells. The Shopify checkout limit is two levels.
  • Use the two-level pattern where the Shopify checkout is the delivery point. Longer chains apply where your setup allows them.

Choosing the upsell product

1 min
  • Make the first upsell your highest-value product that fits the buyer.
  • Judge upsells by the dollars they bring per offer shown. Do not judge by conversion rate alone.
  • Compare two upsells by revenue per offer shown (money earned each time the offer appears). Use this formula:
Revenue per offer shown = price × conversion rate
  • A $100 upsell must convert about five times as often as a $500 upsell. That brings in the same revenue per offer shown.
  • Offer upsells that are highly congruent with the first purchase.
  • They should enhance the product, deliver the result faster, or give a done-for-you (someone else does the work) or automated (it runs by itself) version of it.
  • The best first upsell is often more of the item the buyer already bought.
  • This is especially true for a consumable (something used up, like refills). Buyers who need that item to continue are the most likely to accept.
  • Miniature versions (small, cheaper copies) of the existing product work well.
  • Re-offering the same product also works well. Customers already know it.
  • Keep upsell price at or below 14 dollars.
  • The seller reports about 40 percent of buyers accept the upsell when it is combined with SMS (text messages), email and retargeting (showing ads again to past visitors).
  • Conflict on price level: the 14-dollar ceiling applies to the reported setup with SMS, email and retargeting.
  • Higher-ticket upsells (more expensive offers) that fit the buyer closely also convert. The case below shows this.
  • So judge price by revenue per offer shown.
  • Compare the upsell price before you publish it. Add up the total the customer would pay after accepting.
  • Then check that total against the bundle price (a discounted price for buying several units) on the product page for the same quantity.

Upsell and downsell sequences

1 min

Standard sequence

1 min
  1. Present the first upsell.
  2. If declined, present the first downsell (a lower-priced product from the same category).
  3. If the first downsell is declined, either present the second downsell or direct the customer to the Thank You page (the final page after purchase).
  4. If the customer accepts the first upsell, present the second upsell.
  5. If the second upsell is purchased, present the third upsell or direct the customer to the Thank You page.
  6. If the second upsell is declined, present the first downsell again, then direct the customer to the Thank You page.
  • A downsell is a cheaper alternative offered after a customer declines an upsell.
  • It keeps part of the sale and adds revenue at a lower price point.
  • It is used together with upsells to raise average order value.
  • Older (2021-2022).

Two-level pattern (Monster funnel)

1 min
  • Pattern: on accept, the first upsell goes to the second upsell.
  • On decline, the flow goes to the first downsell, then to the thank-you page.
  • So every path ends at the thank-you page.
  • Build the sequence in ReconvertUpsell (an upsell app):
    1. Set a trigger condition (the rule that starts the offer). For example, use product purchase data: a specific product (at least one item, or the entire order) with unit/item count equal to 1.
    2. First upsell: the same product at 15% off with a timer. On decline: the same product at 30% off (last chance).
    3. Second upsell on accept: a different product in the same niche, chosen via Shopify product recommendation or a selected item, with a discount.
    4. Thank-you page with recommended products, a share button and a collection section (a group of products).
    5. Enable the funnel under Settings > Checkout > Post-purchase page > select ReconvertUpsell.

Chained flow

1 min
  1. Initial offer: BOGO 25% off (buy one, get one at 25% off).
  2. If accepted, upsell a gift card worth 10% off.
  3. If declined, downsell to BOGO 50% off.
  4. If the gift card upsell is declined, show the same gift card offer at a higher discount.
  5. Each accepted or declined step can start a new upsell or downsell. So the flow keeps going as long as the customer accepts or declines.
  • Upsell after an accepted 25% BOGO offer: show a gift card worth 10% off.
  • Gift cards raise average order value.
  • Gift cards also encourage a second purchase, because the buyer has to spend the credit.
  • Downsell pattern: after a shopper declines the first offer, show the same type of deal at a deeper discount.
  • Frame it as a final chance.
  • Example 1: after a declined 15% bundle offer, show the same bundle at 30% off with the line Final offer, this price ends at checkout.
  • Example 2: after a declined free-shipping offer, show a 20% off code with the line One more chance before you leave.
  • Test sequence for a same-item offer:
    1. Make the first post-purchase offer the same product at a strong discount.
    2. If the customer accepts, offer the same product again at an even better price, or a different product. Split test (show two versions to different buyers and compare results) those two options.
    3. If the customer declines, offer the same product again at a bigger discount.
    4. Add a small app sell on the thank-you page.

Discounts and price checks

2 min
  • The highest conversion among post-purchase discounts came from offering the same product at 15-30% off.
  • The seller started with 15% for the first offer. The final decline offer was 30%.
  • Setting range: after checkout, show a post-purchase upsell with a discount on the same or a complementary product (one that goes well with it).
  • The example range is 20 to 40 percent off.
  • Add scarcity (limited supply or time, to create urgency). Use an upsell app to set it up.
  • Conflict on depth: the 15-30% range is the measured best in one store.
  • The 20-40% range is the general setting.
  • Start at 15%. Go to 30% for the final decline, as measured.
  • Widen toward 40% only with a tested reason.
  • Example: right after payment, show the same product or a complementary product at 50% off.
  • The buyer's payment details are already stored, so one click completes the purchase.
  • Discount ladder (steps of lower prices):
    • Second unit of the same product: about 25% off.
    • Three-unit bundle offer: about 35% off.
    • Final downsell: about 40% off.
  • Later steps can carry higher discounts. But each step must pass the price check against its matching bundle price.
  • Upsell price formula and check:
Upsell price = unit price × (1 − discount)
Upsell total = price already paid + upsell price

  • Example with illustrative figures (made-up numbers that show the math): a 40 USD unit at 25% off costs 30 USD.

  • 40 USD + 30 USD = 70 USD. This total must not exceed the 2-unit price on the product page.

  • Worked example for a 50 USD product in a post-purchase offer:

    • 25% off gives 37.50 USD.
    • 35% off gives 32.50 USD.
    • 40% off gives 30.00 USD.

  • Each deeper step must still be checked against the matching bundle price on the product page. Do this before the offer goes live.

  • Mistake: offers with unclear math confuse customers.

  • They see a price that does not match the product page bundle.

  • Then they complain by email, ask for refunds, or open bank disputes (chargebacks, where the bank takes the money back).

  • Each unchecked upsell price is a potential chargeback source.

  • After checkout, the buyer has already committed. So an add-on is an easy yes when its price is clearly better than the bundle price for the same quantity.

  • Multi-unit add-ons (more than one item) are the strongest offers at this point.

Same-item repurchase offers

1 min
  • A repurchase offer is an offer to buy the same item again.
  • Always test selling the same item at a discounted price in a post-purchase upsell first.
  • Same-item offers showed the highest conversion, even for expensive or heavy items.
  • One store averaged around $150 per order.
  • A same-item repurchase offer was reported to work for a store with an average order value near 150 USD and a heavy product.
  • Test a discounted second unit before you assume an expensive or heavy product will not be bought again.
  • Add a low-cost add-on around 5-10 USD, or a post-purchase offer.
  • After a customer has bought, offer the same item again at 20-30% off.
  • The customer is already acquired (already won as a buyer), so the offer is easier to accept.
  • Post-purchase offer with a countdown (a timer that counts down).
  • The countdown runs for about 10 minutes.
  • The second unit is framed as a gift for someone else.
  • The discount is such as 15% off.
  • The decline option is shown as a small text link.
  • Pairing a discount with a time limit, such as 30% off for 10 minutes, creates urgency.
  • Adding a countdown or time window to the offer page supports the discount.

Case: a $7 ebook with a chain of offers

1 min
  • The project started with a $7 ebook (a digital book), The 4 Foot Farm.
  • It was pushed with $15,000 to $20,000 of daily spend on Google Display Network (Google's network of banner ads on other websites).
  • At a $7 sale price, covering $15,000 a day would have needed 2,143 sales every day. That number was out of reach.
  • After checkout, the seller added a chain of one-click offers (each one a single click away):
    • A $9 print edition.
    • A $27 monthly seed subscription.
    • A $20 monthly newsletter.
    • A $497 physical growing kit that produces up to 100 pounds of food in a 4 by 4 foot space, with an optional $100 lifetime warranty.
  • Around 2% of ebook buyers bought the $497 kit. This raised average order value to $40 to $44.
  • At that order value, the daily break-even volume (the sales needed each day to cover costs) fell to 375 sales.
  • A similar ebook had equally strong front-end conversion (the share of visitors who buy the first product). But its upsells were less closely related. It never reached the spend level needed to scale.

Timing and channel

1 min

  • An upsell does not have to happen at checkout.

  • It can be offered right after the first purchase, or later when the customer returns. Plan for both timings.

  • Older (2021-2022).

  • If the checkout must stay short, place upsells and cross-sells in post-purchase email or SMS instead of inside the checkout flow.

  • This keeps the path to payment to one or two steps.

  • You can still offer additional products this way.

  • Before capturing payment (charging the card) for an upsell that ships from your own location:

    1. Email the customer and state the expected delivery time of 10 to 14 days.
    2. Ask the customer to confirm that they still want the order.
    3. Once the customer confirms, capture the payment.

  • Full upsell sequence on a store:

    1. The product page shows a comparison chart where a higher-end version fits.
    2. After Add to Cart, the replace-item pop-up (a small window that swaps one item for another) offers the upgrade.
    3. The cart shows either the original or the replaced item.
    4. At checkout, the customer is offered an extended warranty and intangible products (things with no physical form, such as digital items).
    5. The shipping upgrade is offered before the purchase is completed.
    6. After purchase, a phone call or message thanks the buyer and offers a relevant upgrade if it fits.

Free-plus-shipping and trial offers

2 min
  • Free-plus-shipping (the product is free, and the buyer pays only shipping) is the favorite offer for qualifying buyers.
  • Give a valuable product for free and charge only shipping.
  • This gets it into a new customer's hands. It also reveals which subscribers are also buyers.
  • It is the simplest entry offer for most physical or digital products.
  • Pattern: a free-plus-shipping offer charges only the shipping cost for the main product.
  • Paid ads aim for near-zero net acquisition cost (what it costs to win a buyer, after the shipping fee).
  • The brand recovers its margin later through upsells and add-on products sold to that customer.
  • Older (2021-2022).
  • Example: a $2 product with $2 in marketing cost is sold as free plus $6.95 shipping.
  • The $4 cost is covered by the shipping fee. So the campaign is cash-flow positive (money comes in faster than it goes out).
  • Setup: a common Zipify OCU setup (a one-click upsell app, see below) makes the main product free.
  • In that setup, shipping is set to whatever cost covers your CAC or profit target.
  • Example from a Zipify OCU setup: the main product was free with $13 shipping.
  • That produced +$7 revenue per visitor at a conversion rate of about 30%.
  • Conflict on shipping fee: the $6.95 example and the $13 example both use a free product with shipping fees.
  • The right fee is the one that covers your CAC or profit target for your own product.
  • About 30 percent of people who took a free-plus-shipping offer bought something else immediately.
  • So the next offer is the easiest sale.
  • Present a related product on the post-purchase page, or send buyers to the store.
  • Trial offer: charge a very low amount, most often 1 USD.
  • Bill the full price a few days later, unless the buyer cancels.
  • It forces the visitor to enter a credit card. So it proves they are a buyer.
  • It works best for digital downloads, software and membership sites, which need no shipping.
  • Post-purchase upsells in Zipify OCU may not display in multi-country setups. This happens unless the markets are configured correctly.
  • In the US, the upsell works well without this extra step.

Checkout add-ons

1 min

Bumps

1 min
  • Bumps are small add-on offers placed on the order form before checkout.
  • They work like impulse items at a supermarket checkout. They can change the transaction profitably.
  • Bumps are used to identify and sell to hyperactive buyers at the point of sale.

Tips

1 min
  • Customers may be willing to add a tip at checkout when they are happy with the store.
  • A tip field (a box for an optional tip) can be added as a default in the Shopify checkout.
  • Use this to test the tip idea.

Shipping protection

1 min
  • Shipping protection (an insurance-like add-on) is priced at 3 dollars per order in this Kaching Cart setup.
  • Its cart description reads as protection against the order being lost or damaged.
  • While testing a product, leave out shipping protection and the footer (the bottom strip of the page).
  • When scaling (growing sales), you may offer shipping protection as an opt-in choice. The buyer chooses to add it.
  • Customers should be able to decide whether to add it.

Warranties

1 min
  • Use a warranty (a promise to repair or replace) as the upsell on expensive items.
  • When the warranty is offered as a single checkbox at checkout, and the customer does not tick it, send an automatic email shortly after the purchase.
  • That email carries the warranty offer and a discount.
  • This works best when the item costs enough that the customer is likely to want protection.
  • At checkout, offer an extended warranty as an add-on the customer can select during the process.
  • Put the upsell offer with the highest chance of acceptance into the Shopify product upsell app.
  • Examples of such offers: an extended warranty, or expedited shipping (faster delivery) on expensive items. These go in as the post-cart offer (the offer shown after the cart).
  • Place other lower-priority add-ons on the product page as selectable options.

Shipping upgrades and priority options

1 min
  • Offer a shipping upgrade as a single radio button (a round option where only one can be picked).
  • An example is priority domestic shipping in 2 to 3 days.
  • The standard free shipping shows its average delivery time.
  • A post-purchase priority shipping or priority processing option can add extra margin.
  • Charge about 10 percent of the product price as a small fee.
  • For example, $2.95 to $3.95, depending on the price point.

Testing three upsell types

1 min

  • Test three post-purchase upsell options. Choose them based on the product, the offer and the entry point (the first product or offer that brings the buyer in).

    1. Direct upsell to the high-ticket item with a discount or warranty.
    2. Quantity increase, for example try 3 or 6 bottles instead of 1.
    3. Full-store discount for the first order. Send it immediately after the purchase with a short time limit (in the example, within the next hour).

  • In the example, the full-store discount performed best at the time.

Apps and setup

1 min
  • Upsell app setup: after checkout, add an upsell page that offers additional products at a discount.
  • Use the app AfterSell. This raises average order value.
  • Zipify OCU is a post-purchase upsell app.
  • Offer editor settings for upsells in the Source D setup:
    • Set Free Shipping as the default. In the offer editor, enable the Free Shipping option. Also enable the setting Hide shipping line if the shipping amount is 0.00, so no shipping line appears.
    • Upsell Product block: Recommendation Type can be Recommended Products (OCU) or a set product.
    • Autoselect variant (a version of a product, such as a size or color) chooses the variant automatically.
    • The purchase option can be One-time purchase or Subscribe and save (regular delivery at a discount).
    • Subscription section: you can hide the offer whenever the order includes subscription products.
    • You can turn on the subscription option.
    • Or show a subscription offer only when the customer first bought the product as a one-time purchase.
  • ReconvertUpsell: a funnel offer can be changed under the discounts and shipping section.
  • Set it as a percentage or a fixed amount per unit. Set the timer and the button text too.
  • Example button texts: Grab another for 15% off or Pay now.
  • Example offer line: It's not too late to grab another for 15% off.
  • The standard thank-you page after checkout shows order confirmation, shipment tracking and delivery updates.
  • A post-purchase offer page is inserted before it. So the buyer still reaches the order details after the offer.
  • Post-purchase offer pages render differently (look different) on desktop or laptop and on phones.
  • Preview and check the offer page on both before launch. The layout, buttons and visible deal can differ between the two.

Offer copy

1 min
  • A post-purchase upsell (an extra offer shown after the buyer has paid) appears right after checkout (the payment step) with a lower price and urgency copy (wording that pushes the buyer to act now). Buyers are still excited right after buying, so an add-on shown then is more likely to be accepted.
  • Use this template:
Before you go, today only: add [product name] to your order for [discounted price]. Once this window closes, this offer is gone.
  • A post-purchase upsell with a time limit, such as a before you go today only offer at a lower price, raises the buyer's excitement right after the purchase. The offer should state that the deal disappears after the limited time.
  • After the buyer completes a purchase, offer an additional product (one more item to add on) with today only copy. This makes the offer look cheaper and time-limited. It uses the buyer's momentum right after the decision.

Abandoned-cart offers

1 min
  • An abandoned cart is an order a shopper started but did not finish. Use the fourth abandoned-cart email for an offer, such as 10% off, while keeping the goal of completing the purchase.
  • If the cart is below the free shipping threshold (the minimum order total that unlocks free delivery), such as $100, offer free shipping to push it over.
  • Then suggest complementary products (items that go well with the first one) that can be added to the order.
  • For abandoned cart recovery (winning back an unfinished order), offer a discount of around 10 percent rather than a large discount. A moderate offer recovers the sale without training shoppers to wait for deep discounts.

Testing and measurement

1 min
  • A/B test (show two versions to different visitors and see which one performs better) post-purchase upsells every day.
  • A profitable upsell can cover the loss on the front-end sale (the first, main purchase), and everything after that is profit.
  • After a single-product purchase (the buyer bought only one item), a post-purchase offer accepted by only about 3 out of 100 buyers was treated as a low rate.
  • Measure the acceptance rate (the share of buyers who say yes) of each post-purchase offer separately so weak offers are visible.
  • Judge each upsell by revenue per offer shown (the money earned each time the offer appears on screen).
  • Before adding complementary products, test a discounted same-item offer first (the same product at a lower price).
  • Test the three upsell types: direct high-ticket (a costly product offered straight away), quantity increase (more units of the same item), and full-store discount (a discount on the whole store). Compare results per entry point (the page where the shopper starts).

Bonuses and free gifts

Section 7.12 · 12 of 28 in this chapter · 38 min read

In short

  • A bonus is a free extra that comes with a purchase. Use it to make the offer feel worth more, not to lose money.
  • Give a bonus only when the profit on the main product can pay for it.
  • Pick bonuses that buyers need anyway and that cost you little.
  • Keep free ebooks at 15 to 25 pages with real content. Never sell a rebranded ebook unless the licence allows it.
  • Free gifts make buyers add more to their cart.
More key points
  • Show the gift clearly on the product page and in the cart, with its dollar value.
  • Only promise gifts that arrive automatically after payment.
  • Pick gifts your buyer needs that cost you little, and check your profit margin.
  • Test different gifts and keep the one that sells most.

Types of bonuses

2 min
  • A bonus is a free item that comes with a purchase. Bonus offers come in four types: physical products (low-cost items customers need), digital products (online lessons, guides, e-books), expedited shipping upgrades (white-glove delivery), and extended warranties.
  • Physical product bonuses have historically given a stronger conversion lift (more visitors buying) than digital bonuses. Digital bonuses are covered under the digital versus physical comparison below.
  • A bonus is tied to a purchase trigger, such as spending above a threshold or buying a specific product. Physical bonuses are shipped goods, and digital bonuses are delivered online.
  • Bonus items can include free ebooks, video guides, styling tutorials, complimentary gifts, or storage boxes. They increase perceived value (how valuable the buyer feels the offer is) at minimal cost. They also make turning the offer down feel hard for customers.
  • Bonuses such as ebooks, video tutorials on how the product works, or videos on how it improves the buyer's life increase the value of an offer. Add them to the landing page (the first page a visitor sees) as part of the offer.
  • Free gifts, free upsells, installation services, and valuable content such as recipe books add perceived value. Consistently helpful free content throughout the buyer journey can build a higher perceived value than competitors. **
  • A free gift card can be the free gift when a store wants to add value to an order and encourage a larger purchase. It works as an upsell (a bigger or extra option offered at checkout) because the buyer gets extra value without a physical product from the supplier. Show the gift clearly on the offer image so the visitor sees what they get.
  • Add a premium that carries its own value, such as a one-year magazine subscription included with a set, so the same payment looks like more value. Name the premium's price or worth in the letter.
  • Gift with purchase from another range: buying one product gives a free second product from a different range. This is typically a smaller, high-margin item (one that leaves a lot of profit) that adds little weight, so shipping cost stays low.
  • Services the business already provides can be presented as bonuses at no extra cost to the seller.
  • White-glove delivery means the delivery company brings the product inside, unpacks it, removes all packaging, and positions it. It costs an extra $100-$150 per order.
  • Physical bonuses should be items the buyer would need anyway, such as a seasonal ingredient pack, a protectant for leather furniture, or a book shipped with the order (called drop-shipped, meaning the supplier ships it directly to the buyer).

Deciding whether a bonus pays

2 min
  • Plan bonus offers before you buy traffic (paid visitors). Build them into the sales funnel (the path from first click to purchase) as a proven way to raise sales.
  • Offer free gifts or bonuses only when the margin (profit left after costs) allows it. This fits a best-selling product with strong profit per sale in a competitive niche (a crowded market). Free gifts make sense when competitors do not offer them and the product margin covers the cost.
  • Example: a free bonus gift worth about $50 can make sense on a product that earns roughly $400 to $500 profit per sale in a competitive niche. Competitors do not offer it, and the gift drives extra orders. If the margin on the product is thin, do not offer a gift.
  • Include free gifts only when the margin still works after the gift cost. If a supplier charges state tax on shipments and this cannot be resolved, drop the free gifts. **
  • Keep the gift's cost low enough that it does not eat into the margin. You can raise the value the buyer sees by setting a higher price point (the price shown) for the bundle.
  • Bundling a low-cost item with the main product (a bundle is several items sold as one package), or adding a free gift that costs little, can raise perceived value and average order value (the typical amount spent per order). It keeps margin healthy, as long as the gift is relevant and the customer wants it.
  • The bonus strategy is worth more when product margin is high. On a $300 or $800 profit product, a $20 bonus is negligible and can be given freely.
  • Very low-priced add-ons whose shipping cost is close to their price should not be sold alone. Offer them as a free bonus gift with high-ticket (expensive) purchases, or sell them in multi-packs. **
  • Do not sell low-ticket (cheap) accessories on their own. If the margin allows, include them as bonus gifts with a main product instead. **
  • Decide between a free bonus and a cross-sell (a related second item sold alongside the first) by comparing the main product price with the price of the add-on. **
  • Cheap accessories on an expensive product should be listed as free bonuses on the product page. They do not need their own product pages. Example: a 5,000 USD bike with a reflector and water bottle holder. These cost about 30 USD retail and 15 USD to the seller. **
  • Gift bonuses reduce the need for price cuts. A free accessory that costs the store $25 but is valued at $200 can be given with every order. **
  • Competitor stores add a free bonus item, such as a guide ebook, to a bundle. Use a bonus to raise perceived value without changing the base price (the normal price).

Choosing gifts by cost and relevance

5 min
  • Pick bonus items that cost little for you but solve buyer problems, such as storage, styling, cleaning, or wearing advice. Low-cost items like a jewelry box or an AI-drafted ebook (written with AI tools) can carry a large share of the perceived value.
  • Bonus items should be inexpensive for you to offer, even if they look valuable to the buyer. Do not choose items with high wholesale costs (the price the supplier charges you) or ones that threaten your margins. Bonuses are incentives for customers to choose you, not revenue-builders.
  • Bonus items that customers would need anyway with the product are the best choice. Ideally the bonus comes from the same manufacturer, so it can be added to the supplier's order with no extra effort.
  • Physical bonus pairings that work: a book for spending over $50; free ingredients or free bottles with a starter kit; a free protectant with a leather sofa.
  • Physical bonus ideas: a homebrewing starter kit can include a free ingredient sample pack or free bottles if bought before a deadline. Leather sofas can include a free leather protectant shipped with every order during a few promotion days each month.
  • The bonus should be closely related to the product's use or benefit. An unrelated free ebook is weak. For example, a shower filter should get an ebook on skin care for sensitive skin, linked to hard-water skin problems.
  • A free gift that is unrelated to the main product but valued by buyers can make an offer hard to refuse. It also helps the store stand out from competitors. Pick a low-cost gift that buyers want, such as a shaker or water bottle.
  • Test whether the gift matches what the target buyer wants. Free packaging or free shipping insurance can be offered as a free-gift tab on some themes (store design templates). These are not always what buyers value.
  • Choose a gift item that matches what the buyer needs, such as a cleaning product for a sofa.
  • Choose a gift the buyer will value, such as a popular consumer electronic item unrelated to the store's products. Check that its cost fits the margin on the main sale.
  • Design bonuses specifically for the main offer. Do not throw them together. Each one should be valuable enough that the prospect would pay for it alone. Start by asking which bonuses would perfectly complement the main product and add enormous value to the offer.
  • Choose a promotion that feels valuable to customers and is still profitable. A free gift with high perceived value often moves more customers than a slightly larger money-off discount. **
  • A bonus gift with a perceived value of about $100, such as a leather desk mat, can persuade buyers to buy from your store instead of the supplier. Its cost to you is only the wholesale price of the gift. It works best when the supplier does not offer the same extra. **
  • Choose bonus items from the same supplier as the main product to avoid extra shipping costs. Bonus items from different suppliers arrive separately and add shipping. **
  • Add-ons do not need to come from the same supplier as the main product. Choose the add-on that is cheapest to buy and has the highest perceived value for the customer.
  • Add-on pricing: the add-on's real cost is irrelevant to the buyer. Only the stated value matters, so label it with a clearly higher value.
  • Cheap free gifts, such as a $1 item from AliExpress (an online marketplace selling from Chinese suppliers), can be used as a bonus to entice buyers. This works as long as the business keeps a profit margin.
  • Adding a complementary free item, for example socks with hoodies, or a free digital ebook, raises perceived value. It also allows a higher offer price, as long as the item is cheap to produce or obtain.
  • A free digital bonus, such as an ebook, can be added to every order as a zero-cost extra that makes the purchase more attractive. It works when the product margin is too thin for a bigger discount.
  • If a margin is too thin for a $20 gift, give something that costs nothing. Examples: a digital recipe e-book, a video tutorial, or a free online training course. Choose one that adds value for the customer.
  • Educational bonuses such as ebooks raise the perceived value of an offer. This is especially true in health and wellness, kids, and parenting niches. It can allow a slightly higher offer price while still giving the customer a feeling of extra value.
  • Example: a necklace offer gave the buyer one main product plus a second necklace of their choice for free. Most buyers chose a design linked to their home country. The offer raised perceived value while keeping the free item's cost low.
  • Physical bonuses should be items the buyer would need anyway. Free books were also used as a bonus on orders over $50 in one store.
  • Two other examples of bonus items that carry perceived value: a free trending product offered with any order before a deadline, and a bonus of a snorkel with a mask.
  • Bonus pairings that match the main product's use: a free snorkel with a scuba mask, a free carrying case with a camera lens, a free air pump with a $300 air mattress. The pump costs $30 to $40. No air mattress comes with one, so customers must buy it separately.
  • When a supplier includes a bonus item, such as a bag or accessory, show it prominently as a "Free gift" block with a picture and its value. This makes the included extra visible and increases the perceived value of the offer.
  • Free accessories bundled with a main product are generally acceptable if the accessories are also sold separately at MAP (minimum advertised price, the lowest price you may show publicly). Check each supplier's policy if in doubt. **
  • Bundle low-cost extras as free bonus gifts rather than raising the base price. This keeps the advertised price the same, so MAP is not violated. **

Digital bonuses and free ebooks

2 min
  • Digital bonus ideas: an e-book version of a guide given free for orders over $50; a free lesson, e-book, or downloadable video that matches what the customer needs; or a free extended trial of a related service through an affiliate partnership (another company pays you to send them customers), such as a treadmill store offering a weight-management program trial.
  • Digital bonus pairings: spend over $50 for a free eBook; buy a drum set for free online lessons; buy a treadmill for an extended free trial of a weight-loss program such as Weight Watchers.
  • Digital bonuses (online lessons, guides, e-books) underperform physical bonuses in conversion lift, based on practical experience. When using digital bonuses, partner with external instructors or platforms instead of creating your own content. This reduces cost and uses their expertise.
  • Example: a drum-set seller partnered with an online drum lesson platform to offer one or two free introductory lessons. The platform gains new customers, the store stands out, and the bonus has minimal or no cost to the store.
  • Example: treadmill sellers can offer physical bonuses such as a free water bottle, towels, or apparel. They can also offer digital bonuses such as a free meal plan or an e-book on running technique. Test which types customers value most.
  • Educational bonuses should be checked against the product. The best bonus is directly linked to the buyer's use or benefit.
  • A free ebook worth $20 with a purchase increases conversion and can be delivered automatically after the order.
  • A digital bonus such as an ebook was tested as extra value. It did not beat a relevant physical free gift in one store. Prefer a physical gift that is closely related to the main product and its customer problem.
  • Free digital add-ons such as guides or templates can be listed at a stated value and given free with purchase. This makes the gift feel worth more. Customers who missed the add-on earlier can be offered it free later as a reason to buy again.
  • Free digital gift pattern: add a simple free guide to the order to add value without cost. For example, make one with ChatGPT (an AI chatbot) and a couple of images. Some customers may pay a small price for the guide. After that, the offer can be given free to previous buyers.
  • A free digital ebook that costs nothing to create can justify a $5.99 higher price for a bigger bundle tier (a higher-priced option in a set). This increases perceived value at no cost.

Creating free ebooks

12 min

Scope and content

  • Keep the ebook short: 15-25 pages with 4-6 chapters of substantive, useful content. Each chapter must deliver real value. The ebook must contain no placeholder text (dummy filler words).
  • Ebooks cost almost nothing to deliver but noticeably raise perceived value. Customers feel they are getting expert information. The offer feels more complete, even if they never read the ebook.
  • Page count guidance: a free ebook should have around 15 to 30 pages to deliver perceived value. Five pages is too few. Show the page count on the landing page, for example "a 28-page PDF."
  • Choose an ebook topic that matches the product's niche (its small market group) and the buyer's problem. A dog product gets a dog health guide. A fitness product gets a home workout plan. A sleep product gets a sleep guide.
  • The ebook should read as a thoughtful gift, not a sales pitch. Use a warm, direct voice, short paragraphs, subheadings, and bullet points where they help readability.
  • Filler chapters and placeholder text make the bonus look cheap and weaken the offer. Every chapter should be useful on its own.
  • Ebook bonus titles should match the product's buyer. For a beauty product, a skincare guide such as "Five-Step Skincare Routine For Dry Skin" fits. For a posture product, a habits guide such as "6 Daily Habits For Sitting Taller At Work" fits.
  • For a book seller, a good free gift is a short e-book that teaches the reader how to create an e-book. Offer it as a bonus on the Buy 2 and Buy 3 bundles.
  • Make the ebook about how to properly use or optimise (improve the use of) the main product. This justifies higher bundle prices and increases the perceived value of the tier.
  • In a free-gift ebook, place a short tracker or overview of the whole challenge before the day-by-day breakdown. For example, a 7-day reset map. The reader knows the full path before the detailed steps begin.
  • Example of a challenge ebook step: before bed, take a small flashlight and a small mirror. Spend about 30 seconds on three checks: the gumline where teeth meet gums, the back of the tongue for any white or yellow coating, and the gaps between the back teeth. Note one simple observation only, without changing your routine.
  • A short usage guide for a product that buyers may ask about can be made as follows.
    1. Find a store that sells the same product and has a strong product description.
    2. Use that description to understand the product's features and how it is used.
    3. Write and design a PDF of three to four pages that explains how to use the product.
    4. Deliver the PDF to buyers automatically, with a delivery app or a download link in the order confirmation.
    5. Prefer creating the guide yourself over buying Amazon seller material to reuse. Your own version is safer and more specific.
  • Adding a small, unannounced free gift to the parcel costs almost nothing if it is printed cheaply. For example, print three items per A4 sheet in black and white with a simple recipe. Customers who receive such a surprise return more often, refer others, and leave better feedback.

Sourcing and rebranding ebooks

  • Ebook topics that buyers can use: a free ebook such as a resistance band exercise guide, an ab workout guide, or a 15, 30, or 60 minute workout guide. One resistance band seller added a mesh bag to solve an odour complaint and included three free ebooks.
  • Buy a PLR (private label rights, a licence to edit and brand a ready-made ebook) ebook on a health-related topic from a site such as plr.me. These typically cost around $5 to $10 or less.
  • A free-ebook sourcing procedure is set out below.
    1. Choose a topic related to your product or your customer's needs, such as wellness or a product-use subject.
    2. Search that topic on a free-ebook website and download a matching ebook.
    3. Hire a freelancer on Fiverr (a freelance marketplace) to change the cover and branding to match your store.
    4. Offer the rebranded ebook as a free bonus with a purchase. Do not sell it directly.
  • A free ebook rebranded with a new cover and logo can be offered as a bonus. It should not be sold directly because of copyright (the creator's legal right to control copies) concerns. This is a gray area (not clearly allowed or banned), and rebranding is recommended.
  • Experts disagree on selling rebranded PLR ebooks. One says the ebook can be edited in Canva (a design app) and kept as delivered, since the rights allow reuse. It can then be added as a bonus or sold as a product on the site. Another says rebranded ebooks should not be sold directly because of copyright concerns. They should be offered only as a free bonus. Use it as a free bonus in every case. Sell it only if the PLR licence you bought explicitly allows resale.
  • A cheap-upsell strategy with a free ebook as the add-on follows these steps.
    1. Search the supplier marketplace (AliExpress example) for a product related to your main product. Filter by reviews and find an add-on that costs between 10 cents and 1 dollar.
    2. Offer the add-on for free on the store, either beside the main product or as a free bonus when customers buy.
    3. Find a PLR ebook related to the product's benefit on a PLR site. For example, skin care for sensitive skin for a shower-filter product.
    4. Buy the PLR ebook for about 2 dollars 70 cents, rebrand it with your own logo, and give it to customers as a downloadable bonus.
    5. State the ebook's value as 20 dollars in the offer. For example: "Receive a 20 dollar ebook free with your order."
    6. Make the offer very visible on the site. Use a banner, a dropdown, or a product gallery image as the second image.
    7. Set up the add-on as a variant (another version of the same listing) of the main product's Shopify listing (the product page on the Shopify store). Use an automation app (Auto DS was named) so the supplier receives orders for both items together.
    8. Tell customers in the order confirmation email that the items may arrive on different days and not together.
  • Custom add-on packaging made specifically for a product can be expensive. If it must be manufactured separately, suppliers often require a minimum order (the smallest amount they accept) of 500 to 1,000 units. Only use custom add-ons if the supplier already has the item in stock (on hand).

Tiered and selectable gifts

2 min
  • Tiered gifts (gifts that come in levels) make the buyer add more items.
  • Each extra item unlocks another free gift, up to a set maximum.
  • Removing an item removes its gift.
  • This uses loss aversion (people dislike losing something they already have) to encourage larger orders.
  • Example: a tiered gift offer in the cart unlocked three gifts as the buyer added bottles: a recipe book, a mystery gift, and a milk frother.
  • Removing a bottle removed the matching gift.
  • Gift tiers were tied to quantity (the number of items) and capped at a set number.
  • Example: a necklace offer and selectable gifts with a choice of design, as above.
  • Include a card that new customers return with their order to claim a gift.
  • Give existing customers a different reason to receive it.
  • The gift helped the catalogue (a printed or online product list) beat competing catalogues.
  • The gift paid for itself several times through larger orders.
  • Free gift price guidance: a base product at $45 with a free gift gave a total perceived price (the value the buyer feels they get) of around $60, which was a good sweet spot.
  • A total of $80 to $100 was harder to sell because buyers did not see enough value.
  • Example: a free gift that costs about $2 was added to a product sold at $59.99 instead of $60.
  • This pushes the perceived value up.
  • This was the most profitable offer for that store.
  • The gift must still be relevant to the customer.
  • The gift must not eat too much of the margin (the profit left after costs).
  • Bundle-stacking with value display: offer the main product plus a free bonus item. A bundle is several items sold together.
  • Show the bonus's original retail price in parentheses, for example "$19.99 value".
  • Add urgency language (words that push the buyer to act now).
  • This works because the free item increases the perceived bundle value at minimal cost.
  • The specific dollar value makes the gift feel real.
    • Example one: "FREE meal planning guide ($24.99 value) with every 90-day nutrition package."
    • Example two: "FREE stretching routine video ($14.99 value) with every recovery device-today only."
  • Example: a posture corrector (a brace that helps straighten the back) bundle with a free gift.
  • The free "Posture Fix Guide" was displayed at a $19.99 value and included with the corrector purchase.
  • The ad used "limited time only" and "today only" language.
  • The free guide was a low-cost value-add (extra worth) that increased perceived bundle worth without significant cost to the seller.

Presenting gifts on pages

3 min
  • Show the free gift clearly on the product page and in the cart.
  • Show it with a headline, an image, and a claim message (a note confirming the gift) after adding to cart.
  • Customers who do not know about the gift may not add the product.
  • Add free bonus items, such as a digital cheat sheet (a short summary file) or e-book, as a visible line near Add to Cart (the button that puts an item in the cart).
  • In the bundle block too.
  • State the bonus's stated value when it has one.
  • Only promise a bonus that is actually delivered automatically after checkout (after payment).
  • Place an image labelled with the gift and its value under the product description on the product page.
  • Use wording such as "free gift with your order, 39 USD value."
  • Bonuses should be shown on the product page as free gifts, not hidden behind extra steps. **
  • Do not make customers call or take extra steps to receive an offer, such as a gift voucher for large orders. **
  • Extra steps cause distractions and lost sales. **
  • Show the bonus on the landing page (the first page a visitor sees) as part of the offer, next to the main product.
  • Make the offer very visible on the site with a banner (a wide strip at the top of a page), a dropdown (a menu that opens on click), or a product gallery image as the second image.
  • Present a bonus-offer pattern on an expensive product page: "If you buy [expensive product], you get [free item] included." **
  • The customer sees the bonus as added value to a purchase they already want. **

Common mistakes

  • Do not choose bonus items that carry high wholesale costs or threaten your profit margins.
  • Bonuses are incentives, not revenue-builders (they bring buyers in; they do not earn money themselves).
  • Offering a free ebook that is unrelated to the product is weak.

  • Show the gift clearly on the product page and in the cart.

  • Bonus delivery must not be left to customers to claim through extra steps. **
  • Custom packaging made for one product is expensive.

Free shipping, shipping fees and delivery upgrades

Section 7.11 · 11 of 28 in this chapter · 20 min read

In short

  • Free shipping means you pay the shipping cost, so each product's price must cover it.
  • Before you list a product, check that its margin (the money you keep after costs) still covers shipping.
  • Do not list products where shipping costs wipe out your profit.
  • Check what competitors do with shipping, and copy them when it makes sense.
  • Add a markup (extra money on top of cost) to shipping extras like liftgate and inside delivery.
More key points
  • Never add free shipping as a cart row. Name the $0 option instead.
  • Match Google Merchant Center shipping settings to your store's shipping rules, or Google flags problems.
  • Free shipping costs you money. Offer it only when your profit (margin) covers it.
This section has 14 parts. Tap one to open it.

Free shipping as the default offer

2 min
  • Free shipping is standard practice in dropshipping (selling products that a supplier ships straight to your customer, so you never hold stock).
  • Every shipping cost still reduces your margin (the money you keep after paying the product and shipping costs).
  • Free shipping is the default in this model.
  • Each product's shipping cost must be covered by its sale price.
  • Drop or reprice any product whose price cannot absorb its shipping cost before you list it.
  • Suppliers never offer free shipping.
  • When a store advertises free shipping, the store pays, not the customer.
  • This is the standard approach for about 99% of stores.
  • Free shipping means the merchant (you, the store owner) pays the shipping cost, not the customer.
  • Build that cost into the margin calculation for each product.
  • Free shipping can be offered on everything, with extra charges only for special services such as white glove delivery (a premium service where the item is carried inside, set up, and its packaging removed).
  • Offer free standard shipping on all products.
  • Build the shipping cost into the product price, so customers see free shipping and the cost is covered in the margin.
  • Set shipping and product prices so the store can offer free shipping and still keep a profit.
  • The customer does not pay shipping, but the supplier's shipping cost must be included in the margin math.
  • Free shipping on almost everything means you do not need a shipping app connected to a UPS account (a shipping tool linked to the UPS delivery company).
  • Put shipping costs into the margin calculation instead.
  • Shipping is not a separate Shopify charge (Shopify is the software that runs online stores).
  • When the store offers free shipping and pays for it, the built-in Shopify shipping system is not used.
  • Free shipping is often overlooked, but cost-conscious shoppers prefer a store with free delivery over one that charges a few dollars.
  • Absorbing the delivery cost can make your store the first choice over competitors.
  • Offer free shipping and build it into the product price.
  • A visible shipping charge makes many buyers abandon the purchase (leave before paying).
  • Buyers accept a higher product price more easily than an extra shipping fee.
  • For beginners, build shipping into the product price and offer free shipping rather than a separate fee.
  • Separate shipping charges are more likely to cause abandoned checkouts (buyers who leave at the payment step), especially on low-priced products.
  • Showing a $5 shipping fee only at checkout can cause buyers to abandon.
  • Offer free shipping, then sell faster shipping as a paid upsell (an optional extra the buyer pays for).

When to offer free shipping

3 min
  • Free shipping is only worth offering when the margin covers the shipping cost.
  • Free shipping works on expensive products but loses money on cheap ones of the same size and weight.
  • Example: a three-seater sofa sold at $1,500 can carry free shipping with healthy margin.
  • The same sofa at $500 cannot.
  • Free shipping on heavy items is not standard practice.
  • Check what competitors do first.
  • Offer free shipping only when the margin still covers the freight cost (the shipping cost charged by carriers, the companies that move packages).
  • Free shipping is very hard to keep profitable when MAP is less than 2x wholesale. MAP means minimum advertised price (the lowest price a brand lets you advertise). Wholesale means the price you pay the supplier.
  • Free shipping and a price-match guarantee (a promise to match a cheaper price) are common in this market.
  • Both costs come out of the spread (the difference between your selling price and your cost).
  • Offer them only on products where the spread after average shipping still covers ad costs and merchant fees (fees the payment provider charges the store). Otherwise they can erase the margin.
  • Do not list products whose shipping costs make free shipping unprofitable.
  • For the guitar example, list only products priced above $200 when shipping is expensive.
  • At the first click, price usually matters more than shipping, because customers see shipping only later in checkout.
  • High shipping can still send them back to look for other options, so balance both.
  • Before offering free shipping, calculate how it changes your margin on each sale, because it is a cost of the sale.
  • Free shipping works as a purchase incentive (a reason to buy), so weigh the margin impact against the extra sales it may bring.
  • Check shipping rates before you use free shipping.
  • Lower shipping costs by comparing carriers, shipping direct from suppliers, or getting multiple free quotes (price offers).

Deciding whether to keep free shipping when costs are high

Older advice (2019) gives this sequence:

  1. Calculate the profit left per order as a percentage of the sale price, including shipping costs.
  2. If the margin is only about 5% to 15%, work on the margin with the supplier first, by asking for better product prices or shipping rates.
  3. Compare with competitor offers: if they ship free, charging for shipping will reduce conversions (sales from visitors) noticeably.
  4. Only move to paid shipping if the margin cannot be fixed and the product still makes sense.

Competitor-based rules

  • If all competitors offer free shipping, charging a shipping fee usually makes the sale harder.
  • Cover the cost by negotiating shipping terms with suppliers or product terms, not by charging extra.
  • If all competitors charge for shipping, charging for shipping too is reasonable.
  • Adding a shipping cost into the product price makes the listing look more expensive in ads and reduces clicks (visits to your page).
  • If competitors in a specialized niche (a narrow market) charge for shipping, you can charge for shipping as well. (2018)
  • This is an exception to the standard free-shipping approach.
  • Check competitors' shipping policies before deciding how to price shipping.
  • Shoppers see competitor prices and shipping charges side by side in ads.
  • Shipping on large items can be charged to the customer if the market does so.
  • Check this against competitor pricing and whether shipping is built into the price.

Calculating freight cost

1 min

Freight Center procedure

To calculate shipping costs for a free-shipping offer:

  1. Use Freight Center dot com.
  2. Enter the supplier warehouse location.
  3. Enter product weight and dimensions.
  4. Get shipping quotes to a sample city in California, Florida, and Texas.
  5. Average these quotes to determine typical shipping expense.
  6. Factor this into product pricing.

Weight-based formula

Weight-based shipping rate = product weight × rate per pound.

Example: at $2 per pound, a 50 lb (pound) product costs $100 to ship.

  • Product weights must be accurate for this to work.

Calculated shipping at checkout

  • If shipping costs vary and you do not want to build them into product prices, a Shopify shipping app can charge calculated shipping at checkout. (2024)
  • Calculated shipping means the price is worked out from the order's weight and box size. (2024)
  • This keeps profit figures closer to the real freight cost of each order.
  • Charge shipping per brand by enabling shipping in Shopify, or by adding a surcharge (an extra fee) to the product price.
  • Example: a $100 product with a $10 shipping surcharge.
  • When a supplier has high shipping costs, such as an average of $600, either build the shipping into the product price or charge a brand-specific shipping fee. (2025)
  • The choice depends on what competitors do. (2025)
  • Shopify requires you to offer either free shipping or paid shipping, not both.
  • Product-specific shipping upgrades must therefore be sold as product options.
  • Shopify shipping rules as described in 2019: free shipping over $200 applies to all products $200 and above if the free-shipping rule is activated.
  • For products under $200, configure separate shipping rules: charge a fixed amount per order, or enable real-time calculated shipping based on weight and box dimensions.

Note on conflicting Shopify sources: The 2019 setup (free rule for $200 and above, separate rules below) and the 2021 statement (either free or paid, not both) both describe Shopify behavior from older sources. Check current Shopify settings before relying on either.

Free-shipping thresholds

2 min

A threshold is the minimum total a cart (the basket of items a customer is buying) must reach to get free shipping.

Sources give different thresholds. Each applies to a different model:

Source Threshold Context Ids
Source A $200 Standard minimum order value for free shipping on dropship products
Source A $100 Example text: "Free shipping to the continental US on all orders over $100," shown on the product page and in the shipping tab
Source B $500 Start with free shipping on all orders; if margins are too thin, add a $50 flat fee below $500, editable later in Shopify
Source C $39 Example store: free shipping above $39, otherwise a $9.99 fee ,
Source C 19 euros Threshold used by a German store, adoptable in another market
Source D (2026) $25 Single at $19, two-pack at $29.99
  • Use free shipping on all products instead of a threshold.
  • Keep low-priced products out of the catalog, so the store focuses on higher-priced items.
  • Free shipping can be offered for a limited day or week, or through a discount code that activates it.
  • Example: normal shipping of $7 can be shown as free for one day only.
  • Free shipping over $75 on a product priced at $85 means every order ships free.
  • The source says this only really works for clearance items (goods sold to clear stock), because those products already sell at a low margin.

Setting the threshold in the cart

  • Set the threshold above the single-unit price and below the bundle total (several items sold together), so a customer who buys two qualifies for free shipping.
  • Example: a single at $19 and a two-pack at $29.99 with a threshold of $25.
  • Set the threshold between the price of one item and the price of two, so the customer gets free shipping only by buying two.
  • A threshold above the single-item price but at or below the two-unit total is what pushes the buyer up the ladder (to buy more units).
  • A very low product price, such as $9.99, is often a sign that the store charges for shipping separately.
  • Check the store header (the top bar of the site) and checkout for a free-shipping threshold before judging the price.
  • In the example, the threshold was $39, so customers were encouraged to add items until the cart reached $39.
  • Bundles that reach the threshold in one purchase remove the friction (hassle) of adding items.
  • Pairing a single item with a complementary trending product (one that goes well with it and sells now) as a bundle can lift the total order past the threshold.
  • Example: one item at $19.99, or a bundle with a second product at $29.99, keeps shipping free and preserves margin.

Progress bars and threshold display

1 min

A progress bar is a banner that shows how much more a customer must spend to get free shipping.

Configuring the free-shipping bar in Shopify

  1. Go to Header > Free shipping bar.
  2. Turn on Show free shipping bar.
  3. Set the background color.
  4. Enter the threshold amount, for example 1000 dollars.

The cart then reads "spend X to unlock free shipping" and updates as the cart total changes.

Remaining-amount formula

Amount shown on the bar = threshold − current cart total.

Example: 1000 − 799 = 201. If the bar shows a different number, check the cart price against the threshold.

  • With a threshold of 1000 and a 799 product in the cart, the bar shows that $201 more is needed.
  • The bar updates automatically as items are added.
  • A free-shipping bar is only useful when a free-shipping offer exists.
  • If all products ship free, the bar can stay off.
  • In the cart drawer (the cart panel that slides out), show the threshold with the remaining amount.
  • Example: if the cart is under $40, display "add $20 more to get free shipping."
  • This nudges the customer to add an item that raises the order value (the total the customer spends).
  • Show the threshold as a set amount next to Add to Cart (the button that adds an item), not as a vague line such as "qualifying once the threshold is met."
  • Vague shipping wording invites questions at checkout.
  • Shipping options left open or enabled while ads are running can let customers order with a shipping setup that was never intended.
  • Check the shipping settings before launching any paid campaign.

Charging for shipping on low-ticket items

1 min

A low-ticket item is a cheap product.

Example rule for low-priced products

  • Example store rule: free shipping on orders above $39, otherwise a $9.99 shipping fee is charged.
  • Shoppers who want free shipping must buy several items to reach the threshold.
  • For low-priced products (for example $9 or $14), charge a separate shipping fee to keep the margin.
  • Then offer free shipping above a higher cart threshold, such as $30 or $40, to raise average order value (the average amount spent per order).
  • Example of your own: a $12 gadget with a $7.99 shipping fee, plus free shipping above $35.
  • If a product's selling price is low and the margin is thin, raise the price or charge a shipping fee.
  • Pair it with an offer that encourages buying two.

Alternatives to a separate fee

  • If many dropshippers charge separately, a fee of about $5 to $7 per order is common.
  • Customers who see shipping at checkout may abandon the order if the fee is too high.
  • If many customers start checkout but do not complete it because shipping costs appear, add about $7 to the product price and offer free shipping.
  • This applies when the conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy) stays poor despite good ad traffic (visitors who arrive from ads).
  • Charging for shipping while running ads against free-shipping competitors almost never converts.
  • Ads should only go to products where the margin is high enough to absorb the freight.

Heavy and large items

2 min

Margin requirements

  • For large or heavy products, the spread between MAP and wholesale must cover shipping, which can be about $250 per item.
  • Such products should sell for roughly $1,000 or more, with wholesale at half or less of that price, before free shipping is viable.
  • Inexpensive heavy items are a loss.
  • Items that are large or heavy can still be sold if the price is high enough to cover shipping and leave a healthy margin after free shipping.
  • Example: $300 margin left after wholesale on a product with $250 freight leaves only $50 profit. (2023)
  • Charging the customer $250 for shipping keeps the $300 margin at a total of $550. (2023)
  • This only works if competitors also charge for shipping. (2023)
  • Free shipping on products that cost a lot to freight is only worth offering if the margin still covers the shipping cost. (2023)

Listing heavy items with a shipping add-on

  • For heavy items where freight is expensive, one method is to list the product at a lower price and add a required shipping add-on at checkout.
  • Example: a $500 product with a required $300 shipping add-on reaches $800, while the advertised product price stays at $500.
  • Listing a heavy item at $800 with shipping included in the price usually reduces clicks when the competitor shows $500 with freight calculated later.
  • The lower advertised price with a required shipping add-on keeps the price visible while covering shipping costs.
  • For non-MAP products (products not bound by a minimum advertised price) with high shipping costs, raise the product price to include shipping and show free shipping.
  • Customers see the same total at checkout.

Matching competitor prices on heavy items

If competitors charge shipping on heavy items and you need to match their price while keeping the item competitive:

  1. Set your listed price in line with the competitors' prices for those products.
  2. Create a separate shipping profile (a set of shipping rules for chosen products) in Shopify, and add the heavy products to it.
  3. Set that profile so the products are not eligible for free shipping.

This keeps the price competitive for Google Shopping traffic (visitors from Google's product ads) while shipping is charged separately.

Supplier fees and packages

  • Supplier shipping fees should be built into the product price rather than shown as a separate fee.
  • Avoid asterisk shipping explanations (small footnote-style notes) on product pages.
  • When a supplier insists on charging shipping, negotiate shipping and warranty into a single package price.
  • This gives customers one price and supports the store's extended warranty offers (paid longer-coverage deals).
  • Shipping to Hawaii is usually not worth the effort, because costs are so high that most customers will not pay them.
  • See the Alaska and Hawaii section below.

Excluding Alaska and Hawaii

Remove them from checkout options and offer a quote by contact Source A,; Source A, Shipping is too expensive for most products
Do not advertise to Hawaii for most products Source A, Most products
  • Offer free shipping to the continental US only. The continental US means the lower 48 states.
  • Hawaii and Alaska are excluded from free shipping by default because shipping there is very expensive.
  • For Alaska and Hawaii, do not offer free shipping.
  • Remove these states from checkout options.
  • Link a contact address on the contact page, so customers can request a quote (a price offer) that includes a surcharge.

Exclusion procedure

To exclude Hawaii and Alaska from standard free shipping:

  1. In the shipping rules, make Hawaii and Alaska unavailable to select at checkout.
  2. On the shipping policy page, say that customers who want to ship there can contact the store for a quote.
  3. Handle those quote requests manually.
  • Free shipping message example: "Free shipping to the continental US on all orders over $100."
  • The threshold is shown on the product page and in the shipping tab.
  • Free shipping to the continental US is the policy in the 2021 source.
  • Alaska and Hawaii shipping is significantly more expensive, so charge a separate surcharge or flat fee for these areas.

Expedited shipping

1 min

Expedited shipping means faster delivery, usually for an extra fee.

Setting up an expedited option

  1. Offer expedited shipping only with suppliers who can reliably ship fast and with whom you have a strong relationship.
  2. Add expedited shipping as an optional toggle on product pages (for example, "Expedited shipping-ships in 24 hours: yes or no").
  3. Charge a fixed add-on fee if the customer selects the expedited option (for example, $50).
  • Priority shipping or priority processing (orders packed and sent first) can be offered as an add-on fee of about 10% of the product price (for example $2.95 to $3.95) to add extra margin.
  • If competitors deliver in 5 to 10 days, arrange with the supplier to dispatch within 24 hours and deliver in 3 to 5 days.
  • Faster delivery is a visible value that helps the store stand out.
  • Free expedited shipping is a monetary promotion (an offer that waives a fee, like a discount), where the store waives a fee it normally charges, typically worth $25 to $50 for expedited shipping.
  • Orders above a set amount placed by a set date ship expedited for free.
  • It works well just before Christmas, when gift buyers pay attention to shipping speed.
  • The offer can be limited to certain suppliers or to orders above a price threshold.
  • Shopify's default checkout does not allow easy assignment of specific shipping offers to specific products.
  • Use the product upsell app for same-day shipping instead of the shipping page. The upsell app can target each offer to specific products.

White glove delivery and assembly

3 min

Ways to offer white glove

  • White glove can be placed in three ways.
  • Way 1: free on product pages, to lift conversion.
  • Way 2: as a paid shipping upgrade at checkout.
  • Way 3: as a custom quote requested before or after the order.
  • The right choice depends on the niche and on whether margins are high enough to absorb it.
  • Offering free white glove on high-ticket products (expensive products) only makes sense when the product margin is high enough to pay for it.
  • If competitors do not offer the same service, showing it on the product page can noticeably raise conversion rates.
  • White glove delivery can be sold as a paid upgrade.
  • Or it can be given away as a free bonus for very expensive products, when it helps win sales in the niche.
  • Compare several white glove providers, since prices vary widely.
  • Choose one with good reviews and the lowest cost.
  • For a custom desk (a desk made to order) that sells for $5,000, where three competitors use standard delivery, offering free white glove delivery (costing $100 to $300 extra) can win the sale.
  • Each sale won this way adds the margin of that order, so the extra cost can pay off.
  • A low-cost paid option at checkout is white glove delivery sold as an upgrade the customer can add to the order.
  • Use this when your margins cannot absorb a free version.
  • Shipping upgrades such as white-glove delivery, for example $50 to $80 extra, can be used as perceived value on higher-priced products.
  • This can justify a price above a competitor that does not offer them.
  • Do not apply a flat markup (one fixed extra amount added to every price) for premium services such as White Glove across all orders.
  • Costs differ by supplier and product, so a flat charge can lose money.
  • Offer these services per product page.
  • Do not charge flat shipping fees above the product price unless you offer an upgraded service such as expedited shipping, white-glove delivery, or installation.
  • Flat fees without added value don't match competitor offerings.
  • Free white glove delivery suits products where the customer values installation, not just curbside drop-off (leaving the item at the curb).
  • Free extended warranties (longer coverage at no cost) suit products you already sell with a paid warranty.
  • Test whether a temporary free warranty attracts the audience that has been sitting on your list (your email list of past or possible buyers).

Free white glove as a monetary promotion

  • Free white glove in-home delivery can be used as a monetary promotion in the same way as free expedited shipping.
  • It can be restricted to certain suppliers or to orders above a certain price.

Product page banner template

Place the banner directly on the product page where the offer applies.

Limited time: free white glove in-home delivery on purchases of [minimum amount, e.g., $1,500] or more. No code necessary. Includes bringing the product into your home, setting it up, and removing all boxes and packaging.

Assembly and installation

  • State clearly assembly requirements, shipping format, and customer responsibility on product pages.
  • Consider offering assembly as a paid upsell, by partnering with assembly services used by competitors.
  • For products that need assembly or installation, research what your competitors use for assembly services.
  • Partner with the same service provider.
  • Offer assembly as a paid upsell on the product page, so customers can add professional installation if they want it.

Optional services and add-ons

1 min
  • For freight shipments (large items sent by truck), offer optional services such as liftgate (a platform on the truck that lowers the item to the ground), inside delivery (the driver carries the item into the room), and white glove service (a team unpacks, sets up, and removes packaging). Mark them up (add extra money on top of cost). Check with suppliers for any other custom options you can also charge for.
  • Item customizations, such as custom colors, can be marked up and work well for high-ticket items (expensive products). Some customers send a hex code (a six-character color code). Suppliers can often match it.
  • If the manufacturer charges $100 for inside delivery, add a markup such as 10% to the customer's inside delivery line. Record the base cost (what you pay) in the card notes (the notes on the product's record). This keeps the owner's cost visible and the margin (profit left after costs) clear. Use the same markup rule for every quote of that type. (Dated 2021-22.)
  • For an everyday free-shipping offer, do not add it as a line item in the cart (a separate row in the shopping cart). That creates a draft line item (a temporary row) that blocks discount codes (codes customers type to get a price reduction). Name the $0 shipping option instead, for example "free liftgate delivery." (Dated 2022.)
  • Free shipping and paid add-ons: Shopify (the online store software) has a shipping system, and product options work together with it. Together they can handle upgrades only as separate options, since free and paid shipping cannot both be offered on the same shipping setup. (Dated 2021.)

Promotions that include shipping

1 min
  • Free shipping can be used when margin covers it. For non-MAP products (products without a minimum advertised price, the lowest price a brand lets sellers show) with high shipping costs, raise the product price to include shipping, and show free shipping.
  • Free shipping is offered as a limited-time promotion, or through a discount code, as described in the free-shipping thresholds section.
  • Free expedited shipping (fast shipping at no cost), free white glove in-home delivery, and free liftgate are $0 options that can be used as promotions (offers that give the customer value). They can be restricted to chosen suppliers or to orders above a set price.

Merchant Center consistency

1 min
  • When you set shipping fees in Shopify, update the matching settings in Google Merchant Center (Google's tool that holds your product data) with the same rules. Google flags differences between the two as quality issues. (Dated 2022.)
  • Shipping costs in Google Shopping ads (Google's product ads) are shown beside competitor prices (other stores' prices). So the Merchant Center shipping settings must match the store's live shipping rules (the rules active on your store right now).
  • Where a heavy item uses a separate shipping profile (a set of shipping rules for one kind of product, for example to stay competitive on Google Shopping), that profile must be reflected in Merchant Center.

Further details

1 min
  • Use geo-targeting (showing your ads only in chosen places) in paid ads. Limit it to regions near the supplier warehouse (the supplier's storage building) if shipping costs are high. This keeps shipping expenses lower and preserves profit margins.
  • When you offer free shipping, the customer is not charged for shipping, but you pay it. That cost comes out of your own margin. The supplier only gives free shipping if they choose to, so it is not automatic.

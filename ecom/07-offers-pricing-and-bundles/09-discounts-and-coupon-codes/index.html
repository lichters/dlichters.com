Discounts and coupon codes

Section 7.9 · 9 of 28 in this chapter · 25 min read

In short

  • Most stores use a percentage off discount as the default offer.
  • Start testing with a 5% order discount, and never set a discount that leaves a sale at a loss.
  • Give every limited code an end date, so it stops working on time.
  • Limit sign-up codes to one use per customer, so they cannot spread and cost you money.
  • Use a fixed dollar code for pop-ups and emails, and a percent code as the default.
More key points
  • Test every code at checkout with a real item before going live.
  • Use strict expiry dates for time-limited sales, and always-on offers for permanent perks.
  • Google Merchant Center promotions only show after your products sync, and free shipping needs a coupon code.
  • When a customer has two codes, the bigger one should replace the smaller one, not stack on top of it.

Discount types

3 min
  • A percentage off discount is the default offer type for most stores.
  • Percentage and dollar-off discounts have close to the same effect. The Source E test found little difference.
  • A common rule of thumb: use dollar-off when the discount is over $100, and percentage-off below that. Mixing both is acceptable.
  • Percentage is the usual choice in Shopify. Shopify is the online store software. A code such as save 10 would be set to 10%.
  • Fixed-dollar discounts are easier for shoppers to picture than percentages. Use a dollar amount for pop-up and email codes where possible. A pop-up is a box that appears on the screen. Compare it against a percentage offer if conversion stalls. Conversion means the share of visitors who buy. Stalls means it stops rising.
  • Dollar-off offers such as "Save $20," "Save $30 on first orders," or "Save up to $80 sitewide" work best for higher-AOV products. AOV means average order value, the average amount one order spends. They also work well when bundling multiple items. A bundle is several items sold together. Sitewide means across the whole store.
  • Discount scopes to choose from. Scope means which products the discount covers.
    • Site-wide: one percentage on all products.
    • Specific SKUs: a set percentage or dollar amount on selected products. SKU means stock keeping unit, a product's own code.
    • Tiered: the discount steps up with the amount spent. For example, spend $100, then get 20% off the amount after that.
  • Spend-threshold discount: spend 1,000 USD and save 100 USD on any order over 1,000 USD. A threshold is a minimum amount that must be reached. This is simpler to understand than a percentage discount with a dollar cap. A cap is a maximum limit.
  • Spend-and-save example for a homebrew equipment store in January. Homebrew means making beer or wine at home. During the first week, orders over $500 save $50, and orders over $1,000 save $75. Spend thresholds can be varied freely to match the store's average order size.
  • Tiered discounts, such as 5% on orders of $250 or more and 10% on orders of $500 or more, are built by creating separate Shopify discount codes. Each code gets its own minimum order amount. Older (2021).
  • Tiered spend discount used for Black Friday promotions **:
Order spend Discount
400 dollars or more 15% off
800 dollars or more 20% off
1,200 dollars or more 25% off
  • Low-value tiered coupons reward larger orders. For example, $10 off at $500 spend and $20 off at $1,000 spend. Choose tier values based on what competitors offer in your niche. A niche is a narrow market. Competitors are rival stores.
  • Use a discount code rather than an automatic discount when the offer should be targeted. A code means the shopper types it in at checkout. Checkout is the payment step. An automatic discount applies to every cart.
  • An automatic site-wide discount of 10% was active on the example product page. Compare an automatic discount against a coupon code with a deadline when setting up your own offer.
  • A monetary percentage promotion is a fixed percent off, for example 5% or 10%, for a set time. It is applied when the customer enters a coupon code.
  • The monetary percentage promotion can be shown store-wide. It should be sent specifically to the audience that has engaged but not bought. Engaged means they clicked or opened your messages.
  • Time-limited discount codes create urgency. Urgency is a reason to buy now. They can be offered as a reward for joining the email list.
  • Time-limited discount codes can be shown in an exit-intent popup. This popup appears when a visitor is about to leave.
  • Time-limited discount codes can be displayed with a countdown timer for new customers.
  • Limited-time discount or coupon codes use urgency to push buyers to purchase sooner.
  • Limited-time codes can be given to new email subscribers.
  • Limited-time codes can be shown in an exit-intent popup as the visitor is about to leave.

Discount sizes

1 min
  • Starting point for testing: a 5% order discount. You can create as many discount codes with different values as you need.
  • Coupon codes: offer 5-10%. 10% is competitive because most competitors either do not promote discounts or offer less than 10%.
  • Expiring coupon percentages: the typical levels used are 5%, 7% or 10% off. This depends on the store.
  • Competitor first-order discount: about 10% in exchange for an email signup. Competitor example, not dated.
  • Welcome kit for newsletter sign-ups: usually 5-10%. Customers see this as the usual reward. A newsletter is a regular email to subscribers.
  • Typical sign-up incentives are a 5% or 10% discount, or a fixed $5 discount. An incentive is a reward for signing up. A 10% first-order discount is the standard used in this setup.
  • Volume offers, which reward buying more: typically 20 to 50 percent. Discounts of 10 percent or 5 percent are seen as weak by the audience.
  • Bundles: 15% is a reasonable starting point, but it must fit the margin. Margin is the money you keep from each sale after costs.
  • Ceiling for strong offers: a maximum discount of 70 percent off, with 50 percent off as the usual standard. A ceiling is the highest allowed level.
  • Launch discount: 50% off to generate rapid sales.

Sizing discounts to margin

3 min
  • Calculate margin after coupons, not before them. Net margin is what is left after all deductions. If net margin after discounts is only 12 to 15 percent, the current discount structure should be changed.
  • Only offer discounts on your site if you can afford them.
  • Before offering a first-order discount such as 5% or 10% off, check on the pricing sheet that gross profit stays above zero at that discount. Gross profit is the money left after the product cost. A small percentage can turn a product with thin margin into a loss. Older (2021-22).

  • A 5% discount on a product with $1,000 net profit (for example, a $50 discount) remains profitable. It is worth offering if it increases conversion rate.
  • A coupon such as 100 USD off orders over 1,000 USD can be offset by raising prices slightly. Offset means balanced out. If competitors undercut you, a smaller incentive is safer. Undercut means priced lower than you.
  • A smaller incentive such as 50 USD off orders over 1,000 USD is the safer option when competitors undercut you.
  • When margins are too thin, a sitewide discount of 10 percent off all orders can be lowered to 5 percent off all orders.
  • This keeps a discount on the site while protecting margin on each order.
  • Replace a percentage-off code with a minimum-spend code, such as $50 off orders over $1,000. For higher-priced stores, use $100 off orders over $2,000.
  • Create the minimum-spend rule in Shopify's discount codes so it applies automatically.
  • This raises average order value (AOV) while giving less discount per order.
  • Set eligibility by profit, not by a blanket rule. A blanket rule applies to everything without exceptions.
  • Example: 20 percent off orders over 250 dollars requires any starter kit. Free expedited shipping, which means fast delivery, requires a premium kit.
  • Bundle headline math: a bundle is advertised as access to its listed tools at one tenth of their list price.
  • The bundle's title frames this as 95% off.
  • If the extra 10% code applies to the bundle price, the total discount is about 91% off list, not 95%.
  • Compare the final checkout total with the list prices of the tools you would actually buy. Do not compare it with the headline percentage.
  • Discounts go straight to lost profit. Limit discounts or stop offering them entirely.
  • Some stores have kept sales volume while refusing negotiated discounts. Negotiated means agreed by bargaining. So the effect on orders can be tested. Older (2021-22).
  • Do not hand out irresistible deals without a reason.
  • Offering a discount too early gives away margin to people who would have bought anyway.
  • An early discount can also weaken the urgency of future deals. Older (2021-22).
  • Do not run a standing discount on product pages as a permanent sales tactic. This matters most when margins are thin.
  • A permanent 5% discount erodes margin. Erodes means slowly wears away. It does not make the offer more convincing. Older (2021-22).
  • Promotions such as discount codes or sitewide sales should be created with a set start and end date.
  • Test promotions against margin before launch. This way they do not erode profit unintentionally.
  • Price the store at MAP and apply all discounts off MAP. In this text, MAP is the compare-at price, the higher crossed-out price shown next to the sale price.
  • Never raise the price first and then discount it. Older (2021).
  • Average order value in the examples ranged from about $300 to about $1,000.
  • A minimum of about $200 was recommended as a starting threshold. Older (2020).
  • Minimum order values can be set so a code only applies above a threshold.
  • For example, 2000 dollars, which means 10% off orders above 2000 dollars.

Shopify setup

2 min

  • Create the discount code in the Shopify admin before it is shown on any product page. The admin is the store's back office.

  • The code has to work at checkout for shoppers who enter it in the cart.

  • Create any discount code in Shopify's discount section before you show it in the announcement bar or an exit-intent overlay. The announcement bar is the strip at the top of the store. An overlay is a box placed on top of the page.

  • A code that does not exist in Shopify will not apply at checkout. Older (2020).

  • Procedure for a 5% order discount:

    1. Log in to the Shopify admin.
    2. Click Discounts in the left navigation.
    3. Click Create discount at the top.
    4. Select the discount type Amount off order (a discount on the total order amount).
    5. Name the code in the format save plus the number. For a 5% code the name is save five.
    6. Set the discount value to Percentage and enter 5%.
    7. Under customer eligibility, leave the setting on All customers.
    8. Leave the minimum purchase at none and the usage limit at unlimited.
    9. Leave the start date at the default, which is the day the code is created.
    10. Leave the end date empty unless the code is for a timed promotion such as Black Friday or Cyber Monday.
    11. Click Save and confirm the code shows as active in the Discounts list.

  • When a shopper enters the code in the cart at checkout, the percentage is deducted from the entire order total.

  • Customer eligibility can be limited to specific customer segments. A segment is a group of customers, such as past buyers.

  • A segment lets you offer a code for returning customers only.

  • Specific customers can also be selected by their email address.

  • The combination option controls whether several discounts can stack on one order. Stacking means using more than one discount together.

  • Keep the combination option turned off unless stacking is intended. The default start date is the current date and time.

  • The start date defaults to the date and time when the discount is created.

  • Leaving it unchanged makes the code active immediately.

  • An end date can be added to limit the code to a set window.

  • Set an end date for limited promotions such as Black Friday, Cyber Monday, or 4th of July. After that date, the code stops working.

  • Without an end date, a time-limited promotion stays valid indefinitely.

  • Regular Shopify plans do not automatically end a sale price.

  • Set the discount code to stop working at the sale's end time, for example midnight on the last day. You can also use an app. An app is an add-on tool. 2025.

  • When creating a discount code in Shopify, choose the collections it is eligible for. A collection is a group of products.

  • Leave out collections containing products the discount may not apply to. For example, products from vendors whose MAP policy forbids discounts. A vendor is a supplier or brand.

Code naming

1 min
  • Discount code names should reflect their value. Use the format save plus the number written out. A 5% code is named save five.
  • Give every discount a named reason, such as a seasonal event or a Black Friday offer, so it looks realistic.
  • A fixed-amount discount can also be named, for example as an $11 discount.
  • Name a discount code with a short word tied to the product or brand plus the percentage.
  • The shopper sees the saving in the code itself.
  • The code in use was a 15% code with that format. It was linked so the discount applies at checkout.
  • Code names used in the examples: WELCOME10 for a 10% welcome code. SAVE5 is for a $5 pop-up code. save five is for a $5 Klaviyo pop-up code. PARTNER5OFF is for an affiliate partner code. Klaviyo is an email tool. An affiliate partner is a person or site paid a fee for sales they send you.

Limits and eligibility

2 min

  • Limit sign-up coupon codes to one use per customer.

  • Without this limit, a reusable code can be spread widely and erode your margin.

  • Discount codes that SMS apps generate automatically may be reusable by any customer by default. SMS means text messages.

  • This is unlike the one-use-per-customer limit set in Shopify.

  • Before launching an SMS discount flow, check the usage limit of each generated code. A flow is an automatic message sequence.

  • Maximum discount uses can be set to 5 uses in total, 10 uses in total, or once per customer.

  • A code limited to one use in total can be redeemed by only one person.

  • Exclude customers who made recent purchases from discount promotions.

  • Specifically, exclude the most recent purchasers. This reduces complaints and buyer's remorse. Buyer's remorse is regret after buying.

  • If the buyer's email address is not known, a personal code can be limited by a short end date instead of by naming the customer.

  • Adding a single total use means only one person can redeem it.

  • The short window also creates urgency for the buyer.

  • During peak sales, a discount code input can serve different customer segments with different codes. The code input is the box where shoppers type a code. Peak sales means the busiest time.

  • For example, use one code per customer tier.

  • Each code entered in the cart shows its effect on the subtotal before checkout. Subtotal means the total before tax and shipping.

  • Keep a list of coupon codes the assistant may offer on its own. The assistant is the person or AI that answers customers.

  • This way, standard discounts never need approval.

  • Anything outside that list still requires the owner's approval.

  • Sample coupons are coupons for free product samples. Mark them for products that children could misuse as adults only.

  • Children rarely mail coupons in. Pair this with a one-sample-per-household rule.

  • Do not announce coupon redemption dates in advance.

  • Advance knowledge lets resellers buy up newspapers. Resellers are people who buy in bulk to sell again. Buy up means buy in large amounts.

  • Run such coupons in Sunday editions, which are harder to buy in bulk. Editions are issues of the newspaper.

  • Military or first responder discount with low volume. Low volume means few orders.

    1. Create a content page explaining the discount policy. A content page is a plain page on the store.
    2. Ask customers to email proof of service.
    3. Send a discount code after the proof is checked.

    No app is needed at this volume. Older (2022).

Expiry rules

1 min
  • Coupon codes should include expiration dates to create urgency and motivate immediate purchase decisions. An expiration date is the last day the code works.
  • A short validity window, such as 24 hours, creates urgency. The buyer must order before the code expires.
  • Pairing the window with a personal code makes the offer feel specific to that buyer.
  • An end date is a fixed point in time.
  • When a buyer is told when a code expires, state the date, the clock time and the time zone. For example, midnight Eastern time.
  • Saying only a relative word such as tomorrow leaves the deadline unclear.
  • Use one discount code with a fixed percentage that expires at a clear time on the same day.
  • Link the button so the discount applies automatically at checkout.
  • The offer in use was 15% off with a code that expires at midnight.
  • Strict expiry is not required on every offer.
  • Coupons that look permanent lose their urgency.
  • But a discount that is always "on sale" still works for buyers who use it now.
  • Example: a 15% code for first-time buyers that expires in 24 hours, shown in an exit popup.
  • Recurring expiring coupon codes, for example 5% or 10% off, rarely lead customers to see the discount as permanent.
  • Returning customers are happy to use the code and save money. Older (2019).
  • Upfront expiring coupons and a bonus gift can be used to compete with a supplier's own website. A bonus gift is a free extra item.
  • Only do this when you can match the supplier's pricing. Older (2020).

Expiring-code cycles

2 min
  • Expire the promoted coupon code twice a week, typically at midnight on Wednesday and Sunday.
  • Show a new code and offer for the next cycle.
  • These specific days convert best. The source sets the times at midnight EST, which is Eastern Standard Time.
  • Update the code and the expiry text in the bar together each time. The bar is the strip at the top of the page.
  • In each expiring-code cycle, the coupon code changes every time.
  • The discount percentage is only changed occasionally.
  • A store that keeps the same percentage still has to refresh the code and the bar text on schedule.
  • Standard promotion: 10% off with an expiring coupon code.
  • The offer itself stays evergreen, which means always available. Only the coupon code and expiry date change.
  • When one promotion ends, the next one starts with the same offer and a new code. Older (2018).
  • Expiring coupon promotions can run as a banner at the top of the store.
  • The banner ends on fixed weekdays, for example every Wednesday and Sunday.
  • When one promotion ends, the next one starts right away with the same offer and a new coupon code.
  • The banner never goes empty. Older (2018).
  • Alternative rhythm: change coupons on a regular rhythm, such as every Tuesday and Thursday or every Saturday.
  • Let them expire at least once a week.
  • Do not make them expire every single day.
  • Expiring coupon block: it shows a coupon text such as "10% off and free shipping". A block is a ready-made piece of the page.
  • The block is followed by a coupon code field.
  • The coupon code must already exist in Shopify before you enter it in the block.
  • Otherwise it will not work for visitors.
  • Expiring coupon block settings: you can change the word before the date (default "ends").
  • You can set the number of days until the offer expires.
  • You can change the date format. The default date format can be switched to day-month-year for stores outside the US.
  • Dynamic Day Cart Coupon block: it requires a discount code that must be created in Shopify before it is configured. The cart is the page where shoppers review their items.
  • The block then takes three inputs: the discount code, the percentage off, and a custom text that describes the day-based deadline.
  • The block uses a day slot in its custom text. A slot is a blank that gets filled in automatically.
  • The merchant (the store owner) writes the surrounding wording.
  • The offer deadline day is shown dynamically in the cart.
  • The merchant does not need to type a fixed weekday into the text each time.
  • A dynamic day coupon is not needed on a store without traffic. Traffic means visitors.
  • Hide or delete it at launch. Add it once there is traffic.
  • This way, its effect on conversion rates can be compared.
  • Meta ads: a promo code highlighted in the Meta ad setup appears to shoppers in the store. Meta is the company behind Facebook and Instagram ads.
  • The code is applied automatically at checkout.
  • Only use a code that will remain active permanently, such as an evergreen Shopify code.
  • An expired code breaks the running ads.

Coupon pages and promotion copy

1 min

  • Create a coupon page on your store that lists current expiring coupons.

  • Shoppers who are still comparing often search for these pages.

  • It can catch people who need one last push to buy.

  • Build a coupon page that lists your codes with a sense of urgency.

  • The codes do not have to expire in reality. The page only needs to make the deal feel time-limited.

  • Customers find it by searching your store name plus the word coupon. So an internal link is optional. An internal link is a link from another page on your store.

  • You can add a countdown timer.

  • Where the page states a specific end time to the buyer, the code must actually stop at that time.

  • Coupon pages can carry several offers, including product-specific codes.

  • Savvy shoppers who search for coupons do not want to miss a deal.

  • So an expiring offer is the final push to convert them.

  • Test different versions to see which one works in your niche.

  • Coupon page copy pairs a light, friendly welcome with a deadline line.

  • The welcome makes the visitor feel they found something exclusive.

  • The countdown line creates urgency to buy now.

    • Example A: You found our hidden savings page, so take 15 percent off before this code closes in 3 days.
    • Example B: Welcome to our insider deals list, and this bundle discount ends in 48 hours.

  • Discovery check: find discounts to use in this order:

    1. Review the discounts your top competitors run.
    2. Use low-value, tiered coupons rather than deep one-off discounts. Deep means large. One-off means only once.

Welcome and first-order discounts

2 min

  • Competitor stores offer a first-order discount of about 10 percent in exchange for an email signup.

  • Use a signup popup or banner with this offer to build an email list at the same time.

  • The welcome kit discount for newsletter sign-ups is usually 5-10%. Customers see this as the usual reward.

  • Offer a collection link right after the coupon code.

  • Customer VIP sign-up discount example: 30% off an $80 product. This is for customers who sign up for the launch list before it goes live. VIP means very important customers.

  • Create the matching welcome discount in Shopify before building the sign-up form. The sign-up form is where visitors enter their email.

    1. In the Shopify store, open Discounts and click Create discount.
    2. Select Amount off order.
    3. Enter the discount code, for example WELCOME10.
    4. Set the value to 10 percent off.
    5. Set minimum requirements to no minimum.
    6. Limit the code to one use per customer.
    7. Click Save.
    8. Before creating a new code, check whether a suitable code already exists and reuse it.

  • Welcome series coupon: select a static coupon. Static means a fixed code that does not change.

  • Enter the existing code, for example WELCOME10, and set it to 10 percent off.

  • Formula for a first-order offer: [discount amount] + [which order it applies to] + [time limit].

  • Naming all three makes the discount concrete. It also creates a deadline without vague promises. Older (2021-22).

    • Example 1: Take 10% off your first order, valid for the next 48 hours after you join the list.
    • Example 2: Get 15% off your first order, valid for 36 hours after you subscribe.

  • Do not offer a discount in the first two to three abandoned cart emails. An abandoned cart is a basket the shopper left unpaid.

  • Upfront discounts train customers to abandon carts and wait for rewards.

  • Introduce the discount offer only after multiple high-value touchpoints. Touchpoints are messages sent to the shopper.

  • The touchpoints are desire, pain, and risk mitigation emails.

  • Use a two-tier welcome offer.

  • Email subscribers get 10% off sitewide. SMS subscribers get an upgraded 15% code as a VIP reward.

  • The higher tier for SMS rewards the channel that also needs consent. Consent means permission to message.

Pop-up, SMS and email codes

2 min

  • Klaviyo (an email and pop-up tool) pop-up code, made in Shopify (the store software):

    1. Go to Discounts in the left menu and create a new discount.
    2. Enter a name, for example "save five".
    3. Choose fixed amount (a set dollar amount off, not a percent) and enter $5.
    4. Optional: limit the code to one use per customer, or set a minimum purchase (the cart must reach this total first), for example $50.
    5. Save the discount and give the code to the pop-up success step (the message shown after the visitor signs up).

  • Clavio pop-up coupon, made in Shopify:

    1. In Shopify, go to Discounts and click Create discount.
    2. Choose Amount off order (a dollar amount off the whole order).
    3. Enter a code name such as SAVE5.
    4. Select a fixed amount of 5 dollars.
    5. Set the usage limit to one use per customer.
    6. Click Save discount, then enter the same code in the Clavio pop-up coupon field.

  • SMS discount link (a link sent by text message): build it as your store domain (your web address) followed by /discount/ and the code name, for example storedomain.com/discount/CODE. The discount must be set to auto-apply (applied by itself at checkout, the payment step), so the customer does not need to type the code.

  • Before going live, test both the SMS code and the email code at checkout with a real item in the cart. Confirm the discount is applied automatically and the amount matches the offer shown in the message.

  • Test a customer who has both an email code and an SMS code. Confirm at checkout that the 15% code replaces the 10% code instead of stacking (adding together). Run this test with a cart of realistic value, such as about $110 in the example, so the discounted total is visible before payment.

  • Use a pop-up that asks what the visitor wants (for example focus and memory, reduce inflammation, boost immunity, improve gut health) and reveals a mystery discount (a hidden amount shown after they choose). Include an exit option that says no thanks and pay full price.

Shopping, ad and Merchant Center promotions

1 min

  • Discount codes shown in Google Shopping ads (product ads on Google) appear as promotions. Advertising a discount on lower-margin products can cost margin (the profit kept per sale) without adding sales, so offer discounts only where the margin allows. 2025.

  • Merchant Center (Google's product listing tool) promotions appear inside Shopping ads. They are created in Merchant Center, not in Google Ads. They only become visible after the products have synced (uploaded and matched to Google) and the account is serving ads (showing ads). They will not show when the Merchant Center account is first created.

  • Create a Merchant Center promotion as follows:

    1. Open the promotions section in the left column of Merchant Center.
    2. Enter an ID name (a name for your own records) for the promotion.
    3. Select the country in which the promotion should be served.
    4. Enter the promotion details.
    5. Enter the redemption code (the code the customer types in at checkout).
    6. Apply the promotion to specific products or to all products.

  • Promotions must be clear and testable: the coupon code must work on the store, because Google bots (automatic checking programs) try to enter it. Submit promotions in advance so they can be re-submitted (sent again) if not approved. Older (2022).

  • Set up promotions in Google Merchant Center. Percent-off promotions on products can be set up there. Older (2022).

  • Do not offer free shipping in Google Merchant Center promotions without a coupon code if the policy has changed. Instead, use a different promotion type, such as a fixed-amount discount on a large order.

Offer strength by traffic source and product type

2 min

  • Traffic from TikTok (visitors arriving from the TikTok app) expects strong deals. Buy-two-get-10-percent-off and buy-two-get-5-percent-off offers are weak. Audiences look for discounts of roughly 20, 30, 40 or 50 percent.

  • Traffic from TikTok expects strong deals, typically 20%, 30%, 40%, or 50% off. Small discounts such as 5% or 10% off usually do not work for this audience.

  • Strong offers that work on the store include buy one get one free (BOGO, two for the price of one), buy this and get this item free with the product, and a maximum discount of 70 percent off. 50 percent off is the usual baseline. Electronics may not suit buy one get one free, while items like jewelry often suit it.

  • Bundle offer (several items sold together): buy two items and receive a fixed discount of $14. A separate code of $5 was offered for customers who wanted to buy only one item. Both codes were used a lot.

  • Blanket example: the store offered a 30 unit discount on the blanket, with free shipping within Switzerland and delivery in 2 to 3 days from local stock (items already kept in the country). A buy-two offer saved 14, and a single-item code saved 5.

  • Partner and backlink coupon strategy (a backlink is a link from another website to yours; an affiliate partner is someone who sells your product for a share of the sale; a coupon aggregator is a site that lists discount codes):

    1. Create exclusive coupon codes for affiliate partners (e.g., PARTNER5OFF).
    2. List coupons on coupon aggregator websites in your niche (the narrow market you sell to).
    3. Use free shipping coupons, percentage discounts (5% off), or holiday-specific codes.
    4. Monitor which coupons drive conversions (sales from visitors) and repeat the best performers.
    5. Some aggregator sites provide backlinks; prioritize those.

  • Launch discount strategy: offer a 50% off coupon to generate rapid sales. Example: a 50% off coupon resulted in 402 sales in 2 weeks at break-even price (the price where you make neither profit nor loss). Benefits: it drives rankings (a higher place in search results), generates reviews, and creates repeat customers, especially for consumables (products that get used up, like supplements).

  • Asking a supplier (the company that makes or ships your products) to include custom coupon cards in shipments is allowed. Some suppliers agree and others do not have the resources, so the request is worth making. Older (2018).

Where sources disagree

2 min

  • Expiry cadence (how often offers expire): Wednesday and Sunday midnight is the twice-weekly cycle in the Source A material. The Tuesday/Thursday or Saturday rhythm is an alternative, with the same rule of at least one expiry per week and never daily. Use the Wednesday and Sunday cycle when running a banner that refreshes twice weekly. Use the alternative when a store wants a different rhythm.

  • Expiry on every offer. Expiration dates create urgency (a feeling that you must act now). Strict expiry is not required on every offer, because always-on offers still work for buyers who use them now. Use strict expiry for time-limited campaigns and dated promotions. Use always-on offers for standing benefits (ongoing perks).

  • Coupon-page deadlines. A coupon page only needs to feel time-limited (look like it ends soon). The codes need not expire in reality. Where the page or code states a specific end time, the code must stop at that time.

  • Percentage versus dollar-off. Percentage (a percent off the price) is the default and the usual Shopify choice. Dollar-off (a fixed dollar amount) suits pop-ups and emails, and higher-AOV (average order value, the typical amount per order) or bundle offers. Both are roughly equivalent in effect.

  • Size of discount. The starting test size is 5%. Coupons are commonly 5-10%. Volume and traffic offers need 20-50%. Each figure depends on context: test size, competitor baseline (what rival shops offer), or audience expectation. Margin decides whether any of them is affordable.

  • Standing sitewide discounts (a discount on the whole site that stays on). A standing discount erodes margin, meaning it slowly cuts profit. A sitewide 10% reduced to 5% keeps a site-wide discount while protecting margin. An expiring sitewide code is an accepted format.

  • Minimum order values (the smallest cart total that unlocks an offer). Course A's example recommended a starting threshold of about $200 at AOVs of $300-$1,000 **. The Shopify example sets $2,000 as the threshold. Choose the threshold to match your own AOV.

  • Free shipping in promotions. Free shipping is an option on coupons. Under Merchant Center policy changes, free shipping is not offered without a coupon code.

Playbook › 7. Offers, pricing and bundles

Upsells, cross-sells and add-ons on the product page and cart

Section 7.15 · 15 of 28 in this chapter · 30 min read

In short

  • Upsells (offers to buy a bigger or better version) and cross-sells (offers for related products) make each order bigger.
  • Test each offer. Results range from no gain to a big rise.
  • Check the profit on each add-on before you offer it.
  • Hide free items from the cart. Check the cart before you publish.
  • The sources disagree on price limits, so set your own using your profit and shipping costs.
This section has 17 parts. Tap one to open it.

Definitions and core principles

2 min
  • An upsell offers a comparable higher-end or premium version of the item the customer is already buying, or adds value to it. It is presented at checkout or after purchase, when intent is already established.
  • A cross-sell offers a different, related or complementary item that enhances the main purchase, such as a paddle sold with a board. Cross-sell is what happens when a customer is asked whether they also want a matching accessory, not a more expensive version.
  • Upselling means persuading a customer who already wants a product to buy a higher-priced or upgraded version. The goal is more revenue and margin from the same buyer through extras that improve the experience, not new customers.
  • Cross-selling means offering a related or complementary product next to the item the customer is already buying, so they add it to the cart or buy both together. Typical placements are a "frequently bought together" block or a "customers also bought" section.
  • Upsells and cross-sells both aim to raise average order value (AOV) and profit margin. An upsell adds more value from the same customer, usually the same or a higher-priced version of the item. A cross-sell adds a complementary or supplementary product from the same niche.
  • A cross-sell is an offer for a different item than the main product. An upsell is an offer that adds value or a higher version of the item in the order.
  • In e-commerce the classic upsell term is often replaced by "reselling to the same customer". Classic upselling means moving a buyer from a cheaper product to a larger or more expensive one, such as a $100 item to a $1,000 item.
  • Upselling in e-commerce means increasing the money earned from each existing customer, not squeezing them. It works because the customer came to solve a problem, and a second product can solve it better or solve a related problem they commonly have.
  • Upsells and cross-sells raise AOV and profit margin. They work for every store, even one that sells a single product, and should be added at some level.
  • Upselling is judged by margin as well as price. A higher-margin item that customers accept can outweigh a more expensive item with thin profit. The upsell should be a better or more suitable product for the customer, not a pressure tactic.
  • Products that feel like a natural add-on convert better than random extras. Upsell products should complement or supplement the main product so the buyer sees an obvious use for them.
  • Cross-selling is a different item offered in the cart at a reduced price against its regular price. Shoppers see a clear saving on the add-on, while the store earns extra margin on each order.
  • Cross-sell offers should imitate good in-store sales help: suggest only items that actually go with the purchase and fit the customer's need. Showing a large pile of extras at once overwhelms the buyer and reduces conversion.
  • Low-ticket products can be offered as add-ons on a main product page instead of creating separate product pages for them.

Value, purpose and the Value Equation

2 min
  • To create compelling upsells, solve for Perceived Value = Dream Outcome / (Effort + Time + Sacrifice). Example: a supplement upsell of $500 offers the dream outcome (focus, entrepreneurial success, accountability), reduces effort and time (1-on-1 VA coaching, done-with-you approach), and removes doubt (money-back guarantee). Position upsells as faster paths to the desired outcome than the base product alone.
  • Offer complementary products at cart level or inside a bundle when they solve a related need for the same buyer. Shoppers who buy a main product for a specific purpose are more likely to add a closely related accessory than an unrelated item.
  • Bundle upsells and cross-sells of another product that solves a similar problem can raise AOV. This works well in markets where the product problem is common.
  • Accessories that complement the main product, such as a coaster or metal straw for a coffee product, can work well as add-on bundle items.
  • Customers already buying the core product are most likely to add items they need to use it. Proven pairs include snowboard with snowboard bag, trampoline with safety net, fireplace with fireplace screen, and camera with memory card.
  • In a beauty niche with mostly female buyers, a related beauty tool, such as a facial massage set, works well in the cart. The offer should come from the same niche and speak to the same audience as the main product.
  • Only add cross-sell items that customers buy together with the core product, such as ottomans with chairs. Do not add unrelated bulky items such as rugs to a sofa store unless the supplier sells them as a set with the main product.
  • Cross-selling and upselling are about relevance. A cross-sell that is not logically tied to the main product gets ignored.
  • A "frequently bought together" block under or beside the main product is a cross-sell format: a related item is offered in one tap next to what the shopper is already viewing.
  • Marketplace product pages often show a "frequently bought together" block listing complementary items for a combined purchase. A drop-shipping store can copy this as a cross-sell by showing one or two matching products next to the main item at checkout.

Choosing relevant add-ons

2 min
  • Use the Product Upsell Planner spreadsheet to identify which of your best-selling products have complementary items available as upsells.
  • Identify which products have high quality and high margins. Determine which services competitors offer offline (installation, consultation, buyers clubs). Discover non-tangible offerings (warranties, memberships) that customers would genuinely value.
  • Look for products where a better-fitting comparable item exists, and focus there. Upsell options should be shown where they make sense, not on every product.
  • Ask suppliers which add-on or upsell opportunities they can offer. Suppliers often know which accessories and options sell well with their products.
  • For each top product, ask suppliers which accessories sell well with it, since some of the best-selling add-ons are ones you would not expect.
  • Accessories work as upsells and downsells at checkout or after a sale. They are one of the simplest ways to raise margins in an existing store.
  • Upselling and cross-selling are most useful when the store has a clear core product. Once the store focuses on one product type, add recommended products, add-ons and upsells to the product pages, since these make it easier to increase order value.
  • In a competitive niche, maximising AOV matters most: add-ons and bundles can turn unprofitable items into profitable ones. The extra profit from accessories can let you outbid competitors who only break even on the main product.
  • A "frequently bought with" section and bundle sets lift AOV. An $8 product can reach about $60 per order through a subscription plus add-ons, which is what makes paid traffic profitable.
  • Upsells and cross-sells can increase profit margins on a low-priced TikTok product. They raise AOV without raising the main product price too far.
  • Upsell and cross-sell offers can raise the profit margin per order when the core product is priced in the sweet spot.
  • Offer a second item in a different colour or variant, since buyers often purchase two at once. This raises the value of each order without raising ad cost.
  • Customers who buy one unit of a product that suits multiple units can be offered a second unit at a discount.

Types of upsell

2 min
  • There are five types of e-commerce upsell: multiple purchases of the same product, add-on accessories, more expensive products, related products, and bundles of several products.
  • Multiple-purchase upsell: offer the customer who is already buying product X a second unit at a discount on the second item, e.g. "buy two, get a discount on the second one". It works for products that suit multiple units and does not fit every catalogue, so other types are needed for the rest.
  • Add-on upsell: offer an accessory that enhances the core product and makes it solve the customer's problem better. Example pattern: a phone plus headphones that allow private listening at a comfortable volume.
  • More expensive product: an upsell to a more expensive product is allowed and not limited to cheaper add-ons. A customer buying a $500 product can be offered a $1,000 complementary product as a second purchase, with or without a special deal.
  • Related-product upsell: when customers share a passion or project that needs several products, offer a product that is not a direct add-on but serves that project. Example pattern: a grill buyer building an outdoor kitchen is offered an outdoor fridge.
  • Related-product offers do not have to go to every customer. Only the proportion of buyers who want the second product matters; the others may ignore the offer without harm.
  • Bundle upsell: after a first purchase, offer a set of three or four products, often lower-priced add-ons possibly mixed with a more expensive item, presented as working well together and sold at a combined price lower than buying separately.
  • An upsell can be a bundle that combines a core product with related items at one combined price, or an add-on accessory sold alongside it. Both formats work in the same on-site positions, so choose the format for each core product rather than using one format across the whole store.
  • Replace-item upsells are for an upgrade of the same product line, such as a premium fabric with a longer warranty. State the price difference clearly. Do not offer a competitor brand, because that pushes the customer away from the purchase.
  • A comparison chart can act as a light buyer guide on a product page. It shows the standard version next to an upgraded version, for example with a longer warranty or stronger material, and pushes buyers toward the upgrade.
  • A comparison chart upsell works best when two versions of one product differ on one visible specification, such as water capacity or cup size. The chart should show only the differences, so the buyer who viewed the cheaper model can see why the larger one fits better, without criticising the cheaper option.
  • Non-tangible upsells, such as extended warranties, installation services, design consultations or a buyer's club, add value without adding physical stock. Look at what top offline competitors offer as upsells and bring similar services to the online store.
  • Intangible offers are add-on services that cost the store little, such as an extended warranty, same-day shipping or white glove delivery.

Rollout: the 80/20 rule and timing

2 min
  • Apply the 80/20 rule to cross-selling: add cross-sell options first to your best-selling products, then expand to lower-volume items as you refine the approach.
  • Start with the top 20 percent of products by sales, get those working, then roll the setup across the rest of the store.
  • Upsell and cross-sell by focusing on the top 20 percent of products and suggesting one add-on at a time that fits that product and customer. Results vary from no gain to a large lift, such as a 30 percent rise in AOV for those products.
  • Offer add-on options for your top 20 to 30 products where possible.
  • Sources differ on how many products to start with:
    • Source B starts with the most-sold products from a report, entering up to 20 core products in the planner.
    • Source A sets the first scope at the top 20 percent of products by sales.
    • Source A sets the scope at the top 20 to 30 products.
    • Source C says to test the single product first and add complementary products later.
  • Timing differs by store stage. Sources differ:
    • In a brand-new store, do not start cross-selling early. Focus first on getting authorised to sell with the best brands in your core niche, optimising your ads and building the store fully. Add cross-sells once the core store is in place.
    • Cross-selling is a feature to add as the store grows, not something to set up from the first day.
    • Cross-sell early when the supplier already offers items that complete the same collection as your main product. This applies because you are already authorised to sell those items, competitors are likely bundling them, and customers want the matching set.
    • Upsells and cross-sells work for every store, even one that sells a single product, and should be added at some level.
    • For a start, test the single product first and add complementary products later. Refill or subscription items, such as replacement brushes, are an example of add-ons to add afterwards.
  • After the core niche is established and the store is built out and optimised, expand the cross-sell options further. Adding more options earlier spreads focus before the basics are working.
  • Example: a main dining set priced at 2,500 USD could be paired with a matching rug at an extra 800 USD and a matching buffet at an extra 1,200 USD. Because these items are part of the same collection, they are strong early cross-sell candidates.
  • Upsell and cross-sell by focusing on the top 20 percent of products (see above).
  • Average order value of about 350 dollars can be raised by adding closely related higher-price products, not by unrelated categories.
  • Use Shopify product upsell app rules to show offers that depend on the product, such as a snowboard bag for a snowboard buyer.

Upsell planner sheets

2 min
  • Upsell planner sheet fields per core product: Core Product Title, Special Offer? (a Paused column is also present), Type Of Upsell, and Upsell Product 1, Upsell Product 2, Upsell Product 3. Upsell product names must match existing product titles in the Shopify store.
  • Plan at most three upsell products per core product. One or two is fine, and there is no single correct number.
  • For each upsell, record the type (multiple, add-on, more expensive product, related product or bundle) and whether a special offer applies. Upsell products are not paid for with ads, so a discount can be offered even on items normally not discounted.
  • Build one upsell planner list first, pairing each core product with its matching upsell products. The same list then drives the on-site app settings, a separate email flow for each core product, and the script for customer service agents, so all three channels stay consistent.
  • Upsell offers are delivered on site, by email or during customer service, not by paid ads. Because acquisition cost was already paid on the first order, the upsell carries no customer acquisition cost.
  • A special offer on an upsell adds urgency and feels like a deal made just for the customer. A bundle can carry its discount built in, since the overall cost falls when several products are sold together.

Upsell planning procedure

  1. Log into Shopify and pull a most-sold-products report, or use the equivalent report in Google Analytics.
  2. Enter the exact titles of up to 20 core products in the planner.
  3. For each core product, enter up to three upsell products by exact Shopify title.
  4. Enter the upsell type.
  5. Record whether a special offer applies.
  6. Keep the sheet for the next step, planning how the upsell is presented.

Conditional logic and triggers

1 min
  • Use conditional logic to show a related option only after the customer selects a specific item. For example, show a car rack after a paddle is chosen, and a bag after a car rack is chosen. This walks the customer through a process rather than showing every option at once.
  • For a paddleboard store, logical cross-sell steps are the paddle, then a car rack after the paddle is chosen, then a bag after the car rack, and a rash guard as a further option. Each step depends on the item the customer already selected, so the offers follow the buying process rather than appearing all at once.
  • Configure triggers based on the product the customer is viewing and what is in their cart, so each customer sees a targeted offer.
  • A single store can hold several conditional funnels triggered by different products, so each product gets its own flow. Free-shipping thresholds and add-on suggestions are configured as multiple conditions.
  • Low-ticket add-ons can be shown based on what is already in the cart.
  • Cross-sell suggestions can be tied to tags in the theme (see Tools and setup).

Hidden add-on products

1 min
  • Add-on products are set up as product pages that are not placed in any collection, not linked in navigation, and not included in product feeds or ads. They appear only as a drop-down add-on on the main product page. (2023 advice.)
  • Low-priced accessories that should only be sold as add-ons can be set to hidden in Shopify, so they stay out of the catalogue and search. They can still be attached to a high-ticket product through an add-on setup.
  • Cheap add-ons, such as a paddle for a paddleboard, should not be listed as separate products or advertised. Show them only as add-ons and upsells, so they are bought only together with the main item.
  • Low-ticket accessories priced between 10 and 200 dollars are best offered as cross-sells on high-ticket product pages, not promoted separately or sold through a PDF catalogue.
  • Supplemental low-priced products: if offered with a high-ticket item, the ideal scenario is that both ship from the same supplier, with no extra shipping fee. If separate shipping is required, use the product only as an add-on or upsell on the high-ticket product page, not as a standalone product with paid traffic.
  • Example: a high-ticket bow at $800 and a repeat-purchase consumable, arrows at $100 per 20-pack. List the arrows as an add-on upsell for bow buyers, and separately offer them with organic traffic only for remarketing. Do not pay for traffic to the arrow page. Exception: if the repeat-purchase rate is high, the arrows can be included as a bundled product page.

Product page upsells and add-ons

2 min
  • Show upsells as attachments or add-ons to the main product, framed as benefits within the product section, so they feel native and do not interrupt the main purchase path.
  • Place add-on offers on the product page near the add-to-cart button, with a prompt asking whether the customer wants to add the item to the order and showing its price. Example: a $149 accessory bag offered beside a $1,000 paddleboard.
  • Place bundles directly above the description, under the buy button, and add upsells at checkout. One example offered a second pair with 10 percent off, and another offered priority processing at a higher price.
  • Downsells and upsells placed high on the product page increase AOV more than those placed low on the page. Bundles work best when visible early.
  • The main product is sold with the option of adding cheaper related items, such as necklaces or jewellery, directly on the product page. The upsells are shown as add-ons next to the main item instead of on the homepage, so the store stays focused on its core category. Example 1: a bag product page offering a matching wallet and a keychain as add-ons at checkout. Example 2: a candle product page offering a matching wick trimmer and a gift box as add-ons on the same page.
  • Keep the base price in the listing and offer paid add-ons on the product page, so the total rises only when the customer chooses extras. The formula is: low base price in ads, optional add-ons at checkout, total increases at the point of decision. This works because customers click on the price they searched for and only then see the extras. (2018 source.)
  • Example: a $600 camping chair listed at $600 in Google Shopping, with a $40 cooler bag, a $25 cushion and a $15 carry bag offered as add-ons on the product page. The total can reach $680 only if the customer chooses them. (2018 source.)
  • When a key accessory is not included with the product, do not add it to the base price. Offer it as an optional add-on with a yes or no choice and its own price on the product page.
  • Keep the main product page free of unrelated extras, since they add friction.
  • Once the store focuses on one product type, add recommended products, add-ons and upsells to the product pages.
  • Upsell options that work in almost any niche are expedited shipping, extended warranties, and protective or accessory products. For a sofa, examples are a leather protectant, an anti-slip pad, or an upgrade to real leather.
  • Add-on products such as extended warranties or maintenance plans can be sold through the Bold Product Options app. The add-on price appears directly on the product page and the customer adds it to the order there. Alternatives are separate add-on product pages or draft orders.
  • Configuring one cross-sell product in Bold Product Options takes about 10 to 20 minutes. The interface is simple and needs no coding.
  • Example: a store selling pellet grills can offer these accessories alongside the main grill: utensils, pellets, cedar planks, thermometers, smoking racks, grill covers, brisket slicing knives, marinades and rubs, meat shredding claws and cast iron cookware. Pellets, planks and rubs are consumables that bring the customer back for repeat orders.

Cart upsells and cart drawer

5 min

An upsell is an extra offer to buy a better or bigger version. A cross-sell is an offer for a related product. Both appear in the cart, which is the list of items the buyer wants to buy.

Cart upsell setup in Visually

Visually is an app for upsells in the cart.

  1. Add a Products Upsells widget below the last product in the cart. A widget is a ready-made box you drop onto a page.
  2. Choose the Blocks List layout, which is a vertical list (items stacked one above another).
  3. Show three products only, without a Show More button (a button that opens more items).
  4. Set how each product looks:
    • Place Add to Cart on the right.
    • Move the zoom button (the magnifying icon that enlarges the picture) to the bottom left.
    • Show product reviews above the product name.
  5. Style the block:
    • Add a heading such as "Our Personal Favorites".
    • Set the block spacing (the gap around each block) to 10 on both sides.
    • Make the reviews black.
    • Set the Add to Cart border radius (how round the corners are) to 0.
    • Make the zoom icon background transparent, with no border or shadow (no outline or dark edge).
  6. Choose the Personalized algorithm (the rules that pick the products) and the filters listed below.
  7. Set up the test:
    • Set Audience (who sees it) to All Visitors.
    • Set Pages to Cart page.
    • Set Device to Mobile (phones).
    • Name the test, for example "mobile cart sales".
    • Add AOV as a goal. AOV means average order value, the average amount one buyer spends per order.
    • Tick the box, then publish the test.

Visually layouts and algorithms

A layout is the shape the products are shown in. An algorithm is the set of rules that picks them.

  • Visually upsell layouts:
    • Blocks List shows a vertical list of blocks (boxes stacked top to bottom).
    • Columns splits the space evenly for up to 3 upsells.
    • Boxes Slider is a scrollable list of upsells (you scroll through them).
    • Blocks Slider is a horizontal slider of blocks (a row you slide sideways).
    • Grid shows two or more columns of upsells.
    • Choose the layout that fits the cart space and the number of products to show.
  • Visually upsell algorithms include:
    • Manually (you choose the items).
    • Personalized (based on the customer's behaviour, meaning what they do on the site).
    • Advanced Rules (custom logic, meaning your own if-then rules).
    • New Arrivals
    • Viewed Together
    • Viewed With Recently Viewed
    • Purchased Together
    • Most Popular (sold most in the past 30 days).
    • Recently Viewed
    • Purchased With Recently Purchased
  • Personalized was chosen as the default for a cart upsell. It is AI-powered (run by artificial intelligence, software that learns patterns).

In-stock and price filters

A filter is a rule that hides items that do not fit.

  • Filter cart upsells so they show only items that are in stock (ready to ship).
  • Filter cart upsells so they show only items not already in the cart.
  • Filter cart upsells so they show only items priced above zero.
  • Without the price filter, a free add-on such as a free loop can appear as an upsell.
  • A free product offered as a cart upsell can take the top spot and confuse the shopper.
  • So exclude items priced at zero from upsell filters.
  • Check the upsell block in the cart preview (a test view of the cart) before publishing.
  • Confirm no free item appears in the preview.
  • When running a sitewide markdown (a price cut on the whole store), review which products the cart recommendation block shows.
  • Recommendations should be chosen on purpose for the promotion, not left to default logic (the app's automatic choice).

Cart drawer and post-add-to-cart upsells

A cart drawer is a slide-out cart panel. Post-add-to-cart means right after the buyer adds an item.

  • Enable a slide-out cart that shows complementary products (items that go well with the main one). Use the Monster Cart Upsell app.
  • Add a free-shipping threshold (the cart total needed for free delivery), for example free shipping over $50.
  • This encourages buyers to add a cheap item, such as a $20 product.
  • A back-pain store's cart slide-out offers complementary items next to the main product.
  • Examples of those items: a back stretcher, a neck product, a second neck product and a pain relief cream.
  • Every buyer sees a logical next add-on at checkout (the final payment step).
  • Set up a post-add-to-cart upsell sequence before checkout.
  • Add a visible skip button on each step.
  • Example: deodorant offered at £12 and hair care at £24, both skippable (the buyer can skip them).
  • A slider add-to-cart block (a row of products you slide through) can be added after a visitor adds an item to the cart.
  • A pop-up (a box that appears over the page) offering an upgraded bundle can be added at the same point.
  • This is useful for stores with many products because it presents more options.
  • Pre-checkout upsells (offers shown before the payment step) have two standard placements:
    • The product page, where buy-one-get-one (BOGO) and bundle offers are most common;
    • The cart page, where add-ons, free-shipping goals and extra items fit best.
  • Cart drawer threshold messages push customers to add more to reach a reward.
    • Example: adding 10 more items for free shipping.
    • Example: adding 13 more items for 20% off.
    • Another variant: adding a $30 item to unlock free shipping.
  • Tiered cart offers (different rewards at different cart values), using Monster Cart Upsell. Values are examples; adjust to your prices:

about 30 free gift
about 60 to 70 20% off
  • Other examples: add 3.99 to get free shipping, and add 13.4 to unlock 20% off.
  • Sources differ on the free-shipping threshold. The values depend on the store:
    • Monster Cart Upsell example: free shipping over $50.
    • Tiered table: free shipping at a cart value of about 15.
    • Examples: add 3.99 for free shipping, or a $30 item to unlock it.

  • Pre-select low-priced optional extras in the cart (already ticked for the buyer), such as:
    • priority processing (the order is handled first);
    • shipping protection (insurance if a parcel is lost or damaged);
    • extended warranty (a longer protection promise);
    • gift wrapping.
  • Keep each extra under 5 USD, for example 3.99 to 5.99 USD. Most buyers accept it without much thought.
  • Example: a 6-month extended warranty priced around 3.90 to 5.90 USD can be pre-selected in the cart.
  • Pre-selecting it keeps the add-on small enough that most buyers leave it checked.

Pricing, margin and price limits

4 min

Margin is the money you keep from a sale after costs. Net margin is what is left after all costs.

  • Upsell products should cost about 20 to 30 percent of the core product price (the main item).
  • Upsell products should never cost more than the core price.
  • Keep upsells above roughly 50 dollars, because very low-priced add-ons are not worth the effort.
  • Sources differ on the upsell price ceiling (the highest allowed price):
    • Source A: upsell products are 20 to 30 percent of the core price and never above it. This applies to add-on upsells on the product page.
    • Source B: an upsell to a more expensive complementary product is allowed.
      • Example: a customer buying a $500 product can be offered a $1,000 complementary product as a second purchase.
      • This works with or without a special deal.
      • This applies to a second purchase of a related higher-priced product.
  • Sources differ on minimum prices for add-ons:
    • Source A: keep upsells above roughly $50.
    • Source A: low-ticket accessories (cheap extras) priced between 10 and 200 dollars are best offered as cross-sells on high-ticket product pages (pages for expensive items).
    • Source A: cross-sell products under $200 as add-ons to orders of high-ticket items.
    • Source C: pre-selected cart extras stay under 5 USD (3.99 to 5.99 USD).
    • Source C: a small ebook for about 5 to 10 dollars can be sold as a cross-sell alongside the main product.
  • Accessories must be profitable when offered in-store.
  • Example: a $30 accessory at 30 percent margin yields $9. That is not enough if shipping costs $9.
  • Either price the add-on for profit or make it yourself.
  • If the supplier's accessory is expensive, for example an $80 wholesale cost (the price you pay the supplier), do not give it away for free.
  • Offer it as an add-on option priced close to wholesale cost. It still beats competitors that do not offer it.
  • When an add-on comes from a different supplier, check that the margin still works before offering it.
  • Offer upsell add-ons sourced from the same supplier as the main product, so shipping is bundled and simpler.
  • If the same supplier has no suitable add-on, use an add-on from a different supplier.
  • Lead value optimisation (LVO, a way to earn more from each buyer) raises AOV through product options, add-ons and upgraded shipping options.
  • These are the tools to reach a 30% net margin when the base margin (the margin before add-ons) is thin.
  • LVO adds margin on top of the MAP (minimum advertised price, the base price shown) through cross-sells and upgrades.
  • Upgrades include expedited shipping (fast shipping for a fee), extended warranties, and product add-ons.
  • Revenue per order then becomes MAP plus cross-sells and upgrades.
  • This widens the spread (the gap between cost and price) when prices rise.
  • Upsells, downsells (a cheaper option offered when the buyer says no) and complementary accessories (items used with the main product) raise AOV and customer lifetime value (CLV, the total a customer spends over time).
  • For a main product, the buyer typically also needs accessories and consumables (items used up, such as refills). So offer these add-ons in the store.
  • Price tiers or upsells add value for low-frequency purchases (things bought rarely).
  • The store should still be built around first-time buyers and their trust. (2023 advice.)
  • Example: a store adds a Gua Sha Facial Set (a face massage tool set) to the cart as a cross-sell.
    • The regular price was 69 USD. The cross-sell price was 39 USD.
    • The cart value for the same customer rose from 49 USD to 88 USD, with the same ad cost per customer.
    • AOV and profit rose without extra acquisition cost (the cost to win one buyer), so the offer worked.
  • Cross-sell by offering a product only available with a specific main purchase, at a discounted price.
  • For example, a store selling necklaces can offer a matching ring at a discount.
  • The exclusivity makes the offer feel scarce (limited, hard to get).
  • Complementary cross-sell: offer a related product to customers who are already ready to buy, at a discounted price, so the order value rises.
  • Making the add-on exclusive to a specific purchase adds scarcity and fear of missing out (FOMO).
  • Example: a customer buying a necklace is offered a matching ring at a reduced price, available only with the necklace.
  • Increase AOV with add-ons and upsells.

Shopify is an online store platform.

  • Warranty exclusions (what is not covered), such as theft and customer-inflicted damage (damage the buyer causes), should be stated in small print.
  • The terms should be visible but easy to miss.
  • For an electronics product, a warranty can be added at checkout as a cross-sell, not as a separate upsell.
  • Common terms are coverage for 12 months, one year or three years.

Gift options

1 min
  • Offer optional gift wrapping at checkout for roughly $5 to $10, depending on product size.

Finding complementary products

Finding a complementary add-on product

Complementary products are products that go well together.

  1. Find your product on Amazon.
  2. Check the "Frequently bought together" section (a list of items shoppers often buy together) for product ideas.
  3. Research your customers to see what problems they have and what would help them.
  4. Offer the best-fitting product as an add-on.
  • Example: for a cleaning product, a useful add-on is an e-book (a digital book) with tips.
  • You can buy a PLR (private label rights, a product you can rebrand and sell as your own) e-book.
  • You can also make one yourself in Canva (an online design tool).
  • A bought e-book still needs a cover and pictures to look professional.
  • If you have no idea which product to upsell, use Shopify product recommendations (automatic suggestions) or a recommendation app.
  • This approach prefers choosing a specific product in advance.
  • The choice should be based on what each buyer purchased.
  • Find upsell ideas by identifying:
    • high-quality, high-margin products (products that earn a lot of profit per sale);
    • The offline services competitors provide (services shops offer in person);
    • non-tangible offerings customers would value.
  • Look at what top offline competitors offer as upsells.
  • Bring similar services to the online store.
  • A small ebook on a topic related to the niche (your product area), such as the best products for a target group (the people you sell to), can be sold as a cross-sell.
  • The ebook sells for about 5 to 10 dollars alongside the main product.

Cross-sell setup in the theme (without a third-party app)

1 min

A theme is the store's design template. Source: Source A, older advice where it still applies.

Setting up cross-selling in theme settings

  1. Enable the cross-sell option.
  2. Enter the heading text shown above the suggested products, for example "you may also be interested in".
  3. Select the collection (a group of products) that holds the main products, for example a longboards collection.
  4. Add the tags (short labels on products) "cross-sell one" and "cross-sell two" to the products that should be suggested for those main products.
  5. Test by adding a main product to the cart.
    • Confirm the heading and the tagged suggestions appear.
    • Check the cart shows the added items.
  • Cross-selling example: a seller of stand-up paddleboards suggests a paddle to buyers of a board.
  • The customer can add both in one order.
  • The suggested item should logically match the main product.
  • In the Manhattan theme (a store design template), cross-sell suggestions appear right after a customer clicks add to cart, before the cart page opens.
  • The suggestion heading and the tagged products show at that step, so the customer can add them without leaving the flow.
  • For native cross-selling in the Manhattan theme (built in, with no extra app), the main products are defined by the chosen collection.
  • The suggested products are the ones carrying the cross-sell tags.
  • Tags therefore go on the add-on products, not on the main products.
  • Cross-selling is a feature to add as the store grows, not something to set up from the first day.
  • Suggested products should make sense together so that customers add them to the cart.

More rules worth knowing

1 min
  • Cross-selling succeeds when you explain how the related product adds value or solves a secondary need (a second problem).
  • Simply listing options without context does not work as well.
  • Collection pages can raise average order value (AOV) because a customer who adds the winning product can also add other items from the same page to the cart.
  • This is one reason to send ads to a collection page instead of a single product page once a product wins.

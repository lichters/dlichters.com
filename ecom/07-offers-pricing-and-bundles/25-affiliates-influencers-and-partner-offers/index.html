Affiliates, influencers and partner offers

Section 7.25 · 25 of 28 in this chapter · 10 min read

In short

  • An affiliate is a partner who sells your product and earns a commission (a cut of each sale).
  • Give affiliates only your best sellers, not your whole product list.
  • Set each commission from that product's profit, not one rate for everything.
  • Do not run affiliate offers on products you often have no stock for.
  • Keep the cookie window long, 60 days or more. A cookie is a small file that remembers which affiliate sent the buyer.
This section has 13 parts. Tap one to open it.

Choosing which products affiliates may promote

1 min
  • Offer affiliates your top 10 bestselling products, not your whole catalog (your full list of products). Affiliates cannot tell which items are worth promoting or reliably in stock if everything is open to them.
  • Do not give affiliates access to your entire catalog and expect them to find the winners.
  • Offer your best sellers and tell affiliates which products sell.
  • Reason: affiliates want offers that convert (turn a visitor into a buyer).
  • Do not run affiliate offers on products that are frequently out of stock (you cannot ship them right now).
  • Promoting unavailable inventory (the stock you hold or can sell) wastes affiliate traffic (visitors) and effort.
  • You still owe commissions on cancelled orders, and you do not get the payout back.

Setting affiliate commission rates

1 min
  • Choose product-specific commission rates, not one blanket rate (the same percentage for everything) across your entire catalog.
  • Set each rate from the profit margin (the money left after costs, as a share of the price) of that product or brand, not as one blanket rate.
  • The commission must still leave you profit.
  • The commission must also be more attractive than alternatives, such as the Amazon affiliate program.
  • Common ranges for high-ticket products (expensive products) are 5-10% of the sale price.
  • Or use a fixed amount, such as $50 per transaction (one completed sale).
  • Worked margin case (a step-by-step calculation): your supplier margin (the margin on what the supplier charges you) is 30%, and you eliminate advertising costs.
  • With those numbers, an affiliate commission of 8-10% still leaves healthy profit.
  • Your only variable expenses (costs that change with each sale) are customer service and fulfillment (packing and shipping orders).
  • Reconciling the two figures: the 5-10% range is the general benchmark (a standard to compare against).
  • The 8-10% figure applies to a product with a 30% supplier margin and no advertising cost.
  • Calculate from your own margins before using any benchmark.
  • Calculate affiliate commission rates from your product margins and cost structure (all your costs together) before offering them.
  • Affiliate offers at 3-9% commission are not worth it for core products (your main products).
  • Promote an affiliate product only when it helps customers reach the same goal and you do not sell it directly.
  • Example: a treadmill store can promote meal delivery kits as an affiliate, because treadmill buyers often want to lose weight.
  • The store does not sell the meals itself and earns a commission on promotion.
  • Example of a high commission on a digital product (a file or online item with no shipping): a treadmill store offers a $37 ebook (a digital book) on abs with a 75 percent affiliate commission.
  • That means about $28 per sale.

Cookie windows and affiliate motivation

1 min
  • Longer cookie windows (60 days or unlimited) encourage affiliates to keep promoting.
  • They do this because affiliates earn commission on delayed conversions (sales made later, after the visitor left).
  • Affiliates stop promoting when sales do not trigger payouts within your cookie duration (how long the cookie lasts).

Onboarding a new affiliate

1 min

Onboarding means bringing a new partner on board.

  1. Look at the new affiliate's site and the products they already recommend.
  2. Choose the products on your store that fit their audience and that you know sell.
  3. Send those products to the affiliate, or let them switch an existing link to your store if you offer a better rate.
  4. Keep the product list to products you can actually sell, since an affiliate will not sell what you cannot sell yourself.

Affiliates and MAP pricing

1 min
  • MAP (minimum advertised price) is the lowest price you allow to be shown in ads.
  • Affiliate commissions do not violate MAP.
  • The customer still sees the advertised price at MAP.
  • The commission paid to the affiliate is a separate business transaction.

Where to find affiliate programs

1 min
  • An affiliate marketplace is a site where many affiliate programs are listed.
  • For information products (digital guides and courses), use ClickBank, the largest affiliate marketplace for digital products.
  • For physical products (items shipped to customers), use Commission Junction, ShareASale or Amazon Associates.
  • If your niche (a narrow market or topic) has nothing on ClickBank, search for niche resources, forums, communities and blogs.
  • Use these to see what your audience buys.

Partner and cross-promotional bonuses

1 min
  • Partner with indirect competitors: businesses in the same industry that sell something else, ideally a digital product or service.
  • They gain audience, and you gain a bonus offer your margin could not fund alone.
  • Example: a store selling a $500 home-brewing kit (for making beer at home) partners with a homebrew conference whose tickets normally cost $100.
  • The store offers buyers a 75 percent discount or a free pass.
  • It also partners with an online brewing school whose $47 intro course is given free.
  • Both partners gain exposure (more people seeing them) and members.
  • Partner bonuses are digital services from another company, such as a free first part of the service.
  • Other examples: a free trial of 14-day or 30-day length, or an extended trial of a membership.
  • These partners are usually happy to send you customers, so they often agree to free trials or extended discounts.
  • Cross-promotion within one store's funnel (the path a visitor follows from first visit to purchase), using a treadmill dropship store (a shop that does not hold stock; the supplier ships orders) as the starting point:
    1. Capture the visitor's email address, and place a pixel (a tracking code) so Facebook and Google can show them ads later.
    2. They are then offered a free weights store (dumbbells and similar) and a home gym store (both dropship stores).
    3. They are offered the $37 ebook on abs with a 75 percent affiliate commission.
    4. They are offered a Nutrisystem subscription (a meal plan brand), which pays recurring commission (repeated payments) for as long as the customer receives the meals.
    5. They are offered a three-week diet product on ClickBank that pays more than $100 per signup.
    6. Smaller accessories such as jump ropes sit in an Amazon Associate store that is used only for retargeting (showing ads again to people who already visited).

Revenue sharing with partner stores

1 min
  • In a brand partnership (working with another brand) you receive a percentage of each sale, similar to a traditional affiliate relationship.
  • You do not handle fulfillment for those orders.
  • The source does not state a specific commission rate.
  • Offer a partner store an affiliate-style cut only on products whose margin can cover that fee.
  • Sharing revenue is viable only when you can afford the commission.

Setting up a low-cost affiliate funnel

1 min
  • A bootstrapped funnel is one started with very little money.
  • Starting costs for a bootstrapped affiliate funnel total $22.
  • Domain (your web address): $10.
  • First-year hosting (where your website files are stored): $12.
  • Autoresponder (a tool that sends emails automatically): free for 30 days.
  • After the trial, autoresponder prices were about $19 per month.
  • Prices rise as the subscriber count (number of people on your email list) grows.
  • Example: an affiliate program paying $300 per referral (per person you send) for a digital course.
  • If the site costs $22 to set up, the first sale nets about $278.
  • The payout rises when the course price goes up.
  • When you charge a small amount, such as $7, to filter out visitors who only want free information, place the payment step on your own affiliate site.
  • Place it either at the opt-in (the sign-up form) or on the page right after it.
  • Do not run this paid step through Facebook ads.

Influencer and creator fee negotiation

2 min

A creator is a person who makes content and promotes products to their followers. An influencer is a creator with a large audience.

  • Pricing sequence for a creator fee:
    1. Define the usage terms (how you may use their content) and the exclusivity (they may not work for your rivals) you need.
    2. Open the negotiation and document each party's rights.
    3. Assess campaign complexity and the effort required.
    4. Define the exact deliverables (the items the creator must produce) and their scope.
    5. Evaluate the creator's track record (past results) and expertise, then negotiate the final fee with all of these factors included.
  • Check whether a top-tier creator (the biggest names) fits your budget before you pursue them.
  • Influencer fees range from about $50 to about $1 million per campaign.
  • So decide where your budget sits in that range first.
  • Budget and negotiation sequence:
    1. Assess your overall marketing budget and set a reasonable share for influencer marketing that does not compromise your wider strategy.
    2. Prioritize creators who fit within that budget and your goals. If you cannot afford a creator, pick one within your range.
    3. Research industry standards for audience size, engagement rate (the share of followers who like or comment), content quality and niche, so you neither overpay nor underpay.
    4. Adjust the market rate for expertise, campaign complexity and exclusivity, and reflect these adjustments in the contract.
    5. State your budget and constraints openly, and communicate throughout the negotiation.
    6. Show flexibility, for example through long-term collaboration, mutual promotion or affiliate arrangements.
    7. Emphasize the mutual benefit of the partnership, since a brand that offers unique value to the creator's audience can get rate adjustments.
    8. Discuss a long-term partnership, where a multi-period deal can lower the rate, gives the creator steady income and gives you a reliable creator.
  • Paying more or less than the market rate, because you are uninformed about benchmarks for the niche and creator size, leads to overpaying or underpaying.
  • Research benchmarks before making an offer.
  • Pursuing a high-profile creator who is outside your budget wastes time and money.
  • Do not commit to a creator you cannot afford, even if they are your first choice.
  • Find one who fits both your budget and your goals.
  • Creators are often willing to come down to your budget when communication is good and they genuinely want to work with the brand.
  • Be transparent about your budget limits from the start.
  • Long-term partnerships usually lower the cost per campaign (what one campaign costs you).
  • The creator gets regular income, and the brand gets a creator who keeps working with it.
  • Discuss a reduced rate in exchange for a longer commitment.
  • A brand's first offer to a creator was $200, and it was negotiated upward toward $400.
  • The full amount was paid via PayPal (an online payment service).
  • Starting low leaves room to pay more and still close the deal.
  • Set aside about $500 as a test budget for influencer campaigns.
  • The minimum a creator typically accepts is about $200, and some campaigns will fail.
  • Use a discount code (a code that lowers the buyer's price) for influencers only when your margin allows it.
  • A 25% discount code was affordable only because the margin left enough profit on each sale.
  • The code tracked the influencer's campaign.

Maximum affordable fee and break-even

1 min

Break-even means the point where you stop losing money.

  • Calculate the maximum affordable influencer fee before negotiating.
  • Multiply the follower count by the engagement rate.
  • Multiply that result by a conservative conversion rate (a cautious, low estimate) such as 1 percent.
  • Multiply the result by the product price.
  • Compare the expected revenue against the cost of the promotion.
  • This shows whether it is profitable in the worst case.
  • Example: an influencer with 75,000 followers and a 5 percent engagement rate reaches about 3,750 people per story (a short post that disappears after a day).
  • At 1 percent conversion, about 37 buyers at a $39 price bring about $1,462 worst-case revenue.
  • A $600 investment is then covered on the first promotion.
  • Break-even formula (a calculation rule): Break-even units = promotion fee / profit margin per sold unit.
  • Example: a $500 fee with a $25 margin per unit requires 20 units sold to break even.

Influencer payment structures

1 min
  • Cash-flow split (money coming in and going out) when the brand funds all costs up front (paid first): 80 percent of the deal goes to the brand and 20 percent to the influencer for the first three months.
  • After that, a 60/40 split in favour of the brand is mentioned as the next step.
  • Use 80/20 for the initial period and 60/40 afterwards, depending on the phase of the partnership.
  • If you have no cash flow to fund the promotion, offer a 50/50 revenue split instead.
  • The brand provides value upfront by showing results, using relevant case studies (real examples of past results) from other industries if its own track record is missing.
  • Use 50/50 when the brand cannot fund costs, and 80/20 when it can.
  • For smaller creators, a free product exchange is often accepted if the product is good and the creator's niche fits.
  • If they decline, offer payment instead.
  • Payment options for a paid creator post:
    • Pay the full amount up front, or
    • Pay half up front plus the product, and the second half after the video is posted and has stayed live for the agreed period (for example 24 hours or longer).

Affiliate-style creator incentives

1 min
  • An affiliate commission for a creator can be a fixed amount per sale, for example $2 per order, or a percentage.
  • Tiered commissions (rates that rise at higher sales levels) based on sales volume are also possible.
  • Affiliate incentives let a creator keep earning from promoting you after the initial post.
  • This builds a longer relationship.
  • This is useful when cash budgets are limited.

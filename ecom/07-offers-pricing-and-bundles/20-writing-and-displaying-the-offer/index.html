Writing and displaying the offer

Section 7.20 · 20 of 28 in this chapter · 20 min read

In short

  • Say your one clear offer in every ad. Vague offers like "50% off + free shipping" now get ignored.
  • Test one offer per ad, so you can see which one works.
  • Show the exact saving in dollars or percent. Never show a bigger discount than you really give.
  • Check every price and percentage with a calculator before it goes live. A wrong number destroys trust.
  • Bundles and free add-ons make customers feel they get more for the price of one.
More key points
  • Keep the offer simple and the site clear. Confusing offers fail, even strong ones.
  • Discount emails work best when they state the action, the exact percentage, the code, and the expiry time.
  • Do not add extra product offers to abandoned-cart emails by default.
  • Never put a sale graphic over a Google Shopping product image, or the ad can be rejected.

Offer copy formulas and patterns

4 min
  • Say the offer clearly in your ad copy.
  • Generic offers, such as "50% off + free shipping," are now expected. They will underperform.
  • The offer affects two things: click-through rate (CTR, the share of people who click the ad) and conversion rate (CVR, the share of visitors who buy).
  • Choose an enticing offer that stands out from competitors.
  • Use one offer per ad variant (one version of an ad, tested against other versions), so the results can be compared.
  • Most-aware customers (buyers who already know what they want) need only a reason to buy now.
  • Push a direct offer: a percentage discount, a free gift or a promotion.
  • Add a clear buy-now action.
  • Extra explanation is usually not needed at this stage.
  • A prospect (a possible buyer) who already knows the product and wants it needs only a short headline.
  • The headline needs only two things: the product name and a bargain price.
  • The price is the news.
  • The body lists the strongest points.
  • The body ends with a store name or coupon, and a call to buy.
  • This is simple, mechanical work. Pay for it at a rate that matches.
  • The value equation (a simple rule for how buyers judge value) works like this.
  • Value rises when two things go up: the dream outcome (the result the buyer wants) and the perceived likelihood (how sure the buyer feels they will get it).
  • Value also rises when three things go down: time, effort and sacrifice (what the buyer must give up to get there).
  • Copy should raise the first two and shrink the last three at the same time.
  • Higher-priced products need more copy (written sales text) to justify the price and create the need.
  • A low price that looks like clear value needs less copy.
  • A low price that seems too cheap to trust needs more copy to build trust.
  • Vivid, detailed pictures of the satisfaction a product gives reduce how much the buyer cares about the price.
  • Show as many concrete scenes of use as you can, without repeating yourself.
  • More scenes mean stronger demand.
  • Stronger demand also means weaker price resistance (how hard the buyer pushes back on the price).
  • If a product seems to need several units to be useful, frame it as a gift for someone else.
  • This removes the buyer's objection (the reason they say no) that they only need one.
  • Gift framing can support single-unit purchases alongside multi-unit offers.
  • Offer call-to-action (CTA, the line that tells the buyer what to do) formula: pair one concrete price benefit, such as a percentage discount, with a second version.
  • The second version gives a free gift with purchase today.
  • A clear, time-bound incentive (a reward with a deadline) turns interest into action.
    • Examples: "Get your set at 25% off before the weekend ends." and "Order today and get a free travel case with your purchase."
    • The example call to action used a 40% discount on a pair of the product. A second version offered a free gift with purchase on the same day.
  • Stacking pattern (combining several benefits in one line): put a percentage discount, a free bonus and a shipping or time condition together.
  • This gives the buyer who already knows the product more than one reason to act now.
    • Formula: discount % + bonus item + condition (free shipping, online only, or a deadline)
    • Examples: "Take 30% off today and get a travel case free with your first order." and "Save 15%, ship free, and add a refill pack at no cost, online orders this weekend only."
  • Spend-threshold headline (a headline that names a minimum order total) formula: Save $AMOUNT on all orders over $THRESHOLD!
  • It works because it states the exact saving and the condition to unlock it in one line. The shopper knows what to spend.
    • Examples: "Save $25 on all orders over $200!" and "Save $80 on every order over $400!"
  • Seasonal sale formula: [season or event] sale: free shipping plus [discount percentage] off with code [short word tied to the store theme]
  • The event name creates timing.
  • Free shipping removes the cost objection.
  • A concrete percentage with a themed code makes the offer specific and memorable.
    • Examples: "Summer sale: free shipping plus 20% off with code SURFSIDE" and "Winter sale: free shipping plus 25% off with code FROSTBITE".
  • Dynamic day coupon text formula (a discount line that ends with a day limit): a short call to action that names the discount code, states the percentage off and ends with a day name that limits the offer
  • The code, the saving and the deadline are visible in one line.
  • This lowers hesitation (doubt) at checkout, the payment step.
    • Examples: "Enter SPRING15 now and save 15% through Sunday only." and "Use code BREW20 today to get 20% off until Friday only."
  • Bundle title pattern (a bundle is a set of items sold together): name the outcome the bundle gives and the exact saving in the same line.
  • The discount is then visible before the shopper reaches the cart, the basket where items wait for payment.
    • Examples: "Start your morning and save 12%" and "Build your tea set and save 15%".
  • Combined value formula: show the combined value of the items, then the bundle price, then the saving in dollars or percent.
  • Buyers judge a deal by the amount they save.
  • A visible saving makes an add-on (an extra item added to a main purchase) feel almost free.
    • Examples: "Complete the set: total value 74.99, today 59.99, you save 15." and "Add the second item and save 20% on the pair, shipped to one address."
  • Keep the offer simple.
  • An offer that confuses visitors hurts sales more than it helps.
  • Use one clear offer with a simple structure that people can follow straight through to checkout.

Offer naming

1 min
  • Magic Offer naming structure (a recipe for naming an offer): MAGNET + AVATAR + GOAL + INTERVAL + CONTAINER
  • You do not have to use all five elements.
  • MAGNET is the hook, such as free, giveaway, percent off, spring, summer, new or holiday.
  • AVATAR is the target customer or interest.
  • GOAL is the problem solved or benefit, such as pain free, stress relief, hair cure or thicker hair.
  • INTERVAL is the time to the result, such as 5 minutes, 30 days, 21 days or 6 weeks.
  • CONTAINER is the format, such as bundle, program, shortcut, rebirth, attack, blueprint, sprint, catapult, comeback pack or journey.
  • Keep offer names short and punchy.
  • Words that rhyme or start with the same letter sound better.
  • The name should signal the target customer, the problem solved and how quickly the result comes.
  • Example of a combined offer name using an interval, a goal and a container: "five minute hair enhancer bundle."

Ad copy and headlines

2 min
  • Ice Cap migraine relief ad: 25% discount plus free shipping.
  • It uses several short testimonials (customer quotes) as social proof (other people's praise that builds trust).
    • "Finally real relief without meds"
    • "I feel in control again"
    • "Stops my migraines fast"
  • It opens with a pain-point line (a line about the problem the reader has): "Sick of the migraines?"
  • It stresses that 50K+ (more than 50,000) users were relieved.
  • Holistic positioning (treating the whole person, not one symptom) uses the words "wholistic and calming solution." This appeals to health-conscious customers.
  • Ice Cap pricing: 25% discount plus free shipping, with the exact price not shown in ads.
  • This suggests a sufficient profit margin (money left after costs) to absorb the shipping cost while keeping margin on the 25% discount.
  • RYZe Mushroom Hot Cocoa ad: a single image with minimal copy.
  • The copy reads "NO MORE SLEEPLESS NIGHTS."
  • An ingredient list shows benefits. Examples: Reishi (a mushroom) for stress relief, and Melatonin (a sleep hormone) for sleep support.
  • A simple CTA reads "TRY IT."
  • This format works for supplements (products you take to support health) and wellness products.
  • It works when the ingredient proof is strong enough without testimonials.
  • Bottom-of-funnel (aimed at buyers close to buying) static ads (still-image ads) for offers use 25% or 50% discounts (BWF ads).
  • They need little research because the offer itself carries the message.
  • Point the ads at the single collection (a group of products in your store) where a buy-X-get-Y offer applies (buy one item, get another). This way the ad message and the discount match.
  • In the example, the offer needed two items from that one collection. The free item also came from it.
  • Cut any offer copied from an example ad that does not appear on your landing page (the page a buyer arrives on after clicking), such as buy one get one free.
  • The ad promise must match the store.
  • AI-generated product names and descriptions should not be copied and pasted without editing.
  • The output needs human judgement and split testing (A/B testing, where two versions are tested against each other) before it goes live.
  • Full buyer guides target people who are not ready to buy.
  • Create them only after ads are profitable and dialled in (set up well).
  • Comparison charts are the better early option for buyers who are ready to purchase.
  • Compare discounted and undiscounted listings side by side.
  • A product shown with a discount in an ad usually gets a much higher click-through rate than the same product at MSRP (manufacturer's suggested retail price, the list price) beside it.
  • Rule for discount claims in ads and banners:
    • State the exact percentage.
    • State the product scope (which products it covers).
    • State whether a promo code (discount code) is needed.
    • List any excluded categories or products.
    • Never show a discount larger than the one you actually run.

  • Show the offer in the ad text or within the first three seconds of the video.
  • TikTok buyers expect a deal before they consider the product.

Sale banners and product page graphics

3 min
  • A product page graphic sits right above or below the add-to-cart button. It is used for sales or urgency (pressure to buy soon).
  • It can be removed, changed or swapped out with the season.
  • It works best with a promo code and an expiry date.
  • Sale banner formula:
    • A large heading with the discount percentage.
    • A line showing the coupon code as the action to take.
    • A small expiry line in a smaller font.
    • Use white text on a bright background that matches the brand colours.
    • Examples: "Summer Clearance: 15% Off All Boards" with "Use code SUMMER15 at checkout" and "Ends Sunday", and "Flash Sale: 20% Off Gear" with "Enter code FLASH20 at checkout" and "Expires Friday 11:59 PM".
  • A product page sale graphic should show four things:
    • The offer
    • The coupon code
    • where the code is entered
    • optionally, an expiry date to create urgency
  • Keep the wording short so it reads in a second.
  • Sale graphic formula:
    • A bold headline with the offer and the discount.
    • A line with the coupon code.
    • A line saying where to apply the code, such as at checkout.
    • An optional smaller line with an expiry date.
  • The buyer sees the offer, the code and the next action together, right next to the buy button.
    • Example 1: "Spring deal: take 20% off every order / Use code SPRING20 / Apply at checkout / Ends 31 May."
    • Example 2: "Free gift with every order / Add code WELCOME at checkout / Limited to this month."
  • Do not put a sale graphic over a product image in Google Shopping ads (Google's product ads). The ad can be disapproved (rejected by Google). (See Google Shopping and promotion display.)
  • Use a product-page pop-up for items with long lead times, such as six to ten weeks.
  • The pop-up tells the visitor the wait before they add the product to the cart.
  • It asks them to confirm before they continue. This prevents disappointed customers later.
  • Use "Add-on" or "Bundle" imagery.
  • Create an image in Canva (an online design tool), for example with the "product offer" template.
  • The image shows the main product with the free bonus.
  • Place it in the product photo gallery as the second image.
  • On the product page, show the main item in the default photo.
  • Display the add-on items being worn or used by the model in other photos.
  • The upsells (extra sales of add-on items) are visible without cluttering the main gallery.

Procedure: unique offer template for one product (Shopify, an online store platform)

  1. In Shopify, go to Online Store > Themes and click Customize.
  2. Open the product template dropdown (the menu of page layouts), choose Products, and select the default product page template.
  3. Click Create template, name it for the offer (for example "product page 50 off"), and base it on the default product page.
  4. Click the expiring discount code block (a box in the page design), change its text to the new offer (for example "$50 off and free shipping"), then save.
  5. Go to Products, open the target product, and in the right sidebar under Theme template change the default template to the new one.
  6. Save and preview to confirm the unique coupon shows only on that product.
  • A template assigned to zero products is not visible to shoppers.

Procedure: free-gift banner on a product page

  1. Design a free-gift banner image that matches the site's layout and colours.
  2. Show the product image, the product name and its value. For example, add a message that it ships free with the order today.
  3. Place the banner above the product description, close to the add-to-cart button, so every visitor sees it.
  4. Press Enter above the description in the product page editor, click insert image, then save.

Announcement bars and site-wide offers

1 min
  • An announcement bar is a thin strip at the top of the site.
  • A store-wide discount code can be placed in the announcement bar.
  • A code that applies only to specific products is shown on those products' pages through a dedicated product page template.
  • A promotion bar at the top of the homepage offers free shipping plus a percentage discount with a code.
  • This gives visitors a reason to act sooner.
  • The reviewed example suggested free shipping plus 15% off during a seasonal sale.
  • Announcement bars and exit-intent discounts (a pop-up shown when a visitor is leaving) can be swapped for free shipping offers when the margin does not allow a discount.
  • Use announcement bars to explain promotion rules where shoppers are focused.
  • For example, say that a free gift applies without discount codes.
  • Announcements can also link to collections or blog pages using inline HTML hyperlinks (clickable links written in HTML, the code language of web pages).
  • Show the offer, meaning exactly what the customer receives, near the top of the page.
  • Visitors want to know what they are getting before they read further.
  • Homepage sections can be added and filled later to highlight product promotions.
  • Start with only the basic homepage blocks at launch.
  • Add promotional sections as campaigns require.
  • Show the offer, the price and the call to action on the homepage, product pages and About Us page at minimum.
  • The curve offer (the offer named this way) can also appear on the cart page, in emails and in ads.
  • It must always be clearly visible.
  • Give customers with a specific need a direct link from the product page to a matching collection.
  • For example, a sensitive-skin collection introduced with the question "Sensitive skin?"
  • This keeps the main product page focused while still catching niche buyers.

Trust rows and reassurance

2 min
  • Pre-footer (the area just above the page footer, the bottom of the page) trust-building headlines:
    • Free Shipping $100+ (incentive)
    • Surfer Owned & Operated (authenticity)
    • Fast & Secure Checkout (convenience)
    • Easy Returns (confidence)
  • Example trust row from a reseller homepage (a shop that resells other brands' products).
  • It sits right below the header (the top section of the page) and has four points:
    • Free Shipping ("all the time, every time")
    • Give Us a Call (phone number shown)
    • 30 Day Easy Returns ("don't like or need it, send it back")
    • Price Match ("find a better price, we'll beat it")
  • Show free shipping on orders over $50 and a 90-day 100% money-back guarantee in the offer area of the product page.
  • The offer area is the part of the product page with the price and buy button.
  • Free-shipping promotions can be shown on product pages with images and graphics.
  • The shipping option itself appears in the cart.
  • Show free extras such as white glove delivery (a service where items are set up in the home) or bonus gifts on the product page.
  • Show them the same way you show bonus gifts, so buyers see the full value of the offer.
  • Free white glove is worth offering only when the product price is high enough that the margin covers its cost.
  • Note: a no-sales-tax policy can be shown in ads by default if competitors add sales tax and yours does not.
  • Mention the policy on product pages or in the header announcement bar, not in ads.

Sales page structure (long-form offer)

  1. Reveal the special report as the main offer.
  2. Introduce the subscription service, under which the report is received when the prospect joins.
  3. Show what others say, using testimonials for the service.
  4. Use a false close (a fake-looking high price shown first) that anchors a high price. An anchor is a first number that makes the next number look small.
  5. Present extra bonuses and show how each one has value.
  6. Show the value stack with the total value of everything.
  7. Cut the value down and reveal the final price.
  8. Add a guarantee as the risk reversal (a promise that moves the risk from the buyer to the seller).

Price display and savings math

3 min
  • Compare-at price display (the higher "original" price shown next to the sale price):
    • Show the higher compare-at price (for example 79.99 USD) struck through (with a line through it).
    • Show the sale price (for example 39.99 USD) in a larger font.
    • Add a "50% OFF" label.
    • Optionally add a time-limited scarcity line (a line that suggests few are left or time is short), such as "Limited time" or an event sale.
    • Page builders (apps for building page design), such as PageFly or GemPages, allow custom background colours for this highlight.
  • Highlight the discount in the price block in this order:
    1. Product name first
    2. Compare-at price
    3. Sale price in a larger font
    4. The text "50% off"
  • An event or time-limited scarcity line, such as "limited for today" or a seasonal sale, can be added.
  • Visitors read from left to right.
  • The first price they see should be the higher reference price.
  • The real sale price should be larger.
  • The contrast between the two numbers makes the saving visible at a glance.
  • Caution on heavy percentage banners: avoid heavy 50 percent off banners with limited-time urgency.
  • Many consumers now distrust them.
  • Show clear prices and present the store as a trusted brand instead.
    • Reconciling sources: the "50% OFF" label in the price block is used in and. warns against heavy 50% banners.
    • Use the label with clear, real prices and a restrained banner.
    • Reserve heavy urgency banners for offers you can defend.
  • In an offer lead (the opening of an offer), state the regular price, the special price and the dollar saving together.
  • For example, a $59 cover price (the printed price on the cover of a book or magazine) offered at $19 saves $40.
  • The dollar amount makes the value concrete for the reader.
  • Shopify savings label:
    • Enter the sale price and the MSRP (compare-at price) in Shopify.
    • Example: a sale price of $800 and an MSRP of $900 show the $900 struck through, $800 as the sale price, and a "Save $100" label.
    • The label is displayed automatically on every product page without extra work.
  • Do not list the sale price alone as the minimum advertised price (MAP, the lowest price a brand allows you to advertise).
  • Always show the discount against the MSRP.
  • Keep the expiring coupon promotion visible in addition to it.
  • Listing only the sale price gives visitors no sense of how much they save.
  • This weakens conversion even when the price is competitive.
  • Keep the time-limited coupon promotion in a separate banner on the product page, above the price.
  • Do not merge it into the MSRP display.
  • Visitors can then see the MSRP saving and the extra coupon saving at the same time.
  • Price-tag caution for supplier pricing: with MAP suppliers, the displayed price cannot be lowered.
  • Use the Google Shopping promotion feature to show a discount tag.
  • Apply the actual reduction with a coupon code at checkout.
  • Higher-priced products and sale copy: a free gift or discount described on the page should match the price logic.
  • Free white glove and similar extras pay only when the margin covers their cost.

Keeping the math clear

  • Calculate the exact saving before you write it on the store.
  • Example: 20% off two 34.99 USD items is not a 10 USD saving. 20% off 69.98 USD is about 14 USD.
  • A wrong figure damages trust.
  • Savings label formula for a compare-at price: (compare-at price minus sale price) divided by compare-at price, then rounded
  • Example: a $69.99 compare-at price with a $49.99 sale price gives (69.99 minus 49.99) divided by 69.99, which is about 28.6%. It is shown as "SAVE 29%".
  • Savings label calculation: MSRP (compare-at price) minus sale price
  • Example: a sale price of $1,199.99 with an MSRP of $1,299.99 displays "Save $100" automatically on the product page.
  • Discount email and checkout copy must match the math.
  • State the exact percentage and the expiry.
  • Never promise more than the store runs.
  • Discount claims in visuals and text must match the stored discount exactly.

Bundles and add-ons

1 min
  • Bundles and free add-ons make the customer feel they get several items for the price of one. This lifts offer appeal. A bundle is a set of items sold together.
  • Show bundles on the site as a bundle block, dropdown or image gallery. A bundle block is a box that groups the items. A dropdown is a menu the customer opens to see choices.
  • In a bulleted value recap, place a small product image beside each bullet, not text alone. A value recap is a list that shows what the offer is worth.
  • The image makes the stack of items look physically substantial.
  • Show each item's dollar value next to its image. The total is then easy to follow at a glance.
  • Merchandising means how products are arranged on the site. The order in which products appear influences results.
  • Keep the offer simple and the website design clear. Customers must understand how the offer works.
  • Confusing offers fail, even if they are strong.

Emails for offers

1 min
  • Pattern for the discount email: state the action and the exact percentage, then the code and the expiry time. A code is the word the shopper types at checkout. Expiry time is when the offer stops working.
    • Examples of my own: "Save 10% on your bag, valid until midnight tonight" and "Take 20% off your basket before the timer hits zero".
  • Do not upsell an abandoned cart by default. An abandoned cart is a basket the shopper left without paying. An upsell is an offer of extra items.
  • When a discount cuts your margin (the profit left per sale), add a best-seller product block to the offer email.
  • This block works as an AOV booster. AOV means average order value, the average amount spent per order.
  • The block lets the shopper add items with one click. The cart updates.

Google Shopping and promotion display

1 min
  • Google Shopping promotions can show offers such as free shipping or a discount code. Google Shopping is Google's product ad area.
  • Do not put a sale graphic over the product image. The ad can be disapproved, which means Google rejects it.
  • A Merchant Center promotion shows the offer in the Shopping ad. Merchant Center is Google's tool for your product data.
  • The shopper can hover over or click the offer to see the promotion details.
  • Shopping ads get the same extra incentive that promotion extensions give search ads. A promotion extension is a sale line shown under a search ad.
  • Promotion extensions show a sale offer, such as a Black Friday sale, directly under the search ad.
  • This gives searchers an extra incentive to visit the site.
  • They are a strong way to raise CTR (click-through rate, the share of people who see an ad and click it).
  • This feature was recently rolled out, so check its current availability in Google Ads.
  • Google Merchant Center promotions: add a promotion such as a 5 percent off coupon code in Merchant Center.
  • Google then shows a promo tab, a small label, on your Google Shopping ad with the promotion details. This can increase CTR.
  • Older advice, Source A 2021.
  • Price-tag caution for MAP suppliers: see the MAP note under price display. MAP means minimum advertised price, the lowest price a supplier lets you show.

Shopify setup and store-wide settings

1 min
  • Shopify is the platform that runs the store.
  • A store-wide discount code can be placed in the announcement bar. The announcement bar is the thin strip at the top of the site.
  • A product-specific code is shown through a dedicated product page template. A template is a page layout reused for many products.
  • Use the product template procedure above for a unique offer on one product.
  • Use the free-gift banner procedure above for a free gift on a product page. A banner is a wide strip with an image or text.

Further details

1 min
  • A free product offer that requires a $9.95 shipping and handling payment does not fit a store selling gifts. Shipping and handling is the fee to pack and send the order.
  • Placed prominently on the homepage, this kind of offer undermines trust.
  • Review offers like this for fairness and fit before publishing them.

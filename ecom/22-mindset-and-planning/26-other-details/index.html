Playbook › 22. Mindset and planning

Other details

Section 22.26 · 26 of 26 in this chapter · 12 min read

In short

  • Plan in this order: write a 10-year vision, set 3-year and 1-year goals, then pick your 90-day priorities.
  • Choose your dream customer before you pick products or ads.
  • Learn your topic well before you sell it.
  • Do the legal, bank and tax setup in order, with an accountant if you can.
  • Be honest with suppliers and buyers. A "no" from a supplier usually means "not yet."

Planning and review routine

2 min

Business fundamentals action plan: Work through these steps in this order.

  1. Write a vision document, which is a written picture of where the business is going. Describe the 10-year vision.
  2. Use the 10-year vision to set 3-year and 1-year goals. Record them in the tracking document, the sheet where you log progress.
  3. Pick your first Rocks (the few main priorities you must finish) for the next 90 days, based on your goals.
  4. Build weekly and daily to-do lists from the Rocks. Put the most important Rock task at the top of each list.
  5. Add to your calendar a fixed daily time to review your to-do list. Add a weekly time to plan next week's list.
  6. Schedule quarterly review times (every 3 months) to set new Rocks. Schedule one annual review (once a year) to check the business against its goals and set goals for next year.
  7. Complete the customer worksheet. Write what you know about your customer and what you still need to find out.
  8. Complete the competitor analysis worksheet, which compares your rival businesses. Use data from competitors' websites and traffic tools (tools that show how many visitors a site gets).
  9. Review your financial tracking system, which is how you track money. If it does not work, replace it with a proven system.
  10. Calculate your target cost per sale, which is the most you can spend to get one sale, using the traffic calculator (a tool for working out your numbers). Record the numbers. Compare them with your financial tracking to watch profit every day.
  • Keep the action checklist as a printable document. Tick off each item when it is done. This makes it easy to confirm that no planning step (vision, goals, Rocks, worksheets, calendar times) has been skipped.
  • Complete the customer worksheet before deciding on positioning (how you present your store) and offers. It records what you already know about your target customer and which customer facts you still need to find out.
  • Three factors decide whether a dropshipper succeeds. A dropshipper sells products that a supplier ships to the buyer for them. Knowledge alone is not enough:
    • A clear vision of the future you.
    • Discipline: knowing when and how to do each task.
    • Organization: structures, delegation (handing tasks to others) and clear next steps.

Customer and offer definition

1 min
  • Identify your dream customer, the ideal buyer you want, before choosing products and audiences. Dotcom Secrets, a book by Russell Brunson, is recommended for this.
  • Read the Alex Hormozi books (books by Alex Hormozi) for offer ideas. An offer is what you sell and on what terms. Read the first part of Dotcom Secrets to identify the dream customer and dream offer.
  • Do not sell a product on a topic you cannot teach. Learn a niche (a narrow topic or market) properly first. Real expertise lets you judge and improve competitor offers.
  • Build credibility (trust that you know what you are talking about) by showing who you are. Show your experience and why the buyer should trust you, such as your profession or years of practice in the niche.

Brand building and store quality

1 min
  • Build your brand gradually through iterative improvement (small changes, tested and repeated) over years. Do not expect overnight changes. This enables sustainable (lasting) growth and continuous brand evolution.
  • The Oodie brand (a brand name) was built iteratively over 4 to 5 years and continues to be refined. Lasting brand building requires long-term commitment and ongoing iteration.
  • First impressions determine customer lifetime value, which is the total money one customer brings you over time. Treat every interaction (shipping, support, unboxing) as brand building, not as a one-time transaction.
  • Good intentions amplify skill but cannot replace it. Buyers sense whether a seller is trying to help or to extract money. That difference affects long-term results.
  • The purpose of a design is to make the message understood and to lead the viewer toward the action the business wants. If too many elements compete for attention, none of them will be seen first.
  • If a self-built store does not look close to competitor sites, get help rather than continue with a poor result. Comparing your site with competitor sites is a quick check for quality.
  • Ask the supplier before using their reviews on your store. Reviews taken without permission can cause trouble with Google. Only use them after the supplier explicitly agrees.
  • Use an expert reviewer (someone who knows the field) for scientific or technical claims before publication. A writer may draw wrong conclusions from facts. Check any product-specific claim with someone who knows the science.

Legal, banking and tax setup

1 min

Legal and banking action steps, in order:

  1. Decide the country you will sell in. Choose your own country if it has an e-commerce market (buying and selling online). Otherwise, choose the US.
  2. Form the business entity, which is the legal form of your business. In the US, an LLC (limited liability company, a common company type) or an S-Corp (a company type with its own tax rules). In Australia, usually a sole trader (one person running a business alone), or a Pty Ltd (a private company) if an accountant advises it.
  3. Obtain the tax ID, a number the tax office gives your business, for your country. In the US, an EIN (a US tax number for businesses) and, if your state has sales tax (tax on sales), a sales tax permit (the state's permission to collect it). In Australia, at least an ABN (Australian Business Number).
  4. Find a business accountant (a person who handles business tax and books) if you do not already have one. Preferably choose one who knows dropshipping.
  5. Open a business bank account with low or no fees (usually free to open). Get a business credit card with rewards (points or cash back for spending). If no credit card is possible, use a debit card on the Visa or Mastercard network (the payment network).

Supplier relationships

2 min
  • Present your business to suppliers as a long-term operation, not a quick-money project. Make customer service the main priority.
  • Income from a store is not a reason to stop supplier approvals (a supplier's yes to work with you) or product uploads. Growth needs a constant flow of new products and suppliers, not one push.
  • When a supplier problem happens, judge the whole relationship.
    • If the supplier ships correctly most of the time and is otherwise responsive, work through isolated problems.
    • If a pattern of poor behavior emerges, cut them off.
  • Never misrepresent your business. Do not claim years of experience, past sales or a track record you do not have. Lying to suppliers tends to become a habit that spreads to customers and staff and hurts the business in the long run. Being a new business is no disadvantage in itself and does not automatically lead to a no.
  • A supplier refusal for now does not mean failure. If a supplier says they are not taking new retailers, record the reason and the date they suggested, then follow up at that time.
  • A no from a supplier usually means "no for now," not "no forever." Stay open-minded, energetic and confident. Suppliers are businesses that want to make money and want customers who respect their brand.
  • When a supplier says no or gives vague reasons, follow up on a set schedule rather than giving up. Repeated, polite contact with a status update shows commitment and does not get you blacklisted (put on a list of people to avoid). (2023)
  • Confidence on supplier calls comes from knowing your subject well. Do not fake knowledge. Study the material until you can speak about the business with real understanding.
  • When a supplier asks about annual sales goals for a brand-new store, a reasonable answer is a figure in a range between 2 and 500,000. The currency was not specified. (2023)

Handling a no from a supplier, in order:

  1. Thank them. Make sure you have said everything you can to get approved.
  2. Note in the worksheet exactly what they said. Include any date they suggested to call back.
  3. Set a reminder on your phone or in Google Calendar (a calendar app) for that date.
  4. Follow up when the reminder comes.

Competitors and market awareness

1 min
  • Study competitors (rival businesses) as future rivals, but do not take their work. Success in dropshipping should come from your own effort and original work.
  • Viewing Google Ads updates (changes to Google's paid ad system) as chances to optimize (improve) ahead of competitors gives a competitive advantage. Most businesses respond slowly to platform changes.

Sales conversations (High Probability approach)

1 min

  • Traditional selling aims to get the buyer to act. So every tactic becomes a form of pressure that triggers resistance. The High Probability approach (a sales method) replaces that goal with one question: is there a mutually acceptable basis (a basis both sides can accept) for doing business? If not, both sides end the process early without a deal.

  • Set aside old sales beliefs before judging a new method. Habits built on the old goal can conflict with the new one. Discomfort is normal while you practice and fades with repetition. Treat it as the cost of learning, not proof that the method is wrong.

  • Random negative reinforcement (a bad habit that is rewarded by chance now and then) means a few sales won through ineffective tactics can strengthen habits that fail over time. Judge your methods by results across many calls, not by individual wins.

  • Never try to convince a prospect (a possible buyer) that he needs or wants your offer. If he clearly understands what you offer and it does not match what he wants, end the visit. If he really wants it, he will not let you go.

  • Avoid the five-step selling sequence of attention, interest, desire, conviction and action as a selling model. It is built on manipulation, wastes time on people who will not buy, and places the close (the final ask for the sale) at the end when it should be the whole process.

  • When several prospects that sounded qualified (seemed like good matches) all end without a commitment, check three causes:

    • You are still new to the method.
    • You read weak signals as strong ones.
    • You slipped into answering or selling.

    Each cause can be corrected. Only the last one is about your behavior on the call.

Affiliate and lead value

1 min

  • Affiliate marketing (earning a commission for promoting other products) done only for quick money tends to fail. Promoting products you have not used, or do not believe in, shows in your emails and makes your recommendations less convincing.

  • Promote only products you have used, know someone who has used successfully, or have investigated thoroughly with a clear view of pros and cons. Recommending something you believe in is easier and more credible than recommending something you have not tested.

  • Focus on giving value to the audience instead of trying to extract money from them. A sincere recommendation is more likely to produce long-term sales than a short-term push.

  • The five lead value techniques (ways to earn money from people who sign up to your email list, called leads) are:

    • Cross promoting your own related stores (promoting one of your shops inside another).
    • Cross promoting affiliate stores (promoting other shops that pay you a commission).
    • Promoting information products (paid digital guides or similar content).
    • Upselling (offering a bigger or better version) add-ons at checkout.
    • Upselling intangible services (help or services that are not physical items).

    All of them depend on first collecting emails and installing tracking pixels (small pieces of code that record what visitors do) on visitors.

  • Affiliate program setting: members recommend the program with their personal affiliate link, a special web address that tracks who joins through it. You find it under the resources section. Each person who joins through the link earns the referrer 500 USD. Four to five referrals can recover the purchase price. Later spending by referred members can also pay out a share of their purchases.

Platform and ad settings

1 min

Display campaign for dynamic remarketing (setting):

  1. Check Dynamic ads (ads that show each viewer the products they looked at) for personalized ads.
  2. Select the Google Merchant Center data feed (your product list, sent to Google from the Merchant Center tool).
  3. Set focus to viewable impressions (ad views a person can actually see on screen).
  4. Use no filters to show all products.
  5. Set the CPM bid (the price you pay per 1,000 views) to $10.
  6. Target the smallest recent website visitor audience available (the group of people who will see the ad): 7 days, 30 days, or larger if traffic is low.
  7. Turn off optimized targeting (Google choosing extra people to show your ads to).
  8. Turn off audience expansion (letting ads reach more people than you chose).
  • Do not include video extensions (extra videos added to an ad) in display campaigns. Keep dynamic product display ads strictly as images with product links, not video.
  • Do not start campaigns immediately after creating the TikTok Ads account. Wait until the account review finishes (up to 24 hours) and the status shows as Active.
  • On Instagram, a video uploaded as a Reel (a short vertical video) also appears as a regular feed post (a normal post in the feed). So one upload covers both formats. Verify this in the upload screen when you create the Reel.
  • Low reach (how few people see your posts) is often caused by strikes (warnings or penalties from the platform), restrictions or reports rather than poor content. Review account notices and guidelines before concluding that content is the problem.
  • Bing Webmaster Tools (Microsoft's tool for site owners) shows a profile completion percentage, which was 20% right after import (after moving your site data in). Filling in personal, professional or company details is optional. It is unclear whether it affects performance, so it should not be treated as a required setup step.

Learning, skills and mindset

1 min
  • Knowledge alone does not make a dropshipper successful. See the three factors under Planning and review routine.
  • Habits that help owners grow a large business are the same for very large and very small companies. Develop yourself into a person capable of growing a large business. This applies regardless of current size.
  • Customer service skills improve with practice. Expect to become faster and more comfortable over the first months. Learning by doing is the main way to build this skill.
  • Mind OS, a book on the psychology of the self (how the mind works), was recommended as one of the few useful books for building inner confidence. The price quoted was roughly $200 to $500, so it is an expensive purchase.
  • Coding (writing computer code) requires a real interest in the work, because progress is slow and frustrating on many days. Stay committed through setbacks (problems and failures). That is part of building the skill.

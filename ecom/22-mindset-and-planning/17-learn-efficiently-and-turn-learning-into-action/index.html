Playbook › 22. Mindset and planning

Learn efficiently and turn learning into action

Section 22.17 · 17 of 26 in this chapter · 22 min read

In short

  • Doing the steps matters more than talent or tools. Take action on each step.
  • Follow the steps in order. Do not skip ahead.
  • Finish each task before you start the next part.
  • Keep a notebook and take notes by hand from the first day.
  • Progress comes from small daily work. The first build takes at least about one month.
More key points
  • Plan for several months, not one month, to finish the full program.
  • Act on what you learn. Watching or reading without doing wastes time.
  • While you build your first online shop, do not buy new courses or chase every new idea.
  • Test several products and learn from the results. Do not judge after one try.
  • Keep following the steps, do not skip them, and keep learning.

Start with execution, not study

2 min
  • Doing the work matters more than talent or tools. A clear action plan helps a beginner move ahead of people who only study.
  • Knowing the right product, marketing (getting people to notice and buy) and customer service (helping buyers) methods is needed. But knowledge alone is not enough. What counts is applying each method step by step.
  • Reading or studying material has no value on its own. Results come only when you apply each method yourself, with full attention. Beginners who study without doing usually see no progress.
  • Learning by taking in information alone is not enough. Even if you have no questions, take action on what you have learned.
  • Learn by doing. Run ads (paid promotions), review the data with mentors (experienced coaches), review recorded sessions, and test several products. Do not only study theory.
  • Roughly 90% of the time, you already know what needs to be done. The real bottleneck (the slowest point that holds everything back) is execution, not knowledge. More learning is rarely the answer.
  • Marketing knowledge gives little result if the owner applies it with the wrong focus or uses time poorly. Execution is often the real bottleneck, not knowledge.
  • Tools, mentors and communities (groups of fellow students) can guide a business. But you must understand what happens in your store. Deliberate work on the business matters more than passively taking in material.
  • Own the research for your niche (a small market focus, such as one type of customer or one product group) and your products. Doing the research yourself builds the confidence to run the store and get through the hard early months.
  • Experienced operators (business owners who run stores) can get traffic (visitors coming to the store) to live products (products buyers can see) within two weeks of choosing a niche. They start supplier calls (talks with the companies that provide the products) right away and build a demo store (a sample store) while they wait. This is a target for experienced owners, not for beginners (2023).

Study the program in the right order

4 min
  • Follow the program's order. Each step builds on the one before it, so skipping ahead causes problems later. Mark each step as complete so you always know where you stand (2020).
  • Do not skip parts. Work through the training step by step, and ask questions when you are stuck (2021).
  • Follow the steps in order. Do not quit after suppliers say no, because rejections are normal when you contact many suppliers (2020).
  • Follow the build order: choose the niche, build the store, get supplier approvals, set up ads, then track analytics (the numbers that show how the store performs) and reply to customers quickly. Starting ads before the products are live wastes money (2020).
  • The checklist (a list of tasks to tick off) is a fixed order of phases (stages): community and planning, legal setup, niche and research, store build, suppliers, conversion (turning visitors into buyers), traffic, then automation (tools that run tasks by themselves). Each phase depends on the one before it, so complete tasks in sequence.
  • Do not start building the store before the product is chosen. Work in a fixed order: product research first, then store build, then ads. Move on only after the homework for the current step is finished.
  • Store readiness and approvals: the store (your online shop) must be ready before launch, but suppliers approve step by step. Get approvals, upload products, then add traffic, in that order (2023).
  • Each part ends with action tasks that must be finished before the next part starts. The processes behind them are used for every new store, so skipping a task makes later steps harder to follow.
  • If the earlier brainstorming steps (gathering ideas) were skipped, go back and complete them before adding ideas to the niche list. The list is meant to build on that earlier work.
  • Follow the program fully instead of freestyling (doing your own thing). Studying only part of the material and building your own ideas, such as nine different stores, before reaching mass success is likely to fail.
  • Discipline is essential in e-commerce (selling products online). Follow the prescribed process exactly, and do not deviate from it before you have significant results.
  • Three requirements for success: follow the training material, take part in the community, and take action on what you learn. Combine these with the right mindset (way of thinking).
  • Choosing only the parts that feel appealing, skipping the rest, or studying only part of each section usually leads to no results. Work through every step in order and do not shortcut the material.
  • Go through every part of the program, not just the ones that seem known. The only exception for beginners is skipping parts tagged as advanced. Skipping material means acting on incomplete information, which wastes time and ad money.
  • Do not skip any part, even if it looks familiar, because skipped material can lead to costly mistakes later. Treat the action tasks as assignments, since each one builds on the ones before it.
  • Learning the technical side of building a store is useful even when someone else builds it for you, because the training shows how each part is set up (2020).
  • Learning the basics of Shopify (a platform for building online stores) yourself is necessary even if you hire people, because you need to run and understand your own store. Building a first site yourself can bring early sales. Paying a professional later, once you have money, is also a valid path.
  • Building a Shopify store is not technically complex, although it can be tedious (slow and boring). A beginner can finish it by following the instructions in order.
  • Do not spend time on highly technical details of e-commerce before the store is live. Focus on proven steps and learn the rest as the store runs (2024).
  • Learn the foundations of paid traffic before collecting tips, because specific tips expire as ad types and interfaces (screens and controls) change. Without a strong foundation, more tips do not improve results.
  • Study the theory and psychology behind paid search campaigns (ads shown when people search) before building campaigns. Repeat the material as often as needed until the ideas are clear.
  • Pay-per-click advertising (paid ads where you pay each time someone clicks) is new to most beginners. It is normal for it to take time and practice. Confusion at the start is common and is not a personal failing.
  • Go through the full training from start to finish before setting up or changing anything in settings. Do not stop to build things during the first pass. Then go back through it step by step, because seeing the whole system first makes later decisions more intentional.

Choose your starting track

2 min

  • Choose your track by stage. A brand-new business starts with the build track (the steps for setting up a new business). An owner with a business already open starts with the grow track (the steps for growing a business that is running). Do not work through the grow track before your first business is launched, unless you are opening a second or third business (2021-22).

  • Build track, in this order (2021-22):

    1. Welcome
    2. Find Your Market
    3. Build Your Website
    4. Secure Your Supplies
    5. Launch Your Business
    6. Running Your Business Day to Day

    Go through each part in sequence, without skipping or jumping around, and complete the action task each part asks for. Ignore the grow track and the smaller programs until the business is launched, because the order is designed to avoid unnecessary difficulty.

  • The build track has 6 parts. The grow track had 12 parts at the time of recording. Expect to spend at least about one month on the build track (2021-22).

  • The sources disagree on build timing. One says at least about one month. Another says a beginner in high-ticket dropshipping (selling expensive products that a supplier ships to the buyer for you) should expect a few weeks of steady work, not a few days. Fast results come from steady daily effort, not from cramming many hours into one session. Pressure to finish quickly tends to cause burnout (exhaustion from pushing too hard) and dropout (quitting) (2021-22). Use the one-month figure as the planning minimum and the few-weeks figure as the expected range for steady work.

  • When building a first or second high-ticket dropshipping business, stay focused on the build track and ignore other content until the store is ready to launch. Work through the instructions in order, and only later move on to growing the business (2021-22).

  • A high-ticket dropshipping store is ready to sell once every action step has been completed in sequence. Follow the step-by-step instructions exactly as given. Complete the action step that ends most parts before moving on (2021-22).

  • Grow track for a business launched within the last 12 months (2021-22):

    1. Start with part 1.
    2. Work through the parts in order (2, 3, 4, and so on).
    3. Take the action task from each part before moving on.

    There is no time limit or deadline, so move forward in small, regular steps.

  • An owner with several years of business experience should start with the part covering their biggest known weakness, because that is usually where the largest knowledge gaps are. Afterwards, the remaining parts can be worked through, since even stronger areas can be improved (2021-22).

  • Beginners new to online business should first learn the basics and choose a niche. They do not need to finish all training before launching. Work through the action tasks at the end of each training section in order to make progress (2019).

  • The 21-day launch plan and the 7-week quick start guide do not replace the program content. They only guide you through the same material at different paces, so choose one or go at your own speed (2023).

Study-to-action ratio (the balance between studying and doing)

1 min
  • Studying the material should take less than 50% of the total time you spend on the program. Each part needs at least twice its study time as extra time for action and hands-on practice (doing it yourself).
  • Budget at least one hour of learning time for every 45 minutes of material. Pausing, going back and note-taking all take time.
  • Students who make progress put full effort into the product pitch (the sales message) and keep testing. They do not finish tasks in an hour and move on. Many quit before the first or second task.
  • Action tasks that run over a set period, such as 30 days, should start right after the related step. Carry them out over that whole period before expecting results.

Process each part with notes

2 min
  • Take handwritten notes on every part, because the material is too dense to remember without them. Use a physical notebook as the reference sheet for the action phase.
  • Have a physical notebook ready before starting any e-commerce training. Handwritten notes are needed from the first part onward. Do not begin without one.
  • Process each part with notes:
    1. Study a section and pause when needed.
    2. Go back and repeat any part that is unclear.
    3. Write notes on what was taught.
    4. Pause again, go back, and check the notes against the material.
    5. Re-read the notes and check that everything makes sense. If something does not click, go back and repeat.
    6. Allow time for this whole process, rather than going through everything in one go.
  • Notes help the action phase because they let you apply what you have already processed and put into your own words. That is faster than searching the material again for a detail.
  • Learning sticks better when you write your own key points and summaries of what you read or practice, rather than relying on ready-made summaries. Doing the analysis yourself is part of learning. Review your key points again after a few days.
  • Repeating the program a second or third time usually reveals details you missed earlier, because your practical experience changes what you take from each step (2023).
  • Learning a complex platform such as Google Ads (Google's paid ad system) takes repeated study, with pauses and notes over several days. Take the time to set it up correctly rather than running a weak setup while you learn (2020).
  • Learning Google Ads and Google Shopping (product ads shown in Google search) can take a beginner considerable time. Going back over sections again and again, and doing the setup steps alongside the material, is a sensible way to learn them (2021-22).
  • Learning Google Ads takes time, and people grasp the platform at different speeds. Persistence and regular practice lead to better understanding, even when the screens feel confusing at first (2021-22).
  • Reaching real proficiency in Google Ads takes months to years of continuous work. Each round of optimization (improving settings based on results) makes the next one faster and more effective.
  • Go back over traffic knowledge repeatedly, especially once traffic and sales start, to find improvements in each channel (each place visitors come from).

Study and action in alternating blocks

1 min
  • Do not mix studying and doing in the same block. Do not stop the material to carry out tasks, and do not treat the program like entertainment. Study with full focus, because the material is dense and passive studying (half-listening) wastes money.
  • Pace your study so you learn new material and revisit older material at regular intervals. Going too fast (rushing through many parts in a row) or too slow both reduce memory and make earlier content hard to recall.
  • Plan study and action in alternating blocks:
    1. Study one block on one day, focused and taking notes, without rushing through several parts in a row.
    2. Put as much information as possible into your notes during the study block.
    3. On the next day, take action on what you learned the day before.
    4. If you have a long session, such as four hours, split it into two hours of study, a short break, and two hours of action on the material from the first block.
  • Work through the program one step at a time. Complete the action task at the end of each step before moving on. Do not rush through all the material first and only then start building the business.
  • Learning method for a sequenced program with action tasks in each step:
    1. Study the first part.
    2. Complete the action task from that part right away. Start it immediately if it is a multi-day task.
    3. Only after the action is set up, move on to the next part.
    4. Complete that part's action task, then continue to the next part.
    5. Repeat until all parts and action tasks are done, working slower than a normal program pace.

Learn in short units

1 min
  • Long material is easier to act on when split into short units of about 5 to 10 minutes each. Each unit ends with a concrete task that can be checked off. Survey feedback from a community of about 72 members favored this format.
  • Schedule focused blocks for the first pass, with no distractions and no multitasking (doing several things at once). Take notes as you go, especially on product research and ads. Aim to finish the first full pass on days 1 and 2 of the plan.

Daily small targets

1 min
  • Schedule daily study time, even 30 minutes. Move through the program's parts in order, completing each part's action tasks before starting the next (2022).
  • Make a little progress each day by following the steps in order, pausing for each action, asking questions when stuck, and submitting the niche for a check (2022).
  • Progress that happens every single day adds up to the goal. Small, completable daily tasks are more useful than large, vague plans (2018).
  • Instead of looking at a whole part at once, plan one small daily target, such as finishing the first four steps of part one. Ignore the rest until that target is done (2018).
  • Skill comes from repeated practice, and early results are always weaker than later ones. Daily focused practice on one core skill, such as hand-copying strong sales letters for an hour, builds into a valuable ability.
  • Daily reading of business-relevant non-fiction supports personal growth and business performance. It works like structured learning without a teacher.
  • Covering more than one step a day is fine when time allows. The goal is to finish the material, not to hit a daily count. Do not add unnecessary pressure to the schedule.

21-day launch plan

1 min
  • A 21-day launch plan gives a clear daily focus. It prevents overwhelm and distraction when the full program or the store build feels too large (2018).
  • A 21-day launch format gives a community one action task per day, posted with a daily training recording. Questions are answered, and short screen recordings are shared. Covered steps included niche selection, store build, supplier approvals and starting traffic.
  • A full-time launch of a store and its ads can be done in about 21 days at eight hours a day, following a step-by-step launch plan. Most people need 30 minutes to 2 hours per day after launch (2020).
  • The 21-day launch plan involves a daily task that takes between about 5 minutes and 90 minutes. Following it each day means the first store can be launched within 21 days (2020).
  • A 21-day quick start is possible for a beginner, but it requires asking questions quickly when stuck, rather than spending days on one step (2025).
  • A checklist for the business is the action tasks at the end of each training part. Do not move on to the next part until all action tasks are complete (2020).

7-day launch checklist

2 min
  • In e-commerce, a well-run first week aims to reach a store that is ready to launch within seven days.
  • Seven-day launch checklist, in order:
    1. Go through the full training from start to finish without setting anything up, aiming to finish on days 1 and 2.
    2. Go back through each part and complete its action tasks, listed in that part's linked document (a file the program points to), before moving on to the next part.
    3. Go through the #resources and #tools chat rooms in Discord (a chat app with rooms called channels), introduce yourself, and sign up for the weekly live group call.
    4. While still going through the training, research at least 15 product ideas with Winning Hunter (a product research tool). Look for high emotional demand, strong margins (profit left after costs) and a wide audience.
    5. Fill in the product research template (a fill-in form) with pricing, estimated margins, competitor links (links to rival sellers' stores) and audience notes. Submit it through the Discord ticket channel (a chat room where you send a request).
    6. Wait for the product to be approved before building anything.
    7. Build the store with Elixir (a store-building tool) by following the build guide. Optimize (improve) images, copy (sales text), urgency elements (signs that push buyers to act now) and trust badges (logos that show safety) on the product page (the page that sells one product).
    8. Submit the finished store for review via ticket before it goes live.
    9. After store approval, launch Meta ads (paid ads on Facebook and Instagram) using the part's campaign structure (how the ads are set up), creatives (ad images and clips), and recommended testing budget (the suggested amount to test with). Track the numbers from day one.
  • Complete the action tasks listed in each part's linked document before moving on to the next part. Do not advance until the current part's tasks are finished.
  • Research for a new product: invest about one week of research, roughly 8 hours per day (minimum 1 to 2 days), into understanding the customer avatar (a clear picture of your ideal buyer) before building the store and launching ads.

Pace: 7 weeks or several months

1 min
  • Plan to finish the full program over several months, not one month.
  • A deep program has many hours of material and action. It cannot be finished quickly. Expecting a quick finish leads to losing motivation.
  • A steady plan works best: one part per day for six to ten weeks. Do the action for each part.
  • While a learner waits for the next coaching call (a live session with a coach), give them a fixed list of tasks or other material to work through, so they do not stop.
  • The 7-week quick start is the same course at a slower pace than the 21-day plan. It does not replace the core parts. (2023)
  • Timelines differ between sources:
    • The 7-day checklist aims for a store (your online shop) ready to launch in one week.
    • The 21-day plan aims for a first store launched in three weeks.
    • The full program takes several months.
  • Pick the pace that fits your free hours and how deep the material goes.

Avoid over-consumption

1 min
  • Do not only consume training, like YouTube clips, without taking action.
  • Too much study without putting it into practice (actually using it) wastes time. That time is better spent on execution (doing the real work).
  • Learning a lot of marketing and not using it in your business wastes the owner's time. It brings no growth. (2021-22)
  • Endless learning feels productive. It includes scrolling social feeds, saving posts and watching content.
  • Endless learning distracts you from the risky execution work that moves the business forward. It is the biggest trap for people who avoid risk.
  • Skipping action items because they seem unimportant is a serious error. (2021-22)
  • Skipping action items is also a mistake when you want to reach tactics (specific methods) faster. (2021-22)
  • Action items build on each other. They set up your later work. (2021-22)
  • Learning by absorbing information alone is not enough. Turn learning into action at each step (see the action rules above).
  • Do not learn the advanced details of e-commerce (selling products online) before your store is live. (2023)
  • Most of the material covers the steady core steps. You learn the rest from real questions and experience. You do not need outside marketing programs first. (2023)
  • Running a store as a side business can bring fear of not knowing enough about Google Ads (Google's paid ad system) or optimization (improving results step by step). (2023)
  • You learn that rest from real questions and experience. (2023)
  • Most people who buy online business training do not finish it, so no success rate is measured. (2024)
  • Steady work and asking questions are the strongest predictors of results. (2024)

Avoid shiny objects and extra courses

1 min
  • While you run your business plan, remove outside distractions. (2021-22)
  • Do not buy extra courses, learning products or shiny objects (things that look exciting but pull you away from doing). (2021-22)
  • Jumping from one business idea to the next stops progress. This is shiny object syndrome (chasing whatever looks exciting).
  • Commit to one business model and one strategy. Run it through before you judge it.
  • Extra paid programs and upgrades are optional.
  • They help a store that already makes a profit (money left after costs).
  • They are not needed to build a successful dropshipping store. Dropshipping means you sell products, and a supplier (the company that stocks or makes them) ships them to the customer.
  • Tricks can work for a while. Fundamentals (basic rules) hold up over the long term.
  • Focus on fundamentals like branding (how your shop looks and feels), creatives (ad pictures and short ads) and basic ad rules. Do not focus on single tricks that may stop working.
  • Ad platforms (the websites and apps where you run ads) change their features and rules almost every week.
  • Nobody can claim to have fully mastered them.
  • Keep up with changes, but base decisions on the basics of marketing and sales, which stay the same.
  • Use a proven set of techniques (methods) instead of trying every method in the books.
  • Experienced writers keep a handful of techniques that work every time. Start with those few. Test others only when you have time.

Test products before judging

1 min
  • Test several products. Review the results with mentors (experienced helpers).
  • Do not judge the business after a single attempt.
  • Students who make progress put full effort into the product pitch (how you present the product to buyers) and keep testing.
  • Research at least 15 product ideas with Winning Hunter (a tool that finds product ideas).
  • Look for high emotional demand (customers want it strongly), strong margins (good profit per sale) and a wide audience.
  • Learn a new product category with a few hours of basic overviews. (2020)
  • Then learn most of it by running ads and answering real customers. (2020)
  • Pass each unanswered question to the supplier. Then add the answer to your site. (2020)
  • Learn from competitors (other sellers) and their mistakes through their reviews (customer ratings) and experiences. (2021)
  • This is safer than learning only from your own mistakes. (2021)
  • Study documented results from people who already succeeded at the task.
  • Do not repeat their costly trial and error (trying, failing and retrying). Reusing proven methods saves years of testing and money.
  • Writing a strong sales letter (text that sells) is a skill you can learn, not a talent.
  • The steps (mechanics) repeat. A business owner who knows the customer has a major advantage over a freelancer (an independent worker) who must research the customer from scratch.
  • Begin by writing blocks of copy (short pieces of sales text) and stacking them. Do not aim for a finished letter at once.
  • Read the book 100 Million Offers by Alex Hermosi to learn how to build strong offers (deals that are hard to refuse).
  • Ready, Fire, Aim by Michael Masterson is a book on direct response (selling that asks the buyer to act now).
  • Read it if you plan to build a private label line (your own brand of products made by a manufacturer).

Continuous learning

1 min
  • Learning never stops. It is part of the job.
  • Follow digital marketing groups. Ask questions and answer questions in forums (online discussion boards).
  • Marketers who keep learning stay ahead of those who stop.
  • The willingness to keep learning is the most valuable asset an entrepreneur (business owner) can have.
  • Nobody can take it away, and it can carry you a long way.
  • Success in this business requires constant adapting and picking up new skills.
  • The most valuable skill in dropshipping is a willingness to learn.
  • It cannot be taken away, and it carries you through changing conditions.
  • Expect platforms and rules to change. Keep updating your knowledge.
  • Investing in ongoing education is recommended. Examples are podcasts (audio shows), reading, a mastermind group (a small group that helps each other grow), a monthly local business breakfast and yearly conferences.
  • Even one useful idea per session can make a large difference to the business.
  • When you learn a platform like Shopify (a tool for building online shops) from scratch, repeated basic questions in community groups are normal.
  • Rewatching setup tutorials (step-by-step guides) is also normal.
  • Keep returning to the business goal. Do not stop when the technical steps feel overwhelming.
  • Learning web development (building websites with code) works best with a mix of YouTube tutorials, free online programming courses and books.
  • Choose the mix by whether you prefer watching or reading.
  • Consistent practice through trial and error builds the skill.
  • Learn people deeply. Study human psychology (how people think and act).
  • This knowledge directly improves your business decisions, marketing and customer relationships.
  • Copywriting (writing text that sells) is closely tied to general marketing.
  • Reading classic copywriting and marketing books improves your overall skill, not only your writing, when you apply the ideas in practice.

Reading routine

1 min
  • Turn daily reading into lasting knowledge in this order:
    1. Read non-fiction (true books, not stories) for at least 30 minutes.
    2. Highlight the key passages (mark the important parts).
    3. Take written notes on the main ideas.
    4. Discuss what you read with other people, so the ideas stay in memory.
  • Daily reading of business-relevant non-fiction supports personal growth and business performance.
  • It works like structured learning without a teacher.

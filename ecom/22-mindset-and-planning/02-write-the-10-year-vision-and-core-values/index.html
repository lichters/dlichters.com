Write the 10-year vision and core values

Section 22.2 · 2 of 26 in this chapter · 7 min read

In short

  • Write down your 10-year picture of your life and business before you make any plan.
  • The picture does not need the how yet. Work that out later.
  • Aim high. A good goal should feel a little scary or exciting.
  • Choose 3 to 5 core values to guide your decisions.
  • Read your vision every morning, and update it at least once a year.
This section has 10 parts. Tap one to open it.

Why the vision comes first

1 min
  • A business without a written destination (the end point you want) and a plan depends on luck to reach its goals.
  • A clear end point gives the owner a roadmap, a step-by-step plan to follow.
  • Core business basics come first: direction, vision and core values. Direction means where the business is heading.
  • The core basics guide almost every decision. They come before marketing tactics, the specific ways you advertise and sell.
  • Most highly successful owners work from these basics in some form, so do not skip them.
  • Skipping personal vision and development work because it seems unrelated to the business is a mistake.
  • That gap usually shows up later as weak results once the business is running.
  • A vision defines the destination, while your current situation is the starting point.
  • The vision should cover both your personal life and the business. It should also explain why you are starting the business at all.
  • A clear picture of the end-point goal makes building the roadmap much easier.
  • That picture should show how both your life and your business look at that point, because the two become closely linked.

What a vision is

1 min
  • A vision is a detailed picture of the business and your life once goals are reached. It is more than a statement of intent (a simple "I want this").
  • You can write a personal vision the same way, next to the business vision.
  • You must be able to picture your success and believe you can reach your goals. Goals you do not believe in are rarely reached.
  • This applies at any revenue level, meaning the money the business brings in from sales, whether it earns 1,000 or 1,000,000 dollars per month.
  • The vision does not require knowing how the goals will be reached.
  • Describe the picture first, and work out the how later in the planning process.
  • Set ambitious goals that feel slightly daunting.
  • Do not let uncertainty about how to reach them stop the vision work.
  • The real question is where you truly want to be to feel happy, comfortable, and secure, not how to get there.

Size, ambition and time horizon

1 min
  • Use a 10-year time horizon (how far ahead you plan) for the vision.
  • Do not use a short one, such as six months. Long-term vision comes first, and big results are built over longer periods. Shorter targets follow from the vision.
  • There is no right or wrong size for the vision. It can be a very large company or a small business.
  • The vision should still feel challenging to achieve. Small long-term goals rarely keep an owner pushing day after day.
  • A good vision excites you and may feel slightly scary.
  • If the goals cause no fear or excitement, they are probably too small.
  • Write ambitious, deliberately bold goals, not goals limited by what seems realistic today.
  • Set a time frame for long-term goals. For example, reach a strong financial position by about age 40 with five years of focused work.
  • Include family and free time in the timeline.

Writing the vision

1 min
  • Write the vision without outside influence and without copying example visions.
  • Put down what you actually want, without second-guessing it.
  • There are no right or wrong answers in a personal vision.
  • The goal can be very ambitious or modest, as long as it reflects what you want. The document is private and only for you.
  • The Vision on a Page worksheet (a one-page form) contains a completed sample vision. It shows how much detail each area can have.
  • Write your own answers. Do not copy the sample.
  • The vision must be written down, not only held in your mind. The written document is used later as the reference for planning.
  • Even a quick first draft counts as a starting point. Do not wait for a perfect version before moving on.
  • Keep the vision document simple and not overthought.
  • Use a basic document, such as Pages (Apple's word processor) or Word (Microsoft's).
  • Write the answers from memory, without external examples.

The personal vision alongside the business

1 min
  • A personal goal can be to live solely off passive income, meaning money that comes in without daily work.
  • You no longer have to worry about business cash flow, the money moving in and out of the business.
  • The business is the means to fund this life.
  • Describe your personal life in 10 years: family, location, living situation, travel and what you do daily.
  • This can be a few short paragraphs or several pages, depending on how much detail you want.
  • Your personal life often motivates you more than the business figures do.
  • Treat the business as the vehicle that funds that life.
  • Connect the two explicitly in the vision.
  • The personal life section can be short or long. A few short paragraphs (about five) is fine.
  • A detailed text of up to around three pages is also acceptable.
  • In the early stage, the solo owner is the business. You do the website setup, supplier contact and daily processes yourself.
  • Because of this, life and business become tightly connected, so the vision must address both.
  • A team can be added later.

Business vision document: ten steps

1 min

Keep the document simple. Use a basic Pages or Word document.

  1. Enter the business name, followed by the title "10 year vision".
  2. Write a quick overview of one or two paragraphs. Describe what the business looks like in 10 years and how success is measured. Measures include revenue, customers, lifestyle or side-hustle status (a small project that runs next to a job).
  3. Describe the brand, meaning how your business is known and seen. Cover how well known it is, who knows it, what people think of it, how many reviews it has and whether people recommend it without being asked.
  4. Describe the marketing: which channels (places you reach customers, such as ads or email) are used, where marketing is done and how it performs.
  5. Describe the finances: revenue (money from sales), profit, profit margin (the share of sales left as profit), who manages them, and what opportunities the profit creates.
  6. Describe the sales: who sells, how customers and prospects (people who might buy) are reached, and how many people are involved in sales.
  7. Describe the customers: who they are, what they think of the business and what they do for it.
  8. Describe the operations (the daily running of the business) and the team: what the internal team looks like, which roles exist, where staff are based, and who manages the business day to day.
  9. Describe the personal life and lifestyle: daily life, family, location, travel and personal financial situation.
  10. List the 3 to 5 core values.

Vision on a Page: personal areas

1 min
  • Picture yourself 10 years ahead. Describe what your business and your life look like at that point.
  • Cover every area: business, finances, place of residence, family, number of children if you want them, investments, what you do with business income, health, and significant relationships.
  • Take one to two days if needed. There is no need to rush.
  • Write one to two paragraphs for each area on the one-page vision worksheet.
  • Length does not matter. Only make sure every area is filled in.
Area Guiding question
Business What does the business look like?
Finances What does your financial situation look like?
Residence Where do you live?
Family Where is your family, and how many children do you have if you want children?
Investments What are you investing in?
Use of business income What are you doing with the money your business makes?
Health What does your health look like?
Relationships What do your other significant relationships look like?
  • Write one to two paragraphs per area.

Core values

1 min
  • Choose 3 to 5 core values. These are the few rules that guide decisions.
  • More than five is not recommended.
  • The values should describe what the business stands for and what it should be known for.
  • Build the business on these five core values:
    • Take ownership: act on challenges directly instead of making excuses.
    • Work relentlessly to be the best at what you do.
    • Stay humble and keep improving by tracking marketing changes and sharing what works.
    • Treat teammates and customers as equals.
    • Handle every dealing with the highest level of honesty, integrity (doing what is right even when no one checks) and transparency (being open about facts).
  • These values apply at every revenue level, from a first sale to multi-million-dollar annual sales.
  • Define your values before choosing the audience you want to market to. A niche (a narrow group of buyers) and a process that drain you are a sign that your values were never defined.

Daily review and visualisation

1 min
  • Review the vision regularly, ideally first thing each morning.
  • Visualize it with your eyes closed, meaning picture it clearly in your mind.
  • This helps on difficult days when doubt, criticism or setbacks occur.
  • Revisit the written goals every morning so they shape the day's decisions.
  • Define a clear, personally meaningful vision of success before acting.

Review and revision

1 min
  • Treat the vision as a starting point, not a fixed plan.
  • The vision is expected to change as progress is made.
  • Review it at least once a year.

Playbook › 22. Mindset and planning

Define your reason, your meaning of success and your relationship to money

Section 22.1 · 1 of 26 in this chapter · 8 min read

In short

  • Write down your personal reason for the business, and read it when you want to quit.
  • Keep that reason private. It only needs to motivate you.
  • Measure success by your own goals and growth, not by a random sales number.
  • Charge for skill and results, not only for hours.
  • Your hobby does not have to become your business.
This section has 8 parts. Tap one to open it.

Your core reason for building the business

1 min

Knowing your personal reason keeps you going through the slow early phase. So write it down and read it again often.

The reason is private. It does not need to satisfy anyone else.

You need a personal reason that is strong enough to last months. Without it, people tend to quit when they face rejection (customers or sellers saying no), customer service problems, or technical hurdles (problems with tools or your website).

Procedure: finding your core reason

  1. Write down the first reason that comes to mind for starting the business. Examples: quitting a job, extra income, more family time, travel, paying off debt, or a better life for your family.
  2. For each reason, ask yourself why you want that result.
  3. Ask why again for each answer. Keep going deeper until you find the real reason underneath. Examples: health, sleep, or time for a relationship.
  4. Write the core reason down. Keep it where you will see it when you need motivation.

Procedure: defining your reason for investing time and money in dropshipping (selling products that a supplier ships straight to the customer, so you do not keep stock yourself)

  1. Write on paper why you joined the program and why you are doing this. Ideally, do it in the same week you start.
  2. Describe your dream result. Write what you want to do, how your daily life looks, where you live, and what your number one goal is.
  3. Use your answers to find the path. The path is the skills, products and business model (how the business makes money) that lead to that result. Read your notes again when motivation drops.

Keep your dreamline (your written dream picture) private. Sharing it with family or friends can make you lower your goals to match their opinions. The list only needs to motivate you.

Define what success means to you

1 min

Describe your own vision of success and what it means to you personally. Make the picture clear and strong. Do not add limits that are not real. Knowing what you want comes first.

Define success by work that brings you fulfillment (a feeling of real satisfaction), not by random revenue targets (goals for total money coming in). Steady, meaningful action that fits what satisfies you makes you successful by your own definition. Business growth then follows on its own. Older advice, Source B 2021-2022.

Measure success by growth, not by total revenue. Growing from 0 to 1 percent per month (month-over-month, compared with the month before) is real, measurable progress and success.

Making more money is too vague to keep you going. A clear picture of the life you want gives your daily work a purpose. Examples: time freedom (control over your hours), location freedom (working from where you choose), or financial independence (not needing a job to pay your bills).

Set goals you really want. Do not just set income targets (numbers with no real meaning to you). Money alone does not bring happiness. Tie each goal to what the business makes possible in your life.

Motivation and staying power

2 min

Motivators are the things that push you to act. Use two motivators together:

  • Toward: a good result you want, such as financial freedom or providing for your family.
  • Away: a painful situation you want to escape.

Keep both in mind every day.

Your goal must be bigger than the obstacles you will face. Otherwise, the first hurdle stops your progress. Each goal should be strong enough that you are willing to climb every obstacle on the way.

Early on, expect delayed gratification (rewards that come later, not now). You may work for two to three weeks or longer before results appear, and those hours can feel unpaid. Accepting that your work pays off later keeps you working when motivation drops.

Your core reason is a tool that pulls you back to work when distractions or low energy appear. Focus is what produces results. Successful people are not lucky by default. They use their reason to put off short-term comfort.

A clear personal reason keeps your attention on what matters. It filters out distractions. Long, steady focus turns effort into results.

Feeling overwhelmed while building is normal. Know your exact end goal, not just an income target. Examples: paying off debt in 2 years, or funding a 3-month Europe trip next summer. This gives you motivation when daily tasks feel boring.

Passion means being able to put up with present pain because the result is worth it. It is not a constant feeling of happiness. Purpose is what the world buys from you. Passion is what fuels you.

Losing motivation after a fast start is normal for almost every business owner. Return to your original reason you started, such as extra income, leaving a job, or more time for travel. Let that reason motivate you again. Older advice, Source A 2020.

Spreadsheets and CSV files (simple table files that spreadsheet programs open) are only the necessary tools. They are not the purpose of the business. Keep your personal goal in view when the daily work feels heavy. Older advice, Source A 2020.

Procedure: staying motivated while working toward business goals **

  1. Write down the real personal goal the profit is for, such as a family trip, a move or school fees.
  2. Put the written goal somewhere you can see it while you work.
  3. Measure progress against that goal, not only against a daily task count.

Money as a tool and scorecard

1 min

Money is only a tool. Treat it as a means to reach your goals, not as the goal itself.

Treat money as a scorecard. It shows whether the business is working and whether you are making the right decisions. It is not the end goal. Pair the score with what it lets you do, such as buying a home, taking a trip, or buying a car. This makes progress feel meaningful.

Running a business only to make money, with a plan to do something else later, keeps your confidence low. A better approach is to move the business toward your own strengths. Then it becomes the vehicle (the thing that carries you) to what you really want.

When profits rise quickly, a new seller can feel fear of losing the money and hold negative beliefs about earning it. Challenge these thoughts. Otherwise, they can hold back your decisions as income grows.

Chasing status symbols (things that show off wealth, such as luxury cars) as a measure of success tends to bring frustration and unhappiness. Creative work toward goals you truly care about tends to bring lasting satisfaction and material results.

Time, money and income

1 min

Time and money are not directly linked. Income depends on the value, leverage, and scalability of what you sell. Leverage means one sale or product can earn without costing you more time. Scalability means how easily it can grow. Avoid pricing only by the hour.

Shift from payment for hours worked to payment for outcomes whenever possible. Income tied to results can keep growing. Hourly work caps (limits) your earnings.

Case: a 12-year-old tennis racket stringing service

  • Starting point: a used stringing machine (a machine that weaves strings into a racket) bought for about $400 to $500.
  • Local clubs charged $12 to $15 per racket. The stringer started at $25 with no experience.
  • A racket first took 45 to 60 minutes.
  • After practice, it took 16 to 20 minutes.
  • That is about 2 rackets per hour, including setup.
  • This came to about $50 per hour at that age, more than most adults earned hourly.
  • Lesson: a focused skill, charged at a premium (a higher price than normal), can earn far more than the trade-time model (selling your hours).

Passion for the business, not the product

1 min

Passion for your specific niche (a narrow group of products or customers) is not required.

Focus on passion for building your business itself. It decides long-term success more than enthusiasm for the product does.

Channel your motivation toward building a successful business, watching it grow, and carrying it out well. Do not aim it only at your personal interest in the products you sell.

Passion for the product is not necessary for a dropshipping business. Put your passion into building the business, making sales and growing the store.

Passion for the niche is secondary. Passion for business growth and problem-solving is primary. Knowledge comes through experience, not prior expertise (knowledge you already had before). Older advice, Source A 2020.

Passion cannot be faked. Commitment is required for success. Direct that commitment at the business itself, not at the products sold.

The parts of a store worth being passionate about are:

  • Build a good-looking website.
  • Generate traffic (get visitors to your store).
  • Treat customers well.
  • Earn repeat purchases (customers who buy again).
  • Grow the store month after month.

These areas decide long-term success, whatever the product.

Hobby versus income

1 min

A personal passion or hobby does not need to be your income source. If other products are much more profitable (they make more money after costs) and easier to sell, build your business around those. You can then pursue your passion as a leisure activity, separate from work.

Your hobbies do not have to become your business. You can keep an interest purely for fun. Fund it from the profits of a business built on a better opportunity.

Case: a member with a scuba diving hobby

  • He first considered selling diving products because the hobby was strong.
  • He realized he did not need to earn money from the hobby.
  • So he chose a product with better sales potential (how much it could sell) and easier selling.
  • He reinvested the profits into building the business. He used part of the income to fund his diving trips.
  • Result: the hobby stayed a personal pleasure, while the business was built on a stronger opportunity.

Keep several interests running at once, including one creative hobby with no income goal. Dropping an interest often makes the main work feel flat. A hobby done only for enjoyment restores energy for the business.

Choosing what you will do with the income

1 min

Money is a tool for getting what you actually want. It is not a goal in itself. If you cannot name what you would do with the income, resolve that gap before you invest in scaling (growing big).

Name your outcomes. Examples:

  • Giving back
  • Independence
  • Caring for family
  • Experiences
  • Becoming debt-free
  • Leaving a job you dislike

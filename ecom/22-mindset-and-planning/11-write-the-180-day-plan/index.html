Write the 180-day plan

Section 22.11 · 11 of 26 in this chapter · 5 min read

In short

  • Write a 180-day plan (about six months) before you start, and do not change it until its end date.
  • Set no more than five priorities. These are big yearly changes, not small daily chores.
  • Use the plan as your daily guide. The top priority and its tasks become your to-do list.
  • Book a review date about six months ahead, then check what you achieved.

Why a written plan matters

  • Business goals are rarely reached by luck or by disorganized action.
  • A written plan gives your daily to-do lists their direction.
  • A written plan reduces wasted time.
  • A written plan reduces procrastination (putting tasks off) and lack of motivation.
  • Write down a business plan before you start.
  • Treat the plan as a guide, not a fixed rule.
  • Scattered online marketing tips, without a solid base, give poor results. Tips do not show what matters first or how the parts connect.
  • A structured plan works better. It sets your numbers first, then puts the campaign types (paid ad or marketing efforts) in order.

Planning horizon and timing

  • Plan 180 days (about six months), not a full year. A long plan in an uncertain market makes forecasts (predictions) unreliable.
  • Review the plan at the halfway point. Change course if conditions have changed a lot.
  • Plan in January, after the yearly review. A high-ticket dropshipping business (one that sells expensive products that the supplier ships directly to the customer, so you keep no stock) is often quieter then.
  • Being quieter in January makes it easier to look ahead and check if the business is on track.
  • Time is your most valuable resource. Spend it on what makes the most money.
  • Plan the next three months carefully. They decide the following year.
  • In a compressed launch plan (a fast plan with short time frames) for a new branded dropshipping store, product research and validation (checking that a product sells) can start in week 2.
  • Research can overlap with store setup, because it can take only a few days.
  • Ads and testing frameworks (set ways to test what works) then start around weeks 3 to 4.
  • The total timeline depends on how many hours per week you can give the project.

Setting priorities

  • Limit priorities to five at most. More than five are unlikely to be pursued seriously.
  • Priorities should be major business changes for the year, not small recurring chores.
  • Typical six-month priorities include growing organic traffic (free visitors from search engines like Google).
  • Other typical six-month priorities include setting up an email marketing system (a set way to send emails to customers).
  • Other typical six-month priorities include getting 10 new suppliers approved and products uploaded.
  • Big changes such as email marketing, SEO (search engine optimization, making pages easier to rank on Google) or a wider product range take a long time.
  • Commit to them consistently, even when distractions or new opportunities appear.
  • Make outsourcing a priority in a 180-day plan. Outsourcing means paying others to do tasks.
  • Check whether your plan already includes the tasks you need to hand off.
  • If you have fewer than five priorities, fill only the matching Priority task columns. Leave the rest empty.

Task breakdown

  • Example SEO task breakdown for six months: write 12 blog posts (articles on your website).
  • The same example: promote those posts.
  • The same example: run a link-building method (getting other websites to link to yours) on six of them.
  • The same example: rewrite collection descriptions (the text on your shop's category pages).
  • The same example: run an SEO audit (a check of your site for problems that hurt search ranking).
  • The same example: fix duplicate content (the same text on several pages, which confuses search engines).
  • Use the plan as your daily reference.
  • The top priority and its tasks become the day's to-do list.
  • This way you do not decide each morning what matters most.

Writing the plan

  • Planning the 180 days can take ten minutes or a day or two.
  • Finish the plan before starting the next task.
  • Do not overthink it. Get the goals and tasks onto the page first.

Planner sheet and tools

  • The planning tool is a pair of Google Sheets files. Google Sheets is a free online spreadsheet.
  • One file is a 180-day plan sheet. The other is a goals and rocks tracker with quarter tabs (one tab per three-month period).
  • The planning method is called Course B (BSGR).
  • The tool mirrors a 10-year, 3-year, 1-year and 90-day rocks planner. Rocks are big goals for a set period.
  • The planner sheet has these sections and fields. Measurables are the numbers you track. Paint The Picture is a short written description of the goal. Revenue is the total money in from sales. Profit is what is left after costs.
Sheet Fields
BSGR 180 Day Plan Date (plan end and next review date); Revenue; Profit; 180 Day Goals/Priorities (no more than 5); Measurables; Paint The Picture; Priority 1 Tasks to Priority 5 Tasks
90 DAY ROCKS - 1st Quarter 90 Day Goals/Priorities; 90 Day Rocks (up to 7 rocks)
90 DAY ROCKS - 2nd Quarter 90 Day Goals/Priorities; 90 Day Rocks (up to 7 rocks)
  • For the second half of the year, duplicate the original planner.
  • Change its quarter tabs to third and fourth quarter. Do not edit the first-half version.

Checking earlier plans

  • Before writing a new 180-day plan, check earlier long-range plans (10-year, 3-year, one-year and 90-day rocks).
  • Copy across the items that still hold.
  • Revise the items that no longer match the business.

Holding the plan

  • Do not change the plan during its term.
  • The plan stays fixed until the six-month end date. Only then is a new plan written.
  • Once a plan is set, follow it to its end without changing it mid-way.
  • The only exception is a major personal event.
  • Change course only at the scheduled review.

Review date in the calendar

  • Book a full day in your calendar roughly six months ahead.
  • On that day, review the past period.
  • Decide what must change, based on national or international conditions.

The planning cycle

  1. Plan your business priorities and goals for the next 180 days (six months).
  2. Schedule a review date on your calendar, about six months ahead, around mid-year (June or July).
  3. On that date, review what you actually accomplished against your plan.
  4. Create your next 180-day plan with the same planning tool, based on the results and learnings from the previous period.

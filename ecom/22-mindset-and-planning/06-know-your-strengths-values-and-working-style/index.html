Playbook › 22. Mindset and planning

Know your strengths, values and working style

Section 22.6 · 6 of 26 in this chapter · 7 min read

In short

  • Be honest with yourself. Write real answers. There are no right or wrong answers.
  • Look at your past attempts. Find the patterns that made you stop, then fix them in the new plan.
  • Know your natural strengths first. Build your work around them.
  • Plan last, not first. Get clear on yourself before you make the plan. Then stay with one direction.
This section has 13 parts. Tap one to open it.

Start with honest self-reflection

1 min
  • Self-reflection means looking honestly at yourself. These exercises are about your work habits and thinking style.
  • They have no right or wrong answers.
  • Answer openly and honestly. That is what makes them useful.
  • Skipping them because they feel uncomfortable makes them worth less.
  • They are often the most useful part of preparing your business personally.
  • Formal education, a strong school record and exceptional intelligence are not prerequisites (things you need first).
  • Those things are not needed to build a profitable e-commerce business (selling products online).
  • Long-term persistence through slow or losing periods matters more than school credentials.
  • Persistence (not giving up) is the trait that carries successful sellers.
  • Successful sellers check the numbers every day.
  • They also generate ideas and run the team with genuine belief (real belief).
  • Persistence does not mean trying to solve everything alone.

Review your past attempts honestly

1 min
  • Review your past attempts at online business. Be honest.
  • Include what happened and why it ended.
  • Look for patterns that repeat, like quitting early or lacking capital (money to start and run the business).
  • Then fix those patterns in the new plan. Do not repeat them.

Answer each question of this form in writing before you start an online business:

Personal self-assessment form to fill in honestly before starting an online business, answer each question in writing:
1. Name, age, and home country or city?
2. What do you currently do for work or study?
3. Why did you decide to start this business path now?
4. What is your strongest reason for wanting to succeed in ecommerce?
5. What is your main goal for the next 90 days?
6. What monthly or yearly income figure are you working toward?
7. Have you tried ecommerce, dropshipping, or any other online business before? If yes, what happened and why did it stop or fail?
8. What do you see as your biggest obstacle right now? Consider time, confidence, product research, ads, capital, and consistency.
9. What do you expect from a coach, course, or mentor, and how should they support you?
10. Imagine it is 12 months from today and everything worked out. Describe in concrete detail what your work, income, time, and daily life look like.
Commitment (sign or confirm): Guidance, support, and accountability can be provided by others, but results depend on taking consistent action yourself.
  • Vague or fast answers weaken the whole plan.
  • The answers are the base for setting realistic goals.
  • Add specifics: numbers, dates and real constraints (things that limit you).

Map your personality and natural strengths

1 min
  • Everyone has a unique way of thinking and acting.
  • It works automatically. You cannot switch it off.
  • Fighting it drains energy.
  • Working inside it makes tasks feel natural.
  • Myers-Briggs and DISC are personality assessments (tests that describe your personality).
  • They show how you think and deliver value.
  • DISC also helps you read other people in one-on-one talks, so you choose the right words.
  • Free resources for both are available online.
  • The StrengthsFinder assessment, from Gallup (a research company), finds your five natural strengths or talents.
  • It is described in Tom Rath's book.
  • Gallup's study says people run about 80% of their lives from these five strengths.
  • So build your work around them.
  • Books alone rarely reveal your own strengths and working style.
  • Ask people who know you well for feedback.
  • Combine that feedback with structured assessments, like StrengthsFinder.
  • Many people stop using their natural way of working around ages 8 to 14.
  • At that age, they hear that what they do by default is not enough.
  • They are told they must add unrelated skills to succeed.
  • Rebuilding confidence starts by returning to that natural way of thinking and acting.
  • It does not start with more courses.

Define values, passion and purpose

1 min
  • Values are what matter most to you.
  • Values decide which audience you want to serve.
  • They also decide why you want to serve them.
  • This is called market selection, which means choosing who you sell to.
  • When your work fits your values, it becomes more enjoyable.
  • Marketing also becomes easier.
  • Problems that you notice in others are a strong starting point for a business.
  • You often notice them before they do.
  • Those problems usually link to your natural way of seeing things.

Build in the right order

1 min

Work through these steps in order:

  1. Understand your personality and how you take in and handle information.
  2. Identify your proficiencies (your strong areas): the five natural ways you think and act.
  3. Define your values, which decide who you want to work with and why.
  4. Find your passion: the drive to keep going through pain to reach the end result.
  5. Define your purpose: what you offer the world.
  6. Make a plan based on the steps above.
  7. Execute (carry it out).
  • Some plans look complete on paper but collapse when you carry them out (execution).
  • About 50% of these plans fail right away.
  • This usually means planning was done too early.
  • Planning should come late, after personality, strengths, values and passion are clear.
  • Constantly second-guessing your marketing is a warning sign.
  • Here, marketing means your target group (the customers you aim at), your wording and your message.
  • It usually means you do not yet know your strengths and values well enough.
  • To speak clearly to customers, fix the self-understanding first, then the messaging.

Check that your work fits your strengths

1 min
  • Heavy work with small results is a warning sign.
  • It usually means you work outside your proficiencies and natural working style.
  • Re-check which tasks match your strengths before adding more effort.

Choose partners and direction carefully

1 min
  • Repeated partnerships that do not match often come from unclear strengths and values.
  • The cause is often not bad luck.
  • Define both before you enter a partnership.
  • Chasing every new opportunity is called shiny button syndrome (jumping to every new, exciting idea).
  • It keeps you from finishing anything.
  • It drains money and focus.
  • Stay with one direction until your strengths and values define it.

Name your obstacles

1 min
  • Name your obstacles specifically.
  • Use these categories: time, confidence, product research (finding what to sell), ads (paid online advertising), capital (money), and consistency (doing things regularly).
  • Rank the biggest one.
  • Each named obstacle can then match a concrete countermeasure (a specific fix).

Use the experience you already have

1 min
  • Management experience from a corporate job (a company job) transfers well to running a business.
  • This is true even with no prior business ownership.
  • Skills in planning, team leadership and handling pressure apply directly to e-commerce.
  • Prior sales experience makes supplier contact (talking to the companies that supply your products) much easier.
  • Without it, expect the first calls to feel daunting (scary).
  • This is normal.

Case (a real example): investing profits in an offline business (around 2009 to 2010)

1 min
  • Around 2009 to 2010, the owner put profits from an e-commerce business into an offline business (not on the internet).
  • It was a digital signage company (screens that show content in public places).
  • It installed screens in about 30 coffee shop locations in New York.
  • Costs were very high: computers, servers (powerful computers that run systems), screens, installation staff and sales staff.
  • The business made no money.
  • The owner sold it quickly and got back only part of the cash.
  • The lesson: offline businesses, managing many companies, and large teams were not a fit.

Case (a real example): a low-ticket store with China-shipped products (around 2011 to 2012)

1 min
  • A low-ticket store sells cheap products.
  • Around 2011 to 2012, the owner built one that sold products shipped from China.
  • The average order value (the average amount one customer spends per order) was about $50.
  • The owner drove traffic (visitors) to the store.
  • Customers quickly complained about long shipping times.
  • No support systems (help teams) existed for that many customers.
  • Many customers were upset.
  • The products were delivered. The website was shut down.
  • The lesson: this model was not for them.
  • Orders making about $8 profit do not justify a large support team.

Feelings of imposter syndrome

1 min
  • Imposter syndrome means feeling like a fraud, even when you are not.
  • Feelings of imposter syndrome are normal for entrepreneurs entering a new market.
  • Push through them by taking small actions.
  • Do not wait until you feel fully qualified.
  • A fraud feeling at the start usually comes from an unexplored (not yet understood) personality.
  • Clarifying your personality and the value you bring removes most of that feeling.
  • Feeling like an impostor is normal, even for experienced creators.
  • Nobody fully understands where their best ideas come from.
  • Show up and do the work every day instead of waiting until you feel ready.

Measure your confidence

1 min

Repeat this exercise to track how your confidence changes:

  1. On a sheet of paper, rate from 1 to 10 how confident you are in your ability to do your job right now.
  2. Below the rating, list every task you have to do (for example marketing, setting up the store).
  3. Keep the sheet. Repeat the 1 to 10 rating after the gratitude exercise (an exercise about being thankful) to measure the change.

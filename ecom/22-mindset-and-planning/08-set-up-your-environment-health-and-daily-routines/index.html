Playbook › 22. Mindset and planning

Set up your environment, health and daily routines

Section 22.8 · 8 of 26 in this chapter · 8 min read

In short

  • Remove temptations and distractions from your space. Do not rely on willpower alone.
  • Use social media for business only, and follow few accounts so business posts stay visible.
  • Sleep at least 7 hours a night, ideally 8. Do not give up sleep to work.
  • Exercise every day, cook your own food, and protect your health. Your health is the base of a business that lasts.
  • Schedule breaks and keep a fixed daily routine.
This section has 11 parts. Tap one to open it.

Environment before willpower

1 min
  • Willpower runs out each day, so do not rely on self-control (your ability to resist what you want). Remove temptations and distractions from your surroundings, such as unhealthy food, games or negative people.
  • Remove negative influences and obstacles from your environment. Organise your space to save energy. Treat yourself well and reward progress. This is what makes behavioural change last (a lasting change in how you act).

Content and feed curation

1 min
  • Choose content on purpose, and stop watching content that does not serve your goals. Your feed (the list of posts an app shows you) is yours to shape. Curate it, which means choosing what stays in it. Teach the algorithm (the app's system that picks what you see) what you want to see. This takes practice, but the effect builds up over time.
  • Control the content you consume. It shapes how you see things, and through that, your reality. Choose educational and motivational content (content that teaches you or lifts your drive) that supports your goals. Train the social media algorithm by watching the content you want to see.
  • Use social media for business only. Avoid personal content on business accounts. This is an experiment, not a rule.
  • Why limit follows: a feed with thousands of followed accounts makes relevant business posts less likely to appear. The platform limits how much content it shows and fills the space with ads (paid promotions) that you cannot remove. Limiting who you follow keeps business content visible.

Set up social media for business use only:

  1. Turn off all social media notifications (alerts on your phone), except those from Dream 100 groups (your list of the 100 most useful target people or groups for your business).
  2. Follow only Dream 100 members on the platforms. Do not follow friends, family or others, unless a partner or close person would otherwise be upset (a judgement call).
  3. Join only groups that are Dream 100 groups or groups you manage for your business.
  4. Interact only with your business pages.
  5. Keep the feed limited to business-relevant content, so that the business connections you need are not crowded out by other posts.

Focus and work environment

1 min
  • Practise mindfulness (paying calm attention to the present moment): focus on one task at a time and notice what is happening now. Mindfulness reduces anxiety and sharpens focus. It can be used during set periods of the day, without daily meditation.
  • Reduce distractions during focused work with headphones playing calm music or white noise (a steady background hiss that masks other sounds). You can also work in a coffee-shop-type environment. Noise-cancelling headphones (headphones that block outside sound) are a worthwhile investment for people with children or noisy homes.
  • Work from a co-working space (a shared office you rent with other people) or a café with other people working. This makes it harder to drift into home distractions.

Sleep

1 min
Item Value Units
Minimum sleep per night 7 hours
Ideal sleep per night 8 hours
  • Aim for consistent sleep rather than extreme sleep schedules, and avoid oversleeping.
  • Total sleep matters more than the clock. A late-night work session followed by a later wake-up is fine if your total sleep is enough and you are productive in the period when you work best.
  • Do not sacrifice sleep to build a store. Consistent daily action beats working through the night.
  • When the sources seem to conflict (two pieces of advice look different; here is how they fit together): the late-night allowance in applies only when total sleep still reaches the target in (7 hours minimum, 8 ideal). The warning in and applies whenever working late would cut into that sleep.

Exercise and physical health

1 min
  • Exercise every day. Base the decision to train on your prior commitment, not on how you feel that morning. Discipline matters more than daily mood.
  • The type of training is flexible: gym, sports, cardio (exercise that raises your heart rate, such as running or cycling), pilates or similar. The key requirement is that it becomes part of your daily routine.
  • Regular exercise raises self-esteem and self-confidence (how much you value and trust yourself). It improves your ability to finish hard tasks and builds physical and especially mental endurance (staying power).
  • Gym sessions of 30 minutes to 1 hour fit into the daily structure after waking.
  • Include a gym session in your morning routine.
  • Physical fitness supports the energy, focus and long working hours a business needs. Getting tired after only one or two hours of sitting work is a warning sign about your health. Do not treat it as normal.
  • Physical activity helps thinking. Combine work with movement, such as standing, walking or handling materials. Long stretches of sitting still at a screen reduce both energy and output (what you produce).
  • Protect your health as part of the work: sleep well, eat regular meals, exercise and get medical check-ups (visits to a doctor to check your health), because creative work needs energy. Harmful habits reduce long-term output.

Food

1 min
  • Cook your own food and pay closer attention to what you eat. Food is the fuel for your body, and you must live with its effects for the rest of your life.
  • Avoid fast food, frozen food, fried items and sugar. They make you feel worse after meals compared with healthy meals.
  • If you cannot cook all your own meals, home-cooked food prepared by family members still counts as a healthy diet habit. The aim is meals made from fresh ingredients at home, rather than fast food or frozen products.

Reading

1 min
  • Read at least 30 minutes of non-fiction (real facts and ideas, not stories) every day. Choose books that build financial and business knowledge, and avoid light entertainment reading.

Meditation, mindset and personal development

1 min
  • Meditation (sitting quietly to train your attention) is strongly recommended as a daily practice in a fixed, pre-blocked slot (a time you set aside in advance) of 5, 10 or 30 minutes. Ideally, do it right after waking, to start the day with more mental clarity.
  • Set a timer for the planned session length, for example 20 minutes. Ideally the session is in total silence, although the speaker used music to help.
  • Choose a practice time you will actually keep. Morning and bedtime are common favourites, but a lunch break or scheduled break is far better than skipping the practice altogether.
  • Practise relaxation during the day. Briefly recall the physical feeling of relaxation several times, for example by mentally repeating that you feel more relaxed. Doing this several times a day can reduce fatigue (tiredness) and improve how you handle situations.
  • For a solo owner (one person running the business alone) in the early stage, business performance depends heavily on the owner's mental and physical condition. If the owner is not in a good state, the chances of getting the business running successfully drop significantly. So personal development belongs alongside business building.

Daily mindset routine:

  1. Sit calmly and silently repeat the mantra (a short phrase you repeat to focus your mind): I am focused, I am committed, and I am patient.
  2. While keeping your attention on your goals, say: I am focused on reaching my goals, I am committed to my goals, and I have the patience to reach my goals.

Cold showers

1 min

Cold shower routine for extra energy (optional):

  1. Take a normal warm shower.
  2. Switch to cold water for the last 2 to 3 minutes. Alternatively, wash only with the coldest water available.
  3. The speaker's own practice was once or twice per week, after cardio. It can be done every morning when extra energy is needed.

Daily routines

2 min

Fixed routine

  • Wake up at the same time each day and leave home for a set workplace at the same time.
  • Keep a fixed daily routine when travelling or working from home, with set work hours and set family time. Mixing too much work into family time, or the reverse, throws the balance off.
  • Keep a fixed daily routine with a regular work slot, and avoid breaking it. Inertia (the tendency to stay stuck in the same state) stops progress and makes the work harder to restart. The aim is to do the work every day, even when it does not feel productive.
  • Systems thinking (seeing how parts of your life affect each other) applies to health and relationships. Fixed workout times, a consistent sleep schedule and scheduled time with a partner produce more consistent energy and results than leaving them to chance.

Morning routine for setting up the day (start before about 8:00):

  1. Take a 30-minute walk while reading the one-year and five-year goal lists.
  2. Do a gym session.
  3. Review business metrics (numbers that show how the business performs) and finances.
  4. Block focused work time in the calendar (your schedule app) before opening the task list.

Daily structure used by the speaker:

  1. Wake up around 5:00-6:00 a.m.
  2. First hour: no phone, notifications off, focus mode (a phone setting that silences distractions). Use it for mindset preparation: coffee, a 30-minute walk in daylight, and reviewing goals and the kind of person you want to be.
  3. Gym for 30 minutes to 1 hour.
  4. Shower and get ready.
  5. Start work around 7:30 a.m. with the business review: emails, finances, ad performance (how well your paid ads do) and order checks.
  6. Spend about 30 minutes on messages from students or the community.
  7. Work on ads for the first two hours after business and community tasks.
  8. Afternoon: handle ad hoc tasks (unplanned tasks as they come up) such as student calls, mentoring work and strategy preparation. Book them into the calendar as they come up.

Breaks and burnout prevention

1 min
  • Schedule breaks. Sustained motivation requires planned rest. Avoid constant overwork to prevent burnout (complete exhaustion from too much work). End each break with a defined next goal.
  • Rest is part of productivity. A short 15-30 minute break with quiet music and no screens, such as a power nap (a brief sleep) in the early afternoon, can reset energy levels when the body is tired.

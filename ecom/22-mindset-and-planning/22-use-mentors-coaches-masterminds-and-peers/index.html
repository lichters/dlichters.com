Playbook › 22. Mindset and planning

Use mentors, coaches, masterminds and peers

Section 22.22 · 22 of 26 in this chapter · 23 min read

In short

  • Ask a mentor specific, deep questions only. Search the basics yourself first.
  • Pick one strategy and follow it. Stop comparing many methods.
  • Before each paid call, book, fill in the survey, then email your topics.
  • If you are stuck, ask for help early. Do not lose days on one task.
  • Use only the official support email or community. Ignore strangers who offer private help.
More key points
  • Join a mastermind, a small group of 3 to 5 peers who help each other reach goals.
  • Meet on a fixed schedule, such as every two weeks, or the group falls apart.
  • Pick peers at about the same stage of building their business.
  • Give help first, because help comes back to you.

Choosing a mentor and a single strategy

2 min
  • Pay for mentorship (paid guidance from an experienced person) only when you can ask specific, in-depth questions. Generic questions that a quick web search can answer waste the investment.
  • Focus mentorship questions on what cannot be found by a basic search, because a mentor can share knowledge that is not easily found online.
  • Pick a single strategy (one plan for how you run the business) from one mentor and follow it. Do not second-guess among many approaches. This reduces decision fatigue (feeling worn out from too many choices).
  • Learn the basics on your own before getting mentorship. Use the mentor to speed up progress, not to replace learning.
  • Mentorship from an experienced coach is a practical investment for a beginner. It shortens the path through product research (finding products worth selling), ad testing (trying ads to see what works) and payments (setting up how customers pay).
  • Compared with self-funded trial and error (learning by paying for your own mistakes), a coach shortens that path.
  • A beginner with no experienced person to ask tends to repeat avoidable mistakes and lose money on them.
  • Active community channels (chat rooms inside a community) where experienced operators answer questions and give feedback shorten that learning curve.
  • Mentorship gives an exponential advantage (a benefit that grows faster and faster over time).
  • Successful people usually had a mentor. A mentor helps you avoid repeating known mistakes.
  • Free YouTube content is enough to learn ads fundamentals (the basics of ads) and how to read ad logic.
  • Add a mentor or consultation when you hit a specific problem, such as not knowing why ads fail after a first campaign.
  • Keep improving and keep working after the mentorship ends. Success often comes months after the programme.
  • Results come from steady daily work rather than the course itself.
  • The paid mentoring period lasts three months.
  • Within those three months, a beginner should be able to build the store, launch ads and start making sales.
  • If the student is not yet confident running Facebook ads (paid adverts on Facebook) at the end, support continues until they are.
  • In a mentorship program, you choose which mentor to book for each call.
  • You can book several calls with the same mentor or with different mentors.

Preparing questions and surveys

2 min
  • Write the questions down before each call so you can check during the session that every topic you need is covered.
  • Making notes is the minimum requirement even if you are unsure there will be enough time.
  • Ask questions that solve a real problem you cannot work out yourself.
  • Do not ask for things you can look up. Better questions speed up progress more than waiting for answers.
  • If you are unsure whether you are reasoning about a niche (a narrow group of buyers with one shared need) or product correctly, ask a coach on a group or one-to-one call.
  • Bring the specific criteria you are unsure about so the coach can check them.
  • If you feel stuck during market selection (choosing which market to sell to), ask for help instead of letting the same doubts loop in your head.
  • Asking questions is a normal and expected part of building a business.
  • Once you have a shortlisted niche (a niche you narrowed down to), get a second opinion from a coach, a team review, or a market verification check (a check that real buyers exist).
  • Outside input helps you test how you are thinking about the market.
  • Asking questions in a community is a good way to get a single stuck step solved.
  • Rephrase the question and ask again later if it goes unanswered.

    2. Open the link in a web browser.

  • The survey asks basic questions about the person and the business.
  • Coaching calls should not be spent on basic background questions, so the survey handles that information before the call.
  • Each coaching session is one hour, so preparing in advance lets the time go straight to useful help.
  • A single coaching session lasts one hour.

Preparing for paid one-to-one sessions

2 min

  • Without a plan, the session is spent finding data and deciding what to discuss instead of solving problems.
  • Do not book a one-to-one session without a clear topic and a defined outcome you want to achieve.
  • Booking a call just to have a chat gives uncertain value for the money paid.
  • Before each call, the basic pre-consultation survey (a short form before the call) must be completed after booking.
  • Preparation for every upcoming call follows the same sequence: book, complete the survey, then email the topics and details.

    8. Copy the emails you want reviewed, or paste their body text.
    9. Send one email to the support address listed in the course resources before each call. It should contain the list of topics and questions plus any attachments or links.
    10. If you are unsure what material is needed or lack time, send at least the list of topics and questions.
    11. If the reviewer replies asking for specific material, provide it before the call.
  • Before each call, email the support address listed in the course resources with your list of topics, questions, and any attachments or links.
  • This is best practice and should take little time.

  • Do not let fear of running out of time stop you from preparing.

Mastermind setup

2 min
  • Small peer groups called masterminds (groups of peers who meet regularly to help each other) keep members accountable (answerable to others for their goals).
  • Committing to tasks before a recurring call (a meeting that repeats on a set schedule) makes people more likely to finish them.
  • Look in community channels (online groups or forums where members talk) for people on a similar timeline.
  • Agree on a fixed call schedule with them.
  • Stay connected with other business owners. Use masterminds, community groups or small peer calls.
  • This keeps your momentum (steady forward progress) going.
  • If no mastermind exists, start one or post in the community.
  • To find a mastermind, search the community forum (the online discussion board) for "mastermind."
  • Join any that fit.
  • If none exist, post an introduction.
  • In it, state your stage (the point you have reached in the program) and that you want to start one.
  • Masterminds work best when members are at about the same stage.
  • Sharing your current progress makes matching easier.
  • A mastermind gives a home-based operator (someone who runs a business from home) the social contact an office job would otherwise provide.
  • Synergy (a group doing better together than each member would alone) means the group's combined progress beats what each member would reach alone.
  • Peers who hit their targets motivate the others.
  • Members hold each other to their goals.
  • Masterminds need a fixed schedule to stay active.
  • Without a set schedule and responsibility, the group dissolves (falls apart).
  • Mastermind format, standard procedure:
    1. Fix a recurring call every two weeks at the same time.
    2. Give each member 10 to 15 minutes to speak.
    3. Assign one person to take notes.
    4. At the next meeting, review the previous notes, check where each member stands, and touch base (check in briefly) on progress.
  • Three questions to structure each mastermind session:
    1. What are you working on right now? Others may learn from it or find a faster method.
    2. What do you need help with? Members can ask peers privately instead of posting in public forums.
    3. What can you share that will help everyone else? This forces each member to contribute and creates the synergy effect.
  • Join or start a small recurring group call with about five peers in the same field.
  • In these calls, members share mistakes and wins openly.
  • Learning from the group's shared mistakes shortens the learning curve (the time it takes to become skilled).
  • This also reduces isolation (feeling alone) in the early stage.
  • Share what worked and what did not over the past month with a peer group of established store owners (people who already run a shop).
  • Sharing builds familiarity. It tends to draw out others' experiences in return.
  • Post an open question with your own results first. This makes it easier for others to respond.
  • A peer community, such as a Discord group (a chat app with topic channels), is useful for sharing problems.
  • Being heard helps even when no one can solve the problem, because it reduces stress.
  • Active members, mentors (experienced guides) and students alike often offer solutions.

Networking and partner outreach

2 min

  • The work only pays off when it has already been done.
  • Starting late is like digging a water source only after you are already in need.
  • Relationship building produces compounding results (results that grow on top of earlier ones) with consistency.
  • The more regular effort you invest, the stronger the outcomes.
  • Sporadic (irregular) effort yields weaker results.
  • Introverts (quiet people who prefer to work alone) or people who find this outreach daunting (scary or overwhelming) can still succeed through small, consistent steps.
  • Results usually turn out better than expected once action is taken.
  • Do the well-digging activity consistently. Well-digging means slow, steady groundwork done before you need it.
  • Do not worry about how long it will take.
  • Spend a little time each day.
  • Expect many days to have little to do, because most prospects (people or businesses you hope to work with) publish only occasionally.

  • This keeps the process manageable and avoids spamming (sending too many unwanted messages).
  • A Dream 100 list does not have to reach exactly 100 names.
  • The goal is to get as far as you can.
  • It can be built gradually in small daily sessions alongside other tasks.
  • Daily action for this stage: work through the whole Dream 100 list.
  • Follow, subscribe, like, join groups and forums, create forum accounts, and subscribe to email lists for each contact.

Playbook › 22. Mindset and planning

Find and break one weakness with discipline and habits

Section 22.15 · 15 of 26 in this chapter · 10 min read

In short

  • Pick one main business weakness and work on it for six months, not the whole list at once.
  • Keep daily goals small and easy, so you keep doing them.
  • A habit forms after three to four weeks of doing it on every relevant day. Give yourself a small reward right after each time.
  • Small daily gains add up over a year. 1% better each day grows to about 37% in a year.
This section has 11 parts. Tap one to open it.

Why repeated action beats revenue targets

1 min
  • Focus your business plan on repeated actions that matter, not only on revenue targets (money coming in). Small actions compound (grow on top of each other) into much bigger results.
  • Small useful actions done consistently (again and again) lead to growth that is inevitable. Skipping them leads to decline that is just as inevitable.
  • six months of steady daily practice in a skill brings clear improvement. It also opens chances you would not have seen at the start.

Identifying business and personal weaknesses

1 min
  • Weaknesses change as the business reaches a new revenue level (amount of money coming in). A weakness that hurt at startup is often not the main one at a stage of several million dollars. Check weaknesses again every time the business reaches a new stage.
  • a business making about 2 to 3 million USD a year has different weak points than a startup. Use that revenue level as the signal to check weaknesses again.
  • A weakness is also a hidden opportunity. Once you find a weak point, fixing it gives you a clear way to grow.
  • Every weakness hides an opportunity. For example, a business with no organic traffic (free visitors from search engines, not from ads) that depends only on paid ads (paid promotion) has the opportunity to build an SEO strategy. SEO means search engine optimisation: getting free visitors from Google.
  • Weaknesses come from two sources. Check both when you audit (review carefully):
    • The business: products sold, suppliers used, marketing and advertising approach.
    • The owner: not committing enough time, lacking focus.
  • Owners tend to spend most of their time where they feel strong and confident, so weaker areas get ignored. Watch for this comfort-zone trap (staying in what feels safe) when you review how you spend your time.
  • Operational weak spots, such as manufacturing or admin (paperwork and routine tasks), can be built step by step. Aim to move from a low self-rated skill level (how good you think you are) toward a solid one over time.
  • To find a weakness, ask yourself why you are not yet where you want to be in your business or in life. Write the answers in two separate lists, one for business weaknesses and one for personal weaknesses, since the two often overlap.

Documenting the weakness in your review

1 min
  • Find and write down at least one business weakness in your business review. This gives your improvement work a clear focus for the coming period.

Procedure: choosing the weakness from the annual review

  1. Open the annual review (your yearly check-in) that you wrote for the previous year.
  2. Find your biggest business weakness on the list, usually the item at the top.
  3. Write that weakness down as a single note.
  4. Set the goal of fixing this weakness over the next six months.
  5. Plan the concrete steps in the following work sessions.

Choosing one weakness for six months

1 min
  • Pick only one biggest business weakness as your target for the next six months. Do not work through the whole list at once. Write it down as a single note before you plan the steps to fix it.
  • Work on one weakness at a time. Spreading effort across several changes at once lowers the chance that any of them really changes, although the chosen weakness can be a big one.
  • Plan a six-month focus on breaking one chosen weakness. Only one weakness is targeted in that period. The work continues until it is no longer one of your significant weaknesses.
  • Put in place specific steps and habits designed to break each identified weakness. Start practising them right away, not at some later date.
  • At the six-month mark (mid-year review, a check-in halfway through the year), find a new business weakness for the next six months. Rotate the improvement focus from one area to another.

Why weaknesses stay unfixed

1 min
  • Most people fail to fix a weakness for three reasons. They never name it clearly. They try to do too much too soon. They lack the discipline (steady self-control) and consistency to let a change run its course. Writing down a business or personal weakness already puts you ahead of most people.
  • Unrealistic goals, such as trying to fix everything at once, usually lead to failure. A focused, realistic goal for a single weakness is the better approach.
  • Many people quit a change after a week or two because it does not show results fast enough. A new practice or habit needs time before it can take effect.

Self-discipline

1 min
  • Breaking a weakness rests on three elements: self-discipline, consistency and habits. Each one must be built on purpose.
  • Self-discipline is a positive, self-driven decision to commit to a goal and follow the path needed to reach it. No one else can impose it. No outside punishment or accountability (someone checking on you) is involved.
  • Successful business owners differ from unsuccessful ones mainly in self-discipline and consistency. They do not differ in secret knowledge or unique ideas. Without the ability to stay consistent on necessary work, the chance of success drops significantly.
  • The book Do the Work by Steven Pressfield is a short read for beginners who struggle with motivation and need to stay focused on steady action.

Procedure: commitment statement for self-discipline

  1. Write on a piece of paper the statement "I will break my [weakness]", naming the specific weakness.
  2. Place the paper where you will see it every day, such as above your computer, on your mirror, on your fridge, or as your phone or desktop background.
  3. Read the statement out loud every day for about six months, or until the weakness is no longer one of your significant weaknesses in the business.

Consistency parameters

1 min
  • Consistency means following the chosen path the same way every day until the goal is reached or the weakness is broken. Parameters (fixed daily limits for time, money or output) make this possible. Keep them easy to achieve, because overly large commitments lead to discouragement.
  • Time-based parameter, email marketing (sending emails to customers): 15 minutes a day on email work, such as writing copy (sales text), splitting your email list into groups (segmentation), or setting up emails that send by themselves (automations). Do this every day you work on the business. Committing to writing emails for eight hours a day for a month is not achievable for most people, who find long writing sessions mentally daunting.
  • Output-based parameter: set a fixed daily result instead of a time limit, such as one new email per day. The time this takes varies with the length of the email, but the daily output stays the same.
  • Recurring SEO task: 30 minutes a day is an achievable and sustainable parameter. Committing to SEO work for eight hours a day indefinitely is not.

Procedure: setting a consistency action

  1. Identify one task or behaviour that will break the weakness. If the weakness is a bad habit, identify one thing you will consistently not do.
  2. Define an achievable parameter for it in time, money or output.
  3. Write the task and its parameter down.

Habit loop: trigger, action, reward

3 min

A habit is formed by performing three steps consistently for about three to four weeks. For some habits it takes longer. Keep repeating the steps on every relevant day until the action happens without conscious thought.

Loop element What it is Example Source
Trigger Environmental cue (a signal from your surroundings) that it is time to act Recurring calendar entry plus hard-to-ignore notifications on computer, phone and smartwatch
Action Start immediately when the trigger appears Switch to a 30-minute task the moment the trigger goes off
Reward Small, pleasurable, given after every completion Dark chocolate, a favourite TV series, an outdoor activity, or half an hour or an hour of games after two hours of business work
  • Every habit, good or bad, has a trigger. A trigger is an environmental cue that says it is time to act. Removing or changing the cue behind a bad habit is an effective way to break it.
  • A trigger for a daily one-hour writing block can be a recurring calendar entry (an event that repeats on your calendar) saying it is time to start. Add notifications that are hard to ignore on the computer, the phone and a smartwatch alarm.
  • Once the trigger goes off, start the action straight away. For example, switch to a 30-minute task the moment the trigger appears. During the three to four week building phase this depends on self-discipline. Planning your days and weeks around triggers helps you respond when other commitments come up.
  • Give a reward immediately after each completed action, every time. The reward reinforces (strengthens) the habit. It should be small and pleasurable and does not need to be large.
  • Examples of rewards include a piece of dark chocolate after a task, a favourite TV series, an enjoyable outdoor activity, or video games for a set period after a block of work. For example, half an hour or an hour of games after two hours of business work.
  • Habits that give the brain a dopamine reward (the brain's feel-good chemical) are very hard to break, because the brain subconsciously craves that outcome. Replacing such a reward with a different, enjoyable one is more realistic than simply removing it.
  • A habit only needs to be performed on the days it is relevant, for example on the days you work on the business. If you do not work on weekends, the habit does not apply on weekends.
  • Habits are formed and broken more easily when practised in a relaxed state, with a clear picture of the desired end result in mind. Trying hard to resist a habit often reinforces it.
  • Habit change works through both avoiding a habit and deliberately performing a new behaviour, as long as the desired end result stays clearly in mind. Straining against a habit with willpower tends to reinforce it. Practising the new behaviour calmly and without effort is more effective.

Procedure: setting up the weakness-breaking habit

  1. Identify one beneficial habit that reflects the chosen weakness. Identify the specific task you must do consistently to break it.
  2. Set an environmental trigger (a cue from your surroundings) for that habit.
  3. Set a small, pleasurable reward for completing it.
  4. Take the action every time the trigger occurs, straight away. Collect the reward right after each completion. Put all of this in place and start the next day.

Procedure: building a new habit (repeat on every relevant day for about three to four weeks)

  1. Set a trigger, which is an environmental cue telling you it is time to take the action.
  2. When the trigger appears, start the action immediately, with no delay.
  3. Give yourself a reward right after finishing the action, every single time.

Breaking bad habits

1 min
  • Breaking a bad habit is harder than building a new one.

Procedure: breaking a bad habit

  1. Remove or replace the trigger, for example by avoiding the people, places or situations that set off the habit.
  2. Make the action harder to perform, for example by never keeping the item at home or in the fridge.
  3. Change or replace the reward with a healthier or more beneficial one.

Starting small and growing the habit

1 min
  • Making a habit too big or complex, or building several habits at the same time, is a common reason habits fail. An unachievable habit, such as eight hours of SEO work a day, will not be kept up for the three to four weeks needed to form it.

Procedure: growing a new habit gradually

  1. Start with a very small version of the action, such as 5 to 10 minutes of copywriting (writing sales text) practice per day.
  2. Once that is consistent, increase the time by a modest percentage, for example from 10 minutes to 20 minutes per day.
  3. Keep building on each new level. The small steps compound (add up on top of each other) into a much bigger habit over time.

Compounding small gains

1 min
  • Aim to be about 1% better each day than your own version of the day before. Small consistent gains add up to visible results over time.
  • a daily improvement of 1 percent in a business compounds to about 37 percent growth over one year. Small consistent gains therefore add up significantly.
  • Daily compounding check: 1.01 raised to the 365th power (multiplied by itself 365 times) is about 37.8. So a 1 percent daily improvement sustained for a full year produces roughly 37 to 38 percent growth. The gain comes from the daily repetition, not from any single large change.

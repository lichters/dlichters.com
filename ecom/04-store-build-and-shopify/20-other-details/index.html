Playbook › 4. Store build and Shopify

Other details

Section 4.20 · 20 of 20 in this chapter · 16 min read

In short

  • Pick your niche (a small group of buyers with one shared need) before you build any store.
  • Build in order, step by step. Do not lose time on small design details.
  • Launch simply and fast. A plain store with the right product beats a perfect store with the wrong one.
  • Build time varies by source. Measure from product choice to live, and aim for two weeks or less.

Build order: niche and first stages first

1 min
  • Choose your first niche before you build any store. A niche is a small group of buyers with one shared need.
  • The store's design and content depend on the niche.
  • A store built without a chosen niche is out of order.
  • Finish stages 1 and 2 before you start store building in stage 3.
  • Follow the stages in order for best results.
  • Stage 3 is the store-building checklist:
    1. Start with stage 3.0.
    2. Complete the action task (a hands-on job) in each stage.
    3. Click Complete when done. You keep access to reopen earlier stages.
    4. Progress in order through 3.1, 3.2, 3.3 and beyond.
    5. Complete all stages so that every required store part is built.
  • A beginner without web design experience can build a first dropshipping store (selling products that a supplier ships for you, so you keep no stock) by following a step-by-step process, completing the steps in order.

Shopify build sequence

1 min

Shopify is a platform (a service) for building and running an online store.

Standard build (merged from two identical sequences):

  1. Sign up for Shopify and start the store-building guide.
  2. Keep building the store, asking for help when you get stuck.
  3. Finish a demo store (a practice store that buyers cannot use yet).
  4. Remove the storefront password (the lock that hides the store from visitors) so the store is publicly reachable.

Build with supplier approval:

  1. Sign up for Shopify.
  2. Set up the theme (the store's design template), using guides and repeating steps until it works.
  3. Build collections (groups of products) and navigation (the store menu) to group products logically.
  4. Set up the demo store and take it live (open it to buyers).
  5. Call the first supplier (the company that makes or ships the products) and get approved.
  6. Upload all products to the store.

Beginner build with branding:

  1. Choose the Shopify theme and set up the store.
  2. Set up branding, such as a logo (your brand's picture mark), from a freelancer platform such as Fiverr (a site where you hire freelancers).
  3. Add the niche products and product data (product names, photos, prices and descriptions) once supplier approval comes in.
  4. Clean up the design and make product pages tidy.
  5. Launch the store and set the traffic funnel (the path visitors follow from an ad to a purchase) to start selling.

Split-screen method:

  • Follow the guide step by step on one side of the screen and do the same action in the Shopify admin (the back office where you edit your store) on the other.
  • A complete store can be set up in a few evenings or over a weekend.

Design priorities for a new store

2 min
  • Store appearance (how the store looks) is secondary to choosing the right product and the right audience.
  • A plain store selling the right product to the right people will make sales.
  • A beautiful store selling the wrong product will fail.
  • Do not spend time perfecting navigation menus (the links that help visitors move around), link placement or visual design at the early stage.
  • Focus first on the product page and the homepage.
  • A store does not need to look perfect to produce early results.
  • Focus first on three elements: a strong offer (the deal you give the buyer, such as a discount or bonus), a strong ad and good product images.
  • A weak-looking store can still sell.
  • A well-built Shopify store with a clean design and a product page builder (a tool for building product pages) earns more customer trust than competitors with poor sites.
  • Do not judge a store by your own buying habits.
  • Many customers buy from simple stores when the offer is clear and attractive.
  • Building the store can wait until the account (your social media page) reaches about 1,000 followers or starts going viral (spreading fast), if your store-building skills are weak.
  • If you build quickly, you can set it up earlier.
  • Spending months perfecting a custom store before launch wastes time and money.
  • Launch a simple, high-converting store quickly (high-converting means it turns many visitors into buyers), then focus on the product, customer service, logistics (how orders are packed and shipped) and marketing.
  • Beginners often lose days on small details, such as button colour, the add-to-cart design (the button that puts an item in the basket) or whether the supplier can print a logo on the product.
  • These matter far less than getting the product, the ads and the store live quickly.
  • A beginner does not need professional design skills to build a clean, converting e-commerce site (a site for selling products online).
  • The goal is a site that looks clean and professional, that suppliers can review, and that is good enough to launch and make first sales.
  • Perfection delays launch, so a simple site is preferred over a perfect one.
  • Advanced functionality (extra features) and professional designers can be added as the business grows.
  • Build only what is needed for launch and postpone everything else.
  • Time spent on non-essential pages before launch is wasted, which includes blogging (writing blog posts) and any design work that does not help the first sales.

Case: a simple store became a product

  • Starting point: the operator spent a lot of time building perfect stores across several dropshipping projects. This led to the insight that a simple, quick-to-launch store converts best.
  • Action: that insight became Debutify, a plug-and-play theme and software business (ready to install, with little setup) built around fast store launches.
  • Result: the software took about 12 months to get off the ground, with significant initial investment and then sustained marketing before it became a working product.

Conversion-ready store (Phase 3)

1 min

Conversion means a visitor becomes a buyer. A conversion barrier is the biggest obstacle to a sale.

  • Phase 3 store building (Days 31 to 45) must establish trust within three seconds of a customer viewing the landing page.
  • Trust is the primary conversion barrier for a new brand.
  • A store built for conversion in Phase 3 must include:
    1. An Elixir or Shrine theme (named store design templates).
    2. A clean product page design.
    3. Strong offers, such as bundles, buy-one-get-one or free packs.
    4. Customer reviews, trust badges (small logos that show safety, such as secure payment icons) and a satisfaction guarantee.
    5. AI-generated product photos. AI (artificial intelligence, computer programs that create content) makes them.
    6. User-generated content (UGC, photos and posts made by real customers) placed above the fold (the part of the page visible before scrolling).
  • Evaluate store pages for:
    1. High-quality product imagery (clear, sharp photos).
    2. Clear product descriptions.
    3. Customization and variant options (colours, sizes and other choices).
    4. A professional layout.
  • Landing pages for problem-solving products should show the use case (how the product is used) and the effect of the product visually.
  • For example, use a short GIF (a small looping animation) of the relief shown on the body.
  • A visual demonstration explains the benefit faster than text.
  • Trust claims should be specific.
  • In a weak store, replace vague trust claims with named partners, logos and verifiable details (facts a visitor can check).

Using competitor stores as benchmarks

1 min

Competitors are rival stores. A benchmark is a reference point to compare against.

  • Do not use cheap-looking stores, often built by Chinese suppliers trying to compete directly, as design models.
  • Their pages tend to look like scams and damage trust in your own store.
  • A store using a shared commercial theme (a ready-made design many stores copy), such as Atlas, is a sign that it is a dropshipping store and not a brand-built site.
  • Recognizing this helps you separate dropshipping competitors from professional benchmarks.
  • Use only competitor stores that look clean and load at normal speed as design benchmarks.
  • Skip slow or broken sites.
  • Judging whether a store looks good is a visual check any online shopper can make.
  • A professional product page is recognizable without special expertise, so use your first impression as a quick filter.
  • To see which products a Shopify store sells best, add /collections/all?sort_by=best_selling to the store's domain (its web address).
  • This lists its products by sales rank and gives a free way to read a competitor's top sellers before you decide what to test.
  • A pump-and-dump store does not need a full brand build.
  • A simple store with the product name, product photos, product videos and an FAQ section is enough.

Fixing a weak store with limited time

1 min

When you take over a weak store with limited time, set priorities in this order:

  1. Remove every product that carries a trademark (a protected brand name or logo) or licensed character (a brand figure, such as a cartoon) you do not have the rights to sell.
  2. Split the navigation (the menu) and homepage into categories by the recipient or buyer group the store serves.
  3. Rewrite the header (the strip at the top of the page) so the empty space carries a short buyer-facing message, such as a free add-on or a seasonal date.
  4. Delete empty default Shopify sections (ready-made blocks in the page layout), such as an unused video block or an empty promotions box.
  5. Replace vague trust claims with named partners, logos and verifiable details.

Note: the navigation rule for a new store (focus on the product page and homepage first) and the navigation step above (splitting categories in a weak store) apply to different situations.

Store settings and theme details

1 min
  • Shopify Settings > Custom data is an advanced section for metafields (extra custom data fields).
  • A new store does not need changes here and can be ignored at launch.
  • Store-level settings, apps (add-on tools) and other logistics are set up in separate steps after the design and trust pages.
  • Do not spend time on them while building the product page itself.
  • Dynamic placement (where a block appears, which can change by screen size) applies per screen size.
  • A minimum breakpoint (the screen width where a layout rule starts) of 999 with the maximum set to None targets screens from that width upward.
  • Medium screens need their own breakpoint range, selector (the code that picks which element a rule changes) and placement location.

Trust documents, copying and legal protection

1 min
  • Create unique PDF documents (a file format that looks the same on every device), such as warranty terms, installation instructions and product specifications.
  • Place your logo on each one.
  • The logo helps protect them from being copied and supports later takedown claims (requests to remove copied content).
  • Copy-protection code on a store can be bypassed by determined users, so do not rely on it to protect your content.
  • If a competitor copies your store, submit the copying site's links on Shopify's DMCA page (a form for US copyright takedown requests).
  • If the copy is not changed, it is typically taken offline within about 24 hours.
  • If a supplier asks whether your store is EDI compliant (EDI means electronic data interchange, a system that sends orders between companies automatically), explain that orders are sent as automatic purchase orders by email.
  • You do not need to claim an EDI setup.
  • When you want to discuss a specific part of your store, include a link to that section of the website in your pre-call email (the email sent before the call).
  • This lets the reviewer look at it before the call.

Split testing on Shopify

1 min

Split testing means showing two versions of a page to different visitors and comparing results.

  • On-page split testing is harder on Shopify than on platforms like WordPress.
  • Two practical routes exist: a dedicated split-testing tool, or an app that manages the test and collects the data.

Page split test with Google Optimize (as described in the source):

  1. Duplicate the original product page so both versions are identical.
  2. Give the duplicate its own URL (web address).
  3. Set the duplicate to noindex, using a noindex app (an add-on that tells Google not to list a page), to avoid duplicate content problems (the same text on two pages, which confuses Google).
  4. Create the test in Google Optimize so it redirects a share of traffic to the duplicate while the rest stays on the original.
  • Note (added in this rewrite): Google shut down Google Optimize in 2023. These steps need a current replacement tool.

Digital product stores

1 min
  • Shopify is recommended as the hosting platform (the service that runs your store) for a digital product store (a store that sells files, not physical goods).
  • A Shopify app installed from the app store handles automatic digital delivery by emailing the product to the buyer right after purchase.
  • Plr-free-downloads.com offers free private-label-rights (PLR) ebooks across almost every topic. PLR is a license that lets you edit and sell ready-made content.
  • Download one, check the content, then change the cover and layout in Canva (an online design tool) or have it reworked on Fiverr.
  • Sending the PDF unchanged is not recommended.
  • ChatGPT (an AI writing tool) can be used to draft or speed up writing an ebook from scratch.

AI and store-building help

1 min
  • AI (artificial intelligence, computer programs that write and create) powered tools can generate product descriptions, FAQs, reviews and even store layouts in minutes.
  • Store creation that used to take weeks can be done in hours.
  • Before AI, a seller had to write descriptions by hand, build the store by hand, order the product to their own address and photograph it for product images.
  • AI reduces most of this manual work.
  • Simple store issues, such as changing a colour or a button that does not respond, can usually be solved with AI when the platform, theme and exact symptom are given.
  • Only more advanced technical questions need a community or coach.
  • Describe the issue to AI with the platform, theme and exact symptom, for example:
I am using Shopify, I am using the Elixir theme, the add to cart button shows loading but nothing happens after I press it.

Tools and storage

1 min
  • Google Drive (Google's online file storage) can be used as cloud storage (files kept on the internet) for supplier images and files instead of a Dropbox account.

Amazon and eBay listings

2 min

A listing is a product page on a marketplace. A channel is a place you sell, such as Amazon or eBay.

Channel priority:

  • Amazon alone can sustain a business in the early stages because of high conversion rates.
  • Long-term strategy includes diversification across channels (selling in several places).
  • Amazon's buying-mindset audience (shoppers who arrive ready to buy) and FBA (Fulfilled by Amazon, where Amazon stores and ships your orders) infrastructure make it the primary channel to focus on initially, before adding a Shopify store.

Product title:

  • Product title is critical for keyword visibility (how easily shoppers find the product when they search) and conversion.
  • Place your primary keywords (the main search words) first.
  • Avoid leading with an unknown brand name unless you are Apple or Nike.
  • Keep the title to 250 characters or less.
  • Do not repeat keywords, for example do not list garlic press twice.
  • Amazon automatically indexes keyword variations (similar word forms) by itself.

Bullet points (the feature lines on a listing):

  1. Place your two strongest customer benefits in the top and bottom bullet point positions, because shoppers scan first and last.
  2. Populate all five bullet points with distinct features and benefits.
  3. Keep the text scannable (easy to skim) and avoid excessive capitalization or keyword stuffing (packing in too many search words).
  • Bold formatting (double asterisks or bold tags) in bullet points increases conversion rates.
  • In the resistance bands case study, switching the first bullet point to double-starred text, for example Material: Premium Latex with Comfort Grips, produced a 5% jump in conversion rate.

Photos:

  • Use all nine product photo slots with diverse, high-quality images.
  • Include a stock photo of the product, multiple angles, lifestyle photos showing people using the product, and close-ups of unique features.
  • Photos are the only way customers can evaluate a product they cannot hold or inspect in person.

Product description (bottom section, above reviews):

  • Use basic HTML formatting (simple code tags that make text bold or structured), such as bold text, to highlight benefits, titles and key features.
  • Create a strong title that solves a customer problem or highlights the main benefit.
  • End with a clear call-to-action (a sentence that tells the buyer what to do) reminding customers to scroll to the top and click the yellow buy button, as this surprisingly increases conversions.

eBay:

  • On eBay, set the quantity of each listing to 1 so that the sales limit counts one unit instead of the full stock.
  • The quantity replenishes automatically after each sale.
  • An eBay store subscription costs about 20 per month.
  • A Shopify feed (a file of product data that is sent automatically) can push products to eBay after you connect the channel.
  • Upload the same products from Shopify to eBay without creating a separate catalog (a separate product list).

Ad settings linked to the store

1 min

Google Ads is Google's paid ad system. A campaign is a group of ads with shared settings.

Google Ads campaign setup:

  • The languages setting defaults to English during campaign creation.
  • If your target customers speak a different language, add that language option in the campaign settings.
  • Write your ad copy in the selected language to reach customers with that language preference.
  • During campaign creation you may optionally enter your website URL.
  • Entering a URL pre-fills certain campaign settings automatically.
  • You may also leave it blank and configure all settings manually.
  • Either approach is acceptable.

Raising CPC (cost per click, the price you pay each time someone clicks your ad) to spend the budget:

  1. Raise the CPC increment (the size of each price step), for example from 50 cents to 75 cents.
  2. Run for several days, then check whether the daily budget is fully spent.
  3. If not, raise the CPC again to the next increment.
  • Do not increase the CPC daily.
  • Allow multiple days between increments to gather data.

Device bid multipliers (a percentage added to or taken from your bid for each device type):

  • You can set device bid multipliers for mobile, tablet and desktop separately within each campaign.
  • After running ads for a few weeks, analyze conversion rates (the share of clicks that become sales) by device in the campaign reports.
  • If mobile converts significantly better or worse than desktop, adjust device bids accordingly.
  • For example, use a +20% bid multiplier for mobile if it converts better, or -10% for desktop if it converts worse.

Influencer marketing for niche products

1 min
  • Do not spend a large share of effort on influencer marketing (paying social media personalities to promote products) for niche products that are bought from need, rather than from fandom (liking a brand or person).
  • Look for blogs and websites with content for buyers at the decision stage (people close to buying) instead, and pursue banner placements there. (2024)

Finance template (P&L)

1 min

P&L means profit and loss, a report of money in and money out.

Layout for the Source E P&L template:

  • Fill only the Actual columns (the real numbers).
  • Preserve all Forecast numbers (planned numbers) and formulas (calculations).
  • Ensure each total row contains a SUM formula (a spreadsheet formula that adds numbers) covering all line items (each separate entry) in that section.
  • Arrange each month in its own column.
  • Add a Total column that sums across months.
  • Add a % of Revenue column on the far right, showing each total as a percentage of Total Revenue.
  • Break every expense out by month instead of showing totals only.

Launch timing and traffic

1 min
  • Store build and launch can take about 21 days for someone working eight hours a day, using a step-by-step launch plan.
  • Afterwards, ongoing work takes about 30 minutes to two hours per day.
  • A store should take no more than two weeks to build from product selection to live.

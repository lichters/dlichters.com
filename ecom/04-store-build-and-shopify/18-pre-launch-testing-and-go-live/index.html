Playbook › 4. Store build and Shopify

Pre-launch testing and go-live

Section 4.18 · 18 of 20 in this chapter · 24 min read

In short

  • Fix the store before paying for ads. Ads send visitors to the store, and a broken store wastes that money.
  • Remove the password before launch, or no customer can see the shop.
  • Test everything yourself, including links, the add-to-cart button, prices, and a test order, on both phone and computer.
  • Launch with at least two suppliers who have real products uploaded.
  • Check that your store can handle a traffic spike (a sudden jump in visitors) before a big post goes live.
More key points
  • Watch your comments. Customers saying they cannot buy is an early warning that your store may be shut down.
  • Test every link and the checkout before and during a viral moment.
  • If people add items to the cart but do not buy, check payments, shipping, and countries first.
  • Keep checking your store after launch. It needs ongoing care, not a single setup.
This section has 12 parts. Tap one to open it.

Readiness before paid traffic

3 min
  • Optimize the store for conversion before any paid traffic goes to it. Conversion means a visitor buys. Paid traffic means visitors you pay for with ads. Visitors who arrive at a store that is not ready cannot be converted, so the advertising budget is wasted.
  • set up and optimize the Shopify store before spending money on ads. Shopify is the software that runs the online shop. This way, paid visitors land on a working store.
  • Before a campaign goes live, make the store attractive. A campaign is a set of ads. A better offer and a better-made website can beat a competitor's store that sells the same product. Weak competitor stores show room for improvement.
  • Run the full pre-launch checklist before sending any paid traffic. Pre-launch means before the store opens to visitors. This catches mistakes in the theme (the store's design template), the product page, bundles (products sold together) or checkout (the payment page). Fix them before money is spent on clicks.
  • If the store is not ready when a video goes viral, sales are lost. Viral means spreading very fast. The store then has to be rebuilt in a hurry.
  • Build a working store before the content has a real chance to take off. This matters most if you have no web-building experience.
  • Set a deadline for the store to be live within one week. On the deadline day, check each store task.
  • Publishing a store before launch carries no risk. Publish means making the store visible on the internet. You are the only visitor until you send traffic.
  • Keep the store unpublished only if you prefer not to test it yourself.
  • Build the store in this order before launch. This is older advice from the 2021-22 source label Source C. No date is given.

Store setup order before launch:

  1. Set the store name.
  2. Activate payments: Shopify Payments (Shopify's own card payment service), PayPal and Clearpay (a pay-later option).
  3. Add products and make sure the stock count is set. The stock count is how many units you have.
  4. Set up shipping.
  5. Set up policies. Policies are the legal pages, such as refunds and shipping.
  6. Connect the domain. The domain is your web address.
  7. Set up markets for UK, Australia and US. Markets are the countries you sell to.
  8. Choose and set up the theme.
  9. Build pages with Pagefly. Pagefly is a page-building app.
  10. Build the homepage.
  11. Build the product page.
  12. Build the About us page.
  13. Build the Contact us page.
  14. Set up the header. The header is the top of every page, with the main menu.
  15. Set up the footer and menus. The footer is the bottom of every page.
  16. Set up checkout.
  17. Add reviews with Ryviu. Ryviu is an app for customer reviews.
  18. Set up Tag Manager. Tag Manager is Google's tool that holds tracking code. Tracking code records what visitors do.
  19. Set up Google Analytics (GA). GA is Google's tool for tracking visitors.
  20. Set up GA4. GA4 is the newest version of Google Analytics.
  21. Review all Shopify settings.
  22. Check whether redirects from the testing site are still active. Redirects are automatic forwards from one web address to another.
  • Leftover redirects from the testing site can keep visitors away from the live store. Check them before launch.
  • When conversion is below 1 percent with a lot of traffic and few add-to-carts, the offer, theme and checkout are the first suspects. Add-to-cart means a visitor puts an item in their basket.
  • Have someone review the checkout process and the offer for friction before running more ads. Friction means anything that slows or confuses buying.

Store access and password protection

4 min
  • Disable the protection before beginning supplier outreach. A supplier is a company that provides products to sell. Suppliers must be able to review the store during first contact without a password.
  • Keep Shopify Payments turned off until real products are live, so nobody can place an order.
  • Launching while the demo password is still active leaves the storefront hidden from visitors and ad traffic. The storefront is the public side of the shop.
  • Remove the password before sending any traffic.
  • A password left on blocks customers. It can also get your Google Merchant Center and Google Ads accounts suspended. Merchant Center is Google's product listing account. Google Ads is Google's ad account. Suspended means shut down.
  • This often happens during supplier work, when the password is switched on and forgotten. Check that it is off before launch.
  • While the online store is password protected, visitors without the password cannot see any pages.
  • The Pages screen shows a banner (a strip of message at the top), reading Online store is password protected.
  • Use the Disable password button before launch.
  • While password protection is on, visitors who open the store address see an Opening Soon page. It has an email sign-up field instead of the shop.
  • While a store is protected, visitors see an Opening soon page with an email signup field. It also has a link for the store owner to log in.
  • A theme published behind this password is not visible to the public until the password is removed. A theme is the store's design.
  • Remove the storefront password once the demo store is complete. A demo store is a test version of the shop. This makes the store publicly accessible.
  • The store is only publicly live after the password toggle (the on/off switch) is switched off and saved.
  • Switching it off without clicking Save does not complete the step.

Where to find the setting. Online Store is the Shopify menu for the shop. Preferences is a settings page inside it. The label varies by source and Shopify version:

Source Location Setting
Online Store > Preferences > Password protection Turn off the password for the store
Online Store > Preferences First setting shown is Store access, currently set to Password protected
Online Store > Preferences > Password protection section Enable password must be off before launch
Online Store > Preferences Disable password can be used once the store is ready to launch
Online Store > Themes Banner Online store is password protected with a Disable password button

Removing the password, step by step:

  1. Log into your Shopify account.
  2. Click Online Store.
  3. Click Preferences, located at the bottom of the Online Store section.
  4. In the Store access setting, which shows Password protected, toggle the switch off.
  5. Click Save.

After saving, the store is live. Anyone who opens the store URL (its web address) can view it.

  • Speed score is not calculated while a store is protected. Speed score is a rating of how fast a page loads.
  • The speed score becomes available 1 to 3 days after protection is disabled. The store stays hidden from speed measurement until protection is removed.
  • Conflict on removing the password, with costs and plan requirements. A plan is a Shopify monthly subscription level. A free trial is a short free test period.

Removing the password with a paid plan, step by step:

  1. Open Online Store > Preferences in the Shopify admin (the back-office area where you run the store).
  2. Click "Select a plan" to end the free trial. The password cannot be disabled without a paid plan.
  3. Choose a plan. The Basic Shopify plan is the recommended starting plan, at USD 29 per month when billed every 30 days.
  4. Add a payment method. A credit card is required. The trial days still remaining are kept before the first charge.
  5. Return to Online Store > Preferences.
  6. Under Password protection, turn off "Enable password" and click Save.
  7. Check the storefront in a separate browser or private window. Confirm the "Opening Soon" password page is gone.

Link and content checks

1 min
  • Click-test every link on the site. This means clicking each one yourself. Confirm each one opens the correct destination with no 404 errors. A 404 error means the page does not exist. Check in this order:
  1. Header menu links, including every item and every dropdown item.
  2. Homepage collection sections, opening each collection. A collection is a group of products.
  3. Every other linked section or button on the homepage.
  4. Footer links, especially the Quick Links and policy links.
  • Click every link on the homepage, the collection pages, the about page and the policy pages.
    1. Fix or remove any link that goes nowhere, is broken, or reloads the same page.
    2. Remove placeholder text and theme default labels, such as generic collection names. Placeholder text is dummy text. Theme default labels come with the design template. Both are left over from installing the theme.
    3. Check that each product category matches what the store says it sells. Also check that the policy pages are rewritten for this business.
    4. Confirm that the contact method, shipping time and return policy are all filled in.

Note: is listed above as a single procedure with five steps. The numbering in the source is 1 to 5, with the click-test as step 1.

  • Before going live, check for glaring issues such as broken links, product pages missing information, mispriced items (wrong prices) and empty collections (collections with no products).
  • The store should look professional from the first day.
  • Check the About Us page and the rest of the site for typos before turning on ads.

Critical elements: prices, buttons and add-to-cart

1 min

  • A product page with the price and compare price (the crossed-out higher price) accidentally hidden produced 40 add-to-carts and only 4 purchases for one student.

  • Always verify that critical information is visible.

  • A missing or unclear add-to-cart button is a technical oversight. It can silently kill conversions.

  • Verify that all buttons and fields work before running ads.

  • A product listing that shows a price of $0.00 or a broken price looks like a bug and kills trust.

  • Test the product page and cart before sending paid traffic.

  • check the store before turning on ads, in this order.

    1. Make sure the checkout works.
    2. Make sure all links in the header and footer work.
    3. Make sure every add-to-cart button works.
    4. Check the About Us page and the rest of the site for typos.
    5. Once confident, turn the ads on.

Mobile and cross-device checks

2 min
  • Check the store on mobile first. Most shoppers buy from phones, and desktop views often look weaker. Make sure the layout looks trusting and polished on a phone.
  • Check the store on a phone as well as on desktop. The mobile layout can look much better or worse than the desktop version.
  • Review the store on a phone in portrait view (held upright), not only on desktop. Desktop layouts can hide gaps where a product photo or GIF (a short looping animation) fails to fit.
  • Count the taps needed to reach the real product page on the phone.
  • Check the mobile version separately from the desktop view at every stage of editing. Mobile is the main traffic source.
  • Confirm that images, icon bars (rows of small symbols), reviews and FAQs (questions and answers) look clean on mobile before publishing.
  • A product title shown vertically on mobile after Add to cart, while the cart shows it horizontally, looks broken and can cost a sale.
  • A likely cause is a font size (text size) set too large for mobile.
  • Shopify checkout appears as a dropdown order summary (a box that opens and closes) on mobile. It displays differently on desktop.
  • Scroll the whole page on mobile before linking the store from TikTok. Confirm that the lower half loads and that text and images display correctly.
  • Most visitors arrive on their phone.

Mobile-first store review, step by step:

  1. Open the store on a phone and count the clicks to reach the actual product page. A product page that takes around five clicks is a weakness.
  2. Put the product page first. The product image, title, price and add to cart should be above everything else.
  3. Move the product details, refund policy and long text blocks below the main product information.
  4. Place customer reviews above the email signup section and the footer.
  5. Keep the footer last. Remove the "Powered by Shopify" credit only if your theme allows it.

Cross-device check before launch:

  1. Before launch, open the store page and every ad creative on a Windows PC and on a Mac. An ad creative is the picture or video of an ad.
  2. Check the page on an iPhone and on an Android phone.
  3. Fix any layout or visual differences before spending on ads. A page can look correct on one device and broken on another.
  • run a test purchase on desktop and on mobile to confirm orders go through.
  • Also check on mobile that all content stays visible and usable.

Test orders and checkout

2 min
  • Place a test order before launch to check that the whole store works as intended.
  • Reviewing it in the Shopify admin shows what a real customer order will look like.

Test order in Shopify:

  1. Open the store and go to the cart.
  2. Remove most of the extra products. A test does not require buying several units.
  3. Go to checkout and enter any email address that uses the domain example.com. This domain is reserved for tests.
  4. Enter dummy shipping information. Dummy means fake.
  5. Click Complete order.
  6. Open the Shopify admin, go to Orders, and check that the test order appears.

  • In Shopify Admin > Orders, a test order shows payment pending as its payment status. This is because it is not a live order and receives no payment.

  • The fulfillment status (whether the order has been shipped) starts as unfulfilled.

  • The list also shows the sales channel (Online Store), the order amount and the shipping method (here, free shipping).

  • Before launching ad campaigns, place a full test order on your own store. Use your own credit card and go through the entire checkout.

  • Cancel the order after checking that every step works. The money returns to your account.

  • Test the payment flow on a new store.

    1. Place a real dummy order on your own store.
    2. Cancel the order or issue the refund in Shopify. A refund sends the money back.
    3. Confirm the refund arrives quickly, which one seller reported as within seconds.

  • Note on refund timing: says the money returns to the account after cancelling. reports one seller's refund arriving within seconds. Both describe the same test. The refund timing depends on the payment provider and is not guaranteed by either source.

  • create a $1 test product on the store. Add it to the cart and go through a complete checkout, on both desktop and mobile.

  • Then verify that the order confirmation appears and that the payment gateway (the service that moves the money) processes without errors.

  • This validates that checkout works before driving traffic.

    1. Create $1 test product on store.
    2. Add to cart and go through complete checkout.
    3. Test on both desktop and mobile devices.
    4. Verify order confirmation appears.
    5. Confirm payment gateway processes without errors.

  • When you create a test product to check the cart flow, turn off quantity tracking for it. Quantity tracking is the stock count that limits sales. This lets you add it to the cart from the storefront.

  • This setting is only for testing. It is not a recommended way to run a live product.

  • Test the checkout with a real sale before scaling ad spend. Scaling means spending more on ads. This shows that orders can be completed end to end, from first click to paid order.

  • Run the test before expecting any orders. A store that looks finished can still fail at checkout.

Compliance review

1 min
  • Before launch, check the store against the pre-launch compliance checklist. Compliance means following the rules.
  • A second reviewer can look at the same checklist on the live store to catch items that were missed.
  • Link all policy pages in the store footer before running any ads. This is for Google compliance, meaning Google's rules for ads and listings.
  • Legal pages must exist before traffic. These are the refund policy and shipping policy, with company name and email address.
  • Update the Legal section in Shopify Settings.
  • Make the Contact Us page list phone number, email and postal address.
  • Rewrite the policy pages for this business, not the theme defaults.
  • Link Privacy Policy, Terms of Service and Contact Us in the header (top menu) and footer.

Viewing the store as a customer

1 min
  • When you view your own store while logged in as admin (the store owner's account), Shopify displays a bar. The bar says the store is not live and checkout is disabled.
  • Use an incognito window (a private browser window that does not keep your login) or a different browser to see the customer view.
  • Shopify's bottom ribbon that reads Dropship appears only for admins logged into the Shopify admin while previewing the store.
  • To confirm what customers see, open the storefront in an incognito window or while logged out of Shopify.

Launch readiness check, in order:

  1. Confirm that at least two suppliers are onboarded and their products are uploaded to the store.

Family-member test, in order:

  1. Ask a family member or friend who has not seen the store to shop it as if buying one of your products.
  2. Sit beside them while they browse, and watch where they click.
  3. Ask what stands out, what is not working, and what is confusing.
  4. Thank them and make the changes they point out.
  • Launch when the store works and has no obvious red flags. Have a friend or family member test whether they would buy from it, and check that the cart and checkout work.

Checklist before sending traffic:

  1. Payment processor: at least one payment method that accepts credit cards, plus PayPal for top markets.

  4. Legal pages: refund policy and shipping policy exist, with company name and email address.
  5. Checkout page: trust badges (small security logos) and payment icons are added, and taxes are disabled where no tax is charged.

Traffic spikes and viral moments

1 min
  • Before a large influencer post (a post by a person with many followers) goes live, check that the store can handle a traffic spike. One brand saw more than 3,000 people shopping at once, compared with a normal load of 20 to 100 visitors.
  • A store that went viral (spread fast online) was shut down by Shopify (the software your store runs on) two to three days after the first viral sales, when order volume (how many orders come in) was large. Customers reported they could not buy. The seller noticed through comments. The store was restored within about one hour, but more than $5,000 in sales was estimated to be lost during that hour. Once orders reached tens of thousands, Shopify also asked for paperwork, such as proof of inventory (proof of the stock you hold).
  • Check the comments on your videos regularly. Customers writing that they cannot buy is an early warning of a store shutdown. Shopify may also restrict accounts (limit what you can do) when order volume is very high.
  • A broken link in a viral video or post sends traffic nowhere. It directly reduces conversion (the share of visitors who buy). Check the link in every post before and during a viral moment.
  • Shipping or checkout issues (checkout is the payment page) on the store page can cost sales, even when a video goes viral. Check the product page (the page for one item) and the checkout flow every time before you promote.
  • After you add the link to your bio (the link in your social profile), check it regularly. Confirm that the landing page (the first page a visitor sees) and product page load without errors, because an error can appear after the link is applied. Fix any errors in Shopify when they show up.

Zero-sales and add-to-cart diagnostics

2 min
  • Checking a product that gets add-to-carts (a shopper puts an item in the cart) but few sales:
  1. Check which payment methods are offered at checkout, including credit card and PayPal.
  2. Check that the checkout works without errors.
  3. Check the price point against the market.
  4. Check the website speed.
  5. If these are fine and sales are still low, the product is probably not good enough, so test a different product.
  • Checkout test for a store with zero sales after add-to-cart:
  1. Check the shipping setup in Shopify and confirm that shipping rates are correct.
  2. Check the market setup (the country settings) if you sell to several countries. Confirm that each target country is active.
  3. Try to check out with two random addresses in each target country, for example USA and Canada.
  4. If checkout fails for those addresses, fix the setting that is blocking customers.
  • Use this order when ad metrics (the numbers your ads report) are good but there are no sales, while the add-to-cart and checkout ratios are healthy:
  1. Check the shipping options and whether shipping is being charged to the customer.
  2. Check the markets. Make sure every country you run ads to is enabled in Shopify Markets (the settings for selling to other countries, at Settings > Markets). Customers from a country that is not activated cannot choose it at checkout, so they abandon the cart.
  3. Check that the payment providers (the companies that process payments) work. Create a 95% discount code for yourself, then test a payment through PayPal, Shopify Payments or Stripe, whichever you use.
  4. If a payment provider fails the test, uninstall it and install it again. This fixes setup problems in many cases.
  • A common cause of zero sales with healthy funnel numbers (the steps from visit to sale) is a targeted country (a country your ads aim at) that is not activated in Shopify Markets. Then checkout cannot offer shipping to that customer. Check the country list against the countries in your ad targeting.
  • Review the drop-off rate (the share of visitors who leave at each step) in analytics (your traffic reports) to find the bottleneck (the step that blocks the most sales). Check in this order:
  1. Check the homepage.
  2. Check the collection page (a page that lists a group of products).
  3. Check the product page.
  4. Check the cart.
  5. Check checkout.
  • A high bounce rate (the share of visitors who leave straight away), for example 87 percent, is normal for ad traffic landing on product pages. Use Lucky Orange (a tool that records how visitors use your pages) screen recordings to see why visitors leave, then fix what causes it.
  • Diagnosis order when funnel numbers are weak: the offer (what you sell and at what price), the theme (the store's design template), and checkout are the first suspects, as stated in the conversion section.

Post-launch maintenance

1 min
  • Keep updating the store as you learn. Change the header and footer (the top and bottom bars on every page) and apps (add-on tools), and optimize the product page as more visitors arrive. A store needs ongoing maintenance, not a single build.
  • Check the store on mobile (phones) at every stage of editing. Mobile remains the main traffic source after launch.
  • Keep checking store links and product pages after launch, especially after changes to the bio link or the header and footer.
  • Re-check the store's speed score (a rating of how fast the store loads) once the store is public. The score becomes available 1 to 3 days after protection (the password lock) is disabled. A B grade is not a reason to pause sales.
  • A new Shopify store is password protected (locked with a password) by default. Visitors who are not logged in see a coming-soon page (a placeholder page) that asks for a password, instead of the storefront (the public shop).

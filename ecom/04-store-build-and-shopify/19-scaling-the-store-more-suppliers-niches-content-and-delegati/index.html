Playbook › 4. Store build and Shopify

Scaling the store: more suppliers, niches, content and delegation

Section 4.19 · 19 of 20 in this chapter · 35 min read

In short

  • Get approved by one or two suppliers first, then add more brands in stages.
  • Aim for 100 different products before you run Google ads.
  • Test one product at a time on one store under one domain (web address). Move a winner to its own store.
  • Study competitors and submit that research before you build the site.
  • Do not spend big on a store before sales prove that people want the product.
More key points
  • Blog posts are optional. Skip them at launch and add them later, once paid ads bring in profit.
  • You can build the store yourself or pay a team to build it. Either way, the store must be ready for supplier approval (the product maker agrees to ship orders for you).
  • Done-for-you builds often take a few business days. A team builds the store for you. Queues can make it take longer, and the first order sent in is the first one built.
  • Never let an AI tool write straight into the live store. Check its work outside Shopify first.

Adding suppliers and products in stages

1 min

Supplier approval and catalogue build

2 min

A supplier is the company that stocks and ships products for you. A catalogue is your full list of products.

  1. Get approved with the first one or two brands.
  2. Upload every product from those brands, using CSV files (simple spreadsheet files that upload many products at once) for speed.
  3. Upload the next brands only after the first ones are complete.
  4. Aim for at least 100 different products before running Google ads (paid ads on Google).
  • A brand can start as a parked Shopify store with no products. Shopify is software for running online shops. "Parked" means set up but not open to shoppers yet.
  • Launch products later, so audience reactions can guide what to sell.
  • The store can go live (open to shoppers) before every product is uploaded. Advertising can start then too.
  • In markets with many products, upload a first set, launch, and add more after launch.
  • Keep adding quality supplier products while adjusting ads, even if the store is not yet profitable.
  • Find a supplier for the product (this is called sourcing) and confirm they can ship it before you run any ad.
  • Never start an ad for a product you cannot fulfil (ship and deliver to the customer).
  • Look for missing variants (versions of a product, such as colours and sizes) on competitor sites. Competitors are other shops selling the same thing.
  • Examples of missing variants: light and dark wood finishes, or new colours.
  • Upload all available variants so shoppers who want a specific option choose your store.
  • Oberlo (an app that imports products) and AliExpress (a large online marketplace) are named as sources for product supply and price markup. Markup is a price increase added on top of the supplier's cost.
  • Replace AliExpress or other Chinese products on an existing store with US suppliers only if the store's niche fits the niche criteria (the rules for choosing a niche) (2023).
  • A native Shopify supplier integration (a built-in connection to a supplier, made by Shopify) is a win.
  • External enterprise-level apps (large business-grade add-ons made by other companies) are not recommended for a new store (2024).
  • Adding related products to an existing store is fine when these are already connected: analytics (tools that track visitors), Facebook pages, Google Merchant Center (Google's account for product listings) and Google Ads.
  • Do not recreate these connections for a new store.

Product count: sources differ by purpose

Situation Product count Source

Multi-product store running Google ads At least 100 different products
Multi-product store target At least 20 brands and 100 products; more variety drives more traffic and steadier sales than a one-product store

  • A store with no revenue (money from sales) has almost no resale value.
  • Test a few products with a small budget before building a full catalogue.

Testing niches and products in one general store

1 min

Start small and expand on proof

1 min
  1. Start a new product test with one product and a product page (a page for one item).
  2. Once sales show the audience buys, move to a collection page (a page showing several items).
  3. On the collection page, several items increase cross-sales (selling extra items with the main one) and average order value (the average amount spent per order).
  • A general store (one shop selling many kinds of products) can be used for testing with one product page.
  • Building a community and a brand requires a niche store (a shop focused on one narrow market). Move to a niche store once a product is proven.
  • Start with a general store under a brandable random domain (a made-up web address that is easy to remember as a brand), not a single-product store.
  • Then test products one by one.
  • Focus effort on landing pages (single pages built to win one sale) with a strong offer and trust elements.
  • During product testing, a simple store is enough to collect data.
  • Invest in a polished design, stronger branding and a product page builder (a tool for making pages without coding) only after one product shows clear winning numbers.
  • A beginner is better off building one reusable general store personally.
  • Paying a freelancer to build a product page for every test product becomes expensive.
  • Skills carry over to every later test.

One domain, one general store for many tests

1 min
  • Run one brand-style, generic domain name. It should be under 10 characters and easy to say and remember.
  • Examples: made-up names checked with Namelix (a tool that suggests name ideas).
  • Test products by adding a new product page, not a new store.
  • The home page needs only 2-3 sections: a product banner, the product section, and trust badges (small logos that show reassurance) such as priority shipping and a money-back guarantee.
  • Keep one general store domain for all products tested in the same broad category, such as shoes.
  • Buy a new domain and brand only when a proven product moves to its own store or a different market.
  • Using one domain for all tests avoids the cost and friction (hassle) of buying new stores and domains.
  • A single-product focus with a strong product page still builds a brand identity.
  • In a new store, test one product at a time instead of loading dozens of products.
  • Each product gets its own focused page.
  • Every new Shopify store requires its own home page, legal pages, notification settings (automatic emails) and checkout setup (the payment steps).
  • This is the main reason to keep testing products on one domain and only add a new product page.
  • Keeping a single store and swapping products is the cheaper option for early testing.
  • Rebuilding under a new domain costs money and means losing the free trial.
  • To test many niches without paying for a new Shopify store each time, use one general store for all tests.
  • When a winner appears, change the logo and name and keep the store setup, since opening a new Shopify store means paying again.

Product testing flow under one domain

1 min
  1. Build one generic homepage.
  2. Keep the About us, Contact, Track my order, FAQ (frequently asked questions) and all policy pages the same across the store.
  3. Create one product page template (a master page design) and duplicate it for each product, so every tested product has its own URL (the web address of one page). Five products, five URLs.
  4. On the homepage, add a Shop now menu with a dropdown (a list that opens under a menu item) that lists every product.
  5. On the product pages, remove or hide the top navigation (the menu bar at the top) so the page reads like a single landing page.
  6. Launch one product in a single day.
  7. Check performance after 3 to 4 days.
  8. If it fails, create a new product page in about two hours and launch it within two hours.
  9. When a product wins, either turn the store into a niche store or devote a store to the winner.
  10. Keep testing new products under a separate domain.

Single-domain launch with unpublished backups

1 min
  1. Buy one brandable domain with no meaning, under 8-10 characters.
  2. Generate ideas with Namelix or ChatGPT (an AI chat tool). If needed, combine two words into a new name.
  3. Build one high-converting product page (a page likely to turn visitors into buyers) for the first product.
  4. Use a page builder such as PageFly or GemPages (page-building apps).
  5. Build the homepage with one banner that has the store name, a benefit-driven claim (a statement about what the customer gains), and an image.
  6. Under the banner, add a short section about the store and why the product helps.
  7. Then show the product.
  8. Prepare pages for the next products, but keep them unpublished (built but hidden).
  9. After about three days, if the product does not work, publish the next product page under the same domain.
  • Keep unpublished product pages ready while the first product is tested.
  • Judge a product after about three days of ad data (the numbers from your ads) before deciding whether to switch.
  • Judge timing differs between sources: about three days (above) or three to four days (the flow above).
  • Use three to four days for a full read when running the step-by-step flow.

Winner-first store structure

1 min
  1. Start with a general store that carries several test products at once.
  2. Once one product clearly wins, rename the store to the niche. Example: from caricia to caricia shoes.
  3. Keep the same domain name when renaming the store.
  4. Remove the other products and leave only the winning product line.

Store structure approach

1 min
  1. Start with a general store or a niche general store containing several products.
  2. Send ads to a collection page and let customers choose.
  3. Identify the best-selling product.
  4. Add a new theme focused on that product, optionally with a new domain and logo.
  5. Keep the existing Shopify store and pixel.

  • A general store can hold several products tested together.

  • If a product wins, either convert the store into a single-product store or add a new theme focused on the winner.

  • Do this rather than building a new Shopify store, so the existing pixel keeps its sales data.

  • The usual setup is one product per store.

  • Or use one general store to test products, then move winners into their own store.

  • Setting up a new store for a product can be quick, around three hours, if the design is reused.

  • For a fast setup for product testing, use these steps:

    1. Use the Shrine theme, which has an inbuilt quantity discount (a discount for buying more).
    2. Name the store with a general female first name rather than a product name, so the same store can test many products.
    3. After sourcing a product, add it to the store and make the product page live.

  • When testing many products on TikTok (a short-video app), a store can be set up as a general deals-style store with a domain such as onlinedeals.com.

  • Use this instead of one store per product.

  • Testing many products this way needs a lot of content.

  • Use a store that can sell a product line well. Build it around the product's season or niche.

  • Expand with 5 to 10 new products per month to raise the store's product range.

Niche rules

2 min
  • Run each store around one niche, with multiple categories inside it.
  • Example: a standing desk store can have categories for bamboo, whiteboard and L-shaped desks.
  • Keep unrelated niches in separate stores.
  • Single-niche stores tend to have higher conversion rates (the share of visitors who buy), since the buyer experience is focused on one product type.
  • Use niche-specific stores rather than one broad store covering many categories.
  • A niche store can be built even when competitors sell a wide range of categories.
  • A single store should cover one niche only.
  • Build a separate demo store for each new niche, even if you already run other stores.
  • Mention your existing experience to suppliers, and say the new store launches on a specific date (2023).
  • When products do not fit the theme of the first store, open a second store for them instead of forcing them in.
  • The second store reportedly performed well within about three months (older, 2020 is not stated; source undated).
  • Do not create several identical clone stores (copies) with the same name, address and phone number to split a niche.
  • This caused Google Merchant Center suspensions (account blocks) for one operator.
  • Instead, after one sub-niche sells best in the main store, create a new store containing only those products.
  • To turn an existing low-ticket store (cheap products) into a high-ticket store (expensive products), keep the same Shopify account, payment setup and categories when the niche is related.
  • Replace the main products with more expensive items. Update the logo if needed. Link a new domain in the Shopify admin (the Shopify control panel).

Reusing a store when the niche is wrong

  1. Change the store name in the Shopify backend (the back office where you manage the shop).
  2. Replace the logo.
  3. Replace all custom images that carry the old branding.
  4. Check that the colour scheme still fits the new niche.
  • The Shopify account can stay as it is.
  • This is easier than starting from scratch.
  • If the niche of a Shopify store turns out to be wrong, the store can be reused.
  • Swap the products and connect the store to a different domain name in the Shopify settings.
  • This avoids building a new store from scratch.
  • An existing Shopify store can be completely rebuilt from scratch, including the domain name, theme, code (the instructions that build the site) and all products.

Additional stores and domains

Costs of extra stores

1 min
  • Each separate Shopify store costs about $30.
  • Run several stores inside one account with separate themes instead of opening new accounts.
  • Keep the old theme as a saved version.
  • Seasonal stores, such as a Christmas store, can be republished later with the same logo and domain setup.
  • Old store versions remain stored as themes, so you can switch back and forth.

Several stores on one account (procedure)

1 min
  1. Create each new store with the same email address already used for the account.
  2. Go to shopify.com > Log in with email and click the green button "Create another store".
  3. All stores owned by that email then appear in a list in the account, for example on the left side.

Duplicating a store

1 min
  1. Create the new Shopify store with another email.
  2. In the old store, go to Online Store > Themes, download the theme and export it.
  3. Import the theme into the new store.
  4. Export the product and customer data and import it into the new store.
  5. Export the email and SMS lists (for example from Klaviyo, an email tool, or SMS Bump, a text message app) and import them into the new store.
  6. Rebuild any settings that do not transfer, such as logo and notification settings.

Copying competitor structures

1 min

Finding and comparing competitors

1 min
  • Koala Inspector, a browser extension (a small add-on for your web browser), shows which apps, themes, and upselling or cross-selling techniques competitor Shopify stores use. Upselling means offering a more expensive version.
  • It also shows their best-selling products. This reveals the sales tactics that work in your niche.
  • CopyFile is a French-language store analysis tool.
  • It shows a store's theme, lets you export its code and images, tracks daily revenue and a 30-day forecast, shows best sellers and recent orders, and looks up advertising networks.
  • The source reports that its revenue numbers matched the author's own store.
  • CopyFile costs 77 euros per month.
  • A collection URL (the web address of a product list) with the best-selling sort order can reveal the best-selling products of any Shopify store.
  • Append the path collections/all?sort_by=best-selling to the store domain.
  • This works only on Shopify stores.
  • To compare a product across stores, search for the supplier's SKU (the supplier's product code) first.
  • If it does not show up, search by the product name and check whether the product exists on the other site.
  • To check whether two stores have the same owner:
    1. Open the Contact Us page or the Terms of Service page of each store.
    2. Note the email address shown on each page.
    3. If the emails are identical, treat it as the final confirmation that the same people run both stores.

Documenting differences (2023)

1 min
  • When analysing competitor stores, write down specific differences.
  • Write down: product descriptions (length, detail level, technical specs), features or benefits highlighted, warranty or guarantee mentions, shipping or return policies, pricing structure, page layout, customer testimonials, and social proof elements.
  • This documentation guides what improvements to make on your own store.

Source D method: find, copy, adapt

1 min
  1. Find the product and list five to ten other stores that sell it.
  2. Open each store and pick the parts that look proven: offer, photos, FAQ, reviews, testimonials and page sections.
  3. Rebuild the offer on your store with the Coaching Bundles app from the App Store (the Shopify app store).
  4. Copy the photos, FAQ block, reviews and testimonials that fit your product.
  5. Copy the section layouts: video carousel (a slide strip of videos), before-and-after block, timeline block and testimonial slider.
  6. Adapt everything to your own theme and add your own brand name to copied images.
  7. Launch the test store within about two days of finding the product.
  8. Once the test produces sales, customise the landing page so it looks unique.

Rules for copying

1 min
  • Copy only small parts from many competitor sites instead of large parts from one site.
  • A store that mirrors a single competitor looks like a copy and loses credibility.
  • Many competitor stores start with a basic design.
  • They add more polished elements once daily sales are high.
  • One observed store doing over $10,000 per day had added these elements after its early phase.
  • Copying the layout of a proven competitor store is a faster starting point than designing from scratch.
  • It lets you start testing sooner. A store selling the same product has already been shaped by what works in that niche.
  • Building a store from scratch takes a few hours, or almost half a day, for an experienced builder.
  • Budget that time per store when planning pre-launch work.
  • Model your site on at least two to three competitors, borrowing their photos, text and layout structure.
  • Then take the best elements from each and combine them into your own design, which is expected to outperform any single competitor.
  • Beginners in dropshipping (selling products that a supplier ships for you) often do not know what a winning store looks like.
  • Studying proven stores makes building far easier.
  • Modelling your store on existing successful stores avoids starting from zero.

Competitor research before the build

1 min
  1. Choose the product and confirm it through product research.
  2. Find three to five competitor stores that sell the same product and list them in the product research sheet.
  3. Take a screenshot of each competitor's homepage.
  4. Submit the competitor list and screenshots before starting to build your own site.
  5. If fewer than three competitors exist, tell the program team instead of stopping the process.
  6. Build the site only after this research is complete and submitted.
  • Competitor homepage screenshots must be made and shown before the store is built, not after launch.
  • Some programs refuse to approve a site built without this prior competitor research.
  • Building a store from scratch without studying competitors often produces weak quality.
  • Fixing a poor site after launch can take about a week of revision and is harder than getting it right the first time.
  • Building the site first and sending competitor research afterwards wastes time, because a poor build then needs a full week of revision.
  • Competitor research should always come before the build starts.
  • The source range for competitors differs: five to ten stores to browse for proven parts (above), three to five to submit in the research sheet (above).
  • Use five to ten as your browsing pool and three to five as the list you submit.

Store for a proven product

1 min
  1. Find a winning brand in your niche with long-running ads in the Meta Ad Library (the public list of Facebook and Instagram ads).
  2. Study its product offer and page structure as a reference.
  3. Use the ChatGPT prompt to rewrite its copy for your product and target avatar (your ideal customer).
  4. Paste the rewritten text into your store.
  5. Add a few product images and short, clear texts, and keep the design clean.
  6. Add testimonials, an FAQ section and custom reviews.
  • A proven layout copied from another successful store is a far cheaper starting point than hiring a web developer for around $30,000 USD to build the whole store.
  • A whole store like this does not need that.
  • A store-build service can include design, uploading demo products and content pages, and setting up Google Analytics and Google webmaster tools (Google's tools for search visibility).
  • It does not include getting approved with suppliers, which the owner must still do.

Blog and content

1 min

What counts as content and when to start

2 min
  • Content in this framework means anything on a site that is not a product page or an admin page (the back-office pages only you see). Examples are blog posts, infographics, YouTube videos, images, category or collection pages (pages that group products), eBooks, checklists and user-generated content (posts written by customers). Product pages alone cannot fill the gap in the customer journey (the path a buyer takes from first visit to purchase).
  • Creating blog posts is optional and not required before supplier approval.
  • Blog content is not needed from day one. Add it only after paid traffic (visitors you pay for with ads) performs well and makes a profit.
  • Blog content does not bring early sales. Do not copy other sites' articles. If you need content, have it written on freelance platforms (websites where you hire independent workers) such as Upwork or Fiverr, or buy PLR (private label rights) content, which is ready-made writing you buy.
  • In the first stage, focus on the store itself, conversion optimisation (changes that turn more visitors into buyers) and paid ads. Content and blog posts come later, and a test site does not need blog posts.
  • Blog pages and blog posts should not be built during the initial site design. Blogging starts only after launch, because it wastes time before the business goes live.
  • The blog can be a stated preference on the same domain (your web address). Run it on the same website as the store, for example on WordPress (a website builder) with WooCommerce (a shop add-on), so readers move between articles and products without leaving the domain. This is a stated preference rather than a proven requirement.
  • Use the native Shopify blog feature. Shopify is a platform for building online stores, and its built-in blog is the native option. SEO (search engine optimisation, getting found in Google for free) results depend on the theme (the store's design template), post optimisation and plugin speed (how fast add-ons load), not on the platform.

Blog structure and admin

2 min

  • Create one blog per store. Shopify allows multiple blogs, but the standard practice is a single blog per store.

  • Blog post creation in Shopify:

    • Navigation: Admin Panel > Online Store > Blog Posts
    • To create a post: click "Create Blog Post"
    • Fields:
      • Title: the blog post headline (example: "Top Stand-Up Paddle Boards, Top Surfboards for 2024")
      • Document editor: the writing area where you add text, images and videos
      • Excerpt: a short summary shown on the homepage or the main blog page
      • Featured Image: the picture shown at the top of the post
      • Author name: the writer's name; it can belong to a ghostwriter (a hired writer who stays unnamed)
      • Blog: which blog the post belongs to
      • Blog post template: the page layout (the design pattern of the page); use the default (do not change)
      • Comments: switch reader comments on or off
      • Search Engine Preview: editable SEO metadata (the title and description Google shows in search results)

  • Shopify Magic is a built-in AI helper (a tool that writes or edits text for you), marked by a star icon in the blog editor. Use it to rephrase, extend or simplify blog post content.

  • Shopify automatically creates one default blog category named 'news' when the store is set up. A category is a group for sorting posts. All blog posts go to this category by default unless more categories are created.

  • To find and change a blog post's category, open the post in your admin panel (the back office where you manage the store), scroll to the Organization section, and select or change the blog category. Blog categories are created by clicking 'create a new blog' in the Organization section.

  • To change the blog author name, go to Online Store > Blog posts, open the post, then in the right sidebar under Organization change the Author field (2023).

  • Hidden blog posts do not appear in the blog listing of the theme (the list of posts on the blog page). Each post must be set to visible before it shows up in the listing.

  • To create an editorial (a planned article), add a new article in the blog under Online Store > Blog posts, or under Online Store > Pages for a standalone page. Page builder apps such as PageFly or GemPages (add-ons that let you design pages by placing blocks, without coding) can be used to design the layout.

  • Shopify supports both the blog and the pages function for topical authority articles (articles that show Google you know a subject well by covering it in depth), so either can be used for that content. Collection pages are created as separate category pages in the store.

  • Shopify has a built-in blog function, so holiday blog posts can be created directly in the Shopify admin without extra apps (add-on tools).

  • Blogs are built into Shopify. Create one from the Shopify admin by clicking create blog and then adding posts. This is the simplest place to publish SEO content for seasonal promotions (2025).

Help and AI tools for content work

1 min
  • Use Shopify live chat (a chat with Shopify support) for platform-specific setup questions instead of guessing. Ask exactly what you want to do so the answer is correct the first time.
  • For Shopify setup steps, follow the official Shopify help documentation, because it is kept up to date. The admin AI assistant (Shopify's own AI helper) is trained on Shopify's help center and can troubleshoot a step you are stuck on (2024).
  • Never let an AI tool write directly into the live Shopify store (the store customers can see right now). Do the QA (quality checks) outside Shopify first, and only then apply the changes in bulk (all at once).
  • When using custom rules with Shopify and a Claude MCP connection (a link that lets Claude, an AI tool, read and act on your store data), set the rules for internal links (links from one page of your site to another) explicitly. Then have the connection apply only what those rules allow. Keep a manual review step before the rules are executed.

Seasonal setup

1 min

Holiday and seasonal stores

1 min
  1. Have a store template (a ready design you reuse) and product catalogue (your product list) ready before Q4 (October to December, the last three months of the year) begins.
  2. Add multiple product categories (groups of products): Black Friday, Christmas, Valentine's Day and evergreen (products that sell all year) to one store.
  3. Run different ad campaigns (sets of ads with one goal) and creative variations (different ad images or videos) per product, but fulfil all orders through the same store to centralise inventory (stock) and customer data in one place.
  • Create a seasonal section and header message (the banner text at the top of the store) before a holiday, such as a Mother's Day gifts section with a note that gifts arrive in time for May 13. Make it visible early enough for buyers to order and receive the gift before the date.
  • A holiday gift store should look built for the occasion. Use a brand name, logo, colours and wording tied to the holiday, and list only a few products. Shoppers who worry about choosing the wrong gift buy more confidently in a store that looks designed for that purpose.
  • Keep old store versions, such as a seasonal Christmas store, as saved themes (stored copies of a store design), and republish them later with the same logo and domain setup.

Store build or outsourcing

1 min

Two paths and what each delivers

1 min
  • A store can be built in two ways: do it yourself by installing the provided theme and modifying it, or outsource the design to a specialised web design team for dropshipping (selling products that a supplier ships straight to the buyer). Both paths lead to a store ready for supplier approval.
  • You can build your demo store (a test store) yourself, or outsource the entire build to the Source A Done-For-You team (a team that builds the finished store for you) to receive a finished, supplier-ready store. You keep 100% ownership either way.
  • Outsourcing store design is an acceptable choice, while the owner keeps control of supplier and traffic decisions. Many owners build their first store themselves and outsource design once the business earns money.
  • Outsourcing the Shopify store build to a paid setup service is optional and costs extra on top of any training or programme fee. The seller can always build the store personally instead.
  • A done-for-you store build means a team builds a store from a proven design after checking the niche (the product market you sell to). Build quality is guaranteed, but revenue or a fixed return is not. An established profitable store sells at a multiple (a number times) of its monthly net profit (profit after all costs), not at the build price.
  • A custom dropshipping store built by an outsourced web design team is typically delivered in about five business days (working days, not weekends), so the builder does not have to go through the store build steps themselves.

What a done-for-you design changes (2026)

1 min
  • A done-for-you design swaps the theme only. Existing products, brands, pages and customisations are kept. Policy pages (legal pages such as privacy and returns) are updated to meet Google compliance (following Google's rules).
  • Done-for-you store packages can differ. Some do not include Google account setup (creating the Google login used for ads), so check what a package includes before buying.
  • When a done-for-you design service rebuilds an existing Shopify store, the designer works through a collaborator code (a code that gives the designer access as a team member) in draft mode (changes saved but not shown to customers). The current store is not deleted, and no new Shopify account is created. Existing products, brands, pages and customisations carry over, and only the theme and policy pages change.

Queue and timing rules

1 min
  • Web design orders are queued first come, first served, based on when the client submits the store information form, not when they purchase. (2023)
  • A store cannot be rebuilt for a different niche once the web design has been built from scratch. A new niche would require a second store, which is effectively a second job. Small tweaks are still possible (2023).
  • Delay the store build until content shows some traction (growing attention), such as a few thousand views, since a store built before any demand can waste about 10 hours. Build the store alongside content (at the same time) only if you have no web-building experience.
  • A beginner's full build: learn first, then build one reusable store personally (procedure):
    1. Build the store yourself rather than paying a freelancer for each product.
    2. Use the free store-building guide from the Refresh team.
    3. Follow the guide on building a branded product page with PageFly, and build it at the same time.
    4. Use the free Paper Loop template, a website template (a ready page design) from the Website Building source.
    5. Create a landing page (a single page built to get one action, such as a purchase) with PageFly, which is free.
    6. Run ads to the store.

Build yourself first, then delegate

1 min
  • Learn to build a Shopify store yourself first, including theme, colour scheme (the set of colours used), branding (logo, colours and style), copywriting (the written words on the site) and apps (add-on tools). This way you know which design gets the best results. Only then delegate the build (give the job to someone else), giving the person your exact structure.
  • Learning Shopify store building is a lasting skill, not only for your current dropshipping business. Owning it lets you judge a store build and decide what to change in the theme, branding and copy.
  • A delegated store build can save a day or two of work. That time can be used to prepare creatives (ad images and videos) and set up Facebook or TikTok ad campaigns.
  • A delegated store setup can include the theme, logos and branding, the copy, the apps you specify, and the payment providers (services that take customers' card payments), so the store is ready to run ads.
  • The best case is a team member who already has a product they want to test. Give them your store structure, the apps to install and the branding requirements, so they can set the store up for ads.
  • Delegation steps:
    1. Build the store yourself at least once to learn which theme, design, branding and apps work for you.
    2. Give the team member a product that you want to test.
    3. Tell them to build the store following your structure.
    4. Have them create the logos and branding, following your guidelines.
    5. Tell them which apps to install and how to configure them, especially Klaviyo (an email marketing app) and SMS Bump (an app for text message marketing).
    6. Once trust is established, have them install the payment providers so the store is up and running.

Freelancers and task services

1 min
  • Hiring developers or designers for store builds can be done through freelancers (independent workers you hire per job) who specialise in Shopify. Developer's skill matters more than theme knowledge, since HTML (the basic code that builds web pages) skills transfer across themes and platforms.
  • The course's store design and VA (virtual assistant, a remote helper) sourcing is done via Upwork or Taskrabbit, with Source A support for small jobs. No internal VA list is provided.
  • Every few months, have a freelancer audit the store for broken pages, page speed problems and oversized images that should be compressed (made smaller). Use this as a project-based task rather than a permanent staff position.
  • For most Shopify store tasks, spend about 30 minutes using Shopify support documentation and search before paying for help. Hire a Shopify developer only for custom coding beyond that (2024).

B2B and trade pages

1 min

Why a trade page

1 min
  • For business-to-business buyers (B2B, companies that buy from you), such as interior designers, a separate trade page with its own contact form and a list of trade buyers' logos builds trust and repeat orders. Logos can be added as the list of past trade customers grows (2024).
  • High-ticket industrial products (expensive products for factories and businesses) can be offered to business buyers on a separate page or collection named To The Trade. The page should describe business-to-business sales, list the industrial products, and include a contact form so buyers can request quotes for large orders before buying (2022).

Adding a trade page

1 min
  1. Create a page in Shopify named to the trade.
  2. State that bulk orders (large orders) and trade discounts are available.
  3. Add a contact form where buyers can request a custom quote (a price made for their order).
  • No other change to the marketing approach is needed.

Adding a trade page (2025)

1 min
  1. Create a page that duplicates the contact page, including the contact form.
  2. Replace the top heading with To the Trade, and add trade copy describing bulk discounts (lower prices for larger quantities), custom quotes and expedited shipping (faster delivery, usually for a fee).
  3. Link the page in the footer menu (the links at the bottom of every page), and sometimes in the header menu (the links at the top).
  4. Once business orders arrive, add a list of businesses you have sold to, to show credibility (trust).

Contact form and wholesale accounts

1 min
  • On a "To the Trade" page, use a contact form instead of a separate order form, with these fields: name, email, phone number, business name, product of interest and number of units (how many items). Mention that bulk or quantity discounts are available on the page.
  • Create B2B wholesale accounts (accounts that buy at wholesale, meaning low prices) with a Shopify app that assigns account-level pricing (a set price for each customer account). Logged-in trade customers then see lower prices and can add discounted items to their cart.

Free learning resources and reference tools

1 min
  • Free learning resources for web development (building websites) include the YouTube channels Web Dev Simplified and Fireship, plus free online programming courses from Google. Books are also recommended for learners who prefer reading.
  • Stack Overflow (a question-and-answer site for programmers) is useful to find fixes to specific technical problems, though its answers are very technical for beginners. Reddit (an online discussion forum) can be used to ask which developers people recommend, but beginners should expect blunt replies.
  • The Shopify Help Center at help.shopify.com (Shopify's official help website) documents adding products, product details, variants (versions of one product, such as sizes or colours), product media (photos and videos of products), collections (product groups), and importing and exporting (moving product lists in and out as files). Use it to check small questions about fields or variants.
  • Build an operations resource list (a list of guides for daily tasks) for each team member by collecting the Shopify Help Center guides for these tasks: creating collections, creating orders, and creating discounts. Add the links to the corporate board (the company's shared internal page) so staff can learn on their own.

Task-based services for small jobs

1 min
  • Task Husky is a marketplace (a website where you hire people for jobs) for small, one-off site jobs, such as installing a widget (a small add-on box) on a product page. Prices start from about US$39 per task, which is far cheaper than hiring an agency (a company that does projects for clients) for a full site relaunch (rebuilding the whole site).
  • Freelance marketplaces such as Upwork often focus on full site relaunches, so they can be a poor fit for minor updates. Use task-based services for small refreshes and check the price before committing.

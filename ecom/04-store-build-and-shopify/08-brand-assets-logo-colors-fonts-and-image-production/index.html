Brand assets: logo, colors, fonts and image production

Section 4.8 · 8 of 20 in this chapter · 30 min read

In short

  • Check your theme's image sizes before you design anything. Every theme needs its own sizes.
  • Save logos with a transparent background (no coloured box behind the image), as SVG if possible, so they look clean on any colour.
  • Set your logo and favicon in Theme settings, then check the mobile preview.
  • Use 2 to 4 brand colours. Too many colours or fonts make the store look messy.
  • Each collection gets one image, shared by its collection page and the homepage.
More key points
  • Under 300 KB is the target file size for product photos. Under 50 KB is better. KB means kilobytes, a unit of file size.
  • Make product photos square at 1000 × 1000 px. Sources disagree on the exact size, so check your platform. px means pixels.
  • Compress every image before upload. Use JPEG for photos. Use PNG only when the background must be see-through.
  • Keep product photo backgrounds white, and put no text on them unless the text is on the product itself.

Setup order and file preparation

1 min
  1. Before you design anything, check your theme's image sizes. A theme is the design template your store runs on. Each theme needs its own graphic sizes, and a file made for one theme may not fit another.
  2. Designing without checking the theme's sizes gives files that crop (get cut off at the edges), stretch or do not fit.
  3. Look up the sizes in the theme backend (the back office where you edit your store's design). Then resize your images to match before you upload. Oversized images can push section titles (the headings of each part of a page) down the page.
  4. The theme is called "Manhattan" in some places and "Source A" in others. The size figures below come from both names. Confirm they match your theme before you use them.
  5. Upload all brand images at once before you edit the theme. Shopify is the online store platform you use. Go to Shopify admin (the back office of your Shopify store) > Content > Files > Upload files. Uploaded files are not linked to any section automatically. You can pick them later in the theme editor without uploading again.
  6. For bulk uploads, use the Files section to upload many images at once. Large uploads can run in the background while you keep working. Image links can also go into the product CSV (a spreadsheet file of your products) instead.
  7. Write down the download folder and file name as soon as you save a file. Some computers save to Downloads and others to the desktop.
  8. Shopify and Canva (an online design tool) both ask for files, so keep the Canva originals. Canva stores finished designs under All designs. You can download them again without rebuilding.
  9. Store graphics change with sales, holidays and new collections (groups of products). Refresh them regularly, and keep the Canva files so the set can be updated quickly.

Theme setup order

1 min
  1. Open Online Store > Themes, click Customize, and go to Theme settings > Logos.
  2. Upload the main logo, then the mobile logo.
  3. Check the logo in the mobile preview before you continue. A mobile preview shows how the store looks on a phone. About 90% of store traffic (visitors) is typically on phones.
  4. Go to Theme settings > Colors and paste the brand hex codes (short colour codes made of letters and numbers, such as #6D388B) into the existing colour fields.
  5. Go to Typography, upload the font file, and paste its link into the font field. A font is a set of letter shapes.
  6. In Logos, select the favicon and upload the favicon file made from the logo.
  7. Save after each step and refresh the preview.

Canva work

2 min

Free plan limits

  • On the free plan, the Transparent background option in the download menu is greyed out. A PNG (a picture file made of pixels) downloads with a white background. Transparent PNGs need the paid plan.
  • The white PNG shows as a white box around the logo on any coloured section of the site. Remove the background before uploading, or use a paid plan with transparent download.
  • An existing template cannot be resized on the free plan. To get a different banner shape, create a new design.

Working in Canva

  • Start from a template for each type of image, then change the text, colours and images to fit your brand. Template links are faster than building from scratch.
  • Text: select the text and use the text colour (A) icon to choose a brand colour. Use the highlight options for font, bold, letter spacing (space between letters) and line spacing (space between lines).
  • Centring: select several elements, open the three-dot menu, and choose Position > Center.
  • To use a photo as a banner background: search the Elements panel under Photos, drag the photo onto the design, right-click it (or two-finger click on a trackpad), choose Set as background, then move the image to the right position.
  • Once a photo is a background, you can move it inside the frame to position the subject. For example, lower it so a person's head is not cut off at the top.
  • Supplier product photos (photos from the company that makes or ships your products) can be uploaded to Canva the same way as stock photos. This makes all images in a set crop to the same size.
  • Canva's built-in Photos library has many free images for popular niches (product types or markets), such as surfing. For niches with few usable stock images, such as basic furniture or mechanical products, download photos from Pexels (a free photo website), upload them to Canva, and use them like built-in photos.
  • For a website banner, search the Canva templates for "website banner" and use that size as the starting format. Check the result on mobile first, then adjust the desktop version.
  • Canva is enough to create a simple logo and to edit ebook covers and branding at the start, without a designer.

Ebook assets (tools)

  • Use Canva's ebook template: search "ebook", choose a cover, type the title and author name, and export it for the store.
  • Placeit creates 3D book mockups (pictures that show your cover as a real-looking book). Upload the Canva cover, set the book colour to match (for example black), and download the mockup. This needs a subscription (a monthly or yearly payment).
  • Pexels.com provides free stock photos (ready-made photos you can use) for ebook covers. All images are free except those from iStock, which are paid.

Logo and favicon formats

2 min

Logo formats

  • Shopify accepts SVG (a logo file made of shapes, not pixels) for logos. Upload the SVG to the logo settings rather than a pixelated image (one that looks blocky when enlarged).
  • PageFly (a page-building app) also accepts SVG uploads for logos and icons.
  • SVG can be enlarged up to ten times without losing sharpness, because it is built from mathematical shapes and angles.
  • PNG is pixel-based and loses quality when enlarged. Use PNG as the fallback logo format where the platform does not accept SVG.
  • Export logos with a transparent background, so they work on white, dark and dark-mode websites (sites that show dark colours) without a visible box.
  • Upload PNGs with transparent backgrounds to PageFly, so the logo sits on any section colour without a white box.
  • When a logo goes in a header (the strip across the top of each page), export only the logo selection with no extra page margin, so the header height is not affected.
  • Logo for the Source A theme: 800 × 300 px, PNG, transparent background. Pixels (px) are the dots that make up an image. The first number is width, the second is height.
  • Fill the artboard (the design canvas) close to its edges. The logo should fill the template almost edge to edge at the right proportions. This prevents empty margins or wrong shapes when uploaded.
  • Keep the logo simple. A text-plus-icon logo is easier to read at small sizes and looks more professional. A logo that is too detailed or showy is hard to read on the site.
  • A designer logo can be very large, for example 1527 × 518 px, which loads slowly at full size. Crop the surrounding white space, then resize to about 500 px wide while keeping the rectangular shape. This suits the header and checkout (the page where customers pay).
  • Pixel dimensions describe width by height, not file size. A 40 × 40 px image is 40 pixels across and 40 pixels down. Logos and favicons are sized by these pixel dimensions, usually with the Resize function in the editing tool.

Favicon

  • Favicon size: 64 × 64 px, JPG or PNG, transparent background. A favicon is the small icon in the browser tab.
  • Using the same icon as the logo is the simplest, most consistent choice.
  • Create the favicon in Canva:
    1. Open the favicon template from the Source A templates and click Use Template.
    2. Copy the icon from the logo design, paste it into the favicon template, and centre it.
    3. Click Download and keep PNG. On the free plan the file keeps a white background, because transparency is not available. JPEG (JPG) is an alternative and also keeps the white background.

Logo and favicon in Shopify

2 min

Uploading the logo

  1. Open Theme settings (gear icon in the left sidebar), or go to Online Store > Themes > Customize > Theme settings > Logo.
  2. Under Logo, click Select image and choose the file from Files. Alternatively, in the header section, open the logo setting in the theme settings and upload the file.
  3. Click Done and check the header preview. Adjust the size until it displays correctly.
  • The default desktop logo width is 200 px. Change it only if the uploaded file is noticeably larger or smaller, and check the preview.
  • The logo appears in the store header after upload.
  • In one example store, the logo height was set back to 100 after being enlarged. The logo should be neither so large it crowds the header nor so small it is hard to read. Mobile uses the same logo image, but its size is set separately.
  • In Source D' theme, footer logos (the logos at the bottom of the page) are chosen the same way as header logos.

Uploading the favicon

  1. Open Theme settings > Favicon.
  2. Click Select image and choose a small version of your logo, or another image that represents the store.
  3. Click Done.
  • If the favicon is not replaced, the browser tab shows Shopify's default logo instead of your brand. Setting it takes under a minute and matters even for a demo store (a test store).

Brand and metadata

  • In Shopify Settings > Brand, add the logo, brand colours, slogan and description. Do this after the logo and colours are created. Social media pages are linked here later.
  • Add your logo and update the metadata (hidden store information that search engines and link previews read) with your store details. This helps the store look different from copycat stores.

Source D theme settings

  • Theme settings > General Settings contain: Logo, Colours, Typography, Button Styling, Navigation Buttons, Brand information, Search behaviour, Animations and Cart Drawer (the side panel that shows the cart) options.
  • Choose logo images with the Select from library button.

Colour palettes

3 min

Shopify colour fields

  • Theme settings > Colors contains: Solid button label, Accent 1, Accent 2, Secondary text, Outline button, Background 1, Background 2 and Price for you save. An accent colour is a main highlight colour. Click a swatch (colour box) and type a six-digit hex code.
  • The Source E example set includes Text and Background 1 and 2, as well as the fields above.
  • Example A: Solid button label white; Accent 1 brand pink; Accent 2 brand teal; Secondary text black; Outline button brand blue; Background 1 solid white; Background 2 light grey.
  • Example B: Solid button label #FFFFFF; Accent 1 #6D388B, used for solid buttons; near-black #121212 as the dark contrast colour.
  • Keep the theme's default dark and light colours in their roles. Replace only their values with brand colours, so the store stays consistent.
  • Changes show in the homepage preview as soon as you click Save. Scroll through the preview after each change to check buttons, backgrounds and text.

How many colours

  • Use a solid palette (a fixed set of colours) of 2 to 4 brand colours for a professional, trustworthy look.
  • Avoid too many colours. Pick a few and reuse them across buttons, menu links, borders and banners, rather than a unique colour for each element.
  • Keep one accent colour for buttons. If you use a gradient (one colour that fades into another), reuse the same accent rather than pasting new colours each time.
  • Gradient options exist for accent colours, but a simple store can leave them off and use solid colours only. Leaving Accent 2 at no colour is valid when no second accent is needed.

Choosing colours

  1. Search Google Images for "colour palette".
  2. Pick a palette whose colours already fit together.
  3. Copy the colour codes from that palette.
  4. Use those codes as the website colours, so the whole design matches.
  • Match colours to the audience. For an older audience, minimal design with white and grey backgrounds and black text usually works better than bright colours.
  • A simple, clean store uses a header announcement bar (a thin strip at the top with a message) and a limited set of recurring colours. The example store used green, white and grey consistently.
  • If a section should match the site, use the store's colour scheme in AI prompts (instructions typed into an AI tool) for section graphics.

Problems

  • A near-black page background makes black product photos disappear into the page. Use a background colour that contrasts with the product images on any page showing dark products.
  • Footer colours (the bottom section of each page) are set in Theme settings > Colors, scrolled to the bottom. The Footer group contains: Background, Headings, Text, Text link, Text link hover, Menu list link, Menu list link hover, Additional menu link, Additional menu link hover and Copyright text. Social icon style is set in the same section, with the options Light on dark or Dark on light. Adjust the copyright text colour so it matches the rest of the footer.

Brand redesign sequence

  1. Upload the new logo to replace the old one.
  2. Open Theme Settings > Colors and update all colour codes with hex codes from the brand guidelines (the brand's rules for logo and colour use).
  3. Open Theme Settings > Typography and change the heading and body fonts to your paired fonts.
  4. Rewrite the website copy in brand voice (the way your brand sounds in writing): screenshot each section, paste it into ChatGPT, rewrite it in brand voice, and copy it back to Shopify.
  5. Preview all changes and publish.

Typography

2 min

Where to set fonts

  • Theme settings > Typography sets the font for all text on the store by default. Click Change, search Shopify's font library, select a font and click Select.
  • Shopify path: Theme Settings > Customize theme > Typography.
  • For each page type (homepage, collection page, product page), open the theme settings and choose the page type. Change fonts and styles page by page from the dropdown.

Font choice and speed

  • System fonts, such as Helvetica, New York, system_ui and SF Mono, are pre-installed on devices and load fast. Downloaded fonts, such as Abel, Abril Fatface, Acme, Alegreya, Poppins and Playfair, load more slowly because visitors must download them. Prefer system fonts for faster page load.
  • The Shopify font list shows Sans-serif, Serif and Mono fonts, which load faster. Sans-serif fonts have plain letter ends, serif fonts have small decorative strokes, and mono fonts give every letter the same width. "Other" fonts are downloaded to the visitor's computer and can slow load times. Prefer the faster fonts.
  • Adding too many fonts and colours quickly makes a store look messy.

Pairing

  • Set the body font (the font for normal text) to Montserrat or a similar clean, readable sans-serif for accessibility (easy reading for everyone). Choose a distinct display font (a bold, eye-catching font) for the heading font. This creates visual hierarchy (the order of what looks most important) and brand distinction.
  • Typography includes a Font size scale, which enlarges or reduces all text proportionally. It also includes Body font with weight options such as regular, italic, medium and medium italic. Font weight is how thick the letters look. A 110% scale and medium weight are example values that gave bolder, more readable body text.
  • Heading font size is a percentage. It defaults to 130% in the Manhattan theme. Setting it to 100% makes product titles smaller.
  • A light body font weight can make product descriptions look faint. Take a screenshot before you change the font weight.

Using a brand font

  1. Download the free Deja Vu Serif font (or your brand font) and unzip it. Unzipping means opening the compressed download folder.
  2. Upload the TTF file (a font file type) in Shopify admin under Content > Files.
  3. Copy the file link and paste it into the theme's font field, so the store font matches the logo font.
  4. Upload a few options and compare them before choosing.
  • Brand Fetch (a tool that reads a brand's colours and fonts from its website) can report font data wrongly for Shopify sites. Custom fonts often appear as CSS variable names (labels the theme uses for fonts), such as --font-body-family, not real typeface names such as Helvetica or Georgia. Visit the live website or inspect the theme CSS (the code that controls the look) to find the real font. Do not assume the variable name is the typeface.

Layout and buttons

1 min
  • Theme settings > Layout sets the page width. The default is 1200 px, and it can be raised to 1600 px. A wider page only shows on larger monitors, so 1200 px is a safe default.
  • Theme settings > Buttons controls border thickness, opacity (how see-through something is), corner radius (how round the corners are) and transparency for all buttons. The same area includes Variant pills (product option displays), Inputs (for example, the search box size), and Product and collection cards. Default values are fine for a beginner store.

Banners and slideshow images

1 min

Sizes

  • The table gives 1800 × 400 to 900 px height for slideshow and image banners. A slideshow is a set of pictures that rotate at the top of the homepage.
  • For the Source A theme, the standard homepage banner is 1800 × 600 px. Banners are full-width graphics used as slideshow images.
  • A custom banner shape can be made in Canva, for example 1800 × 900 px.
  • Shopify's slide image field shows a recommended size of 1800 × 1000 px. This differs from the 1600 px hero width in the older PicMonkey procedure below. Compare the theme's hint with the size you prepared, and choose the one that fits your slideshow height before uploading.
  • For a wide hero banner (the large picture at the top of a page), a 1000 × 500 px result is workable. A 624 × 628 px square cannot fill a full-width banner and should be rejected for that slot, even though it suits a square image area.

Text on banners

  • The theme can place text over banner images itself. Banners can be plain photos with no text added in Canva.
  • Add text to banners only when a design-level message is wanted.

Hero banner procedure (PicMonkey, 2021-22, older)

PicMonkey is an online photo editor.

  1. Search a stock source (a site of ready-made photos) for an image that shows the result your customer wants.
  2. Check the image dimensions and reject images too small for a full-width banner.
  3. Download the image and open it in PicMonkey.
  4. Use Smart resize (a tool that changes the size of a picture) to set the width to 1600 px, and reduce the height to between 400 and 600 px.
  5. If one dimension is still too large, crop the side you do not need, keeping the main subject.
  6. Download the edited image.
  7. Compress it in ImageOptim (a tool that makes image files smaller).
  8. Upload it to the Shopify slideshow.

Collection images

1 min

Sizes

  • Collection page banner: 1600 × 400 px, JPG.
  • Homepage collection image: 800 × 800 px, JPG.

Collection page banners

  • Collection page banners are plain banner-format images with no text. Each collection gets one, uploaded to that collection. They act as atmospheric backgrounds (mood pictures) at the top of each collection page.
  • They can show lifestyle scenes, zoomed-in details, or colour and fabric swatches (small colour and material samples) that match the products.
  • Each collection can have its own image. A more specific one (for example, living room couches versus living room chairs) helps buyers recognise the category.
  • Before suppliers are connected, generic lifestyle photos are enough for collection banners. Product-specific images can replace them later. Refresh collection images when new products or suppliers are added.

One image for two places

  • A collection's image is shared between the collection page and the homepage. The same collection cannot show different images in each place.
  • Use either the long collection banners or the square homepage collection images, not both for the same collections.
  • Expecting one image on the homepage and another on the collection page does not work. Plan one image per collection.
  • Homepage collection images are square. A long banner reused on the homepage is cropped from its centre, so place the main subject in the middle of any image used in both places.

Product page graphics, blog images and pre-footer

1 min

Product page graphic

1 min
  • Size: 1600 × 200-600 px height. px means pixels, the size of an image in dots. Use JPG or PNG. JPG is a common photo file type. PNG is a file type that can have a transparent (see-through) background. PNG needs a transparent background.
  • The product page graphic appears directly above or below the Add to Cart button, the button shoppers press to buy.
  • Add, change or remove it depending on the season or whether a sale is running.

Blog images

1 min
  • Size: 1600 × 900 px, or any 16:9 ratio. 16:9 is a wide shape. JPG.
  • Blog images are optional. Create them in Canva (an online design tool) only if your store uses blog posts or blog previews.
  • For featured blog tiles (large highlighted boxes), you can use templates (ready-made designs) with text overlays (words placed on top of the picture) instead of plain image backgrounds.
  • Keep the same size across all posts so the blog layout stays consistent.
  • A blog template can carry a title over the image.
  • Each post can use a different background image.

Pre-footer

1 min
  • The pre-footer is the strip just above the bottom of the page.
  • Size: less than 285 × 285 px, JPG or PNG. PNG needs a transparent background.

Mega menu icons

1 min
  • A mega menu is a large drop-down menu at the top of the store.
  • The Source A theme (the store's design template) supports up to five mega menu icons.
  • Each icon is a separate page in one Canva design.
  • Downloading the design produces a separate image file for each page.
  • Size: 250 × 250 px, JPG or PNG, transparent background.
  • Create them in Canva:
    1. Click the mega menu icons template in Canva.
    2. Open Elements (Canva's icon library). Add an icon, for example the favicon icon (the small icon shown in a browser tab) or a wave. Make it full size, centre it and set its colour.
    3. Click Add page and repeat for each additional icon, up to five in total.
    4. Click Download. Each page downloads as a separate image file.

Product photo sourcing and editing

1 min

Why photos matter

1 min
  • Ecommerce sites (online shops) depend on images for two core functions.
  • The first is brand identity (logos and graphics).
  • The second is product visibility, which lets buyers make informed purchase decisions.
  • Images are often the largest elements on a page.
  • They are also the biggest cause of slow load time, meaning how long a page takes to show.
  • Compress them (make the file smaller) offline before upload. Offline means on your own computer.
  • Compression apps are less controllable than a dedicated offline tool.
  • Poor product images are a common reason stores do not convert (turn visitors into buyers).
  • Find better images before concluding the product is weak.
  • Lifestyle images (photos of the product in real use) help buyers see the product in use.
  • They also increase trust.
  • A landing page (the first page a visitor reaches) needs good-quality product and lifestyle images.
  • These make the brand look better and more trustworthy.

Supplier and stock images

1 min
  • A supplier is the company that makes or ships the product. Stock images are ready-made photos from photo libraries.
  • Supplier and stock images come in varying dimensions and file sizes.
  • They usually need editing or resizing to fit specific layout sections.
  • Image sources to check, in this order: Pexels and Pixabay (free photo sites), Google Images, Alibaba and AliExpress (supplier marketplaces).
  • If no good image exists, look for a very similar product.
  • Instagram hashtag research (searching Instagram tags for your niche) can also reveal raw user photos for the niche.
  • Check whether the store's product photos are the seller's own.
  • Matching backgrounds, tables and hands across several images show the owner shot them.
  • This is stronger than generic supplier photos.
  • Use this as a quality check when the store looks different from the supplier listing.
  • Put your own brand name on copied product photos before publishing.
  • This makes them your store's assets, not identical copies of a competitor's.
  • Supplier product photos can be downloaded from the supplier's website.
  • If the file type is not JPEG, convert it (change the file type) with a free online converter.
  • Find the converter by searching for the file type conversion.
  • Generic lifestyle photos are enough for collection banners (large header images for a product category) before suppliers connect.

Editing yourself or outsourcing

1 min
  • During setup, editing single images yourself with PicMonkey (an online photo editor) is often faster.
  • It is often faster than coordinating with a freelance designer (a self-employed designer you hire).
  • It is practical to learn basic image editing skills early.
  • Standard editing sequence:
    1. Edit images in PicMonkey by resizing, cropping (cutting away edges) and adding text as needed.
    2. Then compress the larger files selectively, with platform-specific tools.
    3. Use ImageOptim for Mac, or Windows' built-in compression.
  • Cropping or resizing in PicMonkey does not change the original file on your computer.
  • Download the edited version as a new file.
  • Download as PNG to keep a transparent background.
  • For bulk processing (many images at once), outsource it.
  • Give a freelancer a Photoshop automation or batch script (saved steps that run on many files).
  • The script resizes each image, places it on a white background if needed, and compresses it.
  • Source B paid about $10 per 100 images for this.
  • He called it one of the cheapest things to outsource.
  • Fiverr (a freelance marketplace) retouchers can be hired for about $5 to $10 per image.
  • They can change backgrounds, add pop (make colours brighter and sharper) and clean up pixelation (blocky, grainy edges).
  • AI image tools such as DALL-E (an AI that makes pictures from text) can create or edit product images that look realistic.
  • DALL-E is transcribed as "Dahl" in the source.
  • DALL-E can create new images for a print-on-demand store (a store that prints products only after an order).
  • It can edit an existing product photo.
  • It can produce variations from the same source image.

Supplier file preparation

1 min
  1. Rename the file to include the supplier name, product description and model number. A file name is the name of the file on your computer. Default file names are often gibberish (random letters and numbers).
  2. Give multiple images of one product distinct file names by adding a word such as front, back, side or in use.
  3. Set the alt text. Alt text is a short written description read by screen readers and search engines.
    • It can match the file name, but it is a separate field.
    • Apps that copy the title to every image produce duplicate alt text.
    • That is weaker for image search than unique alt text, but better than none.
  4. Resize the image from 2000 × 2000 px to about 800 × 800 or 1000 × 1000 px.
    • This keeps good resolution while loading fast on mobile (phones).
  5. Save as JPEG, which is smaller than PNG.
  6. Compress the image again with a compressor such as ImageOptim on Mac.

Background and layout

1 min
  • Keep the background white if the image is not a full-size lifestyle shot.
  • Put no text on the image, except branding that is on the product itself.
  • Apply a unique alt tag to every image.
  • Give every image file a descriptive file name.
  • Product images in a grid (rows of product pictures) should share the same proportions, such as square.
  • Mixed sizes in a grid usually come from the dimensions of the uploaded files, not from a theme setting.
  • So resize them to match.

Product image sizing rules

1 min

Target sizes (sources disagree)

1 min
  • Ideal size: 800 × 800 px, square, not rectangular.
  • Minimum size: never smaller than 600 × 600 px.
  • Smaller images may stop a product from being advertised on Google Shopping (Google's product ad service).
  • Minimum size: at least 1000 × 1000 px.
  • Smaller images look pixelated when customers enlarge them (2023).
  • Both minimums apply to different needs.
  • 600 px is about Google Shopping eligibility, meaning whether the product can be listed there.
  • 1000 px is about on-site enlargement quality, meaning how sharp the picture looks when enlarged.
  • The 1000 px minimum is the more recent advice.
  • Maximum: do not exceed 1000 × 1000 px.
  • Larger files slow the site.
  • Mobile users cannot see the extra detail.
  • So oversized images hurt site speed, conversions (sales from visitors) and SEO (search engine optimisation, ranking in search results).
  • A 1024 × 1024 px size was suggested for product images in 2020.
  • It was not confirmed.
  • So check the exact size with the platform or your support contact before uploading the full set.
  • A 1:1 template works for uniform images. 1:1 means square.
  • Place a rectangular image on an 800 × 800 canvas (the blank working area).
  • Scale it as large as possible without distortion (stretching).
  • Square images are not required, but uniform sizes look better side by side (2023).
  • A 500 × 500 px canvas was the example Canva size for a product image design.
  • A square product image at 1080 × 1080 px fits square product tiles and social-style grids.
  • Prepare images at this ratio before upload.

Very large and very small files

1 min
  • Very large supplier images, such as 8000 × 8000 px, slow page loading.
  • Resize them before upload.
  • Keep them at a size that still meets the 1000 × 1000 px minimum.
  • Product images at 2000 × 2000 px are too large and slow the page on mobile.
  • Resize them before upload.
  • Product photos above about 1 MB (megabyte, a unit of file size) are too large and should be compressed.
  • Slow opening of image files or slow uploads is a sign that compression is needed.
  • Product images above roughly 3000 × 3000 px are usually more than a website needs.
  • A 10,000 × 10,000 px image can be reduced to 1000 × 1000 px with no visible loss on desktop or phone.

Consistency

1 min
  • All product images on the site should share one consistent size and shape.
  • Mixed sizes make collection pages (pages that list a group of products) look messy.
  • Some advertising channels, especially Google Shopping, also require standard image dimensions.
  • Do not resize all product images to one identical size.
  • Suppliers send different aspect ratios (shape, width to height), and forced sizing causes cropping.
  • Compress the files to reduce file size instead.
  • Do not convert product images to different aspect ratios (square or landscape) in bulk.
  • Resizing without keeping proportions crops important parts or distorts the image.
  • Shopify (the online store platform) product grids and collection layouts depend on consistent proportions.
  • So resize product images to the recommended sizes before upload.

Hero shot prompt (uploaded item)

1 min

A hero shot is the main, first picture of a product.

Create a square picture of the uploaded item on a plain, pure white backdrop. Position the item in the middle of the frame with soft shadows that give it a sense of depth. Since this is the product's primary hero shot, keep it sharp, evenly lit, and free of any text, props, or background scenery.

Product fidelity: reproduce the item's exact form, dimensions, colour, texture, and surface finish. Do not invent branding or packaging that is not present in the uploaded photo. If the uploaded photo already displays a logo or label, leave it exactly as it appears.
  • The two prompts disagree on shadows.

  • The hero shot prompt asks for soft shadows that give depth.

  • Use the hero shot prompt when you start from the supplier's own photo and need to keep its branding and details exactly.

Compression and file formats

1 min

Targets

1 min
  • Compress images to the smallest possible file size while keeping visual quality.
  • Slow-loading images hurt the site.
  • Uploading uncompressed images of several megabytes significantly slows the site.
  • It also reduces conversion rates, the share of visitors who buy.
  • Product images: under 300 KB, ideally under 50 KB, with quality as close to the original as possible.
  • Compress with TinyPNG or Photoshop before upload.
  • Product photos above about 1 MB should be compressed.
  • GIFs (short looping animations): compress below 800 KB.
  • They can reach 1 MB, but the target is 800 KB.
  • For product pages, create GIFs from one to three second clips of existing video ads.
  • Animated GIFs load slowly.
  • A 1.5-5 MB GIF converted to WebP (a newer, smaller picture file type) becomes roughly 200-500 KB.
  • JPG, PNG and GIF converted to WebP can reduce a 1-2 MB image to around 200 KB or less.
  • Start with product images, because they load first.

Compression tools

1 min

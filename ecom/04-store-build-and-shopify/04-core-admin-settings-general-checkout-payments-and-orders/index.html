Playbook › 4. Store build and Shopify

Core admin settings: general, checkout, payments and orders

Section 4.4 · 4 of 20 in this chapter · 30 min read

In short

  • The only required setup step at first is turning on Shopify Payments, the tool that takes card money.
  • Set the time zone first. It decides when orders and reports are recorded.
  • Let buyers check out as guests, without making an account.
  • For dropshipping, orders must not be marked shipped automatically. Set them to fulfill manually.
  • Before the store goes public, set payment capture to manual so test orders do not charge real cards.
More key points
  • Do not change taxes, duties or sender email settings yet. Those come later.
  • At the start, keep only the Online Store sales channel active. Remove Point of Sale.
  • For shipping, make one free shipping price of $0 and delete the International zone.
  • Enter the tracking number before you mark an order as fulfilled. Marking it fulfilled sends the customer an email right away.
  • Shopify does not send orders to your supplier by itself. You must forward them.

Order processing (dropshipping)

  • Dropshipping means a supplier ships the product to the buyer, not your store.
  • Order processing options after payment.
    • Automatically fulfill the order's line items. (Line items are the product rows in an order. Fulfill means send out or mark as shipped.)
    • Automatically fulfill only the gift cards of the order.
    • Don't fulfill any of the order's line items automatically.
    • Automatically archive order after fulfillment or payment can also be set.
  • Archive means move out of the open list.
  • The Source D 2026 approach left these at the default for the foundation setup.
  • For dropshipping, choose option 3: do not automatically fulfill any line items.
  • The order is then marked as fulfilled manually, once the supplier provides tracking.
  • Tracking is the number that shows where the parcel is.
  • Keep the automatic archiving of fulfilled orders on.
  • Archived orders are not deleted. They are only removed from the open list.
  • Mistake: automatic fulfillment after payment marks orders as fulfilled before a supplier has shipped or provided tracking.
  • Option 3 avoids this.
  • Conflict: Source A recommends option 3 for dropshipping.
  • Source D 2026 foundation keeps the default.
  • For a dropshipping store, option 3 avoids marking orders fulfilled too early.

Checkout apps, custom code and third-party buttons

  • Checkout customization apps (countdown timers, custom images) were removed from the Shopify app store around 2020.
  • A countdown timer is a clock that counts down a deadline.
  • Checkout customization is reserved for Shopify Plus.
  • Shopify Plus is Shopify's top plan for large stores.
  • Shopify Plus starts at about $2,000 per month (as of 2020).
  • Use the native cart and theme settings for standard stores.
  • Native means built into Shopify. A theme is the store's design template.
  • Avoid adding custom code (hand-written code) to the Shopify checkout page.
  • Custom code can cause issues.
  • Extra payment buttons such as Klarna, Affirm or Amazon Pay appear prominently on their own.
  • Those buttons are a better way to add choices.
  • Klarna, Affirm and Amazon Pay are payment options that let buyers pay later or with saved details.
  • Blocks or links for Amazon checkout are not a trust signal (a sign that makes buyers feel safe).
  • Keep your store's checkout on your own platform and payment methods.
  • Avoid links that send visitors to the marketplace (a shop like Amazon or eBay).
  • Use the discount code and discount row settings at their defaults.
  • The discount row is the line that shows a discount in the cart.
  • The footer (the bottom of each page) and the shipping protection row (an optional add-on for shipping problems) are optional extras during testing.
  • The Shopify checkout allows little customization.
  • Conversion means a visitor completing a purchase.
  • Most conversion changes must happen earlier in the journey (the steps a buyer takes before buying).
  • Those changes are on the product page, in the offer and in the shipping and trust messaging.
  • Plan the store around these pre-checkout elements.

Checkout friction and language

  • Minimize checkout friction by accepting common payment methods.
  • Reduce steps to purchase.
  • Every additional step increases cart abandonment (buyers leaving before they pay).
  • Use words that signal security and trust, such as "free" and "tracked".
  • Check the checkout and cart for hidden prices.
  • Also check for mismatched currencies.
  • Also check for a missing add-to-cart button (the button that puts an item in the cart).
  • Shipping charged by mistake is a common cause of abandonment.
  • PayPal one-click checkout: integrate PayPal's payment button so customers can buy with one click after adding a product to the cart.
  • This removes the multi-step checkout and reduces effort.
  • It increases the completion rate (the share of buyers who finish paying).
  • One-click checkout app: displays checkout immediately after the customer adds a product to the cart.
  • It bypasses (skips) the cart review page.

Choosing a processor (the company that moves customer money)

  • Integration means connecting it to the store.
  • Implement it when your region is supported.
  • Supported list: Australia, Austria, Belgium, Canada, Denmark, Germany, Hong Kong, Ireland, Italy, Japan, Netherlands, New Zealand, Singapore, Spain, Sweden, UK and US.
  • Shopify integrates with most payment processors.
  • A store can be built with a gateway (a link between the store and a payment company) other than Shopify Payments.
  • Shopify Payments is recommended, because it is the easiest option.
  • Older advice, Source A.
  • Use a checkout-related Shopify payment processor: Shopify Payments or Stripe.
  • Stripe is another payment company.
  • Check the main payment processor of the target country (the country you sell to) before launch.
  • Use Stripe instead where Shopify Payments is not available.
  • Other payment providers can be used if Shopify integrates with them.
  • The Payments page links to the list of all providers via See all other providers.
  • The recommended setup is Shopify Payments plus PayPal.
  • Accept at least one processor for credit cards and PayPal.
  • PayPal performs particularly well in the USA and the top four markets.
  • One source reports that adding PayPal to a single-processor checkout roughly doubled conversion in one case.
  • Crypto (digital currency such as Bitcoin) acceptance.

  • Apple Pay and Google Pay are phone-based payment wallets.
  • Shopify Payments deposits funds within 2 business days of order completion.
  • A deposit is money paid into your bank.

  • Business or personal account.
  • Either a personal or a business checking account (everyday bank account) works to accept payments.
  • Business checking accounts are easy to open, by phone or online, typically in 20 minutes.
  • Business checking accounts are recommended for long-term operations.
  • Reason: account numbers do not change later.
  • Shopify Payments shows as Shop Pay, the first option listed under Settings > Payments.
  • Enabled payment methods must match those declared in Google Merchant Center (Google's product listing tool).

  • Some themes can hide it.

  • Payment methods shown in the footer: tick the checkboxes under payment methods for every method you accept.
  • Each ticked method then appears automatically in the store footer.
  • Test mode: the payment section of Shopify has an Enable test mode option.

Payment capture method

  • Payment capture is the step that actually charges the card.
  • Capture options.
  • Automatic captures and charges immediately.
  • Manual authorizes the card (reserves the money) but holds the charge until you confirm stock.
  • Set the payment capture method from automatically at checkout to manually (Manage > manually).
  • Orders then only authorize funds. They are not charged until you capture them.
  • This prevents charges on test or demo orders.
  • Mistake: leaving payment capture on automatic lets any test or demo purchase charge the buyer's card immediately.
  • Set it to manual before the store is public.
  • With manual capture, each new order triggers a notification email to the account email.
  • The payment must be captured manually in Shopify to charge the card.
  • Authorization windows (the time the reserved money is held). Sources conflict.
  • Shopify payment authorization defaults to seven days and cannot be extended on Shopify Payments.
  • Older advice, Source A.
  • If a product will arrive after that window, plan a deposit or payment-capture workflow in advance.
  • Shopify payment capture windows vary by card.
  • So a manual capture must be completed within the authorization window.
  • Check the timeframe before relying on a long hold.
  • Older advice, Source A.
  • Use seven days as the planning figure for Shopify Payments.
  • Check the card-specific window before relying on any longer hold.

Authorization-only workflows

  • Set up payment authorizations (not capture) initially, using the payment authorization setting.
  • This holds funds on the customer's card without charging them.
  • You can confirm stock with the supplier.
  • Then decide whether to capture (charge) or release (cancel the hold) the payment.
  • Older advice, Source A.
  • Procedure: capturing payments only after stock is confirmed.
    1. In the Shopify payment settings, turn on the setting that authorizes the card without capturing the payment.
    2. When a customer orders, the card is authorized, which confirms that the funds are available.
    3. Confirm with the supplier that the product is in stock.
    4. Click the capture payment button on the order to charge the customer.
    5. Process the order with the supplier once the payment is captured.
  • Deposit workflow for an out-of-stock item whose authorization will expire.
    • Step 1: ask the customer if they will pay a 50% deposit (a partial payment to hold the order).
    • Step 2: charge that half to the card.
    • Step 3: submit the order to the supplier.
    • Step 4: when the product arrives, charge the remaining balance and send the invoice (the bill) before shipping.

Manual payment methods, draft orders and invoices

  • Shopify draft orders are orders the store makes by hand, before payment.
  • They can be used to create a customer invoice before the order is paid.
  • This lets a store owner send a payable order to a customer manually.
  • Continue using Shopify draft orders to improve customer service and checkout completion.
  • They have a limit on conversion tracking (measuring which visits turn into sales).
  • Despite that limit, they make operations easier.
  • They are an effective way for customers to finalize orders, especially in high-ticket businesses (businesses selling expensive products).
  • Older advice, Source B.
  • Procedure: to accept ACH (an electronic bank transfer) or wire transfer (a bank-to-bank transfer) payments.
    1. Create a draft order in Shopify for the customer.
    2. In the draft order notes section, include the routing number (bank code) and checking account number for the wire or ACH transfer.
    3. Send the draft order to the customer.
    4. The customer sends the ACH or wire transfer to your bank.
    5. Once funds are received, manually mark the order as paid in Shopify.
    6. Note: ACH and wire transfers have no fraud protection (protection against scams) like credit cards.
  • Procedure: for split payment orders.
    1. Create the first draft order for half of the total amount.
    2. Create the second draft order for the remaining balance.
    3. Send both draft orders to the customer.
    4. Specify payment method for each (e.g., draft 1 on card, draft 2 via wire).
    5. The customer pays both.
    6. Mark orders as paid when payments are received.
  • Phone order process.
    1. The customer calls and tells you the product they want.
    2. You go to the website and pull up the product page.
    3. Add it to the cart through your store.
    4. Walk through checkout with the customer on the phone.
    5. Collect name and payment details as the customer reads them to you.
  • Partial refund for return shipping: when a return is accepted and the customer is charged a return shipping fee, issue a partial refund (return part of the money) in Shopify to deduct the fee.

Shop Pay Installments

  • Shop Pay Installments is a Shopify Payments feature.
  • It lets customers split purchases into 2 or 4 interest-free installments (payments spread over time, with no extra charge).
  • The payment option is selected at checkout.
  • On product pages, messaging such as "Pay in 4 interest-free payments" displays below the product price.
  • The calculated installment amount (the amount per payment) displays with it.
  • Both adjust automatically based on the product price.
  • The toggle (on/off switch) applies to the whole store, not to individual products.
  • Once switched on, installment messages can appear on every eligible product page.
  • All payment method changes save automatically in Shopify Payments.
  • No additional configuration is required after enabling Shop Pay Installments.
  • The option turns on in roughly 30 seconds of clicking.
  • It can take a few minutes to appear on storefront pages (the pages customers see) after the toggle is switched on.
  • Once active, the financing message (text about paying in installments) appears across the store's pages.
  • This includes product pages, the cart and checkout.
  • No separate theme editing is needed.
  • Activation path: Settings > Payments, click Manage under Shopify Payments, then Manage payment methods.
  • Locate Shop Pay Installments and toggle it ON.
  • Procedure: enable Shop Pay Installments in the Shopify admin.
    1. Log in to the Shopify admin.
    2. Click the settings gear icon in the bottom left of the admin navigation.
    3. Click Payments.
    4. Under Shopify Payments, click Manage.
    5. Click Manage payment methods.
    6. Find Shop Pay Installments in the list. If it is off, click the slider and drag it to the right to turn it on.
    7. If a save prompt appears, save the settings.
    8. Wait a few minutes, then open any product page on the store and refresh it.
    9. Confirm that the financing message appears on the product page. Check that it also shows in the cart and at checkout.

  • Give the change a few minutes and refresh the product page before assuming the setup failed.

    1. Keep the Affirm account open.
    2. Turn off Affirm on the site for two weeks.
    3. Turn on Shop Pay Installments for the same period.
    4. Compare conversion results at the end of the two weeks and keep the option that performs better.

Orders, fulfilment and notifications

2 min

Fulfilment means packing and shipping an order. Notifications are the automatic emails and alerts Shopify sends.

Order notification emails

  • Go to Settings > Orders: set which email addresses receive order notifications (alerts that a new order came in) and which address the emails are sent from. The customer can choose to receive notifications at any configured email.
  • Set your notification email in Settings > Orders. The Shopify mobile app (the Shopify app on your phone) can play a sound with every sale.
  • Customer order notes field at checkout (the payment pages where the customer pays): leave it on at first, then remove it if customers never use it.
  • Use the Shopify order number and the order's information for customer purchase receipts (proof of purchase). If a customer does not receive the automatic Shopify order email, resend it from the order page or send a screenshot of the order.

Order details

  • Click into an order in Shopify Admin (Shopify's back office, where you run the store). It shows the products bought, any notes the customer added at checkout, the customer's name, email and address, and the customer's full purchase history with the store.

Fulfilment and tracking

  • Shopify Admin > Fulfillment screen > Tracking Information section: select the shipping service and enter the tracking number (the code that shows where the package is) before marking the order as fulfilled.
  • Mistake: marking an order as fulfilled sends the shipping notice (the shipping email) to the customer straight away. Do not mark it fulfilled before the shipping service and tracking number are entered.
  • Procedure: send tracking information to a customer when the supplier ships:
    1. Get the tracking number from the supplier.
    2. Open the customer's order in the Shopify admin.
    3. Attach the tracking number to the order so the customer receives updates.
  • Procedure: orders split across multiple suppliers:
    1. Receive order.
    2. Mark as partially fulfilled in Shopify (only part of the order has shipped).
    3. Add tracking number for first shipment, send first email.
    4. Mark remaining items as fulfilled when second shipment ships.
    5. Customer receives separate tracking email for each shipment.
  • Fulfilment request notification: turn on the option that notifies your fulfilment service provider when an order is marked as fulfilled. Use it when an outside 3PL (a outside warehouse company that packs and ships for you) or fulfilment partner needs the order update automatically instead of by hand.
  • Install a fulfilment app via Apps > Sales channel settings > Shopify App Store (Shopify's shop for add-on apps). Search for the app name, then click Install. Once installed, the app shows Open app.

Draft orders and receipts (cross-reference)

  • Use the draft order and receipt rules above for manual orders. A draft order is an order you create by hand.

Locations

2 min

A location is a place where stock is kept or sent from, such as a warehouse or an office.

Default location and plan limits

  • Settings > Locations: the default single location is the address entered at sign-up. It is sufficient at launch. Extra locations are only needed for inventory tracking (keeping count of stock) across several warehouses or suppliers.
  • Shopify's Locations page shows how many active locations the plan allows (the plan is the monthly package you pay Shopify for), for example "Using 1 of 10 active locations available on your plan". A Point of Sale subscription (the in-store cash register app) is included with the plan, so in-person selling can start from any active location.
  • Up to 1000 locations are allowed on the Shopify Basic plan.
  • Conflict: the Locations page example shows a limit of 10, while gives up to 1000 on Basic. Read the number shown on the Locations page for the plan in use.
  • Keep the default location at the foundation stage (the first basic setup). A location only needs to be changed when stock is held or sold from a different address, or when in-person selling is added.

Dropshipping rules

Dropshipping means the supplier ships the product straight to the customer. You do not keep the stock yourself.

  • Warehouse locations in Shopify should reflect the primary fulfilment location (the main place orders are shipped from), typically the home office for dropshipping. Setting one per supplier is unnecessary, because the supplier location determines the actual shipping, not the Shopify warehouse setting.
  • Set the Shopify default location to the country from which you actually ship (the shipping origin). If all orders ship from the US, set the Shopify location to USA so it matches the shipping origin in Google Merchant Center (Google's tool that holds your product list for Google shopping ads).
  • Point of sale is not used for a dropshipping store, since it requires a physical card reader (a small machine that takes card payments in person).

Customer notifications and sender email

1 min
  • Settings > Notifications contains the sender email settings (the address your emails come from). Do not change them yet. They are updated once the business email setup is finished.
  • The sender email address can be changed later once business email addresses (for example @yourstore.com) exist. Add the logo and brand colours to email templates (ready-made email designs). The default templates (for example order confirmation) can be edited, but editing is not required.
  • The sender email in Settings > General is the address customers see when the store emails them. It may not appear in the same way in every inbox (the place where emails arrive).
  • Revisit brand (your store's name and look), notifications and business contact details once the logo, brand colours, business phone, business email and business address are in place.
  • Policies (the store pages for rules such as returns and privacy) are completed during an earlier step of the setup, so no action is needed in Settings > Policies.
  • Source D 2026 places Policies in the foundation order before any sales channel is opened.

Gift cards

1 min
  • Set an expiration date for gift cards rather than never expiring. This stops unused gift cards (pending gift cards) from becoming old liabilities (money you owe customers) when the business is sold. Apple Wallet passes (gift cards saved to a phone wallet) are optional and not a priority.
  • Shopify has a built-in gift card feature (already part of Shopify). Customers choose the amount they want to pay, and the code is sent by email to be used by the buyer or given to someone else.

Apps, sales channels and domains

2 min

Apps and sales channels: what they are

  • The Apps item is at the bottom of the left navigation menu (the list of links on the left side of the screen). It lists every app already installed and shows recommendations for further apps.
  • Shopify Settings has a combined page for apps and sales channels, reached from the Apps page via its app and sales channel settings.
  • Apps add features to the store. Sales channels are platforms (places where you sell, such as a social media site). Both are managed from the same Shopify account.
  • The Online Store is the default sales channel, because it is where orders are accepted. Add other sales channels only when the store needs to sell somewhere else.
  • Most sales channels used in practice are native to Shopify (built into Shopify) and free to activate. Only some third-party channels (made by another company) or add-ons carry a cost.
  • When this was written, the sales channels available included TikTok, Etsy, the Shopify Marketplace and multi-feeds for Google Shopping (product lists sent to shopping sites). Check Shopify's current sales channel list before relying on them.
  • Shopify sales channels connect the store to traffic platforms (places that send visitors) such as Facebook, Instagram and Google from within the same account.

Starting point

  • Settings > Sales channels: keep only the Online Store active at the foundation stage.
  • Open the Online Store channel and confirm it shows the store name and the primary domain (the main web address of your store) before adding any other sales channel.
  • If Point of Sale appears, click the three dots next to it and choose Uninstall, because this business model does not use it.
  • Keep Online store and Email. Add sales channels such as Facebook, Instagram or Google later as the store grows.
  • Shop (Shopify's own app for shoppers) is an optional sales channel that can stay enabled. Point of sale is not needed and can be removed under Settings > Apps and sales channels.
  • Do not connect sales channels such as Facebook, Shopify Chat (a built-in chat tool) or Wholesale (selling in bulk to other businesses) before the store is launched and selling products. Add them later under Sales channels.
  • Sales channels vary by country. Facebook is free to add (Facebook fees may apply), and the Wholesale channel is unavailable in some setups.
  • Among Shopify's sales channels, Google is generally a poor choice for a new store, while Messenger (Facebook's chat app) is a better alternative. eBay can be connected in Australia.
  • Conflict: Source B 2021-22 calls Google a poor first choice and Messenger better, while Source A's setup adds Google later as the store grows. Treat the Source B advice as older.

Removing apps and channels

  • To remove a sales channel or app: go to Settings (gear icon, bottom left) > Apps and sales channels, hover over the item (move the mouse over it) and click Uninstall.
  • Remove international markets under Settings > Markets (the countries and regions you sell to).

Domains

  • Settings > Domains shows the Shopify domain and the custom domain (your own web address). Both are already configured and need no further action.
  • Check that the purchased domain is primary.

Activity log

1 min
  • Settings > Store activity log: a running list of all store changes. It is useful when sales rise or fall, to find what was changed.

Setup order for a supplier-ready store

2 min

A supplier-ready store is one ready to send orders to suppliers.

  • Procedure: Shopify Settings configuration order for a supplier-ready store (in order):
    1. Store details: set the store name; keep phone, email and billing address until a business phone, email and address exist.
    2. Billing, currency, time zone, units, order ID format (how order numbers look).
    3. Plan and billing: check the trial and billing dates (the free test period and payment dates).
    4. Users and permissions (who can log into the store): add staff if needed.
    5. Payments: activate Shopify Payments (Shopify's own card payment service), complete PayPal setup (an online payment service), set payment capture to manual (you decide when to take the money).
    6. Checkout: customer contact by email, Shop app link on, customer login off, last name only or first and last name, company name off, phone number not included for shipping, email marketing on, tipping off, shipping address as billing on, order processing option 3, archive fulfilled orders on (move completed orders out of the main list).
    7. Customer accounts (customer logins): keep hidden, enable Shop Pay (Shopify's fast checkout) after payments are set up.
    8. Shipping: delete default rates, create Custom flat rate Free shipping at $0, delete the International zone.
    9. Taxes and duties: no changes at this stage.
    10. Locations: keep default location; remove point of sale if installed.
    11. Gift cards: set an expiration date.
    12. Markets: keep only the primary market active.
    13. Apps and sales channels: uninstall point of sale.
    14. Domains: check the purchased domain is primary.
    15. Brand, notifications, languages, policies: revisit after logo, colors and business email are set.
  • Source D 2026 foundation order: first open the account with the business email, then work through Settings > General, Payments, Shipping and delivery, Apps, Domains, Customer privacy and Policies before any sales channel is opened. These steps are the foundation. Advanced settings come after them.
  • At the initial setup stage, most Shopify settings should stay at their defaults (the starting settings Shopify provides). The goal is to learn where settings are located, not to configure heavily.
  • The general store settings are preset. Other default options do not need changes at launch (the day the store opens to customers).
  • At launch, the recommended default options in Settings > Checkout can be left unchanged, apart from the customer contact method, the order tracking link, and the required-field (boxes the customer must fill in) and sign-in settings. Later checkout changes are made in the same section.
  • At the foundation stage, keep Shopify's defaults for advanced settings such as metafields (extra custom data fields), customer events (tracked actions such as page views), post-purchase (the page after payment) and marketing pixels (tracking code that reports sales to ad platforms). Do not spend time on them until the store is scaling (growing sales). The reasoning is to build a stable base first.

More rules worth knowing

1 min
  • By default, Shopify does not send orders to suppliers. Orders must be forwarded to the supplier separately.
  • In Online Store > Preferences, turn off Google reCAPTCHA (a "prove you are not a robot" check) on contact forms (the form where customers write to you) and on login, create-account and password recovery pages. Enable it only if you get spam (unwanted junk messages), because it can require customers to complete a reCAPTCHA task.

Playbook › 4. Store build and Shopify

Product creation and catalog data

Section 4.13 · 13 of 20 in this chapter · 54 min read

In short

  • Start with the bestsellers of each approved supplier, not the whole product list.
  • For many products, use one spreadsheet file (CSV). For a few, add them by hand.
  • Set prices from the supplier's rules: the lowest price the brand allows (MAP) and its suggested full price (MSRP).
  • AI text can invent facts. Get the supplier to confirm every detail before you publish.
  • Keep your stock count correct, or you will sell what you do not have.
More key points
  • Use only the supplier's photos and videos, and only when the supplier has allowed you to sell and show them.
  • Prepare product lists in Shopify's exact CSV format (a spreadsheet file). Start from Shopify's sample template.
  • Check each product before you publish: it must show Active, with the right image, price and name.
  • Export a backup before a bulk edit. Export does not delete products.
  • Never change the header row (the top row with column names) of a CSV.
  • Add products one at a time by hand, or upload one spreadsheet file for many products at once.
  • Every product needs Status set to Active and Online Store turned on, or customers cannot see it.
  • Use the supplier's model or part number as the SKU (your product code), so orders match the supplier's list.
  • Use one product type name per category, typed the same way every time.
  • Use variants on one page for colours or sizes, not a separate page for each.
  • Set up each new product in the same order: supplier name, description, images, price, then move the card on.
  • Sell at MAP, the lowest price the brand allows you to show.
  • Show a crossed-out price only if it was a real price. Otherwise the discount is misleading.
  • Upload simple products by spreadsheet. Add products with many options by hand or with an add-on app.
  • Upload the rival stores' best sellers first. Upload the rest after the store is live.

Deciding what to upload first

1 min
  • Upload the approved suppliers' products first, one batch per approved supplier, so your starting shop covers several brands. Do not wait until every supplier is approved before listing the ones that are already approved.
  • Do not upload a whole supplier catalogue (their full product list) before you know the bestsellers. A small starting ad budget (the money you spend on ads) spread across thousands of products is wasted. Find the bestsellers early and send the budget there first.
  • When a supplier has hundreds of products and no product file (a spreadsheet with product details), upload the most important items first:
    1. Email the supplier and ask for their top 10 best-selling products.
    2. Upload those 10 products first.
    3. Upload the rest of the catalogue afterwards.
  • A new store can start with about 100 products. Established stores (shops that have run for a long time) often carry hundreds or thousands. Add products gradually after the first batch is live (visible to customers).
  • Aim for at least 100 different products. This gives enough variety to rank (appear high in search results) for enough keywords (words shoppers type into Google) to see impressions (how often your product is shown), clicks and sales.
  • Ask suppliers which variants sell best and list only those. Variants are versions of one product, such as a colour or size. The 80/20 rule (a common pattern where a small share of items brings most of the sales) suggests that about 80% of sales come from about 20% of variants. Older advice (2019).

  • Manual upload is simplest but most time-consuming. It becomes impractical for suppliers with 500+ products.
  • Uploading hundreds of products manually is inefficient.
  • A CSV bulk upload can handle 1,000 or more products in one spreadsheet and creates the product pages automatically.
  • CSV is generally the better option for large catalogues. Products can be added or edited one by one on each product page, or in bulk through a Shopify CSV. Shopify is the online shop software you use. A Shopify CSV is a spreadsheet in the format Shopify accepts.
  • CSV is the fastest method when a supplier provides product data. Shopify provides a default CSV template (a ready-made spreadsheet with the right column names) that defines all required columns.

  • CSV is the fastest method besides outsourcing (paying someone else to do the work).

Ask suppliers to send product information as Excel sheets or shared Google Sheets (free online spreadsheets). This lets you edit descriptions, pricing and specifications without them auto-updating from the supplier feed.
  • Use the Shopify CSV template for bulk creation (adding many products) and updates. It also updates older products.

Manual product entry

2 min

Steps for manual upload from a supplier's website

  1. Log into the Shopify admin (the back office where you manage your store) and navigate to Products.
  2. Click Add products.
  3. Enter the product title from the supplier website or product information.
  4. Copy and paste the product description.
  5. Upload the product images provided by the supplier.
  6. Enter relevant tags (labels you attach to products, created previously).
  7. Set your selling price.
  8. Enter the supplier's business name in the Vendor field (the box that stores the brand or supplier name).

Add product steps

  • Open Shopify Admin > Products > Add product (top right button).
  • Product Title field: paste the product name.
  • Description field: paste the description into the WYSIWYG editor (the text box where you see formatting as you type).
  • Product Category field: select the category for your product.
  • Price field: enter the product price.
  • Product Type field: match the product category.
  • Collections section: select automated tag-based collections (groups of products that fill in by themselves, based on tags) to assign the product.

Other manual-entry rules

  • Add products one by one: open Products, click Add product, fill in the details and publish (make the product visible to customers). For larger catalogues, importing a CSV is much faster once you have learned its format.
  • The Shopify product description editor works like a word processor. It supports text formatting (bold, colour, alignment) and embedded images without leaving the field.
  • The Shopify product editor is the single place where all product data is entered.
    • This includes the title, description, price, model number (the maker's product code), images, videos and every other field that appears to customers.
    • Product edits, image changes and data updates all happen in the Products area of the admin.
  • Split-screen layout for manual upload: Shopify admin on one side, the supplier's website on the other.
    • Copy titles, descriptions and images directly between the two without switching windows or applications.
    • This reduces transcription errors (copying mistakes) and speeds up manual uploads for small product counts.

Product fields

1 min
  • Category: search dropdown (a list you open with a click) for your niche (the small market you sell into).
  • Type: the product type name. Set it to match the category.
  • Custom product types: the Type field accepts custom text you type. If the type does not yet exist in your store, Shopify creates it and adds it to the product type list for all future products.
  • Vendor: the brand or supplier name field.
    • Demo products: the store name.
    • Real products: the supplier's brand name, for example "Surfboards Plus".
    • Once saved, the vendor name becomes a selectable option (dropdown) for all future products from that supplier.
    • The Vendor field must be completed for every product during upload.
  • Collections and tags: collections (groups of products on one page) are assigned by automated tag-based rules. Enter tags before upload. Product type, vendor, collections and tags should be filled in consistently.
  • SKU and barcode: optional for demo products. A SKU (stock keeping unit, a code that identifies one exact product) and a GTIN (an international barcode number) are typically provided by suppliers for real products. These go in the SKU and barcode fields.
  • GTIN or SKU: enter the supplier's GTIN or SKU in the product identifier fields together with the rest of the supplier product data.
  • Status: active products are live (visible to customers). Set a product to draft (saved but hidden) if it still needs work.
  • Product type for multi-box products: products that ship in several boxes can keep one SKU per product, with the separate boxes listed in the description.

Pricing: price, compare-at, MAP and cost

3 min

Field definitions

  • Price: the customer-facing selling price (the price shoppers pay).
  • Compare-at price: a higher price that shows a discount.
  • Cost per item: what you pay the supplier (the wholesale price, the price a store pays a supplier). It is for tracking only and is optional for demo products.
  • The pricing block contains Price, Compare-at price (shown as a struck-through price next to the sale price), Charge tax (whether the store charges tax on this product), and Cost per item. Enter the compare-at price above the sale price to display a discount.

Which value goes where

Field What it holds When Source
Price MAP (minimum advertised price, the lowest price the brand lets you show) Supplier provides a MAP
Price Your selling price including your markup (the extra amount you add on top of the wholesale price), not the wholesale price Price is set by you , 2020
Compare at price MSRP (manufacturer's suggested retail price, the full price the brand suggests) Supplier provides an MSRP ,
Compare at price Left empty No MSRP is given
Cost per item Wholesale price, for profit tracking Always, when known
  • When a supplier provides an MSRP, enter it in Compare at price and enter the MAP in the Price field. Enter the wholesale price in Cost per item. Leave Compare at price empty if no MSRP is given.
  • Enter two prices: Price for the actual sale price, and Compare-at price for the MSRP. The theme (the design template of your store) then shows the MSRP as the regular price with the sale price beside it.
  • ** On the product page, show the MSRP as a crossed-out compare-at price, the MAP as the displayed price, and keep the wholesale cost off the storefront (your public shop pages).
  • In the CSV (the spreadsheet file), the price column must contain the final selling price including your markup. Enter this before importing (loading the file into the store).

How the compare-at price displays

  • A product page can show a list price (the original price) next to the sale price, with the saving in dollars and percent. Example: list 999 USD, price 799 USD, save 200 USD or 20 percent.
  • This shows on the product page by default. It does not show on the homepage, which needs custom code (website instructions written just for your store) added by a developer.
  • Pages with a clear list price, price and saving show value to shoppers. This can support conversion (the share of visitors who buy) without changing the MAP.

MAP and compare-at check (spreadsheet)

  1. Get the supplier price list as a CSV.
  2. Sort it by price from high to low.
  3. Add a MAP column with the MAP price for each product.
  4. If needed, export (download) the full product list from the Shopify store and add the MAP and wholesale columns to it.
  5. Check the spread (the gap between two prices) per product with the formula above. Note: the formula referred to in step 5 is not included in this text.

Profit columns for older exports **

  1. Export (download) all products from Shopify as a CSV file.
  2. Open the file in Google Sheets.
  3. Add columns such as wholesale price, cost of delivery, and merchant fees (fees the payment company charges you, for example 2.9 percent) to calculate profit per product.
  4. Use the Shopify app to add wholesale prices if you prefer to add them product by product.

Inventory

1 min

Inventory means your stock count.

  • Track inventory: on each product, the Inventory section has a Track inventory checkbox (a box you tick to count stock).
    • Leave it unchecked if you do not want to show stock levels. Set the quantity to zero to make a sold-out product unavailable for purchase.
    • For demo products and products managed manually, inventory tracking can stay unchecked. Enable it only if you want Shopify to track stock levels.

  • Google Shopping and out-of-stock items

    Google Shopping is Google's results page that shows product pictures and prices.
    1. In Shopify admin, open Products and turn on inventory tracking for the product or variant.
    2. Set the inventory of the out-of-stock variant to zero so it stops showing on Google Shopping.
    3. Set the inventory back above zero once the item is back in stock.
  • Sync apps (apps that copy stock numbers automatically between systems): if supplier inventory is not fully integrated with the store, do not rely on Shopify inventory-sync apps. Update stock manually only for a bestseller that will sell out that day.

  • Bulk stock update

    1. Export your product CSV from Shopify.
    2. Edit the inventory columns in Google Sheets or a spreadsheet.
    3. Save the updated CSV.
    4. Reimport into Shopify.
    5. Select "Update existing products" (not "Create new").
    6. Complete the import. All selected products update without creating duplicates (copies of the same product).
  • Inventory values: must be numbers.
  • Inventory defaults: if supplier data has no inventory quantities, ChatGPT (an AI writing tool) sets inventory to 0 for all products. Before importing, instruct ChatGPT to update inventory values from the supplier data, or edit the spreadsheet manually after downloading.

SKU and identifier rules

1 min
  • Suppliers' SKUs with dashes: search for the SKU with the dashes in quotation marks. Keep the dashes only if your future competitors use them.
  • Product titles include the SKU or model number where this helps buyers find the product. See the title section below.

Variants and options

3 min

Basics

  • Variants are not needed for demo products.
  • Use variants for real products when customers can select different options, such as colours or sizes, from a dropdown.
  • Four colours of the same product are one product, not four. Count variants as part of a single product SKU.
  • The variant shown first on a product page is the top variant in the list. Reorder the list to change the default variant.
  • Native Shopify variants (the built-in option system) with colour names are what add the chosen colour to the cart (the basket). Swatch apps (apps that show colour dots or pictures) only change how the options look. Set up native variants first.

Adding variants in the admin

  1. Open the product in the Shopify admin.
  2. Scroll to the Variants section and add option names such as size or colour.
  3. Enter the option values, then set price, SKU and inventory for each generated variant.

Bundle or discount on some option combinations

  1. Create a separate product variant for the bundle combination (a set of items sold together).
  2. Name the variant to show which options it includes (for example "Option 1 + Option 2").
  3. Set the price as the discounted price.
  4. Customers select this variant to get the discount automatically, rather than selecting individual options.

Free add-on as a variant

  • Open the product's listing and add the free add-on (an extra item given free with the main product) as a variant. One variant is typical.
  • Use the app that connects to the supplier to map the add-on, so both items reach the supplier in a single order.

Option types and apps

  • Shopify allows only three option types (kinds of choice, such as size or colour) per product. When native option types are not enough, use a product options app (an app that adds more choice types).
  • Use variations for versions of the same product, such as size. Use separate add-on products for extras, such as a monitor arm.
  • Product options and variants within one listing can be added with the app "Product Options" (formerly Bold Product Options, now SC Product Options).

Reducing a large variant range **

  1. Install Bold Product Options (an app for extra product options) and work through its setup training, which takes some time to learn.
  2. Ask the supplier for its best-selling colours and variants.
  3. List roughly 10 to 20 top options on the website instead of all of them, since too many choices can stop buyers from purchasing.
  4. Add the chosen variants to the site with Bold Product Options.
  • Many near-identical shades, such as ten different reds, confuse shoppers and lower conversions. Trim the variant list to the best sellers. **

Variants and SKUs on the page

  • Product pages with colour variants that have different SKUs: list the SKU numbers in a section under the main description. Put the SKU in the product title only when each variant has its own product page.
  • If a product has two or more options with different SKUs, choose between one product page with a dropdown and separate pages by checking how the top two or three competitors (other shops selling the same product) list it. Match their approach.

Add-ons

  • Cheap add-on products should not get their own product pages and should not be in the product feed. Offer them only as add-ons on the main product pages.

Variant rows in a CSV

  • Each extra variant is its own row that repeats the same Handle (the web address name of a product). Each variant row carries its own SKU, price, inventory quantity and option values.

Product titles

1 min

Default format

  • Default product title format: supplier or brand name, product name, then dash and SKU number (for example "Brand Product Name - 1234"). Check the top three competitors' naming, and adopt their format if it works better.
  • Product titles should include the brand name and the product name, and may also include a model or SKU number. The exact order and extra elements differ by brand, so copy the format of the top resellers (shops that sell the brand's products) for each brand.

Matching resellers

  1. Pick the brand and the product you plan to upload.
  2. Search for the product on Google and find the top one or two resellers.
  3. Note their title format, such as product name only, product name by brand, or brand plus product name plus SKU or model number.
  4. Use the same title format on your site for that brand when uploading products.

Include the SKU or model number

  • Product titles should include brand name, product name and SKU or model number. Shoppers who search those exact terms are the ones who buy, and that search traffic is worth capturing.

Short search-matched titles **

  • Formula for supplier-imported titles: [brand] + [main colour or variant] + [core product type]. The long technical name from the supplier is shortened to the words buyers type into Google. Size, material and full specifications move to the description. Examples: "Coleman blue two-person tent" and "Weber black gas grill". This works because short, search-matched titles fit how shoppers search in Google Shopping.

Where the rules differ

  • The advice from 2024 and 2021 puts the SKU or model number in the title.
  • The advice from 2018 uses a short title, with the specifics moved into the description.
  • Use the competitor check to decide between them for each brand.
  • Keep the SKU in the title where the top resellers do.

Descriptions and copy

4 min

Launch and rewrite

  • Use supplier descriptions at launch. Rewrite the highest-traffic product pages (the pages with the most visits) later, with original copy in your brand voice. Rewriting every page at the start is not worth the time.
  • Launch with the supplier descriptions. Once traffic arrives, use Google Analytics (Google's visitor-counting tool) to find the top 20% of product pages by visits. Hire a freelancer on Upwork (a website where you hire freelancers) to rewrite only those pages. Do not spend a large budget on e-commerce copywriters (people who write sales text for online shops) before you know which pages matter. **
  • Upload products in bulk first to create a starting point. Then go through each product page and optimise it for the customer. **
  • Load product descriptions in bulk through CSV uploads instead of managing them through a third-party description app (an app made by another company). This keeps the store faster and reduces app dependencies (the number of apps your store relies on).

Source text and AI text

  • AI writing tools (programs that write text on request) can produce product descriptions. Use the output as notes and rewrite it by hand rather than publishing it unchanged.
  • AI-generated descriptions often invent materials, sizes, certifications (official proof of quality or safety) or warranties (promises to repair or replace) for a product that does not yet exist.
  • Each specification (a detail such as size or material) must be confirmed by the supplier before the page goes live (is shown to customers).
  • Otherwise returns (products sent back) and chargeback disputes (when a customer's bank takes money back) become likely.

Prompt: product listing for a photo

Act as a seasoned eCommerce copywriter who builds high-converting Shopify listings for upscale goods. I'll send a sample photo of an item I'm considering listing. It isn't a real product yet, so infer its details from the picture and assume realistic specifics. Deliverables: (1) Devise a luxurious-sounding title that works as a brand, matches what the photo shows, suits a Shopify storefront, steers clear of trademarked or established brand names, and conveys reliability and professionalism. (2) Draft a complete listing: a brief opening paragraph centered on customer benefits; a 'Key Features' list of 5 to 8 bullets; a 'Why You'll Love It' or 'Benefits' block; a plausible, internally consistent 'Specifications' block; and a concise closing paragraph that nudges the reader toward buying. (3) Provide SEO metadata: a meta title no longer than 60 characters, a meta description no longer than 155 characters, and 3 to 5 search terms that fit the item. (4) End with a list headed 'Assumptions to verify with supplier' that names every material, dimension, color, capacity, function, or feature you inferred from the photo. None of these inferred items may appear in the listing as established fact. Voice: businesslike, self-assured, precise, upscale, ready for Shopify, aimed at sales rather than filler. Ground rules: where the photo is ambiguous, choose the most sensible assumption; treat the item as coming from a dependable supplier of good quality; the goal is a listing that impresses suppliers and looks ready to launch; never fabricate certifications, guarantees, test outcomes, or awards. Formatting: clean and Shopify-friendly, with headings and bullets, easy to scan, no emoji, no overblown hype. Output sequence: product title, meta title, meta description, keywords, product description (sections in the order of point 2), then the supplier verification list.

Pasting supplier text without hidden code

  • Copying description text straight from a supplier website carries hidden HTML code (hidden instructions that tell a browser how to show text) into the product page. This can break the whole page.
  • Supplier text may also contain Heading 6 tags (a small heading style, stored as hidden code) that clash with your own structure.
  • Procedure:
    1. Copy the supplier description into a Word document.
    2. Retype or clean the text there so no hidden formatting remains.
    3. Paste the clean text into the Shopify rich text editor (the text box with formatting buttons).
    4. Apply Heading 6 and other headings using the rich text editor buttons, not by pasting supplier headings.
    • Alternatively, paste through an app that strips the code (removes it) automatically if you are not comfortable with HTML.

Search listing (SEO)

  • The search engine listing section (the box that sets how your page looks in Google results) takes the first paragraph of the product description by default. Edit it with product page SEO best practices (SEO means making pages easy to find in search results) so the listing describes the product clearly.

Spec sheets and contact details

  • Before attaching a supplier's spec sheet (a document listing product details) to a product, remove the supplier's name and phone number so customers contact your business instead. For simple edits, Canva (an easy design tool) is enough.

Bulk-edit descriptions

  • Procedure, for example to remove warranty links:
    1. Export (download) all products from Shopify as a CSV. The export does not change the store.
    2. Use find-and-replace (a tool that swaps one piece of text for another) in Google Sheets or Excel to remove the text.
    3. Import the CSV back into Shopify. It updates existing products and does not create duplicates.

Supplier text reused from a standard template

  • Keep the product page's titles and descriptions matched to their own product rows (lines in the spreadsheet) when uploading a CSV.
  • A mismatched title or description creates wrong orders and angry customers.

Regional sizing

  • When selling to US customers, convert supplier sizes, such as European numbering, into US sizes and keep the size chart simple. Mismatched size formats make US buyers hesitate.

Product media

3 min

Source and rights

  • You may download product images straight from a supplier's website. Only if the supplier has authorised (officially allowed) you to sell their products.
  • Use product photos from the supplier, not from a Google image search. The store needs authorisation to use them.
  • Ask suppliers for lifestyle photos, meaning pictures of the product in real use, with natural backgrounds.
  • Ask suppliers for photos from trade shows (industry fairs) and from their office.
  • Do not copy images from other websites. This can lead to copyright complaints (claims that you used someone else's work without permission).

Image standards

  • Product pages should use the supplier's images and product names.
  • The main image should be a standard product photo on a white background.
  • Add lifestyle images as extra photos.
  • Use extra close-up or detail images, for example a crop of a hardware part or finish.
  • Make these close-ups from high-resolution supplier photos (large, sharp pictures).
  • Add close-ups only when they are high quality and help the buyer decide.
  • Compress oversized product images before you upload them. Compressing makes the file smaller.
  • Check that every product image matches its product page.
  • Images are easily swapped during bulk uploads (uploading many products at once).
  • Compare each image against the supplier's listing or brochure.
  • Supplier images stored as Dropbox share links (links to a shared online folder) do not give a direct image URL (a web address that shows the picture itself).
  • Download the files from Dropbox.
  • Upload them to Shopify Files (the image storage inside Shopify).
  • Put the resulting Shopify file links into the product CSV. This is easier than uploading each image by hand.

Video

  • Product videos can be filmed by a person demonstrating the product.
  • That person explains how it feels, who it suits, and the height or weight it is good for.
  • Post the videos to YouTube and embed them (place them inside the page) on the product pages.
  • Short product clips of about 3 to 10 seconds can be uploaded straight to Shopify.
  • Longer product walkthroughs should be embedded from YouTube.
  • Supplier product videos belong in the product description.
  • They do not belong in the image carousel (the slideshow of photos at the top of the page).
  • To use supplier videos hosted on YouTube:
    1. Ask the supplier for permission to upload the videos to your own YouTube channel (your own video page on YouTube).
    2. Create a YouTube channel under the store name if you do not have one.
    3. Upload the videos to that channel.
    4. Embed them on the product pages from your channel, and add a link to the product page in the video description.

3D models and upcoming media features

  • Shopify reports that customers who interact with a 3D product model (a turnable 3D picture of the item) are more than twice as likely to complete a purchase.
  • Customers who do not interact with one are less likely to buy.
  • The product media feature Shopify cites for this is planned to support images, videos, 3D models and augmented reality (seeing the product in your room through your phone camera) on product pages.
  • Prepare videos and 3D models for your top 20 best sellers first.
  • The media features that support them are expected to deliver the biggest conversion gains (more visitors buying) on your best products.

Supplier file cleaning and CSV preparation

8 min

Clean up a supplier CSV (a simple spreadsheet file) (2023)

  1. Make a duplicate (an exact copy) of the supplier's CSV file on your computer.
  2. Open the duplicate and delete all rows for products you do not want.
  3. Delete used items, trade-ins (items bought back from customers), discontinued items and special-order items.
  4. Save the file as a CSV.
  5. Upload the cleaned file to Shopify.

Ask the supplier for the right format

  • If a supplier is not technical, request the Shopify CSV product upload format.
  • This is the exact column layout Shopify accepts.
  • The supplier can fill it out to save you time.
  • Share the Shopify template with them for this.

Convert a supplier product feed (the supplier's product list file)

  1. Get the supplier product feed (typically a spreadsheet).
  2. Download the Shopify CSV template from the Shopify CSV page.
  3. Map supplier data to Shopify columns using Google Sheets or Excel. Mapping means matching each supplier column to the matching Shopify column.
  4. Adjust the data to match the required format.
  5. Upload to Shopify.
  • If it is complex, hire a CSV expert on Fiber or Upwork for a low cost.

Mismatched formats

  • Supplier spreadsheets rarely align with Shopify's CSV format.
  • Required columns (columns Shopify must have) are often missing, misnamed or structured differently.
  • This makes manual editing confusing and error-prone (easy to get wrong).
  • A mismatched CSV causes import errors (upload failures).
  • Convert it before importing.
  • Uploading a supplier's default file unchanged will not work.
  • This is because Shopify requires its own column names.
  • Reshape (rearrange) the file to match Shopify's sample CSV before upload.

Shopify CSV template columns

Column Purpose
Handle Product URL
Title Product name
Body Product description
Vendor Supplier name
Product category Product category classification

All columns must be populated correctly (filled in properly) for successful import.

  • In Shopify CSV imports, Handle is the product's URL path (the end part of its web address).
  • Title is the product name.
  • Body is the product description.
  • Vendor is the supplier name.
  • All other columns match fields in Shopify's product editor (the page where you edit one product).
  • A CSV is a spreadsheet file where each column holds one product attribute (one fact about the product).
  • Attributes include product name, SKU (the unique stock code of each item), price and description.
  • Shopify imports these files to create products in bulk (many at once).
  • The Shopify CSV Template is the file format used to import products in bulk.
  • Use it to prepare product data in a spreadsheet before uploading.

Template workflow

  1. Download Shopify's sample CSV template from its help documentation.
  2. Keep the template's column headers exactly as they are.
  3. Fill one row per product or variant (a version of a product, such as a size or colour), keeping every column in its original position.

Sample-template preparation

  1. Open Shopify's product documentation and download the sample product CSV template.
  2. Open the template in a spreadsheet program such as Numbers (Apple's spreadsheet app), Google Sheets or Excel.
  3. Replace the sample rows with the supplier's product data, keeping the column headers.
  4. Set "handle" to the product URL ending, which is the part that comes after /products/.
  5. Set "title" to the product name, "vendor" to the supplier name, and fill in the product category and type.

Compare the headers and reshape **

  • Older (2021-22). Each variant can have its own price, stock quantity and SKU. Different variants often have different supplier model numbers. Enter them in the variant table that appears after the options are set.
  • Variant procedure, Source A 2025:
    1. Open the product and go to the Variants section.
    2. Click the option to add options, such as Color or Size.
    3. Enter the values, for example blue and green, and click Done.
    4. Set the price for each variant.
    5. Set the quantity for each variant. This requires inventory tracking.
    6. Upload a separate image for each variant.
    7. Click Save, then Preview, and swipe through the variants to check the price, image and stock.
  • Older (2021-22). Use a variant app for products with more options than Shopify's native variant system can handle. Set the app up separately from the standard product variants. Do not mix the app's variants with the native variants on the same product. Test one product before uploading a whole group.

CSV import and export

3 min
  • Older (2021-22). CSV bulk method, in this order. A CSV file is a plain spreadsheet file, and bulk means many products at once.
    1. Discard (throw away) the unsaved product form. Then go to Products > Export > All products > Export products. Shopify emails a CSV of your existing products. This file serves as your template. The template file from Shopify's help pages also works.
    2. Open the file in a spreadsheet. Each line is one product.
    3. Fill the columns from Title through the column before SEO Description. Leave Handle blank. The handle is the short name in the product's web address.
    4. Paste titles, descriptions, prices and SKUs from supplier spreadsheets into the matching columns.
    5. Enter option names and their values. List option values on the lines below the product, in the layout of the template.
    6. Set the taxable and requires-shipping columns to TRUE for taxable physical products.
    7. Upload each image in Settings > Files, copy its URL, and paste it into the Image Src column.
    8. Save the file as a CSV file. The exact menu depends on Mac or Windows. The file must be a CSV.
    9. Go to Products > Import, choose the file, tick the option to overwrite products with the same handle, then click Upload.
    10. Check that the products appear with their data.

  • Older (2021-22). Leave the Handle column blank in the CSV. Shopify creates the handle automatically from the product title. The handle becomes the product URL.
  • Older (2021-22). When importing with the overwrite option ticked, products with the same handle are updated, not duplicated. Use this when re-importing products that already exist in the store.
  • Older (2021-22). Settings > Files stores images uploaded to the store and gives each file a URL. Paste that URL into the Image Src column of the CSV. A local file path (a location on your computer) will not import the image.
  • Older (2021-22). A CSV image link must be a hosted URL from Settings > Files. A local file name in the Image Src column leaves the product without an image.
  • Older (2021-22). Shopify's help pages include a CSV template file. It describes each column and what it must contain. Use it when a column is unclear, before importing.
  • Older (2019). Shopify publishes the required column format for product CSV imports. Search for the Shopify CSV format, then reformat the supplier's file to match it.
  • Older (2021-22). Manual entry suits a small number of products, or a team that finds spreadsheets daunting. CSV import suits hundreds of products at once. Supplier spreadsheets can be pasted straight into it.

Supplier file cleaning

1 min
  • Older (2021). Supplier file cleaning, in this order.
    1. Open the supplier CSV and the Shopify product CSV template side by side.
    2. Reorder and rename the supplier columns to match the Shopify columns. Copying and pasting covers most of the work.
    3. Fill in any remaining fields, then upload the file.
    4. If manual entry is needed, first list the competitors' best-selling products that come from your approved supplier. Then upload everything else in the background.

Bulk edits

1 min
  • Older (2022). Bulk updating of product fields via Shopify CSV import, in this order.
    1. Add a column for the product guarantee or description to a product CSV.
    2. Fill it in for every product from the same supplier.
    3. Import the file to update existing products in one step. The same import can create new products.
  • Older (2021-22). Keep all catalogue changes (your full product list) in a single supplier list. Note whenever a manufacturer updates its price list, so uploads and quotes stay current. Update the price list in every place where it is stored. Assign this update to a staff member as part of their regular tasks.

Outsourcing uploads

1 min
  • Older (2021-22). Set up each new product in the product upload board, a tool where each supplier has a card that moves through stages. Do these steps in this order:
    1. Enter the supplier name.
    2. Enter the product description, or a link to where the description can be found.
    3. Enter the source for product images, such as a shared Dropbox link (Dropbox is an online file-sharing service).
    4. Enter the pricing rule.
      • Use the MAP price list if one exists. MAP (Minimum Advertised Price) is the lowest price the brand lets you show.
      • Otherwise, add a fixed markup, such as 20% (an extra percentage added on top of your cost), and match prices from named competitor sites (rival stores that sell the same product).
    5. Move the supplier card to the next stage once the upload is finished.
  • Older (2021-22). Split large catalog imports into two roles. A catalog import means loading many products at once.
    • One staff member builds the CSV file for simple products. CSV (comma-separated values) is a spreadsheet file.
    • A second person checks product features before the import.
    • CSV errors are hard to trace after import, so products with many options (choices such as size or color) should be uploaded manually or through a variant app instead. A variant app is an add-on that handles product versions, such as sizes and colors.
  • Older (2021-22). For bulk catalog uploads of thousands of SKUs (SKU means stock keeping unit, one code for each product version) with many options, follow this split:
    • Use CSV imports for simple products.
    • Use manual uploads or a variant app for products that exceed Shopify's native options. Shopify is online store software. Native options are the built-in choices it allows.
    • Example: a store with about 3,000 SKUs and products with up to 18 options needed this split.
    • Put only the simpler products into a CSV, since errors in CSV files are hard to find.

Which products to upload first

1 min
  • Older (2021). If you must enter products by hand (manual entry), first list the competitors' best-selling products that come from your approved supplier. Competitors are rival stores.
  • Then upload everything else in the background, meaning while you work on other things.
  • Products that are not in the first batch can be uploaded afterwards, in the background, once the first batch is live (visible to customers).

MAP and compare-at pricing

1 min
  • Products should be listed at MAP as the selling price.
  • Enter the MSRP in the compare-at price field. MSRP (Manufacturer's Suggested Retail Price) is the list price the brand recommends.
  • The compare-at price is the old, higher price, shown crossed out next to the selling price.
  • So customers see the MSRP struck through and pay the MAP.
  • The compare-at price should be the real price the item was offered at. Otherwise, the discount is misleading.
  • For MAP-listed products, the compare-at price is the MSRP.
  • Older (2021-22). Pricing rule for uploads: use the MAP price list if one exists, or otherwise add a fixed markup, such as 20%, and match prices from named competitor sites.

Store-level rules

1 min
  • Older (2020). There is no fixed maximum for products in a store.
  • Stores with about 100 products and stores with 3,000 products both work.
  • Both work as long as the products are quality items from good brands in the niche. A niche is one narrow market, such as one hobby or topic.
  • Source C: the store's markets (the countries you sell to) are set for UK, Australia and US.

Legal pages, policies and trust pages

Section 4.6 · 6 of 20 in this chapter · 22 min read

In short

  • Your store needs six basic pages before launch: About Us, Contact Us, Shipping, Refund or Return, Privacy and Terms.
  • Make About Us and Contact Us as normal pages. Make the legal pages in Settings > Policies.
  • Shopify (the online store software) pages are easy to edit. Read every generated text before you publish it.
  • Before you publish, check that your real address, email and phone number are in each policy.
  • Keep policy pages (contact, shipping, privacy) in a narrow centred column, not full width.
More key points
  • Trust badges (small safety logos) go on product or landing pages. Checkout only allows one logo image.
  • Show your payment options and policy links clearly, or your merchant account can be flagged.
  • Never copy another store's words or health claims. Show only the certifications you really hold.

Core page set and where to create each page

2 min
  • Your store needs six pages, created and published (made live for customers).
  • The six pages are: About Us, Contact Us, Shipping Policy, Return & Refund Policy, Privacy Policy and Terms of Service.
  • Newer guidance (2025) lists six core pages: About Us, Contact Us, Shipping Policy, Refund Policy, Privacy Policy and Terms of Service.
  • Older guidance (2021-22) lists six Shopify pages every store needs: About Us, Contact Us, Shipping Policy, Privacy Policy, Terms of Sale (or Terms and Conditions) and Returns Policy. **
    • Each of these pages must be linked from the homepage and the site menu (navigation).
    • Product pages use one template (a ready-made layout), set up once and applied to all products.
  • Older guidance (2021-22) lists eight pages to set up before launch.
    • Privacy policy, terms of service and contact us.
    • A page explaining why to buy from the store.
    • A price match guarantee (a promise to match a lower price from another seller).
    • An easy returns page, a shipping page and an about us page.
  • Another list of core content pages: About Us, price guarantee, product guarantee, shipping policy, returns and exchanges, order tracking, support contact and FAQ.
  • Buyers check these pages before paying for an expensive item.
    • A missing page directly costs sales.
  • Competitor headers and footers link to Shop, About us, Track order, FAQs, Contact us and Our Policies.
    • The header is the top strip of the page. The footer is the bottom strip.
    • Create these pages before launch. Buyers check them to judge whether a store is legitimate (real and trustworthy).
  • Early supplier-approval stage: only four pages are needed.
    • About Us, Contact Us, Privacy Policy and Terms of Service.
    • Shipping, returns, refund or financing pages can be created later.
  • Before contacting suppliers, the store should have About Us, privacy policy, shipping policy and return policy pages. **
    • Even adapted templates are fine.
    • Suppliers may check your footer links before approving your account.
  • A store with no Terms of Service, Privacy Policy or full Contact Us page looks unprofessional.
    • It also risks trust issues.
    • Legal pages and a complete contact page should be present before launch.
  • Store pages hold customer information (About Us, Contact, policies).
    • This information changes rarely.
    • Customers read it when they decide to buy or when they manage an order.
  • Shopify pages hold customer information that rarely changes or that customers look up often.
    • Examples: About Us, a price match guarantee, a shipping and returns policy, a buyer's guide or a financing page.
  • Legal policies are a separate case.
    • They are created in Settings, not as ordinary pages.
  • Check large brands in the same niche (a narrow product market) to confirm your legal and policy pages cover everything customers look for.
    • Typical topics are order status, payment information, sales tax, shipping rates and the return policy.

    • They were already set up when the pages were created.

Creating a page in Shopify

3 min
  • Steps to create a page:
    1. Go to Shopify Admin > Online Store > Pages.
    2. Click Add page in the top right corner.
    3. Enter the page title, for example About Us.
    4. Paste the finished text into the WYSIWYG editor (the text box where you see the page as it will look).
    5. Click Save.
    6. Click View to check how the page looks on the storefront (the public side of the store that customers see).
  • Page content is typed or pasted into the WYSIWYG editor.
    • What you put in the editor is what the customer sees.
  • Steps to create a standard page: **
    1. Go to Online Store > Pages in the Shopify admin.
    2. Click Add page.
    3. Enter the page name in the Title field.
    4. Paste the page text into the Content editor.
    5. Under Visibility, keep Visible, or choose Hidden or Set visibility date.
      • Visibility controls who can see the page. Hidden means customers cannot see it.
      • Set visibility date schedules when the page appears.
    6. Under Template, leave Template suffix on 'page' unless the page needs a special template.
      • A template suffix is a tag that picks the page layout.
    7. Expand Edit website SEO and set Page title, Description, and URL and handle.
      • SEO (search engine optimization) is the text Google shows in search results.
      • The handle is the short name at the end of the page address (URL).
    8. Click Save.
    9. Click View page to confirm the live page shows the text.
  • The Pages screen has an Add page button.
    • It lists all existing pages with their last-updated time.
  • A page becomes publicly available as soon as it is saved with Visibility set to Visible. **
    • Visibility can also be set to Hidden or scheduled with Set visibility date.
  • The editor of an existing page shows Duplicate, View page and Delete page next to the Visibility section. **
    • Use Duplicate to copy a page that already has the right layout.
    • Then change its title and content instead of building from scratch.
  • Shopify builds the page address (URL) from the handle. **
    • A page saved under the handle shipping is reached at /pages/shipping.
    • A Contact Us page is reached at /pages/contact-us.
    • Set the handle when the page is first saved.
    • Later changes break existing links and search results.
  • After creating or editing a page, click View page to open it on the live store. **
    • Check that the text and layout appear correctly before you move on.
  • Paragraph spacing can be adjusted in the WYSIWYG editor.
    • Add space between paragraphs.
    • Save and view the page afterwards. Pasted AI text can look dense without spacing.
  • Reusable content page templates speed up building informational pages.
    • Examples: About, FAQ and shipping policy pages.
    • The same structure does not have to be created from scratch for each page.
  • Create the Contact us page in this order:
    1. In the Shopify admin, go to Online Store > Pages.
    2. Click Add page.
    3. Under template, choose the template named Contact.
    4. Add a contact form (a box where customers type a message) to the page and save it.
  • Pages can be built and checked in one pass per page.
    • Standard Source E order:
      1. Go to Online Store > Pages and click Add page.
      2. Create a Contact us page.
      3. Create an About us page with the brand story. Remove meta placeholders from the copy.
        • A placeholder is a blank marker, like [brand name], that you must replace.
      4. Create a shipping and delivery page with accurate policy details.
      5. Create an FAQ page if needed.
      6. Read every placeholder and replace it with accurate information before saving.

Generating and customizing legal policies

3 min
  • Shopify has a built-in generator for the refund policy, privacy policy and terms of service.
    • A generator is a tool that writes the text for you.
    • It produces the core legal pages in a few clicks. It is the fastest starting point.
  • Shopify's Help Center holds current instructions for the legal page generator.
    • The Help Center is Shopify's official help website.
    • Search for 'privacy policy' there, because menus and wording can change.
  • Generator path given in one walkthrough: Settings > Checkout.
    • Scroll to the bottom, to the section 'Refund, privacy, and terms of service'.
    • Then use the generate function for each policy.
  • Generator path given in another walkthrough: Settings > Policies.
    • Each written policy can be generated with Use automated policy.
    • Check your own Shopify admin. The two walkthroughs name different locations.
  • You can use the generator without a trial.
    • A trial is a free test period for paid software.
    • Choose the option to skip the Shopify trial and create the policy directly.
    • Then enter company information to receive the document by email.
  • Steps to create the core legal pages:
    1. Open the Shopify Help Center and search for 'privacy policy' to confirm the current instructions.
    2. In the Shopify admin, go to Settings.
    3. Click Checkout.
    4. Scroll to the bottom of the Checkout page.
    5. Find the section 'Refund, privacy, and terms of service'.
    6. Click generate for each policy: refund policy, privacy policy and terms of service.
    7. Read every generated policy in full.
    8. Customize each policy for the store's specific terms, refund rules and any special conditions.
  • Steps to create a return and refund policy with an AI text generator:
    1. Ask ChatGPT (an AI chat tool) for a return and refund policy and include your store name.
    2. Check that your supplier can actually deliver what the policy promises.
    3. Copy the generated text. Replace every bracketed placeholder with your real values.
      • Text in square brackets is a blank to fill in.
    4. In the Shopify admin, open Settings > Policies.
    5. Open the Refund policy, paste the text into the editor and enter the correct store address.
    6. Click Publish (make it live) and confirm the status shows the policy as saved.
    7. Repeat steps 1 to 6 for the shipping policy and add your international shipping rates.
    8. Set up return rules (see the Manage return rules setting). Return rules decide which returns you accept.
    9. Review the Customer privacy settings.
    10. Buy or connect your domain (your store's web address).
    11. Check which apps you actually need before installing any. Apps are add-on tools.
  • Settings > Policies > Shipping policy shows 'No policy set' until you publish one.
    • The editor has a required text field.
    • It has formatting options (Paragraph, menu) and a Publish button.
    • After saving, Shopify confirms with a 'Store policy saved' message.
  • Forgetting to enter the correct store address in the refund policy before publishing is a mistake.
    • The address required is the return address customers should use.
  • Publishing generated Shopify policies without reading them is a mistake.
    • A generic policy (one not written for your store) may not match your refund rules, shipping terms or special conditions.
    • Each policy must be checked and adjusted.
  • Shopify auto-generated policies still need manual additions. (Newer, 2025.)
    • Add the recommended policy changes to the auto-generated templates.
  • Shopify policy templates are not legal advice (advice from a lawyer).
    • By using them you agree to the disclaimer shown on the page. A disclaimer is a warning note that limits what the text promises.
    • Replace the sample store and business details with your own before publishing.
    • Have the wording checked where your legal requirements apply.
  • Until supplier terms are known, a shipping and return policy can be made from a generic template.
    • Once suppliers are approved, update the policy to include supplier-specific exclusions.
    • Exclusions are products the rule does not cover.
  • A store must not look like an unchanged template. (Newer, 2023.)
    • Privacy policy, return policy and other pages must be rewritten for the actual business.
  • Include the company name and at least an email address on the refund and shipping policies.
    • A missing contact is seen as unprofessional.
    • The privacy policy and terms of service are secondary (less important here).
  • Check that the email address and phone number in each policy are correct.
    • Open the refund policy, privacy policy, terms of service and shipping policy under Settings (bottom left) > Policies to review and edit them.

Policy text rules

1 min
  • Template text for pages is a starting point. **
    • It can be used as is at launch (the day the store goes live).
    • Once the business is running, rewrite the pages in the owner's own voice.
    • Use the real details of the business and products.
  • Templates for content pages are only a starting point.
    • Customize them to your own store before publishing.
    • Guarantee and policy pages must reflect the terms you actually offer and the policies of each supplier.
  • Any template for the About Us text is only a starting point.
    • This includes prebuilt general templates and text written from scratch.
    • Edit it to match the real business before publishing.
  • When filling an AI prompt (instruction text for an AI tool) for an About Us or policy page, change only the input fields at the top.
    • The instruction section at the bottom is written for the AI. It does not need editing.
  • Creating an About Us page with the AI prompt:
    1. In Shopify Admin, go to Online Store > Pages and create a new page titled "About Us".
    2. Locate the About Us AI Prompt and copy the section beginning with "You are a professional e-commerce brand copywriter".
    3. Complete the Student Inputs fields (the fill-in boxes of the prompt): brand name, website domain, primary niche, target customers, brand personality, business location, store differentiators, customer service philosophy, shipping and fulfillment approach, and optionally your founder story.
    4. Generate the page copy.
    5. Review the output and make edits as needed.
    6. Insert the final text into Shopify, save the page, and preview it (look at it before it goes live).
  • Setting up all six basic Shopify pages with AI prompts and templates took about 15 to 16 minutes.
    • Treat this as a first-pass benchmark (a rough reference time).
    • Reviewing each generated text against your real business terms takes additional time.

Supplier-specific policies

1 min
  • Handling policies from multiple suppliers (2025):
    1. Add warranty information (a promise to repair or replace) as collapsible sections (boxes that open and close) on product pages for suppliers with unique warranties.
    2. Modify shipping text on product pages for suppliers with different shipping timeframes. Example: 'This product ships in 2-3 days via freight' (heavy shipping by truck).
    3. Create a general return policy for most suppliers.
    4. On the return policy page, add exceptions at the bottom. List the specific brands with different policies. Example: brand A has 90-day returns while most have 30 days.
    5. On individual product pages, highlight any unique benefits. Example: a longer return window.
  • Set up the shipping and return policy with one main policy that fits most products. **
    • Then list exclusions in that policy.
    • For a product that cannot be returned, add a shipping policy tab (a clickable section) on that product page. The tab states it is not eligible for returns.
  • Assigning a supplier-specific return policy to individual products (2024):
    1. Open the theme editor (where you change store design) and go to the Products templates.
    2. Create a new template based on the default product template. Name it after the policy, add a section stating the policy, and save.
    3. Open each affected product, change its theme template from default to the new template, and save.
  • If you mix MAP and non-MAP suppliers, remove the price match guarantee from the footer. **
    • MAP means minimum advertised price, the lowest price a brand allows sellers to advertise.
    • The price match guarantee can stay on individual product pages for MAP-eligible items only.
  • Example: the price match promise on the homepage was shown with free shipping across the country and easy returns ('don't need it? send it back'). **

Contact Us page

1 min
  • A new Shopify store starts with one page already present: a Contact page that uses the default template named Contact.
    • It includes Shopify's built-in contact form.
    • Each submission arrives by email with the sender's message.
  • Leave the default Contact template so customers can submit messages directly to your email.
  • Conflict on the Contact page source (two sources disagree).
    • One view says the automatic Contact page should be renamed when creating the Contact Us page (see the table above).
    • Another says to create it under Add page with the Contact template.
    • Use the automatic page if it exists.
    • Create one with the Contact template only if it does not.
  • Contact page: in Shopify admin, go to Pages > Contact us.
    • Type the phone number, email address and business hours into the page text, then save.
    • The text appears above the contact form.
  • Keep the contact form.
  • Add at least two more contact methods.
    • Examples: hours of operation (for example, 9:00 AM through 5:00 PM Eastern), a real phone number, and an email address or live chat (a chat box where customers type to you live).
    • Save the page afterwards.
  • The contact page must offer at least three ways to reach you: an email address, a contact form and a phone number. **
  • The Contact information policy is completed later, not during initial setup.

About Us page

1 min
  • A new store can begin its About Us text from one of several prebuilt general templates.
    • Or you can write it from scratch.
    • Any template is only a starting point and must be edited before publishing.
  • Include the brand story (why the business exists) and remove meta placeholders from the copy.

FAQ page

1 min
  • Build an FAQ page without an app (an add-on tool):
    1. Create a new page in the Shopify admin titled Frequently Asked Questions. FAQ means frequently asked questions.
    2. Type each question as a headline.
    3. Type the answer below it in normal body text.
    4. Add images only if needed. Fewer apps is better.
  • FAQ content should answer questions your own customers ask about the niche, not generic e-commerce questions. **
    • Add an entry only when a niche-specific question keeps coming up.
  • Include an FAQ section if the product solves a problem.
  • Display a 'Try risk-free' or '30-day money-back guarantee' prominently on the store.

Shipping and delivery pages

1 min
  • Create a shipping and delivery page with accurate policy details.
  • Then create an FAQ page if needed.
  • Show the shipping message 'free shipping from a warehouse close to the customer' as a trust badge (a small message or logo that builds confidence).
    • In the example, the warehouse is in Spain.
    • Place it near the buy area (the part with the buy button).
    • It reduces doubt about delivery time and cost.
  • Handling a damaged delivery, to be stated on the shipping page: **
    1. Ask the customer to inspect the packaging when the parcel arrives.
    2. If there is visible damage, note it on the delivery signature or refuse delivery.
    3. If the customer accepts the delivery and finds damage, photograph the damaged packaging or the products inside immediately with a phone or camera.
    4. Email the photos to the store's support address.
    5. Lodge a return claim (a formal request to send the item back) online through the Returns page.
  • Show shipping carrier logos only for carriers you ship with yourself or that your suppliers use.
    • A carrier is a delivery company.
    • Well-known carriers such as FedEx, UPS or USPS build recognition and trust.
  • To show carrier logos on product pages, place them in the Shipping section (tab) of the product page.
    • Use the text 'We proudly ship with'.
    • Click the image icon (the picture button) in that section and choose the file from your computer.

Privacy and customer privacy settings

1 min
  • Include a privacy policy on the site. **
    • Make sure it is complete before setting up Google Merchant Center.
    • Google Merchant Center is Google's tool that sends your products to Google shopping results.
  • Settings > Customer privacy:
    • Publish the privacy policy on the online store.
    • Set Data sharing opt-out to the recommended setting. Opt-out means you say no to sharing customer data.
    • Keep Shopify Network Intelligence enabled as the default. It is a Shopify data feature.
    • Set Data storage hosting location to United States for a US-focused store. This is the country where customer data is stored.
  • Privacy and terms pages: the policy HTML and layout are covered in the code unit below.
    • HTML is the code behind a web page.

Policy page layout and HTML

1 min
  • Policy pages are text pages such as contact, shipping and privacy.
  • Keep them in a narrow centred column, not across the full width of a large screen.
  • Readers scan a narrow column with short eye movements, as in a book.
  • Do not spend effort making these pages full width. **
  • This policy HTML (HTML is the code that builds web pages) uses one wrapper (an outer box that holds everything).
  • It also uses one block per section (one box for each section).
  • The same inline styles (formatting written directly on each tag) are repeated for each section.
  • Keep the same structure when you paste the policy HTML into the Shopify policy editor (Shopify's tool for writing policy pages).

Trust badges, guarantees and checkout banner

3 min
  • Trust badges are small logos that show buyers your store is safe.
  • Where you place them depends on your store. Use the rule that matches your situation:
    • Footer (the bottom of every page), added through code: open the theme editor (the tool for editing your store's design template), and edit the base code of the footer (its main code file).
    • Insert the trust badges into the footer code.
    • If you do not know how to edit code, hire a developer on Upwork (a freelance website) for a small job. It takes about 20 minutes.
    • Below the add-to-cart button: not used. Only badges that come with the theme by default in the footer are kept. (Newer, 2023.)
    • Product page: trust badges such as authorized retailer, free shipping and product warranty should appear on the product page itself.
    • Place them in the description, specifications (the product details list) or warranty section, and in the shipping tab.
    • Near the buy area (the shipping message, see Shipping section above) and on the landing page (a single page built for ad visitors) or product page. Many more elements can be added there.
  • Trust elements make buyers and suppliers feel safe.
  • Add clear branding (your store's look and name), free shipping, easy returns and a visible contact number before the store goes live. **
  • Shopify's checkout (the payment step) is very limited.
  • Most apps (add-on tools) cannot be added there.
  • The only practical change is an image in the logo area.
  • Put additional trust badges, reviews and guarantees on the landing page or product page instead.
  • Checkout trust banner in Canva (an online design tool) and Shopify:
    1. Open Canva, choose Create a design, and select Custom size.
    2. Enter 1500 x 300 pixels (pixels are the tiny dots that make up an image).
    3. Add the store logo at the top or on the left.
    4. Search the Elements section (Canva's library of icons and shapes) for 'privacy protected'. Add the matching icon, plus a secure-checkout or satisfaction-guarantee icon.
    5. Below the icons, add payment icons such as Visa, Mastercard and PayPal. Optionally, add a 30-day money-back guarantee.
    6. Export the design as an image (save it as a picture file).
    7. In Shopify, go to Settings > Checkout, search for checkout, and upload the image to the checkout image field.
    8. Other checkout parts cannot be edited this way.
  • Alternative checkout logo procedure:
    1. Open Canva and create a custom design at 1500 x 300 pixels.
    2. Place your logo on the left side.
    3. Search Canva elements for 'privacy protected' and place the matching icon on the right side.
    4. Add further trust icons such as secure checkout or satisfaction guaranteed.
    5. Download the image.
    6. In Shopify, go to Settings > Checkout > Checkout settings and upload the image in place of the logo.
  • To add trust icons to checkout, upload a custom image into the logo area under Settings > Checkout > Checkout settings.
  • Create the image at 1500 x 300 pixels.
  • Place the logo on the left and the trust icons on the right.
  • Upload it in place of the logo.
  • Payment options must be shown in the footer and on product pages.
  • A merchant account (the account that takes card payments) was flagged for not showing them clearly. **
  • Place your policies where they are easy to find. This includes the privacy policy, shipping and returns policies, and other required policies.
  • Some suspended merchant accounts (accounts that were blocked) were reinstated. This happened after the policy link was moved into the top menu under About us. **
  • Case: a store had an add-to-cart rate (the share of visitors who click "add to cart") of about 8%.
  • But its conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy) was only 0.29%.
  • The checkout was plain and unbranded.
  • Trust elements were added to the checkout: privacy and secure-checkout badges, a satisfaction guarantee and payment icons.
  • The shipping text was also changed.
  • The conversion rate rose from 0.29% to 2.15% in one day.

Authorized retailer, brand and charity claims

1 min
  • If you sell well-known suppliers, highlight the brand partnership on the site.
  • Also show your authorized retailer status (official approved seller).
  • Include a 'Shop by Brand' navigation section (a menu section listing brands) to catch customers who search for those brand names. **
  • Create an authorized retailer badge: use the supplier's logo with the text 'authorized retailer' below it.
  • It can be built in Canva, which has a free version. **
  • Authorized retailer badges use the same font, size, colour and shape throughout one store.
  • The styling can differ between stores. It must stay consistent within one store.
  • Brand or retailer logos and badges should show only the authorization or certification you actually hold.
  • Remove any claims that cannot be verified.
  • Charity claims need written permission from the charity.
  • Many large charities restrict who may claim to represent or donate to them.

Copied content and claim risk

1 min
  • Copying a winning brand's sentences word for word can get your ads rejected (the ad platform refuses them). It also creates legal risk.
  • The same goes for keeping its health claims, such as blood flow or circulation promises.
  • Rewrite all claims for your own product.
  • Check the claim rules of your market before launch.
  • Handling copied store content:
    1. Collect the link to the copying store and the link to your original page.
    2. Submit a DMCA takedown (a legal request to remove copied content) to the hosting platform, such as Shopify.
    3. Confirm that the claim is true. A false claim can expose you to legal liability (being held responsible in court).
    4. Platforms usually remove a verified copy quickly and do not restore it. **

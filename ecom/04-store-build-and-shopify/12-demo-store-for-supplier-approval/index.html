Demo store for supplier approval

Section 4.12 · 12 of 20 in this chapter · 35 min read

In short

  • A demo store is a test shop with stand-in products. Build it before you contact any supplier.
  • Keep it small: about 5 demo products is the target. 10 to 20 is the upper limit.
  • Use free photos labeled for reuse. Never copy supplier or competitor photos before approval.
  • Use your own domain name (your own web address). Never use a store-name.myshopify.com address in outreach.
  • When the store goes live, replace every demo product with an approved supplier product.
More key points
  • Demo products are sample items for showing suppliers. They are never for sale.
  • Use your own domain name (your own web address), not the free Shopify address.
  • Remove the password and keep payments off until real supplier products are ready.
  • Replace demo products with real supplier products as soon as the first supplier approves.

What a supplier-ready demo store is

5 min

  • A demo store is a fully working Shopify (online shop software) storefront (the shop front customers see) with placeholder (stand-in) products. It is "supplier-ready," meaning ready for brands to approve. It is not connected to approved suppliers and does not accept real customer orders. It becomes a live store later, when the demo products are replaced with approved supplier products.

  • It has a complete layout and design, filled with a small number of representative (typical) demo products. Suppliers review it to picture how their own products would look before they approve real uploads.

  • It is built to be supplier-ready. It looks and behaves like a live store (a store open for real sales). The finished design is used to show brands when applying to become an authorized retailer (a store a brand allows to sell its products).

  • It should be built exactly like the permanent store, with the same design and pages. The only difference is that the real products stay unpublished (hidden from customers) until the supplier approves.

  • The store built during setup is the same store that will later take real sales, not a separate test site. Only the content differs, because no suppliers or real products exist yet.

  • Before suppliers are contacted, fill the store with demo products instead of real supplier products. The store should look as it will when it sells, so suppliers can picture their own products on it.

  • Examples: ShakaSurfboards.com (surf niche) and Happy Paws (pet supply niche) are demo stores built to a supplier-ready standard. A niche is one specific product category. Both are design showcases that resemble a real store.

  • A supplier-facing store should look exactly like a live store. The only difference is that the products are demo items. It must not look half-built.

  • Suppliers review the store before approval, so it should look complete even with only a few products. It should show what the store will look like once the supplier's products are uploaded.

  • Suppliers apply the same doubt a future customer would. A store that looks untrustworthy fails both audiences. Trust signals (things that make a shop look safe and real) are therefore the core criteria (the main tests).

  • Suppliers judge a retailer partly on how the store looks, so store quality matters before the first sale. A professional look raises the chance of approval.

  • The store should be lean (small and simple) but credible (believable). Use sample collections (groups of products, like categories) and sample products that match the target niche. Keep it clean, not a large store with many pages or products.

  • Build it lean but good-looking, without many extra content pages or a large product upload. Add the extras after vendor approval. A vendor is the brand or supplier. The goal before approval is a credible, functional store, not a finished one.

  • Minimum set for credibility: a working logo, a favicon (small icon in the browser tab), colors, fonts, a single slide (one picture in the homepage slideshow), a few collections, a pre-footer (signup area above the page bottom) and an email signup.

  • Postpone (leave for later): announcement bars (thin banners at the top of the page), mega menu details (large drop-down menu settings), blog content, buyer guides and advanced product card settings (options for the box each product appears in).

  • The store must look relevant to its niche before outreach. Add products from the chosen niche, even placeholders, so the brand sees a store that matches its category.

  • Use the niche-specific demo products and stock sources described below.

  • Build a new demo store for each niche, even if you already own other stores. Mention your existing experience when contacting suppliers, and say the new store goes live on a specific date.

  • A demo store can stay small while suppliers are contacted, for example three collections with three products each. It does not need rebuilding when the niche changes.

  • Finish the build first, to the final look, before contacting suppliers. Then start outreach (contacting suppliers). That way suppliers can picture how their products will appear.

  • Timing: the store is built before suppliers are contacted. Demo products are uploaded so suppliers can review it during approval.

  • Before applying to silver suppliers (a supplier tier), build the demo store so the supplier can see a working, professional storefront when it reviews your application.

  • Have a live, high-quality demo store before contacting suppliers, so they can see how their products would look on your site.

  • Supplier outreach comes only after the supplier-ready demo store is complete.

  • At the demo stage, no real products are uploaded and no supplier approval is needed yet. Demo products fill the catalog (the full list of products) so the store looks realistic. They are replaced by live products when the store goes live.

  • Loading real products before a supplier has approved you is outside the demo stage. Use demo products in the catalog instead.

  • Store build order, with the product check first:

    1. Build the store with Elixir (a ready-made Shopify design) by following the step-by-step store build guide.
    2. Optimize the product page: images, copy (written text), urgency elements (things that push a buyer to act now) and trust badges (small logos that show safety or quality).
    3. Keep the design clean and trustworthy.
    4. Submit the store for review via a ticket (a request sent to support) before it goes live.

  • Conflict on sequencing: one source says not to build a store until a product has been approved. Building early wastes time on products that may not pass review.

  • Two other sources assume the product choice is already made. They say to build the demo store before supplier outreach.

    • Applies: validate the product first (check it). Once the product line is chosen, build the demo store before contacting suppliers.

  • Store domain: the store must have its own domain name (your own web address). Suppliers see this as a sign of professionalism and an established business.

  • Shop name and website domain: a Shopify subdomain (a shared address ending in.myshopify.com), such as store-name.myshopify.com, in outreach or on the store makes the business look unprofessional and untrustworthy.

Descriptions

  • Demo product example: name Aloha Blue Longboard Surfboard, weight 13 pounds, with description, key features, and specifications filled in from a provided template.

  • A one- to two-line description lets the supplier picture its product on the site. Do not optimize for sales or conversions. Older (2018).
  • Demo products do not need materials, dimensions, technical specifications, or detailed descriptions, since customers cannot buy them. Generic product descriptions are sufficient. Older (2020).
  • Mock product descriptions and images can be taken from images labeled for reuse in Google image search, combined with basic descriptions. Older (2020).
  • Supplier product descriptions may be used for demo or test products only if they come directly from the brand.
  • Search a sentence from the description in Google. If it appears on many sites, it is the brand's stock text (text many sites reuse). Older (2019).

Procedure: Demo product with AI prompts

  11. Specify the product type (such as Surfboards).

Setting up demo products in Shopify

5 min

Shopify is the online shop software you use to build your store. A demo product is a sample item. You use it to show suppliers how their products will look. It is not for sale.

Upload procedure

Procedure: Uploading a demo product to Shopify

  1. Go to Products, then click Add products.
  2. Enter the product title (the name).
  3. Paste the product description from the template (a ready-made text) and change it to fit.
  4. Select the product category from the dropdown (a list you open with a click).
  5. Enter the product type (a label that groups similar products).
  6. Enter the vendor (the brand or seller name). Your store name is fine for demos.
  7. Add tags (short labels). Tags fill collections automatically. A collection is a group of products shown together.
  8. Drag the product image into the media section (the photo area).
  9. Enter the selling price, the compare-at price (the old price, shown crossed out), and the cost per item (what you pay).
  10. Leave inventory tracking unchecked. Inventory means your stock count. Only check it if you track stock.
  11. Enter the SKU if provided. A SKU is your own stock code for the item.
  12. Enter the product weight.
  13. Skip variants for demos. Variants are versions of one product, such as sizes or colours.
  14. Click Save.

Procedure: Competitor-copy route, Source B A competitor is another shop that sells similar products.

  1. Open the source product page (the original product page) on the competitor site.
  2. Copy the title and paste it into the Title field in Shopify.
  3. Copy the description and specifications and paste them into the Description field.
  4. Save the source images, using no more than two per product. Upload them with Add file under Media.
  5. Enter the price, and optionally the compare-at price.
  6. Set the product type, vendor, tags and status. Tick Online Store.
  7. Set the quantity. Tick the physical product box (a physical product is an item you ship), then save.

Conflict: this route copies images from a competitor. and forbid this. See the legal rules above.

Mistakes and checks

  • A product with status Draft is not shown on the storefront. A product with Active status but without the Online Store box ticked is not shown either. Check both before expecting a product to appear.
  • A product with a quantity of zero and tracking on displays as out of stock. Always set a quantity when Track quantity is ticked.
  • Use Preview in Shopify to see how a saved product appears on the site. Before the site is designed, the page can look unfinished. The preview still confirms that the product data has loaded.
  • Do not assign demo products to collections by hand. Use the automated, tag-based collections (collections that fill themselves from tags) created earlier in store setup.

Theme settings for demo products

A theme is the design package of your store. The theme editor is where you change its look.

  • Skip customizing pages and blog templates in the Shopify theme editor. A blog is a news or article section. This can wait until the store is live and needs more individual content.
  • For a basic demo store, only the sticky content and media settings in the Product information section need changes. Sticky content stays on screen while the visitor scrolls. The other settings can stay at their defaults until the store is ready for live products.
  • Hide promotional elements and coupons. Promotional elements are ads or sale banners. Coupons are discount codes. Keep product pages clean and simple, with a professional layout, clean navigation and functional pages.
  • Hide features that are not yet needed, such as discount codes and inventory levels. At the demo stage, all links should work and every function should behave correctly.
  • Minimum theme elements: see the minimum set in the first section. Postpone these: announcement bars (thin banners at the top of the page), mega menu details (large dropdown menus) and advanced product card settings (the box showing one product in a list).

Pre-launch checklist before supplier outreach

3 min

Outreach means contacting suppliers. A supplier is a company that makes or sells the products you resell.

Procedure: Supplier-ready store checklist, in order

  1. Buy a domain name in Shopify and connect it to the store. A domain name is your web address, for example yourstore.com.
  2. Remove the demo-store notification bar at the top of the site. This is the bar that says it is a demo.
  3. Keep the product count small. Three to five demo products is enough. More is unnecessary.
  4. Replace every generic or fake phone number with a real phone number, or remove the phone number entirely.
  5. Delete unneeded default content, such as the empty blog that Shopify creates. A blog is not required when contacting suppliers.
  6. Open the About Us, Shipping and Guarantee (guarantee/returns) pages. Fill in all text so it reads complete, and click every link to confirm it works.
  7. Click through the site the way a supplier would. Check that it looks presentable and finished.

Procedure: Pre-outreach check, 2023

  1. Is live at your final domain, not Shopify's default URL (the free web address Shopify gives you).
  2. Has all internal links working. Internal links are links between your own pages.
  3. Does not contain broken pages or placeholder content. Broken pages show an error. Placeholder content is fill-in text that is not final.
  4. Has social media links pointing to actual accounts, not default Shopify links.
  5. Looks complete and professional, as suppliers judge your credibility by the quality of the site.

Procedure: Pre-outreach check, 2023

  1. Attach your own domain name, not a myshopify.com address.
  2. Remove all blank image placeholders (empty picture boxes).
  3. Make sure the contact page has a working contact form (a form visitors fill in to send you a message).
  4. Five to 10 products is enough, and checkout does not need to be enabled. Checkout is the step where customers pay.
  5. Remove the store password.

Rules for the checklist:

  • Your store must have its own domain name.
  • Display an actual phone number prominently. Suppliers see a real, reachable phone number as proof that you run a legitimate, accountable business.
  • Replace the generic phone number with a real one, or remove it entirely.
  • All links on the store must work. Broken links suggest neglect and weaken supplier confidence.
  • The store should look presentable and appear exactly as it will when the site goes live. Do not show it to suppliers while it is under construction or contains placeholder content.
  • Pre-launch quality: it must look like a real, working store, with no test pages, no dead links and no spelling errors. It must be trustworthy enough that a customer would buy from it.
  • Before calling suppliers, add a price guarantee page and a privacy policy page (a page explaining how you handle customer data). This takes about 15 to 20 minutes using ready-made scripts (ready-made texts) or a generator (a tool that writes the text for you). Older (2019).
  • Fill the About Us page and the shipping and returns policy page.
  • Mistakes that signal the store is not real: a demo-store notification bar, a generic fake phone number, or unfinished default pages such as a placeholder blog.
  • Mistake: using a Shopify subdomain, such as store-name.myshopify.com, in outreach or on the live store. A subdomain is a free address under Shopify's name.
  • Costs for the checklist items: a domain name costs between 5 and 20 USD.

Build-before-contact procedure, Source B

  1. Set up the store on Shopify. Shopify hosts the store (keeps it online) on its own software, so no custom platform is needed.
  2. Add branding, a logo and basic design, so the site looks like a real e-commerce store. Branding means your name, logo, colours and look. E-commerce means selling online.
  3. Make it a demo website. It does not have to be 100 percent complete, but it must look solid when you show it to suppliers.
  4. Use the demo site when you contact suppliers to show that the business is serious.
  5. After approval, upload the supplier's products with their data, pricing and images. Then optimize (improve) the products and the website.

Password and sales during the demo stage

2 min

Remove the storefront password

The storefront is the public shop side that customers see. The storefront password is a lock page that hides the store from visitors.

  • A new Shopify store is password-protected by default. Remove the password in the Online Store preferences before suppliers review the store.
  • Remove the Shopify storefront password before contacting suppliers. A live store, which is online and open to visitors, makes the business look real and saves suppliers an extra step.
  • Keep the store password off during supplier onboarding. Onboarding means signing suppliers up. Suppliers and pending suppliers (suppliers waiting for approval) often check the site, and a password blocks them.
  • A store with the password page still on looks unfinished to suppliers who visit the URL (web address). Disable the password before sending the URL. The store can be live but not promoted at this stage. There are no ads or search visibility (showing up in Google) yet, and checkout is not connected.

Keep real sales off

  • The store can be made live with demo products before real products are uploaded. Nobody will find it without marketing (advertising and promotion), and checkout is not connected, so no one can buy the demo products. Connect checkout only after real supplier products are uploaded.
  • Demo products are only for showing suppliers how their products will look. They are never sold or marketed. They must be deleted from the account before the store goes live to customers.
  • Demo products left live can be pulled into the product feed (the product list sent to shopping sites), cause feed errors, and let customers check out for items you cannot sell. Delete all demo products before launch.
  • Shopify payment setting: go to Settings > Payments and disable all payment options so no customer can buy. Alternatively, go to Settings > Shipping and delivery and select no shipping zones (the countries or areas you ship to), so no shipping option can be chosen.
  • To stop the store from taking orders, leave every payment method inactive so checkout cannot be completed. Or set the inventory of the products to zero. Older (2021).
  • Turn off or leave unlinked the payment gateways in the Shopify admin (the back office where you manage the store), so no real orders can be placed. A payment gateway is the link to the service that takes card payments. Older (2019).
  • A payment processor (the service that handles card payments) does not need to be set up before supplier calls, because no sales are being taken yet. Older (2019).
  • Conflict on zero inventory: says set quantity above zero for demo products, because zero shows the product as out of stock. says set inventory to zero to stop orders. Applies: keep quantity above zero when the product should show as available. Use zero or inactive payments when orders must be blocked.

Procedure: Launch the store before suppliers are ready, 2025

  1. Remove password from store (make it accessible).
  2. Disable all payment methods so customers cannot complete purchases.
  3. Add demo products to show to potential suppliers.
  4. Keep demo products for the first week while contacting suppliers.
  5. Once the first supplier is approved, upload their products.
  6. Remove demo products.
  7. Continue adding supplier products.

Done-for-you build option

2 min

A done-for-you service means someone else builds the store for you, for a fee.

Two routes to a supplier-ready demo store

  • There are two routes. You can build the demo store yourself, step by step. Or you can outsource the web design (have someone else design it) to a done-for-you service. The done-for-you service delivers a finished store that the buyer owns 100 percent.
  • The done-for-you web design service is a paid upgrade. Buyers who already bought it submit the web design form. Buyers who have not bought it can sign up through an information form to add it to their account.
  • Choosing the done-for-you service lets you skip the self-build steps for the demo store and move straight to contacting suppliers. Choosing to build it yourself means working through every design step first. Supplier outreach starts only after that step is complete.
  • The Ultimate Upgrade done-for-you store includes professional setup of these accounts: Google Search Console (a Google tool showing how Google sees your site), Google Merchant Center (a Google tool that lists your products), Google Analytics (visitor tracking), and Google Ads (paid advertising). The standard done-for-you store does not include these setups.
  • A custom store build has typically taken three to five business days. Reply to the delivery email with any edit requests, since most owners are satisfied on day one.

Website checks before supplier outreach

1 min

What the store must look like before suppliers see it

  • The store must look complete before suppliers see it. That means no placeholder phone numbers or emails, no filler or instructional text, and every link must work.
  • Real phone numbers and emails are not required yet. They are added before contacting suppliers.
  • Store branding (colors, color scheme, business name) must be configured in Shopify before any supplier contact. Finish branding first so the store can be shown as complete.

Checking the store in order

  1. Open your store and go through every page. Look for typos, broken links, placeholder phone numbers or emails, and filler text such as instructional placeholder sentences.
  2. Click every link on the store and confirm each one works as intended.
  3. Send the store link to a friend or family member. Ask them to browse, and collect any feedback on things you missed.
  4. Fix everything they flag, so the site looks complete.
  5. In Shopify Admin, go to Online Store > Themes and click Remove Password, so the store is publicly reachable.

Why an outside tester is needed

  • After long work on a store, the builder tends to overlook obvious errors. An outside person testing the site with fresh eyes helps catch them.

More rules worth knowing

  • Demo stores only need to look realistic for a supplier review. They do not need to prove access to the products. Products can use free stock images and simple descriptions.

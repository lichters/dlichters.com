Product page design and templates

Section 4.14 · 14 of 20 in this chapter · 96 min read

In short

  • Put a Buy now or Shop now button right under the product, and treat every product page as a sales page.
  • Launch a simple page first, then improve it from customer feedback.
  • Check the mobile (phone) version of each page separately, and show pictures and the buy button first.
  • Use Heading 6 to turn long text into boxes that open and close.
  • Before pasting code from a PDF, fix the broken quote marks and symbols.
More key points
  • Keep a product page live when stock runs out. Do not delete it.
  • Give each product its own page template. Otherwise, one change spreads to every product that shares it.
  • Test two page designs with the same ad time before you pick one.
  • Keep Add to Cart and Direct Buy as two separate buttons, so customers can choose.
  • Keep each product page simple: title, short benefit bullets, price, and a few proven blocks.
  • Check every product page on a phone as well as a computer before publishing.
  • Write your own product text, and re-check every number against the live price.
  • Test each change in the saved preview, then reload the live page to confirm it.
  • Use Heading 6 to make a section collapsible (it opens and closes when clicked).
  • Most buyers see only the product page, so give it the most care.
  • Show reviews, FAQs and clear product facts so buyers trust the page.
  • Say clearly when a product needs assembly, is on back order, is pre-order, or was discontinued.
  • Type names exactly, because one wrong letter breaks the link.
  • Keep sold-out products on the site: set the stock to zero, and never delete them.
  • Give buyers one clear main button, and make sure it shows on mobile too.
  • Build one page, then duplicate it for each new product.
  • Check every screen size after each change.
  • Keep unfinished pages as drafts until the layout is done.

Product page structure and conversion layout

3 min
  • Build the product page in this order:
    1. Product title and images.
    2. A Buy now or Shop now button, placed directly under the product, with an Add to cart button above it.
    3. Bundles (several items sold together as one deal), upsells (offers of a better or extra item) and urgency elements (things that push the buyer to act now, such as a countdown).
    4. A short description with bullet points: more images than text, and simple words.
    5. Customer reviews.
    6. The footer (the bottom strip of the page).
  • Customers land on product pages, not on separate landing pages (a landing page is the first page a visitor sees after a click).
  • So optimise each product page as the main landing page, and build it to convert traffic directly (turn visitors into buyers).
  • Once ads are running, send traffic to the product page, not the homepage (traffic means visitors).
  • Reason: visitors can see the product and buy it immediately.
  • For a quick test, a minimal viable landing page is enough (the simplest working version of a page).
  • Get the page live and tested first.
  • Improve it from customer feedback after launch.
  • Do not aim for a perfect design first.
  • Shopify's Dawn theme (a free Shopify store design) includes an instant add-to-cart button for collection pages (pages that list several products).
  • Enable it in the theme settings (the controls of the store's design).
  • No app is needed (an app is an add-on tool).
  • Place the satisfaction guarantee (a promise to refund or replace if the buyer is not happy) high on the page, right after the key product information.
  • Reason: it lowers purchase risk (the chance the buyer regrets buying) early.
  • Visitors who are close to buying often ask: what if the product disappoints me?
  • Show scented or sensory products (products you smell, touch or taste) in a setting that suggests the experience, such as on wood or among natural elements.
  • Use sharp product images that show the product in use.
  • Use tabs (switchable sections under headings) to shrink long information, instead of forcing a long scroll (moving down the page).
  • Keep purchase options, reviews and key facts easy to reach from the top.
  • Add a question section below the reviews.
  • Keep the brand answering questions.
  • Reason: it shows active support and gives visitors answers without leaving the page.
  • Show the offer clearly:
    • Product price.
    • Local shipping and delivery time.
    • Bundle deals.
  • State that stock is held in-country (kept in the same country as the buyer).
  • Competitor copying (copying a rival store's page):
    • Copying a competitor's product page exactly can beat a heavily edited version in some stores.
    • It can bring a higher CTR (click-through rate: the share of people who click) and a lower CPM (cost per 1,000 views of an ad).
    • In one case, extra editing and polish reduced sales.
    • Results vary by product.
  • Test each added feature separately to see whether it increases sales.
    • Examples: a sticky add-to-cart bar (a bar that stays on screen while you scroll), a gift option, or colour swatches (small colour dots to pick a version).
  • Check these features when comparing themes.
  • Include product identifiers in a dedicated section (its own separate block).
    • Identifiers are codes that name the item, such as SKU (the store's own product code), UPC (Universal Product Code: the barcode number) or part number.
    • Reason: customers can search for the item by part number.
  • For fashion stores, add a size guide.
  • Size problems were a likely cause of a chargeback (the buyer asks their bank to reverse the payment) on one fashion store, even though a size guide was present.

Mobile product pages

1 min
  • On phones (mobile screens), a long block of text before the product image hurts the first impression (what a visitor thinks in the first seconds).
  • Lead with visuals and the buy action, not paragraphs of details.
  • Mobile needs an adaptive layout (a layout that rearranges content for each screen size).
  • It should not be a shrunk desktop page (the computer version, made smaller).
  • Keep the product name, what it does or smells like, testimonials (customer quotes) and the quantity selector (the box to pick how many) above the fold (the part visible before scrolling).
  • Check the mobile version separately from desktop (the computer version).
  • Sections, images and paragraphs may show differently or be missing on mobile.
  • Cross-check (compare both versions) and add missing elements to the mobile layout.
  • PageFly (a page builder app: a tool for building page layouts without code).
  • Check each section on both versions when a PageFly product page differs between desktop and mobile.
  • Where layouts differ, apply the same customisation on mobile by selecting the image and pasting the text.
  • Layout options in the Shopify theme editor (the tool that changes how the store looks), older, Source B 2021-22:
    • 3 columns.
    • 2 columns with medium image.
    • A further 2-column option.
  • The 3-column layout shows as several columns on desktop only.
  • Mobile always stacks into one column (items sit one under the other).
  • A two-column layout with a medium image is the traditional e-commerce layout (the usual online shop look) and a sensible default.

Headline, offer and trust elements

1 min
  • Standard Shopify product page:
    • The product title stays the H1 (the page's main heading) for SEO (search engine optimisation: getting found on Google).
    • The product title sits at the top, with price and image.
    • A headline placed above the product description becomes the H2 (a second-level heading).
    • The H2 must not replace the product title.
  • Traditional sales page (older, Source B 2021-22: a long page that persuades step by step):
    • The headline is the H1 and the first thing a visitor sees.
    • The price usually comes at the end of the sales message on this page type.
  • Landing pages need a strong offer.
  • They also need several trust elements (things that build confidence) such as reviews and guarantees.
  • They need persuasive copywriting (words that push a reader to buy).
  • The source mentions five key elements but does not list them.
  • Free gift on a product page:
    1. Lead the product description with text about the product, because search engines read the opening text.
    2. Below the main text, embed (place inside the page) a large image that says the gift is free, shows the gift and states its value.
    3. The image should make clear the customer receives the gift free when ordering from the store.
  • Product-page graphics, such as feature icons (small symbols that show key points), can be made by a designer.
  • They can be hard-coded (written directly into the site's code) instead of installing an app.
  • Back up the code (save a copy) before editing, or hire a developer on Upwork (an online freelancer marketplace) to do it.
  • Duplicated description text (the same text appears in the description and the specifications section):
    • Contact a Shopify developer (a programmer who works on Shopify stores).
    • Find developers certified by Shopify on Fiverr.com or StoreTasker.com (freelancer sites).
    • Check spam folders (junk mail) for replies.

Product information settings (theme editor)

4 min
  • Product Information block (one box of content on the page), recommended settings:
    • Enable sticky content on desktop: On.
    • Desktop media width: Medium. Media means images and video.
    • Constrain media to screen height: On (Original). Constrain means limit the size so the pictures fit the screen.
    • Advanced settings are not needed at this stage.
  • Product Information subsection settings:
    • Variant Picker: Pills (buttons shaped like pills that pick a variant). A variant is a version of a product, such as a size or colour.
    • Quantity Selector: Enabled.
    • Sharing buttons: Enabled.
    • Description placement: Next to Add to Cart button.
    • Buy Buttons: Show dynamic checkout buttons: On (express buttons that let buyers pay quickly).
    • SKU label: "SKU"
  • Price on product pages cannot be hidden.
  • When a Compare-at price (the old, higher price) is set, it shows automatically, crossed out, to show the discount.
  • The product title is pulled from the product name and cannot be edited in the theme customizer (the store design tool).
  • The vendor field (the brand or supplier name) is dynamic (it updates by itself), so it updates automatically when real supplier brands are added.
  • For demo stores (test stores not used for real sales), hide these Product Information subsections:
    • Expiring Coupon (a discount code with a deadline).
    • Estimated Delivery (the expected delivery date).
    • Promo Image/Sale Banner (a sale picture or strip).
    • Inventory Status (the stock level text).
    • Trust Badges (small logos that build confidence).
  • Related Products section (the box of suggested items):
    • It displays "You may also like".
    • It lets you edit section text, layout, colours and number of products shown.
    • For demo stores, hide it temporarily with the eyedropper/hide icon (the small icon that hides an item).
    • Re-enable it later.
  • Stock levels on product pages:
    1. Open Online Store (the Shopify menu for store design) > Themes > Customize.
    2. Use the drop-down (a menu you open with a click) at the top (Home page) and choose Products > Default product > Product information.
    3. Click the eye icon next to Inventory status to hide it.
    4. Do not delete the element, so it can be restored later.
  • Inventory (stock count) numbers can be entered in Shopify even when they are not live (not shown to buyers).
  • Use a placeholder value per product (a fill-in number that is not the real count), for example 10 or 100.
  • This lets the inventory status be hidden on product pages, while the back end (the admin side buyers do not see) still tracks stock.
  • Shopify has no built-in field (a ready-made box) for product availability dates.
  • Add the availability or backorder date as text on the product page (a backorder is an order taken for an item that is out of stock).
  • Shopify free-shipping badge (a small label that says free shipping):
    • Go to Online Store > Themes > Customize > Products, select a product and look for free shipping under availability.
    • If it is not there, it may have been added to custom code (code written by hand) or another setting.
    • Ask Shopify support.
  • Product page template edits for notes:
    • Under the theme's product template (the layout file that shapes product pages), add a note near the Add to cart button.
    • For a back-order note, insert it in the product template near that button.
  • PDF manuals (2022, older; a PDF is a document file, a manual is an instruction booklet):
    • Upload the file to the Files section in Shopify.
    • Then add a link to it on the product page or in the description.
  • Product comparison table:
    • Build a simple table with attributes (features such as size or colour) in rows and products in columns.
    • No specialised app (a special add-on tool) is required.
  • Editing product templates:
    • In the theme editor, the product template shows as Default product.
    • In the sidebar (the side settings panel), add, remove or reorder the variant selector, quantity selector, product recommendations (suggested products) and announcement bar (the strip at the top of the site).
    • Preview before publishing (check how it looks before it goes live).
  • Customising colours with custom CSS (CSS is code that sets colours and layout), for example the colour of struck-through MSRP prices (MSRP is the suggested full price, shown crossed out):
    1. Ask ChatGPT (an AI chat tool) or another AI tool to generate CSS for the specific change.
    2. Copy the generated CSS.
    3. In Shopify, go to Online Store > Themes > Customize > Custom CSS.
    4. Paste the CSS and save and publish.
  • Quantity selector:
    • If a supplier (the company that provides the items) sells in a fixed quantity (only in set amounts, such as a 6-pack), set the default quantity selector to the most common purchase amount.
    • Base the default on what top competitors show.

Product information: custom input, files and line item properties

1 min
  • The file upload option in the theme lets customers upload files directly from the product page.
  • Example: a design or photo for a custom product (made to order for the buyer).
  • This avoids a separate app for collecting files.
  • Line item properties (extra details saved on one item in an order) store customer input, such as an engraving (words cut into the item) name, and display it on the Shopify order.
  • A standard supplier order usually carries only variant details.
  • So the property must be mapped to the supplier's API (the link that lets two systems exchange data) to reach them.
  • Line item properties can be generated dynamically (automatically) from Shopify metafields (extra data fields for products or customers).
  • The supplier's system must recognise the parameter (the piece of data it receives), or the data is lost.
  • Developers are still needed for complex functions (features that work like a program), such as a bundle builder (a tool where buyers build their own set).
  • Page builders like Replo and PageFly (drag-and-drop page tools) generally cannot produce these, as they cannot produce complex functions.
  • Simple landing pages and stock themes (ready-made store designs) with page builders can be enough for brands that do not focus on branding (building a name and look).
  • A simple custom engraving or name field built by a developer was roughly estimated at about $30.
  • A complicated setup or a poorly built theme can cost much more.
  • This figure is a rough guess, not a quote (a fixed price offer).

    3. Change the heading format to Heading 6 (H6).
    4. Remove divider lines (lines between sections) between sections.
    5. Click Save.

  • This keeps long descriptions readable and organised.

    1. In the product description editor (the box where the text is written), highlight a section heading (for example Description).
    2. Click the formatting dropdown (a menu for text style), currently showing Paragraph (normal text).
    3. Select Heading 6.
    4. Click Save.
    5. Repeat for other sections (Key features, Specifications).
    6. Click Preview (see the page as a buyer sees it). Sections now collapse and expand when clicked.

  • It improves both usability (how easy the page is to use) and page digest (how quickly a visitor can scan it).

    • The first section (the description) opens by default.

Expandable tabs, accordions and policy blocks

2 min
  • Accordions (boxes that open and close, like expandable sections).
  • Typical product page tabs: description, specifications (the list of product details), shipping info, warranty (a promise to repair or replace for a set time) and price match guarantee (a promise to match a lower price elsewhere).
  • Copy the supplier's warranty text, but swap the contact email for your own.
  • Built-in tabs: the new Charlotte theme (a Shopify store design) includes product tabs.
  • No tab app is needed.
  • An alternative tab app (an add-on tool) costs about $5 per month.
  • Tabs built with the theme's own code keep the page lighter and faster (less data to load).
  • Use apps only for features too hard to code directly.
  • Accordion setup in Visually (an app for page design; Source E 2025-26):
    • Auto-expand the details accordion on page load (when the page first opens), only for first-time visitors.
    • Leave the details collapsed by default for returning customers.
    • Implementation (setting it up) requires 3 clicks in Visually.
  • Policy blocks in the Manhattan default template (2024):
    1. Shopify Admin > Online Store > Themes > Customize.
    2. Change the dropdown from Homepage to Products.
    3. Click into the Default Product template.
    4. Click Add Block and select Collapsible Row (a box that opens and closes).
    5. Drag it under the product description.
    6. Change the title to Shipping Policy or Price Match Policy.
    7. Add the content text in the editor.
    8. Choose an icon (checkmark, heart, etc.).
    9. Click Save.
    10. Repeat steps 4 to 9 for additional policies. Because this is the default template, it applies to all product pages.
  • Policy information (2025):
    • To add policy information, go to the theme editor, click customise, select the collection or product template, and add sections using rich text (formatted text) or collapsible content (boxes that open and close).
    • Include financing (pay-over-time options), customer support, return policy and price match information as expandable sections.
    • Drag them below the product images and save.
  • Accordions can hold an extra paragraph, such as a free shipping reminder placed below the description and dimensions.
  • This is a theme-level change (a change to the whole store design) and usually needs the theme developer.
  • Tabs on product pages compress (shrink) long information and keep purchase options, reviews and key facts near the top.
  • Duplicated descriptions in the default product layout: see under Product page structure.

Supplier-specific product page templates

    1. In the theme editor, click the Default product template.
    2. Click Create template.
    3. Name the template after the supplier (example: Brand B).
    4. Base it on the Default product template.
    5. Save the new template.
    6. Expand Product information.
    7. Enable and edit the Estimated delivery settings.

    1. In the theme code, duplicate the product page template.
    2. Give the copy a clear name, such as product page template followed by a number.
    3. Change the tabs or shipping-date information in the copy.
    4. Assign the new template to the products with longer shipping times.

    1. Go to Products and create a template based on the default product page, named for the offer.

Variant descriptions and variant selection

5 min
  • Decide whether variants appear on one page or on separate product pages.
  • Check the top two or three future competitors (rival stores you expect to face) in the niche (a narrow market), and copy their approach.
  • Default to one page with variations when the leaders (the strongest rival stores) use variants.
  • Standard and premium versions (different materials and build quality):
    • Look at how the top Google Shopping results (product listings on Google) display them.
    • Copy their structure (one page with variants or separate pages), but write your own words.
  • Bold Product Options: an app (an add-on tool) that lets customers choose custom sizes and materials on the product page.
  • Preset option values (choices set in advance) can update the price in real time (instantly).
  • Free-text entry (a box where buyers type their own answer) may not.
  • Check with the app's support before relying on it.
  • A single Canva image (made in Canva, a simple design tool) of colour swatches (small colour samples) does not let customers click a colour and add that variant to the cart.
  • Clickable swatches need variant options (choices built into the product), typically through an app (2025).
  • Set up variant-specific descriptions (a description for each version of a product) in this order:
    1. Create a variant metafield (an extra data field stored for each version) named "Variant description" with type Rich text.
    2. Create a snippet (a small reusable piece of code) named "variant-description" and paste the complete corrected snippet code into it.
    3. Create a new product page template, hide the "Description" block, add a Custom Liquid block and paste {% render 'variant-description' %} into it.
    4. Assign the new product page template to every product that has variants.
  • The variant metafield "Variant description" (type Rich text, meaning formatted text with bold, lists and links) stores formatted text separately for each variant.
  • The snippet reads it as custom.variant_description and outputs it with the metafield_tag filter (a Shopify code helper that formats the output).
  • Snippet code (variant-description.liquid), exactly as provided. A Liquid file is a Shopify code file.

PageFly layout system

Grid and sections

1 min
  • Web page layouts are built from stacked rows (horizontal strips), and each row holds side-by-side columns (vertical blocks).

  • Stacking many sections on mobile makes a landing page very long and tiring to scroll.
    • Decide early which blocks can share a row with two or more columns on mobile.
  • Uneven grids are built by splitting a row into proportions (shares of the width), such as one full-width row, or a 50% column next to a section split into two 25% parts.
    • Combining rows and nested columns (columns placed inside other columns) creates complex layouts.

Spacing and the box model

1 min
  • In the box model (the way every element is treated as a box with space around it), padding is the space inside an element between its content and its border (edge line).
    • Margin is the space outside the element that separates it from neighbouring elements.
  • In PageFly, set padding and margin under the element's Styling tab.
    • Enter one value for all four sides, or enter separate values for top, bottom, left or right.
  • In PageFly, edits made on desktop cascade (pass down) to tablet and mobile, unless a smaller device has its own value.
    • Edits made on mobile do not flow back up to desktop.
  • If a mobile value is left empty, PageFly applies whatever padding or margin you add on mobile to that element.
    • To remove inherited spacing (spacing passed down from above) on mobile, enter 0 for that value explicitly.
  • Text placed directly against a border looks cramped.
    • Add inside padding so the content sits clear of the edge.
  • An inner block groups several elements so one margin or padding value moves them as a single unit.
    • Use this to center a text stack (a pile of text items) or to space a group of elements from the element above.
  • A common starting value for inside padding on mobile in PageFly is 15 px on each side. px means pixels, the small dots that make up a screen, used as a size unit.
    • Adjust the value for each individual layout rather than applying it everywhere.
  • Excess inside padding in a narrow column squeezes text onto too many lines.
    • Reduce the padding on smaller devices before reducing font sizes.

1 min

2 min
  • Block elements take the full width of their container (the box holding them) and stack on top of each other.
    • Inline elements sit next to each other on the same line and only take the width they need.
  • Inline elements do not accept custom height and width values.
    • Inline-block elements sit side by side like inline elements but accept width and height settings.
  • Images are inline by default, but PageFly displays them at 100% width (the full width of their container).
    • To place several images next to each other, set the image width yourself or switch them to inline display.
  • To set a fixed image width in PageFly, select the image, open the General tab, and set Enable for width to yes.
    • Then enter a pixel value, for example 100 px for a small logo or icon.
  • Set PageFly image alignment under Styling to left, center or right.
    • Setting text align to center on a row centers all text and inline elements inside that row.
  • Use a heading for the page title and smaller headings for sections.
    • Set font family (the typeface, or font style), bold, and size under the element's typography settings (text style settings) to build a clear hierarchy (order of importance).
  • Set an embedded video (a video placed inside the page) to a 4:3 aspect ratio (the shape of the frame, width to height) in the General tab, for a shorter, wider frame.

Positioning

1 min
  • Position is where an element sits on the page. Position relative moves an element away from its original spot using top, bottom, left or right values.
    • The space it occupied stays reserved.
    • Negative or positive margin, by contrast, moves the elements below it as well.
  • Position absolute fixes an element inside its nearest container (the box around it), and that container must have position relative.
    • Without a relative parent (a container set to position relative), the absolute element will not stay where you place it.
  • Position static is the default for every element.
    • Set an element back to static to reset positioning that was changed before, for example on mobile.
  • Set an element to position fixed with top 0 and left 0 to pin it to the top of the browser window (the program showing the site).
    • This makes a sticky navigation bar (a menu bar that stays visible while the visitor scrolls).
  • A PageFly element set to position fixed may not appear at all until it has a width.
    • Setting a width, such as 50%, makes it visible so the position can be checked.
    • The width can be changed afterwards.
  • Use margin and relative positioning for most element placement, because they keep the surrounding layout predictable.
    • Reserve position absolute for small elements inside a container that is already sized and set to position relative.

Flexbox, grid and centering

1 min

    • Then use flex direction (the way items run: row, row reverse, column, column reverse), align items, align content and justify content (ways of lining items up) to control position.
  • Flexbox creates fluid layouts (layouts that stretch and shift with the screen size) that wrap items onto the next line when they reach their minimum width.
    • This suits forms such as name, email and submit fields, where the layout should adapt to the screen size.
  • Grid gives precise control over proportions (sizes) and placement.
    • Flexbox gives easier control over direction and alignment.
    • Use grid for fixed column layouts, and flexbox when items need to reverse order or center easily.
  • To center content vertically and horizontally in PageFly, set the container to display flex.
    • Then set align items, align content and justify content to center.
    • This replaces margin-auto (an old centering trick using automatic margins) and padding hacks (workarounds that use padding).

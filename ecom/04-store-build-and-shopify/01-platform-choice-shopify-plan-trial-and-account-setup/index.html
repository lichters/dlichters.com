Playbook › 4. Store build and Shopify

Platform choice, Shopify plan, trial and account setup

Section 4.1 · 1 of 20 in this chapter · 28 min read

In short

  • Use Shopify for every store. It is one ready-made platform (software you rent to run a shop) that does almost everything.
  • Start on the Basic plan. Higher plans only make sense when sales are large or the team is big.
  • Start the free trial before you choose a plan. Cancel before the trial ends so you are not charged.
  • Use one Shopify account per store. Build the store directly on Shopify, not somewhere else.
  • Prices, trial offers and plan limits change often. Check them on the Shopify checkout page before you rely on them.
More key points
  • Wait to open the Shopify account (online store software) until you start building the website, because the free trial starts at signup.
  • Use a business email if you have one. A personal email such as Gmail also works. Confirm the verification email within about 24 hours.
  • Give helpers a staff account or collaborator access, never your main login.
  • Pay monthly while the store is unproven, and do not sign a long contract at the start.
  • Do not build a full store until the product gets views and is close to 1,000 TikTok followers.

Why Shopify

5 min
  • Shopify is the platform this handbook uses for every store. A platform is the ready-made software that runs a store.
  • Shopify is built only for e-commerce, which means selling products online.
  • Shopify covers these jobs in one place: domain setup (your web address), storefront design (what customers see), product management, inventory tracking (counting stock), payment processing (taking payments), checkout (the final buying step), analytics (sales statistics), email marketing, live chat, and built-in connections to the Google and Facebook sales channels (places where you sell).
  • Shopify's all-in-one scope means no separate tools are needed for store design, product management, payments, inventory, email, order tracking, live chat or analytics.
  • Shopify is the recommended primary platform (the main one) for dropshipping. Dropshipping means a supplier ships each product straight to the customer, so you do not keep stock.
  • Shopify has superior integrations (connections to other tools) and technology built for e-commerce fulfillment (packing and shipping orders).
  • Shopify allows unlimited product uploads on day one.
  • ** Shopify is recommended for dropshipping stores because of its supplier-friendly integrations and app ecosystem. Apps are add-on tools, and the ecosystem is the full range of them.
  • ** Other site builders are not recommended for stores that will run paid ads (paid advertising).
  • Shopify was chosen over newer website builders for its maturity (being well established) and its feature depth (how many useful functions it has).
  • The newer alternatives were considered too young for a serious store at the time of the source.
  • The course recommends Shopify as the single platform for every store.
  • Shopify is described as the market leader (the biggest seller in its field) for about ten years, with the best platform and support.
  • Using one account for all tools is preferred over combining several tools.
  • Shopify is a managed platform. The merchant (you, the store owner) does not host (keep on servers), secure or maintain the software.
  • Shopify has rarely been down for stores.
  • Custom setups add hosting latency (delay from servers) and maintenance work.
  • A custom build (a site coded from scratch) would need separate APIs (connection points between software), such as PayPal and Stripe (payment services).
  • Those APIs would be needed to copy the checkout, supplier connections and order back end (the hidden system that handles orders) that Shopify already includes.
  • Shopify handles the frontend (the storefront customers see) and the backend (the back office that runs ordering, checkout, and product and order management).
  • You work in the backend every day. Customers only see the frontend.
  • ** A hosted platform already includes ordering, checkout, order management and product management. A beginner only adds design and content.
  • ** This avoids a custom website, which typically costs tens of thousands of dollars.
  • ** A first business should not begin with a large upfront website development cost (money paid before launch).
  • That money is hard to recover if the business fails.
  • The priority is to launch quickly on a platform that is easy to use.
  • ** Shopify is recommended for beginners because it combines ease of use, easy daily management and cost-effectiveness (good value for money).
  • BigCommerce, Magento, WooCommerce and WordPress can also work, but they are harder for newcomers.
  • An experienced builder can use another platform if they already know it well.
  • Shopify is used to build an online store in minutes or hours without coding (writing software code) or web development knowledge. It uses free themes (ready-made page designs) and apps.
  • The build is done step by step and does not require prior website experience.
  • Shopify is for beginners with little or no coding experience.
  • Shopify is mobile responsive, which means it adjusts to phone screens.
  • Shopify handles security topics such as data encryption (scrambling data so others cannot read it).
  • Shopify also handles fraud prevention (stopping fake orders) and PCI compliance (meeting the card industry's security rules).
  • Shopify can be used for both physical products and digital products (files or access sold online).
  • Full-time support is available by chat, plus an active community (other users who help each other).
  • Answers to most common questions can be found through Google or the Shopify support pages.
  • Large consumer brands such as Kylie Cosmetics, Tesla Motors and Gym Shark run their e-commerce on Shopify. This shows the platform works at very large scale.
  • ** Many of the largest e-commerce stores still run on Shopify.
  • ** A store can grow on Shopify for a long time before a platform change is needed.
  • ** Shopify is suited to beginners starting a new business from scratch.
  • ** Shopify is the platform used to build the high-ticket store in this course. High-ticket means selling expensive products.
  • The coaching team supports only Shopify stores and does not work with WooCommerce.
  • Feedback on a non-Shopify site will be limited.
  • ** Build the store directly on Shopify, not elsewhere and then imported.
  • ** Imported stores often need a lot of rework.
  • ** A Shopify store stays on the Shopify platform.
  • ** Moving to another e-commerce platform later requires an app-based migration (moving the store with an app) and substantial rework.
  • ** Moving a Shopify store to a self-hosted platform (one you run on your own servers) is not seamless.
  • ** A developer can model the existing store on a new platform, since Shopify's structure is well established and widely understood.
  • Shopify takes a percentage of each sale (a transaction fee), so the platform's cost depends on sales volume. See the plan section below.
  • Shopify is a managed platform that is recommended over self-hosted alternatives. Self-hosted alternatives require the merchant to handle hosting, security and customer data protection.
  • Marketplaces such as eBay and Facebook Marketplace bring organic traffic (free visitors, not paid ones).
  • Users on marketplaces visit with purchase intent (they are ready to buy).
  • Google search traffic requires strong SEO (search engine optimisation, getting found in Google results) or paid ads.
  • Shopify has harder initial traffic acquisition (getting visitors at the start).
  • Shopify enables better long-term retargeting (showing ads again to people who already visited) and scaling (growing sales).

Alternatives and when they fit

2 min
  • WooCommerce is self-hosted and needs separate hosting and plug-ins (add-on tools). **
  • Shopify keeps hosting, security and support in one platform. **
  • WooCommerce plug-ins and add-ons are often paid.
  • Compare the total add-on cost with Shopify before choosing WooCommerce.
  • ** WooCommerce is not recommended for dropshipping.
  • ** Magento only makes sense for stores with around 50,000 to 100,000 or more products.
  • ** Do not mix WordPress and Shopify for new stores.
  • Shopify's blogging system (the part for writing articles) and SEO capabilities are excellent.
  • ** Combine WordPress with Shopify only if you already own an established WordPress site with significant history and traffic, such as a blog subdomain (a section of a site with its own address).
  • ** Use WordPress for standalone blogs or review sites that earn affiliate commissions (money from partner links).
  • ** Use Shopify for stores that sell products directly.
  • WordPress and BigCommerce are alternatives with similar ecosystems to Shopify.
  • ClickFunnels creates single-page checkout funnels (a short page path that leads to a purchase).
  • Shopify product pages achieve the same conversion goals (getting visitors to buy) at lower cost, with better e-commerce integrations.
  • Shopify is preferred for dropshipping because it was designed for e-commerce fulfillment, not generic funnel building.
  • ** Shopify is the only store platform used for these stores.
  • ** Funnel builders such as ClickFunnels are not used in place of Shopify.
  • Shopify gives the seller another sales channel. It lets them send traffic from Google and Bing to their own products.
  • A separate store also avoids losing buyers to other products on Amazon, because visitors who land on Amazon often buy something else.
  • A Shopify store makes retargeting, upsells (extra offers after a purchase) and email capture (collecting email addresses) easier than Amazon does.
  • A Shopify store keeps the focus on your own products, which is hard to do with Amazon's search results.
  • Compare the conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy) of a digital product on Shopify with PayHip (a platform for selling digital products) before choosing a platform.
  • Test both with the same offer to see which converts better.
  • PayHip allows PayPal and Stripe to be connected. These are the two most important payment options for digital products.
  • Check the platform fee per purchase before choosing PayHip, since the fee is not stated here.

Moving an existing Wix store to Shopify

1 min
  • Wix is another website builder. Move from Wix to Shopify for an e-commerce store, because Shopify is built only for e-commerce and has more apps and built-in tools.
  • Keep existing logos, images and written content when migrating.
  • Migration procedure **:
    1. Look for a dedicated importer app (a tool that moves data into Shopify). None was found for Wix at the time of the source.
    2. Export the products from Wix as a CSV file (a spreadsheet file).
    3. Import the CSV into Shopify.
    4. Recreate the collections (product groups), pages and text manually in Shopify.

Choosing and switching a plan

1 min
  • Start a new Shopify store on the Basic plan, not on Shopify or Advanced Shopify.
  • ** Higher plans are worth it only once a store has more staff or a large volume of sales.
  • The switch point to a higher plan is approximately 30,000 to 40,000 USD monthly revenue (money from sales each month). **
  • Verify this against current pricing.
  • Higher plans charge a lower transaction percentage per sale.
  • A higher plan such as Advanced can pay off at high monthly sales, because the lower percentage adds up across many orders.
  • On Shopify Advanced, the transaction percentage was about 1 percentage point lower (1 full percent) than on the plan below it.
  • Advanced was cheaper when billed yearly rather than monthly.
  • Billing yearly was cheaper than monthly for Advanced.
  • ** Shopify offers a discounted annual (yearly) price when paying up front.
  • ** Committing to a multi-year term is not advisable at the start.
  • ** Monthly billing keeps the cost flexible while the store is unproven.
  • ** The annual option can be considered once the store's sales and staffing needs are clear.
  • A plan upgrade can come with a temporary free period (a promotion), such as three free months on Shopify Advanced.
  • After it ends, the store may revert to Basic without notice.
  • Note the expiry date, because a reversion can cancel multi-market settings that depend on the higher plan.

  • The Pending tab shows requests from the agency.

  • Shopify login problems can come from being logged into a different Shopify account.

  • Use the admin URL (yourstore.com/admin) to log in to the correct store.

  • Or try an incognito window (a private browser window) or a different browser. (2025.)

  • To deactivate (switch off) a Shopify store you created by mistake:

    1. Open the Shopify admin.
    2. Click Settings.
    3. Open Plan.
    4. Click Deactivate.

  • Shopify may put the store on hold for a while in case you change your mind. **

Collaborator and staff accounts

1 min

Staff accounts

  • Shopify Staff Accounts let the store owner give team members limited access to the store.
  • This means they get access without sharing the main login. **
  • Create staff accounts for contractors and designers.
  • Set permissions (rules for what each person may open) to restrict access to customer data, payment settings and financial information.
  • Each staff member can have different permission levels.
  • When bringing in a VA (virtual assistant, a remote helper), use staff permissions.
  • Give them only the access they need, such as editing store pages. Keep financial or other sensitive areas closed. **
  • Permissions to grant: Products, Catalogs (product lists), Online store and Store settings.
  • Do not grant access to Orders, Draft orders (unfinished orders) or Customers. (2024.)
  • Settings > Users and permissions > Add staff: invite a person (for example, a hired store builder) by name and email.
  • Then choose which sections of the store they can access.
  • This gives them a separate login instead of sharing your username and password.

Collaborator accounts

  • Shopify collaborator accounts give a staff member or outside partner access to a store.
  • Use them instead of sharing credentials (login details).
  • The collaborator request arrives by email once assets are ready (the store files are finished).
  • For agencies or partners on the 39 USD per month plan, use a collaborator invitation.
  • Partner access does not require a staff account.
  • Give a private supplier access by going to shopify.partners and inviting them as a partner (an outside business with its own access).
  • Grant them partnership access to your Shopify store.
  • The supplier provides the step-by-step instructions. This guide does not name the exact menu labels.

Cost of running the store

2 min

Fixed costs

  • Shopify is a fixed cost (the same amount every month) that must be paid every month while the store is running.
  • Stopping payment can lead to the store being shut down.
  • Shopify costs 29 USD per month when paid for a year in advance.
  • Shopify costs 39 USD per month when paid monthly. (2023.)
  • The Shopify subscription at 29 USD per month is small compared with the cost of a physical retail store.
  • A physical store needs a lease (a rental contract), a rent deposit, build-out (interior construction) and fixtures (shelves and fittings).
  • A store that makes only one 1,000 USD sale per month still covers the subscription cost.
  • Shopify costs in the 2021 setting: a 14-day free trial, then 29 USD per month. **

  • A premium Shopify theme (a paid store design template) can sell for around $300 when bought separately.
  • Stores are built on a theme that handles the background code (the hidden instructions that make the store work). **
  • A hosted platform (a ready-made website service) avoids the cost and time of a custom website.
  • A custom website typically runs to tens of thousands of dollars. **
  • A domain name cost about 14 USD per year. **

Transaction and add-on costs

  • A transaction fee is the cut taken from each sale.
  • Transaction fees per sale by plan are listed in the plans section: Basic 2.0%, Shopify 1.0%, Advanced 0.5% in the 2021 source.
  • Add-ons are extra paid features.
  • Add-on prices: POS Pro (in-store sales software) USD 89/month per location; Remove online store USD 9/month. **
  • Paid apps (add-on tools) appear on the Billing page with their next bill.
  • For plans that cost more, Advanced was cheaper when billed yearly rather than monthly.

Keeping the cost under control

  • Do not commit to a multi-year term (a long contract) at the start.
  • Pay monthly while the store is unproven.
  • Note the expiry date of any free period.
  • A reversion (a return to the normal price) may cancel multi-market settings (settings for selling in several countries).
  • Cancel any unused second account to avoid paying for it.
  • Check plan limits regularly, since a plan change can break an existing store configuration (the store setup).

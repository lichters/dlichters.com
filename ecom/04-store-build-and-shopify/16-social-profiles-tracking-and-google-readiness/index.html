Playbook › 4. Store build and Shopify

Social profiles, tracking and Google readiness

Section 4.16 · 16 of 20 in this chapter · 30 min read

In short

  • Use only real products and real contact details. Fake, demo or placeholder content can get your accounts blocked.
  • Finish the store first: contact page, policies, working links, and no empty "image coming soon" boxes.
  • Connect in this order: Facebook and Instagram pages, the Facebook pixel, then Google Analytics 4 and Google Merchant Center.
  • Every product needs matching codes: a store code (SKU) and a barcode (GTIN) or maker's model number (MPN).
  • When Google or Meta rejects something, check what changed just before the rejection, then contact their support.
This section has 16 parts. Tap one to open it.

Facebook page

1 min

  • To create a Facebook business page (a public page for your store, separate from your personal profile), follow these steps:

    1. Log in to Facebook.
    2. Click See more in the left navigation menu.
    3. Click Pages.
    4. Click Create Page.
    5. Select Public Page.
    6. Click Get started.

  • Set the basic page details:

    • Enter your Store Name (not the full URL) in the Page name field.
    • Select a relevant Category for your business type. If you are unsure, use AI to find the right category for your niche.
    • Set Hours to Always open.

  • Add the contact information:

    • Enter your store URL in the Website field.
    • Enter sales@yourdomain.com in the Email field.
    • Optionally, add a Phone number.
    • Do not use the Address field for ecommerce stores.

  • Set the profile picture and cover photo:

    • Upload your store logo as the profile picture.
    • If the logo does not fit well in the circle, crop it to a clean symbol or icon version.
    • Upload your store header or banner image as the cover photo.

  • Add an action button: select Learn more as the button label and link it to your store homepage.

  • Set your vanity URL (a short custom web address with your name in it):

    • Go to Settings & privacy > Page setup > Name to choose a username that matches your brand, such as facebook.com/YourStoreName.
    • If the exact name is taken, select the closest available alternative.

  • Write a short bio (the description text on your page) based on your store's About Us page. Use AI to draft it, then trim it to fit Facebook's character limit if needed.

Instagram account

1 min

  • To create an Instagram business account:

    1. Go to Instagram.com on desktop, or open the mobile app.
    2. Click Create new account.
    3. Enter your store email (sales@yourdomain.com).
    4. Create a password and complete the setup.
    5. Enter your Store Name as the full name.
    6. Choose a username as close as possible to your store name.

  • To switch Instagram to a business account (a professional account for brands):

    1. Click More.
    2. Click Settings.
    3. Scroll down and click Account type and tools.
    4. Click Switch to a professional account.
    5. Select Business.
    6. Choose a relevant category. Shopping & retail is typical for ecommerce.
    7. Confirm the switch to professional account.

  • Set up your Instagram profile:

    • Upload the same logo or icon you used for Facebook.
    • Paste your Facebook bio into the Instagram bio field, trimming it if needed.
    • Note that Instagram is built for mobile. Some settings, like the website link, may only work through the mobile app, not desktop.

Connect social pages to your Shopify store

3 min

  • Link your social profiles to your Shopify store. In Shopify Admin (the back office of your store), go to:

    • Online Store > Themes > Edit theme > Theme settings (gear icon) > Social media.
    • Paste your Facebook Page URL and your Instagram profile URL.
    • Click Save.

  • Show the social icons in your footer (the bottom of the store):

    • Go to Theme Customizer > Footer settings.
    • Turn ON the option "Show social media icons".
    • Click Save.
    • Preview your store and check that the icons link to both your Facebook and Instagram profiles.

  • Link the pages to the store (2 steps in a short version):

    1. Open the Shopify admin and go to Online Store.
    2. Click Edit theme.
    3. In the sidebar, click Theme settings, the gear icon.
    4. Scroll down to Social media.
    5. Paste the Facebook page URL into the Facebook field. Use the business page, not a personal profile.
    6. Paste the Instagram business account URL into the Instagram field.
    7. Click Save.
    8. In the footer menu, turn on Show social media icons.
    9. Click Save and preview the store to check the footer links.

  • The Shopify social links path is Online Store > Edit theme > Theme settings (gear icon) > Social media. Enter the Facebook and Instagram URLs there and save. In the footer, the option Show social media icons must be switched on to make the links visible.

  • Linking the social pages helps visitors find them. It also helps Google know the pages exist, which can improve ranking (where your store appears in search). The main purpose of the link is to increase the conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy).

  • In the Social media settings, fill in only the platforms you actually use, such as Facebook and Instagram. Leave the other fields empty. Use the full URL of the business page or business account, not a personal profile.

  • To verify the social icons in the footer:

    1. Turn on the option Show social media icons in the footer menu. It is off by default.
    2. Click Save.
    3. Open the store preview.
    4. Hover over the Facebook and Instagram icons. Confirm they link to facebook.com/[your page username] and instagram.com/[your account username].

  • Social media links are entered in Theme settings > Social media, one box per network. The networks are Facebook, Twitter, Pinterest, Instagram, Tumblr, Vimeo, YouTube and LinkedIn. Paste the full profile URL, such as facebook.com/[business name] or instagram.com/[business name]. The icons then appear in the footer's social block and link to those profiles. Until accounts exist, the social block stays empty.

  • The social icon style setting in the Footer colour group also applies to the password page, not only the footer. Choose Light on dark or Dark on light so the icons stay readable on both.

  • To set social links:

    1. Open Theme settings > Social media.
    2. Replace each default URL with your own account link, if you have one.
    3. Delete URLs for accounts you do not have, so the footer does not link to Shopify's placeholder pages.

  • Theme settings > Social media: remove the default Twitter, Facebook, Instagram and YouTube URLs when you do not own those accounts. Otherwise the footer links to Shopify.

  • Set up social media accounts in the Shopify theme at launch for Facebook, Pinterest, email address and Instagram. Twitter is generally not needed for an ecommerce store.

  • Social profiles for a new store only need basic setup, which takes about 20 to 30 minutes. They are needed before running ads, but not before contacting suppliers.

  • Link the store to its social profiles. If the store gets traffic from Instagram, show an Instagram feed on the site. Check which social platforms send the most visitors, and make those profiles visible.

Store rules before Google

3 min

  • Only authentic products from brands you have the right to sell should appear in the shop. Demo or invented items can trigger Google policy problems and put your accounts at risk.

  • Delete all demo products under Shopify Admin > Products.

  • Delete every demo product from the Shopify store before creating Google accounts, so only real products from authorized supplier brands remain. Demo products left on the store can cause problems with Google.

  • Broken links and incomplete pages are a problem for Google. A single 404 page (a "page not found" error) or unfinished page can cause compliance flags (warnings that you may not follow the rules).

  • Click every link in the header menu, every collection link on the homepage and every link in the footer menu. Confirm each one opens the correct page. No link may lead to a 404 page.

  • Remove every placeholder (fake filler content) before submitting to Google. This includes:

    • blank image boxes showing a circle with an X,
    • "image coming soon" graphics or text,
    • any unfinished section. The target is zero errors, zero placeholders and zero demo content.

  • Replace all placeholder graphics before submission. This includes empty boxes with a crossed circle icon and labels such as "image coming soon". Every collection and product image must be real.

  • Final compliance checklist before submitting to Google (a step-by-step check of the store's rules):

    1. Delete all demo products in Shopify, so only real products from authorized supplier brands remain.
    2. Click every link in the header menu, every homepage collection link and every footer menu link. Fix any link that leads to a 404 page.
    3. Replace all placeholder images with real images.
    4. Open Shopify Settings > Policies. Review the refund, privacy, terms of service and shipping policies. Replace all placeholder text, and confirm the email address and phone number are real.
    5. Fill in the contact information section of each policy with a real email address, phone number and hours of operation. Then publish.
    6. Paste each policy page into Google Gemini (Google's AI chat tool) and request a compliance review.
    7. Apply every recommendation. Then send the revised text back to Gemini for a second check. Repeat until it reports full compliance.
    8. Add a real phone number and a real email address to the left column of the footer navigation.
    9. On the Contact Us page, keep the contact form and add at least two more contact methods. Then send the page to Gemini for review.
    10. Submit the store to Google Merchant Center and Google Ads only after every step passes.

  • Before setting up Google Ads or Merchant Center, the store must not be password-protected. It must also have a contact page and a privacy policy. Missing pages are a common reason for account problems.

  • Do not set up Google Ads optimization or Google Shopping feeds before the store's contact page, privacy policy and product pages are complete.

  • Product pages should show the brand name, product name and SKU number clearly. Google Shopping pulls keywords from the content of the product page.

  • Two stores with the same owner that differ in Google Merchant Center status: compare these side by side, then make the flagged store match the working one:

    • merchant center settings (shipping, tax, returns),
    • site settings,
    • privacy policy,
    • contact information.

Contact details and contact page

1 min

  • Show at least two contact methods (email, phone or physical address) on the site. For this model, use a real, working phone number and email address. Place them in the footer as a contact block with both email and phone. Also put them on the Contact Us page.

  • Edit the Contact Us page under Shopify Admin > Online Store > Pages > Contact Us. A default Contact Us page may only show a form. Add more contact methods to the page content.

  • The Contact Us page content should include:

    • business hours (for example, 9am to 5pm Eastern),
    • a phone number labeled as the way to call,
    • a support or sales email address labeled as the way to email,
    • a note that live chat is available, if it is enabled on the store.

  • Fake or placeholder contact details, such as dummy phone numbers or text in brackets, can lead to account problems on Google.

  • The left column of the footer navigation must show a real phone number and a real email address. This is one of the two contact methods Google checks on the storefront.

  • Have the website reviewed by the team before submitting to Google Merchant Center. Expect a response within a couple of days on a weekday. If an email gets no reply, check the spam folder and resend it to support.

Google Analytics 4 (GA4) setup

5 min

  • Google Analytics is a free tool that counts visitors and sales. GA4 is its newest version. Install Google Analytics on the store, because most buyers expect to see it and the broker requires it for listing. Shopify's own analytics is useful, but it does not replace Google Analytics for buyers.

  • Google Analytics 4 can be connected to a Shopify store, so traffic and sales data are tracked in GA4. Set this up before running paid campaigns, so the data is complete from the start.

  • Shopify path to the Google Analytics connection: Sales channels > Online Store > Preferences. The Google Analytics section there shows the existing account. A separate GA4 entry sits above it, and it is managed through the Google channel app.

  • To connect GA4 to Shopify, follow this order:

    1. In Shopify admin, go to Sales channels > Online Store > Preferences.
    2. In the Google Analytics section, above the Universal Analytics entry, click Manage pixel.
    3. Install the Google channel app when Shopify asks for it.
    4. Click Connect and sign in with the same business email used for Google Analytics, Google Ads and Merchant Center.
    5. Click Allow, so Shopify can access the Google data it needs to pass data both ways.
    6. Open the GA4 property dropdown (a property is one Google Analytics account for one site) and select the correct property. For example, choose the one named after the new store and ending in tracking tag.
    7. Click Connect. Wait for the confirmation message before moving on.

  • If the Google account has several GA4 properties, Shopify may preselect the property of the main website. Always check the dropdown and select the property created for the store you are connecting.

  • The Google access request asks for access to many Google services. Allow it, because Shopify needs that access to send and receive data between the store and the Google properties.

  • Adding a GA4 measurement ID (a code that identifies your GA4 data) in Shopify does not require removing the old Universal Analytics ID first. Google removes the old ID over time.

  • Universal Analytics is the older version of Google Analytics. Until the switch, it stops being supported in July 2023.

  • The Shopify integration for GA4 was expected to be ready in March 2023. The planned update was to show the GA4 setup before the switch.

  • At the time of the call, the switch to Google Analytics 4 was about 12 months away. It needed no action yet.

  • Shopify's direct integration did not yet support the newer GA4 property type at the time. So GA4 setup could not be completed in the usual way.

  • Google Analytics integration in Shopify fails with the newest GA version. Revert to the previous GA version to make the Shopify integration work.

  • Creating a Universal Analytics property (the older type) when only GA4 is offered:

    1. Log in to Google Analytics.
    2. Open the Advanced options link.
    3. Click the option to create a Universal Analytics property.
    4. Copy the UA number that is generated, and use it for the Shopify analytics setup.

  • To connect the old tracking ID (Universal Analytics):

    1. In Google Analytics, open Admin, then the Property column, then Tracking Info and Tracking Code. Copy the tracking ID shown.
    2. In Shopify, open Online Store, then Preferences.
    3. Paste the tracking ID into the Google Analytics account field in the Google Analytics section, and click Save.
    4. Tick Use Enhanced Ecommerce (extra shopping data for Google Analytics), and save again.

  • In Shopify, the Enhanced Ecommerce option appears under Online Store > Preferences > Google Analytics. Shopify states it requires the latest version of Google Analytics to work.

  • Use Lucky Orange (a tool that records visitor behavior and makes heatmaps, which are colour maps of where people click) together with Facebook Events Manager (Facebook's event tracking panel). Track the whole funnel, from page view to checkout, and confirm each event fires correctly.

  • Google Tag Manager (a tool that holds all tracking codes in one place) is not needed on day one for a new store. It is for advanced server-side tagging (tracking run on a server, not in the visitor's browser). Use Shopify's native integrations first, because they work immediately.

  • Adding Google Tag Manager to a Shopify store:

    1. Create a Google Tag Manager container (the box that holds your tags) for the store, and copy its two code snippets.
    2. Paste the first snippet into the head section of the theme layout file.
    3. Paste the second snippet directly after the opening body tag.
    4. If the placement is unclear, ask a developer to put the code in the right place.
    5. Add analytics, chat and heatmap tools as tags in the container, instead of pasting separate code into the theme.

  • On Shopify plans other than Shopify Plus (Shopify's top-tier plan), do not install the Google Analytics tag or the Facebook pixel through Google Tag Manager. Use Shopify's own integration instead. Google Tag Manager cannot run on the checkout, so those tags would miss checkout conversions.

  • Loading conversion tags on a Shopify store through Google Tag Manager silently drops the checkout events that conversion tracking and retargeting depend on.

  • Use Google Tag Manager for all tracking code on a WordPress site with WooCommerce, including Google Analytics audience tags. BigCommerce needs similar caution to Shopify. Check whether conversion tags must use its native integration.

  • Google Analytics is used to compare the conversion and quality of traffic sources. It can be set up with a custom report that shows search terms down to individual product level.

  • Use the Google Ads search term report in each campaign and ad group. It shows which search terms produced impressions (how often your ad was shown) and clicks for paid traffic.

  • Shopify Admin > Reports > Sessions by marketing: use the filter to group sessions (single visits to your store) by country, region and UTM parameters (tags added to links to show where traffic came from).

  • Analytics often show many sessions landing on the homepage, even when traffic is sent to product pages. So homepage fixes still matter. Look at total sessions per page, not only landing sessions.

  • Finding product pages to optimize first:

    1. Open Google Analytics.
    2. In the left navigation, click Content, then All pages.
    3. Choose a period, such as one month.
    4. Start rewriting descriptions on the pages that receive the most traffic. A few pages often carry most of the visits.

  • Shopify Settings > Customer events: this is where tracking pixels are added to the store later. Nothing needs to be changed at initial setup.

Product data and Google Merchant Center

5 min

  • Google Merchant Center is Google's tool for product data. The list of products it reads is called the feed. Link the product feed to Merchant Center through the API (a direct, automatic data link between two apps). The feed then updates about every 24 hours. Manual feeds can fall out of sync when prices change on the store.

  • The price in the Merchant Center feed must match the price shown on the product page. Compare-at prices (the crossed-out "was" price) are fine. But a mismatch between feed and site breaks Google's terms of service.

  • Switching themes normally keeps the Google feed intact, if the feed is connected through the API and the new theme uses the same product structure. A manual feed may require re-review after a theme change.

  • Tax and shipping set up in Shopify must be mirrored in Merchant Center, because the settings in both places must agree. Mismatched tax or shipping information is a common reason for account issues.

  • Product identifiers are needed for Google Shopping. Use the UPC (a 12-digit barcode number used in the US) when the supplier has one. If there is no UPC, use the MPN (manufacturer part number, the model number) as the product identifier.

  • GTIN (global trade item number, the broader barcode family) and UPC are different identifiers. GTIN is the broader product number. If a supplier provides no GTIN, leave that field empty and enter the MPN instead, to avoid a data mismatch.

  • Google Merchant Center product identifiers: if you use the MPN instead of the GTIN, leave the GTIN column blank and fill the separate MPN column. Make sure the MPN field is what you submit, and what appears in the product feed.

  • If suppliers provide no GTIN or MPN, create your own naming convention, such as APR-123 for each product. This way, product feed fields are not blank and do not cause rejections.

  • When you create your own product listing without a GTIN or barcode, use an MPN instead. You can make the MPN from your own SKU with a suffix, such as -NO.

  • Add the supplier's SKU (your own product code), MPN and barcode to each product page where they exist. Matching identifiers help keep products approved in Google Merchant Center.

  • Google Merchant Center product approval can fail when the supplier's identifiers do not match Google's required field. For example, an MPN may be rejected in favor of an SKU. Google's live chat support can check the store and product data directly. The supplier's identifiers must be entered in the correct field.

  • Missing GTIN errors in Google Merchant Center are fixed by filling in the MPN field (manufacturer part number) instead. The MPN appears as a column in the product feed.

  • To resolve a missing GTIN error in Google Merchant Center:

    1. Add a manufacturer part number (MPN) to each product.
    2. If the supplier does not provide an MPN, create one for each product yourself. Use a consistent pattern, such as a brand letter followed by a sequential number, for example V1001, V1002.
    3. Save the products and let the feed update.

  • A Google Merchant Center error saying images are too small is fixed by uploading larger product images. Ask suppliers for high-resolution images, since large images help customers too.

  • Google Shopping expects the white-background image as the main image. You can change which image is submitted to the feed in the feed app. Keep white-background photos as the main image for both the store and the feed.

  • Fixing the store image that Google shows in search or Shopping results:

    • Step 1: For Google Shopping, choose in the feed app whether to use the default product image or a variant image.
    • Step 2: For organic search results (free, unpaid results), you cannot assign the image directly. Contact Shopify live chat to check the theme or code settings.
    • Step 3: Check whether the change came from a theme or code update.

  • The Shopify Google Shopping feed app can upload product data. Product metafields (extra custom data fields on products) do not need a separate third-party app to be imported through a CSV (a spreadsheet file).

  • The Google Shopping feed app should not be replaced by the built-in Google Channel app. Google is taking over Shopify's native Google Channel app. This channel is less customizable and has reported glitches. Use Feed for Google Shopping from Symprosis instead, for more control and reliability.

  • Brand name in the SEO title affects how your product shows in Google Shopping. If the brand is only in the meta title (the hidden title shown to search engines), and not in the product title, it will not appear in paid or free Google Shopping results. Competitors show brand names, so add yours too.

  • Product titles for Google Shopping may include the supplier name. Follow what the top competitors for that supplier's products do.

  • For multi-variant products (one product with several options, such as colour or size), check the top Google Shopping and organic results. If they use one page with variants, do the same. If they use separate pages per variant, do the same.

  • Google Shopping product results can show star reviews. Reviews appear through judge.me (a review app). They increase the click-through rate (the share of people who click after seeing your listing), because they make the listing stand out.

  • Google Seller Ratings (star ratings for your store shown in Google) require a review app from the Shopify App Store. Many of these apps are expensive. After installing one, apply to Google, which typically approves established stores quickly.

  • Product descriptions over 5,000 characters can cause Facebook to refuse publishing the product to the catalog. Shorten them, or find another route, because the full catalog is needed for remarketing (showing ads to past visitors).

  • Use a separate product description field for Facebook when the main description is too long to display. Many feed apps let you choose which description text is sent to Facebook.

  • Check whether an earlier change caused a Facebook product rejection. Look for changes to Facebook settings, installed apps or the theme made just before the rejections started. Products that were approved for years should not suddenly be rejected.

  • Some Shopify settings can be changed in the Facebook channel and the theme. Check these first when rejections start.

Feed app and product errors

2 min

  • The feed app (an app that sends your products to Google or Bing) is used to fix product errors. Fix product errors in Shopify when a feed app is installed:

    1. Open Manage products in Shopify, where the app shows recommended actions for each product.
    2. For a product with the error "SKU not submitted", add the variant SKU to the product variants.
    3. For products missing a Google product category, select the correct Google product category on each product.
    4. For products with an invalid GTIN or barcode, correct the barcode value.
    5. Select multiple products to view all their errors at once, and work through them.

  • After the first setup, click into each product in the Symprosis app. Review the assigned Google Product Category and update it if it is wrong. Changes sync automatically to your Google Merchant Center account.

  • To create a custom label (your own tag on a product group) for a profit tier:

    1. Open the Shopify store and access Some Process (a Google Shopping feed app).
    2. Click Manage Products.
    3. Use filters to find all products in a profit tier. For example, filter by Product Type for t-shirts.
    4. Select all matching products.
    5. Click Do Edits in Bulk.
    6. Click the field for Assign Custom Label Zero.
    7. Type the label name, for example "high profit".
    8. Click Apply in Bulk.
    9. Wait about four hours for changes to publish to Google Ads.

  • When a product sync from the store to Facebook is rejected repeatedly, use the process app that alerts on errors to find what must change in the affected products. Do not keep syncing without it.

  • A Google Search Console error, such as a syntax error in the site links search box, cannot be solved from a general answer. Email the support team with the exact message, since they may already have a developer fix.

  • Google Search Console issues such as "unstructured parsable data" on duplicate Shopify URLs are normal. Check that the main collection URLs are indexed. The error pages are often variants that you do not want indexed.

Indexing and Search Console

1 min

  • Google Search Console is Google's free tool that shows how your site appears in search. Blocking product variants there is correct. When a visitor clicks a product option (color, size), the URL changes with query parameters (extra text after the address, such as ?color=blue&size=large). Search Console marks these variant URLs as blocked by robots.txt (a file that tells Google which pages to skip). This is the correct setup. Only the main product URL should be indexed. Variants must not be indexed, because they are duplicate content (the same content on several addresses).

  • To speed up indexing (getting Google to save a new or updated page) of a single page:

    1. Open Google Search Console.
    2. Paste the full URL into the "Inspect any URL" field.
    3. Click "Test live URL" to confirm Google sees the live page.
    4. Click "Request indexing". Use this for individual pages you want indexed quickly. Google will crawl the site anyway, since the sitemap (a list of your pages) is submitted in Search Console.

  • Google is taking over the Shopify channel, and the same product feed rules still apply for Search Console. Keep the store's main pages linked and indexed.

Facebook and Meta catalog and pixel

3 min

  • Facebook Business Manager is Meta's free tool for running business accounts. Before setting up ads or the store, create these, in order:

    1. Create Facebook Business Manager.
    2. Create a Facebook business page.
    3. Create an Instagram page.
    4. Add the Facebook pixel (a small code that tracks visitors) to your Shopify store.
    5. Enable Auto-Advanced Matching on Facebook. This matches customer details to visitors.

  • Build the store in Shopify, and check that the pixel is connected. Verify that the pixel ID (the number that identifies the pixel) matches on Shopify and in the Facebook app.

  • Install the Meta pixel on the Shopify store, and turn on auto advanced matching before creating any campaign, because campaigns depend on pixel data.

  • A Facebook page and an Instagram page are both needed before ads run, since Meta places ads on both platforms.

  • To connect the pixel to Shopify through the Facebook and Instagram channel app:

    1. In Shopify, open Sales channels and click Facebook & Instagram, then Install.
    2. Click Add sales channel, then Get started.
    3. Click Connect account and connect your personal Facebook account.
    4. Select the business account you created in Business Manager, and grant the maximum available permissions. Then click Save.
    5. Connect the pixel you created, accept the terms and conditions, and click Submit for review.
    6. Once the review is submitted, sales events should flow from the store to Facebook, and tracking runs correctly.

  • To connect Shopify to Meta (a 2022 setup using the Facebook sales channel):

    1. Log in to the Shopify admin.
    2. Under Sales channels, click the plus icon and add Facebook. If Facebook is already installed, open its settings and update it instead.
    3. Continue through the install. Log in to Facebook with your password, and grant access to everything.
    4. Select the correct Business Manager and the correct Facebook page. If an Instagram account is connected, it appears here and can be linked too.
    5. When asked to sync product catalogs, choose create new for a store that should have its own catalog. Name it, and continue.
    6. Select the Facebook pixel used for that store, and continue.
    7. Check the summary. The Business Manager, Facebook page, Instagram profile, product catalog and pixel must all match the store.
    8. Turn on Automatic Advanced Matching, and continue until the screen shows you are all set. Then click done.
    9. Under the Facebook shop option (sell your products on Facebook), click start setup.
    10. Enable data sharing, and choose the Maximum level.
    11. Select the pixel for the store again.
    12. Click create new to create the Meta Commerce account. Review the terms, accept them, and click finish setup.
    13. Back in Shopify, under Sales channels > Facebook, confirm the status reads that you are ready to use Facebook shop.
    14. Verify the result in Commerce Manager (see the data sources and items checks).

  • In Shopify, the Facebook sales channel is still labelled Facebook, even though the platform is now called Meta. Look for it under Sales channels with that name.

  • Facebook Business Manager compliance check (a check of problems with your ads, catalog and billing):

    1. Log in to business.facebook.com.
    2. Click the hamburger menu (the three-line icon) in the top left. Hover over it.
    3. Click Account Quality, or go to Manage Business > Account Quality. This page shows all compliance issues, organized by type (ads, catalog, billing, etc.) and what needs to be fixed.

  • Facebook catalog rejections for some variants can be escalated by contacting Meta support. Use the support options for business accounts, and explain that some variants are approved while others are rejected.

  • If many Instagram followers ask to buy, build a store specifically for Instagram traffic, since Instagram allows a link in the profile. This can be done early, even on the first day of an account.

  • Link the TikTok or Instagram profile to a product page, not to the store home page. Many visitors arrive from a video and go straight to the product page without visiting the homepage, so a homepage link loses sales.

  • Set up the Facebook pixel by pasting the Facebook retargeting pixel code directly into the store's back end (the admin area). Previously, a developer had to add tracking code to every product page, the cart and the checkout. With the pixel in place, conversions and audiences can be built from site behavior.

Pinterest

1 min

  • Install the Pinterest app from the Shopify App Store to connect the store to Pinterest.

  • To claim your website on Pinterest (proving you own the site, called domain verification) with the HTML meta tag method:

    1. In Pinterest, open your profile and click Settings.
    2. Go to the claimed account section and select Claim website.
    3. Click Claim and choose the option to add the HTML tag in the header.
    4. Copy the tag.
    5. In Shopify, open the theme code editor and open the theme.liquid file.
    6. Paste the tag just below the canonical URL line (the main address of the page) in the header.
    7. Save the file.
    8. Return to Pinterest and click Continue.
    9. Enter the store name and click Verify.

  • The Pin It button lets a visitor save a product image from the store to one of their own Pinterest boards. Each pin links back to the original product page on the store. The pin shows up on the user's board and in their followers' home feeds.

  • On Shopify and BigCommerce, the Pin It button and Rich Pins are built into the store and should be enabled automatically. Check that both are switched on, because the store owner is still responsible for that setting.

  • Rich Pins add live product data to a pin, such as the current price and in-stock status, pulled automatically from the store. This extra information makes buyers more informed before they click, which can improve conversion.

TikTok and links

1 min

  • Link TikTok videos to the product page of the product getting attention. Do not link to the catalog or the homepage. Every extra step to find the product increases the chance that visitors leave.

  • Use a single direct product link for TikTok, rather than a homepage. The link then opens the product page immediately. A homepage is not required for an organic product store (one that gets free, unpaid traffic).

  • Multiple TikTok accounts can link to the same Shopify store. Using two separate store links for different pages is generally recommended when you run different pages.

  • Changing the domain of a Shopify store while keeping the TikTok pixel:

    1. In Shopify, open the TikTok channel app, and disconnect it.
    2. Reconnect, and select the same pixel, so the old data is kept.
    3. Enter the new domain URL in the URL field of each new campaign.

Bing Shopping

1 min

  • Bing Shopping is Microsoft's shopping search. In the feed app, go to Settings > Bing Shopping Settings. The field "sale price with price" should be checked. Then the pricing is mapped as follows: Shopify "compare at price" becomes Microsoft "price", and Shopify "price" becomes Microsoft "sale price". If the checkbox is unchecked, the app submits the sale price as "price", even when a discounted price exists. Save the settings afterwards.

  • The Microsoft Merchant Center connection requires the feed app to link the Shopify store with the Microsoft Merchant Center store. The app acts as a bridge between them. Without this connection, the product feed does not reach Bing Shopping.

Google Ads

2 min

  • Google Ads is Google's paid ad tool. Each store also gets its own Google Ads and Merchant Center account. This keeps the numbers of each business separate and easy to see.

  • Redeem the $100 Google Ads credit (free ad money):

    1. In the Shopify admin, open Discounts in the left navigation.
    2. Scroll to the bottom of the page.
    3. Click the small link to redeem the $100 Google credit, which is still labelled AdWords.

  • Shopify admin > Marketing > scroll to the footer menu. There you find the voucher or coupon code for the Google Ads credit (100 USD in 2020). Use it when creating the new Google Ads account.

  • Google Ads audiences (groups of people to show ads to) can be built with your own search terms:

    1. Go to Google Ads > Audiences > New audience > Custom segments > Add new custom segment (your own list of search terms).
    2. Enter a segment name, such as Competitors.
    3. Add the competitor brand names as searched terms, and save.
    4. Select this segment as the audience for Display, Discovery or YouTube campaigns.

  • Google Shopping is the product results area in Google's shopping tab. If the Google Shopping tab is missing, the search location may be a country where Google Shopping is not available. Use a VPN (a tool that makes your connection look like it comes from another country) set to the target country to see the results you need.

  • The displayed price in a search result can use a different currency from the one the local shopper expects. In one example, an Australian searcher saw a price in New Zealand dollars. So check the currency Google displays for your product price, and do not assume it matches your visitors' location.

  • Google Seller Ratings and other review data are separate from ad settings. Keep them accurate.

  • The Google access request asks for access to many Google services. Allow it, because Shopify needs that access to send and receive data between the store and the Google properties.

Account suspension and support

1 min

  • Reinstating a suspended Merchant Center account (an account Google has shut down) takes four steps:

    • Step 1: Go through the store compliance checklist again, and fix every item that is not fully correct.
    • Step 2: Request an appeal in Merchant Center. If no appeal link appears, contact Google through its support pages.
    • Step 3: Use phone support if it is offered. Otherwise, use live chat, which is often the most helpful.
    • Step 4: Expect to follow up several times until the right person reviews the case.

  • To fix an unverified business phone in Google Merchant Center:

    1. Go to Settings, and find the company phone number marked as unverified.
    2. Click Verify.
    3. Answer the automated call Google places to that number.
    4. Enter the five-digit code it gives you. After that, contact Google support again to clear the account message.

  • Google Merchant Center and Google Ads share the same support teams. A support request can go through Google Ads help. Sign in, describe the problem, and use phone support if it is available, or live chat if not.

  • Check whether a change caused a rejection. See the Facebook rejection note above: look at changes made just before the problems started.

  • Shopify's Google Shopping feed app is one of several feed apps. Keep one feed app connected at a time to avoid conflicts.

  • Product identifiers are required before a listing can be approved. If an identifier is wrong, fix it in the field the platform expects.

Older and dated notes

1 min

  • Google Merchant Center and Google Ads notes in this section describe the setup at the time they were written. Check the current rules in Google's help pages before acting on any date-based note, such as the July 2023 Universal Analytics stop date.

  • Google Tag Manager is not needed on day one. It was flagged as an advanced step for later.

More details

  • Shopify draft orders use a separate checkout system to which you cannot add conversion tracking tags; therefore, any sales completed via draft orders will never appear in Google Analytics or Google Ads conversion reports, even though they are real revenue.

  • When checkout links break during purchase, Shopify incorrectly attributes the conversion to itself as a traffic source; in the example shown, this false attribution accounts for 3 percent of total revenue.

  • SKUs can also be added as Shopify tags, but for Google to read them reliably, place the SKU list in the product description as well.

  • Setting the brand in Google Merchant Center via Feed for Google Shopping: open the app settings, go to account linking, and map the brand field to the Shopify Vendor field.

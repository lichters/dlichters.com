Playbook › 3. Suppliers and fulfillment

Shipping setup, policies and delivery expectations

Section 3.13 · 13 of 22 in this chapter · 56 min read

In short

  • Ask suppliers first to ship on their own account. You then get one bill, and they handle damage and returns.
  • Open your own UPS or FedEx account only if the supplier clearly refuses.
  • Insure every shipment.
  • Get a tracking number for every order and send it to the customer.
  • Offer free shipping as the normal choice, and build the shipping cost into your product prices.
More key points
  • Keep the money you keep per sale (margin) at 25 percent or more after shipping.
  • If a supplier's shipping cost is too high, show its products only in nearby regions.
  • Set shipping prices from an average cost, not from one quote.
  • Promise delivery dates you can actually meet, and state them clearly.
  • Ship through the supplier first, because suppliers usually have the best shipping rates.
  • Give every shipping zone at least one shipping rate before launch, or customers cannot check out.
  • Keep shipping cheap: about $10 at most, or build shipping into the product price.
  • Only promise delivery times you have measured with your supplier.
  • Remove all template text and the word "dropshipping" from your shipping policy.
  • Give customers a local tracking number, so they can follow the parcel.
  • Ask your supplier to send that number to your store automatically.
  • Add shipping insurance to every order.
  • Tell customers about possible delays and the fees they may pay.

Using Your Own Carrier Account

1 min

Opening the Account

3 min
  • Do not open a shipping carrier account before contacting suppliers.
  • Ask which carrier the supplier recommends. Suppliers know which carriers already collect at their warehouse (a storage building) at the best rates.
  • For standard UPS or FedEx shipments, create your own account with each carrier.
  • Give the supplier your account numbers, so it ships on your account rather than its own.
  • Create a free UPS or FedEx account online.
  • Share your account number with suppliers. They use their own access to create shipping labels and charge the cost to your account.
  • This requires almost no effort from you once set up.

Setup for small parcels (small packages) when a supplier will not ship on its own account (UPS or FedEx):

  1. Open a free account with ups.com or fedex.com.
  2. Send your carrier account number to the supplier.
  3. The supplier creates the shipping labels on your account for each order.

Shipping options when a supplier does not ship on its own account:

  1. Give the supplier a FedEx or UPS account number so it ships on your account. Both can be opened free online.
  2. If that is not possible, ask the supplier which freight carrier or freight broker it recommends.
  3. Partner with the recommended carrier or broker for those shipments.
  4. For each customer order, place the order with the supplier after the sale.
  5. Then use the chosen shipping method.

When a supplier will not ship on its own account:

  1. Ask the supplier which shipping company or freight broker it recommends. It already works with other retailers and knows which carrier handles most of its orders.
  2. Contact the recommended carrier or broker.
  3. Set up the account or partnership with that company for your shipments.

Choosing a method with a supplier that does not ship on its account (2023):

  1. Contact the supplier and ask about its preferred shipping method.
  2. If items are large and heavy, use a freight broker via freightcenter.com. The supplier may provide a freight account, or you create your own.
  3. If items are standard parcel size, ask the supplier for its UPS or FedEx account number. Or create accounts under your business name.
  4. Communicate the chosen method clearly, so both parties carry it out correctly.
  • Ask suppliers who they recommend as a shipping company. Do not just ask whether they ship on their own account.
  • Suppliers that do not ship themselves still know which carriers their other retailers use.
  • Ask which carriers the supplier recommends and which large retailers it uses.
  • Choose carriers from that list.
  • Ask suppliers which carrier their largest retailers use.
  • Open a business account in your own name.

Opening a business account for a better rate:

  1. Ask the supplier which carrier its top retailers use for shipping.
  2. Contact that carrier and open a business account in your own name.
  3. Tell the carrier where you ship from, and that current pricing is not competitive.
  4. Ask for a discount on the account.
  5. Expect discounts to grow with shipping volume.
  • Template for asking a carrier for a discount:
Hi [carrier contact name],

This is [your name] with [business name]. We have opened a business account with [carrier name] and we ship our products from [ship-from city, state].

The current shipping pricing is not competitive for us. Is there any discount you can apply to our account? We expect our shipping volume to grow, so we would also like to know how discounts increase as we ship more.

Thank you,
[your name]
[business name]
[phone number]
  • Shipping discounts from carriers are usually based on volume (how many packages you send) (2023).
  • Higher volumes through a carrier, or shipping through a supplier who ships a lot on its own account, generally produce the best rates (2023).
  • Shipping services that charge a fee on top of carrier rates are only useful for stores with their own warehouse and high shipping volume.
  • For drop-ship stores, these services are generally not worth the cost.
  • Before accepting a third-party group shipping discount (a deal from a company outside you and the carrier), compare its rates against your actual shipping lanes. Lanes are the routes your packages travel.
  • Some lanes may get better rates while others get worse. Do not assume a group deal is a good deal for everyone.

  • Large, heavy items need a different carrier (2025).

  • International UPS shipping is typically very expensive.
  • If the cost looks unreasonable to you, customers will likely see it the same way.
  • Sales reps (salespeople) at supplier brands are the best source for shipping advice.
  • Ask which carrier they prefer, what pricing they get, and how their largest online retailers ship.
  • Before you use the same carrier as a supplier's other retailers, check its label status. Label status shows whether the shipping label has been created and scanned.
  • If a supplier has a label pending status with FedEx for more than about 24 hours, call the carrier.

  • Do not pay the markup.
  • If a supplier offers a better price when you handle shipping yourself, ask whether it means you buy the stock in advance (buy products before customers order).
  • Or ask whether it means you pay for shipping.
  • If you ship with your own UPS or FedEx account, or through a freight broker's bill of lading, this can save money.
  • Ask the supplier whether it can use the same shipping method you select (2023).
  • If the price still does not match, shipping on your own account is the next step (2023).

Matching the carrier price with your own UPS account (2023):

  1. Ask the supplier to ship with the same shipping method you select, so the price matches your quote.
  2. If it cannot match it, open a free UPS account for your business.
  3. Give the supplier your UPS account number.
  4. The supplier creates each shipment in UPS and enters your account number in the billing section.
  5. UPS bills you for the shipping. You pay the supplier only for the cost of the goods.
  • Template for querying a supplier's higher shipping price (2023):
Hi [Supplier contact name], we were reviewing the quotes for our shipments and noticed yours are substantially higher than the ones we get from UPS. Just wanted to make sure you are choosing the same shipping method, or are we missing something here? Could you check whether you can use the shipping method we select so the price matches? [Your name]
[Company name]
  • Supplier surcharges (extra fees) for shipping on their own account are a reason to compare with self-arranged carrier quotes before accepting.
  • For example, a surcharge may be 5 dollars on regular items and 25 dollars on large items.
  • Shipping on the supplier's account versus on the seller's account works like this. When the supplier ships on its own account, it charges the product plus shipping in one invoice.
  • When the seller ships on its own UPS or FedEx account, the supplier charges only the product. The carrier bills the shipping separately.

Negotiating shipping costs with a supplier:

  1. Bring real numbers to the supplier. Include the number of sales, where orders shipped, shipping costs, and the margin (profit left per sale) you are left with.
  2. Ask whether the supplier can ship through its preferred carriers on its own account, which often gives better rates.
  3. Ask for incentivized pricing (special prices offered to encourage a deal) or a wholesale discount (a lower price for buying in bulk for resale). The product then becomes profitable for both sides.
  4. If the supplier ships through freight, get separate freight quotes from freight brokers. Compare them with the supplier's rate.
  • Template for asking a supplier to cover shipping on its account and cut costs:
Hi [Supplier name], last year we spent about [98,000] with you on shipping and fulfillment. Your shipping volume is likely higher than ours, so could you check whether your freight accounts or carrier rates can cover our orders at a lower cost? Our target for next year is about [75,000], and I would like to agree on a plan to reach it. Could we set up a call this week to review the rates? Thank you, [Your name].
  • Some suppliers do not coordinate shipping, or their shipping is too expensive. So the store may use a separate freight provider such as UPS.
  • In that case, product cost and shipping cost must be tracked separately.
  • Both need to be compared against the supplier's pricing.
  • Record each supplier's shipping and fulfilment details. Fulfilment means packing and sending orders.
  • These details help an assistant arrange pickups. Include supplier pickup times, warehouse hours, the contact person, and which carriers collect from the supplier's area.

Insurance, Damage and Claims

1 min
  • Insure every shipment. Insurance is the baseline protection (the minimum safety net), regardless of which carrier is used.
  • Insurance stops occasional damage from costing the store money.
  • Insure every shipment, whether the supplier ships it or you ship from your own shipping account (2025).
  • This way, damaged or lost goods can be replaced without an out-of-pocket loss (money you pay from your own pocket) (2025).
  • Insurance is selected as an option when the shipment is created (2025).
  • Shipments sent to customers should be insured against loss and transit damage.
  • Suppliers usually insure shipments made on their own accounts.
  • Insure shipments yourself only if damage is rare with that supplier (2024).
  • If the supplier ships on its own account, it handles claims (2024).
  • Before paying extra for specialized shipping on fragile goods (easily broken items), check each supplier's products for complaints:
    1. Search Google for the supplier's name and product names together with "reviews".
    2. Look for repeated mentions of returns, refunds or damage from other stores' customers.
    3. If no clear pattern appears, ship with a recommended carrier and insure every shipment.
    4. Only use costlier specialized shippers if damage remains a recurring problem.
  • Check the carrier's insurance terms before relying on insurance.
  • Some policies exclude parcels shipped in manufacturer boxes printed with product names or SKUs (product codes). This can lead to denied damage claims.
  • Insurance is covered by the supplier when it ships on its account. The store can then focus on tracking the claim and the replacement.

Tracking and Multi-Supplier Shipments

1 min
  • Require suppliers to send a tracking number (a code to follow the package) when an order ships.
  • A supplier that offers no tracking at all is a risk. Ask it to at least reply to purchase orders (orders you place with the supplier) with tracking numbers.
  • In-stock orders should have tracking information sent within 24 hours of the order leaving the warehouse.
  • If the customer has none after four business days, they should follow up by email.
  • Customers accept multiple packages if they receive tracking numbers (2024).
  • When customers order products that ship from multiple suppliers, send tracking information for each shipment as it ships separately (2024).
  • Handling a multi-item order shipped from different suppliers:
    1. Expect the items to ship separately from different warehouses, and tell the customer this is normal.
    2. When part of an order ships, enter the tracking number in the Shopify order backend (the admin page where store orders are managed). Enter it for only the products that shipped.
    3. Keep the customer updated so each separate shipment is expected and understood.
  • When a store's products come from several suppliers, supplier-specific shipping costs can make some products unprofitable.
  • Check shipping cost and the supplier's location before deciding where to advertise.
  • Free tracked shipping instead of standard shipping can increase trust.
  • It can also reduce checkout drop-off (customers leaving before they pay).
  • Weekly shipments for bulk ordering:
    1. Collect all orders placed Monday to Friday.
    2. On Friday, batch-ship (send as one group) all week's orders to DHL (a shipping company) in one consolidated shipment (combined into one).
    3. Do this instead of sending individual drops.
  • Regional assignment of suppliers reduces shipping times. Instead of one centralized warehouse (one main storage site), assign suppliers by region (2025).
  • For example, Northeast suppliers handle Northeast orders, and Southeast suppliers handle Southeast orders (2025).
  • This speeds delivery without increasing costs (2025).

Free Shipping and Pricing It In

1 min

Deciding whether to build shipping into the price

2 min
  • Offer free shipping on all products as the base option.
  • The business pays the carrier (the shipping company) for each parcel.
  • Rates are checked after orders come in. They are not worked out at checkout (the payment step).
  • Free shipping is paid out of the seller's own margin (the money kept per sale after costs).
  • Sell a product under a free-shipping threshold (the minimum order amount for free shipping) for example under 3,500 only if the margin covers the shipping cost.
  • Shipping fees vary by supplier (the company that ships your products) (2025).
  • Because the store offers free shipping on everything, build the shipping cost into product prices.
  • Each order must still leave a minimum of 25 percent net profit (money left after all costs).
  • If a supplier cannot be priced to cover shipping, limit its products in advertising to nearby regions.
  • If you charge shipping at checkout, decide whether to build it into the price or charge it separately.
  • To decide, check what the top 2 or 3 competitors do for the same brand (2023).
  • Free shipping is the usual standard in these niches (product groups), so it does not set you apart.
  • Check what the top two to five competitors charge for shipping, and price your products to match.
  • Those stores are your direct rivals on Google Shopping (Google's product listing ads) (2024).
  • Free shipping from suppliers is a plus when you launch a store with a few suppliers (2024).
  • When a supplier says shipping is free, confirm whether the shipping cost is built into the product price.
  • This changes the margin calculation.
  • Passing international shipping costs to the buyer is possible only if the final price still works for the product.
  • Shipping should never cost more than the product itself.
  • For heavy items, avoid real-time shipping calculation (a shipping cost worked out live in the store).
  • Instead, build the shipping cost into the product price and offer free shipping, if the product is still profitable.
  • Shipping that a supplier charges directly to the customer can be handled in one of two ways.
  • Option one: add the cost to the product price.
  • Option two: create a separate Shopify (the online store software) shipping profile (a set of shipping rules) for that supplier's products (2024).
  • Some suppliers require the seller to create and send a shipping label (the printed postage for a parcel).
  • In that case, the store pays that shipping cost directly.
  • For heavy items, check this cost before listing. It can exceed the margin and make the product unprofitable (2024).
  • A competitor may advertise free international shipping for a bulky or heavy product. Cross-border (between countries) shipping of large or heavy goods is very costly.
  • There are two possible explanations. Either the cost is built into an expensive price, or the seller stocks the same product in warehouses in both countries.
  • A claim of truly free shipping only works for very high-priced, small items.
  • Check which explanation applies before copying the model.
  • Free shipping is excluded for Hawaii and Alaska, because it is too expensive to offer for free.

Calculating an average shipping cost (2018 method)

3 min
  1. Note each approved supplier's shipping origin (where it ships from) and where your customers are located, for example the share of sales per state.
  2. Ask each supplier for shipping quotes (price offers) for its most popular product to those regions, ideally while applying for approval.
  3. Calculate an average shipping cost per product. Weight it by the share of your business in each region, for example a 50 percent split between two regions.
  4. Add that average into the product price. Do not mark the price up more for shipping.
  5. Check that the resulting net margin still meets the 25 percent minimum while free shipping is offered.
  6. If the margin still does not cover shipping for a supplier, limit that supplier's products in advertising to nearby regions (see geo-targeting, showing ads only in chosen regions).
  • Example: a stand-up paddle board costs about 50 dollars to ship within the same state.
  • The same board costs about 150 dollars to ship from a supplier in a distant state.
  • If half of the customers are in each zone, the average of 100 dollars is the figure to build into the price.
  • If a product's shipping cost can use up nearly all of its net profit per order, do not sell it to the expensive regions.
  • For example, 150 dollars of profit against 150 dollars of shipping is too little.
  • This situation should be rare.
  • If a supplier cannot quote shipping for individual items, ask for package specifications (the size and weight of each item and box).
  • Ask for them in a spreadsheet or CSV (a plain spreadsheet file).
  • Then estimate costs from those numbers.
  • Template for requesting shipping quotes from a supplier:
Hi [Supplier name], we have been approved and are preparing our store. Most of our customers are in [State A], about [X]% of our business, and we also sell to [State B]. What would it cost to ship your most popular product, [product name], to a [State A] address and to a [State B] address? Please include the package weight and dimensions with each quote.
  • Estimating a typical shipping cost from a supplier's warehouse (2025):
    1. Get the zip code (US postal code) of the supplier's warehouse.
    2. Request sample shipping quotes from that warehouse to several random US zip codes. For example, New Jersey to New York, Florida, Texas and California.
    3. Average the quotes to estimate the typical shipping cost per order.
    4. Use this average, not a single quote, when calculating margins.
  • Track the shipping cost against the margin before you cut prices.
  • Shipping costs can quickly erase margin on heavy items.
  • So apply the calculated average, not a single quote, when setting prices.
  • Shipping costs can jump sharply when a product moves into a higher shipping size class (a size group carriers use to set prices).
  • Even a one-inch increase in length can matter.
  • Ask suppliers for the dimensions that define each shipping class.
  • Also ask which packing materials they use.
  • Do this before deciding how to box or group products.
  • Enter accurate product shipping dimensions for every item, especially large ones.
  • Wrong sizes can make shipping costs higher than needed.
  • Enter package sizes such as box dimensions in Shopify so that carrier-calculated rates (rates the carrier works out) are accurate.
  • Branded mailer boxes (shipping boxes printed with a logo) can be sourced from a friend's business in exchange for free packaging.
  • The parcel then carries that business's branding as part of the arrangement.
  • This lowers packaging cost in the early phase of a small brand.

Tracking

1 min

  • To give customers a local tracking number instead of a China-origin number, follow this procedure. A tracking number is a code that lets the customer follow their parcel. China-origin means the parcel started in China.

    1. Ask the supplier or agent to ship with a local carrier. A carrier is the delivery company, such as USPS, Royal Mail or Australia Post. Also ask them to hide the origin information.
    2. Ask them to send the local number to Shopify automatically once the courier issues it. Shopify is the software your online store runs on. A courier is the company that delivers the parcel and issues the number.
    3. If no local number appears quickly, send the original tracking number and ask them to hide the origin.

    The local number often appears the same day or within a few hours for destinations like UK, Germany, Australia and New Zealand. For other destinations, it may only appear when the parcel arrives in the destination country.

  • A tracking number that covers only the first leg (for example a China number) may not show delivery progress or days left. A leg is one stage of the trip. The local carrier number is needed for that. Tracking with a local carrier like FedEx or DHL shows the remaining days.

  • Once an order ships, the customer receives a tracking number by email so they can follow the package.

  • Procedure for Google Merchant Center shipping: Google Merchant Center is Google's tool for showing your products on Google.

    1. Check the Google Merchant Center shipping settings.
    2. Locate the free shipping entry. An entry is one line in the settings.
    3. Enter the average delivery timeframe for the whole catalog. A catalog is your full list of products.

    The delivery time applies to the whole catalog, not to each product.

Multi-Supplier and Add-On Shipments

1 min
  • Products sold as add-ons or upsells should come from the same supplier as the main product. An add-on is an extra item sold with the main product. An upsell is an extra offer to buy something more. This way the order ships together without an extra shipping fee. Items from another source ship separately and need their own handling.
  • Use a separate delivery template for each supplier whose window differs from the default. A delivery template is a saved set of shipping times and rules. A window is the number of days it takes to deliver.
  • Record each supplier's shipping arrangement separately in the table above, because each one may book its own carrier.

Shipping Insurance and Damage

1 min
  • Add shipping insurance to every shipment. Insurance pays out if a parcel is lost or damaged. When the supplier ships from its own account, insurance is typically the default. The seller should confirm it in advance. An account here is the shipping account used to send the parcel.
  • Who handles problems depends on whose account is used. A claim is a request for money back from the insurer. A supplier shipping on its own account handles replacements and claims. A store shipping on its own account must file a claim and wait for repayment.

Customs, Duties and Cross-Border Shipments

1 min
  • When a product ships from stock in the same country, the customer pays no extra import fees. Stock means products kept in a warehouse, ready to ship. Import fees are charges for bringing goods into a country. Customs clearance only matters for cross-border shipments. Customs clearance is the official check of goods at the border. Cross-border means across a country's border.
  • For cross-border shipments, carriers such as FedEx, UPS and DHL usually handle customs clearance. They contact the customer when duties must be paid. Duties are taxes on imported goods. In practice, the seller typically pays those fees. Some carriers were not verified for this.
  • Delivery times can be affected by customs, weather and other factors outside the store's control. Policy means your written store rules. Name these factors in your policy. This sets expectations and reduces complaints about delays.
  • For international orders where product dimensions vary widely, the store can give shipping prices on request rather than publishing one rate. Dimensions means the size of the parcel. A quote is a price offered for one specific order. The customer confirms the quote before the order and payment are taken. Payment goes through a secure online payment gateway. A payment gateway is the service that processes card payments. This approach suits low-volume stores, meaning stores with few orders, that cannot yet predict shipping cost per destination.
  • Tell customers that international delivery can take longer than the stated estimate and that you only ship to selected countries. Customers should also receive a tracking number by email once the order ships.
  • Customers pay customs fees, taxes and import duties on international orders. Your policy should state this.

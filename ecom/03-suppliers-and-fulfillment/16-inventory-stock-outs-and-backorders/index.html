Playbook › 3. Suppliers and fulfillment

Inventory, stock-Outs and backorders

Section 3.16 · 16 of 22 in this chapter · 36 min read

In short

  • Track stock only for your top 20% of products, the ones that make about 80% of sales.
  • Ask each supplier how it shares stock levels, and confirm it for every supplier.
  • Keep ads running only when the restock date is about 2 to 3 weeks away or less.
  • Set stock to zero only when a customer cannot order the product at all.
  • Verify stock with the supplier before you sell or promise delivery.
More key points
  • Offer backorders (orders taken now for stock you do not have yet) only when you can give a real delivery date.
  • Tell customers the shipping date before they pay, and again in the order confirmation.
  • Do not sell a supplier's products when the supplier has no restock date.
  • Pre-buy stock only for an established store with a proven sales history.
  • In Q4, the hard part is delivering orders on time, not getting sales.
  • Do not sell products you cannot deliver before the holiday. Mid-December was the cutoff used.
  • Keep enough stock, and talk to your supplier every week. Silence can close your store.
  • Never show sold-out products as available, and never trust the stock list alone.

1 min

Backorders and customer communication

1 min

Accepting backorders

1 min
  • Offer backorders for out-of-stock products when you can state a reasonable delivery window. (A backorder means the customer orders now, and the item ships when stock returns.) Many customers will wait for an item they cannot get elsewhere.
  • Under-promise and over-deliver on backorders: quote a longer lead time than the expected one. (Lead time is the wait from order to delivery.) Example: if stock is expected in four weeks, promise six weeks, so early delivery earns better reviews and referrals (customers recommending you to others).
  • Backorders can be accepted, but the order confirmation should give the expected ship date. It should also state that the product is backordered. This prevents complaints and refund requests later. (2020)
  • Only pre-sell a backordered product when the supplier has given a reliable ETA. (Pre-selling means selling before you hold the stock. A supplier is the company that holds the stock and ships it. ETA means estimated time of arrival, the date stock is expected.) Customers will cancel or complain if a long backorder is not communicated. (2021)
  • Fast-selling products that run low at the supplier can be listed as back-order with a deposit option (a deposit is part of the price paid up front) (see above; older advice, 2019).
  • If a supplier is out of stock and gives no restock date (the day new stock arrives), do not sell or advertise that supplier's products. If restock is coming soon in a meaningful quantity, pre-selling can make sense. Decide supplier by supplier. (2021)

The notice should state: due to the coronavirus, supplier ABC is currently not shipping until [estimated date] so customers make informed decisions before payment. (2020)
  • Warehouse closures in major hubs can delay shipping and require special arrangements with customers. (2020)

Procedure: handling a backordered product

1 min
  1. Check the ETA for the backordered item with the supplier before speaking to the customer. (2020)
  2. Look for a similar product in your store, or find one from another supplier at a similar or lower price. A slightly more expensive alternative can also work if the extra cost is small. (2020)
  3. Contact the customer by phone, or have the phone team do it. Explain that the ETA applies to every seller of the product. (2020)
  4. If the customer will wait, offer to ship first out the door when stock arrives. If the wait is long, give the new ship date and make the same offer. (2020)
  5. If the customer needs the product sooner, offer the alternative. A cheaper alternative can come with a partial refund (money back on part of the price), for example $100, and ship within 24 to 48 hours. A slightly more expensive alternative can ship with no extra charge to the customer. (2020)
  6. If no similar product exists, look for similar products even from smaller or less established brands. (2020)

Procedure: order check before taking payment

1 min
  1. When an order arrives for a product that may be out of stock, check inventory with the supplier or warehouse before taking payment. (Inventory is the stock you hold or can get.) (2020)
  2. If it is unavailable, email or call the customer to say the card has not been charged yet. (2020)
  3. Tell the customer the backorder length. (2020)
  4. Let the customer choose to keep the order pending (waiting, not final) or cancel it. Release any pending charge (undo a payment that is waiting to be taken). (2020)

Procedure: out-of-stock product with no confirmed ETA, offer a similar item

1 min
  1. Ask the supplier for an ETA, and relay it (pass it on) to the customer if one exists. (2024)
  2. If there is no ETA, look through your own catalog (your full list of products for sale) for the closest similar product. Match type, size and color, for example a white standing desk about 80 inches long. (2024)
  3. Contact the customer and explain that the warehouse is out of the item, with no confirmed return date (no set day the item comes back). (2024)
  4. Offer the similar product. State the price difference in dollars, and say whether it is in stock and can ship the next day. (2024)
  5. Ask whether the customer wants the order changed to the replacement product. (2024)

Procedure: backorder strategy with suppliers

This product is currently out of stock and is expected back on [date] / has no clear restock date. Here are similar products we have in stock and ready to ship today: [product 1 link], [product 2 link], [product 3 link].

Confirming a backorder in the order email

1 min
  • After a purchase of a backordered item, send an order confirmation email to the buyer. Ask them to confirm they saw the backorder note and accept the estimated ship date. (2024)
  • After the order comes in, manually email the customer to confirm the ETA for shipping. (2024)
  • If a customer still orders organically (without a paid ad, through search or word of mouth), email them after the order to confirm they accept the ETA. (2024)

Handling payment for backorders

1 min

Shopify settings without an inventory app

1 min

Shopify is the online shop software. An inventory app is an add-on that tracks stock for you. Use this procedure when no inventory app is installed. (2021)

  1. Keep a product listed as in stock while it is available or expected back soon. Show an estimated arrival date on its page. (2021)
  2. Mark the item out of stock only when its backorder typically lasts a month or more, and you do not want new orders. (2021)
  3. Set Shopify so checkout preauthorizes the customer's card without charging it. (Preauthorize means reserving the amount on the card without taking it. Checkout is the payment step.) (2021)
  4. Capture the payment (actually take the money) only after the supplier confirms the item is in stock. (2021)
  5. For items with a long lead time, do not capture the payment, which cancels the charge without a refund. (2021)

Pre-orders and made-to-order products

1 min

Pre-orders

1 min
  • Pre-orders (customers pay now for a product not yet in stock) are a workable approach when suppliers are out of stock. Reserve inventory for pre-order customers, and set up delivery deals with customers so shipments are not missed. (2020)
  • Pre-order buyers should be informed of shipping timing and warehouse status. Warehouse closures in major hubs can delay shipping and require special arrangements with customers. (2020)
  • Standard stock-based fashion needs a lot of cash, because stock must be bought up front. A pre-order model reduces this cash flow pressure (the money you must have ready at one time).
  • When a dropship supplier (a supplier who ships orders straight to your customer) says stock will arrive in about a week, the seller can start running a pre-order or a "high demand, secure yours before stock runs out" message in the meantime. Ask the supplier whether stock is limited per batch or can be replenished continuously, and whether they will hold inventory for the seller.

Made-to-order and custom products

1 min
  • Sell made-to-order products (made after the order is placed) only if they are made fairly close to the customer, and customers are told about the lead time before ordering. Presenting the custom production as a benefit works better than presenting it as a delay. (2021)
  • Made-to-order furniture that ships after about 10 days was accepted by customers when the page explained that it was custom made locally. Customers were willing to wait for it. (2021)
  • Products with a long lead time, such as 16 weeks for made-to-order items, should be marked on the product page right by the add-to-cart button, with the production and delivery estimate. Taking full payment at the time of order is appropriate when the item is custom made.

Procedure: made-to-order item with a one-to-two-week production window

1 min
  1. Put a note beside the add-to-cart button on the product page. Quote the supplier's exact phrasing, such as saying the item is made to order and dispatches within one to two weeks. (2024)
  2. Once a buyer places an order, email them by hand to confirm they understand the build time. (2024)
  3. After the customer replies with their confirmation, forward the order to the supplier. (2024)

Confirmation email for a made-to-order order:

Subject: Confirming your order [order number]

Hi [customer name],

Thank you for your order of [product name]. Just want to confirm that you're aware this product is built to order and ships in [lead time, for example one to two weeks].

Please reply to confirm, and we will send your order to production right away.

Thank you,
[store name]

Deciding whether to pre-sell

1 min
  • If a supplier is out of stock and gives no restock date, do not sell or advertise that supplier's products. If restock is coming soon in a meaningful quantity, pre-selling can make sense. Decide supplier by supplier. (2021)
  • Only pre-sell a backordered product when the supplier has given a reliable ETA. Customers will cancel or complain if a long backorder is not communicated. (2021)

Substitutions and replacements

1 min

Choosing a substitute

1 min
  • If a supplier is out of stock but the product still shows as available on your store, offer customers compatible alternatives. This can turn orders that would have been lost into sales. Keep the availability status in mind when managing supplier inventory. (2025)
  • When an ordered product is out of stock, handle it in this order: 1) Order a similar product as a replacement. 2) If no similar product is available, find new suppliers that offer an alternative product.
  • When an ordered product goes out of stock, ship the in-stock items first and send the missing bundle component (an item sold as part of a set) later. Suppliers usually restock accessories quickly, and customers accept the split if they got the deal.
  • Some dropshipping suppliers, when a product runs out, ship a different brand's product without asking the store owner. Check supplier practices early, and set rules for substitutions, because this damages the brand and customer trust.
  • For an out-of-stock product with no ETA, the replacement offer is the step in the procedure above (see ).

Procedure: similar in-stock products on the product page

1 min
  1. Cut paid ad spend (money paid for ads) for the product page. (2024)
  2. Add a note next to the add-to-cart button saying the item is out of stock, and showing the ETA if one is known. (2024)
  3. Keep the page live so free organic traffic (visitors who arrive without paid ads) can still arrive. (2024)
  4. Below the note, show a section with similar products that are in stock and ship within 24 hours, so organic visitors can still buy. (2024)

Discontinued products

1 min
  • When a supplier discontinues a product (stops making or selling it), keep its product page but mark it as discontinued. Show two or three similar products that are still in stock, and stop running ads to that page.
  • Keep discontinued or unavailable products on the website as not orderable, with no ads, so they still pick up organic search traffic. (2020)
  • Products that are discontinued by a supplier are handled with the substitution steps above.

Case: discontinued model with a phantom stock unit

1 min

A store received an order for a discontinued model. The supplier's inventory system listed a stock unit that did not exist (a phantom stock unit). The store explained the situation to the supplier's representative, who was not responsive at first. The store then renegotiated the price difference, got the old dealer cost (the price the supplier charged the store), and charged the customer the new price. The store made more profit on that unit than it would have with the correct price.

Case: a discontinued product on a government order

1 min

A government agency placed a sizable order. The manufacturer (the company that makes the product) had discontinued the product that same month, so the order could not be fulfilled as planned. Rather than cancel, the seller contacted two manufacturers who had earlier rejected them because they had not been in the industry long enough. Both agreed to fulfill the order. After the customer approved the change, the seller shipped a product of higher quality than the original. The seller kept the customer as a repeat buyer and added a new supplier with a new catalog.

Shortages and supplier reliability

1 min
  • When a supplier runs short of stock, every seller who uses that supplier pushes the same limited inventory. So shift traffic and ad spend to suppliers that still have stock, and strengthen those relationships. Suppliers that stay reliable during shortages tend to become the strongest long-term partners.
  • When supply is limited, work with the available suppliers, upload the approved products, and optimize them (improve their pages and details). Circle back to the other suppliers once supply normalizes. (2022)
  • If many product pages from one supplier are out of stock across different competitor stores, the supplier may be out of business, and it is worth reconsidering them. A single out-of-stock product is not a warning sign on its own. (2021)
  • Overseas dropshipping shipments can take several months to arrive, which damages trust and repeat purchases. Keeping your top 10 best sellers in stock locally, where possible, shortens delivery and reduces buyer hesitation.
  • In dropshipping, keep your top 10 best sellers in stock locally, if possible, to offer faster delivery to your best customers. This reduces the long wait that makes buyers hesitate.
  • Fitness and hobby niches (narrow product markets, such as fitness gear) had the longest supplier lead times around Q4 (the last three months of the year, October to December). The cause was inventory and shipping congestion (jams of stock and shipments). Inventory is expected to normalize around Q1 of the following year (January to March).
  • Chinese suppliers often have a large backlog (a pile-up of unfilled orders) and slow replies in the weeks after Chinese New Year (the major holiday when many Chinese factories slow down). Plan ad spend and stock with delays of two to three weeks in mind.
  • Demand for home gym and workout equipment spiked during the 2020 COVID-19 lockdowns (the stay-at-home orders of the pandemic), and popular items sold out quickly. (See also the factory backlog note above.)

Price changes

1 min
  • Price changes from suppliers are communicated by email to all retailers, with the new prices and effective dates. So you normally do not need to check prices constantly. (2021)
  • Suppliers normally send price changes by email with an effective date. Product integrations for pricing or stock are often not available. Track price changes by watching supplier emails and their effective dates. (2025)
  • Suppliers usually announce price changes by email to their retailer list, stating the effective date and time, and attaching the new price sheet. If a supplier changes prices without notice, ask them to include you in all future price-update emails, so you can update prices before they take effect. (2025)
  • Most suppliers update pricing about once a year at most. They usually send a price sheet plus emails about new products or price changes, so weekly price checks are not needed. (2025)
  • Update product pages only when a supplier reports a change, such as a new size, SKU, variation or price change. Create a new product page for the new item, and link to it from the old page. (2026)

Procedure: new size, SKU, variation or price change

1 min
  1. Wait for the supplier to notify you of a new size, SKU, variation, or price change. (2026)
  2. Create a new product page for the new item. (2026)
  3. Put a link on the old product page pointing to the new one. (2026)
  4. Update the old page as needed. (2026)

Pre-buying inventory

1 min

Principles and caution

1 min
  • Do not pre-buy inventory from a supplier just to get a discount. Pre-buy only when your sales volume (how much you sell) is high enough that you need to secure stock. (2020)
  • Buying inventory in advance only makes sense once you have a bankroll (money set aside for spending) and a reliable sales history for a product. A common approach is to place a deposit before the busiest season, such as Q4, while the supplier keeps the stock in its warehouse and continues to drop ship. (2021)
  • Pre-buying stock keeps inventory secured while you can still dropship the product. Other options are a third-party fulfillment center (an outside company that stores and sends your orders), which can get expensive, or your own warehouse, which makes you run a separate fulfillment business (storing, packing and shipping orders yourself). (2026)
  • Do not ask a supplier to pre-stock inventory for a new store. Pre-purchasing inventory only makes sense for an established store with years of sales history with that supplier, checked before the Q4 peak season (the busiest sales period), and only for products where stock is running low. (2024)
  • Do not buy inventory to supply a marketplace such as Walmart (a large online store where many sellers list products). If you want to test it, list only a few brands whose suppliers reliably keep stock, instead of the whole catalog.

Pre-purchase before the holidays

1 min
  • Pre-purchase inventory from a supplier you sell consistently when stock runs low, such as before the holidays. The supplier holds it in its warehouse and ships when customers order, avoiding the need for your own warehousing.

Procedure: pre-purchased inventory for a best-selling product that sells out often

1 min
  1. Estimate average monthly units sold (single items), for example 20 units a month that mostly sell in the first 10 days. (2026)
  2. Ask the supplier to hold a higher monthly quantity in their warehouse, for example 40 units, and ship orders for you. (2026)
  3. Ask for a discount for ordering upfront. Suppliers often agree, since bigger orders give them faster cash and bigger manufacturer orders. (2026)

Q4 and holiday capacity planning

1 min

Q4 means the last three months of the year, October to December. Capacity means how many orders your supplier can really fill and ship.

Why Q4 is the hardest part

1 min
  • In Q4 (the last three months of the year), the hard part is not getting sales. The hard part is deliver reliably and on time, because buyers are already in a buying mood. Stock availability (having enough product on hand) and shipping time are the key bottlenecks.
  • During the holiday season, do not add products whose delivery before the holiday cannot be guaranteed. Mid-December was the cutoff used for products that would not arrive in time.

Procedure: Q4 logistics with a supplier-based approach

1 min

A supplier is the company that stocks your products and ships them to customers.

  1. Do not jump on products that are already selling strongly without securing supply (making sure enough stock exists), because the supply often runs out first.
  2. Limit the number of product variants (versions of one product, such as colors or sizes). For example, reduce from five colors to two.
  3. Buy a small stock (roughly 50 to 100 pieces) and place it in a warehouse in China.
  4. With guaranteed stock, you can predict supply, fulfill orders (pack and ship them) faster, and guarantee shorter shipping times. The same advice applies to Chinese New Year, a major holiday in China when factories and shipping slow down.

Weekly supplier calls and communication

1 min
  • Schedule weekly video calls (on Zoom or Skype) with your supplier during Q4 planning and sales periods. Use them to verify fulfillment capacity (check they can ship all orders), confirm inventory levels (count the stock), and troubleshoot issues (fix problems) in real time. Missed communication during high-volume periods (times with many orders) causes supply chain failures (breaks in getting products to buyers). This leads to chargebacks (refunds forced by the bank after a complaint) and account suspensions (accounts shut down).
  • A store with $500,000 in revenue was lost in one Q4 when the supplier quietly ran out of stock and did not communicate for 2-3 weeks. Daily sales of $3,000-$4,000 continued, but no shipments left the warehouse. Customer chargebacks piled up, the payment processor (the company that handles card payments) terminated the account, and Facebook shut down the ad account (the account used to run paid ads) because of the complaint volume.

Scaling capacity and event lead times

1 min

Scaling means spending more on ads to sell more. Lead time means how long it takes from order to delivery.

  • Before scaling a product, have the supplier backend ready. The backend is the supplier's order system and stock. It must include reliable suppliers and stock available to fulfill the orders that ads will generate.
  • Ask the supplier for the maximum number of orders they can handle comfortably per day, and use that number as the scaling threshold (the limit where you stop increasing ads). Suppliers often over-promise (claim more than they can really do), so set the threshold lower than their stated maximum.
  • Ask the supplier how long processing takes for your actual order volume. Processing time (time to pack an order) can rise sharply during high demand. In one case, it went from about one week to much longer.
  • Running out of stock during scaling causes angry customers and a fall in revenue (money coming in). Plan supplier capacity before increasing ad budgets (the money spent on ads).
  • For date-specific events such as Valentine's Day, count the supplier's shipping time, which was around two weeks in one example. Start ads about four weeks before the event so the product arrives in time and refunds are avoided.

Mistakes that cost sales

1 min
  • Running out of inventory (stock) means no sales and no income. The seller must monitor stock levels closely.
  • Running out of stock during scaling causes angry customers and a fall in revenue.
  • Marking products as sold out with zero inventory while customers can still buy them can lead Google to suspend a Merchant Center account (the account that lists your products on Google Shopping).
  • Some dropshipping suppliers (suppliers who ship for you) ship a different brand's product when a product runs out, without asking the store owner.
  • A supplier's system can show stock that does not exist. Do not rely on the inventory feed (the automatic stock list sent to your store) alone for an order.

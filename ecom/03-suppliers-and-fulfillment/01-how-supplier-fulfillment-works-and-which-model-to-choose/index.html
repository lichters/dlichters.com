Playbook › 3. Suppliers and fulfillment

How supplier fulfillment works and which model to choose

Section 3.1 · 1 of 22 in this chapter · 10 min read

In short

  • In drop shipping, your store holds no stock. The supplier ships each order straight to the customer.
  • Test products with a dropshipping supplier first. Switch to a private supplier (one that works only with you) after the product sells well.
  • Use one main supplier when packaging and branding matter. Use two or more when you want to compare customer experience.
  • Move a product to an outside warehouse company when it sells about 1,000 units per month.
This section has 7 parts. Tap one to open it.

Core Order Flow in Drop Shipping

1 min

The basic fulfillment sequence

  • In a drop shipping model, the store holds no stock. Stock means the goods you keep on hand to sell.

  • Each customer order triggers a separate order with the supplier. The supplier ships the goods directly to the customer.

  • Fulfillment means handling an order from start to finish.

  • A connected supplier or fulfillment company does these steps:

    • It receives each order.
    • It inspects the product quality.
    • It packs the product.
    • It ships the product directly to the customer.
    • It handles after-sales issues, such as refunds and returns (problems after the sale).

The standard sequence is:

  1. The customer places an order on your store.
  2. You pass the order information to your supplier or manufacturer.
  3. The supplier picks and packs the order.
  4. The customer receives the order at their doorstep.

Payment and supplier charging

Shopify is a platform for building online stores. The 2020 Shopify-based flow works as follows:

  1. The customer pays on the Shopify store. Shopify Payments (Shopify's own payment system) pays the money out within 48 hours.
  2. Ask the supplier to ship the specific products to the customer's address.
  3. The supplier charges your card and ships the item to the customer.
  4. Confirm with each supplier when it charges the card. Timing differs per supplier.

Payment and supplier charging

The 2021-22 flow for a single order is:

  1. The customer buys the product on the store and pays immediately.
  2. The seller receives the payment in the bank account.
  3. The seller places an order with the supplier for that exact product and asks the supplier to ship directly to the customer's address.
  4. The seller pays the supplier.
  5. The supplier packs and ships the product from its warehouse. It is delivered to the customer's home.

Choosing and Managing Drop Shipping Suppliers

2 min

Test first, then specialize

  • A supplier is a company that stores and ships products for you.

  • Start testing products with dropshipping suppliers such as CJ or Hyper SKU. These allow low-risk testing of many products.

  • Move to a dedicated private supplier once a product has proven demand.

  • During the testing phase, use two suppliers. AliExpress is an online marketplace where many sellers list goods. Use it for sourcing (finding and buying products).

  • Use CJ Dropshipping as the fulfillment provider, the company that packs and ships.

  • Switch to a private supplier only once a product is proven.

  • When testing a new product, a dropshipping supplier is a good way to start.

  • The cost per unit (cost of one product) is higher.

  • It removes the need to stock inventory before the product is validated (proven to sell).

Supplier count

  • Use one main supplier when you plan custom packaging or simple fulfillment.

  • Multiple suppliers mean separate shipments. There is no saving from combined orders (orders shipped together to save money).

  • Switching suppliers later costs time on packaging and branding.

  • A single Shopify store can also be fulfilled by several suppliers at the same time, for example CJ Dropshipping and another supplier.

  • Testing two suppliers on the same product shows which one gives customers the better experience.

  • Both approaches are valid.

  • Use one main supplier when packaging and branding matter and shipment consolidation (shipping orders together) is valuable.

  • Run two or more suppliers on the same product when you need to compare customer experience.

Timing and delivery expectations

  • Do not worry about exact delivery times in the early stage of a product.

  • Once orders grow, find a new supplier before delivery delays become a problem.

  • When order volume grows, move to a private supplier to cut processing and shipping time.

  • A private supplier with about 10 days to delivery was described as a good target.

  • The fulfillment option should be chosen only after the product is validated.

  • Before that, decide on a shipping method with the supplier.

Products to keep on drop shipping

  • High-ticket products are expensive products.
  • Keep them on the traditional dropship model if good suppliers already exist.
  • Check how many customers buy them before committing.
  • Private labeling means selling under your own brand name. It is not needed when strong products are already available.

High-ticket dropshipping has three main risks that must be managed. Managing these three keeps the model viable:

  • Freight cost: the cost to get the product into the country.
  • Return risk: the chance that customers send products back.
  • Damage risk: the chance that products arrive broken.

Products to avoid

  • Do not sell used or second-hand products.

  • Inventory is always limited, and the business cannot scale (grow large).

  • Understand how suppliers, product sourcing and fulfillment work before choosing products.

  • They determine how orders reach the customer.

Hybrid Model and Own Warehousing

2 min

Hybrid model

  • The hybrid model combines direct dropshipping from suppliers with your own fulfillment center (your own site for packing and shipping).
  • It is a valid business model. The same core principles apply.
  • Fulfillment operations are a separate business. They need more capital (money to invest) and time.
  • Not recommended for a first store.

Early in-house fulfillment

  • In the early phase, fulfillment can be done in-house, by your own people. For example, friends and a rented warehouse.

  • This costs less than an outside 3PL. A 3PL is a third-party logistics company, an outside firm that stores and ships your stock.

  • Consider outsourcing (giving the work to outside companies) once volume grows.

  • For reference, a warehouse of about 50 square meters in Switzerland cost about 200 per month.

  • Warehouse costs depend strongly on the country.

Three fulfillment setups

A setup is the way you run your orders. Bulk stock means buying many units at once.

Three fulfillment setups exist. Each trades off cost, delivery time and capital risk:

Setup Description IDs
Single-order dropshipping Supplier such as CJ Dropshipping or Zendrop ships each order
Bulk stock in a Chinese warehouse or 3PL Stock held centrally and shipped from there
Bulk stock shipped to your own address You hold the stock and ship it yourself

Transit times and market coverage

From a Chinese warehouse, delivery takes:

  • About 5-7 days to the US.

  • About 3-5 days to the UK and Europe.

  • A Chinese warehouse holding bulk stock can ship to the US, UK, Europe, Australia and New Zealand.

  • It uses one stock pool, one shared stock count.

  • Managing many SKUs (stock keeping units, each one product version with its own count) across several markets becomes hard to keep in sync.

  • This suits a few main products.

  • Shipping stock to a warehouse in the target country can improve conversion rates (the share of visitors who buy) compared with shipping from China.

  • Holding stock is only worthwhile while the season continues.

Benefits of holding stock in a warehouse

  • A warehouse that holds your stock lowers the risk of bulk buying.
  • Unsold units (items not yet sold) can be returned, or new stock can be sent quickly.
  • You avoid waiting weeks for a restock (buying more stock) after selling out fast.

Third-party logistics providers

  • Deliver is a 3PL that offers Prime-like 1-2 day shipping. Prime is Amazon's fast delivery membership.

  • It is only worth using at heavy daily volume (many orders each day) from a single country.

  • ShipBob is a US-based third-party warehousing company. It stores and ships stock.

  • The source reported poor customer support and unnecessary charges.

  • The source planned to test ShipDudes, a warehouse based on the US East Coast.

Amazon FBA

1 min

When Amazon FBA fits

  • FBA means Fulfillment by Amazon. Amazon stores your stock, packs it and ships it to customers.

  • Amazon FBA is an option for dropshipping if you sell in a limited set of countries.

  • Amazon takes about 30% commission. Commission is a fee on each sale.

  • Customer data is limited.

  • Consider ordering inventory only with a clear sales forecast (an estimate of future sales) from the last 2-3 months.

  • Amazon offers its own warehousing and third-party logistics services to sellers who use FBA.

  • This can reduce handling for sellers who buy inventory in bulk.

  • Inventory decisions still require a clear sales forecast. The upfront stock (stock paid for before it sells) is a financial risk.

  • Amazon can hold your inventory in its fulfillment warehouses and ship customer orders from there.

  • It is a practical starting option for a product that you sell on Amazon first.

Amazon FBA workflow

  1. Place order with supplier.
  2. Wait for lead time, typically 4-8 weeks. Lead time is the wait from order to delivery.
  3. Receive inventory.
  4. Send products to the Amazon fulfillment center.
  5. Amazon handles all storage, packing, shipping, customer refunds, and payment processing.
  6. Receive the wire transfer (bank transfer) to your bank account every 14 days.
  • Your role is marketing and inventory management only. Inventory management means keeping track of your stock.

Pre-order, Made-to-Order and Print on Demand

2 min

Definitions

  • Pre-order fulfillment means ordering from the supplier only after the customer has paid.

  • No stock is bought in advance.

  • Made-to-ship fulfillment is a production model.

  • The garment is made and shipped to the customer as orders come in.

  • Print on demand (POD) is a fulfillment method.

  • Designs are printed onto garments only after an order is placed.

Production time and customer communication

  • Made-to-order and custom products can be sold.
  • Make the production time very clear on the product page.
  • Follow up after purchase so the customer knows the lead time.

Print-on-demand books

  • Print-on-demand book suppliers can often customize the cover (outside of the book) and the inner content (the pages inside).
  • This typically costs little or nothing extra and needs little time.
  • Ask the supplier which customizations they offer before editing anything.

Personalized products

  • Personalized products, such as custom Christmas ornaments, need a designer to create the designs.
  • They also need a personalization form, where the buyer enters details. This is usually added through a Shopify app (an add-on program for Shopify stores).
  • These products are harder to dropship than standard products because they require more setup work.

Digital products without fulfillment

  • Digital ebooks (electronic books) need no shipping or stock.
  • Create the PDF (a file format for documents) with ChatGPT (an AI writing tool) for content.
  • Use Midjourney (an AI image tool) for images and Canva (a design tool) for layout.
  • Upload it to the Digital Downloads app, which delivers the file to the buyer automatically after purchase.
  • The buyer receives the PDF immediately.

A staged launch of an ebook product works as follows:

  1. Create only one ebook first and run ads (paid online adverts) to test demand.
  2. After the first sale, email the buyer that fulfillment is delayed.
  3. Create and send the ebook within two to three days, or the same day if possible.
  4. After the first and second sale show demand, create the full ebook range, improve the offer, refine interest targeting (choosing who sees your ads more precisely) and then scale.

Brand Partnerships as an Alternative

1 min

How brand partnerships work

  • Karo is an app for brand partnerships.
  • Brand partnerships through Karo let you list (show for sale) another Shopify store's product on your own store.
  • You do not hold inventory or handle fulfillment.
  • The order is recorded in both systems and labeled as fulfilled by the brand partner.
  • The model is similar to dropshipping, but works like affiliate marketing. With affiliate marketing, you earn a cut of the sale.

Adding partner products

  1. Open Karo's Brand Partnerships side, the part of the Karo app for partnerships.
  2. Search for another Shopify store in the network that sells a product you want to offer but cannot source from a dropshipping supplier.
  3. Add that product to your store.
  4. When a customer buys it, the order is created in both systems and marked as fulfilled by the brand partner.
  5. The partner handles fulfillment, and you receive a sales commission.

Moving Between Models

1 min

Triggers for moving to a 3PL

Switch a SKU to a 3PL when either of these is true:

  • It sells about 1,000 units per month.
  • Or it gets at least 10 orders per day for that unit, sustained for at least three weeks.

Stepwise progression

  1. Start with supplier dropshipping, for example CJ Dropshipping or Zendrop, while you are still testing products.
  2. When a product reaches about 5-10 orders per day, move it to a personal agent (a helper who handles buying and checking goods for you) that has a minimum volume requirement (a minimum number of orders).
  3. When a SKU reaches about 1,000 units per month, or about 10 orders per day for at least three weeks, move it to a 3PL with bulk stock.
  4. For stock arriving by sea, project needs about 20-30 days ahead and reorder accordingly. Projecting needs means estimating how much you will sell.

Moving to a private supplier or own warehousing

  • Once a product has proven profitable, consider moving to a private supplier or your own warehousing.
  • Use this step only after validation, because the upfront commitment is larger than with dropshipping.

Moving from drop shipping to a private supplier

  • When order volume grows, move to a private supplier to cut processing and shipping time.
  • Find the new supplier before delivery delays become a problem.

Playbook › 3. Suppliers and fulfillment

Approaching suppliers: scripts, calls and positioning

Section 3.8 · 8 of 22 in this chapter · 45 min read

In short

  • Call suppliers by phone first, and ask for the person who opens retailer accounts.
  • Research each supplier before you call, and mention something specific from its website.
  • Never claim things you do not have. If your store is new, say so.
  • Go live only after at least two approvals. Aim for five.
  • Do not mention dropshipping (selling products the supplier ships straight to the customer) in your first pitch.
More key points
  • Tell suppliers you pay upfront for each order and do not want credit. This makes approval easier.
  • Be honest. Never claim sales you do not have, and never list a product below the price the supplier sets.
  • Call first, then email. Follow up every 2 to 3 days. Stop when they say yes, no, or "not right now."
  • Show each supplier how you will bring them sales and take good care of customers.
  • Keep your launch date and plan short and clear. Do not over-explain your business.

Why Suppliers Approve Retailers

2 min
  • Suppliers check every new seller on three traits: a professional, working website already running; a clear plan for making sales; and proven, excellent customer service.
  • Show the three things: a good website that presents their brand well, a clear plan for extra sales through your store, and excellent customer service that protects their reputation.
  • Approval is not guaranteed. It does not depend only on website quality, traffic (number of visitors) or business experience. Even well-prepared sellers can be turned down. Success depends on how you communicate, not on outside proof (titles or certificates) alone.
  • Suppliers accept a new seller only when they believe it is a real, qualified (suitable) business that will bring extra sales to their brand. Your job in the first contact is to make that belief clear.
  • Suppliers want sellers who prove themselves before approval, because a seller represents their brand. They expect the seller to follow rules such as MAP (minimum advertised price, the lowest price you may show for a product) and to support customers after the sale.
  • Suppliers approve sellers they expect to care about customer service, run a lasting business and bring traffic that adds value to their brand. Show that intention in the approach call and in the store.
  • Suppliers generally want to work with new stores that are excited about their products. Clear enthusiasm and specific requests are usually well received.
  • Suppliers sell business to business (B2B, selling to other companies), to physical and online stores. An online store is just another retail customer for them. Approval is usually easy because you offer a sales channel (a way to sell) that fits what they already do. **
  • High-ticket (expensive) suppliers usually do not sell to the public. They earn by selling to retailers, so they wait for serious businesses that will manage sales to customers. The approval call is a request for a partnership, not a favour. **
  • The products already exist on the market, and demand (people wanting them) already exists. The store does not need to create demand. It taps into existing demand for these products. **
  • Suppliers evaluate outreach (your contact) from their side. They accept a seller only when they believe it is a real, qualified business.
  • Suppliers often ask the same questions when reviewing an approval request. Prepare answers in advance so the first conversation does not stall.
  • Dropshipping brands (brands where the supplier ships directly to customers, so you keep no stock) usually require approval before a store may sell their products. Copying a product list from the web and listing it directly is not a valid route. Plan a contact and approval step for every supplier.
  • Suppliers are approached directly. The store becomes an authorized seller (an approved seller) through approval, not by paying for supplier access or joining supplier directories (paid lists of suppliers).
  • Ready-made phone and email scripts (word-for-word templates) are used to ask suppliers for approval as a seller. Approval is obtained without spending money.
  • Contacting suppliers and getting authorized to sell their products costs nothing. The first real spending starts with ads (paid promotion), so request supplier approval before committing any budget to traffic.

Positioning: How You Come Across

2 min
  • Present yourself as an attractive opportunity they would regret losing, not as a seller who needs them to succeed. Make them wish they had accepted you sooner.
  • Sounding desperate, or showing that you need the supplier to succeed, damages your position. They should believe they would make a mistake by passing on you, not that you depend on them.
  • Present the relationship as the start of a long-term partnership, not a one-time deal. Suppliers want sellers who grow with them and bring steady repeat revenue (money that comes back again and again), not one-off buyers.
  • The quality and content of each conversation matter far more than how often you call. Make each call count. Do not contact suppliers repeatedly.
  • Suppliers get many calls about the same products, so they ask what sets you apart. Show that your business is long term, that customer service is your priority, and that you understand their brand and why it fits your store. Do not only ask to list their products.
  • Approach suppliers owner to owner, because suppliers need sellers who promote their products. Frame your outreach as giving them an opportunity to be sold through your store.
  • Describing your launch plan works best when you have actually prepared it. Present yourself as someone about to take a share of the market, and show you understand the subject well enough to be believed.
  • Do not call a supplier with a generic (vague) request, such as asking whether you can dropship their products. Research their brand first and explain specifically why your store is a good fit for them.
  • Suppliers judge sellers by whether they will last. Show that you treat the relationship as lasting, not as a quick resale opportunity.
  • Some suppliers want to see your marketing plan before they approve you. Before calling, prepare a short outline of how you will sell their products, such as through Google Shopping ads (product ads in Google search) and retargeting (ads shown again to people who already visited your site). **
  • Nervousness about calling is normal. Most suppliers are professional and welcome the contact. The discomfort decreases with practice, so use it as energy rather than trying to remove it before calling. **
  • If you feel nervous, start with smaller, less visible suppliers to build confidence and practise the pitch (your short sales talk). Approach the larger brands after you are comfortable with the conversation and have a few approvals behind you.

Phone First

2 min
  • Always call suppliers first rather than emailing. A phone call is the fastest way to reach the right decision maker (the person who can say yes), here the eCommerce account person (the person who handles online-store accounts), on the first attempt.
  • Call every supplier that has a phone number first. Use email or an online form as a follow-up (a second message) or fallback (a backup plan). **
  • Contact suppliers by phone first rather than email. Phone calls achieve higher success rates and lead to stronger business relationships. **
  • Email is unreliable for first contact. Many recipients never read emails. Inboxes get cluttered, unfamiliar senders are often ignored, and mailboxes can reach their storage limit. Your chance then depends on luck. **
  • Contact suppliers by phone when a phone number is listed, because phone contact gets better results than email. Use email only when no phone number is available. **
  • Calling suppliers gets better responses than emails. Emails are easily ignored. A phone call creates a direct connection and a sense of urgency (a feeling that it needs an answer now). (2023.)
  • Phone calls are the recommended way to reach suppliers. Once you reach the right person and describe what you want, the application usually takes 5 to 10 minutes to complete. Approval often follows right after it is returned. **
  • Call suppliers instead of sending a long letter, because letters are easy to set aside or miss. A phone call reaches the right person quickly and gives a faster answer on approval. **
  • Reseller approval requests (asking a brand to let you resell its products) can be sent by phone, by email, or through the supplier's online application form. The first contact states that you want to sell their products through your business and asks for their approval. **
  • Call suppliers rather than submitting only the trade or dealer intake forms (sign-up forms for business buyers). A supplier listing a trade or designer program does not stop you from getting approved. Those programs mainly serve larger corporate or B2B buyers.
  • Phone suppliers early in the morning, around 8:00 to 8:30 AM in the supplier's time zone (the local clock where the supplier is), before their workday fills up. Choose the suppliers you call by time zone, so the morning call makes sense for each. (2023.)
  • Calling suppliers early in the morning, around 8:30 to 9:00 AM, can get you through to decision makers before their workload builds up. If responses are poor at other times, test this window for a few days. (2023.)
  • Before calling, check the business or retail contact hours (the times they take trade calls) on the supplier's contact page. These may be listed in a specific time zone, so schedule calls within that window. Some manufacturers run a separate business line (a phone line just for trade buyers) apart from their consumer support number. **
  • A personal meeting is rare. It happens in under 1% of supplier contacts. When a supplier wants to fly in, politely decline. Suggest meeting at a trade show (a fair where businesses meet) or a local event instead, unless they are already nearby. (2023.)

Preparing Before You Call

1 min

Research the Supplier

2 min
  • Research each supplier's website and company positioning (how it presents itself) before calling. Find specific connection points. Use them in the pitch to show real interest in the brand, not generic outreach to every supplier in the category.
  • Research each supplier before calling, as you would prepare for a job interview. Suppliers approve sellers who show they understand and value the supplier's brand. This improves approval rates.
  • Before a supplier call, learn its story. Read its About and home pages. Then open the conversation by saying what you like about the company and why you want to sell its products. **
  • Pitch with something specific from their website, such as better materials or a virtual showroom (an online tour of products). Explain how it fits your customers' expectations. Generic praise weakens your approval chance.
  • Evaluate the supplier before calling. Check its company background, history, products and how it sells.

Supplier research method (2024 version, as written):

  1. Google the brand or product category.
  2. Check that the brand sells through online stores and is not a dropshipper.
  3. Open the brand's About us page.
  4. Spend about three to five minutes reading what the brand is proud of and wants customers to know.
  5. Use those points in the supplier conversation, for example its customer service focus, long history or shared location.

Supplier research method (undated version, 5 to 10 minutes per supplier):

  1. Open the supplier's website from your research worksheet.
  2. Read the About Us section and homepage. Find what the brand says about itself, such as its philosophy, materials, quality or what sets it apart.
  3. Check for extra content, such as a virtual showroom or 360-degree tour (a full view of a space you can turn around in), and browse it.
  4. Note what stands out and what the brand wants customers to know.
  5. Plan to reference one or two of these points on the call. Explain why this brand is a better fit than other suppliers. Spend about 5 to 10 minutes per supplier.

Both research methods are current. Use the 3-5 minute reading of the About page (2024) for a quick first pass. Use the full 5-10 minute method when you prepare a supplier you plan to call.

Know What You Bring

1 min
  • Know what you bring to the table: your store, your audience, your marketing channels and your sales plan.
  • Prepare answers to likely questions, so you do not depend on improvising under pressure.

Prepare the List

1 min
  • Open your completed master supplier list (your main spreadsheet of suppliers). Make sure each supplier has a URL (web address), phone number and email.
  • Have at least 20 suppliers researched and filled in before starting supplier calls. Work in batches, for example researching 20 suppliers one day and calling them the next. **
  • Work in this order: **
    1. Research the contact information for the first 20 suppliers.
    2. On the next day, call those 20 suppliers.
    3. On the following day, research the next 20 suppliers.
    4. Repeat the cycle.
  • Calling one supplier and then researching the next one slows the process down. Expect voicemails (recorded messages) and callbacks, so a prepared list should be ready before calling begins. **
  • The scripts, emails and day-by-day timing in the supplier contact plan are starting points. You can edit them to fit your store and wording. Keep the overall order of calls and follow-ups intact. The sequence is built to keep outreach going until a supplier gives a definite answer.
  • Prepare for the most likely supplier questions in advance. (see above)

Order and Pacing of Outreach

1 min
  • Contact suppliers you expect to approve you first. Reach out to top-tier suppliers only after your business information is complete. Keep adding suppliers over time, rather than waiting for every approval before uploading products. **
  • Start at the top of the supplier list. Once approved, sign the agreement, upload the products, then move on to the next supplier. Have products from the first two approved brands live with traffic before you proceed. (2023.)
  • Silver suppliers come first. They are the majority of the list, and gold suppliers are left for later. Call each supplier, even when its website has a dealer form. Introduce yourself and your store, and say you are about to submit the form. When a dealer application arrives, fill it out and return it immediately. Stop contacting new suppliers while several applications are pending, so each is completed and approved before you move on. Once all silver suppliers are handled, contact the gold suppliers. **
  • Order in a niche (a narrow product group) with many suppliers: **
    1. On competitor category pages, note which suppliers' products appear first, since these pages sort by best-selling products.
    2. Do not contact the top-ranked suppliers first.
    3. Contact about 10 suppliers that rank lower but still carry products in the niche.
    4. Get the first 5 to 10 approved and build comfort with the approval process.
    5. Return to the top-ranked suppliers and seek approval from them.
    6. Contact all remaining suppliers.
  • Credibility approach when a brand accepts a new reseller (2025): become an authorized seller, and upload the supplier's products to the store. If margins (the profit left after costs) are thin, price them above the supplier's minimum price (the lowest price the brand allows). Exclude them from paid campaigns. They can still be found through organic search (free search results, not ads) and free Google Shopping listings, while paid ads go to proven sellers. Then reach out to more suppliers and mention the existing brand.

Call Sequence and the First Conversation

Goal of the First Call

1 min
  • The goal of the first contact is only to reach the decision maker, not to close the deal. Keep the early conversation short. Focus it on introduction, your planned launch and your reasons for choosing them.
  • Keep calls short. Stay professional. Focus on clarity and confidence.

Full Call Procedure (17 Steps)

2 min
  1. Research the supplier's website and company positioning to see why their products fit your niche.
  2. Find the supplier's phone number through Google search or directly on their website. Look for a toll-free number (a number that costs the caller nothing).
  3. Call and ask for the person in charge of eCommerce accounts. If that person is unavailable, request their email and/or direct phone number for a callback.
  4. When you reach the eCommerce decision maker, begin by briefly explaining that you own an eCommerce store focused on their niche. Say you want to add quality suppliers to your catalog (your list of products).
  5. Give a brief overview of your company. Do not refer to any other websites you operate.
  6. Explain that your team is finishing a major project in their niche that you are about to launch.
  7. Share your excitement. Note that your team has researched the niche extensively and is confident you can dominate it with your marketing approach.
  8. State that you are building out your catalog before launching a major marketing push on [specific date: 1-2 months from today].
  9. Reference specific findings from their website that show why their products are a perfect fit for your customers.
  10. Express confidence that your company will become one of their top retailers within weeks of launching.
  11. Emphasize building a long-term relationship, similar to partnerships you have developed with other suppliers in other niches.
  12. Clearly state that you are not looking for special payment terms, only a solid business relationship.
  13. Ask directly how to apply for an eCommerce account.
  14. If they say no or that they are not taking new retailers, explain that it is a shame they will miss out on the significant launch you are planning.
  15. Ask who specifically needs to be involved to make the partnership happen.
  16. Even if declined, understand that they will remember this call and who you are. This positions you for future contact.
  17. Document the conversation completely: the person's name, the date and all details discussed.

Eight-Step Workflow (Decision Maker Focus)

3 min
  1. Open the master supplier list. Make sure each supplier has a URL, phone number and email.
  2. Call first whenever a phone number exists. Use email only when no phone is available.
  3. On the call, introduce yourself and your store. State the planned launch and why you like their products. Ask how to apply for a supplier account.
  4. Ask to be connected to the decision maker, the person who sends the approval form or grants access.
  5. Speak with the decision maker. Reference something specific you learned from their website. Ask for the dealer application form to be emailed.
  6. As soon as they promise the form, give your email address, thank them, and confirm you will return it right away.
  7. Fill out and return the form the same day you receive it.
  8. Log every contact in the tracking columns (the spreadsheet columns where you record each contact). Then follow up on the agreed date.
  • When a supplier agrees to send an approval or dealer application form by email, give them your email address during the call. Thank them, and confirm you will send it back right away. Then check your inbox, fill out the form and return it immediately.
  • Call the supplier and ask to reach the right decision maker during that same call. Ask them to email the approval form right after the call. If a promised callback or email does not arrive within a few days, follow up. Return the completed form as soon as possible. **
  • Do not ask a supplier many detailed questions before they send their approval form, because many answers are in that form. Ask follow-up questions after the form is returned. **
  • Trade account contact (2022):
    1. Call and ask for the person in charge of opening new accounts.
    2. If it is a small company, that person may answer directly. If it is larger, ask who handles it.
    3. For email outreach, a short intro works. State you are interested in selling their products. Ask who opens new accounts for internet retailers. **
  • Getting a supplier approved by phone:
    1. Call the supplier rather than relying on email, since phone gives a faster answer.
    2. Say you want to open an account as an online retailer. Ask who handles new retailer accounts.
    3. If nobody answers, ask how to email or whom to contact for a new account.
    4. Follow up by email if no one calls back, because follow-up is part of the process.
    5. If no phone number is listed, use supplier research tools to find other contact numbers.
  • Calling a supplier to ask about dropshipping stores (undated):
    1. Call the supplier and ask whether they work with dropshipping stores.
    2. If they agree, ask them to send you the supplier application. Give them your email address so they can send it.
    3. Fill in the application and return it to the supplier as soon as possible.
    4. When the supplier sends you their product information, upload their products to your store right away.
    5. Repeat these steps with more suppliers until at least five suppliers are approved and all their products are live on your store.
  • Trade account procedure (2020):
    1. Contact the brand by phone or email and ask for an account.
    2. Ask which format they use for inventory updates (stock list changes) and whether they can send a regular report.
    3. Ask for the top three best-selling products, with their weight and packaging size.
    4. Record every answer, the reason for any rejection, and the date to follow up.
    5. If the brand does not respond, follow up every few days. If it rejected you, follow up when the stated condition is met.
  • Approval workflow (2023):
    1. Call the supplier first. Ask for the name and direct email of the person responsible for retailer relationships, even if they say to email.
    2. Send a short, direct email to that contact. Mention the phone call, the store link and a request for the next steps.
    3. If there is no reply after a few days, reply to your own first email with a short follow-up.
    4. Keep applying to more suppliers until you have at least two approvals.
  • Outreach for approval (2023), in short form: call the supplier and ask for the name and direct email of the person who handles retailer relationships, even if they first say to email. Send a very short email to that contact. Name the person you spoke with, include the store link and ask for the next steps. If there is no reply after a few days, answer your own first email with a brief follow-up. Keep contacting more suppliers until at least two are approved before going live.

## Presenting Your Business Honestly

- Stay honest when contacting suppliers. You do not need to lie about who you are or what you have done to get a good supplier relationship.
- Supplier sourcing templates and Q&A forms are available in the sourcing section to structure supplier outreach. Use them to present your business accurately.
- **Never claim a longer history** than you have. If your store is not live yet, point to any earlier online or offline business experience you can honestly name.
- When a supplier asks how long you have been in business, the question is about whether they can trust you to stay around and treat their brand with respect.
- Established suppliers judge whether a new store will last mainly by its track record (its past results). A store that is not live yet should say so. It should point only to real earlier online or offline business experience, with no inflated history.
- Our store is a new venture, and we are launching it now as part of our launch strategy. [If you have prior experience: Before this, we ran [previous business, for example an eBay store or an offline business] for [number of years].]

### Template: New Venture With Optional Prior Experience

Our store is a new venture, and we are launching it now as part of our launch strategy. [If you have prior experience: Before this, we ran [previous business, for example an eBay store or an offline business] for [number of years].]


### Using "I" and "we"

- In outreach emails from a one-person store, writing "I" instead of "we" is acceptable. A small mismatch like this is unlikely to end a supplier deal. Using the word "niche" in supplier communication is also fine. *(2023.)*
- When speaking to a supplier, using "we" to refer to your business is acceptable **as long as you do not claim anything untrue**. The "we" refers to your company, not to a false claim about size or history. **

### Expertise in a Technical Niche

1. **Do not claim** expertise you do not have.
2. **Study** the niche and its product types at a surface level. For example, watch a few hours of basic introductory material.
3. **Tell** the supplier honestly that you have a working knowledge and aim to become an expert.
4. **State** that you want a good working relationship, so you can educate customers and build a resource section on the store.

## Demo Store, Launch Date and Existing Stores

### Demo Store

- When contacting suppliers, always have a **demo store** (a sample store showing how products look) ready. Mention it during approval conversations. Show it as a living example of how their products will appear on your store. **
- Always tell a supplier **in advance** that your store is in demo mode. This frame stops suppliers from misreading a small product catalog or placeholder items as proof of an unfinished or fraudulent business.
- Sample wording for demo mode: "My name is [Your Name], I've been building e-commerce stores for [your years of experience], please visit this site to see how your products would look. The store is launching on [specific launch date]."
- During supplier review, you can share your store template with a stated launch date. The supplier can then see how their products would look on it. Do not present the unfinished store as an active shop with products for sale. **
- Do not start with cold calls (unrequested calls) to request authorization, even when a demo store already exists. First work out **why a supplier would choose you**, and set up legitimacy signals (signs that you are real) beforehand.
- Note the sources.  says always have the demo store ready and mention it during approval conversations.  says a demo store does not justify a cold call that asks for authorization. Use the demo store in the approval conversation, **after** you have established why the supplier should choose you.

### Existing Stores

- If you already have successful online stores (dropshipping or otherwise), mention this when contacting suppliers. It helps with approval, whether or not the stores are in the same niche. **
- **Never claim** to have existing stores or sales if you do not have them. Describe only the store you are actually building.

### Launch Date

- When calling suppliers, specify a launch date **1-2 months** in the future. Use an actual date you have set for your marketing launch, not a hypothetical timeline. This lets you reference real results in follow-up calls and strengthens credibility.
- State a real planned launch date when speaking to a supplier, and keep it **no more than two months** in the future. A later date makes the supplier treat you as not relevant now and ask you to come back later.
- When giving a supplier a launch date, a window of about **three to four weeks** is typical. A fixed date, such as January 1, can also be given.
- Describing your launch plan to a supplier works best when you have actually prepared it.
- Use the launch strategy, not previous store history, to build excitement. Reference the launch date to set expectations. **

## Traffic and Sales Questions

- During a launch, if you have no traffic yet, tell suppliers you plan to send **hundreds of qualified leads** (people likely to buy) to your website every day. Do not present current traffic numbers you do not have.
- Suppliers want partners who add value, mainly through orders. Projected sales are essentially a guess, so **do not invent** a sales forecast. State a baseline (a minimum starting point) instead. A brand must be able to reach **at least 10,000 USD per month** in sales through your store. Otherwise it is not a good fit.

What I can tell you is that if I cannot get you at least 10,000 USD a month in sales, I do not think it would be a good fit for us. So our baseline goal is at least 10,000 USD a month in sales for your brand on [your store domain].


- For traffic, use the statement from. Avoid any figure you cannot support.

## Warehouse and Stock Questions

- Suppliers ask about your warehouse (a building where stock is kept) to learn whether you are an online-only seller. Such a seller needs them to stock and ship the products. Your answer should explain your **focus**. Do not present the lack of a warehouse as a gap.
- Do not answer the warehouse question by saying you are new and may have a warehouse one day. That sounds like a weakness and invites doubt. State it as a deliberate business focus.

### Template: Warehouse Answer

That is not something we do. Our focus is on marketing, customer service and retail. ### Credit References, Prepay and Bank Details

### Credit terms

- Make clear that **you are not asking for credit terms.** Credit terms mean paying later, for example 30 or 60 days after an order. Suppliers usually give them to physical retailers (shops with a real building) that buy in bulk (large amounts at once). A dropshipping business should not request them. Dropshipping means you sell products that the supplier ships to your customers for you, so you never hold stock. **
- State that **you pay upfront for each order**, before the product leaves the supplier's warehouse (the building where it stores stock). Because the supplier is paid before shipping, it carries no financial risk, which makes approval easier. **
- Use prepay terms with suppliers. Prepay means **paying for orders before they ship.** (2018.)
- If a supplier asks for trade references (other businesses that confirm how you pay your bills), **you may use references from another niche.** Tell the supplier you are not asking for credit terms, since references are usually requested to check payment history for credit. **

### Credit references on applications (2018 and 2023)

- If a supplier application asks for three credit references (people or companies that confirm you pay on time), **you can leave that section empty** when you use prepay terms, because it only matters for credit terms. **
- When a supplier asks for bank details or a location, this is usually a credit check. If you are prepaying orders, **you do not need to provide bank information.** On the form, cross out the bank section and write prepay terms. (2023.)

### Contact plan procedure

1. Open your completed master supplier list (your full list of suppliers) and **generate a contact plan** for the first supplier.
2. **Start Day 1 with a phone call** asking about their application process and identifying the decision maker. Use the email template only if they request it.
3. **Record** the date, the contact person's name and your conversation notes.
4. **Follow the 7-day contact schedule** with the mix of phone calls and emails provided.
5. **Stop contacting** when you receive approval, rejection or "not right now."
6. After your first approval, move on and **upload that supplier's live products** (products shown on your store).
7. Return to the start of this procedure and **generate a new contact plan** for your second supplier.
8. **Repeat steps 1-7** until you have approval from five suppliers.
9. Continue this process with additional suppliers from your list to build silver and gold tier approvals (higher approval levels) over time.

3. **Upload their products to the store** the same day or the next day.

### Trade Shows, Meetings and Territory

### Trade show approach (2025)

A trade show is an event where brands meet sellers.

1. **Remove the password** from the store before the event so suppliers can see it live.
2. **Print about 200 business cards** with your logo, name, store email and cell number (Vistaprint, an online printing company, is one option).
3. At each booth (a brand's display area), **introduce yourself and your company**, and say you want to sell the brand's products online.
4. **Ask** whether they work with online retailers and who to speak to if they do not.
5. **Collect each contact's business card.**
6. That evening, **email each contact** a short note asking them to get in touch once they are back home.
7. If there is no reply after about a week, **send one more follow-up.**

### Territory

- When suppliers ask "What is your territory?" (the area you sell in), they are sending you to the correct regional sales representative (the person who handles a region). **Simply state your location** (city and state or geographic region). They will assign you to the territory rep responsible for your area. **

### Documentation

- **Document every conversation**: the person's name, the date and all details discussed. Even if declined, the supplier will remember the call, which positions you for future contact.
- **Record each answer**, the reason for any rejection, and the date to follow up.

### Phone-first outreach sequence

- **Build a supplier list first**, then contact each supplier by phone and by email. Once a supplier approves, upload its products to your Shopify store (online shop software) so customers can see them. In theory this is simple; the difficulty is carrying out the calls and emails.
- Follow this order for each product in the target country (the country you sell to):
  1. **Find the suppliers** of the product in the target country.
  2. **Call each supplier** and ask for approval to retail (sell to customers) their products through your website.
  3. After approval, **list the products** on the store.
- If calling feels uncomfortable, **start with brands you care less about.** This builds confidence before you call the brands that matter most.

### Your position with suppliers

- Approach suppliers as **a mutually beneficial business opportunity** (a deal where both sides gain), not as someone asking for a favor or begging for approval to sell their products. Both sides can make money from the arrangement.
- Suppliers benefit because you bring them sales and improve their cash flow (the money moving in and out of their business). **This win-win basis is what makes the relationship work;** it is not a favor you ask for.
- Suppliers do not have to pay to have their products listed on your website. You handle marketing, customer service and payment, and **you pay them only for the products you order.**
- Suppliers already prepare and ship orders for their existing retail clients, so dropshipping orders add little new work for them. **Their risk is minimal.**
- In the worst case you sell nothing for a supplier, which leaves their net result (their final gain or loss) at zero. **They cannot lose money from the relationship,** but they can gain.
- Suppliers compete with other manufacturers (companies that make products) and distributors (companies that move products to sellers) for the most visible spot on retailer websites. **This is why they want their products sold through a new store.**
- Suppliers judge a seller by professionalism and business seriousness, not by where the seller lives. **A non-US seller who holds a US reseller permit** (a document that lets you buy products to resell) **and an EIN** (Employer Identification Number, a US tax number for your business) **can approach US suppliers.** Follow the call script closely and stay calm.

### Preparing before the call

- **Fix your website launch date** before you contact any supplier, so you can give it without hesitation. (2024.)
- **Do not reveal your exact location** in early conversations. In traditional industries this can reduce trust or cause resentment. Set privacy settings so suppliers who connect with you on Facebook cannot see personal photos, such as work from the beach. (2024.)
- **Prepare your own traffic ideas** (ways to bring visitors to your store) and a basic business plan. Suppliers need to believe you will add sales that require little work from them. (2024.)
- **Prepare a short explanation** of how you will add sales to the brand and how you will provide excellent customer service. Brands approve a reseller when it can show both.

### Presenting your business

- Suppliers unfamiliar with dropshipping need a pitch that explains the benefits and how the model works for them. **Covering that education** is a key part of getting them on board.
- Present your business in this order:
  1. **Show the supplier the value** you will bring to their brand.
  2. **Explain how you will market** their products.
  3. **Describe your business.**
  4. **Explain why you want to work with this specific supplier.**

 
- Explain that you run a **long-term business** with customer support as your top priority, and that you are not a short-term or fly-by-night operation (a business that disappears quickly). (2024.)
- Approval depends on the supplier believing you **will benefit its brand.** Suppliers mostly want to know that you will look after customers and run a real business. (2023.)

### Scripts and email templates

### First message to a supplier

Keep it short, say you want a long-term partnership, and ask concrete questions. Questions on dropshipping, delivery time to each target market, shipping fee and method, and custom packaging or logo options let you check quickly whether a supplier fits you, before deeper negotiation.

Hi, I'm [YOUR NAME], a seasoned dropshipper. I'm already selling this product and want to find a supplier for a lasting partnership. If that interests you, could you help me with a few questions?

  1. Do you provide dropshipping?
  2. What is the typical delivery time to the USA, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and Europe?
  3. What would shipping cost, and which carriers or methods would you use?
  4. Can I add custom packaging and my own logo to the product?

Thanks in advance. I look forward to hearing from you.


- Use the sentence "I'm already selling this product" **only if it is true.** (2024 source; honesty principle, meaning always tell the truth.)

Use this to offer the supplier a choice of how orders are shipped. Fulfilment means picking, packing and shipping orders.

Hi [Supplier name], we work in a few different ways depending on what suits you best. If you would rather ship it yourselves, that works too, and we are happy to go with whichever option is easier for you. We have had strong demand for your [product or brand] and have turned down some sales recently, so if you decide to work with us, let us know and we can set up the account.

### Answering supplier questions

### Yearly purchase budget

**Do not give a fixed number.** Explain that your budget is effectively unlimited as long as products convert (sell) at a 10x ROAS (ROAS means Return On Ad Spend: the sales you earn for each dollar spent on ads, so 10x means $10 in sales for every $1 in ads). Explain also that spending starts at a small daily test budget. (2024.)

### Monthly sales estimate before products are live

A usable answer is a rough target, such as $10,000 per month for the brand. Be clear that there is no real visitor data yet, and that the actual result depends on traffic (visitors) and listing performance (how well your product pages do). **Never state zero sales as if it were a real figure.** (2018.)

**Give the date fixed in advance,** without hesitation.

### Application forms and terms

- When a supplier application asks what collaboration type you want, **choose retailer,** because you sell as a retailer on your own website. If asked about other sales platforms or physical stores, answer no when you only sell on your own website. (2024.)
- **You usually do not need to send your own terms** (your own contract conditions). The supplier reviews the agreements it provides. (2024.)

### What suppliers weigh

- Suppliers weigh approval by downside risk (the chance of loss). A retailer that brings no sales costs them little time. A retailer that damages their brand is their worst outcome, so **your pitch should show you will protect their reputation.** (2022 source; the same point appears twice.)
- Suppliers are approving a future, not current results. **Approvals are about the store's future potential and the niche,** not current store results. (2020.)

### Honest positioning

- State clearly during approval talks that **you will never list a supplier's products below the MAP** (Minimum Advertised Price, the lowest price you may show online) **or the price range it specifies.** Suppliers need to believe this before they approve you. (2024 source.)
- Present the plan honestly and **do not overstate** the store's performance. (2020.)
- If the store is still in development and has only a few sales, describe it honestly as a store that is launching and plans to scale traffic (grow visitors) and bring in the supplier's brand. **Avoid sharing specific sales figures that would not impress a supplier.** (2023.)

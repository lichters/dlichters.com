Playbook › 3. Suppliers and fulfillment

Other details

Section 3.22 · 22 of 22 in this chapter · 19 min read

In short

  • Ready-stock suppliers already hold the books. Others don't. Test demand yourself before you scale up.
  • Check the minimum order size (MOQ) and order one sample before you commit.
  • Check the real delivery time and the order count. Views alone prove nothing.
  • Keep one listing per product, so sales data stays in one place.
  • Ask your payment providers for lower fees. One phone call saved one store $100 a month.
More key points
  • Growth can trigger a retroactive audit, a check that looks back at past years.
  • That check can bill you over $10,000 for past premiums (the regular insurance payments).
  • This can happen even if you never filed a claim (a request to the insurer to pay for a loss).
  • This is older advice, from 2022.

Supplier and product sourcing

1 min

E-book and book supply

2 min
  • E-book suppliers fall into two groups: those with ready stock and those without it. Ready stock means the supplier already holds the books. Ready-stock books are reached through direct factories or through agents, who are middlemen.
  • Leftover stock of a book, called balanced stock, usually appears when demand has cooled off. Such books are often available with no minimum order quantity (MOQ), the smallest order a supplier accepts. This lowers the cost of testing them.
  • Treat leftover stock as a supply signal. Verify demand with your own test before you scale up.
  • If a supplier offers a book in MOQ stages, the book has real demand.
  • Use the supplier's MOQ status as one of the green-light checks (go or no-go tests) before testing.
  • Custom bottom-of-funnel image ads (ads aimed at people close to buying) are the simplest test of whether customizing a book is worth the cost.
  • Run these ads on the customized product before paying for full personalization. Let the response decide.

Getting a customizable PDF e-book (a digital file you can edit) from a hardcover (a book with a stiff cover):

  1. Search Amazon for a similar book.
  2. Buy the hardcover and have it delivered to your home.
  3. Send the hardcover to your agent, who scans it into an e-PDF (a soft copy).
  4. Once the PDF is ready, have the agent print hard copies from it when needed.
  5. List the product with two sale options: sell as PDF or sell as hardcopy.

In the Singapore example, the Amazon hardcover cost S$7.03, with S$12.17 shipping to Singapore. Sales taxes may apply at checkout, the final payment step.

Rewriting a book PDF before printing:

  1. Ask the supplier for the PDF of the book. You do not need the physical book.
  2. Upload the PDF to ChatGPT, an AI chat tool.
  3. Ask ChatGPT to rewrite the whole PDF with the content changes you want. Start with the first pages, such as the table of contents and the chapter 1, 2 and 3 headings.
  4. Check the rewritten PDF for unwanted changes.
  5. Send the rewritten PDF back to the supplier, who prints the book from that file.

Case study: the owner found a very niche book (a narrow topic) on Amazon because no PDF version existed in the market.

  • The owner bought a copy from Amazon and waited about a month to receive the PDF from the US.
  • The slow delivery led to late fulfillment (orders shipped late), chargebacks (customers reversing payments through their bank) and a reserve hold (the payment provider keeping part of your money).
  • The author publicly accused the seller of selling without consent, which led some fans to dispute charges (challenge payments) with their bank.

To speed up delivery of a physical book sourced from Amazon, a contact in the US can buy the book locally and scan it into a PDF.

  • This lets the digital copy reach a seller abroad quickly.
  • The PDF can then be customized with a new cover and internal pages.

Printing copyrighted books (books protected by copyright, the legal right to copy) through a third-party agent (an outside middleman) in China can be fast.

  • It carries copyright and dispute risk.
  • This option was mentioned only for urgent situations.

Validating a product before testing

2 min

Run a sample purchase (a test order) to validate (check) a product before promoting it (advertising it):

  1. Order one unit of the product from the supplier.
  2. Check the order count on the supplier listing and the seller's history.
  3. Decide whether it is worth testing based on sales volume, not views alone.

Validate a product on AliExpress before testing it:

  1. Search for the product on AliExpress.
  2. Check the number of units sold on the listing.
  3. Treat roughly 1,000 to 10,000 units sold as a sign that the product is selling.
  4. Ignore low comments or likes on ads when you use this check.
  • Products listed on Amazon can also be dropshipped. Dropshipping means the seller takes orders, and the supplier ships them.
  • This means the shipping time may be just as slow. Check where the product ships from before assuming fast delivery.
  • Check order counts for each listing of the same product separately. Different sellers and regional AliExpress versions can show very different numbers.
  • One European listing of a product had over 5,000 orders. Another listing of the same product showed about 213 orders.
  • A sample order reveals more than the order count.
  • It also shows the real delivery time to your market.
  • That delivery time decides whether viral interest (wide sharing online) will still be there when the product arrives.
  • Order one unit before you commit to a product that depends on fast shipping.
  • A product with no Amazon listing is not automatically a sign of poor quality. It may simply be new to the market.
  • The absence of a listing alone is not a reliable basis for judging quality.
  • To test a spotted trending product fast, buy a unit on Amazon so you can film with it immediately. Filming means making video.
  • Do not wait for a slow supplier shipment. The referenced creator received the item the same day it was ordered.
  • Look for emerging products (new products gaining attention) on AliExpress before they go viral on TikTok.
  • Test a product there if it fits your content.
  • Being the first creator to test a new winning product lets you capture most of its views and sales.

Trend and supply data

1 min
  • Kalodata, a product research tool, has a Local Warehouse Dropshipping tab. It lists products that ship from local warehouses.
  • Local warehouses usually mean shorter delivery times than cross-border shipping (shipping from another country). Check this tab when delivery speed matters for your offer.

Marketplace and channel rules

1 min
  • Consolidate duplicate listings. This means merging copies of one product into one page.
  • When a product has multiple product identifiers, the data splits. An ASIN is Amazon's own product number.
  • Example: two different ASINs for the same book, BOGSW62L47 and BOGSJY9PQT.
  • Review velocity (how fast reviews arrive), bestseller rank (position in sales ranking) and ad conversion (share of ad clicks that become sales) get split across listings.
  • This dilutes all performance signals.
  • Consolidating duplicate listings concentrates data.
  • It improves ranking velocity, visibility and performance metrics.
  • On eBay, new sellers can only list 10 products initially.
  • They must work their way up slowly because of seller restrictions and account maturation (the time an account needs to build a track record).
  • Health & Personal Care on Amazon requires three supplier invoices (bills from your supplier).
  • These invoices prove you own the product and are purchasing it.
  • Gather these documents during the supplier approval process.
  • This business model ships domestically within the US only, meaning inside the country.
  • Cross-border shipping (sending abroad), customs (border checks) and international tax rules are not covered.
  • Research international orders separately before you accept them.

Payments and provider costs

1 min
  • Shop Pay, Shopify's fast checkout button, operates only in the US.
  • For international sellers, use Shopify Payments (launching soon), Clear Paper or Color as alternatives.
  • Negotiate provider discounts every three months. Ask Shopify, the payment processor (the company that handles card payments) and PayPal for lower fees.
  • One store saved $100 per month (over $1,000 per year) with a single phone call to Shopify.
  • A credit card fee cut of even 0.1% adds up over 12 to 24 months.

Physical samples and local distribution

1 min

Sample rules

1 min
  • Deliver samples locally through stores rather than by mail. People will travel to pick up a free sample more readily than they will write for one.
  • Where possible, send samples to a nearby retailer and name that retailer in the offer.
  • Distributing samples unsolicited (without being asked) to homes or through dealers in bulk is usually a waste.
  • Many never reach the intended person, and the product's image suffers.
  • Samples should only go to people who asked for them after hearing your story.
  • Limit samples to one per household and say so in the offer.
  • Repeat requests from a few people are not buyers, and they cost the business money.
  • Calculate the cost of repeat sampling into your sample budget.
  • Sending a free sample to a few distributors and dealers at the same time as ads run can create a national presence in a single week.
  • The free packages can cost less than other ways of forcing distribution.

Dealers and retail distribution

1 min
  • Consignment shipments (stock the store only pays for once it sells) to retailers are generally a poor distribution method.
  • Retailers resent them, and getting paid (collections) is difficult.
  • Use models where the retailer buys the product, or where demand is created by ads first.
  • For a sample coupon (a free-offer voucher) that is redeemable at any store, send the ad proof (a copy of the ad) to dealers in advance.
  • Also send the list of publications with their circulation (number of copies) so they can stock.
  • Each coupon redemption then counts as a full-price sale for the dealer.
  • This motivates them to stock.
  • When samples are requested at a central store, that store gets the traffic.
  • Other dealers accept this as long as they share in the sales.
  • Routing traffic to supplied stores solves the problem of uneven stocking.
  • Dealers often discount advertised products.
  • They rarely push another company's product hard.
  • Plan distribution around your own selling effort, instead of expecting retailers to sell it for you.

Rollout sequence for local distribution:

  1. Begin with advertising in a few local markets, one town at a time.
  2. Name the stores that already stock the product in each ad.
  3. Add each new stocking store's name to later ads as dealers join.
  4. Move to wider national advertising once local distribution is in place.

Retail and wholesale buyers

1 min
  • Retail buyers, especially grocery chains, often ask for syndicated sales and scan data when a brand is being listed.
  • This data is called spin data, sales figures collected from store checkouts.
  • Prepare this data and the sales performance behind it before pitching the retailer. Otherwise the listing process stalls.
  • Hire someone who handles retail data requests once wholesale accounts (bulk accounts with stores) grow.
  • The store-building method also works for B2B niches (business-to-business markets) such as restaurant furniture or kitchen equipment.
  • The site must be customized for business buyers.

Store building and brand

2 min
  • Do not buy an existing store to skip building one.
  • A store worth buying, even one making only a few thousand dollars per month, is worth tens of thousands of dollars.
  • Buying a cheap site usually means buying something that can be rebuilt quickly. (Source 2018.)
  • Products left over in a supplier's catalog, such as coffee tables or bookshelves from a sofa supplier, can become the basis for future stores. (Source 2018.)
  • Look for commonalities among the extra products.
  • Build a store around each one.
  • Brands that are actually searched by name are preferred.
  • Lesser-known brands with vague names can be promoted through internal promotions (offers inside your own store) to remarketing audiences (people who visited before).
  • They can be promoted this way rather than relying on search traffic.
  • For websites such as Shopify, use SVG or PNG for a logo file.
  • Shopify accepts SVG logos.
  • SVG keeps sharp quality at any size because it uses vector math (drawing with shapes) instead of pixels (tiny dots).
  • Before uninstalling an app (removing an add-on), confirm it leaves no leftover code (code left behind).
  • Do this by searching the store's page source (the hidden code of a web page) for the app or developer name. (Source 2023.)
  • Do not share store links with other sellers or publicly.
  • Competitors who copy niches quickly flood the market.
  • Show example store structure internally instead. (Source 2023.)
  • Outsourced content writing (paying others to write), such as product descriptions, collection page text and blog posts with keywords (search words), can start from day one.
  • Organic traffic (free visitors from search) builds over time.
  • Outsourcing paid ads early is not recommended because there is no baseline (a starting point of normal results) to judge results against. (Source 2023.)
  • Native translation (translation by a fluent local speaker) for a new market: a freelance full-page translation (by an independent worker) costs about 15 to 20 dollars.
  • It can go up to 30 to 40 dollars for a full page.
  • Google Translate or DeepL (automatic translation tools) is not recommended because it lacks quality.

Upsells and cross-selling

1 min

An upsell is an extra offer. Cross-selling is offering a related product. Secondary store cross-promotion setup:

  1. Build a second store in a related niche using the same theme, with a different logo and colour scheme.
  2. Add a note to the order confirmation email of the first store. The note thanks the customer and offers 10% off their first purchase at the second store.
  3. Include the coupon code and the link to the second store in that note.
  4. Repeat the process in reverse, offering first-purchase discounts on the first store to customers of the second.
  5. Keep the two stores separate and do not combine their products.

Sourcing an upsell product:

  1. Check whether your existing suppliers already sell the upsell product.
  2. If not, search eBay for sellers who private label (sell their own brand of) that product and have strong feedback (good buyer ratings) and high sales volume.
  3. Contact those sellers and ask for their best price for dropshipping the product.
  4. Add the product as an upsell. Branding and search traffic matter less once the customer has already committed to the core product.
  • Upsell products do not need to be branded or searched for.
  • The customer has already decided to buy the core product.
  • Pick items that just make sense with the purchase, with good reviews and a useful function.

  • Until then, have enough existing creatives for the first week.

  • The first screen of a product page must load quickly and show the product clearly.
  • Cheap bulk traffic, such as buying 20,000 website visitors for $5 on Fiverr (a freelance service marketplace), does not produce sales.
  • These visits are not targeted (aimed at real buyers) and are fake, so they do not convert (turn into sales).

Outreach, partnerships and affiliates

1 min

Supplier and contributor outreach

1 min

Email each supplier or brand manually when a contributor or expert has recommended its product.

  1. Identify any suppliers or brands recommended by contributors or mentioned in the content and already sold on your store.
  2. Email each brand manually, never through a mass email tool like Klaviyo (email software for bulk sending).
  3. Say you published the post, name the contributor and the product model they recommended, include the link, and ask the brand to share it on social media, on its website and in its email newsletter.
  • Brands are likely to share content when a respected expert publicly praises their product.
  • It gives them endorsement they can use.
  • Personal outreach also builds a stronger supplier relationship.

Supplier outreach email, written manually and never sent as a mass email:

Subject: We featured your [Product name and model number] in a new guide

Hi [Supplier contact name],

I wanted to let you know we just published a new blog post: [Article title], [URL]

[Contributor name], [Contributor role or expertise], contributed to the post and said your [Product name and model number] is their favorite, because [Contributor's reason].

We would really appreciate it if you could share the article on your social media, on your website, or in your email newsletter if you have one.

Thanks,
[Your name]

Affiliate networks and referrals

1 min
  • Commission Junction and ShareASale are affiliate networks. In them, store owners sign up as vendors and pay affiliates (promoters) a commission on sales they refer.
  • Search their affiliate program databases (lists of member programs) for stores in your niche.
  • Those stores can be promoted to your audience.
  • Referral commissions work best when you list and sell the products yourself.
  • Refer a customer to the brand only when you cannot close the sale (get the customer to buy).
  • This works under a prior agreement on the commission. (Source 2025.)

Team, outsourcing and community

1 min

Hiring a virtual assistant or freelancer

1 min
  • Interview a prospective VA (virtual assistant, a remote helper) or freelancer before hiring, especially when the role is ongoing.
  • They will interact closely with the store and strongly influence its success.
  • The interview tests attitude and reasoning ability alongside skill.
  • These traits are hard to teach, while Shopify store mechanics can be taught.
  • The goal is not for the candidate to answer every question perfectly.
  • Look for a positive attitude, the ability to think through a problem, initiative and sound reasoning toward a correct answer.
  • During the interview, listen actively and ask follow-up questions to each answer.
  • The interview is also a chance for the candidate to decide whether they want to work with you.

Use the following sets of interview questions, organised by skill area, to evaluate a virtual assistant or freelancer.

General

1 min
  1. Tell me about any work you've done with ecommerce stores and walk me through it.
  2. Can you send me a portfolio or CV showing your past work?
  3. Are there past clients or colleagues from relevant projects who would be willing to speak with me about your work, and could I get their contact details?
  4. Recall a situation where you ran into a problem you had no immediate answer for. How did you work through it and fix it?
  5. What activities do you enjoy in your free time? What is one thing that genuinely irritates you?

Customer Service

1 min
  1. What customer service experience have you had before?
  2. How would you handle a furious customer over the phone?
  3. What would you do if a customer asked something you couldn't answer?
  4. What could you do to make the shopping experience better for a customer of our store?
  5. How would you describe your general philosophy toward customer service?
  6. Tell me about a moment when a customer gave you praise. How did it make you feel?
  7. If a customer asks to talk to the manager or owner and that person isn't around, what do you say to them?

Product Description and Content Writing

1 min
  1. Have you written anything similar that I could review?
  2. Walk me through how you come up with content.
  3. Would you be willing to complete a sample task, such as a small number of trial product descriptions?
  4. What separates an excellent product description from an average one? What role does a product description play on a website?
  5. Describe a time you had to write about a subject you had no background in. How did you get up to speed on it and then write about it?

Product Uploading and General Admin

1 min
  1. Do you know Shopify?
  2. If so, how would you carry out a bulk product import using a Shopify CSV file (a spreadsheet upload format)?
  3. Describe a time you needed to pick up a new system or tool to do your work. What steps did you take?
  4. On a packed day with many different tasks, how do you organise yourself and decide what comes first?

Mentoring and community

Risk and insurance

1 min
  • Insurance audit: a check of your insurance records. It is called retroactive because it looks back at past years.
  • Trigger: the audit can be started by growth, meaning your business getting bigger.
  • Cost: the audit can result in a bill for past premiums (the regular payments you made for insurance) of more than $10,000.
  • No claims needed: this can happen even if no claims occurred, so you never asked the insurer to pay for a loss.

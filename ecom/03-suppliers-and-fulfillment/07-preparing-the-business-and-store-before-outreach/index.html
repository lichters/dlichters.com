Playbook › 3. Suppliers and fulfillment

Preparing the business and store before outreach

Section 3.7 · 7 of 22 in this chapter · 19 min read

In short

  • Suppliers approve you only if you look like a real, trustworthy business.
  • Build a finished store first, then contact suppliers.
  • Before you apply, set up a business email, business phone, business address and tax number (EIN).
  • Aim for approval from five suppliers first.
  • Suppliers may see demo products on your store, but you must say clearly that you are not selling them yet.
More key points
  • Before you contact any supplier, click every link and make sure the site has no password lock and no spelling errors.
  • Have your legal company, business email, business phone and business address in place first.
  • Your first outreach aims at five supplier approvals.

Why suppliers screen before they approve

2 min
  • Trust is the main factor in supplier approval.
  • Suppliers share their full catalog (complete product list) with authorized retailers.
  • Suppliers depend on the retailer to show the brand to customers.
  • So approval is a judgment: are you legitimate, and will you act responsibly?
  • Brands put their reputation in the retailer's hands.
  • They need to be confident you will look after customers and are not a scam.
  • The supplier's worst case is a retailer who takes orders but never fulfils them (never ships them).
  • Another bad case: a retailer takes the supplier's full catalog, photos, names and descriptions.
  • That retailer collects payments and never forwards orders or ships products.
  • Customers then see the brand as a scam.
  • So suppliers carefully check (vet) retailers and avoid those who show this risk.
  • Suppliers do not refuse retailers in principle (as a rule).
  • Retailer sales are how suppliers earn revenue.
  • Each supplier you contact needs proof that you are a real business that treats customers properly.
  • Good suppliers screen applicants (check them first).
  • Good suppliers approve only professional sellers who can explain their business clearly.
  • Showing a professional store during the application improves approval chances.
  • Brand owners want resellers who protect brand value.
  • They want products shown professionally on a working, believable site.
  • A finished store is evidence that makes approval more likely.
  • Suppliers expect you to have already put in time and some money.
  • A visible setup, like a working site and business emails, shows commitment before any approval.
  • Established brands can be worth millions, so brand owners screen applicants.
  • Brand owners want to avoid scammers and unvetted resellers (resellers nobody has checked).
  • A working website, business emails and consistent branding show you can be trusted with their products.
  • Cold outreach (contacting someone who did not ask) from someone with no website, brand or email looks unprofessional.
  • Cold outreach also wastes the supplier's time.
  • Contact suppliers for reseller approval only after a real business presence exists.
  • Remove red flags (warning signs) before you contact suppliers.
  • Removing red flags significantly increases approval rates.
  • This works because it shows you are a professional, trustworthy business.
  • Suppliers judge store quality and professionalism before a partnership.
  • A polished, complete store signals a legitimate business.
  • Suppliers check a dropshipping store (you sell, and the supplier ships the order) for trust and completeness before approving it.

Sequencing: store, supplier list and outreach

2 min
  • Build and launch the store first, meaning the online shop, before contacting suppliers.
  • No legitimate supplier will approve you or accept orders without first reviewing a finished, professional storefront (the public side of the shop).
  • Suppliers are more likely to approve a retailer with a working, professional storefront.
  • Suppliers are less likely to approve one that has only an early-stage idea.
  • A finished demo store (a sample shop shown to suppliers) means design and technical work is done before outreach.
  • That shortens approval timelines and reduces back-and-forth (messages going back and forth).
  • The store must exist before you approach preferred suppliers (a named group of suppliers).
  • Preferred suppliers usually will not approve a retailer with no store yet.
  • Build the website first.
  • Then apply for supplier approvals, because suppliers approve stores that already exist.
  • Before a supplier discusses approval, it usually asks where you sell.
  • Have a live, high-quality website ready to show.
  • The site lets the supplier picture its own products on your store.
  • Silver-tier suppliers (suppliers at the silver level of their program) approve new retailers through their own approval form.
  • They review your live store (online and public), so it must be fully built and online before you apply.
  • Build the store before contacting suppliers.
  • Suppliers review the site, and it must look good enough to support your approval rate (the share of applications approved).
  • A finished website shows suppliers how their products will look.
  • A finished website also shows that you are serious.
  • Figures show approval rates are significantly higher when you have a website to show.
  • The Shopify (a platform for building online stores) demo store should be fully set up and look finished before the supplier review.
  • Suppliers review the demo store during the approval talk.
  • A store that is not ready weakens the approval request.
  • A professional store and business setup make suppliers respond more positively.
  • Prepare both before the first outreach.
  • Store approvals are easier when the store looks live, even with a few demo products.

Timing conflict: store first or minimum essentials first

1 min
  • Three positions exist on timing.
  • The store-first position is repeated across most points above.
  • Two other positions appear:
  • Minimum essentials: do not wait for the demo store to be perfect.
  • Complete the minimum essential elements by end of day.
  • Then immediately email the supplier with your store link and request next steps.
  • Timing matters more than completeness.
  • Start early (2023): do not wait until everything on the store is perfect.
  • Supplier approval takes time, and delays add up.
  • So start outreach as soon as your niche (the product area you choose to sell) is chosen.
  • Split sequence: for high-ticket dropshipping (expensive products you sell without holding stock), build the supplier list before the website is finished.
  • Park the list until the store is built and set up.
  • Approval to sell the products on your site comes in a later stage.
  • Finish the supplier research now.
  • Set the list aside until the website is built.
  • Contact suppliers only after the store is set up.

When each applies:

  • Store first is required where the supplier reviews a live store, as with silver-tier and preferred suppliers.
  • Minimum essentials is the 2020 approach. It depends on the basics being in place before you send the store link.
  • Early outreach from 2023 starts at niche selection. Research and list building can begin right away.
  • While the first approval request waits until the store looks presentable.

Supplier list and target

1 min
  • Add each supplier's website, phone number and email to a master supplier list (one main list of supplier contacts).
  • Keep all supplier contact details in one Master Supplier List.
  • The target for the first store is approval from five suppliers.

Sourcing and approval order

1 min
  1. Add each supplier's website, phone number and email to a master supplier list.
  2. Finalize the master supplier list.
  3. Set up a business email address, business phone number and mailing address.
  4. Get approved by your first five suppliers.

Legitimacy build order

1 min
  1. Register the business as an official business entity (the legal form your company is registered under).
  2. Set up official business email addresses.
  3. Set up a business phone number separate from your personal phone.
  4. Prepare a supplier contact plan.
  5. After approval, upload the live products you are authorized to sell.

Business entity and tax identity

1 min
  • Register the business entity before contacting suppliers if you are committed to the venture.
  • Switching later from a sole proprietorship (a one-owner business with no separate legal form) to an LLC (a common US business form) or corporation means resubmitting all documents to each supplier.
  • Before approaching silver-tier suppliers, have a registered business entity in place.
  • Also have a business email address and a business phone number in place.
  • Suppliers check these during review.
  • A registered entity, business email, dedicated business phone and polished demo store signal trustworthiness during silver approval review.
  • Roughly 90% of suppliers ask for a sales tax certificate (a state paper proving you are a registered seller for sales tax).
  • Suppliers expect a business EIN (Employer Identification Number, a US business tax number) rather than a personal Social Security number (a US personal ID number).
  • An LLC name, EIN and sales tax certificate together are what is needed to start securing suppliers.
  • Many suppliers ask for your EIN when you start working with them.
  • So have it ready before you contact suppliers.
  • Some suppliers require an EIN before approval.
  • Others do not require it.
  • If the EIN is still pending, tell the supplier it is pending and keep reaching out to suppliers that do not require it.
  • Suppliers usually ask for the business tax number during account approval.
  • In Australia, that number is the ABN (Australian Business Number) or ACN (Australian Company Number).
  • So have it ready before contacting them.

Conflict on certificates by state

1 min
  • Position A says not to apply for new certificates per state, because suppliers pushing this are using outdated practices.
  • Position B says that when a supplier requires certificates in all 50 states, consider not selling for that supplier.
  • Position C says a certificate must be obtained before buying from any supplier that requires it.
  • These apply to different cases.
  • Get the certificate for your own state through the state website.
  • Decline a supplier whose demand covers many states, since this is time-consuming.
  • Position A applies to supplier demands for certificates from states where you do not operate.

Business address

2 min
  • Use a dedicated business address instead of your home address.
  • Use it on supplier applications, your store website and other business accounts.
  • This keeps private information from being published online.
  • Publishing a home address on a store website exposes private information to anyone online.
  • Avoid this from the start.
  • Use a virtual or mailing address (a mail address that is not your home) for public business information.
  • One expert saw no evidence that a home address on a Google account causes suspension (the account being shut down).
  • Get the business address set up before contacting suppliers, because supplier approval forms ask for a business name and address.
  • A virtual business address is optional.
  • It separates your home address from business documents.
  • A mail service holds the mail.
  • It either forwards the mail on a schedule or waits for pickup.
  • A supplier that asks for a business address can be given the virtual address.
  • The virtual address is used for bank statements, credit cards and suppliers.
  • The virtual address matters only if the supplier requires one.
  • A standard business address is the usual choice.
  • If a supplier needs a US mailing address and the seller is based abroad, use a US mailing address such as a UPS Store (a shipping and mailbox store chain) location in the seller's US shipping area.
  • A P.O. Box (a post office mailbox) looks less professional than a business address with a suite number (a unit number in a building).
  • In the United States, a USPS P.O. Box must be listed as a P.O. Box.
  • That label signals a small box inside a post office.
  • A UPS Store address does not require the P.O. Box label.
  • One expert prefers a suite or unit style address, because the address is printed on the store.
  • A P.O. Box can also serve as a virtual address.
  • UPS Store locations are widespread in the United States.
  • A ZIP code (US postal code) search for New York City returned more than 28 locations.
  • The cheapest UPS Store option is a folder in a cabinet behind the counter.
  • Mail is stored there for pickup or forwarding.
  • Prices vary by location because UPS Store locations are franchises (independently owned branches).
  • Some UPS Store locations offer a mailbox you open with a key.
  • Others offer the folder-in-cabinet option.
  • Ask the specific location which option it offers before you sign up.
  • Request the smallest size available and ask for pricing to keep monthly costs low for a business address.
  • Post Scan Mail (a mail-receiving and scanning service) receives your post at an address you choose a plan for.
  • For an extra fee it can open and scan paper mail and email you a copy.
  • Its starter plan costs $15 per month in the United States.
  • Where possible, have business correspondence delivered electronically by email.
  • Only mail that must arrive on paper needs a physical address or a scanning service.
  • Keep the exact same business address across the website, Google Merchant Center (Google's product listing tool) and Google Ads (Google's paid ad tool).
  • Resubmit after any correction.
  • Even a small mismatch can trigger a suspension or delay the account's reinstatement (getting it switched back on).

Procedure: set up a UPS Store business address in the United States

1 min
  1. Open the UPS Store website.
  2. Click Find a Store.
  3. Enter the ZIP code where you want your business address.
  4. Review the listed locations and click on the stores to check them.
  5. Visit the store in that area or call it.
  6. Ask what the mailbox costs, request the smallest size, and ask for the folder-in-cabinet option, which is the cheapest.
  7. Choose whether you pick up your mail in person or pay the store to forward it to you.

Business email

2 min
  • Set up a business email address, phone number and mailing address before applying to suppliers, since suppliers are approached as a business.
  • Contacting suppliers without a business email, business phone number and mailing address can lead to rejection.
  • This happens because suppliers expect a verifiable business (one they can check).
  • The target for the first store is approval from five suppliers.
  • The business email, phone number and mailing address are needed before contacting them.
  • They are needed because suppliers need a professional business identity to approve you.
  • Contact suppliers from the store's own email address rather than an email tied to the business entity.
  • Send supplier approval emails from a business address on your own domain (your own web address, such as yourstore.com).
  • Do not use a free personal webmail account such as Gmail or Yahoo.
  • Free addresses look unprofessional and can slow supplier approval.
  • Use the same business address on the website, for customer replies and for supplier communication in the long term.
  • Use one primary login address, such as sales@yourdomain.com, for the Google Workspace account (paid business email and tools from Google).
  • Add further addresses as aliases (extra addresses that land in the same inbox).
  • Aliases such as returns@ and a personal first-name address deliver to the same inbox, so one login manages all of them.
  • Adding an alias does not require a separate paid account.
  • Use a personal alias with first and last name for supplier approval emails, so the supplier sees a real person from the business.
  • Use the sales alias for general sales contact.
  • Use the returns alias for return or refund communication.
  • Separating addresses by purpose makes replies easier to sort.
  • Suppliers see the sender's name and address before approving a business account.
  • A professional address with the first and last name builds more trust than a personal email.
  • Use the firstname@ alias with the full name for supplier contact.
  • In supplier forms' marketing, purchasing, accounts payable (the team that pays bills) or sales contact fields, a generic support or email address at the store's domain is acceptable when no real contact exists.

Business phone number

1 min
  • Create a dedicated business phone number before contacting suppliers.
  • A store's public phone number should not be a personal line.
  • Supplier callbacks would otherwise get mixed up with spam and unknown calls.
  • A separate business number lets you answer supplier and customer calls without guessing who is calling.
  • OpenPhone and Google Voice (apps that give you a second phone number) are available tools for creating business phone numbers.
  • Use a US business phone number even if you run the business from outside the US.
  • Suppliers ask for phone numbers, and US-based stores usually need one anyway.
  • Many members operate from abroad without problems. (2023.)
  • Purchase a local Skype phone number (a phone number from the Skype app) for your target market.
  • For example, use a US number if you are calling from overseas.
  • Suppliers see your local number rather than an international one.
  • This improves answer rates (how often calls get answered).
  • Avoid collecting customer phone numbers before launch, unless the supplier asks for them.
  • Pre-launch sign-ups cannot find you before the store goes live.

Procedure: business phone setup

1 min
  1. Decide whether the personal phone number is acceptable for now, or sign up for Quo's Starter plan (a business phone app plan) to get a business number.
  2. If using Quo, install the app and log in so calls work from the phone.
  3. Add the business phone number to the header (the top of each page) and to the Contact us section of the footer (the bottom of each page) in the Shopify theme (the store's design template).
  4. Click Save.
  5. Make sure the app is ready before supplier calls begin.

Demo store setup

2 min
  • Use 5 demo products.
  • Five is enough to show suppliers what the store looks like and how their products would be presented.
  • A working homepage (the front page), one collection page (a page that lists a group of products) and product pages (one page per product) are enough.
  • These show the supplier what their products will look like.
  • A newer store can list a few products from a supplier at a higher markup (an extra amount added to the supplier's price) to build credibility when approaching more brands. (2025.)
  • Choose the products with the best margins (the profit you keep per sale).
  • Do not include those products in paid ads.
  • Uploading the full catalog is not required.
  • Do not use Oberlo (an app that copies supplier products into your store) for demo products.
  • Build them manually.
  • Demo products do not need real specs (technical details), materials or dimensions.
  • Generic descriptions are enough when the products are not for sale.
  • Do not use dropshipping apps to fill the store before supplier approval.
  • Use stock images (ready-made photos from a photo library) to build the site for suppliers to review.
  • Build the demo store with stock or generic products so the supplier can picture how its products would look on your site.
  • The demo is not meant to mislead them.
  • Do not display one supplier's branded products on a demo store to pitch a different supplier.
  • The first brand may find them through Google alerts (email updates when the web mentions a term) and state that the store is not authorized to sell its products.
  • A good-looking live store that already sells other brands makes suppliers more willing to work with you.
  • Show your site when you approach a supplier.
  • Well-organized filters (tools that narrow products by type) and tags (labels on products) on the storefront show future suppliers how the website functions.
  • Additional tag lists can be added over time as more suppliers are onboarded (brought on board).
  • The store must show a logo, a favicon (the small icon in the browser tab), consistent colors, clean collections with images, working links and a visible email signup.
  • A buyer's guide (a helpful page that explains how to choose) on the store is optional before contacting suppliers.
  • It adds credibility and trust.
  • Link the domain to Shopify before suppliers review the store.
  • Before you contact suppliers, a few days of study of store-building and traffic (visitors to the store) basics makes it easier to explain your business.
  • That study also tends to increase approvals.
  • You do not need to put that study into practice yet.

Password removal and purchase blocking

1 min
  • The storefront must be publicly viewable before you contact suppliers.
  • Suppliers check the URL (the web address) themselves.
  • Removing the password protection (a lock that hides the store from the public) makes this possible.
  • The site must be supplier ready: the domain is linked to Shopify and the storefront password has been removed.
  • Suppliers should see the store only once it is fully ready.
  • Do not set a store password when showing demos to suppliers.
  • To prevent purchases on a demo store shown to suppliers, disable all payment options (ways customers pay).
  • Or leave the shipping profile (the rules for how orders ship) with no selected zones (shipping regions).
  • Then no one can check out.

Demo-mode disclosure

1 min

Demo mode means your store shows placeholder products that are not for sale yet.

  • If a supplier sees its products on your store before launch, explain that they are only demonstration placements (products shown as examples). Tell the supplier this lets it see how its products will look. You are not selling them yet. This also shows you can build and maintain a website. (Written 2021 to 2022.)
  • Do not present a demo as a live shop. The demo is for showing layout and presentation, not for selling. It is not meant to mislead the supplier.

Trust signals and pre-outreach checks

1 min

Trust signals are signs that show you are a real, reliable business. Pre-outreach checks are the checks you make before you contact suppliers.

  • Before contacting suppliers, the store must look trustworthy. Links must work. There must be no typos or errors, and trust signals must be in place. Conversion optimization (changing the store so more visitors buy) can wait until after the supplier approves you. (Written 2024.)
  • Pre-outreach checks before contacting suppliers:
    1. Make sure the website is not locked behind a password.
    2. Click every link to confirm it works.
    3. Spell-check the whole site (use a tool that finds spelling mistakes), including collection names (the names of your product category pages) and the About Us page, for obvious errors.
    4. Confirm the basic structure is complete before sending supplier applications.

Credibility building

1 min

Credibility means how much people trust that you are real.

  • A social media presence is not make-or-break (decisive) for supplier approval. But suppliers often check that a company is legitimate by looking for active pages under the company name. Active means recently updated, not empty. (Written 2021.)
  • A newer store can build credibility by listing a few supplier products at a higher markup (the extra amount you add on top of the supplier's price), as described above. Do this rather than uploading a full catalog (your complete product list).
  • Present the store and business setup professionally. This is part of the approval case, together with the entity (your legal company), email and phone already in place.

Readiness checklist

1 min

Before supplier calls, prepare the following:

  1. A live, high-quality demo store that suppliers can review. Live means public and open to visitors.
  2. A clear plan for generating additional sales for their products.
  3. An explanation that your focus as a business owner is exceptional customer service.

Before the first supplier contact, confirm:

  • Registered business entity (your legal company, registered with the state), business email, business phone and business address are in place.
  • EIN (Employer Identification Number, your US business tax ID from the IRS), sales tax certificate (the permit that lets you collect sales tax) and any state certificate (a permit from your state) that suppliers require are ready to send. If the EIN is still pending (not yet issued), know its status.
  • The store is live with no password. Every link works. The text is checked. The demo products are in place.
  • The master supplier list (your full list of suppliers to contact) is finalized.
  • The first outreach (your first contact asking for approval) aims at approval from five suppliers.

Playbook › 3. Suppliers and fulfillment

Freight, heavy items and white glove delivery

Section 3.14 · 14 of 22 in this chapter · 48 min read

In short

  • Large and heavy products can cost so much to ship that they lose money. Check the shipping cost before you list or advertise.
  • Get quotes (price offers) from several freight companies. The same shipment can differ by 100 to 300 dollars.
  • For heavy items, set a minimum price so the profit covers shipping.
  • Decide who books the shipment: the supplier's account or yours.
  • Small packages go with UPS, FedEx or USPS. Large or heavy goods go by freight, which is booked through a freight broker (a middleman who finds freight companies for you).
More key points
  • Ask your supplier which shipping companies its biggest sellers use, then ask those companies for price quotes.
  • The BOL (bill of lading, the freight shipping label) is what your supplier needs before the freight company can pick up the goods.
  • Standard delivery is curbside (left outside the door). Liftgate and white glove (inside delivery and setup) are paid options, not free extras.
  • Heavy items cost a lot to ship, so run paid ads only near the supplier's warehouse.
  • Get several freight quotes before you list a heavy item.
  • Check that your price still leaves profit after shipping.
  • Mark large items as not eligible for return, and ask how the supplier packs them.
  • Bulky products are charged for the space they take up, so big items can cost a lot to ship.
  • Before you list a bulky product, check its packed size and shipping method with the supplier.
  • Get two shipping price offers, compare them, and choose the cheaper one.

Why heavy items change the margin

1 min
  • Large and heavy products cost more to ship.
  • That leaves less profit per sale. This profit is your margin (the money you keep from each sale after costs).
  • When you compare a cheap product with an expensive one, include the shipping cost in both.
  • Large and heavy items need their own shipping calculation.
  • Their cost depends on weight and size (dimensions means length, width and height).
  • A small change in cost can change the margin much more than it does for small items.
  • Large or bulky products, such as home decor, can be expensive to ship.
  • Calculate the shipping cost against the ad price and margin before you test.
  • Shipping can make a product look profitable in ads but unprofitable in real life.
  • Large and heavy products usually ship at freight rates (truck shipping), not standard parcel rates (small-package services such as UPS or FedEx).
  • Confirm the weight and size limits with carriers (delivery companies) before you set the selling price.
  • Heavy and bulky products stay profitable only if the margin can absorb freight costs (cover them).
  • Compare quotes from several companies.
  • Check the supplier's preferred carrier first. A supplier is the company that sends you the product.
  • Heavy items can cost more to ship than the margin allows.
  • Example: a 60 lb (pound) item may cost 160 USD to ship.
  • Calculate the average freight cost before you list (put in your store) and advertise the product.
  • Example: a 430-dollar shipping cost on an order that earns 90 dollars of margin makes the item unprofitable.

Check shipping cost before listing or advertising

1 min

Set a price floor by category

1 min

A price floor is the lowest price you can sell at and still profit.

  • For heavy or bulky items, ask suppliers for average shipping costs to the markets you plan to serve before you choose a product.
  • Set a minimum price so the margin covers shipping.
  • Do not list cheaper items in that category.
  • Older (2019) example: If a heavy item such as a gun safe costs about $250 to ship, a $200 product cannot be sold profitably.
  • If the lowest profitable price is $600, every item below $600 is excluded.
  • Customers who want cheaper items are left to competitors.
  • If a brand's terms require a freight shipment on your account, and the product margin works without MAP, freight is worth doing. MAP (minimum advertised price) is the lowest price a brand lets you show in ads.
  • Ask the supplier which freight company it recommends, and work with that company.
  • A 30% markup on heavy products may not cover shipping costs. A markup is a percent added on top of your cost.
  • Shop freight quotes from multiple carriers. Do not accept only the supplier's shipping quote.
  • For a large or heavy product, nationwide shipping can cost 100 to 150 dollars.
  • For huge items, it can reach 200 to 250 dollars.
  • At a low price, this is not profitable with free shipping. Free shipping means the customer pays nothing for delivery, and you pay.
  • Carriers such as freight companies can cost around $150 per shipment.
  • At that cost, items priced at $250 to $300 are unprofitable.
  • Ask each supplier which carrier it ships with.

Estimate a whole range of products

2 min

Procedure: estimating shipping costs for many supplier products without quoting each one (2024)

  1. For each product, calculate the spread in a spreadsheet. The spread is the retail price (MAP) minus the wholesale cost (the price you pay the supplier).
  2. Sort the products from highest to lowest spread (the gap between buying and selling price).
  3. Look at the products with the smallest spread at the bottom of the sheet.
  4. Ask the supplier for the package size and weight of those 20 to 30 low-spread products.
  5. Get free shipping quotes for those products from freightcenter.com.
  6. Do not list products where the shipping cost would use up the margin.

Procedure: estimating shipping cost for heavy items before pricing (2024)

  • Enter the supplier warehouse address and several made-up customer addresses across the country into freightcenter.com.
  • Read the average shipping cost per item from the results.
  • Set the selling price so the product stays profitable.

Procedure: estimating whether a large, heavy product is worth selling before you have supplier prices

  1. Assume a wholesale cost, for example half of the expected selling price.
  2. Ask supplier candidates for the box size and weight of the product.
  3. Get shipping quotes using these dimensions and weight. No account login is needed.
  4. Check whether the spread after shipping is big enough to leave a solid margin.

Procedure: get estimated freight rates before orders come in (2024)

  1. Use a free instant freight quote site such as freightcenter.com. An instant quote shows the price right away.
  2. Enter the supplier's ZIP code as origin. A ZIP code is a US postal code. Origin means where the goods leave from.
  3. Enter several customer ZIP codes in different regions, for example one near the supplier, one on the East Coast and one in the West or South.
  4. Compare the quotes to estimate shipping costs.

Exact costs need both addresses and the package size.

Procedure: average freight cost for free-shipping pricing

  1. Get the supplier's typical product size and weight.
  2. Use a freight quote tool such as freightcenter.com for freight shipments.
  3. Set the ship-from address to the supplier's warehouse, for example New York.
  4. Get quotes to ship-to addresses (customer addresses) in several regions, for example New York, Florida, Texas and California.
  5. Average the quotes and build that cost into the price of free shipping.

Choose who books the freight: supplier account or your own

1 min

Supplier account

1 min
  • Some suppliers allow you to ship on their account.
  • They charge you for product and shipping together.
  • Others require you to ship on your own account.
  • The ideal setup: the supplier quotes the order, creates the shipment and the Bill of Lading (BOL), and charges shipping on its own carrier account.
  • The store does not set up freight shipping in Shopify (online store software).
  • For large and heavy items such as furniture, it is normal for the supplier to ask the retailer to ship on the retailer's own shipping account.
  • Expect this request when a supplier names a shipping account.
  • When suppliers ship on their own account, they typically get volume discounts on freight.
  • So the shipping cost is better than you can negotiate alone.

Procedure: freight shipping when the supplier ships on its own account (2024)

  1. The supplier books the freight carrier and sends a tracking number (a code to follow the package) when the shipment is ready.
  2. Forward the tracking number to the customer.
  3. The freight carrier contacts the customer to schedule delivery, for example on a specific day between 12 and 3 pm, since large heavy items are not dropped at the door.

Procedure: freight shipping with a supplier that ships only to you (2024)

  1. Get quotes from a freight broker for the shipment.
  2. Choose the price and carrier you want.
  3. The broker issues a bill of lading (BOL).
  4. Send the BOL to the supplier.
  5. The supplier arranges with the trucking company to pick up the goods at the warehouse and deliver them to the customer.

Your own account

1 min
  • Shipping on your own account makes you responsible for anything that happens in transit (while the goods are moving).
  • Accept it only for large or heavy items when no other option exists.
  • Do not write the supplier off immediately.
  • Some suppliers ship on their own account, and some require you to ship on yours.
  • When you do, arrange the shipment as described below.
  • If a supplier says it will not ship on its own account, ask which of its top retailers use which carrier.
  • Those retailers usually have the best rates.
  • Contact that carrier to arrange pickup at the supplier's warehouse.
  • Ask the supplier to send a quote and to check whether its carrier is competitive.
  • Use the supplier's carrier if it is in line with the market.

Procedure: freight shipping when you ship on your own account (not UPS or FedEx) (2024)

  1. Create the shipment through a freight broker such as freightcenter.com.
  2. The broker generates a BOL (Bill of Lading), a document describing the shipment from the supplier warehouse to the customer address.
  3. Send the BOL to the supplier.
  4. The supplier arranges pickup at its warehouse with the freight shipper, who delivers to the customer.

Procedure: arranging freight shipping on your own account (2025)

  1. Shop online for freight quotes from multiple carriers.
  2. Compare pricing across different quotes.
  3. Select the quote you prefer.
  4. Pay for the shipment.
  5. Receive a BOL (Bill of Lading), which shows the pickup location (supplier warehouse) and the delivery location.
  6. Email the BOL to the supplier.
  7. The freight company contacts the supplier to arrange pickup date and time.
  8. The freight company picks up the order from the supplier warehouse.
  9. The shipment gets a tracking number and delivers to the customer.

Book the shipment and the bill of lading

1 min

What the BOL is

1 min
  • A bill of lading (BOL) is the freight equivalent of a parcel shipping label.

Regional Shipping Cost and Geo-Targeting

  • Shipping cost varies with origin and destination.
  • Shipping within the same state is cheaper than shipping across the country.
  • Cost estimates for heavy items should use realistic average values, not a single fixed number.
  • For bulky products sold with free shipping, prefer a supplier located near the main customer region.
  • In the example, shipping within one state cost $150, versus $300 across the country.
  • That difference alone changed net profit (profit after all costs) from $300 to $450 per order.

Bulky Products: Packaging and Shipping Method

1 min
  • Volumetric weight is a shipping price rule. It charges for the space a box takes up, not only for its weight.
  • This rule starts to matter above about 1 kg.
  • Bulky items are charged for the space they take up.
  • So large or heavy products, such as furniture, can become very expensive.
  • They may need special shipping lines.
  • Large high-ticket products are expensive items, such as outdoor structures.
  • They usually ship disassembled, meaning in pieces inside boxes, not fully built.
  • Before you list a bulky product, confirm the packed form and shipping method with the supplier. A supplier is the company that provides the product to you.
  • This tip is from 2021-22, so it may be outdated.

Further Details: Freight Cost Check for a New Supplier

1 min

Freight means shipping goods in large amounts. A carrier is the shipping company. A quote is a price offer.

  1. Ask the supplier for a freight quote for the first order, from the carrier they use.
  2. Get a second quote on freightcenter.com. Use these details:
    • Product dimensions (size).
    • Supplier's pickup location (where the goods leave).
    • Customer's delivery location (where the goods arrive).
  3. Compare both quotes.
  4. Choose the cheaper option, or negotiate with the supplier using the lower quote.

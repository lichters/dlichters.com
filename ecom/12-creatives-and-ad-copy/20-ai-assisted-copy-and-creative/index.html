AI-assisted copy and creative

Section 12.20 · 20 of 26 in this chapter · 79 min read

In short

  • Give the AI one long, complete brief first: the product, the customer, the problem, the features and their benefits.
  • Then improve the result with several short follow-up messages. Do not start over each time.
  • Check every AI output for facts and claims before you publish it.
  • Write the core copy yourself. Use AI as a helper, not as the final writer.
  • Keep your reference files in one project, so every new chat starts with the same facts.
More key points
  • Copy the prompts in the code blocks exactly, and fill in only the [brackets].
  • Check every AI output before you use it. AI can be wrong or invent facts.
  • Never add numbers, reviews, expert names or customer quotes that you do not have.
  • Before every ad launch, turn off Meta's automatic AI changes.
  • Check every AI text and image yourself before you use it. AI (a computer helper that writes or draws) makes mistakes, and a spell checker does not check if something is true.
  • Never publish a claim you cannot prove.
  • Give the AI your brand, your customer and your product facts. Vague input gives generic output.
  • Expect several rounds. Change one specific thing at a time.
  • Test AI images quickly, then send the winners to your designers for polished versions.
  • Check every word in an AI-made image before you publish it, because AI often gets text wrong.
  • Write all numbers as words before you make audio, because the voice tool mispronounces digits in most languages.
  • Keep the scene prompt plain and add the photo style as a separate step.
  • Use a face-forward portrait for any character that must speak.
  • Have a knowledgeable person check every AI script and text for false facts before you publish it. This matters most for health, religious and other sensitive topics.
  • Choose the main message (the angle) first. Then let the AI write the script around it.
  • Check every image, infographic and subtitle yourself. AI makes mistakes in text, faces and small details.
  • Test every AI text against real sales results before you spend more money on it.

Briefing the AI before prompting

2 min
  • Before you ask an AI tool for ad copy (the written words of an ad), know the product's desire, meaning what the buyer wants to reach or escape.
  • Know the product's features (what the product is or has).
  • Know the benefits (what each feature gives the customer).
  • Know the target persona (a made-up picture of your typical buyer).
  • Know the problem the product solves.
  • The AI only amplifies the inputs it gets.
  • Weak inputs produce weak copy.
  • Copy made without product inputs comes out generic, meaning vague and able to fit any product.
  • Always give the AI the unique selling points (USPs, the things that make your product different).
  • Give it the target audience.
  • Give it the framework (a set structure for the ad).
  • Do this before you ask for headlines (the big top line of an ad) or scripts (the spoken words of a video).
  • Generic AI copy written without knowing the target audience stays generic.
  • Give the model the audience, the problem, the market position (where you stand against rivals) and the unique features first.
  • Check every output for accuracy before you publish it.
  • A general chatbot, such as ChatGPT (an AI chat tool), used to write ad copy usually gives weak results.
  • This is true when it has no best-practice training (proven rules for good ads) or explicit instructions.
  • Teams that skip this training often decide AI does not work and go back to human copywriters (people paid to write ads).
  • Write detailed instructions and best-practice rules first.
  • Then judge the output.
  • Keep one brief in a spreadsheet (a table in a program like Excel).
  • The brief holds the desire, features and benefits, persona, awareness stages (how much the buyer knows about the problem and the solutions), sophistication stages (how many similar claims the buyer has already heard) and principles (proven persuasion rules).
  • All of these feed the headline and the body copy (the main text under the headline).
  • With the brief kept up to date, you can generate Facebook ad copy, landing page copy (text on the page buyers arrive at after a click) and video scripts from the same inputs.
  • Start with one long, complete brief.
  • It covers the product, desire, features and benefits, audience and chosen principles.
  • Ideally, dictate it by voice.
  • Then refine with a few short corrections.
  • In a worked example, strong copy took about five rounds.
  • The number of prompts is not the goal.
  • Use short prompts with minimal wording. Refine in several rounds.
  • In one comparison, a single long voice prompt followed by a few corrections was faster than many separate prompts.
  • Read this together with the long first brief: the brief comes first, then short corrections.

Basic AI-assisted ad copy workflow

1 min

  2. Ask for three headlines and three body copy texts for the Facebook and Instagram ad.
  3. Check whether the headline names the problem and hints at the solution.
    • If the market is crowded, ask for the unique feature to appear in the headline.
  4. Paste the list of psychological principles (proven persuasion methods) and ask for the copy to be rewritten with the best ones.

  6. Refine in several short follow-up prompts instead of starting over.

Paid-plan ChatGPT workflow

1 min
  1. Describe the product in plain words.
    • Include its main target audience, the situation the audience struggles with, and its unique features.
  2. Send the psychological principles, desire, benefits and persona you wrote.
  3. Ask for three headline examples and body copy.
  4. Review the output.
    • Remove generic phrases, for example "experience the magic of".
    • Tighten the benefit and the unique mechanism (the way the product works to fix the problem).
  5. Give the market awareness and sophistication stage, together with the competitors' claims.
    • Then ask for a rewrite that features the unique mechanism in the headline.
  6. For paid social ads (ads you pay to show on social media), ask for a shorter version.
    • Include bullet points, a clear call to action, a limited-time offer and fear of missing out (FOMO, the urge not to miss out).
    • Add emojis if you want them.
  7. Ask for variations of a headline that use a named principle, for example how to intensify a headline.

Two-step breakthrough prompt

1 min

Breakthrough advertising is a classic method for writing ads that match what buyers already know. Send step 1 first, then wait for the confirmation before you send step 2.

STEP 1 PROMPT (send first, then wait):
You are an experienced direct-response copywriter. I am selling [PRODUCT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION] to [TARGET MARKET, e.g. owners of large dogs who let them onto the bed]. The main problem I want to solve is [MAIN PROBLEM]. The unique features are: 1. [FEATURE 1: what it is and what it does for the customer]. 2. [FEATURE 2: what it is and what it does for the customer]. Write 3 headline examples for a [AD PLATFORM, e.g. Facebook ad] and 3 pieces of body copy using breakthrough advertising best practices. I will send you psychological principles and my market awareness and sophistication stages next. Do not write any copy until I send them. Confirm that you understand.

STEP 2 PROMPT (send after the confirmation):
Here are my market position and principles. Market awareness stage: [STAGE, e.g. solution aware]. Market sophistication stage: [STAGE, e.g. stage three: prospects have heard all the claims]. Psychological principles to use: [PASTE THE PRINCIPLES LIST]. Using this, rewrite the headlines and body copy. Each headline must include the unique feature or mechanism. Give each headline its own body copy. Format the output for [AD PLATFORM] with bullet points and emojis so it is easy to read. Keep the required elements: [REQUIRED ELEMENTS, e.g. offer, guarantee, call to action].

Primary text process (one chat per product)

1 min 
Generate Facebook ad copy for a product using the following process. Input: the product page URL, the ad creative description (the hook, the visual and the angle), and the brand name. Output: 3 headlines and 3 primary texts. Headline 1 (Problem-Aware): a headline about the offer. Headline 2 (Solution-Aware): a curiosity gap that makes the reader want the mechanism. Headline 3 (Transformation): an identity shift that makes the reader see themselves already using the product. Meta primary text and headline prompt (alternative format)

2 min

Meta is the company behind Facebook and Instagram. This prompt sets a stricter layout: 12 words per paragraph and four emoji lines.

  • Use this prompt when you want a fixed structure.
  • The process prompt above produces three headlines and three primary texts with V1 to V3 labels.
  • Use the process prompt for product pages with full offer details.
  • Use this prompt for quick, high-intent variations (versions aimed at buyers ready to buy).
Act as a direct-response copywriter who focuses on high-converting Meta ads. Your job: review the product or creative you're given, pinpoint its central marketing angle, and then produce three alternative high-intent primary texts plus three headlines.

Input fields: Brand or product name: [Insert brand or product name here]. Audience notes: [Insert any notes about your audience here, or leave blank so the AI infers them]. Creative attached: [Yes or No; refer to the file name if relevant].

Stage 1 - Creative and avatar review. Write a brief analysis in three sections:
1. Marketing angle: the hook (the instant visual, emotional, or vanity payoff) and how it naturally moves the reader toward the functional, long-term, or technical advantage of the product.
2. Avatar: the buyer's core pains and desires, and the precise reason this creative and angle will halt their scrolling.
3. Awareness level: the single main awareness stage this ad aims at (Unaware, Problem Aware, Solution Aware, Product Aware, or Most Aware).

Stage 2 - Primary text. Produce 3 Meta-compliant primary text variations for the brand or product, each following this exact layout:
- Paragraph 1: an arresting hook centered on the main benefit or pain point (12 words at most).
- Paragraph 2: a transition linking the hook to the product's distinctive mechanism, quality, or solution (12 words at most).
- Benefit stack: exactly 4 standalone lines. Every line opens with one fitting emoji, followed by a short, punchy benefit written as [Benefit name].
- Paragraph 3: a clear call to action or a closing value proposition that is hard to dispute (12 words at most).
Hard rules: no paragraph may go over 12 words, so count each paragraph's words before you output it. Keep emojis out of the paragraphs; they belong only at the start of the 4 benefit-stack lines. Do not make any claim the product page fails to back up.

Stage 3 - Headlines. Write 3 headlines: an offer headline aimed at problem-aware readers, a curiosity-gap headline for solution-aware readers, and an identity-shift headline for readers focused on transformation. Where it fits, use the current price, original price, and discount as displayed on the product page.

Deliver the Stage 1 analysis first, then the three primary text variations, then the three headlines.

Principles for AI-assisted creative

1 min
  • AI has changed ad creative more than any shift since social media took hold.
  • It lets a team produce ideas, variations and finished assets (ready-to-run ads) much faster.
  • It gives more variety and far less manual effort.
  • Using AI to generate ads without knowing how to direct it usually produces low-quality ads that perform worse.
  • The tool alone does not create good creative.
  • The operator's strategy decides the result.
  • Brands that keep winning with AI-assisted creative follow a small set of underlying principles.
  • These principles are not a single tool or prompt trick.
  • Understanding them lets an advertiser adapt when platforms change their rules.
  • Input quality decides the result of ChatGPT concept work.
  • A human must check the output.
  • Overreliance makes the work weaker.
  • So write the core copy yourself and check it against customer pain points (the problems customers feel).
  • Adjust copy and creative direction to match your brand voice (the way your brand sounds).
  • Also adjust it to the customer psychology insights from your research.

Editorials (70/30 split, AIDA)

2 min

An editorial is a long, article-style sales page. The 70/30 split means 70% of the text tells the story and 30% introduces the product and the action. AIDA stands for Attention, Interest, Desire, Action: the four steps a good sales text goes through.

  1. Copy the text of a proven editorial you want to copy the structure from.
  2. Paste it into ChatGPT and ask for a response with the same structure, adjusted for your own product.
  3. Describe your product in detail, including how it is different, its outcomes and its results.
  4. Check that the output keeps the 70/30 split and the AIDA order, then edit it for accuracy and claims.
You are a direct-response copywriter. Below is a proven editorial page that uses a 70/30 split and AIDA order. Write a new editorial for my product, [PRODUCT NAME], in the category [PRODUCT CATEGORY], for this ideal customer: [IDEAL CUSTOMER DESCRIPTION].

Keep the same structure:
1. Headline: benefit-driven, with a bold claim, a curiosity element or a news angle. Give 5 options.
2. Attention and interest: open with a character who fits the ideal customer and tells a story about the problem. Agitate the problem and explain why it happens.
3. Desire: explain why current solutions on the market fail to work well, then present my product as a new approach. Let an expert or a satisfied customer recommend it in the story, and do not name the brand early.
4. Social proof: include one customer result with a timeline (for example after one week and after two weeks), a data point or graph placeholder, and a count of other customers who had good results.
5. Action: in the final 30% of the text, introduce the product and end with the button text [CALL TO ACTION, e.g. check current availability] linking to [PRODUCT PAGE URL].

Use only the facts I provide here: [PRODUCT FACTS: key features, outcomes, measured results, price]. Do not invent statistics, reviews, expert names or customer quotes. Mark any missing information as [PLACEHOLDER]. Add a photo placeholder wherever a picture would help the story. End each paragraph with a hook that leads into the next one.

  • AI UGC video ads for books were produced with Arcads. A second AI video generator is also mentioned, most likely Kling 3.0. Confirm the second tool name before relying on it.

  • Write all numbers as words. Write names and foreign terms the way they sound. This helps the text-to-speech tool (software that reads text out loud) pronounce them correctly.
  • Choose the angle first, the main reason the ad gives for buying, from the research sheet (your notes about the market). Then have the AI draft the script around it.

Quality control and proofing

Checking generated copy and images

1 min
  • Check every important fact in the generated copy and brief before you publish. AI tools like ChatGPT can make mistakes.
  • Check that the offer (the deal the customer gets, such as a discount) is visible in the final design.
  • Rewrite template copy (ready-made text) for your brand instead of keeping the defaults, even if you plan to edit it again later.
  • Check every word of a generated infographic (a graphic with text and charts) before you publish it.
  • Always check the generated text for correct offers before you run it.
  • Check that every image shows a clearly different model (the person in the picture). Regenerate any image that repeats a person.
  • Check that the output looks realistic and does not misrepresent the product before you use it in ads or on the store.
  • Review and adapt every AI output for a specific product and region (country or market) before use.

Checking subtitles and video text

1 min
  • AI subtitles (the words shown on screen that match the speech) make errors on complex or less common words. The tool is built around basic vocabulary. Always compare the generated subtitles with the original script in a second window. Correct each mistake before export (saving the final video file).

Testing copy against results

1 min
  • Test every generated text against real conversion results (how many viewers actually buy) before you scale it, meaning spend more money on it.
  • Check that the output still matches the brand voice (the way the brand speaks) and the offer after you apply a saved prompt.
  • Run a quick test first. If the test works, send the concept to designers for more variations (versions).

Human control

1 min
  • AI can handle scale (large amounts of work) better than one human. But a human must stay in control and check that the AI learns from the right sources. Oversight is needed for nuance (subtle meaning) and final decisions.
  • Use one clean, well-lit product photo as the source. Blurry or low-quality uploads produce weak variants.

Case: AI presenter ads at scale

1 min

Simple Brand, supplement, US (2025)

1 min
  • Starting point: a small brand, Simple Brand, sold a dietary supplement (a product taken by mouth, such as a vitamin) in the US. Its product showed strong sales on Kalodata (a research tool that shows product sales data) in summer 2025.
  • What was done: the brand ran a large set of ads that were all AI-generated, with different AI presenters (an AI lady, an AI doctor, an AI man). It launched on July 9, 2025 and had about 71 active ads (ads still running) in Meta Ad Library (Meta's public list of all running ads) after roughly one month.
  • The main brand behind the product had about 650 active ads, all AI-generated with avatar variations (versions of a digital presenter).
  • Result: the ads kept running for about a month. This shows the spend (money put into ads) returned enough money to continue. The brand scaled the presenter versions that held up.

Note on conflicting rules: the case shows an AI doctor presenter performing well. The persona prompt (the instructions for a made-up character) says never to design a medical doctor or any persona that suggests medical qualifications. The 'Doc Ellie' title example in the same prompt conflicts with that rule. Follow the prompt rule for your own personas.

Note on conflicting advice on AI people: real users build trust faster early on. The case scaled AI presenters that held up in tests. Use real people for early trust and AI presenter variants for testing and scaling.

Limits of AI output

1 min
  • AI tools like ChatGPT can make mistakes. Check every important fact in the generated copy and brief before publishing.
  • AI tools work as a virtual assistant (a helper that does tasks) rather than as a lead (the main guide). Their output is only as good as the input. They miss contextual details (background clues) that a human finds in forums and reviews.
  • Use an AI tool to draft ad copy from audience research (what you learned about your buyers). But feed it the right inputs. The quality of the output depends on the quality of the information given.
  • The AI generates text and images from the prompt (the instruction you type). Unprompted visual or layout suggestions were weak, so tie image prompts to a specific offer.
  • Image generators return the same person when prompts describe models identically. Describe each model differently.
  • AI-generated infographics can contain small text errors.
  • Generated subtitles make errors on complex or less common words.
  • ElevenLabs (a text-to-speech voice tool) reads digits correctly only in English.
  • Realistic-looking AI people build less trust than real users early on.
  • Decisions that need a trained opinion must be defined in the training, not left to the AI.
  • Generated product visuals must be checked for realistic product representation before use.
  • A generated text still needs to be tested against real conversion results before it is scaled.

Testing presenter avatars with AI-generated ads

1 min
  • AI-generated ads let you test many presenter types (avatars, which are digital presenters) for the same product at low cost.
  • Work in this order:
    1. Produce several presenter versions of the same product ad.
    2. Run them and find the one that keeps running.
    3. Scale that version.

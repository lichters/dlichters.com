Playbook › 12. Creatives and ad copy

Replicating and remixing proven competitor ads

Section 12.5 · 5 of 26 in this chapter · 27 min read

In short

  • Copy the structure of a winning ad, never its exact words, look or footage.
  • Change at least one element before you use a competitor's idea.
  • Test every idea yourself. Keep it only if its click and result numbers reach your goals.
  • Save 20 top videos per product on Kalodata, or 3 to 5 top ads on TikTok Creative Center or Facebook Ad Library.
  • Copy the structure of a proven ad, then change the words, reviews and brand details.
More key points
  • Change one key element of a copied ad, such as the hook, before use. Exception: the first launch of a ripped ad.
  • Change the metadata (hidden file details) of every copied ad, so platforms do not see duplicates.
  • Test with a small budget first, then check purchases and ROAS before you spend more.

Core rule: copy the structure, never the words

3 min
  • Do not copy competitor ad creatives straight from the Meta Ad Library (Meta's public list of running ads) without changes. Meta (the company that runs Facebook and Instagram) gives preference to the competitor's established account history, so direct copies do not get equal reach.
  • When you use a competitor creative (their ad video or image) as a reference, edit at least one element. Choose the hook (the first seconds that catch attention), the body copy (the main written text), or something else that makes your version different.
  • Imitation means learning from structure and form, not copying word for word. The line between plagiarism (copying someone else's work) and genius is fine. Imitation is a logical learning step before innovation (new ideas). Great poets imitate and improve; small poets steal and spoil.
  • Competitor ads are the best starting point for your own creative (the video or image you run). If they cannot be used, build your own ad from competitor content and improve it rather than copying it.
  • A competitor ad works best as a structure to follow, not a look to copy. Reuse the arrangement of headline, product and offer. Take colours, fonts (letter styles) and brand identity (the look and feel of a brand) from your own brand. This keeps the test about the structure and avoids using another brand's identity.
  • Look for patterns across several videos, such as a hook or ad copy used again and again. Replicate the pattern with your own footage (raw video you shot or recorded). Do not copy a brand's custom content. For drop-shipping style videos (ads for products you sell without holding stock, called dropshipping), produce a better version or rearrange existing clips.
  • Use a competitor's idea as a starting point only if you test it yourself. Keep it only if click numbers (CTR, click-through rate: the share of viewers who click the ad) and result numbers reach your targets. Otherwise change the creative or the offer.
  • Copying other dropshippers' creatives is acceptable at the very beginning. In the long run, your own creatives perform better. Order the product and film your own material. This usually beats reusing other ads.
  • Use a proven line from your Structure Library (a saved collection) and its Proven Line-Patterns (lines that already worked) as an example of a psychological move (a way of making people feel or act). Do not use it as a phrase to repeat. Default to reproducing the move with fresh words. Use a proven line word for word only if it is genuinely the strongest option, never to avoid the work of writing a fresh version.
  • Competitor creatives can serve as inspiration. Take their images or GIFs (short looping animated pictures), edit them, and improve headlines (the big title text) and angles (the main idea of the ad) with editing tools. Do not use them unchanged. The aim is a clearly better version than the competitor's ad or landing page (the page people reach after clicking).
  • Recreate a viral competitor video (one that spread widely) by improving its hook or other weak parts, not by copying it.
  • Repurpose existing competitor footage by cutting out their text and logos, changing the brand colours and adding a new narrative (a new story). The goal is a different story built on the same footage, not a copy of the original ad.
  • Exact copy-paste is risky. The platform can detect it. The competitor may get the original ad taken down, which takes your ad down too. Always alter the metadata (hidden details such as file name and tags), the logo and the details of any replicated creative.
  • Copying a competitor's ads and store exactly can produce high CPM (cost per 1,000 views) and poor CTR (click-through rate). Change elements such as music, speed, length and hook. This stops Meta from treating them as duplicates (copies) of another advertiser's content.
  • Instead of reusing a brand's ad video, copy its hook or structure. Find your own clips on AliExpress, Alibaba or YouTube (online shopping and video sites). This keeps the selling pattern, avoids the brand's content and reduces copyright risk (the chance of legal trouble over someone else's work).

Sourcing proven creatives

2 min

Where to look and what to save

  • Ripped creatives are videos that already perform for other advertisers. Find them on Kalodata (a tool that tracks ads and products), the TikTok Creative Center (TikTok's free ad library) or the Facebook Ad Library (Meta's public list of running ads). Their audience psychology is already proven, which makes them a safe first test.
  • Ad libraries show what creative types work, including posts that go viral. Identify which trends, post types and ad types work. Then recreate the winning approach in your own style without copying it.
  • Pool-building (collecting a large set of options): Kalodata lists more than 4,000 video and ad creatives for one product. Use that pool to pull hooks and testimonials (customer stories on video) and build many variations, not one or two new ads.
  • Content from other products in the same category can serve your product. Eyeliner ads, for example, can supply hooks and testimonials for another eyeliner. This widens your pool well beyond one product's ads.
  • Do not run the same top-performing competitor ads that many advertisers already show on Facebook. The audience has probably seen them. Bring content the market has not seen yet, even when the product is the same.
  • Choose only videos that feature real people. Abstract videos and product-only videos are excluded.

Filtering and quantities

  • For every saved video, also save its caption (the written text under the post), text overlays (words shown on screen), hook and call to action (CTA: the line or button that asks the viewer to act). These are the basis for later comparison.
  • Download only clean versions without a watermark (a logo or name placed on the video). A watermark shows another account's branding inside your ad.

Selection checks

  • Judge each candidate on two checks. The opening hook must grab attention. The video must show a problem your product actually solves. A video can pass the hook check and still fail the second one, so both checks are needed.
  • Skip a video if the outcome it shows is not the result your product delivers, even when the hook performs well.
  • Filter for high CTR and proven conversions before you test a video in your own account. Recycling earlier ads with the same structure also saves production time (the time spent making an ad).

Analysing a winning ad

2 min

Framework for a working ad

  1. Read the hook and check whether it uses the customer's own language.
  2. Identify the awareness stage (how much the buyer already knows about the problem or product) that the ad targets.
  3. Identify the unique mechanism (the reason the product works differently). It explains what makes the product different.
  4. Note where the ad sends traffic (visitors): the product page, an advertorial (an ad styled like an article), or an inverted product page (a page that puts the story before the product). Also note what offer is shown.
  5. Check how long the ad has run and how many duplicates (copies of the same ad) use it.
  6. Rebuild the same structure around your own product. Change the words rather than copying the text.

Breaking down a video

  • Break each successful competitor creative down every five seconds to identify the hook, the angle (main idea) and the copywriting (the written sales text). Then write similar copy using the same angle.
  • Study the competitor's ad for the same product. Save the videos, review what worked, and copy the structure, text-to-speech (a computer voice that reads text aloud) and sounds in your own way. This is often more effective than writing a script (the written plan of spoken words) from scratch.
  • Copy the style of the largest pages or videos for your product. Then change it based on what viewers say in the comments, and add something dramatic or unusual. Changes that work include new trending sounds, different backdrops (background scenes), switching between a male and a female presenter, trending video effects, voice-overs (spoken narration added over the video) and challenge-style framing (a format where the person takes on a challenge).

Tool for ad analysis

  • Use Facebook Ad Library or Kalodata to find bottom-of-funnel (ads aimed at people close to buying) image ads from any competitor in any niche (a small market segment). Screenshot the ad (take a picture of the screen), upload it to Claude, and ask what makes it work and why it is effective. Then have Claude create a prompt (an instruction for an AI) so you can produce similar ads for your niche. This reverse-engineers winning structures (takes them apart to see how they work) without copying them.

Prompts for analysis and recreation

2 min

Analysis prompt for a proven rival video

(Send it with the video file and the exported brand data. Procedure in.)

Here is a proven rival video, sent along with its video file and the brand data I exported. Give me a complete breakdown of why it wins. Go through it frame by frame, and for each moment describe what appears on screen, what is spoken, and what effect that has on the viewer. Give special weight to the hook in the opening seconds and to the individual words and phrases doing the heaviest persuasive work, explaining per frame why each one lands. Using the supplied brand data, identify the persona, the core desire, the emotions in play, the awareness level, the concept, the angle, the USPs, and the offer behind the video. End with a brief list of what to keep and what to modify if this same structure is applied to a different product.

Recreation prompt for your own product

(Send it with the video file, the brand data and your product files.)

Here is the winning video from my competitor and its exported brand data. Recreate this ad script exactly as it is, keeping the same structure, the same order of scenes, the same timing, the same hook, the same emotional beats and the same length. Replace only the product, its facts, prices, ingredients and claims with those of my product, using only the facts in the attached product files and inventing no new claims. Output the full script for my product, scene by scene.

Rewrite prompt for ad copy and product page text

I sell [PRODUCT] in the [NICHE] niche in [MARKET]. Below is the ad copy and product page text of a brand whose ads have been running for a long time: [PASTE AD COPY AND PAGE TEXT]. Rewrite the whole text for my product and keep the same structure, hooks, sections and emotional angle. Write it for this target avatar: [TARGET AVATAR: age, gender, main problem, what they want]. Do not copy any sentence word for word. Avoid medical or guaranteed-result claims and keep all claims within what is allowed for [MARKET]. Return the output in this order: 3 hook variations for ads, 1 primary ad text, 1 headline, then the product page sections in order: hero headline and benefits, testimonials, FAQ.

Replicate a proven competitor video (Method 1)

  1. In the Brands tracker (a tool page for following brands), open the brand's top ad from the Ad Copies, Personas or Overview lists. Personas are made-up pictures of a typical buyer.
  2. Click View ad, then download the video.
  3. Generate the transcript (written copy of the spoken words) with Transcribe ad with AI, then copy the full script.
  4. Go back to the brand page. Use Export CSV (save as a spreadsheet file) on the tabs to export the brand data: ad copies, hooks, personas, angles, desires, emotions, awareness stages and USPs (unique selling points: the main reasons to buy).
  5. Open Gemini (or Claude, both AI chats), log in, click the plus button and attach the video file.
  6. Attach the exported brand data CSV (the spreadsheet file).
  7. Paste the analysis prompt above and send it.
  8. Attach all files you have for your own product and send the recreation prompt above.

Ripping and editing a winning video

4 min

Ripped-creative test (unchanged launch)

  1. Find top-performing videos on Kalodata, the TikTok Creative Center or the Facebook Ad Library. Filter by high CTR, high spend or proven conversions.
  2. Save at least 20 top videos per product if you use Kalodata, or 3 to 5 top ads per product if you use the TikTok Creative Center or the Facebook Ad Library. Prefer high engagement plus comments.
  3. Save the captions, text overlays, hooks and CTAs of each video for reference.
  4. Download the clean videos without watermark using Snaptik, ssstik or Downloader Bot (free tools that save videos).
  5. Upload the videos directly to Meta Ads Manager (the tool for running ads on Facebook and Instagram) without any editing. TikTok Ads (TikTok's ad tool) is also possible.
  6. Do not change the hook, caption or length. Test the product and the offer.
  7. Once you get consistent profitable sales with ripped creatives, begin hook testing (trying several hooks to see which works best) to raise CTR.
  • The purpose of the first launch is to test exactly what already works. So do not change the hook, the caption or the video length.
  • Testing ripped videos unchanged isolates whether the product and offer work, because the videos have already been proven with real viewers.
  • Changing the hook, caption or length of a ripped video before the first launch removes the advantage of testing a proven ad. The result no longer shows whether the original works.
  • Case: a seller (an operator) sold a product using only ripped creatives, with no original content. The proven videos were downloaded without watermark and uploaded unchanged to the ad account. The reported revenue was more than 16,000 USD in a single day from ripped ads alone. This shows that ripped creatives can scale.

Remove identifying mentions

  • Remove every spoken or on-screen mention of TikTok Shop from a copied video. Also remove every mention of Orange Cart (a shop app name) before you use it in your ads.
  • For proven videos, remove only the problematic parts. Adding new hooks or heavy edits to a video that already sells can break what works.

Ripping and editing a competitor's winning video (method for the Ad Library and TikTok)

  • Save the competitor's video from the Ad Library. Open the ad in the Facebook Ad Library, right-click the video and save it to your computer. If the file does not play, change the format to MP4 (a common video file type).
  • Download the competitor's current videos. Upload them to your Business Manager (Meta's account hub for ads and pages), and test how they perform for your product.
  • Once you see results, produce your own edited version of the winning creative so it is not a copy of the competitor.
  • Change the logo and the call to action at the end, or combine several videos, so the result is not identical to the original.

Editing a winning video: step by step

  1. Find competitor ads and download the ones performing well.
  2. Keep the first 1 to 5 seconds (the hook) unchanged.
  3. Replace the rest of the clips. For example, swap a 10 to 15 second clip with a 20 to 25 second clip.
  4. Change the slides and add your logo and product name.
  5. Use the result as a new creative.

Adapting a competitor's TikTok ad

  • To adapt a competitor's winning TikTok ad, change the caption colour, add your own logo, replace the caption text and adjust the speed. You can also swap or reorder clips in the middle, but keep the opening hook unchanged.

Replicating a competitor ad without copying it exactly

  1. Keep the same hook and the same overall structure, because the hook is what made the ad work.
  2. Change the logo, the metadata and some details of the creative, so the ad is not an exact copy.
  3. If the competitor logo is blurred in the centre of the video, do not reuse that video.
  4. If the competitor logo sits to the left or right, replace it with your own logo, or cover it with a coloured bar and white text.
  5. After about 7 to 10 seconds, replace or reorder the clips, because the early hook matters most.
  6. Keep any clip that shows the benefit and proof the competitor uses, since it may be what converts.
  7. Add your own logo at the top of the frame.
  • A clip that many advertisers run with the same hook usually comes from a common supplier source, such as AliExpress, YouTube or Alibaba. Reusing that common clip is acceptable if you keep the same hook and change the logo, captions and later clips.

Adapting a stock-footage ad to a new niche

Stock footage means ready-made video clips you can download.

  1. Find a matching stock clip on Pexels (a free stock video site). For example, a person riding a bike in winter.
  2. Download the clip and keep only the first one to two seconds as the hook.
  3. Add the niche-specific voice-over or caption.
  4. Edit the on-screen text with CapCut or Camtasia (video editing apps) to match the niche. For example, "the best thermal gloves for bike riders in winter" instead of "the best thermal gloves for winter".
  5. Show the product after the hook.

Repurposing footage from the start of a test

  • Reusing an existing video for testing saves time and budget (money). This lets you test several products at once. Once a product shows good performance, invest in custom content (videos made just for you).
  • Video creative alone is only a starting point. Keep the first 3 to 5 seconds (the hook) unchanged, because it usually carries the performance. Alter the rest by swapping or reordering clips, changing caption colour or placing a white layer behind existing captions, speeding up clips, and adding your own logo. If you cover the original captions, use the same caption text on top. Replace the hook with your own only when you build custom content later.

Hook and structure remixes

4 min

Changing only the hook

  1. Take a winning video ad.
  2. Change only the hook.
  3. Keep the rest of the video the same.
  4. Launch the variant (the changed version) in a new campaign (a group of ads with one goal) with your best interests to test it.

Mashups of existing material

  1. Collect several ads that already run for similar products. For example, a surprised-reaction clip and product b-roll (extra shots of the product in use).
  2. Cut the clips together into one mashup (several clips joined into one video) that fits the season's vibe, such as fall colours and sounds.
  3. Run the versions against each other and keep the one with the best conversion rate (the share of viewers who buy).
  4. Rotate new mashups when a version stops performing.

Hook from one ad, testimonial from another

  • Case and method: a NAD+ (a health supplement) berry drink mix was running top ads on Meta and TikTok. The leading ad was shown with a figure of 27k.
  • Several competitors the speaker knew personally were running the same ads. Running them again would reach a saturated audience (people who have already seen the ads too often).
  • The seller searched Kalodata. The seller found the same NAD+ product in drops form (liquid drops) with its own set of video ads. The seller saved a hook from one of those ads, which showed a benefit for looking younger.
  • In CapCut (a video editing app), the seller cut the hook off after it ended. The seller combined it with a testimonial from another ad, in which an older parent looked young because of the product, and produced a new creative.
  • The seller reported that this method produced the first winning ad from the first winning product. Kalodata listed over 4,000 creatives per product to repeat the process.
  1. In Kalodata, set the region (country) to United States and the period to Last 30 Days. Choose a product that is already advertised.
  2. Do not use the top-performing ad itself. Look for the same product in another form (for example, drink mix versus drops) that has its own video ads.
  3. Open that product's Video & Ad list (Kalodata's list of videos for a product). Watch the videos until you find a strong hook, meaning the opening seconds that stop the scroll (make people pause as they scroll past).
  4. Save the video by downloading it without watermark, keeping the plan download limit (how many downloads your plan allows) in mind.
  5. Import the clip into CapCut using Stay in original location (a CapCut import option that keeps the file where it is).
  6. Cut the clip right after the hook ends. Do not keep the full video.
  7. Find a testimonial clip, where a person describes a result, from another ad for the same product or from the same category.
  8. Combine the hook and the testimonial into one new creative.
  9. Repeat with other hooks and testimonials from the list, which holds thousands of creatives per product. Test the new versions.
  • Cut each hook clip at the point where the hook ends, so the saved file contains only the hook. Playing the full ad wastes the test, because the hook is the only part you take from that ad.
  • Pairing a hook from one ad with a testimonial from another creates a new creative. It is new to the audience even when the product stays the same. The combination gives a fresh test without new filming.

Keep the hook close to the competitor's

  • Replicate the hook of a winning competitor video closely. The hook usually drives most of the engagement (likes, comments and shares) for both the competitor and the copy.
  • Keep the creative elements the same, especially the video creative and hook. Each change is an extra variable (a thing that changes the result), which makes it unclear what is working. Avoid changing the music, caption or hook without a test.
  • Changing the music in a copied winning ad flopped, while the original music performed. An extra variable can break a creative that works for a competitor.
  • When copying a winning ad, change the hook, the first image or the first seconds so the ad is unique. The more different the creative, the better. An identical copy is the lazy option.
  • Test a winning video approach by starting with existing high-performing videos from the market. Edit the first three seconds to find the best CTR. Only then produce unique videos or go to creators or influencers (people with large social audiences who promote products).
  • A winning video follows a clear structure: a strong hook, grabbing attention, a benefit-driven storyline (a story built around what the buyer gains) and a call to action. Copy a competitor video that already works, and add two to three original creatives to build the test.

Patterning an admired ad

  • Choose a high-performing ad for a similar product and copy its structure, such as headline length, number of captions and sequence of blocks. Do not copy its layout. Keep the format only as a guide and write new words for the new product. Do not copy the layout or wording so closely that the ad could be mistaken for the original.

Keep the benefit and proof

  • Keep any clip that shows the benefit and proof the competitor uses, since it may be what converts.

Remapping layouts and patterns

1 min
  • Cut out the text and logos from competitor footage (video clips), change the brand colours, and add a new narrative (the story the video tells).
  • Recycle a proven ad's structure (the order of its parts). Keep it, then swap in new headlines (big title lines), copy (the written words) and reviews. **
  • Recreate the winning approach in your own style. Use the same kinds of posts and ads that already work on the platform (the app or site where ads run).
  • Ads should look local to the target country (the country you sell to). Show locally known stores, brands or products. This builds trust. It stops the ad from looking like a random internet ad.
  • Competitor-footage tests: keep one clip that shows the benefit (what the product does for the buyer) and the proof (evidence, such as results). Change the layout (how things are arranged on screen) and the captions (on-screen text) around it.

Language arbitrage and market adaptation

3 min

Converting a winning ad to a new language

  • Language arbitrage means moving a proven ad into a new language. Turn a winning ad into English. Replace the voice-over (spoken narration) with ElevenLabs (an AI voice tool). Change the book cover with GPT image editing (an AI tool that edits pictures).
  • Transcribe a winning ad with Google AI Studio (Google's AI tool, running Gemini, its AI model). It turns the ad into text, including its primary text (main text above the ad) and headline. Claude (an AI assistant) then rebuilds the messaging (the words you use to sell).
  • When you expand to new markets (countries) with existing winning ads, apply the same competitor research process to your successful creatives (ads and their videos or images). Then reproduce them in the language and cultural context (local habits and customs) of the new market.
  • When you enter a new market, translate and improve a competitor's proven ad copy and landing page (the page buyers reach after clicking an ad). Do not copy it unchanged. Ad copy tests (tests of the written ad text) are a key way to find better results for the same product.

Adapting a competitor's foreign-language creatives

  1. Save each image, one after another, from the competitor's store or ads.
  2. Translate the text in each image with a translation tool, such as Google Translate.
  3. Recreate the image in Canva (an online design app) with the translated text. Or crop out (cut away) the text if the image does not need to show the product's function.

Testing a Spanish book in English

  1. Choose a Spanish book that shows demand in Kalodata (a research tool for products that sell on TikTok) and has a clear problem it solves.
  2. Remove the Spanish text from the captions and product page details (the store page for the product).
  3. Translate the content into English. Produce an English voice-over with ElevenLabs.
  4. Replace the product page content with the English version.
  5. Test the English version with English ads.
  • Converting a Spanish book to English is possible but not guaranteed. It needs a higher skill level to translate fine details well. Avoid it as a beginner. Consider it once the basics and scaling (spending more on what works) are in place.

Improvement cycle for a winning creative

  1. In Ads Manager (the tool to run and track ads on Meta, which owns Facebook and Instagram), identify the creative with the most website purchases (sales made on your site after an ad click).
  2. Convert the winner to text in Google AI Studio (Gemini), capturing both the primary text and the headline.
  3. Rebuild the messaging with Claude. Define the angle (the main point you push), the promise (what the buyer gets) and the objection (the doubt the buyer has) it addresses.
  4. Adapt the winning message into new formats: podcast, conference, seminar, claymation (clay animation), cartoon and UGC AI ads. UGC means user-generated content, videos that look made by real customers or creators.
  5. Run a 3-day launch test in a testing CBO (campaign budget optimization, where the platform splits one budget across your ads). Evaluate website purchases and ROAS (return on ad spend, the money earned for each unit spent on ads).
  • Take the best-performing creative (the one with the best results). Pass it through the transcribe-and-rebuild cycle. Release two new formats that carry the same message.

What to change and what to keep

Testing copies

1 min

Test design

  • Test the new version with a controlled budget (a fixed, small amount of spend) before scaling.
  • Testing a winning competitor ad:
    1. Pick the competitor ad that is scaling (its spend and sales are growing).
    2. Copy the hook exactly.
    3. Edit the later part of the video (clips or pacing, the speed of the cuts).
    4. Change the metadata (for example video speed or clip layout) with a metadata-editing tool (a tool that changes hidden file details), so the ad is not identical.
    5. Test the new version with a controlled budget before scaling.
  • Keep a competitor's idea only if the click and result numbers reach your targets. Otherwise change the creative or the offer (the deal, price or bonus).
  • Testing competitor creatives for a new product:
    1. Download the videos competitors run.
    2. Upload them to your Business Manager (Meta's tool for managing ad accounts and assets).
    3. Test their performance for your product.
    4. Produce your own edited version once you see results.
  • Rotate new mashups (versions that blend parts of other ads) when a version stops performing.
  • Run a 3-day launch test in a testing CBO and evaluate website purchases and ROAS.
  • Ripped creatives: start with unchanged videos for the product and offer test. Then begin hook testing to raise CTR once profitable sales (sales that make money) are steady.

Test order

  1. Start with existing high-performing videos from the market. Edit the first three seconds to find the best CTR.
  2. Only then produce unique videos or work with creators or influencers.
  3. Reuse existing videos to test several products at once. Invest in custom content for products that perform.

Cases

1 min
  • A German acupuncture pen store launched by copying a US competitor's product page and ad feed (the list of ads a brand runs) in full. With a EUR 50 test, the first day brought five sales. The product scaled to about EUR 5,000 to 6,000 per day at roughly 30 percent margin (the share of each sale kept as profit). Sales dropped later as the creatives wore out (stopped working). New creatives were sourced from a freelancer (a hired person paid per job) to replace them.
  • A ripped-creative operator sold a product with no original content. The operator reported more than 16,000 USD in revenue (total sales money) in one day from ripped ads alone.
  • The NAD+ hook-and-testimonial remix (a re-edit combining a hook with a testimonial, a customer review video) described above produced the first winning ad from the first winning product.
  • A ripped copy sent to a drop-shipping style test: a copied competitor page and ad feed, with the EUR 50 test above, followed the same path as the acupuncture case.

More rules worth knowing

1 min
  • Wordtune (an AI writing tool) can rewrite copied competitor landing-page text into your own wording while keeping the same benefits. Use it after you have collected the competitor copy and checked which benefits it states.

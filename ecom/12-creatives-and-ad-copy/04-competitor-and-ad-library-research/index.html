Playbook › 12. Creatives and ad copy

Competitor and ad-library research

Section 12.4 · 4 of 26 in this chapter · 27 min read

In short

  • Ads that have run for months are usually making money. Study them, but never copy them word for word.
  • Check three things before calling an ad a winner: how long it runs, how many copies of it exist, and whether it gets traffic.
  • Big brands spend a lot on testing, so their ads are a good place to start looking.
  • Pick test videos by sales numbers first, not by how nice they look.
  • Learn why an ad works for its buyers. Do not clone it.
More key points
  • Study the 20 to 25 best ads first. Find the hooks (opening lines or seconds that stop people scrolling) and visuals that work most often.
  • Read at least 100 comments on viral posts. They show what buyers ask, fear and love.
  • Study your competitors (rival sellers) before you write anything. Copy the pattern, never the exact content.
  • Save good ads in a swipe file (your own collection of examples). Review it regularly.

Finding winners: run time, spend and scale signals

3 min

Run time as a shortcut

  • Run time (how many days an ad stays live) is a shortcut for spotting winners.
  • Ads that have run a long time are usually profitable.
  • Model the headline, image, video and copy of ads that have run for months.
  • Any ad running for months has been validated as a winner in the free Meta Ad Library.
  • You can swipe it (save it and study it) for analysis.
  • In the Meta Ads Library, the longest-running ads usually have the best return on ad spend (money earned for each dollar spent on ads).
  • Scroll to the bottom of the results to find them.
  • Use them for ideas on structure and copy. Do not copy them.
  • Caution: do not assume the longest-running ad is the winner.
  • Some advertisers keep ads live with no sales, to mislead competitors.
  • Check running days together with duplicates and traffic before copying an ad.
  • Running time and duplicates together give a stronger signal than either alone.
  • Where an ad appears as many separate variations, treat it as proven.
  • Look for a creative (an ad) that runs in many ads at once.
  • 8 or more ads is the scaling signal. Scaling means spending more on what already works.
  • Recent-launch check: the Brands tracker (a tool that follows one brand's ads) counts new ads per week.
  • 3 to 5 new ads per week signals active scaling.

Spend and brand size

  • Prioritize big brands that spend heavily on Facebook. For example, around 1 to 5 million USD per month.
  • Big brands test creative (ads) constantly.
  • Study what large brands run.
  • Copy their layout, fonts, colors and ad formats (the kind of ad, such as video or image).
  • They have spent heavily on testing, so their proven choices are a useful starting point.
  • Check the Facebook Ad Library for the ads large brands in your niche keep running.
  • Those brands test heavily and keep what works.
  • Use the winning styles as a starting point. Then test them for your own product and niche.
  • Research direct competitors (sellers of the same product).
  • Above all, research large brands in the niche.
  • Large brands spend heavily on testing creative ideas and marketing angles (the main reason or approach an ad uses).
  • Save their ads and landing pages in one sheet for reference.

Recency and engagement

  • Find competitor creatives from the last two weeks. Search 5 to 10 product keywords and related hashtags on TikTok.
  • Recent videos are less likely to have been shown to your audience.
  • A good creative to test is one uploaded recently.
  • It should not yet be used by many competitors.
  • It should already get a good like rate (likes compared to views) on PP Ads (an ad search tool).
  • A recent competitor video with a strong like rate shows real engagement (people actually reacting).
  • On TikTok, compare a creative's likes with its ad impressions (how many times the ad was shown).
  • Strong impressions and a good like count mean the creative resonates (connects with people).
  • It is worth replicating (building a similar version of it).
  • Kalodata's Video & Ad list shows each ad with revenue, view count and post date.
  • Use these figures to pick hooks from ads that already sold, not ads picked at random.
  • The list has an Export button (it downloads the list as a file).

Stopped ads

  • If a competitor stopped a creative for a product, the cause may be account issues, not poor performance.
  • You can still test the product.
  • Check results after a few days.
  • Change the creative, offer or market if sales do not come.

Searching the Meta Ad Library step by step

3 min

Competitor research in the Ad Library

  1. Open the competitor's Facebook page.
  2. In the right column, click Page transparency (a box showing the page's info).
  3. Click See more to view the page creation date, name changes and the number of people managing the page.
  4. Click the Ad Library link, or the button to see all ads, to view active and past ads.
  5. Check the start date, format and copy (written text) of each ad. Switch the country to see regional ads.
  6. Revisit the page regularly to see which ads they keep running.

Finding and replicating winning creatives

  1. Open the Meta Ad Library. Search your niche and the competitors and large brands in it.
  2. Prioritize big brands that spend heavily on Facebook (see spend guidance above).
  3. Look for creatives that run in many ads at once. 8 or more ads is the scaling signal.
  4. Compare the creatives of the same brand.
  5. Note the hook (the first seconds that stop people scrolling) that repeats, while the later clips change.
  6. Replicate the repeated hook in your own ads and test it against your own video.

Using the Ad Library for wording and downloads

  • Search the Facebook Ad Library for the niche, such as home decor.
  • This finds wording ideas for the primary text (main body) and headlines (titles) of ads.
  • Search the product name in the Meta Ad Library.
  • Select All ads, turn on the Active status filter, and set the country to United States.
  • Check whether the same video runs many times as separate ad variations (versions).
  • Many active variations signal a proven ad.
  • To download a video from the Ad Library: play the ad.
  • Right-click it and choose Save video as.
  • Type the file name with the extension.mp4 (the ending that tells the computer it is a video).
  • If the file is saved with another extension, download it again. Then rename the extension to.mp4 so it opens as a video.
  • The Meta Ad Library lets you download a competitor's ad creative for review.
  • Save the files and study the hook, pacing (speed of cuts) and format. Do this before building your own test version.
  • Do not copy the footage itself.
  • Download the video creatives of shortlisted products (your short list of picks) from the Meta Ads Library.
  • Save them in the shared research folder.
  • Use them as a reference when briefing (giving instructions to) your own test ads.
  • This way the team works from the same examples.

AdvertSuite ad research (5 steps)

  1. Define a S.M.A.R.T. objective (a clear, measurable goal: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound). Say which ad types you want to find and why.
  2. Select the 'Keywords' search mode. Choose 'Ad Text', 'Comments', or 'Landing Page Text' as the search field. Filter by country, date range, ad position, demographics (age, gender and similar groups) and technology if needed. Sort by 'Longest Running' or by engagement (likes, comments, shares).
  3. Start with broad keywords related to your industry. Then add filters.
  4. Examine the results to see what is working now.
  5. Capture each ad in a Trello board (an online notes board).
    • Screenshot the full ad from source.
    • Copy the text to a Google Doc and link it.
    • Add a link to the landing page.
    • Screenshot the landing page. Use the FireShot Chrome extension for long pages.
    • Screenshot the AdvertSuite details page.
    • Capture relevant comments.
  6. Analyze each ad by asking 30 questions. Examples:
    • Who the advertiser targets.
    • What the short-term goal is.
    • Which features and benefits are shared.
    • What the core attention grabber is.
    • What mood is conveyed.
    • Which objections (buyer doubts) are addressed.
    • Which calls to action (CTA: what you want the viewer to do) are used.

Trello as the research database

  • Create columns (lists) by keyword or category.
  • Add cards (single notes) with ad details.
  • Give each card labels (platform, ad type), a description (AdvertSuite search settings) and comments.
  • AdvertSuite lets you filter by country, date range and ad position.
  • Ad position means newsfeed (the main scrolling feed) vs. sidebar (the side column).
  • You can also filter by gender, language, age and technology used.
  • FireShot is a Chrome extension (a small add-on for Google Chrome).
  • It takes full-page screenshots of pages longer than the screen.
  • Use it to capture complete landing pages for analysis.

Tracking competitors in the brand tracker

4 min

What the tracker shows

  • A tracked brand's detail page has these tabs: Overview, Ad Copies, Ad Headlines, Ad Hooks, Landing Pages, Personas, Concepts, Angles, Desires, Emotions, Awareness Stages and USPs.
  • Each tab breaks down that element across the brand's ads.
  • Check which hooks, angles or desires a competitor uses most.
  • Together these tabs show how a competitor is positioning (placing itself) and selling right now, based on its live ads.
  • A competitor's top personas (types of buyers), concepts (ad formats), angles, USPs (unique selling points: why buy from them), emotions and desires come from its live ads.
  • They show what it is actually running.
  • Copying or varying the strongest of these is faster than guessing the strategy.
  • The tracker's concept categories are: Demo / How-It Works, Before-And-After, Educational Explainer, Social Proof / Reviews, Promotion / Discount, Problem-Agitate-Solve, Bundle / Multi-Pack Offer and Social Proof Emphasis.
  • Problem-Agitate-Solve means: name a problem, make it hurt, then show your fix.
  • Check which of these a competitor scales most.
  • The tracker's angle categories in the example included Problem-Agitate-Solve, Customer Before-And-After Reveal, At-Home Microneedling Substitute (a home replacement for microneedling, which uses tiny needles on skin), Budget Comparison, Comparison Vs Microneedling, Side-By-Side Comparison and After Demonstration.
  • Each angle is tracked with the number of ads that use it.
  • In the awareness-stage view, the sort control (the sort menu) offers Usage over 7 days or 30 days and Longest Running.
  • Hooks and landing pages that appear across many ads are strong candidates to study first.
  • So are hooks and landing pages that stay live for months. Study these first.

Procedure: analyse a competitor brand

  1. Open the brand. Read the overview cards: top landing pages, personas, concepts, angles, desires, emotions, awareness stages and USPs.
  2. Count the new ads uploaded each week. 3 to 5 new ads per week signals active scaling.
  3. Check ad attribution by format (the split of ads by type, such as video or image). If almost all ads are video, the brand is scaling video.
  4. Open the Ad Copies tab. Find the copy with the most attributed ads (ads linked to it). That is the main winning copy.
  5. Then check the headlines and hooks.
  6. Open the Landing Pages tab. Note the number of pages and the share of ads sent to each.
  7. Open Personas and Concepts to see which audiences and formats the brand uses.
  8. Open Angles, Desires, Emotions, Awareness Stages and USPs.
  9. Open the top ads. Read the ad details: running days, duplicates, landing page and price.
  10. Generate the transcript (the written text of the spoken words).
  11. Download the top video for frame-by-frame analysis (checking the video one picture at a time).

Reading a top ad's transcript

  • In a tracked brand's overview, open a video ad from the list. This gives you its transcript.
  • Read the spoken hook and script word for word before rebuilding a similar ad.
  • Video ads in a brand's list can be opened for transcripts, speakable scripts (lines written to be read aloud), and framework or pattern analysis.
  • Use these to see how a competitor's ad is structured before writing your own.

Export hooks and generate variations

  1. Open the brand and go to the Ad Hooks tab.
  2. Click Export to download the hooks as a CSV file (a spreadsheet file).
  3. Import the CSV into an AI tool.
  4. Ask the AI for more hooks similar to the exported ones.
  • Pro tip: export the top hooks, personas and angles from a brand.
  • Feed them into an AI tool such as Claude.
  • The AI can surface further personas and angles beyond those the tool lists.

Worked case: acne-scar serum brand

  • Case: an acne-scar serum brand (a face product for marks left by acne) was on the tracker with 133 active ads.
  • It was tracked since 6 May 2026.
  • It was added 22 hours before the data was pulled, so the counts were still filling in.
  • It uploaded 3 to 5 new ads per week. Almost all were attributed to video.
  • It had no image or carousel ads (a carousel is a set of swipeable images).
  • 133 ads ran the same main ad copy. Its headline reached 23,000+ people.
  • The top persona was people with acne scars.
  • Next came budget-conscious skincare shoppers and adults with acne scars.
  • The top desire was fading acne scars.
  • The top emotions were hope and relief.
  • The top USPs were a 90-day money-back guarantee.
  • Also a microcrystal technology as the main mechanism (the main way the product works).
  • Also bundle pricing under $40 (several items sold together for less than $40).
  • Traffic went almost only to product pages. 125 ads (81.2%) went to the main product page.
  • 8 ads (5.2%) went to a second page.
  • There were no listicles (list-style articles), advertorials (ads that look like articles) or pre-sell pages (pages that warm buyers up before the shop).
  • The analysis showed the brand lagging on its funnel (the path from ad to purchase).
  • So a similar product with a stronger funnel and a better offer could compete.
  • The top video was downloaded, transcribed and analysed frame by frame in Gemini (an AI tool).
  • Its script was then adapted to a new product.

Worked example: supplement brand

  • A supplement brand with 67 active ads had Promotion / Discount as its top concept.
  • Its top USP was Fast and Free Shipping.
  • Its top desire was a limited-time deal.
  • This combination shows a brand that relies on urgency (pressure to buy now) and price incentives.
  • It can be copied directly.
  • Or it can be varied with a different angle.

Reverse-engineering a winning video in Kalodata

2 min

Reverse-engineering means taking a winning ad apart to see how it is built.

Procedure

  1. In Kalodata, open the product page.
  2. Set the region to United States and the date range to Last 30 Days.
  3. Check that the product sells at scale (large amounts) in Core Metrics (the main numbers): Main Revenue, Item Sold and Avg. Unit Price (average price per item).
  4. Open the Video & Ad tab. Pick the video ad with the highest views and revenue in the period.
  5. Watch the video. Note the hook, the problem named, the testimonial structure (a customer telling their story), the solution, the benefits, the social proof (showing other people who bought and liked it) and the call to action.
  6. Search the product name in the Meta Ad Library (see Ad Library settings above).
  7. Check whether the same video runs many times as separate ad variations.
  8. Use the findings to build your own version of the structure, not a copy of the video.

Reference numbers

  • Winning reference ad: about 8 million views and about 165k revenue in the last 30 days.
  • The video ran about 1 minute 57 seconds on the player.
  • Kalodata's Video & Ad view lists competitor video ads for a product.
  • The Storyboard Export lets you export an ad's storyboard (a picture sheet of its scenes) for analysis.
  • Highest Revenue Videos and their conversion rates (the share of viewers who buy) show which ad creatives generate the most sales.
  • Good ad editing and detailed bottom-funnel creatives (ads aimed at people ready to buy) show a product's ads are worth studying.
  • They may be worth copying in a customized form.
  • Look for creatives that show the product in use or in a result-focused way.

Worked case: calcium multi-balm stick

  • Case: a Korean calcium multi-balm stick (a skin-care balm in a stick) was sold in the United States and on TikTok Shop.
  • It reached about 50k revenue per day and about 1.5 million in the last 30 days.
  • Its best ad was a phone-filmed creator testimonial (a creator sharing their experience, filmed on a phone).
  • That ad reached about 8 million views and about 165k revenue in the same period.
  • The ad ran about 1 minute 57 seconds.
  • It opened with a woman naming the product as a Botox-like balm (a balm people compare to Botox, a wrinkle treatment) to stop the scroll.
  • She then showed a before and after image.
  • She named smoker lines and under-eye darkness.
  • She told a story about an aunt who smoked for over 20 years.
  • She added her own insecurity.
  • She then claimed she is a nurse.
  • She listed adenosine, glutathione and a patented calcium-vitamin D ingredient (legally protected) with about ten benefits in a row.
  • She cited five-star reviews.
  • She cited a four-week wait from stock shortage (not enough items in stock).
  • The ad ended with urgency (pressure to act fast).
  • The same video ran as a repeated Meta ad.
  • It had about 6,500 keyword results in the Ad Library.

Selecting and editing test videos from Kalodata

1 min

Procedure

  1. Open the Video & Ad list in Kalodata. Set the time filter to Last 30 Days.
  2. Look for videos with high view counts and strong sales.
  3. These signal a viral, trending ad (one that spreads fast).
  4. Choose 12 to 24 videos for a test. Start with at least 12.
  5. Take half of the selected videos (about six) from page 1.
  6. Take the rest from pages 2 and 3.
  7. Keep only videos that show a real person. Drop abstract or product-only videos.
  8. Drop videos whose hook or demonstrated outcome does not match what your product delivers, even if the hook itself is strong.
  9. Drop videos whose spoken language is not English. You cannot check or match their message to your store.
  10. Do not spend much time judging production quality. The sales data is the main signal.
  11. In CapCut (a video editing app), cut every part that mentions TikTok Shop or Orange Cart (a checkout name shown in the ad).
  12. Cut every offer that is not on your landing page. This includes buy one get one free if your website does not show it.
  13. Make no other changes. Add no new hooks. Add no extra edits to videos that already sell.

Why this selection works

  • Do not judge a video mainly by how polished it looks.
  • If a video shows strong sales, it is already working.
  • Its results matter more than its quality.
  • Pulling some winners from deeper result pages targets ads that fewer competitors are looking at.
  • This gives the test fresher material than only the top results.
  • Spending a long time deciding whether a video is good wastes time.
  • Use the view and sales data first. Sort the videos on that basis.
  • Kalodata's result list offers a Download Video (Without Watermark) option. A watermark is a logo or name overlaid on the video.
  • This gives a clean file to edit before running the ad.

Analysing the psychology of winning ads

2 min

Prompt: psychological pattern analysis of a swipe file

A prompt is a written instruction you give an AI tool. Key words in the prompt below:

  • Swipe file: your saved collection of successful ads.
  • Avatar: your imagined typical customer.
  • Schwartz's five-stage awareness model: five levels of how much a buyer knows about their problem and the product, from Unaware to Most Aware.

Use this prompt unchanged when you have a collection of successful ads:

When you receive a collection of successful ads, study each one to uncover the psychological pattern underneath the wording. For every ad, identify which emotion it activates (fear or desire), the core logic that makes its promise credible, the specific opening technique that halts scrolling, and the sequence of beats it follows. Then group the ads by shared emotional target and persuasion approach, identify fears the avatar likely holds that none of these ads address, and classify where the audience sits on Schwartz's five-stage awareness model (Unaware through Most Aware).

Swipe file size

  • Gather 5 to 10 winning ad scripts to analyze as your swipe file.
  • If using Kalodata, take the top performers.
  • If using the free Meta Ad Library, any ad running for months is validated as a proven winner.

Ad style analysis

  • Always study what competitors are doing in their ad copy.
  • Improve on what is already working.
  • Use competitive copywriting as inspiration for your own difference.
  • Do not use it as a template to copy.
  • Ad style analysis compares each pair of styles.
  • The first pair needs at least 10 examples each.
  • Track pattern changes over time.
  • The pairs are:
    • professional vs user-generated content (UGC: content made by ordinary customers, not studios);
    • emotional vs logical appeals;
    • benefit-focused (bright side) vs problem-focused (dark side);
    • simple vs complex explanation;
    • short-form vs long-form;
    • serious vs humorous tone;
    • male vs female presenters;
    • solo presenter vs group;
    • studio vs real-world setting;
    • text-heavy vs image-focused;
    • technical vs lifestyle framing;
    • identity-led (speaks to who the buyer is) vs benefit-led.
  • Also note outliers. These are results that go against the trend but still succeed.
  • Model proven landing pages and ads instead of inventing new ones and hoping they work.
  • Find what competitors already run. Examples: opt-in pages (a page that collects an email address), sales pages or quiz pages.
  • Build something similar before testing refinements (small changes).
  • Following a proven model saves a lot of time and money.
  • Copying one source closely is imitation that reads as a knockoff (a fake copy).
  • Borrowing from many sources is research that produces something new.
  • Copy the reasoning behind a style. For example, why an ad hook works for its audience.
  • Do not copy only its surface look.
  • Copying a competitor's ad without understanding why it works, and without setting expectations, is a common cause of failure.
  • Competitor research is useful only to extract transferable lessons (lessons you can use elsewhere).
  • Do not use it to clone their ads.

Analysing hooks, visuals and comments

1 min

Hooks and visuals

1 min
  1. Analyze 20 to 25 of the most successful hooks (opening lines or seconds that stop people scrolling) from TikTok, Instagram and Facebook ads. Find the patterns that work most often. Note which hooks have run longest.
  2. Analyze visuals (the pictures and video shown in an ad). Check these:
    • picture of the benefit (the good result);
    • picture of the problem;
    • picture of the product;
    • product in action (the product being used);
    • environments (places shown);
    • people shown;
    • what visuals create belief;
    • production quality (professional vs. authentic UGC, meaning user-generated content, which looks like ordinary people filmed it);
    • color schemes;
    • text overlay styles (words placed on top of the image).
  3. Analyze at least 100 comments on viral posts (posts that spread fast). Group them by:
    • questions;
    • objections (reasons people say no);
    • enthusiasm;
    • testimonials (happy-customer statements). Note these too:
    • sentiment (whether the tone is positive or negative);
    • "wish I had known" and "I need this because" comments;
    • tagging behavior (people tagging friends);
    • identity-resonance comments (people saying the ad describes who they are).
  4. Study emotional storytelling:
    • transformation ads (before and after change) vs. feature-benefit ads (lists of features and what they do);
    • character arcs (how the person in the ad changes);
    • before-state portrayal (how the "before" situation is shown);
    • vulnerability moments (when the person admits something hard);
    • at least 10 ads that tap identity-level emotion (feelings about who someone is).
  5. Map content structures (the order of steps in an ad; see table below). Note:
    • The balance of emotion vs. logic;
    • typical ad length;
    • objection handling (answering doubts);
    • narrative patterns (story shapes).

Content structures in winning content

1 min

A funnel is the path a buyer walks, from first interest to buying. A symptom is a sign of the problem. An offer is the deal (price, bonus, guarantee).

Symptom → Problem → Solution → Product → Offer Full funnel from symptom
Problem → Solution → Product → Offer Starts at the problem
Solution → Product → Offer Starts at the solution
Product → Offer Starts at the product
Offer only Starts at the price or deal
Identity → Problem → Solution → Product → Offer Identity-first approach
  • Deliverables for this section (what you must hand in):
    • 20 to 25 viral hooks with links;
    • 20 to 30 representative comments;
    • 15 to 20 visual approaches;
    • 7 to 10 effective structure patterns;
    • 10 to 15 successful calls to action (CTA: the line that tells people what to do);
    • 5 to 7 trending visual styles;
    • 3 to 5 trending hashtags.

Problem and solution messaging

1 min
  • Problem-and-solution messaging (saying what hurts, then how the product fixes it) on the product page and in the ads is an effective way to explain why a product is worth buying. Check how a competitor (a rival selling similar things) presents the problem and solution before writing your own.
  • Competitor analysis on the landing page (the page visitors arrive on after clicking an ad):
    1. Open each competitor landing page you found.
    2. Note the marketing angles (the main reason or emotion used to sell), the copywriting (the written words) and the offers they use.
    3. Identify weaknesses such as brand colors or layout that you could improve.
    4. Use these findings to create a stronger version and beat the competition.

Interrupt-style ad models

1 min

Interrupt-style ads stop the eye at once.

Collect interrupt-style ad models:

  1. Search Google Images for tabloid newspaper headlines (big, loud news-style headlines).
  2. Note the unusual or shocking images used to grab attention.
  3. Note how headlines stay short and make the reader curious.
  4. Adapt these patterns to your own product and audience in your ads.

Finding gaps and building differentiation

2 min

Differentiation means what makes your product stand apart from others.

  • Gaps in your analysis are fears or mechanisms (how the product works) that the avatar (your typical buyer, described in detail) likely has, but that none of the winning competitor ads address. These are white-space openings (empty spots nobody fills) worth testing.
  • Identifying gaps means recognizing unaddressed emotional pain, unmet anxieties or unstated objections (doubts nobody voices) in the customer data that the swipe file (see below) does not hit. These signal potential differentiation angles (ways to stand apart).
  • Before analyzing your audience, do competitor creative research first. Understanding how competitors frame problems and structure messaging simplifies the audience investigation. You already know which angles and language resonate in your market.
  • Competitor creative research process:
    1. Identify your 3 most important competitors.
    2. For each competitor, find their 3 most commonly used creative types (a creative is any ad picture or video): UGC, lifestyle images, high-production video, memes, etc.
    3. For each creative type, document:
      • The concept or angle (testimonial, unboxing, advertorial (an ad written like an article), problem-solution);
      • hooks used in the first 3 seconds;
      • problems and desires addressed;
      • benefits;
      • features;
      • pain points;
      • objections overcome;
      • target character attributes (traits of the person the ad speaks to) and age;
      • scenery;
      • slang or buzzwords.
  • Start every market analysis by checking how many similar products have already advertised to the same prospects (people likely to buy). Competitors' ads are the best evidence. A few hours of research should gather samples of every competing ad.
  • For a new competitor that scales (grows its ad spending fast), the ad is usually the main reason it works, not the website. The product matters most, but the ad hook and visual approach are what sell it.
  • Afterlip (a product-finding tool) shows trending products and features beyond Dropispy (another such tool). Check that the products found are not oversaturated (already sold by too many sellers) before testing them.
  • Review competitors' Facebook Ad Library (Meta's public list of all running ads). Identify the ads they run for a long time, because repeated ads signal winners (ads that make money). Analyse their storyline and marketing angle. Then replicate or adapt that angle for your own product.

TikTok research

Swipe files and boards

What a swipe file is

1 min
  • A swipe file is a digital or analog (paper) archive of ads, creatives, headlines, page layouts and other material you have saved from others. Open it when you need ideas. Reuse the patterns, not the exact content. Photos taken with a phone are enough to build one.
  • Keep a swipe file of brands that inspire you. Review it regularly, for example every other day. This shows how similar products are marketed in your niche.

Building a swipe file

1 min
  1. Save any ad, landing page or creative worth reusing to your swipe file immediately.
  2. Keep the file in a form you actually use, digital or analog. Add to it regularly.
  3. When you need inspiration for a new ad or creative, open the file. Look for patterns that match your product and audience.
  4. Adapt the pattern to your own offer rather than copying it directly.

Boards in Foreplay and similar tools

1 min
  • Foreplay is a swipe file tool where brands save ads. Use it to collect proven examples, such as Black Friday ads from e-commerce brands, before producing your own creatives.
  • Foreplay is a paid ad inspiration tool, about 50 USD per month. It saves ads from the Facebook Ad Library into boards through a Chrome extension (a small add-on for the Chrome browser). It works as a mood board (a collection of ideas) rather than a performance tracker. It does not show metrics (numbers like sales or clicks).
  • Collect ad inspiration in this order:
    1. Find ads in the Facebook Ad Library. Check how many days each has been running. Longer-running ads suggest performance.
    2. Save ads that look like good performers or show good design practice into a board in an ad collection tool such as Foreplay, using its Chrome extension.
    3. Follow industry experts who post their ads. Build a swipe file from them.
    4. Analyze saved ads for the psychological principle, layout and spacing that make them work.

Reverse-engineering a saved ad

1 min

Reverse-engineering means taking something apart to see how it was made.

  1. Pick a product, ad or brand you admire.
  2. Write down what it leaves out or could have done better.
  3. Create your own version that fills one of those gaps.
  4. Compare your version with the original. This shows which choices are your own tendencies.

Automating competitive ad intelligence

1 min

  • Set a trigger (an automatic start rule) for when a competitor uploads a new ad to their ad library. The automation:

    • downloads the ad;
    • optionally uses an AI step to transcribe it (turn speech into text);
    • feeds it to Claude (an AI assistant) to rewrite it for your brand's voice and offer;
    • saves the adapted version to Google Drive or Google Docs.

    This builds a continuously updated competitive creative reference library (an always-current folder of rival ads) with zero manual effort.

Search, shopping and display ad copy

Section 12.14 · 14 of 26 in this chapter · 29 min read

In short

  • Pick one action first. Decide what the searcher should do, such as buying or taking a quiz, before you write the ad.
  • The ad's only job is to earn the click. The landing page (the page the click opens) makes the sale.
  • Put the searched keyword in three places: the display URL (the web address shown in the ad), the headline and the description.
  • For Responsive Search Ads (ads where Google mixes your text), write about two-thirds keyword headlines and one-third offer headlines. Never write all 15 yourself.

Principles for search ad copy

3 min
  • Write for one action. Before you write a search text ad (a short text ad shown when someone types a search), define exactly one action the searcher should take.
  • The ad sells the click, not the product. Its only job is to make the searcher curious enough to visit the landing page, where the offer and the full sales argument belong.
  • The landing page must match the search term. The visitor should find what the ad promised. The ad only creates curiosity and sets expectations, so its copy can stay short within the limited character space (the maximum number of letters and spaces allowed).
  • Earn the click to lower effective cost. The main job of a Google search text ad is to earn the click. A higher click-through rate (the share of people who see the ad and click it) usually lowers the effective cost per click (what you pay for each click) compared with competitors bidding on the same queries.
  • Lead with the buyer's benefit. Start with what the buyer gains, not the product name. Either move the buyer away from a pain (a problem they want gone, for example weight gain) or toward a gain (for example muscle growth). Benefit lines can be framed around the seller ("we have helped a million customers") or around the buyer ("seven ways to build muscle").
  • Trigger an emotion tied to the outcome. Write for how the buyer will feel after the result, not only for what the product does. "Feel beautiful" instead of "look beautiful" connects emotionally when the product improves how people live.
  • Avoid copying competitor offers. If every competitor in the auction (the bidding for ad spots) uses the same offer, such as "40% off", the ads blend together and clicks drop. Check competitor ads before you write yours and find a distinct angle.
  • Reflect the searched keyword in three places. Include the targeted search keyword (the word the searcher typed and you bid on) in the display URL, the headline and the description text. Google bolds the matching keyword in these three places, which makes the ad stand out and shows relevance (how closely the ad matches the search).
  • Address the reader directly. Write as if speaking to one person, using direct words such as "you" and "you can" instead of dry facts.
  • State the next action explicitly. Always include a clear call to action (the words that tell the reader what to do) and state the action directly. Do not assume the searcher knows what to do next.
  • Match the call to action to the landing page. Use a call to action that tells the searcher what happens after the click, such as a shop page, a quiz or a product comparison, so the click matches their expectation.
  • Use symbols where they fit the offer. The registered trademark sign (®), the percent sign (%) and the dollar sign ($) can make a Google text ad stand out. Use them where they fit the offer.
  • Use ad extensions. Ad extensions are extra lines under the ad, such as links or contact details. Choose which ones to use based on the specific ad type during setup.
  • Keep the copy simple. Ad copy does not need overthinking. Keep it simple, personal and clear about the next action. The detailed setup of campaigns (the top folder of ads and budget) and ads follows in the campaign setup stage.
  • Show the promotion when one exists. Shoppers search for the exact product and compare sellers. Name the product and its use clearly, and show the promotion in the ad when one exists, since a promo tag (a small promotion label) raises clicks.
  • Match the ad to its page. Google Shopping ads (product ads with pictures and prices) always link to product pages, so ad creative and page content should match.

Search text ad formulas and structures

1 min

Standard formulas

3 min

  • Result-plus-keyword formula. State the result the buyer wants, in a time-bound and concrete way, then add a keyword the searcher typed. The searcher sees the exact outcome they came for, which raises relevance and clicks. Examples: "Stop back pain in 30 days" with keyword "back pain relief" in the display URL; "Dry skin fixed by Friday" with keyword "dry skin cream" in the display URL.

  • Simple Google text ad structure. Use these five parts in order:

    1. Headline: include the keyword or the buyer's goal.
    2. Description line: state one benefit or feature.
    3. Second description line: add social proof (proof that others use or trust it, for example a customer count) or an emotional hook (a line that stirs a feeling).
    4. Call to action that sets the expectation for the landing page.
    5. Put the keyword or search query into the display URL path (the folder-like part after the web address).

  • Three-line text ad pattern. The headline names the result the buyer wants and includes the searched keyword. The first description line turns that result into a feeling or confidence benefit. The second description line adds social proof with a customer count. The call to action states the next step. The keyword is repeated in the display URL path. Each line answers one question the searcher has at that moment (is this what I want, why this one, what happens next), and the URL confirms the match. Examples:

    • Example 1 (US sleep supplement): headline "Fall asleep faster in 7 nights" with keyword "sleep aid" in the URL; line "Wake up rested, not groggy"; line "Trusted by 250,000 sleepers, see the results"; URL path sleep-aid.
    • Example 2 (running shoes for flat feet): headline "Run pain-free this month" with keyword "running shoes flat feet" in the URL; line "Every stride feels supported"; line "Chosen by 80,000 runners, shop today"; URL path running-shoes-flat-feet.

  • Keyword-and-benefit formula with personal address. Put the targeted keyword in the display URL, headline and description. Address the reader as "you". End with an explicit instruction such as "Get yours now". The keyword shows relevance, bolding draws the eye, the direct address feels personal, and the explicit instruction removes doubt about the next step.

    • Example 1 (running shoe keyword): Headline "Trail Running Shoes - Free Returns", description "You'll find trail running shoes built for rocky paths. Shop the range today."
    • Example 2 (dog leash keyword): Headline "Reflective Dog Leashes For Night Walks", description "You can walk your dog safely after dark. Order your reflective leash now."

  • Offer-and-shipping headline formula for online stores. The headline is the product category plus a clear price offer (such as a percentage off or an "up to" discount), then a short benefit such as free shipping. The description line adds a reason to act now, such as a limited-time sale, a free-shipping threshold, a fast pickup option or a generous return window. Shoppers scan these quickly, so the offer and the shipping or return promise do most of the persuading.

    • Example 1: "Ceiling Fans - Up to 35% Off | Free Delivery Over $75", description "Weekend Clearance Ends Sunday. Order Today for Next-Day Dispatch and 45-Day Returns."
    • Example 2: "Kitchen Organisers - 25% Off Storage Sets | Fast Local Pickup", description "Limited Stock at This Price. Free Shipping on Orders Over $60 and Easy 30-Day Returns."

Branded product search ads

1 min
  • Exact name headline. A branded search is when the searcher types the brand's own product name. The headline is the exact brand name plus product name. The description repeats that name, then adds stock status, shipping speed, price and a short call to action such as "order now". The ad mirrors the search, so the searcher sees right away that the exact product is available.
    • Example 1: Headline "Northline Trail Backpack 30L". Description "Northline Trail Backpack 30L in stock, ships fast, $89. Order now."
    • Example 2: Headline "Brightwave Air Purifier H13". Description "Brightwave Air Purifier H13 in stock, ships fast, $179. Shop now."

Informational and transactional headlines

1 min
  • Informational headlines (guide titles). Informational searches are people looking for help, not yet buying. Use a "how to choose" or "what to look for" phrase, then the product type, then the use case or the reader's situation. A headline that reads like a helpful article matches their intent and avoids a sales tone. Examples: "How to Choose Ski Boots for Wide Feet" and "What to Look for in Hiking Boots for Rocky Terrain".
  • Transactional headlines. Transactional searches are people ready to buy. Use "Shop" plus the product type, plus the use case, plus one purchase trigger such as free shipping or free returns. This works for bottom-of-funnel queries (searches made close to buying), where the searcher is ready to compare or buy. The same wording fails on informational searches because it signals that a sale is the only goal. Examples: "Shop Waterproof Hiking Boots for Wet Trails, Free Returns" and "Shop Kids Rain Jackets for Muddy Play, Free Shipping".

Restricted categories

1 min
  • Savings offer instead of a direct call. Restricted categories are product types that ad platforms limit. For remarketing copy (ads shown again to people who visited before), replace a direct call to a person with a plain savings offer that names the store and a percentage. Formula: offer percentage + product or store + store domain, without addressing the viewer as someone who needs the product. Examples: "Save 5% on your next order at [store domain] this week." and "Get 10% off [product category] at [store domain] before the offer ends."

Dropshipping keyword signals in search ads

1 min
  • Phrases that surface dropshipping products. Dropshipping means selling products that a supplier ships straight to the buyer, so the seller holds no stock. Search ad keywords such as "percent off", combined with a discount figure and phrases like "get yours today", "get yours now" or "get yours", are used by about 90 to 95 percent of dropshippers in their ad text. These keywords therefore surface dropshipping products.

Google Responsive Search Ads

1 min

Responsive Search Ads are ads where you give Google several headlines and descriptions, and Google mixes them to find the best combinations.

Headline and description mix (current, 2025-26)

1 min
  • Use AI for volume, with a clear brief. AI-generated ad copy solves the volume problem of manual copywriting. It produces on-theme, intent-matched variants (versions that fit the theme and what the searcher wants), which Google rewards through Quality Score (Google's rating of how relevant your ad is). Understand what effective copy does (matching search intent and enabling combination testing) before you feed material into AI.
  • Split headlines into two intent categories. Give about two-thirds of the headlines to keyword-relevance headlines that mirror the search theme. Give about one-third to promotional headlines containing offers, guarantees or trust signals (free shipping, lifetime warranty, verified reviews).
  • Do not write every headline yourself. Do not write all 15 Responsive Search Ad headlines yourself. Fill the required headlines with keyword content and reserve slots for promotional headlines controlled at the ad group level (the set of ads sharing the same keywords). This lets Google test which combinations perform best.
  • Split descriptions the same way. Use the same two-part split for descriptions: about two descriptions with keyword-relevant content, plus one or two descriptions with calls to action, social proof or differentiators (what sets you apart).
  • Punctuation limits. Responsive Search Ad headlines cannot contain exclamation marks. Each description may contain a maximum of one exclamation mark.
  • Vary lengths. Vary headline lengths instead of making them uniform. Different screen widths and ad spaces need both short and long text options to display well.

Older text ad guidance (2021-22)

1 min
  • Headline 1 carries the keywords. Write 3 headlines, each maximum 30 characters. Only the first headline always displays fully; the others may be cut off. Always put target keywords and the product identifier (the model name or number) in Headline 1.
  • Headline 1 matches the search term. Include the target search terms (the keywords you bid on) in Headline 1, so what the customer searched for matches what they see.
  • Headline 2 names one differentiator. For product-specific search ads, focus Headline 2 on one best feature or unique selling point (the reason to pick you) that sets you apart from competitors. Do not use a generic description.
  • Use the description space. Write 2 descriptions, each maximum 90 characters including spaces. Use most of the space to show as much information as possible.
  • Create curiosity in descriptions. Hint at benefits without fully explaining them. Guide readers to click through to the landing page to learn the full story. The landing page answers their curiosity and gives conversion opportunities (chances to make a sale).
  • Address the reader with "you" and monetary bonuses. Use "you" to address the reader directly. Include special offers or bonuses with their money value (for example, "$700 in bonus accessories") to spark curiosity and desire.
  • Use two curiosity hooks. Build more than one curiosity hook (a teaser that makes people want to know more) across your descriptions. Tease a key benefit in the first description without fully explaining it. In the second description, add another benefit, bonus value or special offer. Answer both hooks on the landing page to drive conversions.

Example: product-specific search campaign

1 min
  • Campaign and ad group structure. Name the campaign with product identifiers combined with geographic targeting (the countries or regions you show ads in), for example "Product IDs - Search Text - US". Create ad groups organised by specific product models, such as the Sunray Cayenne Sauna HL400D.
  • Bid and keywords. Set the default cost-per-click bid (this example uses $0.50), which is the most you pay per click. Target keywords with broad match modifier syntax (the + symbol before core terms, such as +cayenne +hl400d), which means those words must appear. This catches variations while keeping relevance.
  • Headlines. Write three headlines with varying character limits (30, 30 and 21 characters). Lead with product identification and purchase intent, then highlight key benefits such as space efficiency for compact areas, and emphasise shipping advantages.
  • Display path. Use a display path that reflects the product URL structure, for instance bathvault.com/sunray/cayenne-hl400d.
  • Descriptions. Compose two description lines with character limits (71 and 83 characters). Explain the functional advantage, such as fitting into small outdoor spaces, and name a concrete incentive, such as $700 in bundled accessories.
  • Extensions. Add call extensions with business contact information, and callout extensions (short highlight lines) that show competitive strengths: price-match policies, service quality and complimentary shipping options.

Callouts and sitelinks

1 min
  • Use callouts, not sitelinks, on conversion ads. Conversion ads are ads aimed at sales. Use callout extensions (not sitelink extensions) on product-specific conversion-focused ads. Sitelinks (extra links to other pages) distract from the sale by sending people to shipping pages or other products. Callouts can highlight offers like free shipping, guarantees or special bonuses.

Pinning and display paths

1 min
  • Pin headlines that must appear. Responsive search ads do not always show every headline and description, and the ad editor warns about this. To guarantee a specific headline or description appears, pin it to a fixed position (lock it to one spot) in the ad. Then check the desktop and mobile previews before you save.
  • Display path fields. Each text ad has two display path fields after the domain, in the form www.yourdomain.com/Path 1/Path 2. Each path field is limited to 15 characters. Use them for short, relevant labels that reflect the brand or product category.
  • Repeat the ad group theme. The display path fields are the visible part of the ad URL beyond the final URL. Repeat the ad group theme there, so the searcher sees a reason to click. Enter the theme keyword there when all ad group keywords are very similar.

Lead generation search ads

1 min

Lead generation ads collect interest before any sale, for example through a guide or a quiz.

Principles

1 min
  • Grab attention with a different angle. The headline should grab attention and hint at what the reader will learn by clicking. Do not use the same product-focused wording as competitors. A different angle makes the ad stand out in search results.
  • No sitelinks. Do not add sitelink extensions to lead generation ads. Sitelinks pull attention away from the one goal of the ad, which is getting the visitor to the content page.
  • Call to action and no answer in the ad. Lead generation ads need a call to action in the description, such as "Take the quiz today". The ad should tease the content without giving the answer away.
  • Combine curiosity and fear. Use one or more curiosity hooks plus a fear or desire to avoid mistakes. These are the strongest emotions when searching for expensive products.

Headline and description patterns

1 min
  • Negative or contrarian headline. Open with a warning that goes against what competitors for the same search term say, such as telling the searcher not to buy before reading something. Nearly every other ad for the term promotes buying, so the contrarian line stands out. It sparks curiosity because the searcher feels they may be missing information. Use this sparingly, since negative headlines are rarely appropriate.
  • Fear-of-mistake description. Tease the content without revealing it. State that the reader will learn how to avoid a costly error for this product type. Buyers of expensive products fear choosing the wrong item and having to return it, so the promise draws clicks. The content page must deliver the promised answer. Examples: "Learn the one measurement most buyers get wrong before you order." (standing desk) and "Find out which grind setting avoids the bitter cups beginners always buy." (espresso machine).
  • Curiosity tease description. A short description names a benefit of the content and ends with a teaser, such as "Learn which models are best for you right now." This keeps the ad curiosity-driven instead of sales-driven. The reader expects to learn something rather than be sold to.
  • Quiz headline and description. A headline invites the searcher to find their answer, such as "Find Out Which X You Need". Follow it with "Take the Online Quiz". Then write a description that promises a personal result and a call to action, such as "Take the quiz today." Quizzes attract people who want the right choice. People enjoy the game-like format, which also sets the ad apart from competitor ads.
  • Topic-tease headline. For content-led search ads, name the expensive decision or the search term, then promise an answer, such as "Find out which [product] suits your needs before you buy." The headline should raise a question the searcher wants answered, rather than name a product to sell.

Shopping product titles

4 min

Length and visibility

  • Know the character zones. Shopping product titles have a 150-character limit, but weight is not evenly distributed. The first 25-35 characters are nearly all visible in mobile previews and carry the most impact. The zone between characters 35 and 80 has secondary impact. Characters beyond 80 are rarely seen and carry minimal weight.
  • Conflict: which length carries the most weight. Sources differ. Source E 2025-26 identifies the first 25-35 characters as the preview-visible zone. Source A states that Google weighs the first 70 characters most heavily. Apply both: the first 25-35 characters decide what a mobile shopper sees, and the first 70 characters is the zone that carries the most weight. Put the core keyword in the first 25-35 characters and the remaining core terms before character 70.
  • Keep high-value terms out of the back half. Do not place high-value keywords or unique selling points beyond character 80 in shopping titles. The back half has minimal visibility and weight; reserve it only for secondary details.

Structure

  • Lead with type, keyword and differentiator. Lead shopping product titles with product type + strongest keyword + one differentiating attribute, all within the first 25-35 characters. This section determines preview visibility, especially on mobile devices.
  • Order the second zone by search volume. The second keyword zone extends to approximately 70-80 characters. After the lead, order remaining content by search volume: key specifications and materials first, then use cases, then secondary details.
  • Include the attributes customers search for. A product title should include, in roughly this order: brand or manufacturer, gender, color, size, product type or category, and any other attribute customers search for. Each attribute you include lets the product show up for more specific searches.
  • Use the title formula. Brand + Gender + Specific product name + Color + Size. Each element matches a separate search phrase, so one title covers many relevant queries. Examples: "Northwind Women's Insulated Rain Jacket Navy Medium" and "Acme Men's Trail Running Shoe Black Size 10".
  • Use plain color names and no SKU codes. Leave supplier SKU codes out of product titles unless keyword research shows that people search for them; check this in Google Keyword Planner. Use plain English color names, such as green instead of a supplier's technical color name, because they match what shoppers search for.
  • Put variants in the description. Put variants, long-tail phrases and synonyms in the product description rather than cramming them into the title. The description is where you can add many search terms without weakening the title.

Campaign type

Sources differ on the first word of the title. Apply each by campaign type:

Campaign type Lead with Source
Prospecting shopping campaigns Descriptive, searchable product terms customers actually type; brand name at the end after a separator, or omitted if it consumes critical keyword space
Branded shopping campaigns Brand name first, followed by product descriptors and keywords
General title order (brand-first formula) Brand or manufacturer first, then gender, color, size, product type
  • Prospecting campaigns. In prospecting shopping campaigns, lead with descriptive, searchable product terms customers actually type. Position the brand name at the end after a separator, or omit it if it consumes critical keyword space.
  • Branded campaigns. In branded shopping campaigns, reverse the structure: begin with the brand name, then follow with product descriptors and keywords.

Bundles, promotions and sizes

  • Multi-pack quantity first. For multi-pack and bundle products, place the quantity at the title start (for example, "Four Pack" or "Twelve Pack"). This makes the count visible in previews and justifies the higher price before clicks.
  • Promotional language after keywords. Google's official policy discourages purely promotional titles, but well-placed promotional language in the 35-80 character zone, after core keywords, can improve conversion because it reads as a direct response to the search. Test while monitoring policy compliance.
  • Keep size out of the title. Avoid including size variants in shopping product titles. Shoppers reject listings whose size does not match their needs, clicking away before reviewing the product. Add size information to Google Merchant Center size fields instead.

Feed and store titles

  • Do not copy store titles. Do not copy titles directly from the Shopify store into the Google shopping feed. Shopify titles target website visitors; shopping feed titles target Google's search algorithm and customer intent. They require fundamentally different structures.
  • Feed text must match search terms. Product titles, descriptions and SKU numbers on the product page all feed the Google Shopping results, so write them to match the terms customers search for.
  • Do not copy the supplier name or SKU. Copying the supplier's product name or SKU as the title limits the searches a product can appear for and lowers click-through and conversion. Such titles often also miss the gender or the correct color.

Example: five title variations for one product

Product: a 15-calorie Southwest Ranch sauce from Taste Flavor Co. Develop five title variations where each emphasizes a different product strength.

  • Version 1: Leads with the low-calorie advantage and creamy texture, adds sugar-free and avocado oil details, and suggests wings and fries as applications.
  • Version 2: Balances the calorie count with gluten-free positioning and lists meal contexts: tacos, salads and grain bowls.
  • Version 3: Targets keto shoppers explicitly, highlights avocado oil and pairs with roasted vegetables.
  • Version 4: Combines multiple dietary benefits (low calories, gluten-free, sugar-free) within a meal-prep frame using chicken and rice.
  • Version 5: Emphasizes sugar-free and seed oil-free claims while identifying multiple uses: salad dressing and wings.

Rotating which benefit appears first lets each title capture different search patterns, so the brand can maintain separate product listings that each address a specific buyer priority.

Shopping product images

1 min
  • Leave margin around the product. Google automatically crops and centers Shopping images, so the product should leave spacing around it instead of filling the frame edge to edge. Check generated images for this margin before upload.
  • Edge-to-edge images get cropped. A product that fills the whole frame edge to edge will be cropped by Google's automatic cropping of Shopping images. Leave margin around the product in every generated image.
  • Show the product up close in lifestyle images. A lifestyle image where the product is clearly visible up close helps the shopper recognize the product before clicking. The claimed benefits are higher CTR and fewer bounces from the site, which should be verified through the split test.

Dynamic product ads and carousels (Meta)

2 min

Dynamic ad text

  • Question, product name and offer. Dynamic ad text follows one pattern: a question that names the product, followed by a specific offer and the live price. It works because people recognize the product and see the price before clicking, which reduces wasted clicks. Two examples in the same pattern: "Still thinking about {product name}? Save 10% on {product price} this week." and "Ready for {product name}? Get free shipping on {product price} today."
  • Headline template. Full template text:
Do you already adore {product name}? Today you can take 15% off its usual retail price of {product price} on our website. Place this as the dynamic headline at the top of a Facebook dynamic product ad, where the {product name} and {product price} values are pulled in automatically from the product feed. Keep the wording brief so the item name and the discount are noticed right away, and choose the discount level based on your profit margin.
  • Show the price in the image. Put the price inside the product image as an overlay for dynamic product ads. Shoppers then see the price before clicking, which avoids clicks from people who only leave after seeing the price.
  • Dynamic text works with the feed. Dynamic ad text is one lever for DPA CTR. It works together with product feed optimization.

Carousel settings and copy

  • Carousel primary text formula. Name the store, state that it offers the newest items, and point to products the person already viewed. This works because the reminder of the store and the recent items match the visitor's intent. Examples: "Still thinking it over? Your recently viewed picks are waiting at Store Name." and "Found what you were looking for? Grab the latest arrivals from Store Name before they sell out."
  • Keep the product name field. Headline in a dynamic product carousel: keep the dynamic product name field so each card shows the product name pulled from the product feed. Product description: leave it empty or insert a feed field such as price.

Creative source

  • Pull creative from product pages. Creative for remarketing ads should be pulled from the store's own product pages instead of custom videos. This keeps the ads cheap to produce and consistent with the product listings.

Dynamic remarketing (Google)

3 min

Copy patterns

  • Three-part remarketing pattern. A short headline with the store name and a call to action, a long headline that prompts the visitor to return and finish the purchase, and a description that states who the store is. The formula works because it names the brand, reminds the visitor of their unfinished intent, and reinforces trust. Examples:

    • Example 1: short headline "shop [brand name]", long headline "pick up where you left off and checkout today", description "the official [brand] store for [product category]".
    • Example 2: short headline "get [brand] now", long headline "your cart is still waiting for you", description "the trusted home of [product type] with fast shipping".

  • Headline formula with the store name. Use a short headline with the store name and a verb such as "Shop" (about 2 to 3 words), a long headline that tells the visitor to finish the order they started, and a description that states it is the official store. The visitor already knows the product, so the copy should only bring them back to finish, not introduce the brand. New examples in this pattern:

    • "Shop [Brand] Essentials" with long headline "Your cart is still waiting, finish checkout" and description "Shop the official [Brand] store today."
    • "Shop [Brand] Now" with long headline "Pick up where you left off at [Brand]" and description "Order from the full [Brand] range on our official site."

  • Enter several variations. Enter multiple headline and description variations. Google tests them automatically and shows more of the variations that get clicked most.

  • Variations inside one ad. Multiple headline and description variations inside one ad let Google test them against each other. Google then shows the best performing combinations to more people. For a first remarketing ad, one ad with one set of text is a reasonable starting point.

  • Only viewed products appear. Dynamic remarketing ads show only the products each visitor actually viewed, so a visitor who looked at just a few products sees only those few in the ad and preview. The ad therefore adapts to each person's browsing without manual changes.

Ad builder settings

  • Turn off auto video. For a dynamic remarketing ad, leave the automatic video option off. Enter headlines, long headlines and descriptions, which appear under the product images. Add several variations in the same ad so Google can test them and show the best performers to more people. Headlines and descriptions are also required for previews on YouTube and Gmail.
  • Use the feed for product display. In the ad builder, the final URL is the website homepage. The business name field gets the business name, and the ad preview shows products automatically pulled from the Merchant Center feed.
  • Use headline ideas. The headline field offers a generate ideas option. Enter a few words about the business and Google suggests headline ideas, which can be used or ignored.
  • Turn off auto video and native formats. Under additional formatting options, turn off "Use auto-generated video" and turn off "Use native formats". This keeps ads in the dynamic format shown in the preview.
  • No video in remarketing. Do not use video in remarketing campaigns. Their purpose is to show visitors the products they already viewed, and auto-generated video does not look good for this.
  • Add a logo and two images. Add a logo in the ad builder. The campaign also needs two images, one landscape and one square, which Google can suggest from the site.
  • Fix errors on Next. If the ad builder shows errors on Next, the usual cause is missing images. Adding one landscape and one square image and clicking apply changes fixes it.
  • Preview with sample products. Google's ad preview shows how the dynamic product ad looks with sample products. The preview updates as headlines and descriptions are entered. Previews for YouTube and Gmail need more assets, so they only appear after headlines and descriptions are added.

Image formats and template

  • Exactly two formats. Google Display remarketing ads require exactly 2 image formats: 1 landscape image and 1 square image.
  • Conflict: image counts by campaign type. Google Display remarketing ads use exactly two images (one landscape, one square). Standard Google Display static campaigns use about five images that cover the target sizes, with the 300 x 250 pixel size included. Apply the two-image rule to remarketing and the five-image rule to standard display campaigns.
  • Browse-selection template. Template text:
Browse [Store Name]'s selection

Retargeting messages

1 min
  • Restate the benefit with a time-limited discount. A retargeting ad for people who already saw the advertorial restates the benefit and adds a time-limited discount, either a percentage or a fixed amount off, to make the second visit worth it. The ad then sends the visitor straight to the offer page. Examples: "Still thinking about a calmer night for your dog? Come back today and get 15 percent off your first order." and "You looked at this before. Take 8 euros off your bundle if you order in the next 24 hours."
  • Email-based retargeting copy (prompt). Full prompt text:
Thanks. I'm also going to run retargeting ads on Meta and the Google Display Network. Based on the email copy above, write ad copy for both. Make a separate version for Meta and one for Google Display Network. Use the same discount code, discount percentage, and delivery deadline as in the emails, and give each ad a short headline and body text.
  • Follow-up retargeting copy (prompt). Full prompt text:
Once that's done, I'll also run retargeting ads on Meta and the Google Display Network. Write the ad copy for those placements, using the email copy from above as your source.

Display banners and static display ads

3 min

Banner copy and placement

  • Banner copy: brand and tagline. Banner ad copy should include your branding and a brief tagline describing what you do, the problem you solve, or your key strength. Keep text minimal; it is not a sales pitch, just enough to grab attention. Visitors learn full details after clicking through to your landing page or homepage.
  • Example banner tagline. "award-winning Shopify themes, legendary support." This works because it highlights the product advantage while addressing a known customer pain point: poor support from competing theme makers.
  • Partner sites. For banner ad placements on a partner's site, you usually supply the creative (the banner file) yourself and the site owner places it. Partners rarely design banners for you.
  • Clickable image with a visible call to action. On Google Display Network static ads, the whole image is clickable, but the call to action still has to look clickable. It does not need a fancy button, but it must not look like plain decoration.
  • Show business identity. Static display ads must show the business identity, for example the plain-text brand name, the base URL or a logo. In some countries this is a legal requirement so that users do not mistake an ad for editorial content.
  • Copy length by placement. Display placements offer less text room than Facebook placements. Use more copy and more product detail wherever the layout has more space, and keep the copy short where space is tight. The message stays the same across placements.
  • Small formats need short copy. On small display formats such as 300 x 250, there is little room for body copy. Shrinking the image can free some space, but long text will not fit. Keep the copy to a short line about the problem the product solves.

Product reminder ad elements

Key attributes of a product reminder ad:

Element Requirement
Image The best-quality image of the product that is available, ideally showing the product in use or in its intended setting. Resize it to fit each placement. If the product photos are weak, use the best available and accept the limit.
Headline Highlight the primary benefit of the product, not the product name.
Body copy At a minimum, a short description of the problem the product solves. Where there is more space (Facebook), add benefits and the USP. Much of this can be taken from the product description.
Call to action Test "Shop Now", "Buy Now", "Add To Cart" and "Learn More". Learn More sometimes performs well.
  • Benefit headline pattern. State the single benefit that proves the product performs where cheaper alternatives fail, written in plain words, instead of repeating the product name. Shoppers may recognize the name but buy the outcome, and a benefit that competitors lack separates the product from others in the same category. Examples: "Keep your garden shed dry through a wet winter." and "A tent that stays standing in a storm, not just on a sunny day."
  • Do not headline the product name. Do not use the product name as the headline of a reminder ad. Shoppers need a reason to click, and the name alone does not give one.

Image formats, sizes and file rules

  • Use JPEG under 150 KB. Upload Google display ads as JPEG rather than PNG, and keep each file under 150 KB. A 299 KB PNG was rejected by Google with an error.
  • Upload around five standard images. Upload around five standard display images that cover the target sizes, with the 300 x 250 pixel size included, so that they all show in the campaign. Google accepts image ads as GIF, JPG and PNG, and HTML5 or AMPHTML ads as ZIP.
  • Performance Max assets. Setting up a Performance Max campaign requires creating assets: square and rectangle image banners, five text descriptions and the business name. The setup guides you through these assets step by step.

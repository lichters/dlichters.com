Playbook › 12. Creatives and ad copy

Other details

Section 12.26 · 26 of 26 in this chapter · 11 min read

In short

  • Build your five reference files and finish the business form before you make any ads.
  • Do not send visitors to your store until it is ready and has at least 50 real products.
  • Ad advice for courses or services may not work for selling products.
  • Learn by copying good examples first, then make your own work.
  • Some platform changes are only tests or advice, not confirmed rules.

Setup and onboarding

1 min
  • Build five reference files for your project (a project is your own workspace in an AI tool): Voice Guide (how your brand sounds), Structure Library (how your messages are laid out), Banned List (what you never say), Avatar (a detailed picture of your ideal buyer), and Psychology Lens (how your buyer thinks and decides). Upload each as a separate file into your Claude or ChatGPT project knowledge (the files the AI reads inside your project) once created.
  • Complete the business onboarding form (the setup questionnaire about your business) in full.
  • Answer every question before you create any ad creatives (ad designs, images, and videos). Those answers set the direction for all creative and marketing decisions.

Naming a new store

1 min

Use this prompt for brand name ideas (a prompt is the exact text you give an AI assistant):

Suggest 10 brand name ideas for a new online store in the [NICHE] niche. Mix two relevant words where it helps, keep the names short and easy to remember, and note that the exact domain may not be available so combinations should be checked. Use the same conversation thread for follow-up requests such as more playful names.

Store readiness before traffic

1 min
  • Bring in traffic (visitors sent to your site) only after the site is optimized (set up to sell well) and has real products. Then visitors who arrive are able to buy.
  • A store should carry at least 50 products.
  • With only 10 products, the chance that a visitor buys one of them is too low.

Scope of ad copy by business model

1 min
  • Ecommerce ad copy (the written words in an ad for an online store) formulas (set patterns for writing) differ from ads for digital courses, consulting services, or other online business models.
  • Advice about online advertising may not apply to product promotion. The copy structure (how the message is built) and persuasion strategy (how it convinces people) are fundamentally different.
    • Older advice (2021-2022).

Coupons and reviews

1 min
  • YouTube product reviews (videos where people test and rate an item) with embedded coupon codes (discount codes built into the content) should appear in top search results alongside your product name.
  • These become your most-used coupon codes after the first-email offer (the discount sent in your welcome email).

Delivery tiers for large or heavy products

1 min

When the product is large or heavy and requires freight shipping (delivery by truck for big items), offer two delivery tiers (two service levels, each with its own price):

  1. Standard: free or freight shipping to curbside (left at the curb, at the edge of the property); the customer unboxes and sets up.
  2. Premium: upcharge (an extra fee on top of the price); professional installation and setup included.
  • Older advice (2020).

Outreach to website owners about broken links

1 min

Outreach means contacting other people to ask for something. A broken link is a link that now leads to an error page (here, error 404, meaning "page not found").

Use this outreach email and follow-up (a second message sent later):

Subject: Broken link on [page title]

Hi [first name],

While reading your page [URL of their page], I noticed the link to [name of the resource] now returns an error (404). I thought you would want to know, since it is a great resource and the broken link could frustrate your readers.

If you want to replace it, I have a page on [topic of the resource] that fits the same spot: [your URL]. Feel free to use it. If not, no problem at all, I just wanted to help.

Best,
[your name]

---

Follow-up (send a few days later):

Subject: Re: Broken link on [page title]

Hi [first name],

I wanted to follow up on my note about the broken link on [URL of their page]. If [your URL] fits, it is yours to use; if not, I will leave it there.

Best,
[your name]
  • Older advice (2021-2022).

Door-to-door sampling

1 min

Use this script for a trial approach (a visit where people try a free sample before they buy):

"Hello, I am visiting you today to offer you a free sample of [PRODUCT_NAME]. I have [NUMBER_OF_OPTIONS] options here, so please choose the one you like best. There is no charge and no obligation to buy. I will come back in [NUMBER_OF_DAYS] days to hear how you liked it, and you can tell me what you think of it at that time."
Do not ask for an order at the first visit. Let the sample carry the claims, and only return to hear the response.

Focus rule

1 min
  • Choose one idea and push for it.
  • Before a meeting, choose the one outcome you want.
  • At a networking event (a gathering to meet business contacts), choose one person and the one thing to say to them.
  • This focus usually produces a result more often than covering many goals at once.

Learning copywriting

1 min
  • Beginners find it very hard to design ads from a blank canvas (an empty page). Start by imitating well-made examples found on YouTube and social media.
  • Imitating an existing principle is a practical way to learn motion graphics (animated visuals) and layout (how things are placed on the page) before creating original work.
  • Breakthrough Advertising by Eugene Schwartz is a recommended read for beginners learning copywriting (writing words that sell) for ads.
  • 100 Million Dollar Offers and Dotcom Secrets are commonly suggested for offer thinking (how you design what you sell) and funnel thinking (the path a buyer follows from first click to purchase).
  • Take notes while reading and turn them into concrete ideas for the store. This is what makes these books useful.
  • For anyone who wants to study copywriting in depth, the first book to read is Breakthrough Advertising by Eugene Schwartz.
  • It is widely regarded as the core reference for writing good copy (sales words) and marketing.
    • Older advice (2021-2022).
  • Avoid buying used copies of Breakthrough Advertising by Eugene Schwartz on Amazon. Sellers often charge far more than the normal price.
  • The book is not cheap even new, so buy it from a legitimate (genuine, trusted) new-book source.
    • Older advice (2021-2022).
  • Breakthrough Advertising by Eugene Schwartz was out of print (no longer printed) for a long time and has been republished recently.
  • The new edition can be ordered through a site linked to Brian Kurtz, a longtime marketing figure.
    • Older advice (2021-2022).

Copywriting case studies

1 min

Video sales letter method

1 min

Eugene Schwartz-style copy system, as described by its author. A copy system is a set method for writing sales words. A video sales letter is a sales pitch delivered as a video.

  • Starting point: Before adopting the method, the author averaged $2 to 3 million in sales per year.
  • His hit ratio (the share of sales letters that worked) was 20 to 40 percent. In plain words: only one or two of every five sales letters worked.
  • First use: In early 2017 he used the method for the first time, in a video sales letter.
  • It went live in July 2017. Sales started coming in within an hour.
  • That one letter generated more than $30 million.
  • Scaling: He applied the method to every project.
  • His copy generated over $120 million in about two years, across different offers and products.
  • His hit ratio rose to 60 to 80 percent.
  • Output: His output also doubled.
  • It went from one full package every two months (a full package is a complete set of sales pieces) to one new sales letter per month.
  • Some full packages were finished in as little as two weeks.
  • Other results: A crypto offer built on the method brought in over 100,000 new paid names (new people who paid) before the platforms banned it.
  • Mentored copywriters (writers the author trained) also reached multi-million-dollar sales.
  • One mentee's first letter generated over $5 million in five months.

Industrial parts seller (direct mail)

1 min

Direct mail means printed letters sent by post.

  • A dull industrial parts seller (a company selling machine parts to factories) sent 100 pink-and-red letters.
  • The headline was about 69 things to do after work when parts arrive on time.
  • The catalogue (product booklet) had a sticker reading "BORING BUT NECESSARY".
  • The mailing produced 22 phone calls and 18 first-time orders.
  • All of them became long-term accounts (customers who keep buying).
  • That was worth more than $200,000 in new business that year.

Health charity telethon (sponsorship letter)

1 min

A telethon is a long TV fundraising show. A sponsorship letter asks a company to pay to support an event.

  • Starting point: A local chapter (local branch) of a health charity needed corporate sponsors for its annual telethon.
  • Typical nonprofit letters to companies described the charity's own needs.
  • Rebuild: The writer rebuilt the letter around three donor questions (questions a giver asks before paying).
    • What benefit justifies the cost?
    • Who else already chose this cause?
    • Where would the money come from in the budget?
  • Offer: The letter offered sponsorships (paid support) that needed little or no company cash.
  • Staff events and customer promotions would raise the funds instead.
  • Results: One employee promotion raised over $50,000.
  • A small company's bowl-a-thon (a bowling event for fundraising) raised $5,000.
  • Response: Experts had predicted the letter would fail.
  • The response rate (the share of people who replied) was only 0.5 percent. But the responders (people who replied) were important new donors.
  • One of them gave $13,000. That covered about half of the local telethon's costs that year.
  • The campaign was the chapter's most successful fundraising effort by return on investment (ROI: what you earn compared to what you spend).

Mortgage broker (first sales letter)

1 min

A mortgage broker finds home loans for buyers. A loan officer works for a lender and handles loans.

  • Starting point: A newly independent mortgage broker had recently left a loan officer job.
  • He wrote his first sales letter after using a copywriting book and seminar kit (a training pack).
  • Mailing: He mailed an eight-page illustrated booklet.
  • It had black text on white paper and handwritten notes in blue ink.
  • It cost $1.00 per piece, or $5,000 for 5,000 pieces.
  • He paid for the first mailing with a credit card.
  • Results: Within the first 30 days it brought in $40,000 in fee income (money earned as fees) from funded loans (loans that closed and paid out).
  • Within 60 days the total reached over $75,000.
  • He reported this as a return of 15 times the cost.
  • Follow-through: He kept mailing the same letter.
  • After four months his bank balance had grown substantially. He credited the letter as the foundation of his business.

Wartime candy (product-led insight)

1 min

A product-led insight is an idea that comes from the product itself.

  • Forrest Mars spotted a chocolate pellet in a candy shell in soldiers' field kits during the Spanish Civil War.
  • He patented (legally registered as his own invention) his own version in 1941.
  • The candy entered wartime rations (the food soldiers and civilians were allowed) and then reached the general public after the war.
  • Mars then hired a copywriter (a writer of sales words) for television advertising.
  • The key insight came from the product itself: it melts in your mouth, not in your hands.
  • Within four years, Mars was selling one million pounds of the candy per week.

Platform updates (2026)

1 min

A platform update is a change to how a social app works.

Instagram

1 min
  • Instagram's new handwritten fonts were built from real creator handwriting. Creators are people who make content.
  • They were scheduled to launch on 30 June 2026 for Reels (short videos) and Stories (short posts).
  • They were released in partnership with creators, so testing them can make videos look more native (at home in the app) and personal.
  • Instagram was testing auto-generated text for Stories, called Captions.
  • An overlay is text placed on top of an image or video. The feature was meant to improve accessibility (easier use for more people) and reach (how many people see your post).
  • Creators can use these to add readable text without typing it manually.
  • Instagram's Edits app includes Reels "Suggested Fixes".
  • It is an AI feature (software that makes its own suggestions) that proposes in-app edits (changes made inside the app).
  • The edits aim to improve retention (how long viewers keep watching) and watch time on underperforming Reels (short videos with weak views).
  • It was rolling out at the time of the update.
  • Instagram photo dumps (posts with several photos in one set) now allow a separate caption for each photo.
  • Creators use this to add context to each image, instead of one caption for the whole post.

Snapchat Spotlight

1 min
  • Spotlight is Snapchat's short-video feed.
  • Since the week of 29 June 2026, photo posts on Spotlight must include a song.
  • The photo is shown as a roughly 15-second still video (a photo that does not move) with the audio.
  • This is meant to improve discoverability (how easily people find your post) and reduce silent image posts.
  • To post a still photo on Snapchat Spotlight:
    1. Add a song so the photo plays as a 15-second video.
    2. Add a short caption and a currently popular track so it is served (shown to viewers) like a regular Spotlight video.
  • The update commentary (what experts wrote about the update) suggested adding a short black-bar caption (text on a black strip) and a popular track.
  • This would make the post served more like a regular Spotlight video.
  • This was advice from the commentary, not a rule confirmed by Snapchat.
  • Snapchat Topic Chats are in testing in the US, Canada and New Zealand.
  • They work like hashtags (labels that group posts by subject) and group community conversations around shared interests.
  • Adding a topic to a post places it in that conversation.

YouTube Shorts

1 min
  • YouTube Shorts (short vertical videos) was testing a "Save to device" option (a download button).
  • The option includes any on-screen edits.
  • It was unclear whether viewers could save only their own videos or other creators' videos too.
  • The feature was still in development.

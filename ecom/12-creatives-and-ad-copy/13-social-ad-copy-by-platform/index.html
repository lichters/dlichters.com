Social ad copy by platform

Section 12.13 · 13 of 26 in this chapter · 25 min read

In short

  • Write Facebook and Instagram ad text like a real person telling a story, not like an advert.
  • Put your main point in the first lines, because people only see the start before they tap "more".
  • Use the closing words "click below to get yours here" only in the main text, never in the headline.
  • Every headline needs proof (reviews or a rating) or a clear deal (such as free shipping) that people see at once.
  • While testing, use one version of the text per ad, so you know which one works.
More key points
  • New or freshly made videos get more reach on TikTok. Old copies lose reach.
  • Use 20 to 30 days old organic posts for paid Spark Ads. Keep big viral posts out of paid tests.
  • Test both formats (TikTok Spark Ads and normal TikTok ads) on the same product.
  • Pinterest needs 7 to 15 days to learn. Results are poor at first, so do not judge early.
  • Each platform needs its own kind of creative. Show the product early in the video.

Facebook and Instagram primary text: principles

3 min
  • The primary text is the main written part of a Facebook ad. It sits above the image.
  • It is the most important part of the ad.
  • People see it first, so it must look like a normal post, not an advert.
  • Many advertisers fail here. They write in marketing language, so the ad reads like a company promotion.
  • Getting the primary text right matters more than perfecting headlines or descriptions.
  • Write it as a first-person story, meaning told as "I".
    • Part 1: the problem you had.
    • Part 2: trying the product.
    • Part 3: the positive result.
  • Use casual phrases such as "I've been noticing," "switched to," "honestly," and "seriously loving." They make it sound like a real person.
  • The text must look organic, meaning natural and not paid for.
  • It should feel like a personal recommendation or story, not ad copy.
  • Finish with the exact closing words: "click below to get yours here."
  • The first one to two lines get most of the attention.
  • Open with one of these:
    • a bold claim,
    • a headline that names the benefit,
    • a question that names the viewer's pain point (the problem they have).
  • On mobile News Feed (the scrolling list of posts on a phone), only the first three lines show before the "see more" link.
  • This is older advice from 2021-22.
  • Put the key message and the reason to click in those first lines.
  • Keep paid social copy short, direct and in bullet points.
  • Include a clear call to action (CTA, the request that tells people what to do next, such as "Shop now").
  • Include an offer (the deal).
  • Very long text is hard to read on feeds such as Facebook.
  • Short, simple copy still works well for proven products.
  • Competitor ads with minimal text are a valid model.
  • Judge results by how the ad performs, not by how long the text is.
  • Test both long and short copy.
  • On many niches and products, longer copy often wins. This is older advice from 2021-22.
  • The opening line of a long Facebook ad must be interesting enough to make people click to read more.
  • Text past the visible start is cut off.
  • Formula for ad copy of a proven video (a video that already sold well).
    • Step 1: open with a surprised first-person line that names the buyer's hope.
    • Step 2: introduce the product by name as the answer.
    • Step 3: promise one result, without fuss.
    • Step 4: remove the main objection about feel or texture (the doubt a buyer has about how it feels).
    • Why it works: the surprise stops the reader. The named product makes the offer specific. Removing the doubt answers it before it forms.
    • Example one: "Thought I would never find a moisturizer that sinks in fast. Meet [PRODUCT], my go-to for softer skin without the greasy feel."
    • Example two: "Tired of serums that pill under makeup? Meet [PRODUCT], my secret to a smooth base without the heavy layer."
  • Pattern for a home decor ad (products for decorating a home).
    • Use an emotional benefit statement.
    • Add a call to the customer to build a space.
    • It sells the feeling of the product, not only its features.
    • This fits the ad's job of attracting clicks.
    • Example one: "Turn any room into a place you love with pieces that feel personal."
    • Example two: "Add warmth to your space with decor picked for everyday comfort."

Facebook copy generation procedure

1 min
  1. Identify your product name and the main customer problem it solves. Examples: acne problem, focus problem, skin dryness.
  2. Open ChatGPT (an AI chat tool) and use the Primary Text prompt. Fill in [INSERT PRODUCT HERE] and [NAME MAIN PROBLEM CUSTOMERS HAVE].
  3. Generate 2 primary texts from ChatGPT.
  4. Copy one or both primary texts into the primary text field of your Facebook ad.
  5. Add your website URL (the web address of your store) to the primary text field in the ad.
  6. Create two headlines.
    • Use the first headline for your offer. Example: "Buy 1 Get 1 Free Ends Midnight."
    • Generate the second headline with the Headline prompt in ChatGPT.
  7. Use the Description prompt in ChatGPT with your product name and customer problem.
  8. Copy one description from ChatGPT into the Facebook description field. It must be shorter than the headline.
  9. Check all three elements before you launch the ad.

Facebook copy generation example

1 min
  • Example: an organic hyaluronic acid serum powder for dry skin. Hyaluronic acid is an ingredient that holds moisture in skin.
    • Primary post: "I've been noticing my skin getting drier and duller lately, especially with the seasons changing. Switched to this organic hyaluronic acid serum powder, and my skin's honestly never felt more hydrated and smooth. Seriously loving the natural glow it's giving me! Click below to get yours here."
    • Headline: "Finally found something organic that actually keeps my skin hydrated all day. No more dry patches or dullness! Click below to get yours here."

Ever since I started using this organic hyaluronic acid serum powder, it's been noticeably softer, hydrated, and glowing naturally. Seriously, mixing it fresh each morning has been a game-changer."

Facebook headlines

1 min
  • Keep headlines short. They must read fully on a phone.
  • Place an emoji (a small picture symbol) only at the end.
  • Use an emoji only when it matches the event and the brand's tone.
  • An unrelated emoji adds noise, not attention.
  • Create two headlines with different purposes.
    • One holds your specific offer, such as "Buy 1 Get 1 Free Ends Midnight."
    • The other is generated with the headline prompt in ChatGPT.
  • Check each ChatGPT headline for the phrase "click below to get yours here."
  • If the phrase appears, remove it.
  • This closing belongs only in the primary text.
  • Headlines must stand alone as strong statements or offers.
  • The headline is the most visible text.
  • Include either a trust factor (proof the brand is safe) or an offer.
    • Trust factor examples: a number of happy customers, or a star rating such as 4.8 out of 5 from reviews.
    • Offer examples: free shipping, or buy one get one free.
  • Headline formula: [trust element or offer] + [short supporting detail].
  • Shoppers look for proof the brand is trustworthy, or for a concrete deal. One of these must show right away.
    • Own examples: "Over 2,500 five-star reviews" and "Free returns on every order."
  • Headlines show only on Facebook. They do not show on Instagram.
  • Social proof (signs that other people trust the product) for the description field. Use this format: "[Rating] out of 5 by [number] happy customers".
    • "4 out of 5 by 15,000 happy customers"
    • "[Decimal rating] out of 5 by [number] happy customers," for example "4.8 out of 5 by 15,000+"
    • "[Number] happy customers worldwide," with no star rating

Facebook descriptions

1 min
  • The description is shorter than the headline.
  • The platform display needs brevity, meaning short text.
  • The description sits under the headline in small type.
  • It should carry an offer or a scarcity signal (a sign that stock is limited).
    • Examples: "Limited time only" or "Get yours today."
  • Description formula: [urgency or scarcity phrase] + [action to take now].
  • An urgency line gives a reason to click right away.
  • Keep it to a few words.
    • Own examples: "Only 48 hours left at this price" and "Order before this batch sells out."
  • Keep the description very short.
  • Use one benefit, feature or trust line.
    • Examples: free shipping, or 24/7 support (help available at any time).
  • Longer text gets cut off.
  • Conflict on whether the description shows.
    • One source says it is never shown on Facebook or Instagram, so it does not need filling in.
    • Another says it is a visible line that carries the offer or social proof.
  • Check the Advanced preview (a view of every placement, meaning every spot where the ad can appear) before you decide.

Facebook ad copy structure

1 min

Use this structure in order.

  1. Headline: a pain point, a controversial statement, a question to the customer, or a big claim such as a rating or ranking.
    • The product name can also work as the headline.
  2. Product introduction line.
  3. Benefits: two benefits and one feature. A feature is a fact about the product, such as its size or material.
  4. Trust factor: a customer count or the accepted payment methods.
  5. Star rating: four star icons, followed by a rating such as 4.8 out of 5 from 1000+ customers.
  6. Offer plus scarcity: a discount with a limit, such as 50% off for the next 50 orders.
  7. Call to action: Shop now, with the link.

Trust labels and call to action

1 min
  • Trust labels are small badges under the ad.
  • To add them, open Edit info in the ad and click Create new label.
  • Add lines such as free shipping, free returns, PayPal accepted, Klarna, Google Pay or Apple Pay.
  • The labels appear under the ad before the customer clicks.
  • Call to action button: Shop now.
  • Destination: the product page URL, not the home page.
  • The product page holds the product information the viewer needs after clicking.
  • Use Shop Now as the call-to-action button for standard sales ads.
  • For product retargeting (showing ads to people who already looked at a product), the CTA is Shop for product retargeting.
  • It does not appear in every placement. Use it where it shows.
  • Add a strong offer, such as 40% off, near the start of a video to grab attention.
  • Ad copy for a proven video's link card (the box under the video that holds the link).
    • Put a bestseller label in the headline.
    • Add a Shop now button.
    • Add a time-limited discount line, such as "50 percent off today."
  • These three elements together push the click at the end of the ad.
  • Use a real sales rank or a real offer.

Ad setup in Meta Ads Manager

2 min

Meta Ads Manager is the tool where Facebook and Instagram ads are built.

  • Creative setup: under Set up creative, add the headline in the Headline field.
    • The "Add headline" option lets you add more variations.
    • Add the primary text in its own field.
    • The ad preview shows how the ad looks in Feeds (the main scrolling list), Stories (full-screen vertical posts) and Reels (short vertical videos).
  • Media picker (the window for choosing an image or video): under Ad creative, the source tabs are Media from you and Media from AI.
    • Business, Instagram and Page media appear under the Account and Page tabs.
    • Only one image or video can be selected per ad.
    • A search field filters the media library.
  • Text section:
    • Primary text (a line explaining what the ad is about).
    • Headline.
    • Description (extra detail, optional).
    • A Call to action button.
  • Several headline options let Meta show the version it predicts will perform best.
  • Creative testing (comparing several versions of the ad) can compare up to 5 creative versions.
  • This is not needed for a warm-up, the first phase when a new ad is started.
  • Text & Links fields:
    • Primary text (main copy).
    • Headline (short, optional).
    • Description (additional details, optional).
  • The Destination is Website.
  • The Website URL must be the landing page, the page people arrive on.
  • The display link is the web address shown on the ad. It must match the website URL.
  • Settings > Ad > Languages: an ad can be created in several languages.
  • Facebook shows each viewer the version that suits them best.
  • Add a language version when the store serves more than one language.
  • Multiple text options: the primary text and headline fields can hold several options.
  • Meta shows the option it predicts will perform best.
  • For full control over the copy, add only one primary text.
  • Skip Meta's generated variations if you want full control.
  • Facebook's AI-suggested copy variations are optional.
  • Use them only once they look good on their own.
  • Ad preview: the preview on the right shows the ad as it will look on Facebook.
    • It includes the catalog (the product list) below the ad when enabled.
    • Advanced preview shows every placement the ad may run on.
    • Check it before publishing.

Placements, crop, video length and previews

2 min

A placement is a spot where the ad appears, such as Feeds, Stories or Reels.

  • Crop means cutting the image to fit a shape.
    • In Ad > Creative > Crop, choose 1:1 or 9:16 for the placements that need it.
    • 1:1 is square. 9:16 is tall, like a phone screen.
    • If Original is kept, the image may be cropped automatically. This is unreliable.
    • To control the crop, upload a separate 9:16 version with Replace in the vertical slot.
    • Horizontal formats are rarely shown. You can ignore them.
  • Primary text position: on Facebook the primary text appears above the ad.
  • On Instagram it appears below the ad.
  • Carousel (several cards you swipe sideways): in a Meta carousel ad, the primary text appears above the cards on Facebook.
  • In a carousel, the primary text appears below the cards on Instagram.
  • One primary text entry serves both placements.
  • The preview shows how it appears on each.
  • Carousel closing card: the destination URL from the final ad setup step is also used for an extra closing card at the end.
  • A click on that card takes the viewer to the store.
  • Ad copy length on Meta: keep it to three lines, because Meta shows only three.
  • Use emojis, a clear offer and a direct link.
  • Video length cap: some placements, such as Instagram, cap video length at 15 seconds.
  • A 30-second video still publishes, but it will not show in that placement.
  • Make shorter cuts.
  • Make both vertical and landscape (wide) versions.
  • Story aspect ratio warning: if Facebook or TikTok Ads Manager shows a story aspect ratio warning under automatic placements, you can ignore it.
  • You can ignore it when the video follows the recommended format.
  • Copy space by platform: Google Search ads (text ads on Google search results) have limited space. They need short, concise copy.
  • Facebook ads give substantially more space. This allows longer paragraphs, detailed benefit explanations and customer review quotes.
  • Adjust copy length, depth of explanation and proof to each platform's limits.

Facebook ad testing structure

1 min
  • An ad set is a group of ads that share one audience and one budget.
  • Inside one ad set, create several ads with different images and text.
  • This lets you split test them, meaning compare them against each other.
  • Each ad holds its own image, headline and link text.
  • The ad set keeps the audience and budget.
  • During testing, use one ad copy version per ad.
  • Product test structure: read the 5 headlines and 5 primary texts the prompt makes.
  • Do not use all five.
  • Pick the two headlines and two primary texts that feel strongest.
  • Product test entry: enter the two headlines and two primary texts into the ad.
  • Add one description.
  • Paste the landing page URL as the website URL.
  • Select Shop Now as the CTA.
  • Only one description is needed per ad in a product test.
  • Add it alongside the two headlines and two primary texts.
  • Conflict on copy per ad.
    • One rule says use one copy version per ad, when each version must be read on its own.
    • A product test puts two headlines and two primary texts into one ad.
    • Choose the structure that fits the test goal.
    • A single primary text gives full control and skips Meta's selection.
  • Image ads and carousel ads can perform as well as video ads on Facebook.
  • Test them as a separate format next to the winning video ads.
  • Image ads with a short text overlay (words on the picture) and an emoji were reported to work well for a brand.
  • When a proven video ad is the source, keep the proven video.
  • Add a call to action, urgency text (words that create time pressure) and other conversion elements (things that push a sale).
  • Change only what is needed for conversion.

Facebook audience-matched copy

1 min
  • A creative is the ad's image, video and text.
  • Facebook reads the text and the context of the video or image.
  • It includes objects it detects in the visual, to decide who sees the ad.
  • Creatives should be relevant to the target audience.
  • A narrow interest may need messages that clearly match it.
  • Creatives should speak to the target audience directly.
  • Name who they are, or the specific problem they face.
  • Facebook uses the visual context and text to match the ad to the right audience.
  • Pattern for niche creative and copy: name the specific audience and its specific problem or situation in the ad.
  • Show that situation in the video or image.
  • A broad message to a broad audience performs worse than one for a narrow group.
    • Example for a self-defense product: "Heading out on a late-night walk? Keep a personal alarm in your pocket."
    • Example for a pet hair brush: "Shedding dog on your bed every morning? This brush pulls out the thick German Shepherd undercoat."
  • Interest targeting means choosing people by their hobbies or interests. Broad targeting means a wide audience with no interest filter.
  • When budgets are small, test a specific interest, for example camping, snowboarding, pets, or home and garden.
  • Make the audience smaller.
  • Make sure the ad clearly matches the interest.
  • In the case described, broad targeting was not working. Interest-specific targeting performed better.

TikTok ad copy

2 min
  • Creatives must show what is being sold.
  • TikTok users need a clear hook (an opening that grabs attention).
  • They also need an explanation of the offer.
  • Good TikTok creatives explain product features through sound and visual effects.
  • They use no on-screen text, no talking presenter and no sales argument.
  • This satisfying, fun style makes viewers feel entertained, not sold to.
  • Native pattern (an ad that fits the look of the feed). A newspaper-style analogy shows that traditional bold ad formatting gets skipped.
  • An advertorial (an ad that looks like useful content) holds attention. The viewer realizes it is an ad only later.
  • Make the ad match the native look and feel of the feed.
  • Provide value first.
    • Example for a kitchen gadget: a 10-second clip of a slicing-hack sequence with sound and no text, showing the product working.
    • Example for a pet accessory: a quick "what your dog does when you leave" scene that reveals the product as the answer at the end.
  • Ad copy pattern: a four-to-five-word phrase.
  • Take the phrase from how a customer would describe the main benefit, in the customer's own words.
  • It matches the native, conversational tone.
  • It names the concrete result the buyer wants.
    • Examples: "My back finally stopped aching" for a posture corrector; "Sleep feels different now" for a sleep mask.
  • Offer-led text: a clear discount plus an urgency phrase.
    • Example: a percent-off message and a limited-time notice.
    • Example patterns: "Save 40% today only" or "Free shipping ends tonight."
  • Short video ad text: name the product, add a short offer with its price, and keep the call to action simple.
  • The price sits in the text, so viewers understand the offer in one line.
    • Examples: "Portable blender for only 39.99" and "Foot massager for only 49.99."
  • Ad copy formula: an offer line that states the discount and a call to action.
    • Example: a percent-off figure followed by "get yours today" or "ending soon."
    • The discount and urgency are the first things a TikTok viewer reads.
    • The line fits the ad text limit.
    • Examples: "40 percent off, shop today before the sale ends" and "50 percent off this week, get yours now."
  • Tone: use a fun, relatable tone in ad text and reactions.
    • Examples: a funny quote, or a customer's reaction after receiving the product.
    • Use this instead of only a discount message.
    • Offer messages still work well and are common.
  • Thumbnail (the still image shown before the video plays).
  • A human face smiling has worked better than a product image or store graphic in tests.
  • The face need not be tied to the product.
  • The brand name and a logo should stay visible.
  • Reuse across platforms: a TikTok ad that works on TikTok can usually be reused on Facebook and Instagram placements.
  • A Facebook ad generally does not perform on TikTok.
  • When expanding to other platforms, use TikTok-native creatives as the source.

TikTok duplicate content and freshness

1 min
  • TikTok shows duplicate content to fewer people. Duplicate content means the same or nearly the same video, posted again.
  • Reach means how many people see your post.
  • TikTok treats ads like ordinary videos.
  • Creatives (the ads themselves) that were already posted a lot, or have run for a long time, lose reach.
  • So new or freshly uploaded videos are preferred.

TikTok Spark Ads

1 min
  • Spark Ads are TikTok ads that boost a normal post from your own account.
  • A post is "organic" when nobody pays to show it.
  • A Spark Ad can lower that post's organic reach. So use posts that are roughly 20 to 30 days old or older.
  • Recent viral organic posts are better left out of paid tests.
  • In tests, Spark Ads performed better than regular TikTok ads. So test both formats on the same product.
  • Spark Ads are the preferred option when you run ads from an organic account.
  • Spark Ads keep the comment filters set on the account.
  • Comment filters are rules that hide or flag certain comments.
  • Comments on traditional TikTok ads are harder to monitor.
  • So filter negative comments on the account before you run ads.
  • A Spark Ad was rejected by TikTok, meaning TikTok refused to run it.
  • Afterwards, organic views on the linked account (the account the ad is connected to) dropped below 600 and stayed low.
  • Rejected ads may coincide with a drop in organic reach. So review the rejection before testing more ads from the same account.

Instagram Reels and Trial Reels

1 min
  • Reels are Instagram's short vertical videos.
  • For Reels repurposed (reused) from TikTok, add a call to action (a line that tells the viewer what to do), such as Shop Now.
  • Also make the text more ad-like near the end of the video.
  • The owner reported no account penalty on Instagram.
  • TikTok was more sensitive to reports (complaints from viewers).
  • Trial Reels is a test area for Reels.
  • It works as a low-risk testing ground for content styles.
  • You can remove a Trial Reel from the profile grid (the photo wall on your page) afterwards. This keeps a polished brand page looking good.
  • Creators also use it to test many hooks (the first seconds that grab attention) before pushing an ad on Facebook.
  • When Instagram is a main source of income, the ad can link to the Instagram page instead of the store.
  • The goal is for customers to check out the brand page first.
  • This is an exception to the usual approach of linking directly to the store.

Pinterest audience and targeting

2 min
  • Pinterest has 400+ million users.
  • 80% of Pinterest users have bought items on the platform before.
  • Pinterest's main audience is women aged 30 and older.
  • This group is the majority, so it fits products for women best.
  • Pinterest needs 7 to 15 days to optimize (learn what works and improve).
  • Expect very high CPM (cost per 1,000 views) and poor results in the first 2 to 3 days while the platform tests.
  • Not all products work on Pinterest.
  • The platform works best for niches (narrow markets) linked to DIY (do-it-yourself), crafts, and inspiration content.

Audience size:

  • Use stacked interests, meaning several specific topics that fit the product and the target audience together.
  • Pick seven to ten specific sub-interests (or five to twenty). A sub-interest is a narrow topic inside a broad one.
  • Do not use only broad parent categories (wide main topics).
  • Keep audience size between 5 and 10 million as the sweet spot (the best range).
  • Up to 30 million is acceptable. Above 30 million is too big.
  • Select individual sub-interests, not only the parent interest.
  • Pinterest reports which interest drove sales only for individually selected interests.
  • The breakdown view (the report that splits results by interest) then shows which interest is performing.

Demographics (age, gender and location of the people you target):

  • If only women fit the product, select female only.
  • An age range of 21+ is good. 25+ was also reported to work well.
  • Location can be all US or a specific country.
  • Language and device restrictions are not needed.

Optimization and delivery:

  • Select Checkout as the conversion event. A conversion event is the action the ad tries to get.
  • Also select automatic bidding, which means the platform sets the price for you.

Campaigns:

  • Keep keywords and interests in separate campaigns (separate ad setups).
  • Keywords are search words people type.
  • Interests are topics people follow.
  • Start with interest-based targeting (showing ads by topics people follow).
  • Test keyword targeting (showing ads to people who search certain words) only after the product is confirmed as a winner (a product that sells well).

Keywords:

  • Pinterest changed its search results. Keywords now appear as separate, segmented items, meaning each keyword stands as its own group.
  • This makes the platform more advertiser-friendly.
  • Promoted pin targeting (paid pins shown to people) is built on these keyword segments.
  • So choose keywords that match the exact terms users search for.

Pinterest creative

1 min
  • A CTR (click-through rate) is the share of viewers who click.
  • Pinterest creatives should be TikTok-style vertical video.
  • Use the 9:16 format (tall, like a phone screen).
  • Keep the video 15 to 20 seconds long. 20 seconds is seen as best.
  • Use quick transitions (fast cuts between scenes) within one to two seconds.
  • Show the product in use within the first three to five seconds.
  • In tests, such clips reached over 3 percent CTR.
  • In tests across beauty, health, home decor and home improvement, video beat static images (still pictures).
  • Fashion may be an exception, where images can work.
  • Creative can be reused for six or seven months, because there is little saturation (an ad getting stale from being seen too often) on Pinterest.
  • In tests, TikTok-style videos produced the best CTR. Simple image pins and standard pins performed worse.
  • Pinterest users recognize product images immediately, so the product must be visible.

Pinterest ad setup and copy

1 min
  • A pin is a Pinterest post.
  • When creating a Pinterest ad, click Select pins or Create pin, and upload a video from your computer.
  • Use the same headline as the TikTok ads as the title.
  • Add a short description of three to four lines. It should name the pain point (the problem the buyer has) and the benefit.
  • End the description with a call to action, such as "flawless skin is just a click away."
  • The description can be generated with ChatGPT (an AI writing tool).

Creating a video pin at the ad level:

  1. Click Select pins.
  2. If you have no existing pins, click Create pin.
  3. Upload the video from your computer.
  4. Enter the ad headline in the title field (the same one used for TikTok ads).
  5. Enter the ad text in the description field.

Pattern for Pinterest ad description copy:

  • Write three to four lines that name a pain point, state the product benefit, and end with a call to action.
  • You can optionally finish with a short benefit-driven line.
    • Example one: "Tired of frizzy hair after every wash? This comb smooths in one pass, so tap now for salon-smooth results at home."
    • Example two: "Sore feet at the end of the day? These insoles add cushioning under every step, so shop today and walk easier tomorrow."

YouTube ads

1 min
  • YouTube ads need studio-quality creatives (polished, professional-looking).
  • The hook (the first moment that grabs attention) should come within the first six seconds.
  • Videos that work on TikTok or Facebook often do not perform the same way on YouTube.
  • Production polish (how well the video is made) is not a must.
  • YouTube video ads can be made cheaply, even with a phone and a plain setup. In that setup, the presenter stands in front of the product and talks.
  • Authenticity (being real, not staged) can outperform high production values.
  • A phone-shot video of a person talking in front of a product, with a near-zero production budget, can become one of the most-watched YouTube ads of its kind.
  • Simple YouTube ad formula: a product video with product promotion, aimed at a retargeting list (people you already reached who have not purchased yet).
  • Retargeting means showing ads again to people who already know you.
  • Add a top-of-funnel video (the first step of the path from stranger to buyer), aimed at competitor fans (people who follow rival brands).

Demand Gen ads

1 min
  • Demand Gen is an ad type that pairs headlines with images.
  • Informational images (pictures that teach something) should be paired with a headline that matches the image.
  • The image's job is to create curiosity, not to give the answer.

Native ad networks

1 min
  • Native ad networks, such as Taboola, place advertorials (ads written like articles) on large news sites.
  • Meta ads means ads run by Meta, the company behind Facebook and Instagram.
  • Native ad networks are a separate channel from Meta ads.

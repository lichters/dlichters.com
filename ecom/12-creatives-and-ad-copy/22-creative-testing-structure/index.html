Creative testing structure

Section 12.22 · 22 of 26 in this chapter · 26 min read

In short

  • Make many ads and test them often, because only a few will work.
  • A weak ad can make a good product look bad, so test new ads before you drop a product.
  • Test one thing at a time, starting with the hook (the first 3 to 5 seconds).
  • Find winning audiences first. Then test new ads on those audiences.
  • Put at least 15 percent of your weekly ad budget into testing.
More key points
  • Test one thing at a time. Keep the offer fixed while you test ad images and videos.
  • Start with simple still images. They are fast to make, so test them before you make video.
  • A winner needs at least 3 real sales and enough spend. One sale can be luck.
  • Compare ads by ROAS first, then CTR, then how long people watch.
  • Keep testing new ideas. Reuse each winner in new tests.

Why creative testing comes first

2 min
  • A creative is the ad itself: an image, a video or a carousel (several pictures in one ad). Creative testing means trying several ads to see which ones work. It is one of the largest parts of scaling paid ads, whether you do it yourself or hire someone.
  • Make many creatives and test them continually. Only a small share of them will work.
  • A weak creative can make a product look like a failure, even when the product works. Test new creatives before you decide a product is not viable.
  • Structured testing frameworks (fixed plans for tests) during the ads phase keep your decisions based on results, not opinion.
  • Testing many creatives early matters more than the size of the budget.
  • For example, test about double the number of creatives on the first day. Then add budget only to the creative that looks promising.
  • Creatives are usually tested for the first time around week three.
  • A creative that fails tells you what to change. It is not a final verdict on the product.
  • Small budgets do not give enough data to judge an ad.
  • Decide quickly on what performs early. Waiting for a month of data at 50 per day is not practical for most sellers.
  • Creative testing often needs enough spend for the algorithm (the automatic system that decides who sees your ad) to learn.
  • At very low daily budgets, such as 5 per day, controlling spend manually gives faster, clearer results.
  • Simple homemade or cropped creatives can beat professionally shot ones. Appearance does not reliably predict performance.
  • Decide which creative stays by the data and the sales it brings.
  • Unusual or imperfect designs can beat polished ads.
  • Test unusual looks, even when they do not match typical advertising polish.
  • Test different concepts, such as humour, education, before-and-after and shock. Let audience results decide.
  • Launching quickly and improving based on feedback costs less than planning one polished ad and launching it late.
  • Test creatives aimed at different types of audience. You cannot know in advance which audience will respond.
  • Find the creative that works, then scale it (raise its budget).
  • Combine educated guesses about why an ad should work with tests of different angles (the reason or problem the ad talks about). Psychology alone cannot predict results.
  • Buyer context often only becomes clear once an ad is live. Launching and testing usually beats waiting for a perfect creative.
  • Test formats on your own product before you commit budget to one type.
  • In one fashion store, image ads made most of the revenue, while video ads did not work.
  • A product that splits its audience can be tested with a creative that deliberately provokes a clear reaction.
  • The mix of positive and negative responses shows which buyers lean toward the product.

When to start testing

1 min
  • Start creative testing when click-through rate (CTR) is poor. CTR is the share of people who click the ad after seeing it. Weak CTR means you need new creatives right away.
  • Also start once a product is a consistent seller (it sells steadily) and has been profitable for more than two to three weeks.
  • Start creative testing only after two weeks of consistent profitable results, with two or three winning audiences.
  • Early in a product test, focus on finding winning audiences first.
  • Before you launch a test, have these ready:
    1. A finished landing page (the page people see after they click).
    2. 4 to 5 creatives (images, videos or carousels).
    3. Ready ad copy (the written text around the ad).
  • Move to hook testing only after you get consistent profitable sales with ripped creatives. A "ripped" creative is a proven ad that you copy and adapt. At that point you have a validated angle, avatar (the type of buyer you target) and product.
  • Hook testing comes after ripped creatives prove profitable. Its aim is to raise CTR.
  • Starting it earlier mixes the creative variable with the product test.
  • Start creative testing when a product is already a consistent seller. Use the audience stage first to find where it sells.

Test structure: audiences first, then creatives

6 min
  • Stage 1 is audience testing for a new product.
    1. Make three to four videos for the product.
    2. Run them in an audience testing campaign across several audiences, such as interests, countries and age groups. An interest is a topic people follow, used to choose who sees the ad.
    3. Goal: find two to three winning audiences. Their results should show that the product sells and the message reaches the right people.
    4. Once you have two to three winners, move to the creative testing campaign below.
  • Creative testing campaign setup, using ABO with the Sales objective. ABO means each ad set gets its own budget. The Sales objective tells the platform your goal is sales.
    1. Create an ABO campaign (Sales objective) for creative testing. An ad set is a group of ads that share an audience and budget.
    2. Add three ad sets. Each targets a proven winning audience. For example: one winning interest, one lookalike winner (new people who look like your buyers), and one more consistent winner.
    3. Put the same three video variations in each ad set.
    4. The variations differ only in the first 3 to 5 seconds. This opening is the hook, the part that stops people scrolling. The rest of the video stays identical.
    5. Run the test for 2 to 3 days to collect enough data.
    6. Choose the winner by ROAS (return on ad spend: money earned per money spent on ads) first. If ROAS is equal, use CTR. If CTR is also equal, use average play time.
    7. Use the winning creative in audience testing campaigns, CBO scaling campaigns, and wherever new audiences are tested. CBO means one budget shared across the ad sets, and the platform decides where it goes.
    8. Reuse the winning creative for audiences that are dying down (audiences that stop responding over time).
  • Separate campaigns: run an interest testing campaign (winning creatives, new interests) and a creative testing campaign (winning interest, new creatives). This way a new creative gets its own budget.
  • Creative testing in a separate campaign, with a new CBO or ABO campaign.
    1. Pick two or three interests that perform well right now.
    2. Place them in a new CBO or ABO test campaign with two ad sets.
    3. Test three new creatives in each ad set.
  • Duplicate the winning ad set so its audience and placements (where the ad appears) stay identical. Then test new creatives in the duplicate.
    1. Duplicate the winning ad set so its audience and placements stay identical. To duplicate means to copy.
    2. Put the duplicate into a new creative testing campaign.
    3. Change only the launch date and replace the videos with the new creatives.
    4. Keep the targeting unchanged. Then any difference in results comes from the creative, not the audience.
    5. Run the test and compare the hook or creative variants inside the same audience.
  • Test new creatives on a winning audience.
    1. Take the winning ad sets with the best audience, age, gender and interest.
    2. Create a separate campaign with the new creatives. Compare CTR.
    3. Move the best-performing creatives into the running ad sets.
    4. Turn off ads that spend 20 to 30 USD without sales. Keep the ones that produce sales.
  • Keep new creatives out of ad sets that contain the old winner. Facebook and TikTok send most of the budget to the creative with the most data. This hides how well new creatives would perform.
  • Facebook creative testing structure.
    • Keep the winning audience and copy.
    • Change only one variable at a time: the hook, the creative or the creator (the person in the ad).
    • Put each new creative in its own ad set. Or put several new creatives together in one ad set so they compete.
    • Do not mix them with the old winner.
  • TikTok launch structure.
    • One winning ad set and winning creative can be duplicated into a new campaign.
    • Test new creatives in a separate ad set. This stops budget going to the old winner.
  • Standard Pinterest test structure: one campaign, one ad group (a Pinterest word for ad set) with stacked interests (several interests grouped together), and three creatives. Let it run for at least five to seven days, and let the platform optimise (adjust itself to get better results).
  • Structure each test as one campaign, one ad group and three creatives. If one creative is rejected, two others still give a valid test.
  • Creative testing setup based on a winning interest.
    1. Find the winning interest or audience first.
    2. Duplicate that ad set, for example three times, keeping the same interest.
    3. Keep only one ad (creative) in each ad set.
    4. Compare which creative has the highest CTR.
    5. Only increase the budget once a winning audience is found. Then scale the winning campaign by raising its budget.
  • Keep each angle in its own column. Do not mix problem types inside one column. A column here means a separate test group.
  • Testing angles separately shows which problem drives clicks and purchases. Empty columns are kept for future angles.
  • Full setup for a Meta CBO test ad. Meta is the company that runs Facebook and Instagram.
    1. In the ad set, select Advantage+ creative (Meta's automatic setting for ad style). Choose the ad format Single image or video. Leave Multi-advertiser ads unchecked. This option would show your ad next to other brands' ads.
    2. Upload 6 to 10 creatives. The target for a first round is about 9 creatives across ad sets.
    3. Choose the Facebook Page and the Instagram account.
    4. Confirm the website URL points to the correct product page.
    5. Press Next. Then use the regenerate option on the primary text and headline Meta generated, to draft copy.
    6. Paste the copy from your external prompt (a prompt written outside Meta) into the ad's primary text and headlines. Check the spacing and line breaks.
    7. Press Publish to put the ad live.
  • Keep ads per ad set small, about three to four creatives. This keeps spend per creative under control, so you can read each result.
  • Facebook is good at choosing the best creative for a specific audience. Several options give it more chances to work.
  • Reuse the same set of three to four ads across ad sets with the same goal. Meta then tests them and spends on the best one. A single ad is never enough.
  • Full setup needs about 13 ads in total across the campaigns.
  • Lead generation (collecting contact details) and promotions need 3 to 4 ads each.
  • The goodwill campaign (a campaign to build trust, not sell) needs one video.
  • Each sandbox test (a small, separate test setup) needs two ads.
  • For a clean creative test, place each of four creatives in its own ad set, with about 5 to 15 dollars of spend per ad.
  • This costs more, but it shows which creative wins on CTR and CPC (cost per click: what one click costs).
  • In one ad set with four ads, one creative can take most of the budget. Then the result is harder to read.
  • Do not blindly trust Facebook's spend allocation.
  • If a creative gets little spend, duplicate the ad set into its own ad set. Force spend onto it for a test period, for example three days, before you decide it is weak.
  • Standard test per product uses about three creatives: one or two videos and one or two pictures.
  • Test one interest set at a time. For per-ad-set amounts, see the budgets section below.
  • Test each new creative with about 10 to 20 dollars of spend in its own single-creative ad set. This shows how each one performs on its own.
  • Combine creatives in a shared ad set only after each one has shown its results separately.

Isolating variables: one change per test

1 min
  • Test one variable at a time. Start with the hook, because it is the most important variable at the start. Thumbnails (the picture shown before a video plays), overall style and ad copy come later.
  • Keep each level above the one being tested fixed. Then only one variable changes at a time.
  • Creative testing works as a chain:
    1. An ad angle becomes a concept (the storyline).
    2. The concept becomes one script (the words and scenes).
    3. The script gets three hook variations.
    4. Those hook variations run as ads.
  • Static ads means image ads and carousel ads.
  • A concept can be built as either a video or a static ad. The concept is the angle or script, not the format.
  • Keep a control ad (an unchanged ad used for comparison) with the same interest, copy and hook. Change only one element, such as the mid-section. Then any lift (improvement) is caused by that change.
  • During creative testing, keep the offer and ad copy identical across all ads. Change only the video, clip or creator. The creative is then the only variable.
  • Change only one variable at a time. In creative tests, keep the targeting identical to the proven winning audience. Then the difference in performance comes from the creative, not the audience.
  • Test one variable at a time. Keep audience, creative, copy and targets identical. Change only the offer. Then a conversion uplift (more buyers) can be attributed to the offer.

Hook, copy, thumbnail and angle testing

5 min
  • Each new video should come with about three hook variations. Only the first five seconds differ. Meta then optimises the combinations.
  • Test hooks by making one video with three different first 3 to 5 seconds. Keep the rest identical.
  • Get hook ideas from what is trending on TikTok.
  • For image ads, make three images around the same marketing angle.
  • Ad copy angle testing: keep the winning video and hook identical. Change only the ad copy. Each version speaks to a different motivation within the same winning audiences.
    • Formula: one copy per audience motivation, with the video held constant.
    • Example 1: for a camping product, one copy focuses on night-time visibility and another on gadget features.
    • Example 2: for a fitness product, one copy focuses on saving time and another on feeling confident during workouts.
  • Ad copy matters more once several audiences need different angles.
  • Test ad copy only after spending more than about 1,000 per day on testing.
  • While you test copy, keep the video and hook identical.
  • Thumbnails and ad copy are tested separately from the video.
    1. Keep the same winning video. Vary only the thumbnail (thumbnail 1, 2, 3) to find the thumbnail winner. Thumbnails are the first thing customers see, and they can raise CTR.
    2. Keep the same video and thumbnail. Vary the ad copy (ad copy 1, 2, 3) to find the ad copy winner.
  • Testing a new angle on a video that did not get the expected views.
    1. Pick a good video idea that did not perform.
    2. Add a hook that has already worked for you in the past.
    3. Publish the new version and compare its results with the previous one.
  • Find the strongest marketing angle without a large budget.
    1. Pick one marketing angle.
    2. Make a creative for that angle alone. Or use only its first 5 to 10 seconds as the hook, and add UGC-style clips (a homemade look, like a customer's own video).
    3. Test each angle's creative separately on Facebook.
    4. Once one angle clearly wins, add a landing page section for it. Move the other sections down.
  • Creative testing by splitting a winning ad.
    1. Split a winning ad into separate features or benefits, one per creative.
    2. Run them for a few days.
    3. Pick the best-performing problem or angle.
    4. Create new creatives around that problem.
    5. Keep improving existing ads and test new concepts continually.
  • Creative testing as a test of four creative types against interest audiences.
  • Test a winning angle in video before you scale it.
  • Launch VSL ads (video sales letter: a longer video that sells) first. Launch static image ads afterwards. VSLs convert better and test the full message.
  • The winning angle is then turned into static image ads.
  • Replace one element of a Google text ad at a time. Iterative testing (small, step-by-step changes) isolates single changes. It shows which word or phrase your audience prefers.
  • Only one element varies. All other copy stays identical. Performance shifts then show what caused them.
  • Examples: "Join the Community" versus "Become a Member" (different verb, same offer), or "Learn More" versus "Discover More" (same action, different tone).
  • Evolutionary text ad testing uses fundamentally different messages. For example, lead with a customer pain point, or lead with the solution benefit. This shows which overall approach converts best.
  • Examples: "Tired of Back Pain" (pain-point focus) versus "Professional Spinal Support" (solution-forward). Or "Don't Get Left Behind" (urgency) versus "Trust the Industry Leader" (authority and quality).
  • Iterative testing changes only one element per test. Keep everything else identical. For example, change the call to action from "Buy now" to "Shop now".
  • Iterative testing is worth doing, but it produces only small differences.
  • Evolutionary testing compares two very different ad styles. For example, a question ("Struggling with acne?") against a direct statement ("The acne solution").
  • On Google text ads, evolutionary tests usually produce bigger gains than tweaking single words. So test new concepts more often.
  • Test sales copy on real members of the target market before launch.
    1. Give the copy to 3 to 5 people who fit the buyer profile and do not know the product.
    2. Ask each reader to say in their own words what the product does and what they should do next.
    3. Mark every sentence where the reader hesitates, misreads or asks a question. Rewrite those sentences with simpler words and one claim each.
  • Free pretest for a sales letter, in order. A pretest is a check before launch.
    1. Read the letter aloud. Fix any tongue twisters or awkward phrases.
    2. Read it to several people who match the target customer, possibly in a casual setting. Watch whether they ask how to buy. That signals the approach works.
    3. Have a young reader read it aloud. Change any words or phrases they struggle with.
    4. Check whether typical customers have questions the letter leaves unanswered. If so, add a page that answers them.
  • Test a voiceover against text-only overlays before you commit. A voiceover (spoken audio) reaches people who cannot read the screen. Text reaches people who cannot hear.
  • Many text-only ads perform well.
  • Case: a text-only ad with rain overlays and heavy effects reached about 2.5 ROAS and was treated as a failure.
  • A simple UGC-style ad with plain text overlays and a voiceover reached 4 to 6 ROAS.
  • Subtitles: when a video from abroad is strong but not in the local language, add subtitles if the creative is good.
  • Otherwise, create or hire a similar one.
  • Image ads were the main format used for testing new products.
  • Testing AI presenters for a proven product. An AI presenter is a computer-made person who speaks in the ad.
    1. Take an ad angle and offer from a brand whose ads have run for a long time.
    2. Create AI presenter versions of the same ad. Give each a different avatar type, for example a doctor, an everyday woman, or a man.
    3. Keep the product, script and offer identical. Then only the presenter changes.
    4. Let the versions run and compare their results.
    5. Scale the avatar that performs best and stop the rest.
  • Test platform-specific hooks. A hook here is a strong opening line, title or caption.
    1. Write two or more strong hooks, titles or captions for the same video.
    2. Publish each variant on a different platform. For example: variant A on TikTok, variant B on Instagram, and a third variant on YouTube.
    3. Compare the results per platform.
    4. Adjust the messaging for each platform based on what performed best there.

Offer testing

1 min
  • Keep the offer out of the ad creatives (the ad images and videos) while you test. An offer is the deal you promise, such as a discount or a free gift.
  • The offer changes often.
  • Once a winning offer is found, add it to the ads.
  • Every offer change then means updating the ads too.
  • Test offers with simple static creatives. Static means still images with no motion.
  • Examples of offers to test: buy one get one free, a free gift, or 40 percent off.
  • The goal is to see which offer responds best across markets (countries) and products.
  • Image creatives can be built fast from templates (ready-made layouts).
  • Test image creatives before you make video creatives.

Format choice: static before video

3 min
  • Video takes longer to produce.
  • Use video once a static layout has shown it converts. "Converts" means it brings buyers.
  • When product footage (raw video of your product) is limited, run quick image ads first.
  • Quick image ads show which market responds.
  • Then make one full video only for the winning market.
  • If footage is good enough for a clear story (narrative), use it. But the hook and the offer come first. The hook is the opening that stops people from scrolling past.
  • Use simple image ads with product pictures. Tools include an AI tool, Photoshop, or a freelance graphic designer (a person you hire).
  • The goal is to test quickly before committing to video.
  • Test formats on your own product before committing budget (money) to one type.
  • Swipe file creatives (ad layouts copied from a collection of proven examples) have a full testing workflow.
  1. Choose a product and find a matching swipe file layout. Layout examples: Them vs Us, testimonial (a customer quote), benefit callout (a highlighted benefit), meme, or offer.
  2. Replace the text with your branding, product benefits and offer.
  3. Swap in your product cut-out (the product with its background removed). Then adjust colours and gradients (smooth blends of colour).
  4. Publish the variants as ads. Compare which layout performs best for each product.
  5. Scale the winning layout (spend more on it). Build video versions only after the static test shows a clear winner.
  • Some concepts do not show the product at all. Example: a close-up of two faces, one showing a problem and one showing a solution.
  • Test these concepts when competitors use this style successfully in search.
  • Test such images against product-visible versions. Do not replace the product versions with them.
  • Replacing the image of a working Shopping listing (a product ad in Google's shopping results) without a split test can lose performance you already earned. A split test runs two versions side by side.
  • Duplicate the listing first, so the new image competes against the original.
  • Study competitors' ads in the Meta Ad Library (the public archive of Facebook and Instagram ads).
  • Note the hooks that repeat across their ads.
  • Note the ad copy (the written text of the ad).
  • Note the story structure.
  • Reused TikTok-style video pins on Pinterest held a CTR (click-through rate, the share of people who click) of roughly 3 to 4 percent for about a month.
  • That is well above the 1 percent day-three minimum. This is the lowest CTR allowed on day three, and it works as a go or no-go gate (a pass or fail check).
  • Simple video formats: a new Spark Ad test on a new account. A Spark Ad boosts one of your organic posts (normal, unpaid posts).
  1. Choose the best organic video.
  2. Use no interest targeting (no choosing of topics people like). Use only an age range such as 18 to 45, depending on the product.
  3. Let about 20 dollars of spend run completely.
  4. Do not reopen Ads Manager (the ad dashboard) for the account afterward. The account was seen to get an organic boost (free extra reach) once the spend finished.
  • Creative testing: find the strongest format.
  • Testing many creatives early lets the platform tell you which format responds.
  • For example, run an image test and a video test in one ad set (a group of ads that share budget and audience).

Pre-launch checks

1 min
  • Before launching, have a finished landing page (the page people reach after clicking the ad).
  • Also have 4 to 5 creatives ready.
  • Also have ready ad copy.
  • Check the spacing and line breaks of the ad copy before publishing.
  • Check that the website URL (web address) points to the correct product page for that product.

Thresholds and stop rules

1 min
  • A clear winner is a video creative with at least 3 sales.
  • It also needs a positive ROAS (return on ad spend, the money earned per dollar spent) above break-even (the point where you neither lose nor make money).
  • One sale can be luck. Wait for more data before calling a creative a winner.
  • To pick a winning creative, read hard metrics (real results such as purchases) once the ad has spent around its target CPA (cost per acquisition, the most you can pay for one sale).
  • If it has not spent that much yet, use softer signals such as attention and CTR to decide which ad deserves more budget.
  • A reused creative with 3 to 4 percent CTR above a 1 percent day-three minimum passed the go or no-go gate.
  • Turn off ads that spend 20 to 30 USD without sales.
  • Keep the ads that produce sales.
  • Stop a product if its metrics show no promise after break-even spend.
  • A hook and creative combination counts as a clear winner only when it performs best in every ad set of the test, not just one.
  • A result that wins in one audience (the group of people the ad is shown to) but loses in the others should be retested before it is scaled.
  • Start creative testing when CTR or CPC (cost per click, what one click costs) is poor.

Declaring winners

1 min
  • Choose the winner by ROAS first.
  • If ROAS is equal, use CTR.
  • If CTR is equal, use average play time (how long people watch on average).
  • A winner must perform best in every ad set of the test.
  • Decide on the winner with the hard metric once the spend passes the target CPA.
  • Before that, use CTR and attention.
  • Once one angle (the main message or approach) clearly wins, add a landing page section for it.
  • Then move the other sections down.
  • Keep the three best performers after the test.
  • Compare the highest CTR creative in each ad set.
  • Scale the winning layout and video version only after the static test shows a clear winner.
  • Decide which creative stays by the data and the sales it brings, not by how polished it looks.
  • Let the platform decide which performs best, then turn off the weak ones.
  • Decide quickly on what performs early.
  • Waiting a month at 50 per day is not practical for most sellers.
  • Keep creative testing going.
  • The winning creative is reused in audience testing campaigns, CBO scaling campaigns (CBO means campaign budget optimization, where the platform shares budget across ad sets), and new audience tests.

Batch naming and benchmarks

1 min
  • Test creatives in batches (groups).
  • Name ads by batch number, not by individual creative number.
  • The best-performing creative becomes the benchmark (the reference to beat).
  • Each new variation is tested against the benchmark.
  • Any variation that beats the benchmark moves into a scaling campaign.
    • Example: Batch 12, Creative B vs Batch 11 benchmark. Move it to scaling if it beats the benchmark's cost per purchase.
    • Second example: Batch 7, skit hook (a short scripted scene that opens the ad) vs Batch 6 benchmark. Scale only if it beats the benchmark's ROAS for three days.

Testing cadence and weekly planning

1 min
  • Testing creatives is a weekly job.
  • Plan 6 to 9 videos per week from 2 to 3 concepts (core ideas).
  • Test a minimum of three concepts per week.
  • Creative testing frequency: 2 to 3 times per week at small scale.
  • At higher scale, test 10 to 15 times per week.
  • Angle tests run in cycles.
  • After a winning angle is found, keep testing new concepts continually.
  • While you test, keep improving the existing ads.
  • A creative test that starts with a lot of spend will show a high CPM (cost per thousand views) at first.
  • For new creative tests, expect high CPM at first, no matter how good the creative is.
  • New ads lack social proof (signs that others like them, such as likes and comments).
  • CPM falls as real user engagement builds up.
  • Creative testing begins on a small budget of about 5 per day per ad set to see whether an audience responds.

Common mistakes

1 min
  • Changing many things at once makes results unreadable.
  • Keep a control ad (an unchanged ad used as the reference). Change one element at a time.
  • A single creative inside a shared ad set can absorb most of the budget.
  • Test each new creative in its own ad set first.
  • Forgetting to test a new creative separately lets the winning creative hide new results.
  • Judging an ad before it has spent enough.
  • Wait for the target spend before reading hard metrics.
  • Launching a single polished ad late.
  • Launch many creatives quickly and iterate, meaning improve and repeat.
  • Changing the offer and the creative in the same test.
  • Keep the offer fixed while testing creatives.
  • Starting hook testing too early. Wait until ripped creatives (ads copied from proven examples and adapted) have proven profitable.
  • Blindly trusting platform budget allocation (the platform's own choice of where money goes).
  • Force spend onto a creative before calling it weak.
  • Declaring a product dead after one weak creative.
  • Test new creatives first.

Further details

1 min
  • Test ads for a product in the US first.
  • If CTR stays low with the same product, test it in another market such as Canada or Australia.
  • Australia is named as a good test market.
  • Keep testing ad creative and the wording of customer messages.
  • Small changes in phrasing can have large effects on sales.
  • Spending money on testing was a core part of this seller's approach.

Playbook › 12. Creatives and ad copy

Store, email and organic content copy

Section 12.24 · 24 of 26 in this chapter · 46 min read

In short

  • Sell the pain first, then the good result, then the facts.
  • Sell the life the product creates, not the product itself.
  • Reuse your best product lines in emails and ads.
  • Make only claims you can prove.
  • Ask for a testimonial (written or video praise) within 24 hours of purchase.
More key points
  • Make your ads and emails say the same thing, so customers hear one clear offer.
  • Post on Facebook and Instagram one to two times per week, every week.
  • Test free posts first. Pay for ads only on the posts that work best.
  • Read every AI-written text yourself before customers see it.
  • Free videos that are not paid for should not ask people to buy, by default.
  • After one video takes off, the next one can ask clearly for a sale.
  • Post four to five good videos, about two hours apart, while the hit is still growing.
  • Have three weeks of content ready before you post your first Reel.
  • Test each new Instagram feature on your own account before you rely on it.

Product page copy: persuasion order

3 min
  • Work through product copy in this order:
    1. List the problems and pains the product solves. Include the problems customers feel now, which may have brought them to the page.
    2. For each problem, name the opposite desire: the good result the buyer wants.
    3. Describe the end result the product gives in the buyer's life, for example at home, in the car, or wherever they use it. The product is not the final pleasure. The result is.
    4. Write the copy so it shows you understand the buyer's pain, and that the product gives the pleasure they want.
  • Marketing means teaching the market that a business can solve problems, fill gaps or reach goals better than any other business. Product copy has the same job: show the buyer that this product solves their problem in a way no other product does.
  • The real pleasure for a buyer is not owning the product. It is what the product does for them. Describe this result, not the ownership.
  • Use this order for product copy:
    1. Repeat the customer's pain or problem back to them, so they start to feel the bad emotion it causes.
    2. Paint a picture of the good result the product brings, so they feel what it is like when the product works for them.
    3. Tell a story that makes the reader imagine the situation and feel something. This creates emotion and a feeling of connection.
    4. Show proof. Use a demonstration, or testimonials (written praise from customers) that say what they loved.
    5. Only then give logical support: features and specifications (specs), such as size, weight, speed and colour options. Add manuals or other documents, and social proof (signs that other people trust the product), such as reviews.
  • Pain before pleasure, and pleasure before features. Talking about pain alone creates only bad feelings. Follow it with the good result the product gives.
  • Emotion-before-logic pattern: open with a pain point, move to the result the buyer wants, support it with a short story or proof, then close with specs and reviews that justify the choice.
    • Kitchen knife, pain and pleasure: "Tired of slicing dinner into uneven chunks every night? Imagine prepping in half the time with a blade that glides through tomatoes like butter." Then add: "A 20 cm forged steel blade, 180 g, with a 5-star rating from 2,300 buyers."
    • Dog harness, pain and pleasure: "Walks that end with a yanked neck and a frustrated owner? Picture every walk calm and enjoyable, like the one Maria shared after a week with her rescue dog." Then add: "Padded chest strap, reflective stitching, sizes XS to XL in four colours."
  • A story in product copy does not have to be fiction. A realistic scenario the buyer can picture creates emotion and a connection with the brand. It makes the benefit feel real, not abstract.
  • Product copy should open with emotional hooks before proof points. These hooks are the unique mechanism (the special way the product works), power bullets (short, strong points) and benefits (the good things the buyer gets). Proof comes second. Proof confirms an emotional choice with logic. It does not start the persuasion.
  • Product copy should lead with how the product makes the customer feel and how it fits their life. Do not lead with generic claims such as "high quality" or "shipping in two days."
    • Pattern: name the situation the buyer cares about (for example a family gathering or a gift moment). Show the outcome they want. Then give one concrete benefit.
    • Example one: 'Make your next family dinner easier and bring everyone together with this product.'
    • Example two: 'Give your partner a gift that feels personal and arrives ready to use this week.'
  • Parts written for product page copy (benefits, unique mechanisms, problem statements) can be reused directly in emails and ads. You do not need to rewrite them from scratch.
  • Before you write ad copy from scratch, audit (check) the website's current copy for benefit-led lines, bold headlines and strong claims. Adapt these proven lines for Facebook and TikTok ads, so the language already matches the audience.

Product page copy: headlines and claims

1 min
  • Primary promise formula (the opening headline of a product description): [verb promising an outcome] + [everyday benefit] + [in the setting where the customer lives] + [superlative claim for the category]. A superlative is a "best" or "most" claim. It names the desire directly. It hooks the reader, but the rest of the copy must prove it.
    • Example 1: 'Keep your coffee hot all morning with the most leak-proof travel mug available at this price.'
    • Example 2: 'Sleep cool every summer night with the most breathable cotton bedding in its price range.'
  • Desire headline: name the result the buyer wants, using the words from your customer research. Tie it to how the product is used.
    • Example: 'Gear that keeps your longest weekend ride comfortable'
    • Example: 'Stylish kit that holds up on every climb'
  • Scarcity lines: name what the shopper loses if they leave, such as low stock or a price that is ending. Use one only if it is true.
    • Example: 'Only a few units left in your size'
    • Example: 'This colourway is sold out soon, so hold it now'
  • Shipping claims: do not put 'Free Shipping' in product page copy if Google Merchant Center (Google's tool for shopping listings) flags it as redundant. Put the shipping offer in the meta description instead. The meta description is the short summary search engines show under your page link. Or use the free shipping settings in Shopify (the online store platform). (2023)

Product page structure

1 min
  • Source E pattern: write the product description in three blocks (2025-26):
    • 'What it is' paragraph: material, use, colours and size range.
    • 'How it works' list: three features, each tied to a function.
    • 'What you will feel' list: short benefits you can sense.
    • Why it works: it sells the product at the top, keeps the buy button high on the page, and keeps the details easy to scan.
    • Example pattern: 'Water-resistant upper' followed by 'helps keep damp floors out'. 'Foam insole' followed by 'steps feel softer by the door'.
  • Structure that worked in one Source B example:
    1. The unique mechanism.
    2. A bridging paragraph (a short link paragraph) that introduces the extra benefits.
    3. A bulleted list of extra features. Each benefit gets a short paragraph.
    4. The CTA (call to action: the line that tells the reader what to do next).
    • If a health promise is restricted and the product is health-related, replace the main promise with a convenience promise.
  • Use a clear visual break between the bridging paragraph and the feature list, such as an optional features heading, if it helps reading. Headings are optional.
  • Conversion (a visitor who buys) depends on a clear, hard-to-refuse offer and a product with real demand. No product page can save a product that nobody wants or that faces heavy competition.
  • Study high-performing brand product pages (for example Blendjet and hoodie brands). See how they organise their copy, and compare them with weak product pages. Frameworks such as AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action: a classic sales order) are a starting point.

Product page proof and reviews

1 min
  • Include 3 to 5 customer reviews directly in the product page copy, in the proof section. Take them from your review widgets (review display boxes) and place them in the sales copy.
  • Reviews can appear as text, images or video. Pick reviews from existing review widgets and add them to the copy.
  • A video walkthrough shows the product in real use. It proves the claimed function, where features can be shown visually. It does not suit every product type.
  • Mention media coverage when a product has been featured in known outlets. These include news, magazines, newspapers, current affairs shows, reality TV shows, blogs or online publications such as Buzzfeed. Add optional links to the original coverage. Audiences see media-featured products as high quality.
  • Keep proof for claims on the product page. This includes testimonials, clinical studies and statistics. Abandoned cart emails (reminders sent to people who left items in their basket) can use them too. Keep them on the product page or in a pop-up. Do not link them externally, because external links break the purchase flow.
  • Authorised retailer badges, procedure (2024):
    1. Add brand logos or text to the home page as a 'Brands We Carry' section.
    2. Add 'Authorized Retailer for [Brand Name]' text to product descriptions.
    3. Create a 6th product image in Canva (a free design tool) showing an 'Authorized Dealer' badge.
    4. Place this image at the end of the product image gallery, after images 1-5.
    5. This works across the home page, collection pages and product pages.

Product descriptions at catalogue scale

2 min
  • Run the product description prompt product by product across the whole catalogue. A short, fixed format keeps the work fast and avoids paying others for product copy. (2025)
  • If a supplier (the company that sends you the products) has no description, write one before the product goes live. Writing many descriptions by hand takes a long time. For example, 500 products need a lot of focus, so outsourcing is an option.
  • Prioritise unique product descriptions by traffic (number of visitors) (2021):
    1. Pull a monthly report in Google Analytics (a free tool that counts visitors).
    2. Open the Content section.
    3. Sort pages by traffic.
    4. Add unique copy to the highest-traffic pages first, then work down the list.
  • Beat Amazon with unique, benefit-led product descriptions, not generic supplier text. Amazon's own descriptions are short. A longer, story-driven description with a call to action is a clear advantage.
  • For product page images and copy, reuse supplier assets when they are allowed. Logos, product images and descriptions from the supplier can be used directly if there are no restrictions. Change them only for SEO (search engine optimisation: making pages easy to find in Google) where needed.
  • Add GIFs (short looping animations) cut from the product video to the product page and to ad creatives (the ad designs). This is a low-effort test of a richer description. Test them against the current description.
  • The Source E pattern for organic product posts (posts that are free, not paid) is covered under social content. For product page copy, use the structure above.

Product description prompts

Direct-response copywriter prompt. Use it with the product name or URL filled in (2024):

Adopt the persona of a direct-response copywriter and draft a product description for [product name and/or URL] following this arrangement: a headline, a short description of one to three sentences, a features-and-benefits block, and bulleted points. Base everything solely on the supplied product information and avoid fabricating specs, measurements, materials, or assertions. Before release, reword the draft into a natural, human-sounding voice while keeping the same arrangement.

Benefit-driven description prompt, with placeholders filled in:

Write a benefit-driven product description for [PRODUCT] aimed at [TARGET CUSTOMER]. Focus on the feelings and benefits the customer, and their [PET or other user] if relevant, will experience after receiving the product. Then turn the same content into bullet points for the first section of the product page, and write a short four-line paragraph for each of these features: [FEATURE 1], [FEATURE 2]. Keep the same topic throughout and set the tone to [TONE, e.g. emotional or funny].

Product videos and media

2 min
  • Add videos to product pages only if the supplier provides high-quality video files that truly show the product. Do not source or create videos for every product. If the supplier sends videos, embed them from YouTube (place them on the page using a link code) instead of uploading them straight to Shopify.
  • Short product videos placed right at the point of sale can answer the buyer's last questions. Examples: unboxings, ease of use, education and common FAQs (frequently asked questions). They can replace or sit alongside a written description. The shopper gets the answer without leaving the buying decision. (2025-26)
  • Short product videos of 15 to 30 seconds, modelled on the style used by Zappos (an online shoe and clothing store), can be made for each product. You can make them even without the physical item.
    • Show the product. State the top benefits, such as easy-adjust knobs or easy to clean. Add a CTA such as 'Buy today'. Mention authorised dealer status if it applies.
    • Example: a 20-second video for a best-selling product, where a presenter says the three strongest benefits and ends with 'Buy today'.
    • Example: a green-screen clip (a presenter in front of a digital background) describing a kitchen gadget's ease of use, ending with 'Order now'.
  • To reuse supplier product videos on a store YouTube channel, follow these steps in order (2025):
    1. Ask the brand for permission to upload its product videos to your own channel.
    2. Create a YouTube channel for the store.
    3. Upload each supplier video with a correct file name and the right tags (keywords that help people find it).
    4. In each video description, add a link to the matching product page on the store.
    5. Embed each video on its matching product page.
  • Asking viewers a question in a YouTube description can prompt replies and engagement. This can help the algorithm (YouTube's system that picks what to show) and conversion. Choose questions that push the viewer to respond.
  • Customer videos and photos are valuable UGC (user-generated content: pictures and videos made by customers). Use them as social proof on the site and in ads. Repost them on YouTube, where they can rank (appear high in results) in Google. Google often places video results above other organic (free, not paid) results.

Store structure and landing pages

1 min
  • Traffic split for ecommerce stores: send 90 to 95 percent of traffic to the product page. Send only 5 to 10 percent to the home and collection pages. Optimise the product page first. Home and collection pages need little attention.
  • When a store sells several items in one niche (a narrow market) with a similar theme, a home page showing all products together can work. Each product should still link to its own product page. Keep visitors on the landing page (the page they arrive on) where possible. Add add-to-cart buttons directly on it.
  • Hero section principle: above the fold (the part of the page visible without scrolling), the hero section (the big top area of a page) should carry a big benefit call-out (a bold statement of the main gain). It should also have striking visuals, trust, social proof and risk reduction. The visitor should understand the offer without scrolling far.
  • Hero section workflow with AI:
    1. Know the hero section's job. Visitors should immediately see what the product is, what the offer is and how it helps them.
    2. Give the AI the product and the problem it solves. Then ask for a big benefit statement.
    3. Ask for trust elements, such as social proof and authority.
    4. Ask for copy that reduces risk, such as guarantees.
    5. Combine the outputs into the hero section.

Post-purchase and packaging

1 min
  • Branded insert cards (printed leaflets put in the parcel) can be a built-in upsell channel. An upsell is an offer for a bigger or extra purchase. A card can hold a personal thank-you message, the brand logo, a preview of upcoming products and a discount code for the next purchase. This turns the package into a re-engagement tool, which brings a customer back.
  • Shopify order confirmation for returning customers. Use it as written, with the placeholders in square brackets replaced (2024):
Thank you for placing your order with [YOUR STORE NAME]! Did you know we also offer [NICHE] products at [NICHE STORE NAME]? As a thank you, all of our returning customers save [10 percent] for the next 7 days, so don't wait. Enter coupon code [GRATITUDE] at checkout to redeem your savings. Your coupon code works at [NICHE STORE URL] for the next 7 days.

Email: role, length and format

2 min
  • Email copy and website copy have different jobs. The email's main job is to get the reader to click through to the website. It does not need to make the full sale. The sales pitch belongs on the product page or the destination page.
  • Inbox attention spans are short. Readers get more emails per day than they did five or ten years ago. An email has very little time to hold a reader. Keep it brief, rather than covering every selling point.
  • Long emails are often not read. Product images, videos, full copy, price and the full sales argument work better on the website. The website shows them far better than an email can.
  • When an email links to a blog post, it does not need to prove the post is good. It only needs to make the reader click. Do not try to prove the post is great.
  • Image-heavy emails are more likely to land in the Promotions or Spam tab (the folders where Gmail puts bulk mail). Simple text emails that use a name and read like a normal message tend to reach the inbox and get opened more. Split test plain text against image plus text. A split test compares two versions to see which one works better.
  • Promo block for a time-limited sale, as a reusable structure. Keep the same structure for every promo, so readers learn where to look (2025-26):
    • A headline with the sale name, for example 'Flash deals'.
    • A time window line, for example '24-hour flash deals'.
    • An extra discount on sale items, for example 'Extra 15% off sale items'.
    • One or two sentences that tell the reader why the sale matters.
    • A SHOP NOW CTA button.
    • Full block as a reusable structure:
Email newsletter promo block for a time-limited sale, as a reusable structure: a headline with the sale name (for example 'Flash deals'), a time window line (for example '24-hour flash deals'), an extra discount on sale items (for example 'Extra 15% off sale items'), one or two sentences that tell the reader why the sale matters, and a SHOP NOW call to action button. Keep the same structure for every promo so readers learn where to look.
  • Pain-point email pattern (2025-26): open with the frustration the customer feels when the product fails. Phrase it as a question. Then show you understand it, say how the product removes it, and give the CTA.
    • Example: 'Tired of shoes that pinch by mile three?'
    • Example: 'Sore wrists after every typing day? Fix that today.'

Email: subject lines and sign-up

1 min
  • Problem-focused subject and teaser formula. A teaser is the short preview line that shows under the subject:
    • '[Emotion word] because [common problem]'
    • 'Entrepreneurs: [time-based guilt problem]'
    • 'Students: [achievement-limiting problem]'
    • Example: 'Guilty because you don't spend time with family?' (problem exposure), followed by 'World of distractions makes it impossible. We fixed this.' (solution claim)
    • Example: 'Can't stay focused at work?' (problem), followed by 'Entrepreneurs are losing 10+ hours weekly to distractions.' (severity), followed by 'New focus solution reduces distraction by 90% in 7 days.' (outcome)
  • Sign-up headline: name the discount and the condition of the first purchase. Phrase it as a question or an invitation. Stating the reward up front makes giving an email address worth it.
    • Example: 'Want 15 percent off your first order?'
    • Example: 'Get a free gift with your first order when you join today.'
  • Sign-up button: use a verb that describes what the visitor gets, such as unlock or reveal. The button then promises the reward instead of asking for a form to be filled in.
    • Examples: 'Unlock my 15 percent off' and 'Send me my code.'
  • Decline link (the "no thanks" option): write the opt-out (the link to refuse) as a short, friendly line. It should keep the visitor's dignity and make the choice feel deliberate.
    • Examples: 'No thanks, I will skip the savings' and 'Not right now, I will pay regular price.'
  • Exit-intent popup copy. An exit-intent popup is a box that appears when the visitor is about to leave the page:
Use this copy for your exit-intent email popup: Headline: 'wait, don't leave us'. Body: 'enter your email below to receive a special discount from [Store-Name]'. Button: 'send me the discounts'.

1 min

Testimonial request email sent within 24 hours of purchase. Subject: hey, we would like to refund you your purchase. Body: thanks for buying, I really appreciate that you trust me so much. You are eligible for a refund of your purchase. The only thing I need from you is a testimonial. Please fill out this form, which asks for your first name, last name, a video upload, and a checkbox confirming you agree that the content can be used in our ads.

Hi [customer name], thank you again for your purchase of [product name] from [store name]. Would you be willing to leave a short review? If you are comfortable, you could send a photo of the product taken with your phone. A text review is also great. Thank you so much for your support!

Subject: store review

Hi [name],

You offered to review my store after the call. Here is the link: [store URL].

Thank you,
[your name]

Abandoned cart and recovery messages

4 min

An abandoned cart is a shopper who added items but did not pay. Cart recovery means messages that bring them back.

  • Second abandoned checkout email (sent to a shopper who left before paying): write the draft directly in Shopify (online store software). Copy the complete text and paste it into ChatGPT or another AI tool. Request improved clarity and tone. Then replace the original draft in Shopify with the AI-improved version.
  • Proof (customer results or reviews that show the product works) belongs on the product page, not in the cart email. See the proof rule above.
  • Cart-recovery email prompt, Gary Bencivenga style. Gary Bencivenga was a famous copywriter, a writer of sales text. Use with the brand name filled in:
Write a cart-recovery email for [brand] that reads like a Gary Bencivenga piece. It should bring a shopper back to checkout by speaking to the strongest want they have. Keep the voice chatty, as if one person is talking to another, and take tone, values and word choice from the brand profile supplied earlier in this chat. This is the first message this shopper receives from us, so leave out the subject line and preview text, and offer no discount. Sell only what finishing the order gives them. Structure: (1) a brief, punchy headline naming our single most valuable selling point, preferably one that also answers a common hesitation, such as fast delivery, a 30-day guarantee, or Australian-owned. (2) A sub-headline built on their desire. (3) Wording in the target market's own language that says 'you left this behind'. (4) A picture of the items they abandoned. (5) A short call to action reading either 'Complete Your Order' or 'Return to Cart'. (6) A scarcity or urgency line suited to the audience, invented fresh rather than borrowed from any example, such as one about low stock after a viral moment. (7) A short opening centred on their desire that invites them back to the cart. (8) Persuasive answers to the three most common customer reservations, never using the word 'objection'. (9) A line saying help is available at [email]. (10) A strong closing call to action. (11) A sign-off. Aim for high burstiness and perplexity: original phrasing, varied sentence length, emotional pull. Adapt language to the target market. Keep it short enough to scan, put spacing between every block, and never repeat the same claim in two blocks.
  • Subject line prompt for the email above (2025-26). The subject line is the title shown in the inbox. The preview text is the short line after it. Use this after the cart-recovery email exists:
Write several subject line options for the email above, drawing on that email's own subject and preview content. Give each option its own preview text. Each option takes a different angle: the main selling point, the shopper's core desire, or the item left in the cart. Keep every subject short enough to display in full on a phone screen. Present the output as a list, with the subject first and its preview text directly beneath it.
  • Messenger cart reminder formula (Messenger is Facebook's chat app). Name the item, state that it is still in the cart, and add one clear link with no pressure. The tone can be casual, with an emoji or light personality, because Messenger feels like a text from a friend.
    • Example 1: 'Hey {first name}, the {product name} you picked is still waiting in your cart: {link}'
    • Example 2: 'Still deciding? Your {product name} is saved and ready whenever you are: {link}'
  • Day 3 to 4 Messenger offer (sent on day 3 or 4 of the cart follow-up). Add a cheerful closing line when it fits the brand. Keep the sender personal:
Messenger message for the day 3 to 4 offer: "Hey {first name}, your {product name} is still waiting for you. Here is a code for {free shipping or 10% off}: {code}. Go straight back to the store here: {link}"
  • Day 7 Messenger offer with a deadline. Replace the 12 hours and 15% with the real offer. Keep the deadline visible.
Day 7 offer message with deadline: "Last chance, {first name}. You have 12 hours to use this 15% off code: {code}. This is the best offer we can give you. Go back to your cart here: {link}"
  • Pre-sale content (content that warms people up before they are ready to buy):
    • Engage cold users first. Cold users are people who do not know your store yet.
    • Then move them closer to the product. Use more commercial content at each retargeting step. Retargeting means showing ads again to people who already visited your store.
    • Early pieces are educational. Later ones compare or explain the products your store sells.
  • Make the remarketing ad headline (the big title line of an ad) match the email subject line sent at the same time. Then the same people see one consistent message in email and ads. (2024)
  • Use past subject lines and ad headlines that performed best on opens (emails opened), clicks and sales as a starting point for Q4 messaging. Q4 means the last three months of the year. Model new ones on them, rather than reusing them word for word. (2024)
  • Create ad copy (the written words in an ad) from the approved email sequence, not from scratch. Each ad should match the message of the email sent on the same day. The audience then hears one consistent offer. (2025)

Lead magnet ebooks

3 min

A lead magnet is a free gift given in exchange for a customer's email address. An ebook is a digital book, usually a PDF file.

  • Lead magnet offer formula. The formula is written as a fill-in pattern. This frames free content as premium while creating urgency (a reason to act now):
    • '[Product category benefit] solved with [number] proven [format] in [time]. [Social proof or scarcity]. Normally [original price], get it free. [Scarcity limiter, e.g. only 100 copies available].'
    • Example: 'Seven best relaxation techniques to relax at home in less than 5 minutes. Usually $19.99, download free. Only 100 copies available.'
    • Example: 'Five energy-boosting rituals for busy entrepreneurs in under 10 minutes. Usually $29, free today only. Limited to first 50 subscribers.'
  • Ebook cover line pattern (the text printed on the cover): a named brand or expert presenting a solution, followed by the author's name. For example, 'Your mentor presents [solution], written by [author name]'. This makes the cover read as a credible guide to a specific problem.
  • Ebook title formula: a number or timeframe, a concrete promised result, and the topic or audience. The title should promise a clear, specific outcome that matches the product. (2026)
    • Example 1: '5 Desk Stretches To Ease Stiff Shoulders'
    • Example 2: '30-Day Starter Plan For Beginner Runners'
  • AI chat tools can contain factual or wording mistakes. Review the full ebook text before publishing it to customers. Health-related guides should carry a short educational disclaimer (a note saying the guide is for learning only) and point to a qualified professional. (2026)
  • An ebook used as a free gift should be 15 to 25 pages with 4 to 6 chapters. The product is mentioned only in the closing section. The ebook gives real value first and supports the product second. (2026)
  • Creating a free ebook gift with AI (2026):
    1. Use the ebook prompt, replacing the target audience, the product name and the problem with your own.
    2. Check the generated text for placeholder text and for any leftover call-to-action placeholders.
    3. Ask the AI to turn the text into a stylish, complete HTML file (a web page file) with the product name, and ignore the CTA link.
    4. Review the HTML and the design notes.
    5. Ask the AI to convert the HTML into a PDF.
    6. Download the PDF and upload it to the Digital Downloads app (an app that delivers digital files to buyers) as an asset (a file you own and sell).
    7. Attach the e-book product to the gift position in the bundle (a package of several products sold together).
  • Structure of a free-gift ebook, in order (2026):
    1. Title and subtitle.
    2. Welcome note or short author intro that explains what the reader will get.
    3. Four to six chapters, each with real, practical content, subheadings, short paragraphs and bullet points.
    4. An actionable exercise (a small task the reader can do) in the chapter where it fits.
    5. A closing section with a single soft next step.
    6. An educational disclaimer at the end.
  • Example of an actionable exercise: a 30-second self-check at night with a flashlight and a mirror, followed by a single simple observation. It gives readers a concrete task without asking them to change their routine. (2026)
  • Choosing a lead magnet topic:
    1. Pick the sites, communities or influencers where you want to advertise.
    2. Look at their homepage, feature articles and what is trending or popular there.
    3. Check which books or resources on the topic sell best.
    4. Create your own related version of the most popular idea and offer it as the free item.
  • Design the lead magnet PDF as a brochure (a multi-page printed-style document):
    • The first page introduces the company and why it is in business.
    • Every page footer carries an offer and a link to the store.
    • The conclusion explains why to buy.
    • Save it as a PDF with clickable links so readers can act from their device.
  • Outsource lead magnet ebook design on Fiverr or Upwork (freelance websites) for about 20 to 50 dollars, after you have written the content yourself in a Google Doc. Only the footers, logos and backgrounds need a designer.

Blog and SEO content

2 min

SEO means search engine optimisation: making your pages easier to find in Google. A blog article is a written post on your website. A keyword is the search phrase you want to be found for.

  • Create one custom image per blog article that contains the target keyword and matches the brand. Most competitors skip this step, so it helps you stand out.
  • Google Docs article template (a shared document in Google's word processor). Keep everything in one document so there are no multiple file versions:
    1. The keyword at the top.
    2. The article draft.
    3. The edited version, in the same document.
    4. Meta descriptions (the short summary under a result in Google) and headlines.
    5. Images and anything else related to the article.
    6. A content suggestion section, filled by the data entry assistant with what the top 10 results contain. The top 10 results are the first ten results Google shows for your keyword.
  • Optimise existing articles first. Use a sales copywriter to improve headlines and meta descriptions for articles that already exist. This is the highest-leverage step for traffic that already comes in, meaning it gives the biggest result for the effort. Old-article optimisation can be tested before new articles are written.
  • Create custom images for articles to keep a consistent brand look. Put the target keyword in the image alt tag (hidden text that describes the image for search engines), so search engines understand the image.
  • Content is a versatile asset. Content means any post, article or video you publish. It supports:
    • organic search (free results in search engines, not ads);
    • Google search text and display ads, and YouTube ads and organic traffic;
    • LinkedIn for B2B (business-to-business, selling to other companies);
    • Google remarketing, and Facebook and Instagram retargeting;
    • cold social traffic (people who have never heard of you) on Pinterest, Facebook and Instagram.
    • It also improves conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy) and makes email marketing easier.
  • Four-step content marketing process:
    1. Plan: write 12 content ideas that answer the customer's most common problems and pain points in as much detail as possible. Start with easy, low-hanging topics and move to research-based topics later. Twelve pieces give roughly three to six months of content.
    2. Produce: write it yourself or outsource it to a freelance writer, which makes consistent output more likely. A freelance job can be posted on ProBlogger (problogger.com) via its jobs board. ProBlogger is a website for blog writers.
    3. Post: publish two new pieces of quality content per month for 12 months.
    4. Promote: actively promote each piece across traffic channels, since posting alone does not grow traffic.
  • Affiliate blog post for an information product. Affiliate means you earn a commission for sending buyers to someone else's product. An information product is a digital course or guide sold online:
    1. Find the information product on ClickBank (a marketplace for digital products) or in your niche's resources, communities or forums.
    2. Watch reviews or unboxing videos of the product so you understand what it is, without buying it.
    3. Write a blog post on your Shopify blog that gives useful information about the product and explains why you recommend it.
    4. Close the post with a call to action and your affiliate link, for example a link to join the program.
    5. Retarget visitors of the blog post with Facebook ads.

Organic social: cadence and content

4 min

Organic means free, unpaid posts. Cadence means how often you post. A niche is a narrow market, such as grilling.

  • Post one to two times per week on both Facebook and Instagram, without exception. Inactive pages whose most recent post is months or years old signal to customers that the business may no longer be operating.
  • Basic organic posts, about a couple per week, add credibility but do not drive early revenue. Posts can be scheduled weeks or months ahead, and AI can help draft them. (2025)
  • For a brand that posts organic content, publish a higher volume of posts showing the product in use. This supports reach (how many people see your post) and virality (fast spreading through shares). Choose posts that show the product in use rather than giveaways, because they attract people more likely to buy. (2025-26)
  • Test organic posts first, then move the best performers into paid ads. Running organic posts as ads without adjustment produced no sales in one brand's test over three months. (2025-26)
  • Share the product images and pins (images saved on Pinterest, a visual search site) on Facebook and other social networks too. Visual content performs well there, so one set of images can serve both platforms.
  • Keep most reels (short vertical videos) evergreen, meaning still relevant months later. A common split is about 70 percent evergreen content and about 30 percent trend-based content (content that follows what is popular right now). (2025-26)
  • Keep posts professional and straightforward rather than chasing viral content. Do one of these:
    • Announce new authorised supplier relationships (a supplier is a company that makes or ships your products).
    • Highlight specific products you recommend.
    • Create product spotlights that explain one to three key reasons why customers should benefit from each item.
  • Use AI (a chat tool) to generate 30 weeks of weekly content ideas if you need guidance on post themes. Keep the focus on posts that build business credibility rather than chase viral engagement.
  • Start with easy wins in your niche. Example: home grilling for a grilling brand.
    1. Create a Facebook Business Page.
    2. Create an Instagram account.
    3. Post memes related to your niche. A meme is a funny picture or text that people copy and share.
    • Steps 1 to 3 in the order given.
  • Share diverse content types that resonate with your audience: blogs, recipes, stories, photos, videos and memes.
  • Start with content that is funny or educational. Humour is the easiest way to earn engagement (likes, comments and shares) early. Test formats until one earns shares, and keep making content people want to share.
  • Post content people are proud to share. Sharing should raise their social standing with friends by making them look funny or informed. Ask whether a friend who shares the post will look good for doing so.
  • Meme-style audience-building pattern:
    • Set two contrasting options that the niche cares about, such as a premium version against a mass-produced one.
    • End with a short request to like and share if the reader agrees.
    • People who identify with one side engage quickly and invite friends to join.
    • Example: 'Hand-ground coffee versus the instant stuff: which side are you on? Like and share if you agree.'
    • Example: 'Hand-rolled pasta versus the boxed kind: tag the friend who still buys boxed.'
  • Blanket statements that could describe any brand get no engagement, because people see many similar posts every day. Content has to be specific to the niche and stand out from competing posts.
  • Product post captions (the text under a post) for organic social use three parts:
    • a short everyday moment or season the product fits;
    • The product photo;
    • one light call to action, such as a new arrival, a restock or a link to browse.
    • The mood line makes the post relatable without pushing a price.
    • Example 1: 'Mornings start easier with our new travel bottle. Back in stock, link in bio.'
    • Example 2: 'Rain outside, warm drinks inside. Our insulated mugs now come in three new colours, see the collection.'
  • News-based posts: ask the AI for recent news in a niche and for post ideas that tie your brand into it. Web search in the tool may return only a few results, so verify the news before posting.
    • Template:
Ask for the latest news in [niche], explain that you sell [product], and request news items that could serve as material for social posts linking your brand to them.
  • Organic videos should be entertaining and show the product in use. Do not list ingredients or specifications (technical details like size or materials). Informational talk reduces watch time (how long people keep watching).
  • Overly polished, brand-heavy content lowers performance on short video. Aim for authentic, watchable content with a clean background and good camera quality rather than heavy branding.
  • Organic videos should avoid explicit buy or shop calls to action (a CTA is a clear request, such as "buy now"). Paid ads should include a clear call to action and a scarcity element (a limit on stock or time that gives a reason to act now).
  • Mistake to avoid: blanket content is ignored. See the niche-specific rule above.

Organic social: captions, polls and interaction

3 min

A poll is a question with answer choices. A sticker is a small interactive element added to Instagram Stories (short posts shown at the top of the app). The algorithm is the app's system that decides who sees each post.

  • Write the way you would text a friend, in captions and replies. Humour, voice notes and voiceover make content feel real. The more casual the tone, the better it resonates. (2025-26)
  • Questions in captions invite comments and act as free market research, showing what the audience thinks and wants. They also make followers feel more connected to the brand. (2025-26)
  • Question-and-reply procedure:
    1. End the post caption with an open question that invites an opinion or a verdict.
    2. Check the comments on the post.
    3. Reply to each answer, ideally with a follow-up question about what the commenter meant.
    4. Keep the dialogue open for as long as people keep responding.
  • Caption question pattern: end with a short, open question that asks for a verdict, an opinion or a shared experience. It gives people a reason to respond and turns the post into a conversation.
    • Example 1 for a kitchen gadget: 'Did this actually save you time, or is it just a gimmick?'
    • Example 2 for a skincare brand: 'Have you ever had a breakout before a big event? What helped you?'
  • Asking a question and then not replying to the answers wastes the engagement the question created. Reply to comments so people see the brand is listening.
  • Instagram's polls-in-captions feature is newer and actively promoted by the platform. Creators use it to boost engagement. Newer features a platform pushes are generally worth adopting early. (2025-26)
  • Polls in captions: write the caption first, then add a poll below it. Viewers can vote while they watch the reel, without leaving the caption or writing a comment. (2025-26)
  • Two-option poll pattern: pose a light, fun question about what the audience prefers next, such as behind-the-scenes content against a tutorial. Keep it quick to answer, so people vote in seconds. A single tap is an easy ask, and the result shows which content the audience wants more of.
    • Example 1: 'Coffee at home or at the café?'
    • Example 2: 'Morning routine or packing hacks next?'
  • Polls can also use emoji voting, where each choice is represented by an emoji. Low-effort formats like polls and emoji votes are the easiest way to start audience engagement. (2025-26)
  • Instagram Stories interactive stickers include polls, question boxes and quizzes. Use them repeatedly in a recurring series, so the audience expects them and returns to the same format. (2025-26)
  • Emoji sticker on Instagram Stories: place a heart (or another emoji) sticker with a short prompt. For example, ask viewers to tap it if they have been following for a while. Tapping sends an immediate reaction back to the story, a one-tap way to take part.
  • Offer low-barrier ways to take part, such as tapping a sticker, double tapping or dropping one emoji. Do not ask for a full comment or direct message every time. Lower effort brings more participation. (2025-26)
  • Add new interactive elements to content followers already know and love, such as the regular weekly stories. Engagement then becomes part of an existing habit.
  • Polls in Instagram captions help the algorithm and give the brand insight into what its audience wants. A poll below the caption is a quick way to get engagement and feedback from the same post. (2025-26)
  • Pair a quick poll with an open question. Use the poll for a one-tap yes-or-no or either-or choice. Ask a more in-depth open question in the comments for people who want to write more. Low-effort and high-effort participants then have separate ways to respond to the same post. (2025-26)

Viral follow-up and organic-to-paid

2 min
  • Organic (free, not paid for) video rules change by stage. Two rules fit two situations:
    • Default organic videos: no direct ask to buy or shop. A call to action (a clear request, such as "Shop now") is left out.
    • After a viral video (one that spreads very fast): the next video can be more product-focused. It can have a clear call to action and a direct offer. Viewers often need an offer more than entertainment to convert (to become buyers).
    • After a viral video: follow-up videos can be calmer and more professional. They can have more calls to action and some classic ad tactics (proven advertising methods).
  • Once one organic video goes viral, make the next one calmer and more professional. Viewers who come from the viral video then see a calm follow-up. This builds trust and makes them more likely to visit the store and buy.
  • Extend a viral video's effect and turn views into sales:
    1. Upload four to five good-quality videos while the viral effect is still active, about two hours apart.
    2. Once the viral effect slows, boost the video with TikTok coins (paid in-app currency that pushes a post to more people).
    3. Or run Spark Ads on the video to reach more viewers. Spark Ads are TikTok ads that promote an existing post.
    4. Add an urgency call to action at the end of new videos to convert viewers. Urgency means a line that tells them to act now.
  • Case: a slip-resistant slipper product went viral with a video of about 44 million views, posted September 14, with no paid ads. The video quality and audio were poor. But the clip opened with a slipping risk, showed the slippers solving that problem, and repeated the demonstration. The account had uploaded several times a day, and this one video drove most of the result. The store was later no longer available.
  • Organic-only success can be judged by checking for no repeated ad-style videos. Ad-style means videos that look like paid ads. Paid-boost patterns (signs the account paid to push posts) are also a check. The people reviewing the case believed the slipper account was fully organic, because no other video showed signs of paid promotion.

Images and alt text

1 min
  • Image alt text (a short written description of an image, read by screen readers) describes what an image shows.
  • Alt text helps visually impaired users (people who cannot see well). It also helps Google understand the images, including how they appear in Google Images.
  • Edit alt text in bulk (many at once) to save time when a store has many product images.
  • Blog article images and alt tags: see the blog section above. It covers one custom image per article with the target keyword (the search phrase you want the page to rank for) and the keyword in the alt tag (the hidden alt text field).
  • One set of images can serve Pinterest (a visual search and pinning site) and Facebook alike.

Sales copy inputs from the customer and the store

1 min
  • Build desire, pain and scarcity lines from real customer words and real stock situations. Desire means what buyers want. Pain means what hurts them. Scarcity means limited stock or time. Use customer research (what buyers actually say) as the source of wording.
  • Use scarcity only when it is true.
  • Lead with feelings and situation, then one concrete benefit, rather than generic claims.

Organic Instagram features

1 min
  • Replace Audio is an Instagram feature launched July 21. It lets you swap the music on already published public feed posts and carousels (posts with several swipeable images).
  • Replace Audio keeps your results. Changing the track does not reset the engagement the post has already earned, and likes and reach stay.
  • Replace Audio is useful for older posts that unexpectedly go viral months or years later. Switching their sound to a track that is trending (popular right now) can help the post reach the new audience it is being shown to.
  • Instants is a separate Instagram app. It also appears as a plug-in (a small add-on inside another app) in the Instagram DM tab (the direct messages inbox).
  • Instants now has video recording. Users can send short disappearing clips to close friends, not only photos.
  • Brands can use Instants for spontaneous (unplanned) behind-the-scenes clips. Examples are a quick video while packing orders in the warehouse or during a product photo shoot. The goal is to give followers an inside look.
  • On Instagram carousel posts, you can write a different caption for each slide instead of one caption for the whole post. A five-slide educational carousel should get five unique captions.
  • Per-slide captions make a carousel more interactive. They give viewers a reason to keep swiping and spend longer on the post.
  • Per-slide captions also let you share more information without overloading one caption.
  • Instagram is expanding screenshot alerts to Stories (short posts that disappear after a day). Creators get notified when someone screenshots their Story.
  • The creator believed this applies only to subscription and close friends Stories. This was not yet confirmed.

Trend-driven organic content

1 min
  • Jimothy the Raccoon is a raccoon in Seattle with a very distinctive body shape. He became a viral internet meme (a joke image or clip shared again and again), with fan pages and people posting his sightings.
  • Some brands tied him into their content. One bakery etched his image into a sourdough loaf. The trend can be searched on TikTok for inspiration.
  • Tying a viral trend into one piece of content while it is peaking (at its highest popularity) is a low-cost way to plan organic reach.
  • It works best when the trend is used in the same week it is popular.
  • Reposting content you already have to Snapchat takes almost no effort. It can help reach a new audience.
  • Founder-led stories (posts where the business owner tells their own story) are a stronger option if you want a more personal presence on Snapchat.

Testing new Instagram features

1 min
  • Test the new Instagram features on your own account to see whether they give a reach boost (more people seeing your posts).
    1. Replace the audio on older carousel posts that have gained traction (are getting attention).
    2. Write a unique caption for each slide in new carousels.
    3. Post a short video via Instants, such as a warehouse or photo shoot clip.
    4. Compare reach and engagement (likes, comments, shares) before deciding which features to keep using.

Viral Reels production and posting

2 min
  • Produce and post viral Instagram content for a product. Reels are short vertical videos on Instagram.
    1. Order the product from Amazon to your own address so you can check it firsthand.
    2. Do not post any Reels until you have produced 3 weeks' worth of content.
    3. Study the largest pages and videos that made content for your product.
    4. Copy their video style, change elements based on comment feedback, and add something dramatic or crazy.
    5. Produce 2 to 3 videos per day. Make the full 3 weeks of content before posting. After 1 to 2 weeks, raise the daily count by 1 to 2 videos. This avoids shadow bans for spam. A shadow ban means the platform quietly hides your posts.
    6. Once all 3 weeks of content are ready, move on to the posting routine.
    7. Post on a strict schedule of 2 to 3 times a day for 3 weeks. For example, post at 2 pm EST, 6 pm EST and 10 pm EST. EST means Eastern Standard Time, a US time zone.
    8. After 1 week, change posting times if you spot time-of-day trends (patterns tied to certain hours).
    9. Post content immediately, sticking to your content strategy (your plan for what to post).
    10. Use the hashtags (words marked with #) with the highest volume (most searched) and those your competitors (other sellers) use. Write hashtags inside short sentences that describe the audience and the look. For example, a shark slides product tagged with a university students group and a fashionable look.
    11. Read the feedback and comments on every video. Look for recurring comment themes (topics that keep coming up).
    12. Reply to as many comments on your videos as possible.
    13. Aim for 10k views in the first 5 days to build momentum (early energy that helps reach more people).
    14. Use boost post (paying Instagram to show a post to more people) on early content. This adds initial momentum to your account or helps gain followers.
  • Target 2 to 3 videos per day on Instagram. Finish 3 weeks of content before the first post, then post on a schedule of 2 to 3 times daily for 3 weeks.
  • Ramp up daily video output gradually. After 1 to 2 weeks, add 1 to 2 more videos per day. This way the account does not look like spam and risk a shadow ban.
  • Posting reels before 3 weeks of content is ready makes the account look like spam. So does posting identical clips without changes. Both can lead to shadow bans.
  • One Reels video reached 18,000 views on day one and about 3.5 million in total.
  • Reels can drive large view counts even for products that will not sell well on them.
  • Cross-post proven TikTok content to Reels once TikTok sales appear.

Landing pages

1 min
  • A landing page is a single page built to get one action from visitors. Its hero section (the big top area people see first) carries most of the conversion weight (the power to turn visitors into buyers).
  • So the hero section must answer the buyer's questions immediately. It must cover:
    • What they get.
    • The main benefit or desired outcome.
    • Why they should trust you.
    • Reduced risk.
    • The offer.
    • A call to action.
  • The rest of the page is for visitors who still need more information.

Email and SMS copy

1 min
  • Email copy and ad copy use the same persuasion mechanisms (proven ways to get people to act) as product copy. They are formatted differently. Writers should apply the same building blocks to these formats, adapting length and layout to each.
  • For welcome emails (the first email a new subscriber gets), the offer must be clear and easy to understand.
  • Strong products sell themselves. A simple discount drives the click (the tap that opens the email or link).
  • Long product copy matters less than a clear offer line.
  • Welcome email prompt (a set of instructions for an AI writing tool). Fill in the placeholders, which are the words in curly brackets, with your own details:
You are an ecommerce email copywriter. Write a welcome email for {BRAND_NAME}, sent to new subscribers who signed up through {SIGNUP_OFFER}. Include a warm greeting, one short sentence about the brand, the offer {OFFER_DETAILS} stated clearly, and one call-to-action button text. Keep the entire email under 200 words, use plain and concise language that is easy to understand, and focus on the offer rather than long product descriptions.

Content mix and formats

1 min
  • Content should be produced in a mix of content types rather than one format. The format itself is not fixed. The same idea can be a blog post or a video.
  • Six types are the core of a content production system (your repeatable way of making content):
    • How-to.
    • Listicle (a list-style article).
    • Ultimate guide.
    • Best of the interwebs (a roundup of the best finds online).
    • Expert roundup (several experts answering one question).
    • Controversial content (posts that take a clear, debated stance).
  • Other formats are allowed when they serve a clear purpose.
  • Controversial content works because people engage strongly with it.
  • Taking a clear opinion may push some people away. But it ties the rest closer to the brand.
  • Staying in the middle makes a brand boring and unnoticed.

Video content for stores

3 min
  • Video content for an online store works best when it is evergreen (it keeps bringing viewers for years after publishing).
  • It is not only for a short social media feed. On those feeds, the algorithm (the platform's ranking rules) stops showing a video after a while.
  • Evergreen value depends more on how the video is used and distributed than on how it is produced.
  • Video formats that suit a store include unboxings (filming the opening of a product), how-to videos, listicles, product reviews, customer video reviews and FAQ videos (answers to common questions).
  • Unboxing videos filmed from above are a simple format. They can attract searches for a popular product.
  • A reusable content strategy: write a blog post first. Then turn the same content into a video by recording narration (a spoken voice-over) that reads the post aloud.
  • Publish the video on the store's content page and on YouTube (a video site). Then both the text and the video can rank (appear high in search results) and bring traffic.
  • This is a low-effort entry point for a store that already produces written content.
  • Production quality matters less than openness, honesty and helpfulness for content videos. This is not true for video ads.
  • A good video moves quickly, skips padding (filler), opens with a clear introduction that says what the viewer will learn, and is concise.
  • A strong introduction tells the viewer what they will get from the video in the first moments. YouTube rewards videos that viewers watch for a long time.
  • Viewers who leave early lower the ranking of a video. So an intro that explains why to watch to the end matters more than a fast start.
  • Simple video production workflow for a store owner:
    1. Record the raw video (unedited footage) or audio without worrying about mistakes. Retakes and bloopers can stay in.
    2. Send the raw files, for example from the phone via cloud storage (online file storage), to a freelance editor (a hired, independent video cutter).
    3. The editor cuts out mistakes and pauses, and assembles the final video so it flows.
    4. Publish the edited video.
  • LED Grow Lights Depot is a reseller drop-shipping store. A drop-shipping store sells products that suppliers ship directly to buyers, so it does not hold or produce them. A reseller sells other brands' products. This store sells grow lights from many brands.
  • Its YouTube channel had about 22,000 subscribers after roughly two years.
  • Its videos earned between about 21,000 and 35,000 views each.
  • It produced reviews, buyer's guides and top-7 lists.
  • Its videos are often slideshows or product walk-throughs with recorded voiceover. The descriptions link to products, so the videos drive traffic back to its store.
  • The channel shows that simple, low-budget videos can attract engagement and traffic without expensive equipment.
  • Video content for a drop-shipping store can be made as a slideshow of product images with recorded narration. This gives a list or guide style video at very low production cost.
  • Descriptions should link to the products shown and to related pages on the store.
  • Content videos do not need professional production to work. A creator with no prior video experience can start immediately.
  • A creator gets better by making videos regularly.
  • Opening line for a video, said in the first moments before the main content:
In this video you are going to learn [the specific outcome the viewer gets] and why [the reason it matters to them].

Product review videos

1 min
  • Structure of a product review video for a store:
    1. Show the product and list its features (what it has).
    2. Explain its benefits: what it does for the viewer and how it does it.
    3. Say why the viewer should buy it.
    4. Mention drawbacks honestly, if there are any.
  • For product review videos, choose a popular product that the store sells as the subject. Reviewing a product people already search for makes the video easier to find.

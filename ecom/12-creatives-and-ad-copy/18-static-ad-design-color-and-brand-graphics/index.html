Static ad design, color and brand graphics

Section 12.18 · 18 of 26 in this chapter · 46 min read

In short

  • Canva is an easy design tool. You move items with your mouse. The free plan is enough for all your store pictures.
  • Save your 3 brand colors and fonts in the Brand Kit first. Use the same colors and fonts everywhere.
  • Use the exact sizes in the size table. A logo is 800×300 pixels, and a favicon (the small icon in a browser tab) is 64×64 pixels.
  • A see-through background is a paid Canva feature. There is a free workaround.
  • Make text easy to read. Use big contrast (a clear difference between light and dark) and one main message per image.
More key points
  • Keep ads clean and simple. Extra decoration or space costs money and often brings no more sales.
  • Test before you pay more. Use a test (comparing two versions) to prove that bigger type, costly art or color earns its cost.
  • Reuse one proven picture. Change it only when a test shows a new one wins.
  • Never put a product or logo on a profile or cover photo. These images should show the person or lifestyle only.

Canva setup and Brand Kit

1 min
  • Canva is a beginner-friendly design tool. You move items with your mouse, which is called drag-and-drop. It is good for making store pictures without design experience.
  • The free plan is enough for all store image files. But exporting with a transparent background (a see-through background with no white box behind the image) is a paid feature. See Logo and icons.
  • Canva Pro is the paid version of Canva. It has a 30-day free trial. After the trial, it costs $12 per month. You can cancel before billing starts. Its Magic Media (an AI image maker), BG remover (a tool that removes the background behind a photo) and Animate (adds movement to a design) features are used to create themed ad and email images.
  • Set up the Brand Kit (a saved set of your brand colors and fonts) for consistency. Go to Canva Home → Brand Kit → Brand Colors. Add 3 brand colors. Black and white can still be used in addition. This speeds up design work and keeps your look consistent.
  • Use one set of colors and fonts (letter styles) across all designs for one brand. The Brand Kit stops you from restyling each design from scratch. It keeps style consistent across all your assets (all your image files).
  • Canva is the tool for creating ad images and graphics.
  • Canva is suitable for designing product banners (wide promotional images) and other promotional images. Photoshop (a professional design program that is harder to learn) also works. It works as long as the designer is comfortable with the tool.

Logo, favicon and icons

3 min
  • Logo size (Manhattan theme): The Manhattan theme is the design template for your Shopify store (Shopify is the online store software). Use 800×300 pixels. A pixel is one tiny dot in a picture. Use PNG format (an image file type that can hold a see-through background). A transparent background is preferred.

Create a logo:

  1. Use the words from your domain name (your web address) as the logo text.
  2. Edit the font (letter style), boldness, size, line spacing (gap between lines) and letter spacing (gap between letters).
  3. Keep the design simple: text plus one small icon.
  4. Add the icon via Elements (Canva's menu of shapes and graphics) → search → Graphics.
  5. Avoid overly complex logos. Keep them clean and readable.

  • Transparent background is a paid Canva feature. Export with a transparent background is paid.

  • Free workaround:

    1. Download the PNG with a white background.
    2. Use a free online background remover tool.
    3. Upload the transparent PNG to Shopify.

  • Favicon size: Use 64×64 pixels. Use JPG (a common image file type) or PNG. A transparent background is preferred. The favicon is shown in browser tabs (the small icon next to your page name).

Create a favicon:

  1. Open the favicon Canva template (a ready-made design layout).
  2. Copy and paste your logo icon into the favicon template.
  3. Download as PNG with a transparent background if possible.
  • Mega menu icon size (Manhattan theme): Use 250×250 pixels. Use JPG or PNG. You can add up to 5 icons. A mega menu is the large drop-down menu at the top of your store.

Create several mega menu icon options:

  1. Open the Mega Menu Icons template.
  2. Create different icon options with Add Page (a new page in the same design). Examples: a letter icon like "S", a wave icon, a surfboard icon.
  3. Download all pages at once as a set.

Icon sources and colors:

  • Canva's Elements panel has a Graphics tab for icons and illustrations. It has a Photos tab for stock photos (ready-to-use professional photos). Free stock photos are not available for every niche (every product area). Basic or technical products may need images from external stock sites.

  • Icons for mega menus and USP sections (USP means unique selling point, the reason someone should buy from you) can come from Canva's free graphics library. Some icons allow more than one color. Keep icons consistent in style and color so they look like one set.

  • Some free Canva graphics can be recolored in more than one color. So one icon can use two or more brand colors without being drawn by hand. Single-color icons can only be recolored to one color. Check the color count when choosing icons for a multi-color mega menu or logo.

  • USP icons work best as simple single-color icons on a white or brand-colored background, with a short label. Use the brand palette (your brand colors) so they match the rest of the store.

  • Pre-footer icon size (Manhattan theme): The pre-footer is the section just above the bottom of your store. It is titled "Why Buy From Us". Use less than 285×285 pixels. Use JPG or PNG with a transparent background. You can add up to 7 icons. Examples: Free Shipping, Secure Checkout, Customer Support, Niche/Founder-owned. (A niche is your product area. Founder-owned means the founder runs the business.)

Create pre-footer icons:

  1. Use the icon template.
  2. Elements → search icons → Graphics.
  3. Download as PNGs.
  4. Keep icon style consistent across all icons. Use the same visual feel and color scheme.

Banners and product page graphics

3 min

Banner without text:

  1. Elements → search niche keyword → Photos.
  2. Drag the image onto the canvas (the blank design area).
  3. Right-click → Set image as background.
  4. Adjust the crop (cutting off the edges to fit) and position as needed.

Banner with text:

  1. Text → add heading.
  2. Optional: add shapes (for example, a rectangle) behind the text for contrast.
  3. Or use Canva templates (ready-made designs) and swap in your own images.

Import external stock photos into Canva:

  1. Download the image from a stock photo site.
  2. Drag the image into Canva.
  3. Use Uploads → drag onto the design → Set as background.

Use your own or downloaded photos:

  1. Drag the image file from your computer into the Canva window to upload it. Wait for the upload to finish. Large files can take a minute.
  2. Go to Uploads and drag the image onto the design.
  3. Right-click the image and choose Replace background (swap out the background).
  4. Canva crops the image to the size the template needs.

  • Product page graphic purpose: It is a promotional banner on product pages. Use it for sales, urgency (pressure to buy now), or coupon codes. It can be updated or removed anytime.

  • Create it using a template with bold brand colors.

  • Text examples: "Huge Sale", "Save 10% with code [CODE]", and an optional expiry date.

  • Collection page banner: Use a template. Set niche-relevant images as the background. Keep the design simple with no text. A collection is a group of products in your store.

Button for a banner in Canva:

  1. Open Elements and add a square shape.
  2. Resize the square to fit the button label. Set its color to a brand color.
  3. Add a heading with the label (for example "Shop now") and drag it over the square.
  4. Select both elements. Use the three-dot menu and choose Position > Center.
  • Outline on text: Select the text and open Effects. Add an Outline around the letters. Test the outline color against the background first. A poor color pairing can make the text harder to read.
  • Gradient banner: Select the banner box and click Colors. Choose a gradient fill (a color that fades from one shade to another) instead of a solid black fill. The banner blends with the image and stays readable.
  • Promotional banner on an ad: It must be clearly visible. Use a high-contrast color (a strong difference between light and dark) and a readable font. The banner colors should harmonize (look good together) with the creative (the ad image). The font should match the product page.

Add a Black Friday banner to a proven winning ad:

  1. Pick an ad that already performs well.
  2. Add a banner at the top with the offer text. Black Friday is a big shopping day after Thanksgiving.
  3. Use a box with a high-contrast color and readable font.
  4. Use a gradient fill instead of plain black if the banner should blend better with the image.
  5. Match the font to your product page so the customer journey (the path a customer takes from ad to purchase) looks consistent.
  • Discount badges (small sticker-style labels) should be the most eye-catching element on a sale design. Place them so the eye lands on them first. Their color should come from the brand palette. The badge must still stand out against the photo.
  • Monthly promotion images: Use Canva for simple designs, or hire someone on Fiverr (a site for hiring freelancers). Create a few unique images for each month's promotion. Use them across Display (ads on other websites), Email and Social campaigns. Adjust the aspect ratio slightly for Discovery ads (ads shown in feed-style spots).
  • Black Friday and holiday templates: Search Canva for "Black Friday" to find ready-made designs. Use them as thumbnails (small preview images) and ad visuals. This saves production time when running volume tests (testing many versions).
  • Banners for affiliate use: An affiliate is a partner who earns a commission on sales. Banners made for affiliates can serve as display network ads (ads across many websites) or on individual websites. A designer may call their service AdWords (the old name for Google Ads) or AdRoll (a retargeting ad company) banners. Those same files still work for affiliate banner placements.

Layout and visual hierarchy

5 min

  • Design should support the copy (the written words). It should focus attention on main points. It should guide the eye through the design.

  • Fonts, style and design matter. Aim for legibility (easy to read) and simplicity.

  • Start with a simple design. Slowly add complexity only if needed.

  • If it feels unbalanced or off, it is.

  • Ad graphics affect the conversion rate (how many viewers buy). Poor visuals make a store and its ads look untrustworthy.

  • Learn core design and photography principles before launching many ads. Do not rush the launch.

  • Canva offers free graphic design and video editing courses that help beginners.

  • If brand weight (how well-known your brand is) is low, make the product benefit and offer absolutely clear in one image.

  • Include: the product visual, the offer amount, scarcity (a pressure like an event end date) and core benefits.

  • Everything must be readable at a glance.

  • Eight basic design principles are best practice for harmonious (well-matched) layouts. They are: emphasis, contrast, white space (empty space), movement, repetition, proportion, balance and alignment.

  • Apply them together in every creative. The only exception is a jarring (harsh or clashing) effect that you want on purpose.

  • Emphasis means making the most important element stand out through color. Minimise the others when needed. Decide first what the viewer must see first, such as the offer, the sale tag or the buy-now button. Then emphasise that.

  • Visual hierarchy means ordering all elements by importance. Use color, size, placement and direction to show each level. For example, a title in one color, a subtitle in a second color and body text in a third color make the reading order clear.

  • Contrast comes in four types: color contrast, size contrast, type contrast (different font styles) and shape or direction contrast. Contrast matters most for sale tags, buttons and other elements that must stand out.

  • Size contrast can change how equal objects look. The same circle looks smaller when surrounded by larger shapes. It looks bigger when surrounded by smaller ones. Judge sizes in context, not alone.

  • Mistake: Too much information in one design causes cognitive overload (the brain gets overwhelmed). Viewers then leave without reading it. Titles pushed against the edge of a layout also make a design look cramped. It has no breathing space.

  • Movement means the layout guides the eye from the most important element to the next. For example: title → subheading → text → call to action (the button or line telling people what to do). Arrange elements so this path flows without breaks.

  • Repetition: Repeated headers, colors, button styles and navigation items show that elements belong together. They create consistency. Use the same format for similar elements across a page or campaign.

  • Proportion: Larger elements are read as more important than smaller ones. So size is a tool for emphasis. Keep real-world sizes realistic (for example, a product next to a cup). Oversizing is fine only as a deliberate choice.

  • Golden ratio is a proportion system used to size and place elements so they look harmoniously related. It is often shown as a spiral. The exact formula is not given here. Verify it before use.

  • Balance: Harmony comes from spreading visual weight across the design. Use a center line, a diagonal or a top-bottom axis (an invisible line through the middle). Balance can be symmetrical (mirror-like) or asymmetrical (different on each side). A large or bold element can be offset by a group of smaller elements on the other side.

  • Color can create balance in an asymmetrical layout. This works when colors switch sides in a symmetrical way. Elements of similar weight, such as a product and its new version, can balance each other without being identical.

  • Alignment means lining up elements to a shared edge, such as the left edge. It gives order, improves readability and lets people scan quickly. Misaligned text blocks make a design look sloppy and slow down reading.

  • Even spacing, consistent alignment and symmetry make a design look balanced and professional. Use the alignment and space evenly tools in Canva. Do not place elements by eye.

  • Symmetry: Symmetrical layouts feel ordered and complete. But a design does not need full symmetry. Asymmetrical layouts still feel harmonious when elements of similar visual weight sit on both sides of a central line.

  • Reading order: Western viewers scan from left to right and from top to bottom. So place the headline at the top left, with the most important visual next to it. Put the offer, price and call to action in that order along the path toward the bottom right.

  • Lines or shapes that shrink toward a central point create a sense of movement into depth. Pointing shapes, such as an arrow or a letter with a directional stroke, pull the eye along a path to the next element.

  • Example: A bold headline in bright red on a near-black background creates strong color contrast. It draws the eye first. Use this pairing when the headline must be read before anything else.

  • Example: A contrasting background panel (a box behind content) placed behind secondary information, such as a benefits list, makes that information stand out. The panel should be clearly different in color from the area around it. The eye then reads it as a separate block.

  • Example: A cool blue and a warm orange work well together as a palette (a set of colors). Use the orange as the accent (the highlight color) for the promotion or price. It stands out against a blue-based design.

  • An element can become more important through a distinct texture (surface look) or a different shape and style. Size and color are not the only tools. A sale tag or key offer can use this to stand apart from the rest of the layout.

  • Transparency or opacity (how see-through something is) controls how strongly an element stands out. Lower it to push an element into the background. Keep higher values for the element that should be emphasized.

  • Visual hierarchy can be built in 3D with shadows, as well as in flat design.

Break down an existing ad or template and check its design:

  1. Find which elements are grouped by proximity (being close together), similarity (looking alike) or shared space. Find which are separated.
  2. Check how much negative space (empty space around things) surrounds the product and the main message.
  3. Read the layout in the usual reading order. Note which element the eye reaches first, second and last.
  4. Draw a diagonal line across the layout. Check whether the visual weight on each side is roughly balanced.
  5. Confirm that the promotion and the call to action are easy to find. Check that the layout does not have too many competing elements.

Grouping and perception (Gestalt principles)

1 min

Gestalt principles describe how people see a whole before its parts. They fill in gaps, dislike incomplete shapes and group things by similarity and difference. Designers use these tendencies to arrange ads, banners and product pages.

  • Closure: Viewers mentally complete a shape when the designer leaves gaps or implied lines in the negative space. This lets a design use fewer elements and still read as a complete image.
  • Common fate: Elements that point or move in the same direction are read as one group. An element that breaks that direction and color pattern stands out.
  • Continuation: The eye follows the smoothest line through a layout. Place the most important elements along that path. This guides the viewer toward the intended message or action.
  • Similarity: Elements that share color, shape or size are grouped, even when far apart. To make one element stand out, make it clearly different in color, shape or size.
  • Figure ground: The brain separates a focal foreground object from its background. Some images can flip which part reads as foreground. Use clear separation so the product or focal point is immediately readable.
  • Law of Pragnanz: The brain reduces complex or unclear shapes to the simplest known shapes. Adding color or separation can reveal the simple parts inside a complex shape. This helps when designing logos and icons.
  • Overlapping elements link them more strongly than simply placing them near each other. A deliberate overlap can show that two items form one unit. Use overlaps sparingly, because a crowded overlap reduces readability.

Depth and shadows

1 min
  • Shadows add depth to show importance. A larger, darker and broader shadow below an element makes it look further above the background. That signals higher importance. This follows Google's Material Design (Google's design rulebook). It is often used for sales tags and other key elements.
  • In Canva, a drop shadow (a shade cast under an object) can be set with a lower opacity and a blur. This creates a lifted, subtle depth effect. Heavy shadows look dramatic. Use them sparingly, only where the lift should signal importance.
  • Use a three-dimensional layer approach on top of the flat 2D hierarchy. Bring important elements forward with shadows and push secondary elements back. This is another way to show importance, beyond size and color.
  • Drop shadows create visual hierarchy by making key elements stand out. One example is a satisfied-customers counter.
  • Use a shadow or gradient behind text or key elements. This creates visual hierarchy and makes text easier to read. Use a white gradient behind dark text and a dark gradient behind white text.

Product graphics and templates

2 min

Quick image ad with Canva:

  1. Get a product photo.
  2. Remove the background, which can also be done inside Canva.
  3. Open Canva templates and search by product features or by offer type such as a free gift.
  4. Choose a template, place the product in it and add the offer text.
  5. Make small brand adjustments so the ad looks consistent with your store.

Build a product graphic in Canva from a template:

  1. Search for the product type in Canva to find product templates.
  2. Open a template that fits the layout you want and replace the text and imagery in it.
  3. Copy a product image from the web and paste it into Canva so it uploads to your Uploads section.
  4. Open Edit photo and run the Background remover on the product image.
  5. Brush out any leftover background or unwanted parts with the brush control.
  6. Position the product near the center of the design using Alt or Option drag and the arrow keys, then add a shadow from the element shadow options.
  7. Add the offer text, group it, and move the group behind the product if it should sit beneath it.
  8. Apply your brand colors from the Brand Kit, and mute harsh colors by adding a gray tone rather than leaving them at full strength.

Offer badge pattern: A short label such as "Limited edition" or "Free gift" is placed on a shape or band near the product, and it reads as an urgency or bonus signal. Pattern: [short scarcity or bonus word] + [product or offer noun], placed in the top or bottom corner of the image in a contrasting brand color. Example you can adapt: "Only 50 Left" and "Free Carry Case Included."

Product layouts: Product layouts that work well usually have a product image, space for several short benefits, and a clear call to action. Find inspiration by browsing product templates and copying the structure, not the exact design.

Them vs Us creative:

  1. Place your product cut-out on the right side of a two-column layout.
  2. Place the competitor product on the left side.
  3. Desaturate or grade the competitor background so it looks weaker than yours.
  4. Add a subtle pattern and a white gradient overlay on top for texture.
  5. Write the product benefits as short points next to your product.

Photo framing: Use photo framing to show the product being sold. When a model's face takes up most of the image, reposition or reframe the photo so the product stays visible.

Overlay text on product images: Graphic overlays with text on product images (offer, benefits, social proof) can be used in ad imagery. Google may stop showing an image that contains text, but the product is not banned, so the image is simply not shown. This was being tested as a way to create a strong hero section without scrolling.

Ad breakdown: See the procedure under Layout and visual hierarchy.

Color and Photoshop techniques

1 min
  • Mixing a color with tint, tone or shade rather than picking a new color keeps related colors consistent across a design. Muting a bright color by adding gray usually makes it easier to read next to brand colors.
  • Photoshop gradient: The Gradient tool creates an editable gradient on any layer, and clipping it to an object applies it only to that object. Double-click a color stop to change the gradient color.
  • Blend modes: In Photoshop, blend modes change how a layer interacts with the layers under it: Darken shows only the darker parts, Lighten shows only the lighter parts, and Color Burn or Color Dodge create texture effects such as a moon texture on a shape.
  • Texture or detail overlay pattern: A texture image placed above a shape with the blend mode Color Burn adds darkness and detail while keeping the base color. Pattern: base shape + grayscale texture above it + Color Burn blend + mask to limit the effect to the area that needs it. Example you can adapt: a cracked stone texture over a dark badge, and a fabric weave overlay over a sweatshirt product shape.

Color: meaning, mood and balance

7 min

Principles

  • Color sets mood, tone and brand perception, so pick the base color first according to the meaning you want to convey and how you want the brand to be perceived. Choose the color meaning deliberately before building the rest of the palette.
  • Warm colors (red, orange, yellow) feel energetic and attention-grabbing, while cool colors (green, blue, violet) feel calm, relaxing and cooling. Use this split to decide which family fits the emotional effect of a design.
  • Use a limited number of key principles when building a palette: choose a color for the brand meaning, use neutral colors as backdrops, control contrast with tints, tones and shades, choose a typography that suits the brand, and test colors and type in action.
  • Adding white, gray or black to a palette prevents colors from clashing and looking like a rainbow. Adjusting how much white, gray and black you add is one of the most effective ways to balance a design.

Color terms

  • Hue is the base color, and a color can change in lightness through tint (white added), tone (gray added) and shade (black added). Tint, tone and shade are the main tools for adjusting a palette to create balance.
  • Chroma measures how pure or saturated a hue is, with higher chroma giving more intense colors. Saturation is about removing color, which makes the color look washed out, as opposed to adding white, gray or black.
  • Hexadecimal color values use six characters, with the first two for red, the middle two for green and the last two for blue. Many page builders, including Shopify page builders, use RGB values with three numbers from 0 to 255 instead.

Individual colors

  • Red: Red is an intense attention color that can overwhelm quickly, so use it sparingly as an accent. Brighter reds feel more energetic, while darker reds feel more powerful and elegant.
  • Red across cultures: In Western markets red signals anger, alert, danger and passion, in China it signals prosperity and happiness, and in some Eastern cultures it is worn by brides. Check the target market before using red as a main color.
  • Orange: Orange signals vibrancy, energy, change and creativity, and it draws attention without the aggression of red. Its typical use is for buttons, alerts and clickable elements, and it can look friendlier and more inviting than red.
  • Accent colors: An accent color should appear only in small pieces such as links, buttons or a title detail, not flood the whole page. Used this way it draws the eye to clickable or important items.
  • Yellow: Yellow is the brightest, most energizing and warmest color and is associated with happiness and cheerfulness. Softer or muted yellows feel calm and neutral and are often used as a gender-neutral choice.
  • Yellow across cultures: Yellow is associated with deceit and cowardice in some contexts, and with hope in Egypt, courage in Japan and merchants in India, so meaning varies by country. Darkened yellows and golds can look antique.
  • Green: Green stands for growth, new beginnings, renewal and abundance, and it is strongly linked to wealth and stability. Dark green signals affluence and prosperity, bright green feels energizing, and muted olive green feels natural.
  • Green can also signal envy or jealousy and a lack of experience. It combines the calming effect of blue with the energy of yellow, which makes it suitable for health, well-being and nature-related designs.
  • Blue: Blue meaning depends strongly on hue and shade: light blues feel refreshing and friendly, bright blues feel energizing, and dark blues signal strength, trust, reliability and peace. Dark blue is therefore a common choice for finance-like or trust-focused brands.
  • Purple: Purple combines red and blue, so it carries both energy and calm. Dark purple signals royalty, wealth and luxury, while lighter purples like lavender feel romantic, and purple is associated with creativity and imagination.
  • Purple is the mourning color for widows in Thailand, so check cultural meaning before using it in a target market.
  • Black: Black is the strongest neutral color and is associated with power, elegance and formality, while negative associations include mystery, death and mourning in many Western countries. Black is widely used for typography and for making images stand out against it.
  • White: White stands for cleanliness, purity, simplicity and health care, and it works as a neutral backdrop that lets other colors shine. White is worn by brides in the West and represents morning in much of the East, so consider the target audience.
  • Brown: Brown is hard to balance because of its lightness and darkness, and it is associated with earth, wood, stone, reliability and warmth. Avoid using heavy brown in large amounts, since it can make a design look heavy.
  • Ivory and beige: Ivory is a tinge of white with cream, and beige is a tinge of brown; both act like grays or neutrals and are used in a similar way to black and white in different shades.

Neutrals and grays

  • Neutral colors take on the characteristics of the colors around them: gray next to blue feels cooler and gray next to red feels warmer. Use neutrals as backdrops that are shaped by the accent colors placed beside them.
  • Charcoal gray instead of pure black softens contrast on screen, making white text easier on the eyes while keeping strong readability. Light grays can replace whites and dark grays can replace blacks in layouts and typography.
  • Gray text on white backgrounds is easier to read and softer than black on white, but contrast must still meet web accessibility standards so that users with weaker eyesight can read the content.
  • For section backgrounds, use an extremely light tint of the palette color rather than a dark gray, because heavy gray behind colored elements looks jarring. A near-white tinted background separates sections gently against the text.

Palette patterns

  • Analogous: Analogous palettes use colors that sit next to each other on the color wheel and feel harmonious. Beginners should use analogous palettes, adjusting tint, tone, shade and chroma, because they are the easiest way to build balanced designs.
  • Monochromatic: Monochromatic palettes use one hue with different tints, tones, shades and chroma levels. They are easy to build and reliable for clean designs.
  • Complementary: Complementary colors sit opposite each other on the color wheel and create strong contrast. Use one as an accent and not in equal amounts, and tone it down with gray, white or darkening to avoid a jarring result.
  • Complementary colors must be used carefully, since a complement of a pale pink can clash badly. Darkening or muting the complementary color, or using it only as a small accent, can make it work.
  • Blue and orange are a complementary pair that works well when orange is kept as an accent against blue. Keeping the design built on similar colors first and only then taking bold contrast steps produces the best results.
  • Triadic: Triadic color schemes use three colors evenly spaced on the color wheel and are very difficult to get right, so beginners should avoid them.

Build a palette:

  1. Choose a base color that fits the meaning you want to convey.
  2. Pick neighboring colors on the color wheel or variations of the same color.
  3. Use the color wheel in the design software to add white, gray or black.
  4. Move the color up (towards white) to lighten, down (towards black) to darken, and diagonally from top right to bottom left to add gray.
  5. Test the palette until it looks balanced.

Tool: Adobe Color can create palettes from color wheel options such as analogous, monochromatic, triad and complementary, and it saves palettes as color schemes. Search by a theme such as nature to generate starting palettes.

Canva palette suggestions: Canva can read the colors of a placed photo and suggest matching palettes, so product or brand colors can be applied to a layout quickly. Use the suggested palette as a starting point and adjust it to your brand colors.

Buttons and backgrounds

  • Button colors should stand out against the background and be darker and more saturated than surrounding colors, while avoiding pure black because it is too heavy. A call-to-action such as "Shop now" should be the most noticeable element to support visual hierarchy.

Apply the palette in practice (procedure):

  1. Start with a color palette in Adobe Color or a similar tool.
  2. Download a font pair, such as a serif title font and a sans-serif body font.
  3. Place a title, subtitle and body text in the design software and set sizes.
  4. Use dark text on white backgrounds, keeping the text readable without being too contrasting.
  5. Lighten or darken colors on dark backgrounds, such as adding white to make text punch out.
  6. Test the palette in action before committing.

Start simple: Build from similar colors first and only then take bold contrast steps.

Typography

1 min
  • Google Fonts: Google Fonts provides free font families and pairings that can be downloaded as full families. Use them to test typography pairings alongside a color palette before building designs.
  • Serif and sans-serif: Serif fonts have small decorative strokes called serifs and often look traditional or formal, while sans-serif fonts are cleaner, simpler and more minimalistic. Sans-serif fonts are usually more readable for body text, while serifs often work well for titles.
  • Font pairs: Font pairs can combine two serifs, two sans-serifs, or a serif with a sans-serif, and a common pattern is a serif for titles with a sans-serif for paragraphs. Each font has its own voice, so choose fonts that match the desired look and feel.
  • Choose a font pair (procedure):
    1. Identify the niche or desired mood.
    2. Search blogs, font library catalogs or design platforms for font pairings that fit that niche.
    3. Download and test the pairs next to each other.
    4. Adjust font weight, size and layout until the look matches the intended feeling.
  • Use a plain sans-serif font such as Open Sans for readable ad copy, and replace it with your brand font if you have one.
  • Use a line height of 1.5 times the font size for good readability and a balanced layout. Most website builders apply this automatically, but adjust it when text feels cramped after increasing the font size.
  • Font weight affects importance and mood, with heavier weights usually used for titles. Letter spacing should be wide enough for easy reading and should not be too condensed.
  • Ascenders are the upper parts of letters like f, h and capitals, while descenders are the lower tails of letters such as g and p. Knowing these terms helps adjust line height and text spacing.
  • In Canva, the logo and headline fonts should match the store: use the heading font for headlines and the body font for body text.
  • Change any all-caps template text so it fits the brand style.

Text legibility on mobile and over images

1 min
  • Mobile first: Text placed on an image shrinks when the ad is shown on mobile, so keep headlines large and short. Most people see ads on phones, and a very wide banner gets squeezed into a small, thin strip. Square or near-square images keep overlaid text readable. Check text legibility at phone size.
  • Gradients behind text: To keep overlaid text readable, place a white gradient behind dark text and a dark gradient behind white text.
  • Dark and light backgrounds: On dark backgrounds, lighten the text color by adding white or more color, because dark text on a dark background is unreadable. On light backgrounds, darken the text instead of using pure white to keep it legible.
  • Text over a busy photo or video in Canva:
    1. Align the text block to the left edge so it looks clean.
    2. Change the text color to white (or black on light backgrounds).
    3. Add an effect such as a lift or a dark drop shadow, then blur it slightly and lower its opacity if it looks heavy.
    4. If the text is still hard to read, add a gradient graphic (search "gradient" in Canva graphics) that goes from a solid color to transparent behind the text, using black for white text and white for black text, and lower its opacity if needed.
  • Mistake: Text placed over an image with both very light and very dark areas, such as bright sky above dark buildings, becomes hard to read. Before adding text, check the tones under the text block and fix the problem with a gradient or shadow if the contrast is low.
  • Text limit: Keep text under about 20% of the image. Above that, Meta still runs the ad but limits its reach or raises the cost per result.
  • Snapper's grid feature shows how much of the image the text covers, so you can check the text limit before publishing.

Packaging and print

1 min

Packaging copy prompt:

I'm making custom packaging for [PRODUCT, for example a fleece legging] shipped in a [PACKAGING TYPE, for example a poly mailer, 10 x 13 inch]. Attached are inspiration images of other packaging designs and our brand guideline. Based on our brand voice, give me options for words to print on the packaging in these groups: 1) inside flap or inner message, 2) texture or product callouts that describe benefits in plain, non-technical words, 3) short playful brand-voice lines. Keep every line short, on brand, and suitable for [SEASON OR THEME, for example the cold season]. Then recommend one line for the front and one line for the side, with a short reason for each.

Mailer decision case: The lesson chose a 10 x 13 inch poly mailer for leggings and a fleece product because apparel ships well in this format. The packaging design was made for that exact size, and the same process applies to any product type.

Free-gift ebook visual system: A free-gift ebook can use a consistent visual system: a simple motif repeated on the cover and in the diagrams, a serif font for headings, a sans-serif font for body text, and a monospace font for small labels. This gives a calm, editorial look that suits a health or wellness topic.

Imagery, emotion and brand fit

1 min
  • Emotional messages can be carried by imagery instead of long text, so image and video creatives can lead with feeling and leave the specifications for later. Audiences differ in how they think, some in words and some in pictures, so combining copy with strong visuals reaches more buyers.
  • Use 4:5 for feed image and video ads, or 9:16 for TikTok-style vertical video. These are the recommended formats for image and video ads.
  • Applying brand guidelines to a multi-format template:
    1. Select all artboards in the template and copy them (Cmd+C).
    2. Open the brand guidelines document and paste the artboards there, so all work happens in one file.
    3. Select an element, then pick the brand color from the Selection colors panel; click See all colors if the palette is needed.
    4. Shift-select the artboard titles to apply the same color change to all selected artboards at once.
    5. Select all headline text and switch it to the heading font; select all body text and switch it to the body font.
    6. Change any all-caps template text so it fits the brand style.
    7. Drag the matching logo version (for example the black or black-and-white logo) from the logo panel onto each artboard and align it with the center tool.
    8. Duplicate the brand photos, use Remove Background for a cut-out effect, and adjust brightness and saturation in Photo Filter to match the brand energy.
    9. If a photo crops off the model's head, use Remix > Extend Photo with 9:16 vertical format, then regenerate and remove the background if needed.
    10. Move the photo so the product is clearly visible instead of the face.
    11. Rewrite all copy using the brand voice in ChatGPT, then paste the chosen options into each artboard.
    12. Review each draft for brand fit, then repeat the same process for the next template.

AI-generated and edited images

1 min
  • AI-generated images are made by a computer from a text instruction. They are often hard to change after they are made, so the layout of text and other elements may need changing in a design tool. Canva (an online design tool) can edit an AI-generated image, so the work does not have to restart in the chat each time.
  • Edit image: Open the image in the editor, click Edit image, then Magic Layers. Magic Layers (a Canva feature that splits a picture into separate parts you can change) splits the image into editable layers. Layers can then be removed, moved, swapped for another picture, or given new headline text.
  • Canva Magic Layers separates an AI-generated image into editable layers. It is used to remove elements, move them, or replace headlines and offer text. You do not need to generate the whole image again.

Seasonal and holiday ads

2 min

Canva steps for a holiday Instagram ad

  1. Create a design with the Instagram Post size 1080×1080. (px = pixels, the tiny dots that make up a picture.)
  2. Paste the product image with a white background.
  3. Remove the image background with Magic Background Remover (a Canva tool that cuts the product out of its background).
  4. Add a seasonal background from Magic Media (a Canva tool that creates a background picture from a text description), for example a spooky Halloween background.
  5. Add the text and apply Text Effects.
  • Canva is an online design tool for building social media ad images.
  • Its Magic Background Remover and Magic Media features can cut out a product photo and add a seasonal background quickly.

Procedure for a holiday-themed ad and email image in Canva

  1. Click Create a design and choose the Instagram post template, a square at 1080 x 1080 pixels. Use it for Facebook, Instagram, and Google remarketing (showing ads again to people who already visited your store).
  2. Paste a product image from your store into the design and enlarge it.
  3. Click BG remover (background remover) to remove the white background. This feature requires Canva Pro (a paid Canva plan).
  4. Enter a prompt (the text instruction you type for the AI) in Magic Media, such as "spooky Halloween backgrounds" with the filmic style (a movie-like look). Click generate image, which returns four variations.
  5. Choose a background, go to Layer, select Send to back (place it behind everything), and enlarge it to fill the canvas.
  6. If the background competes with the product, lower its transparency (make it more see-through).
  7. Click Text, add a text box, search for a themed font such as Halloween, and set it large.
  8. Paste the promotion text, for example "10% off Halloween", and use Effects > Background for a box behind the text.
  9. Click an element, then Animate, and choose a motion such as moving up.
  10. Click Share and download as an MP4 (a video file) for Facebook remarketing ads, or as a GIF (a short looping animation file) to embed in emails so the animation plays in the inbox.

Layout and visual hierarchy

2 min
  • Keep creatives (ad designs) clean, clear and simple, because simplicity beats finesse (fancy polish).
  • Unpolished design is acceptable only when it is deliberate or native to the platform (made to look like the platform's own content), such as memes that match the style of the feed.
  • Learn five basic graphic design skills for ads: spacing (empty room between elements), alignment, grouping related items, color and balance.
  • Spacing and grouping are among the most common weaknesses in beginner creatives.
  • Use a larger opening block or a bold highlight to start the reader.
  • Large opening type draws readers in. Smaller body type (the main text) later does not stop them once they are reading.
  • Use a bold guarantee or a key claim as the lead-in (the first line that leads into the offer).
  • Leave no wasted space in direct response ads (ads that ask for an immediate reply or order).
  • Every line should carry a selling point (a reason to buy). Avoid borders or decorative blank areas.
  • Include a coupon (a discount code) as a reminder device when the reader needs to act later.
  • Returns (results) scale with ad space (how much room the ad takes).
  • Halving a full-page ad's copy area on the same page roughly doubles cost per reply (the price paid for each response). Doubling the space roughly doubles replies.
  • Justify any extra space or larger type with a traced test (a test you can measure and track) before buying it.
  • Use normal, readable body type in the style of newspapers and magazines.
  • Larger type does not increase response and costs more. Louder, bigger type adds cost without adding sales.
  • Use graphic devices that add voice to the copy, not decoration. Examples: bullets, numbering, underlining, bold type, varied type, simulated handwriting, boxes, asterisk lines, yellow highlighter overprint (yellow color laid over text), shaded screens (tinted background panels), photographs with text wrapped around them, and many subheads (small headings inside the text).
  • The copywriter (the person who writes the words) should choose which devices to use and where, not leave that to the artist (the designer).
  • Default to a clean layout for ads.
  • Illustrated letters with photos, charts, cartoons and deliberately messy handwriting can outperform neat letters in split tests (tests that compare two versions side by side).
  • Test a busy version before rejecting it as unprofessional.
  • For tall, narrow display formats (ad spaces shaped like a tall column), stack two product images with the same copy in the middle to use the extra space. This avoids empty areas in the layout.

Color and typography

1 min
  • Use color only where tests show it pays, such as for food, dessert or fruit that looks more appealing in color.
  • In general, black and white ads perform as well or better. Color chosen to be attractive for its own sake rarely pays.
  • Pick fonts (letter styles) that fit the brand.
  • Niche-specific fonts (for example Font Space for themed fonts, checked for commercial use, meaning you may use them for business) or Google Fonts (free fonts from Google) for readability are both options.
  • Keep text short and well-spaced.
  • Avoid squeezed lines and cramped line heights (the space between lines of text).
  • Comment-card style reference (a Facebook-style comment card): light gray background, red-accented text and links, and a soft drop shadow (a soft dark edge that makes the card look lifted), with the card slightly overflowing the image edge for depth.
  • Open Sans Bold (a font) was suggested to match the look, but this was an untested guess. Compare against a real reference before copying the style.

Pictures, art and reuse

1 min
  • Use pictures only when they sell better than the same space filled with text. Examples: showing the product itself, or people the buyer admires in a setting they desire.
  • Decorative or eccentric (odd, unusual) pictures take attention from the offer and should be avoided.
  • Example: a striking picture (an eye-catching image) increased an ad's space by 50 percent. It raised cost per reply by the same 50 percent, with no extra sales.
  • The added cost produced no benefit, so the picture was removed.
  • Use expensive art only when tests show it earns more than cheaper art.
  • Mail-order advertisers (sellers who sell by post) often get good results with simple illustrations.
  • Art cost is small compared with the cost of space, so verify its value with a test.
  • Pictures can account for one-third to one-half of an ad campaign's cost (a campaign is a set of ads run together).
  • So pictures need the same proof as copy.
  • Some advertisers paid up to 2,000 dollars per drawing. That premium is justified only when a test shows expensive art beats cheaper art.
  • Reuse the same proven picture across ads when possible.
  • New customers rarely remember a picture from an earlier ad, and repetition lowers cost.
  • Change the picture only when a test shows a new one wins.

Canva, Snappa and workflows

3 min

Tools

  • Snappa (a web tool for making ad images) and Canva are the recommended tools for creating ad images in multiple sizes.
  • Snappa is used for all ad images, including video cover images.
  • Canva is a long-established alternative.
  • Canva can make ad creatives from basic color theory (rules for how colors work together), balance and graphic design knowledge.
  • Photoshop (a professional, paid image editor) is not required for good spacing, color and clear messages.
  • Canva can make a checkout banner (a strip on the payment page) at 1500 x 300 px with a logo, privacy/secure icons and payment processor logos (the logos of the companies that take payment).
  • Canva has a typical Facebook-style comment design.
  • Facebook comments are harder to recreate, so a designer may need Photoshop for the background fill and text replacement.
  • If you do not want to design retargeting images yourself, outsource (hire someone outside) to freelance graphic designers on Fiverr, Upwork or freeup.com (websites where freelancers offer work).
  • Retargeting means showing ads again to people who already visited your store. Choose designers with experience in display network (Google's network of ad spots on websites) or Facebook ad images.

Resizing one master design in Snappa

  1. Open the finished base design. It can be started in any size.
  2. Click Resize in the left menu.
  3. Choose a pre-made size, or enter a custom graphic size in width and height in px.
  4. When asked whether to override the current graphic or open a new one, choose to open it as a new graphic. Snappa carries all elements over to the new template size.
  5. Move the text and other elements to fit the new shape. Increase text sizes where there is more space, and adjust spacing.
  6. Resize the product image to fit the layout and place it.
  7. Save the design, then Download it.
  8. For the next size, open the saved design and repeat from step 2. All elements carry across.
  • Most static display sizes (fixed-size ad spaces that do not move) share similar shapes, so after the first design, resizing takes little time.
  • The tall filmstrip format needed the most re-spacing and took a couple of minutes.
  • Design one master layout and resize it to save the most time.

Building a retargeting image set

  1. Create one image for the new retargeting campaign.
  2. Produce that image in every required size for each network (an ad platform such as Google or Facebook). The networks are Google static (fixed-size ads), Google responsive (ads that resize themselves to fit), Facebook, Twitter and Taboola.
  3. Upload the sized versions to the networks.

The goal is to load one image across all networks without redesigning it each time.

Rebranding a landing page template for a new niche with Canva

A landing page is the single page a visitor arrives on. A niche is a small market. Rebranding means changing the look to match a new product.

  1. Copy the image URL (the web address of the picture). Open the product image in the browser (right click, Inspect; Inspect shows the page's code). Remove the surrounding size and parameter text (extra code after the address that sets the size) so the full-size image is loaded.
  2. Paste the image into Canva. Remove unneeded shadows and reflections.
  3. Pick fonts that fit the brand: niche-specific fonts (e.g. Font Space for themed fonts, checked for commercial use) or Google Fonts for readability.
  4. Upload font files to Canva. A zip (a folder packed into one file) containing the font files can be uploaded.
  5. Take colours from the product image using Canva's colour suggestions. Use a dark background for a sophisticated look.
  6. Keep text short and well-spaced. Avoid squeezed lines and cramped line heights.
  7. Export the three main graphics as PNG (a picture file type) and compress them (make the file smaller) before upload. The examples were about 900 KB for two images.
  8. Upload the images to Shopify (an online store platform). Then in PageFly (an app for building store pages), choose the fonts and apply them to headings.

Banners and display ads

1 min
  • On Facebook, text covering the image may not exceed 20% of the image area.
  • Text on Facebook images is optional, but can help text stand out when used.
  • To check the 20% limit, lay a grid overlay (a see-through grid placed on top) over the image in any image tool.
  • A retargeting image has one job: stop the scroll on social media (make people stop scrolling).
  • It can do this by showing something the viewer has seen before, such as branding or a known product picture.
  • Or it can stand out visually, for example with contrasting colors (very different colors next to each other) or something interesting.

Store graphics and hero images

1 min
  • A desktop hero image (the large main picture at the top of the homepage) should be about 1600 px wide and 400 to 600 px tall.
  • A taller hero image pushes the rest of the homepage below the fold (the part a visitor must scroll down to see), and the customer may never see it.
  • A checkout banner at 1500 x 300 px can carry the logo, privacy/secure icons and payment processor logos.
  • Landing page graphics: export as PNG and compress before upload.
  • Then check the fonts in PageFly under the procedure above.

Logo, profile and cover images

1 min
  • The profile photo is a square 1:1 portrait (width and height equal), direct to camera, warm and friendly.
  • It must not look like a corporate headshot (a formal business photo), stock-photo perfection (a too-perfect ready-made photo) or heavy polish.
  • Never use a logo or a product shot as the profile picture.
  • The cover photo uses a 3:1 aspect ratio (three times as wide as it is tall).
  • It shows a warm, aspirational (inspiring), editorial lifestyle scene from the character's world (the made-up person the page speaks as), such as a desk setup, gym or outdoor space.
  • It may show the character or the scene alone.
  • Profile and cover photos must contain no product shots, logos or text overlays (words placed on top).
  • Putting a product shot or a discount graphic on the cover or profile photo immediately signals a brand and undermines the character.
  • Keep those images strictly lifestyle and personal.
  • Whitelisting (running ads through someone else's page) character page images use two formats.
  • Format 1: a square 1:1 direct-to-camera profile portrait.
  • Format 2: a wide 3:1 lifestyle scene for the cover.

Letterheads and printed samples

1 min
  • A picture on the letterhead (the printed top of a letter) must add essential background to the offer, such as the setting of a product's use.
  • Decorative pictures alone may not help. Test a plain letterhead against a pictorial one before you commit.
  • A colored nature scene printed across the top and one side of a letterhead doubled the orders for a book series offer compared with a plain letterhead.
  • A small full-color reproduction of the product in the upper left corner of the letterhead added nearly 50 percent to the pulling power (how well it gets a response) of one letter.
  • When a business was built around one person, a photograph of that person on the letterhead added 10 to 20 percent to the pulling power of the letters.
  • Attach a small sample of the product to the letterhead, such as a fabric swatch for a coat or a leather swatch for luggage, so the reader can judge quality.
  • A tactile sample (something the reader can touch) is an effective attention-getter when the product has a visible quality.

Video thumbnails

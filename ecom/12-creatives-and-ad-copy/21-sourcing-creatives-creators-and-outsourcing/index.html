Sourcing creatives, creators and outsourcing

Section 12.21 · 21 of 26 in this chapter · 15 min read

In short

  • Get your first ads live fast, then expect most of them to fail, so plan for many tests.
  • You do not need to make everything yourself, but you must know enough to give clear instructions to helpers and judge their work.
  • Start with cheap content: your own phone videos, copies of competitor ideas, or image ads. Pay for creators only after a product shows it sells.
  • Outsourced ads do not guarantee results. One batch of 100 ads produced only about 3 winners.

Build or buy: in-house versus outsourced production

2 min
  • Creatives are often the bottleneck. A creative is an ad's image or video. The main problem is usually getting winning creatives (ads that sell) into your ad account quickly.
  • Outsourced creatives from freelance marketplaces do not guarantee results. Freelance marketplaces are websites where you hire freelancers, people who work for you on their own.
  • Building your own skill takes weeks or months.
  • You do not need to make every creative yourself.
  • You should still learn enough marketing to brief an outsourced designer or editor. To brief someone means giving them clear instructions.
  • A practical approach: get a first set of creatives live quickly. Then learn basic copywriting (writing words that sell) and basic ad knowledge. This lets you judge and direct outsourced work.
  • At the test stage, paying someone to film is usually too expensive to justify. A test is a short trial to see if a product sells.
  • Start with self-shot footage (filmed by you) or masked footage (video with faces hidden).
  • Consider hiring a filmer only after a product proves itself, meaning it shows it sells.
  • High-quality video ads from outside agencies can cost thousands of dollars per month.
  • Image ads let you test more creatives faster when budget is limited.
  • Video ads can come from four sources:
    • DIY iPhone-style videos you film yourself
    • Product videos supplied by vendors (the companies that sell or make the product)
    • Web designer animations of your infographics (simple charts and pictures)
    • Static image ads (still pictures), which work equally well and may be better for starting
  • Creatives should be recorded or sourced from freelancers when the owner (the person running the business) cannot find the right people in their own circle.
  • Fiverr and Bilo are freelance websites where you can find these people.
  • Outsource the parts of video production you dislike. Examples are on-camera recording, editing, scripting (writing the words) or voiceover (spoken narration).
  • This matters most if you feel nervous on camera.
  • Not appearing on camera is fine. Product videos and voiceover content work without the owner's face.
  • Email marketing can be outsourced to an agency (a company that does work for you) once it shows revenue (money coming in).
  • In one example, the outsourced email campaigns were kept because they produced revenue.
  • In the same example, Google Ads results were mixed for this product type. Google Ads are paid ads on Google.
  • Most traffic in that example came from organic sources, meaning free visitors who did not click paid ads.

Sourcing order for a product test

1 min

Source and build creatives for a product test in this order:

  1. Look at competitor ads (ads from other sellers) and supplier content for the same product.
  2. Shoot your own content in the same style, using a smartphone, if the product allows it.
  3. Use a script (written words to say or show) adapted from the content you saw. Change only a little, and test it for a short period.
  4. Edit pictures with a simple tool such as Canva (an easy design app), or a freelancer for Photoshop (an advanced image editor) work.

If the budget allows, the priority order for footage is:

  1. Buy the product, shoot your own video with it, and return the product afterward. This custom content is the top priority.
  2. If that is not possible, re-shoot a proven competitor video.
  3. When you re-shoot, keep its hook (the first seconds that stop people scrolling), message, vibe (mood) and caption (text shown with the video).
  4. When you re-shoot, change only the setting and, optionally, the music.
  5. If neither works, search TikTok with 5 to 10 product keywords and hashtags (words starting with #). Use videos from the last two weeks.
  6. Build a creative folder of 5 to 10 or more videos from these sources.

Own footage as a competitive advantage

1 min
  • Ordering the product yourself and shooting original video creates a competitive advantage, meaning an edge over rivals.
  • You become the only seller with custom footage of that product.
  • Competitors are forced to use generic supplier videos.
  • Ordering products for content is not necessary at the start of testing.
  • A sample (one unit from a supplier or Amazon) can be used and then returned if you need product shots.
  • Reusing existing winning creatives with clip changes saves time and money.
  • Product video demonstrations for dropshipping businesses (you sell products that a supplier ships to customers) require a physical sample to film.
  • Without the product, this proof point is not an option.
  • Ordering the product yourself is the recommended route for reliable supply. It takes longer.
  • Real customer photos can serve as ad creatives, if you have permission.
  • Real customer photos often look more professional than staged photos.
  • Asking customers to tag the brand on Instagram (mention it in their post) gives a steady source of such content.

Competitor and supplier footage when no own footage exists

2 min
  • When no UGC (user-generated content, meaning made by customers or creators, not by the brand) is available, you can still test.
  • Use YouTube, AliExpress, Alibaba or competitor videos.
  • Reshape them into the same 30-45 second structure.
  • Give them a strong hook and a call to action (CTA, the line that tells people what to do).
  • This is a valid way to test a product before investing in creators.
  • Source product images and videos from AliExpress, Amazon, Google Image Search, Instagram (search by hashtag #product-name), TikTok, or competitor stores.
  • To test without your own designs, take product photos from AliExpress or Alibaba. You can also ask a manufacturer for pictures.
  • Run ads with these photos.
  • A test budget of around 200-300 USD is a typical starting point.
  • When no creators are available for a classic product video, stock clips (ready-made video from libraries) can be sourced from general websites.
  • Stock clips can also come from marketplaces such as AliExpress and Alibaba.
  • Add captions (on-screen text) and video structure.
  • This way, the product is not presented as a plain sales pitch.
  • Fashion product videos can be found on TikTok.
  • People sell products organically there, meaning without paid ads.
  • This gives enough material for ad creatives.
  • A Chinese short-video app is a source for new product clips.
  • Fresh product videos often appear there before they reach other markets.
  • Use it together with YouTube Shorts (short videos on YouTube) and Instagram when no own footage is available.

Freelancer workflow for footage from existing ads

Use this when the seller cannot shoot their own footage.

  1. Send the freelancer the AliExpress product link and ask them to find the product.
  2. They search TikTok and Facebook for existing product videos and clip them (cut out parts).
  3. They cut the clips together and remove the original ad copy text.
  4. Deliver several variations where the first three to five seconds differ from each other.
  5. Share the TikTok Ad Center or an ad library link (a public list of running ads) with the creative editor. They can then study trending ads and how they are built before making variations.

Mixing hooks from other footage

1 min
  • When the seller cannot shoot their own clips, search YouTube Shorts and Instagram for clips.
  • You can also mix hooks from them.
  • Or hire a creator on Fiverr for about 20-30 dollars.
  • That creator can deliver content in two to three days.

Building an ad from sourced footage

1 min

Source footage for an ad:

  1. Write the narrative (the story) as a list of scenes.
  2. For each scene, search for product or stock footage that matches the emotion.
  3. Download each clip and store it in the correct folder.
  4. Speed up slow stock clips so they match the ad's pace.

UGC creators and creator casting

1 min

Casting

1 min
  • Use the customer avatar (a clear picture of your typical buyer) to choose creators for videos.
  • If buyers are women aged 40 and older, use a UGC creator from that group.
  • They resonate most with the exact audience.
  • Your own appearance may not match the target audience. Example: a Black African creator selling to Gulf or primarily European markets.
  • In that case, create two test versions.
  • Version one: self-made UGC.
  • Version two: outsourced UGC creators who match the audience's appearance and accent (way of speaking).
  • Run both at the same time, and compare conversion rates (the share of visitors who buy).
  • This shows which group connects best with buyers and drives more sales.
  • For a video ad aimed at a closing audience (people close to buying), the presenter can appear on camera.
  • Or a hired person who looks like the customer avatar can deliver the message.
  • Upwork, a freelance website, was mentioned as a place to find such a speaker.
  • Creators who are building their own presence will often make content for free products or small payments.
  • This is a useful source of cheap content.
  • Choose creators whose photo quality matches your brand.
  • Creators can supply high-resolution (sharp, large) photos and video for your own social channels.
  • Ask micro-influencers (creators with smaller followings) to send their photos and reshare the content.
  • This reduces the need for a photographer.

Payment

1 min
  • Creator payment for video content is $45 to $60 per 60-second clip.
  • Or $45-60 for 12 five-second scenes, broken down by hook, use case and call-to-action.
  • Request raw clips (unedited footage) rather than edited videos.
  • Raw clips let internal editors combine scenes into 5-10 different variations.
  • Fiverr creators for short content can deliver in two to three days for about 20-30 dollars.
  • See also the table below.

Minimum spend and testing philosophy

1 min
  • Creators often demand a minimum spend per creative. Example: 3x average order value (three times the average amount a customer pays per order).
  • They want this to prove the ad works.
  • Media buyers (people who buy ad space and manage ad budgets) prefer letting the algorithm choose winners. The algorithm is the ad platform's automatic system.
  • Media buyers prefer doing this without spend minimums.
  • Clarify your testing philosophy (how you decide what to test) upfront.
  • Set budget guardrails (spending limits) to prevent mid-campaign conflicts, meaning arguments during a running campaign.

Ordering UGC for Facebook

1 min
  • When ordering UGC for Facebook ads, the video ad type is the usual choice on Billow.
  • It is built for paid placements, meaning ads shown in paid spots on the platform.

Freelance marketplaces: Fiverr, Upwork and Bilo

1 min

Fiverr for banners and design

1 min
  • Fiverr is a freelance platform where graphic designers create web banners.
  • Designers offer various price points and turnaround times (how long they take).
  • Use Fiverr instead of hiring an in-house designer.
  • Designers deliver multiple banner sizes and formats.
  • **
  • To find designers on Fiverr, go to Graphics & Design.
  • Then select Web Banners from the subcategory menu (the list of smaller categories).
  • This section shows freelancers who specialise in web banner design.
  • **
  • When hiring a Fiverr designer, choose someone with at least 50+ completed jobs.
  • Also look for a rating between 4.8 and 4.9 stars.
  • Review their portfolio (samples of past work).
  • Select a designer whose style matches your brand.
  • **
  • Fiverr sellers are more cost-effective than agencies for creative production.
  • Most agencies subcontract (hand the work to others) to Fiverr anyway.
  • Hire Level 2+ sellers (Fiverr's more experienced rank) who speak English fluently.
  • This helps you avoid revisions (redo requests) and quality issues.

Commissioning banners on Fiverr **

1 min
  1. Provide the designer with your branding guidelines (your brand rules).
  2. Explain what the banner should communicate or convey.
  3. Supply product images if specific products must appear.
  4. The designer creates the banners in multiple sizes.
  5. The designer sends the finished files to you.
  6. Download the files and upload them to Refersion (affiliate software, a tool for partner programs).

Affiliate banners **

1 min

An affiliate is a partner who sells your product and earns a commission (a share of the sale).

  1. Determine which offer types you want to promote: business-wide offers or product-specific offers.
  2. Commission banner ad designs on Fiverr matching each offer type.
  3. Upload the completed banner ads to Refersion or your affiliate software.
  4. Make them available for affiliates to download.
  5. Affiliates can use them independently, without further action from you.

Hiring ad designers

1 min
  • To hire ad designers, post a small test gig (a small paid job listing) on Fiverr.
  • You can also post a job on Upwork.
  • Use one ad for the test, and test several designers.
  • Review each portfolio.
  • Send examples of the style you want as inspiration, not copies of existing ads.
  • Bilo job posts usually need two to three days before applications arrive.

Testing a copywriter or illustrator

1 min
  • Test a sales copywriter's efficiency before hiring.
  • Give each candidate a small project. Example: five headlines and five meta descriptions (the short text shown under a page title in search results).
  • Measure the time, and hire the best one.
  • In one test, candidates took between 2 and 6 hours for the same small task.
  • Custom illustration was budgeted at about $5 per image.
  • Workflow (steps):
    1. The illustrator (the person who draws) delivers an initial sketch.
    2. The owner gives feedback on the sketch.
    3. The illustrator delivers the final design.

Copywriting and copy production

1 min

Who writes the copy

1 min
  • In the early stage of an e-commerce business (an online shop), the best-paid copywriters are usually unaffordable.
  • So owners often have to learn to write product descriptions and emails themselves.
  • Copy is a direct sales lever, meaning words that directly drive sales.
  • Building this skill in-house can shortcut the cost of outsourcing it.
  • **
  • Product descriptions, sales emails and other sales copy can be written by owners, employees or virtual assistants (remote helpers).
  • They need to learn a tested copywriting method, a proven way to write sales text.
  • Delegating copy production to a trained team member is a practical option for small teams.
  • **
  • Hire a sales copywriter to write headlines and meta descriptions.
  • Expect about $40 to $100 per hour.
  • Large companies often employ people who only write headlines and meta descriptions.

Checking a copywriter

1 min
  • Excessive fluff (empty, padded words) and useless sentences often signal that the writer did not research the product.
  • When hiring or testing a copywriter, check a trial text for this.
  • Reject writers whose copy is full of empty language.
  • **

Product descriptions on Upwork **

1 min
  1. Post a job for product descriptions on Upwork.
  2. Hire at least three or four writers.
  3. Give each writer the same test product page to write.
  4. Compare the results and keep the writer who performs best.

Customer and brand photos

1 min
  • Collect real customer product photos in three steps: **
    1. Email customers who bought the product.
    2. Ask them to send photos taken with a good camera or a phone.
    3. Offer a payment for usable photos, for example 50 dollars via PayPal, to encourage participation.
  • A friend who is a photographer shot the product for about 200.
  • A typical professional shoot can cost about 1,000.
  • Using a network (friends and contacts) to reduce costs is a practical option early on.
  • A professional product photo shoot with editing costs around 1,000 to 2,000 USD.
  • A low-budget shoot with friends is an alternative for an early-stage store.
  • Picture quality will be lower with that option.

Influencers and authorities

1 min

Find influencers (people with large followings who promote products) or authorities (experts people trust) connected to your product:

  1. Search Pinterest or Google for influencers, actors, artists, celebrities or doctors who use or fit your product category.
  2. Note each person's name.
  3. Find and record each person's social media handle (their username).
  4. Note whether they already use or have used your products.

Refreshing and testing creatives

1 min
  • Freelance marketplaces such as Fiverr can supply replacement ad creatives.
  • Use them when the original creatives stop converting, meaning they stop leading to sales.
  • Refresh creatives as soon as CTR drops and CPM rises.
  • CTR is click-through rate, the share of viewers who click.
  • CPM is cost per 1,000 views.
  • Refresh them because creative fatigue reduces sales over time. Creative fatigue means people stop reacting to an ad they have seen too often.
  • Create many creatives and test them.
  • Budget for a large number of creative tests rather than a few high-quality pieces.
  • Hit rates are often low.
  • A seller does not need to make every creative personally.
  • But the seller should be able to judge and direct the outsourced work.

Performance Max and Automation

Section 10.20 · 20 of 23 in this chapter · 16 min read

In short

  • Let the computer set prices per click, but keep yourself in charge of goals, campaign setup and product data.
  • Do not make Performance Max your main campaign. Keep Standard Shopping as the core.
  • Give Smart Shopping about one month before you judge it, and do not change it every day.
  • Check every AI keyword for real search volume before you spend money on it.

Performance Max and Automation

1 min

Humans and Machines

1 min
  • The best approach mixes people and machines. You set the goals, campaign structure, tests and feed quality (the product list you send to Google).
  • Automation (software that makes choices for you) calculates bids (the price you pay per click) across many dimensions (details such as device, place, time or customer type).
  • Manual control alone leaves dimension value unused.
  • Trusting automation blindly leaves nothing to check the results.
  • Systems that learn from results can find patterns that human-written rules miss.
  • An early drop in ROAS (return on ad spend: money earned for each unit of money spent on ads) during the learning phase (the first weeks when the system collects data and adjusts itself) is normal.
  • Do not reverse it immediately just because of that early drop.
  • Judge automated bidding only after it has had time to learn.
  • Keep a human in control of bids, budgets and structure.
  • Use AI (artificial intelligence programs, such as chat assistants) mainly for research and analysis.
  • Do not hand a live Google Ads account (Google's ad platform account) to an AI agent (a program that acts on its own) to run unattended.
  • Errors add up when they repeat across a large account.
  • One bad bid or budget change applied across an account can burn a lot of money before anyone notices.
  • Some account mistakes take weeks or months to repair.
  • Current AI models handle short, single tasks well.
  • They fail on long chains of steps, repeated tasks and tasks that need exact steps.
  • They may skip a step or enter details incorrectly, which is costly in a live ad account.

Account Maturity and Starting Point

2 min
  • Standard Shopping (normal Google product ads where you set the bids yourself) is preferred over Performance Max (Google's automated campaign type that runs across many Google places) for most merchants.
  • Performance Max gives Google full control of targeting (who sees the ads), placements (where the ads show) and bidding.
  • Performance Max gives less transparency (less view of what happens).
  • Standard Shopping campaigns are preferred for non-impulse products (things people think about before buying).
  • Standard Shopping campaigns give control over keywords and clear data on what performs.
  • Standard Shopping stays the core of the account.
  • Performance Max, if tested, sits beside it.
  • For most new stores, start with Alpha Beta (a standard Shopping setup) campaigns.
  • Run Performance Max alongside them, not instead of them.
  • In a new account with no conversion history (no record of past sales counted by the ad system), do not use Smart Shopping (Google's automated campaign type) for all products.
  • Use standard Shopping campaigns with individual bids (a set price per product) until Google has conversion data.
  • Smart Shopping and other automated bidding (the system sets the bids for you) are not recommended for new accounts.
  • Google has no store data yet and will spend money broadly.
  • Start with manual bidding (you set the bids) and a controlled setup.
  • Consider automation only after the account has a history of profitable data.
  • Automated bidding works best when the account has a large history of conversions.
  • The platform then knows which clicks are likely to buy.
  • Without that history, automation mainly spends on random clicks.
  • For most accounts, scaling manual campaigns (growing spend step by step on campaigns you run by hand) gives a higher ROAS more easily than testing smart campaigns.
  • Scaling the manual campaigns is the default choice.
  • Switching a working account to Smart Shopping while raising budgets can make the numbers unreadable.
  • If an agency did this during a scale-up (fast growth), switch back to manual campaigns and scale there.

Performance Max: Behavior and Decline

3 min
  • Performance Max campaigns first target the hottest existing audiences (groups of people Google shows ads to), meaning people who visited the store.
  • These campaigns produce high ROAS at the start.
  • As these audiences see the ads again and again and run dry, Google expands to wider, colder audiences (people who do not know your store yet).
  • Colder audiences are less likely to buy, so ROAS drops significantly.
  • Performance Max, including feed-only campaigns (campaigns that run only on the product feed), often performs well for a few weeks or months.
  • Then return on ad spend drops sharply, because it increasingly retargets (shows ads again to) existing visitors until those audiences run out.
  • When this happens, add other Google traffic sources.

Performance Max: Results Seen in Sources

1 min

  • Results were not clearly better than manual campaigns.
  • So underperforming campaigns were turned off and the others kept running.
  • An agency-managed Google Ads account (run for a client by a marketing firm) with Performance Max returned about 4x to 5x ROAS.
  • Most of the returns came from that campaign type.

Smart Shopping: Status and Campaign Types

1 min
  • Smart Shopping campaigns were sunset (shut down or phased out) by the end of Q3 2022 (July to September 2022).
  • Merchants migrated to either Standard Shopping or Performance Max.
  • Smart Shopping is being sunset and replaced by Performance Max.
  • Standard Shopping is the campaign type to keep as the core.
  • Smart Shopping campaigns are generally not recommended.
  • Performance Max may be tested with about 10% of the ad budget.
  • It only works well with a large history of past conversions.
  • Shopping campaign types include standard Shopping, dynamic ads on the Google network (ads that build from your product list), YouTube shopping, Gmail shopping and product listing retargeting on the display network.
  • Smart Shopping is Google's automated campaign type.
  • It automatically includes retargeting, dynamic retargeting (ads built from products people viewed), YouTube ads and Gmail ads.
  • Smart Shopping is most worth running when the account already has a high impression share.
  • It covers Shopping, retargeting, dynamic retargeting, YouTube ads and Gmail ads.
  • It is valued for simplicity, performance and reach.
  • Smart Shopping gives little control.
  • You cannot see placements, performance by dimension such as gender, location or income, or the split between cold and retargeting traffic.
  • Negative keywords are not available.
  • Placement exclusions (blocks on certain places) can only be set at the account level (the whole account).
  • Smart Shopping works best with one brand per campaign.
  • Product groups should contain 100 or fewer products.
  • Each small product group should generate more than 20 conversions per month.

Smart Shopping: Learning Time and Stability

1 min
  • Pausing a new Smart Shopping campaign before it has enough conversions cuts off its learning.
  • Google suggests giving it about one month to learn.
  • Judge it only after that period.
  • Frequent changes to Smart Shopping campaigns disrupt the automated learning.
  • So settings should be left alone for a stable period.
  • Make adjustments deliberately, not on a daily basis.
  • One estimate put Smart Shopping's success at about 50 to 60% of the time.
  • Sometimes it performs very well, sometimes it needs more learning, and sometimes it must be turned off in favor of Alpha Beta.
  • Plan for all three outcomes.
  • Automated Google recommendations and Smart features mainly raise spend rather than performance.
  • For high-ticket dropshipping (dropshipping of expensive products), managing settings manually performs better.
  • So use your own judgment.

Smart Shopping Rollout Procedure

1 min

Procedure to roll out Smart Shopping safely:

  1. Start with one brand at a time.
  2. Exclude that brand from the standard Alpha Beta campaign.
  3. Place the brand in a Smart Shopping campaign.
  4. Prefer brands with 100 or fewer products, since smaller sets are easier for Google to learn.
  5. Confirm the brand already has more than 20 conversions per month.
  6. Prioritize brands that already convert well and have a high search impression share, because they gain the most clicks and conversions.
  7. Do not change the campaign frequently; allow about one month for learning.

Build the shopping structure in layers: keep Alpha Beta as the baseline (the reference you compare against), peel one brand into Smart Shopping, then test Bing (Microsoft's search engine), other product listing ads, YouTube shopping ads and Gmail shopping ads.

Compare each new element against the Alpha Beta baseline before expanding it.

Testing Automation Safely

1 min

Procedure to test a change safely with Drafts and experiments (Google's tool for testing changes on part of your traffic):

  1. Open Drafts and experiments in the left sidebar of Google Ads.
  2. Create a draft from the campaign; the draft lets you change the campaign without affecting live traffic.
  3. Make the changes to test in the draft, for example grouping products instead of segmenting by product ID (the number that identifies each product), turning on automated bidding, or adding negative keywords.
  4. Run an experiment from the draft.
  5. Set how much traffic or budget goes to the test, for example a 50/50 split or a smaller share of 20, 30 or 40%.
  6. Compare the results and decide based on the experiment outcome.

Procedure to test automated bidding on an existing Shopping campaign:

  1. In Google Ads, open Drafts and Experiments.
  2. Create a draft from the original campaign.
  3. In the draft, turn on bid automation.
  4. Run the draft as an experiment against the original campaign and compare the results.

An experiment tests automated bidding on a share of traffic instead of the whole campaign.

  • If the automated version wins, the test shows where to scale.
  • If the manual campaign wins, that result confirms the existing setup beats automation.
  • Either outcome is useful.

A couple of years earlier, automated bidding beat manual setups only about 10 to 20% of the time, and that share was rising.

Test automated bidding on your own account instead of assuming either outcome.

With a large amount of account data, a smart campaign (a campaign that uses Google's automation) can be tested at a small budget in parallel with the manual campaigns.

The manual campaigns stay switched on during the test.

Case: Switching a Shopping Account to Smart Shopping

1 min
  • A client's Shopping product listing ads were slowly declining.
  • The campaign manager switched the account from the standard Alpha Beta shopping setup to Smart Shopping.
  • Afterwards the chart showed large daily swings.
  • Revenue peaks reached around $25,000 on some days.
  • A reference line sat near $12,500, and many days went above it.
  • Cost and conversions were high but jumped up and down.
  • The recent period clearly outperformed the older period on sales and ROI (return on investment: profit compared to money put in).

AI for Keyword and Search Term Work

2 min
  • Use AI for keyword research (finding search words), search term analysis (checking the real words people typed before clicking) and negative keyword lists (words to block).
  • Do not use AI to manage the ad account itself.
  • These tasks carry the least risk and give the biggest time saving.
  • Any large language model (a chat AI) can be used for the negative keyword and search term prompts (written instructions for the AI).
  • This example used Gemini (Google's AI chat tool) in a browser chat for these tasks.
  • For a large search terms export (a file listing every search term), attach the full file to an AI chat together with the audit prompt.
  • Wait for the run to finish.
  • A file of about 13,000 rows took a few minutes to process in Gemini.
  • Review the output only after the run is complete.
  • AI generates plausible-looking keywords without knowing whether anyone searches for them.
  • So it can produce long lists of terms with zero search demand.
  • Never build campaigns on AI keyword ideas before validating their search volume (how many times people search per month).

Keyword validation procedure:

  1. Use AI to generate keyword ideas and group them by theme and intent (the goal behind a search).
  2. Take the keyword groups into Google Keyword Planner (Google's free tool that shows search volume).
  3. Check each group for real average monthly search volume and keep only terms that people actually search.
  4. Feed the validated set back to the AI as seed keywords (starting words) to find more ideas, then validate again.

Keyword and campaign prompt. This prompt is written for eCommerce and DTC (direct to consumer: selling straight to buyers) brands:

Act as a senior Google Ads strategist working for eCommerce and DTC brands.

1. Business summary from the website alone: Describe what the company sells, its main product category, the problems its customers face, its core benefits and unique selling points, its trust signals, current offers, pricing and guarantees, and any compliance limits (claims that cannot appear in ads).

2. High-volume keyword themes: Using that summary, group keyword themes by product sold, priority and search intent. Keep each ad group narrow enough that matching ad copy can be written for it. For every theme, state its intent stage and priority. Never invent keywords nobody searches for. Mark every theme that still needs search volume confirmation.

Search-Intent shopping funnel (Low/Medium/High value campaigns)

Section 10.10 · 10 of 23 in this chapter · 22 min read

In short

  • Search intent is how ready a person is to buy. Judge every search term by it before you pay for clicks.
  • Sort every search term into three value tiers: low, medium or high. Count how many products the term could match.
  • Bid the most on high value terms, a middle amount on medium value terms, and the least on low value terms.
  • Set campaign priority in reverse: low value terms get High priority, and high value terms get Low priority.
  • Expect about 10% of search traffic to be high value and about 75% to be low value.
More key points
  • Negative keywords are blocked search words. They stop your ad from showing for those searches, so each search lands in the right campaign.
  • Brand searches (someone types your brand name) convert better than generic searches (someone types a general product term). Keep them in separate campaigns.
  • Alpha campaigns hold only brand names, product names and product codes (SKUs). Generic terms go to Beta.
  • Check your search terms regularly and block the ones that waste money.

Search intent and value tiers

7 min
  • Search intent (the buying goal behind a search, in plain words: how ready the person is to buy) must be judged for every term from keyword research (finding the words people type into search engines), whether for paid ads (ads you pay for per click) or SEO (search engine optimisation: getting free results in normal search). It decides how much a click from that term is worth to the business.
  • The same product can match searches with very different purchase intent. A broad category search shows little buying intent, a search with size and feature descriptors shows more, and a search with brand and model name shows the strongest buying intent for the exact product.
  • Search intent differs between high-ticket products (expensive items) and low-ticket products (cheap items). The customer journey and the bidding approach must differ too. A method that works for cheap products can overspend badly on expensive ones.
  • Google does not sort search terms into value levels by buying intent. Google cares about this less than the advertiser does. The advertiser must therefore tell Google when to show ads and when not to.
  • Google Shopping (Google's product ads with pictures and prices) suits middle- and bottom-of-funnel search terms (the funnel is the buyer's path: top is early research, middle is comparing, bottom is ready to buy). Branded and product-specific queries bring largely relevant traffic. Generic top-of-funnel terms, such as a broad category phrase sent to one product page, usually do not convert (turn into a sale). Exclude such terms from Shopping and address them through another channel such as content.
  • Tag each search term by buying journey stage: research (top of funnel), comparing (middle of funnel) or ready to buy (bottom of funnel). Top-of-funnel terms can be valuable for awareness (people knowing your brand), but they usually convert less and should be judged against that.

Value tier table

Three value tiers classify every search term.

Low Ambiguous; the searcher may want images, repairs, used items, rentals or information 1 to 2 words Only a product type coffee grinder Lowest
Medium Specific; the searcher has narrowed the choice 3 to 5 words, sometimes more A group of products defined by type plus attributes such as size, capacity, zones or features burr coffee grinder with 300 g hopper Middle

Low value terms

  • Low value terms usually have a generic one- or two-word wording. The searcher may only be browsing, or may need service for a product bought elsewhere.
  • Clicks on low value terms cost the bid (the price you pay per click) but rarely lead to a sale. The chance of conversion is unknown.
  • Low value terms have ambiguous intent and are usually one or two words. It is unclear what the searcher wants.
  • Low value terms can still convert, but at a lower rate.
  • Shoppers sometimes click a store's ad for a product they bought from someone else. They expect help with service or a wider range. So a click does not mean a buyer.
  • Some clicks from broad terms will bring contacts you cannot serve. This raises the real cost per sale.
  • Include these unrelated contacts when judging whether a search term is profitable (earns more than it costs).

Medium value terms

  • Medium value terms add attributes such as size, capacity, zone count or feature. These show real buying intent and point to a group of matching products.
  • The retailer can reasonably expect that a visitor from such a term will buy something similar from its range.
  • Medium value terms show clearer intent, such as a product type combined with a size or feature.
  • They still fit several products in the catalogue. Their conversion chance is higher than for low value terms but lower than for high value terms.

High value terms

  • High value terms name one product. They do this by brand plus model name, or by part number, SKU or another unique identifier.
  • If a brand sells several near-identical products, a brand-plus-description term is worth less than one containing the exact model identifier.
  • High value terms match exactly one product in the catalogue, typically a brand name combined with a specific model name.
  • Because the intent is certain, high value terms have the highest conversion chance and the highest value to the business.
  • A high value search term matches a single product. It is typically brand plus model number, part number, SKU or another unique identifier.
  • If a brand has several products matching a brand-plus-description search, that term is worth somewhat less than one containing the unique identifier.
  • A search that combines a brand name with a product description is high value only if that brand sells just one product matching the description.
  • If the brand has several near-identical models, treat that term as slightly lower value than a term containing the exact model number, part number or SKU.
  • A brand name plus a descriptive term, without the model number, counts as high value only if that brand has a single product matching the description.
  • If the brand has several matching products, treat the term as somewhat lower value. Check the brand's range before assigning value.

Classifying a search term

  • To classify a Shopping search term, count how many products in the catalogue it would fit.
  • A term that matches only one product is high value. A product type narrowed by size or feature is medium value. A broad generic word that could serve many unrelated purposes is low value.
  • Classify each search term by how many products it could match. Many products means low value. One product group defined by attributes means medium value. One exact product means high value.
  • Examples from the same source: walking shoes is low, waterproof trail walking shoes women size 10 is medium, and Trailmax X3 women trail shoe size 10 is high.
  • Search terms are valued by how precisely they identify what the buyer wants.
  • Low value terms are ambiguous and typically one or two words.
  • Medium value terms identify a group of products. They usually have three to five or more words.
  • High value terms match exactly one product through brand plus model name, part number or SKU.
  • Use this three-tier classification to decide how much each search term is worth to bid on.
  • Example ladder for a surfboard store: surfboard is a low-value term because the intent is unclear.
  • 6 foot performance short board is a medium-value term. It narrows the product type and size.
  • A brand plus model name, such as Channel Islands OG Flyer surfboard, is a high-value term. It points to one specific product.
  • Example with one product, an inflatable paddleboard: a one-word term like paddleboard is low value.
  • A term with size and type, like inflatable paddleboard 12 ft solo, is medium value.
  • A term with brand plus model number is high value.
  • Each step narrows the match to fewer products and shows stronger buying intent.

Procedure for valuing search terms:

  1. Export the search terms from your keyword research.
  2. For each term, decide whether it names only a product type, a product group defined by attributes, or one specific product through brand plus model or part number.
  3. Label each term low, medium or high value.
  4. Set the highest bids for high value terms, moderate bids for medium value terms, and limit bids on low value terms.
  5. Add negative keywords (search words that stop your ad from showing) for terms with no buying intent for your products. Then review the terms that trigger your ads.

Share of traffic

  • In any single market, only about 10% of search traffic is high value at a given time.
  • Medium value traffic is slightly larger than high value traffic.
  • About 75% of traffic is low value.
  • Expect most search traffic to be low value and budget accordingly.
  • At any given time, the bottom of the funnel usually represents only about 10% of most markets.
  • The majority of potential buyers sit at the top of the funnel.
  • For high-ticket dropshipping, keywords are usually sorted into three value levels: low, medium and high.
  • Three or four levels are most common. More are possible but rarely needed.
  • Competitors and the market are not the only variable. The same tiers apply to any product.
  • A term's value comes from what it identifies, not from its brand alone.

Note: states that the funnel segments search intent, not brand ownership. Branding is one way to see the difference in specificity, and unbranded product lines can use the same three-level split.

Bidding by value

4 min
  • Bid more for search terms with a higher chance of converting.
  • Low value terms need many more clicks per sale, which raises the cost per sale.
  • Bidding the same amount on every tier means spending far more for fewer conversions. It wastes budget on low value terms.
  • In the shopping funnel, bid the most on high value terms, a middle amount on medium value terms, and the least on low value terms.
  • Low value terms are not dropped. They are bid cheaply because they convert at a lower rate.
  • Bids are set by value to the business.
  • High value terms get high bids to win the auction (the instant contest Google runs for each search).
  • Low value terms get low bids to limit cost, because they have a low chance of conversion.
  • On high value search terms, bid high enough to win the first position in the Shopping results.
  • Only do this when the store is well optimised for conversions (set up so visitors buy) and outperforms competitors.
  • Without that, the high bid will not turn into profitable sales.
  • When one bid applies to all relevant search terms, raising the bid to win high value traffic also raises the cost of low value traffic.
  • This coupling is why each keyword value level needs its own bid.
  • A standard Shopping ad structure charges the same bid for every keyword level.
  • So the same cost per click is paid for low value and high value searches.
  • This overpays on low value traffic and erodes profitability.
  • Mistake: Targeting large amounts of low value traffic makes Shopping campaigns unprofitable, because spend grows faster than sales.
  • Aim to capture as much high value traffic as possible.
  • Capture an acceptable share of medium value traffic.
  • Limit exposure to low value traffic.
  • Competitors running a basic Shopping setup pay similar amounts for every keyword.
  • They must spend heavily on high value terms to stay competitive. This leaves them less profit per sale.
  • A store that bids by keyword value and keeps more margin per sale (money left after costs on each sale) can therefore outbid them for longer.
  • Most competitors pay the same average bid for branded and generic queries. So they lose money on the generic ones.
  • Segmenting (splitting searches into groups) and bidding low on generic terms lets you keep those clicks at a positive ROI (return on investment: profit compared with money spent) while they do not.
  • Searches that trigger the same Shopping campaign do not convert equally.
  • Specific branded queries can convert around 5-10%. Generic queries may convert near 0.5%.
  • Because of this spread, each search group needs a different bid.
  • Example: the specific branded term Oakley Holbrook men's sunglasses has high intent.
  • A 0.50 USD bid won only about 300 clicks, because competitors outbid it.
  • Winning every click for this term could lift revenue from about 1,000 USD to about 3,000 USD for the same effort.
  • Bid high on specific, high-intent branded terms so you capture every impression (each time your ad is shown). Do this as long as the product stays inside your profitable ROI range.
  • Bid low on generic, low-intent terms. They bring traffic without large losses.
  • Bid less on generic queries, such as a search for a generic product type.
  • Bid more on branded queries, because branded searchers show stronger purchase intent.
  • Example in the same pattern: bid less on a generic query for a standard kitchen knife set, and more on the brand name of the same seller.
  • The same logic applies to every account structure.
  • Searches for an exact product name, model number or SKU convert much better than searches for a general category term.
  • Move the budget toward brand names, product names and SKU numbers.
  • Exclude generic or irrelevant terms.
  • Broad generic searches convert poorly, because the shopper may not want the specific item shown.
  • Searches for product names, brand names or SKU numbers convert much better and should receive the budget.
  • Broad keywords usually have a low click-through rate (CTR: the share of people who see your ad and click it) because the ad does not match what was searched.
  • Google Shopping shows the exact product. So specific product or niche keyword matches tend to have a higher CTR.
  • Decide which keywords to expand based on whether their clicks turn into sales, not on click count alone.
  • If clicks do not convert, add the terms as negatives or lower their CPC (cost per click).
  • Brand, product-name and SKU searches convert well, because the shopper already knows what they want.
  • These searches are the main source of sales for lesser-known brands.
  • **** High-intent keywords such as brand names, product names and SKUs get fewer searches but convert better.
  • **** It is acceptable to spend more on them.
  • **** Generic terms should receive the smaller share of the budget.

Campaign priority and bid inversion

2 min
  • Campaign priority tells Google the order in which to check identical campaigns when a search comes in.
  • Google checks the High priority campaign first, then Medium, then Low.
  • It enters the first campaign where the search term is not blocked by negative keywords.
  • Mistake: Campaign priority settings are inverted relative to search value.
  • The low-value campaign gets High priority.
  • The medium-value campaign gets Medium priority.
  • The high-value campaign gets Low priority.
  • Getting this wrong breaks the whole funnel.
  • Without a funnel, a single Shopping campaign pays the same for weak and strong keywords.
  • Google tends to route search terms into the campaign with the highest bid.
  • The funnel's priority and negative keyword setup prevents this.
  • Setting: New standard Shopping campaigns default to Campaign priority: Low (default). So every copy must be changed by hand.
  • Open the campaign's Settings.
  • Set Campaign priority to High, Medium or Low.
  • Click Save.

Priority and campaign roles (three-campaign funnel, older 2021-22):

Medium Medium value, branded medium-value terms Medium 60 cents Master Generic, Product Ids

  • In a three-campaign Shopping funnel, the High priority campaign with low bids targets low-value generic terms.
  • The Medium priority campaign with medium bids targets branded medium-value terms.
  • The Low priority campaign with high bids targets high-value terms that contain a product-specific identifier.
  • The store's own brand name is a valuable search term. Shoppers already know the store and have a high chance of converting.
  • Such terms belong in the high-value campaign.

Conflict: places branded medium-value terms in the Medium campaign. places the store's own brand name in the high-value campaign. Both apply. The store's own brand name goes to the high-value campaign, and branded medium-value terms go to the Medium campaign.

Building the three-campaign funnel

3 min
  • For a high-ticket dropshipping store, build a Google Shopping funnel of at least three campaigns.
  • Three is the standard starting point. Four to six are rarely needed, since most accounts need only three.
  • Each campaign in the funnel targets one search intent level.
  • Low value: generic terms, lowest bids.
  • Medium value: more specific or brand-plus-category terms, middle bids.
  • High value: terms naming one exact product, highest bids.
  • Separate campaigns make it possible to bid differently per value level. You stop paying the same for weak and strong keywords.
  • Campaigns must be identical (same ad groups, product groups and products) except for priority, bids and negative keyword lists.
  • Duplicating a campaign is the fastest way to build the other two.
  • Some Google Shopping funnels have been built with up to nine campaigns.
  • For about 90% of high-ticket dropshipping cases, three campaigns are enough.
  • Add a fourth or fifth campaign only when the account clearly needs it.
  • The shopping funnel requires a very precise campaign setup.
  • If it is not configured exactly as described, it will not work and will not produce the intended results.
  • The shopping funnel gives control over two things. The first is which search terms the ads show for. The second is how much is paid per click based on search intent.
  • This control is the reason the structure exists.
  • Set up the Google Shopping campaigns in a three-tier structure.
  • Add a trademark search text ad campaign (text ads that show when people search your brand name) for the brand name.
  • A three-campaign setup keeps paid traffic in separate campaigns, such as branded search, Shopping and display remarketing (showing ads to people who visited before).
  • Each stage can then be measured and budgeted on its own.

Build procedure

procedure: Build the three-campaign Google Shopping funnel in this order.

  1. Create the first Shopping campaign in the Google Ads account. Choose Standard Shopping (not Smart Shopping, Google's automatic campaign type). Select the Merchant Center account (Google's tool that holds your product list) and the country of sale.
  2. Enter the campaign name. Choose Manual CPC bidding (you set the price per click yourself). Apply the shared budget from the shared library. Set campaign priority.
  3. Set Targeting. Keep Search Network (the sites where search ads show), optionally with YouTube, Gmail and Discover. Do not include Google search partners.
  4. Create the first ad group (product shopping type, named by brand). Enter a starting CPC bid, and save.
  5. In product groups, subdivide by the Custom label 1 brand label (your own tag on each product). Save without editing bids. Exclude "Everything else in All products".
  6. Subdivide further by the Custom label holding product titles. Select all products, and save without editing bids.
  7. Duplicate the campaign with Edit > Copy and Paste. Rename each copy (High, Medium, Low), and set its campaign priority.
  8. Set bids per campaign: Medium campaign at 60 cents, Low priority campaign at about $1.20. Apply negative keyword lists.
  9. Apply Master Generic negatives to all three campaigns. Apply Brand Names negatives to the High priority campaign only (and to the Medium campaign, see below). Apply Product Ids negatives to the High and Medium priority campaigns.
  10. Enable all paused campaigns once products are approved in Merchant Center.
  • procedure: Subdivide product groups in this order. First subdivide by the custom label holding the brand name. Then subdivide by the custom label holding the product title.
  • Do not use the default item ID, because it shows an unreadable string.
  • Select all products, and click Save without editing bids.
  • Then change bids on individual products as needed.
  • Two custom labels are needed in Merchant Center or the product feed (the list of your products sent to Google).
  • One label holds the brand name. The other holds the product title as shown on the store.
  • Subdividing product groups by these labels gives readable names. The default item ID grouping does not.
  • Every new product added to the store needs its custom labels added manually in the product feed.
  • Without them, the product does not populate into the ad group and product group segmentations.
  • When new products or brands are added, update the product ID list or brand name list. These lists feed the negative keyword and campaign separation.
  • Otherwise their search terms land in the high priority campaign at a low bid. They then lose out on high-value terms.

Negative keyword lists

2 min

  • **** Negative keywords are search words you block, so your ad does not show for them. They are central to the Shopping funnel, the set of campaigns that move shoppers toward buying. They let you shape campaigns around search terms (the exact words people type into Google) of different value. Creating the lists is part of setup, and using them to manage campaigns is an ongoing task after launch.

  • mistake: Using negative keywords incorrectly stops the Shopping funnel from working well. List setup and later changes to them need care.

  • **** Negative keyword lists are the method that tells Google which search terms to push between the three campaigns. Each term must be blocked from the campaigns that should not serve it.

  • **** Negative keyword lists tell Google which campaign a search term may appear in. This keeps each term in its correct campaign and keeps the priority structure working. Without them, low-value terms leak into high-bid campaigns. A bid is the amount you pay for one click on your ad, so a high-bid campaign is the most expensive one to run.

  • rule: The three lists have distinct roles:

    • Master Generic Negatives holds search terms that the products should never match in any campaign.
    • Brand Names holds the names of the brands the store sells, so branded searches are pushed past the lowest-bid campaign.
    • Product Ids holds the product identifiers (the unique codes for each product), so exact product searches are pushed to the highest-bid campaign.

  • setting: Negative keyword list assignment for the funnel:

    • Master Generic Negatives applied to all three campaigns.
    • Brand Names applied to the High priority campaign only, because search terms with brand names move past the lowest-bid campaign.
    • Product Ids applied to the High and Medium priority campaigns, because very specific product terms move to the Low priority, highest-bid campaign.

  • mistake: The Brand Names negative list was wrongly applied to two campaigns. Apply it only to the High priority campaign. Check each negative list's campaign assignments (which campaigns each list is attached to) after applying.

  • Google Shopping chooses the search terms unless you exclude them. So you shape traffic with negative keywords, not with long-tail targeting. Long-tail terms are longer, more specific phrases. On search text ads, more specific long-tail terms usually raise CTR (click-through rate, the share of people who see your ad and click it) and lower traffic.

  • **** Google does not reliably judge search intent (the goal behind a search) and often shows ads for loosely related searches. Leaving these matches unreviewed burns budget (spends money on clicks that do not pay off). Review which search terms trigger the ads and exclude the poor ones with negative keywords.

  • **** The beta campaign also needs strong negative terms such as brand names and SKU numbers. The alpha campaign only needs the universal negatives. Alpha and beta are the two campaign types explained below.

  • Block descriptive search terms that convert well but are not brand-specific as negatives in both Alpha and Beta. A conversion is a sale or sign-up. Then raise CPC in Beta (the cost per click) and check whether higher-quality traffic comes in. Do not create a third campaign for this at the start.

Brand names and the two-campaign alternative

1 min

  • Searches for a brand name convert well, because the shopper already knows the brand. Example: Lifestyles infrared sauna converts higher than infrared sauna or should I buy this, because generic searchers are earlier in the funnel, still comparing options.

  • Source A's account structure separates Alpha and Beta, and the bids differ by query type. A query is the exact search a person types. Procedure (undated): To split a Shopping campaign into a branded and a generic campaign (branded means it contains a brand name; generic means it does not):

    1. Open the account and click the edit button, then copy and paste the existing product listing ad campaign (the Shopping ad that shows a product photo, price and store) so you have two campaigns.
    2. In the generic campaign, go to the Keywords tab, click Keywords, and add negative phrase keywords for your brand terms (brand name, brand possessive, model names) so only generic queries trigger it. A phrase keyword matches the words in that order.
    3. In the branded campaign, add negative exact keywords for the generic terms (for example sunglasses and men's sunglasses) so only branded queries trigger it. An exact keyword matches only that exact search.
    4. Set low bids in the generic campaign and high bids in the branded campaign.

  • Blocking low-converting generic terms completely gives up profitable traffic. Keep them in a separate generic campaign with a low bid instead, where they can stay ROI positive (ROI, return on investment, means the money earned is more than the money spent).

  • Two-campaign vs. three-campaign: Source A's segmentation uses two campaigns (branded and generic). Source B's funnel uses three priority levels and states that three campaigns are standard for most high-ticket cases (expensive products). The sources do not state which to use in which case. The three-campaign funnel is the standard the older source sets for high-ticket dropshipping (selling products bought from a supplier who ships directly to the customer).

Alpha and Beta campaigns

2 min

  • A Google Ads account for e-commerce is organised in two layers: campaign stage (MOFU or BOFU) and campaign type. MOFU means middle of funnel, the research phase. BOFU means bottom of funnel, the ready-to-buy phase. Each campaign should sit in one stage and one type so the structure stays readable.

  • Build Google Ads campaigns bottom-up. Start with BoFU audiences (people ready to buy). Avoid spending on TOFU (top of funnel, awareness only) as the entry point. Expand upward to MOFU and TOFU once BoFU revenue scales (grows).

  • Search terms that convert well in Shopping campaigns are the money terms. They are moved into their own cold-traffic Search campaign (cold traffic means people who do not know you yet), so budget and bids can be controlled for proven keywords.

  • 2025, rule: Alpha campaigns should contain only brand names, product names and SKU numbers as keywords. A SKU is a store's own product code. Descriptive generic terms belong in the Beta campaign, where CPC can be raised to win that traffic.

  • Example for a chandelier store: an exact product name, model number or SKU search belongs in the Alpha campaign. A generic search such as 16 bulb chandelier is not added as a keyword anywhere. Beta picks it up, because such users convert less often than people who search for the exact product.

  • Undated procedure: To create Alpha campaigns from high-performing search terms:

    1. Identify all search terms that generated at least one conversion in the Google Ads account.
    2. Create manual exact-match keywords for each converting term in a dedicated Alpha Shopping campaign. You add each keyword yourself, and it matches only that exact search.
    3. For search terms generating 5 or more conversions, also incorporate those terms into product titles and content strategy for SEO (search engine optimisation, getting free rankings in search results) benefit.

    Conflict: The undated procedure in step 2 adds every converting term to Alpha, while the 2025 rule restricts Alpha to brand names, product names and SKU numbers. Use the 2025 rule for keyword selection. Use step 3 of the undated procedure for title and content work on terms with 5 or more conversions.

Search term review and funnel maintenance

1 min
  • Review the search terms that trigger your ads regularly, and exclude the poor ones as negatives. Google does not reliably judge intent, so unreviewed matches burn budget.
  • Tag each term by journey stage (research, comparing, ready to buy). Terms that are clearly converting deserve their own ad group (a sub-group inside a campaign) or campaign, so they can get more budget and tailored copy (ad wording written for that search). Terms with few clicks but a relevant non-brand, top-of-funnel intent can still be kept as potential keywords.
  • Judge each search term by profit, including the unrelated contacts from broad terms. Do not judge a term on click volume.
  • Broad generic terms such as leather jackets can see large marketplaces and fast-fashion sellers take most of the Shopping placements (the spots where ads show). A small brand in that query needs its own photos, a distinct offer or a clear angle, or its listing blends into the same results.
  • Exclude generic top-of-funnel terms from Shopping and address them through another channel such as content.

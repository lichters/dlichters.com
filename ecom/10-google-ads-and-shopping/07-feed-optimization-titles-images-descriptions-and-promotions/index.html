Feed optimization: titles, images, descriptions and promotions

Section 10.7 · 7 of 23 in this chapter · 23 min read

In short

  • Your product title decides which searches can show your product, so write it with care.
  • Put the most-searched word first in every title, then brand, product name and details.
  • Use the words customers type, not your own clever names.
  • Use a plain white background for main product photos, with no text or logos on them.
  • Check what customers search about four times a year and update your titles.
More key points
  • Test one thing at a time: the picture. Keep the title, price and other details the same.
  • Keep the listing with the higher CTR (click-through rate: the share of people who see your ad and click it).
  • Check approval in Google Merchant Center (Google's tool for managing your products) before you rely on a promotion.
  • Make sure every coupon code (a discount code shoppers type in) works for the whole promotion. If it stops early, Google can suspend your account.
  • Check your titles and feed regularly, not only at launch.

Feed Control and Launch Sequence

2 min
  • The product feed is the list of your products that you send to Google. It is the only thing you control for Shopping Actions (Google's shopping program).
  • So optimise titles and descriptions in the feed. Optimise means improve them so they match what people search for.
  • This is the same work as regular Google Shopping, and you should do it anyway.
  • Shopping campaigns are ads run in Google Ads (Google's ad tool) that show your products. They match searches using the product data sent from Merchant Center (Google's tool where you upload your feed).
  • Product data is built from titles and descriptions. So product data quality decides which searches a product can appear for.
  • Changing titles and descriptions changes your ad reach.
  • Feed quality decides how well Google understands a product.
  • Weak or wrong data makes ads show for less relevant searches.
  • Optimise the feed before you run Shopping ads.
  • If ads already run, review the feed regularly to find fields you can improve.
  • Focus feed work on three fields. They carry most of the effect on Shopping results:
    • Product title (most important)
    • Product description
    • Barcode (GTIN, UPC or EAN) where one exists. A barcode is a number printed on products. GTIN, UPC and EAN are the names of different barcode types.
  • Upload the supplier's product descriptions and images as received. Then get Google Ads running for every product.
  • Optimise the best performers later. The top 20% by impressions, clicks and sales usually drive most results. Impressions mean how many times your ad was shown.
  • Standard Shopping setup uses the product images from your product pages. So you need no separate ad images to start.
  • Title decides whether the product appears. Image decides whether the shopper clicks.
  • The title is the biggest lever for search reach.
  • All feed parts (images, product types, attributes) feed the same system. Attributes are details such as size or colour.
  • Search ads and Shopping work differently. In search, ad text and keywords are separate.
  • In Shopping, the product title is the keyword. Google reads titles to match products to searches.
  • If a product is approved in Merchant Center but gets few organic Shopping impressions after months, follow these steps:
    1. Confirm some impressions occur. This shows the feed works.
    2. Treat the remaining problem as visibility (how easily people find you).
    3. Fix it with product page SEO (search engine optimisation, the work that helps pages rank in free results). This improves regular Google results and organic Google Shopping listings (organic means free, not paid).
  • A title made for paid Shopping usually helps free product results too. So one shared title can save time.
  • The exception: if organic shoppers search with different, non-buying terms, a shared title could lower free traffic.

Title Structure and Keyword Placement

5 min

Core rules

  • Product title structure decides search visibility. Weak titles reach fewer searches.
  • Titles with no keywords, or with brand names pushing search terms out, lead to low CTR (click-through rate: the share of people who see your ad and click it). They also waste impressions.
  • Core keywords must appear in product titles. Google scans titles to decide which searches trigger ads.
  • Use terms that actually convert for your store. Converting means leading to sales. Do not use only terms that sound good.
  • Google reads titles from left to right. The first words carry the most weight.
  • So place the most important search term first. Keyword importance drops along the title.
  • Titles matter most in a feed. Shoppers compare them side by side with competitors in product listing ads (ads showing photo, title and price).
  • A study cited in the source found that optimised titles produced more clicks and more impressions.
  • The first words matter most. The results page often shows only the start of a long title.
  • Put customer search terms into the Shopping title. Matching what shoppers type increases clicks and CTR.
  • Titles should reflect how buyers search. Google Shopping ads are triggered by buying-intent terms, which are words showing someone is ready to buy.
  • Words such as buy and online in titles can increase Shopping impressions. The benefit comes from matching searchers close to purchase.
  • Do not put promotional phrases such as free shipping at the start of a title. Customers do not search that way. The phrase also pushes relevant keywords to the end.
  • Do not copy competitors' product titles, even if they look good. Competitors often have weak quality scores too.
  • Write titles that stand out and still include searched terms.
  • Broad category searches are top of funnel. The funnel means the stages from browsing to buying. Top of funnel means early browsing.
  • Send broad category searches to an educational category page, not a single product page.
  • Use product pages for buyers who already know the product they want.

Title word order

Recommended formula for generic and unbranded products (generic means no brand name; unbranded means no brand):

Position Element Example
1 Keyword (strongest converting term) Strappy Wedding Heels
2 Brand (brand name)
3 Product name (product name)
4 Descriptors (colour, size, etc.) Lace Ivory Size 8
  • Shopping title formula: keyword first, then brand, then product name, then descriptors such as colour and size.
  • Example: Strappy Wedding Heels Lace Ivory Size 8. Do not use a brand-first title instead.
  • The old structure was Brand + Product + Description.
  • The improved structure for more clicks and higher CTR is Keyword + Brand + Product + Descriptors.
  • Use the customer's words for descriptors. For example, use gray instead of a brand colour name such as afternoon cloud.
  • Include brand names in titles where brands sell items that go together. Add them unless there is a specific reason not to.

Procedure for rewriting titles with search terms:

  1. Open the search terms report (the list of what people typed before clicking) in Google Ads. Use the search box at the top to find it. Find the terms that brought buyers to your products.
  2. Note which terms have the highest conversion rate (share of visitors who buy) and which have the highest CTR.
  3. Move the strongest terms to the start of each title. Then add brand, product name and descriptors such as colour and size.
  4. Use the customer's words for descriptors. For example, use gray instead of afternoon cloud.
  5. Repeat the review regularly, because new search terms appear over time.

Procedure for adding converting terms (quarterly cadence, meaning every few months):

  1. Pull the search term report. Find search terms that already convert.
  2. Add those converting terms to the beginning of the relevant product titles to earn more impressions.
  3. Repeat this review about once a quarter, around four times a year.
  4. Optionally, use a feed optimiser (a tool that edits feed data) that supports A/B testing. A/B testing means comparing two versions to see which works better. Use it to test title changes before rolling them out.

Examples of impressions gained by adding converting generic or brand terms to the front of a title. Use only terms that already appear with conversions in the search term report:

  • Adding bridal shoes to a sandal title.
  • Adding tuxedo to a tuxedo title.
  • Adding a brand name such as Adidas slides to a sandal title.

Keyword placement by attribute

  • Put a model or manufacturer part number into a title only if customers search for it. A part number is the code the maker uses for a product.
  • Check the search term report for that number. If it appears there, include it. If not, it does not need to be there.
  • Put SKU and barcode information in the product description. A SKU is the supplier's stock code for a product.
  • Buyers search for these identifiers. They help the product rank for those terms.
  • Keep the product's keyword focus to the brand name, the supplier's name and product identifiers. Identifiers are product name, SKU, MPN (manufacturer part number) or other supplier code.
  • Do not chase extra keywords on the product page (2023).
  • Whether a title includes a SKU is a supplier-by-supplier decision.
  • Check how the top competitors title the same products in search. Then follow their pattern.
  • Google Merchant Center is not supposed to flag SKU numbers in SEO titles.
  • If titles are flagged, check the content against the flagged issue. Do not remove SKUs by default.
  • Use exact supplier product names and SKUs in listings.
  • Organic search rankings speed up when you sell specific brands and use those names.

Titles for variants and bundles

  • Google matches Shopping products to search terms mainly through the title. So a variant with a different title is a different feed item. A variant is a version of a product, such as another colour.
  • For bundles (several items sold together), keep the same core product title. Add a short suffix such as Gold Bundle to tell them apart.
  • Whether to use separate product pages for each variant, or one page with variants, is decided by checking the top competitors in your niche. A niche is a small market focus.

Title Research and Competitor Checks

1 min
  • Competitor check procedure:
    1. Search on Google in an incognito window (a private browser window) for the keywords your ad shows for.
    2. Compare your listing to the competitors that appear.
    3. Improve your listing title and details to match or beat the better listings.
    4. Check the CTR again after the changes.
  • Shopping competitor procedure:
    1. Open your Google Ads Shopping campaign.
    2. Check which products get the most impressions and where clicks land.
    3. Visit your store's pages for those top products.
    4. Compare each page to competitors' product pages for identical or similar items.
    5. Find specific improvements that can win that traffic away from competitors. Look for their weaknesses.
  • Generic keywords on product pages can still trigger impressions. Brand-name searches will be fewer.
  • Google Shopping ads match searches to the keywords on each product page in the feed.
  • Keyword discovery procedure for titles:
    1. Use AI to write several title versions for the same product. Each should highlight a different keyword or customer angle: dietary benefits, ingredients, meal context or product type.
    2. Pull out the main keywords or benefit angles from each version. Examples: keto-friendly, low-calorie, gluten-free.
    3. Enter each keyword into Google Ads Keyword Planner (Google's tool that shows how often terms are searched). Add product-type endings. Examples: keto-friendly sauce, low-calorie dip, gluten-free dressing.
    4. Compare monthly search volumes (how many people search a term each month) to find the strongest buyer demand.
    5. Put the title versions that match the highest-search-volume keywords into your Google Shopping feed.
  • Use negative keywords (words that block your ad from showing) where a title names another product.
  • Example: a front bumper titled with a Dodge Ram model may show for people searching for the vehicle itself. This causes low CTR in the car parts space.

Product Title Duplication and Portfolio Strategy

1 min
  • Shopping results reward a portfolio (a large set of products) with good titles. One perfect listing is not enough.
  • Several products from the same seller can appear in one results row. So build strong titles across the whole catalogue, not one ad.
  • Feed duplication means making several titles for the same product. Each title targets different keywords, uses or customer angles.
  • This greatly widens your audience and keyword coverage. One product can appear several times with titles built for different search intents.

Branded vs Unbranded Titles

3 min

Sources differ on whether brand or keyword comes first. Use the rule that fits your product type:

Product type Title order Source Status
Generic or unbranded, keyword-led Keyword first, then brand, product name, descriptors Current (Source A, undated; 2023 formula)
Branded product with a searched brand and model Brand, model number, product type, one or two key dimensions Older (2021-22)
High-ticket with a manufacturer name customers already search Manufacturer's product name, usually the converting title Older (2021-22)

Details for each row:

  • Generic or unbranded: keyword first. Then brand, product name and descriptors.
  • Branded products: use brand, then model number, then product type, then one or two key dimensions.
  • Customers search for the brand and model string first. Placing it at the start keeps the most-searched terms in the strongest spots.
  • Examples in the same pattern: Northwind Aurora 900 Pool Heat Pump 15kW and Harbor Lux HL-40 Walk-In Bath Tub 60 x 32 in.
  • Manufacturer name: for high-ticket dropshipping (selling expensive items the supplier ships, without holding stock), the manufacturer's product name is usually the best-converting title.
  • This holds in roughly nine out of ten cases, because customers already search for that name. Exceptions exist but are rare.
  • Brand inclusion: including the brand name is usually fine. It is often useful when brands sell items that go together. Add it unless there is a specific reason not to.
  • Unique brand, higher price: if your Google Shopping price is higher than competitors', the first image and title must signal a more premium brand.
  • For that case, combine a unique brand name with the main product keyword.
  • Also collect reviews. They help justify a higher price in results.
  • Branded or unbranded: the first image is the main differentiator. Shoppers see it first.
  • Use a branded product name that also contains the main product keyword.

Unbranded and invented names:

  • A self-invented product name has no search demand. Shoppers will not find it.
  • To make people find it, you would need to pay for separate awareness campaigns. This is costly and rarely worth it.
  • Do not invent product names for high-ticket listings.
  • Listings with invented names got noticeably fewer impressions than listings using the supplier or manufacturer name.
  • The most converting search terms are the most specific to the product. This is true with or without a brand.
  • Titles should describe the product accurately, so they match what customers type.
  • Rewrite supplier titles that do not match what customers type.
  • Fix poor titles before launch. They can be refined later.

Procedure for unbranded product titles:

  1. Research the terms customers use to describe the product type.
  2. List the product's features, such as size, colour, material, shape and model.
  3. Combine three or four of the most searched features into the title. End it with the product noun (the word naming the item, such as "surfboard").

Example of a title that fails and a title that works:

  • A title that only says surfboard rarely converts on Google Shopping. It is top of funnel, meaning early browsing.
  • A title combining size, colour, material and shape with the noun matches the searches buyers actually enter.

Product type and attributes in titles:

  • In Google Shopping, the title is a main signal for which searches a product can match.
  • A title that names the product type plus key attributes (capacity, zones, size) makes the product eligible for more relevant searches.
  • You control this through feed data, not by choosing search terms directly.
  • In Google Shopping you cannot choose keywords directly, as you can in Search.
  • So add the relevant keywords into product titles and store content. Then the product appears for those searches.
  • For a flip-flop product, keep the core product type and the most important attributes in the title.
  • Put gender, brand, colour, style words and synonyms in the description. Synonyms are words with the same meaning. Examples: casual sandal, slip-on or thong sandal.
  • This lets one product match many related searches without a long title.

Product Descriptions

1 min
  • Google Merchant Center may reject products with no description. So do not paste only the title.
  • Generate a real description as soon as possible.
  • Put the description first, with features under it. Google Merchant Center reads the start of the description first (2023).
  • Google weighs roughly the first 300 words of the description most heavily. Put the key matching terms there.
  • Text beyond that matters less for matching.
  • Put SKU and barcode information in the description, since buyers search for these identifiers.
  • Keep the description focus on brand, supplier name and identifiers.
  • Synonym and variant terms, such as casual sandal, slip-on or thong sandal, belong in the description, not the title.
  • The 2023 rule and the flip-flop example cover different terms, so use both.
  • If product page data differs from the feed, the mismatch can read as a poor landing page experience. The landing page is the page a shopper reaches after the click.
  • Most high-ticket dropshippers keep page data close to the feed. So this is less likely to be a problem for them.
  • Complete feed data feeds into the Quality Score (Google's rating of ad quality). Quality Score covers title, description, Google product category, MPN and GTIN where they exist, gender, age group, condition and image quality.
  • Gaps or weak data lower the audience quality the ad can reach.

Images: Background and Policy

3 min

Background

Sources disagree on the background requirement. Here are the values and when each applies:

Source Requirement When it applies
Source A Main photo must be white. Get at least one white-background image per product from the supplier before launching. Use as the default for all Shopping products.
Source A Blue background can run without issues, but white or transparent is preferred by feed providers. Use only where a blue background is already approved and no white version exists.
Source B Flat-background images must use plain white, not black, green or patterned. Lifestyle photos may use other backgrounds. Apply to flat product shots. Lifestyle images are a separate category.
  • Google Shopping and remarketing campaigns (ads shown to people who visited before) use white-background product images.
  • Google's written image policy may lag behind what is accepted for Shopping.
  • So check the current policy before assuming a style is forbidden.
  • Competitor ads with other image styles show what passes review.
  • Plain white is the safe default.
  • But on a results page full of white backgrounds, it is hard to stand out.
  • Lifestyle images (the product shown in use) or in-context images can create differentiation.
  • Keep the supplier's stock image as the main image, unchanged. Shopping needs a white-background main image.
  • Only supplementary images on the product page can be customised. For example, you can edit them in Canva (an online design tool) to make a private-label product look unique. Private-label means your own brand on a supplier product.

Procedure for changing a background to white in Canva:

  1. Upload the product image with the blue background to Canva.
  2. Use the background eraser tool (a tool that removes the background).
  3. Set the background to white and save the image.

Text, logos and overlays

Sources agree that the main image must contain no text, logos or overlays. An overlay is anything placed on top of the photo. Apply these rules:

Element Rule Source
Text on image Merchant Center rejects product images that have text. Use a clean photo as the feed image.
Text, badges, overlays Do not put text or overlays, such as free shipping badges, on product images in Merchant Center. This can get products disabled. Free shipping can still show in the ad as a setting. (2023)
Own text or logos Product images may not contain text, except branding that is part of the product itself. Do not add your own logos, slogans or words to the background.
Supplier logos Remove supplier logos or graphics from the primary image, because Shopping does not allow such graphics. (2023)
Borders and overlays The Merchant Center main image must not contain any extra element. A border or overlay counts as an error and blocks approval. (2024)
  • Use unique themed images for social platforms. Shopping and remarketing use white-background product images. Social media performs better with themed visuals (2024).

Image delivery and troubleshooting

  • Running Shopping campaigns shows the product image next to the ad by default.
  • You need a Merchant Center submission and a Shopping campaign setup for this (2021).
  • Shoppers see the image before they click. The photo carries much of the decision to click.
  • A product that looks like every other listing gets fewer clicks.
  • Image decides whether a shopper clicks. Title decides whether the product appears.
  • Google Shopping ads use images from your product pages. So you need no separate ad creatives in the standard setup.
  • Shopping ads depend on custom content, such as professional photos and edited videos.
  • Generic supplier footage is not enough for dropshipping.
  • If Merchant Center approved a feed image but it does not show next to listings, check the image URL.
  • Click the image links to confirm they open. A broken link stops the image from showing (2023).

Procedure for moving an approved AI image into a Shopping listing:

  1. Generate and select an image that meets the front-facing, spacing and no-text requirements.
  2. Download the image file.
  3. Upload it to the matching product listing in Google Merchant Center.
  4. If the product already converts, duplicate the listing and run the new image as a split test. Do not replace the original. A split test shows two versions side by side.

Merchant Center is where product images are uploaded to individual Shopping listings. It is also where you create a duplicated listing for a split test.

Image and Title A/B Tests

1 min

Procedure for a clean image split test (a test that splits shoppers between two versions of one picture):

  1. Do not simply replace the image of a listing that already converts well (gets many buyers).
  2. Duplicate the product listing in the product feed (the file that lists your products for Google).
  3. Swap only the image on the copy. Keep the same title, price and product data.
  4. Run both listings inside the same Shopping campaign (paid Google ads that show your products). Use the same bid (the price you pay per click), the same budget (the money you spend) and the same audience (the people who see your ads).
  5. Wait until both have enough impressions (times your ad was shown) for the CTR (click-through rate: clicks divided by views) to be meaningful.
  6. Keep the listing with the higher CTR as the winner, and delete the loser.
  • The image must be the only variable (the only thing that changes) in a Shopping image test.
  • Otherwise, the CTR difference cannot be credited to the image.
  • The same price, bid, budget and audience keep both listings competing under identical conditions.
  • Title tests can be run through a feed optimiser (a tool that improves your product feed) that supports A/B testing (comparing two versions). Do this before title changes are rolled out (released to all products).

Star Ratings and Reviews

1 min
  • Apps from the Shopify App Store that add rich snippets (extra details in results, such as stars) show star ratings in Google Shopping ads.
  • Prices vary widely, so compare several before choosing one (2020).
  • Collect reviews, since Google displays them in Shopping ads.
  • Reviews help justify a higher price in results, especially where your price is above competitors'.
  • Google Merchant Center's Review feeds section (where review data is listed for Google) can be uploaded directly or through an app.
  • Review that section regularly.
  • Star ratings are among the elements that affect whether a Shopping ad gets clicked, alongside title, image, price and brand.
  • In a study of thousands of Shopping ads, the title was the most influential element for CTR, ahead of images, star ratings and price.

Merchant Center Promotions

1 min

Setup

1 min

Procedure for creating promotions (2023):

  1. Create the promotion in Google Merchant Center, or in Shopify's Google channel settings. Follow the Shopify support guide for promotions in Google Shopping ads.
  2. Set the promotion's discount code (the code shoppers type), date range and eligible products.
  3. Confirm the promotion is approved in Merchant Center before relying on it in ads.

Example procedure with coupon codes (2025):

  1. Create the promotion in Merchant Center, for example 10% off with coupon code FF.
  2. Ensure the coupon code is active on the website for the entire promotion period.
  3. Double and triple check that the code works throughout the duration.
  4. If the code expires or stops working before the promotion end date, Google may suspend the Merchant Center account (lock your account).

Setup facts:

  • Promotions are added in Merchant Center as an add-on (an extra) to the Shopping account. (2025)
  • Promotions are created in Merchant Center.
  • A Google support document explains how to set one up for Shopping ads.
  • Promotions attach discount or offer messages to products shown in Shopping results.
  • Google Shopping ads are supported by the Merchant Center promotions setting. (2022)
  • Create coupon-code promotions in Merchant Center to show the discount on Google Shopping listings.
  • Promotions are created inside Merchant Center to show discount offers in Google Shopping results. Set them up before expecting them to appear on listings. (2025)
  • Review these Merchant Center sections regularly.
  • Promotions section: shown next to product titles in Shopping ads.
  • Review feeds section: uploaded directly or through an app.

What promotions can show

1 min
  • Google Shopping promotions can show badges (small labels, such as low price or great pick) alongside ads quickly. They do not need store history (a past sales record).
  • Promotions are the fastest way to match competitors' badges (2022).
  • Promotions can add extra clickable text next to the store name in results. This can improve CTR (2021).
  • Shopping ads can show promotional text, such as a price advantage over another retailer.
  • In the example shown, a 31% price drop was displayed in the ad.
  • Promotions display discount labels such as save 350, expires in 4 days.
  • Advertise active discounts through the promotion function in Merchant Center.
  • Then the discounts appear in Shopping ads, which gives a visual advantage over competitors.
  • Use Merchant Center promotions so your offer appears in Google Shopping results.
  • This gives an advantage over stores that do not run promotions there.

Where promotions are not shown

1 min
  • Google Shopping promotions are not shown in the free organic section (free listings, not paid ads). (2025)
  • Low-margin products (products that earn little profit per sale) can therefore stay in the free listings. (2025)
  • Set up promotions only for the best-selling, highest-margin products in the new best-seller campaigns. (2025)

Eligibility and rules

1 min
  • Promotions accept only percent-off or fixed-amount offers (a percent off, or a set amount off).
  • A bundle (several products sold together) is treated as a new product that must be submitted to Merchant Center. It is not a promotion on an existing product. (2020)
  • Google's shipping promotion policy changed. (2024)
  • A free shipping promotion may be rejected for requiring a coupon code.
  • Check the Merchant Center promotion policy before running it. (2024)
  • Do not create a free shipping promotion with a coupon code just to satisfy the policy.
  • This can interfere with other coupons.
  • Use another structure, such as a small fixed discount on a large order. (2024)
  • Free shipping can be shown as a setting or tag (a label on your product data) in Merchant Center.
  • These special tags can appear in Google Shopping ads.

Case

1 min
  • A store with little sales, despite on-site optimisations (changes to its own website), added Google Shopping promotions to its shopping ads. (2025)
  • Sales went from near zero to roughly 20 to 25 within a couple of weeks. (2025)

Feed Effects on Quality Score, CTR and Impressions

1 min

The product feed (the file that lists your products for Google) affects three things: Quality Score (Google's rating of your ad quality), CTR (click-through rate) and impressions (how often your ad is shown).

Quality Score

1 min
  • Complete feed data feeds into Quality Score.
  • Platform gains (improvements that build on each other) stack in a positive feedback loop. Each gain feeds the next.
  • A better title raises CTR.
  • Higher CTR raises Quality Score, which lowers CPC (cost per click) and improves position (where your ad appears).
  • Each optimisation makes the next one more valuable.
  • Case: an advertiser (a seller running ads) selling a golf-ball type product optimised its campaign for CTR.
  • CTR rose by 108%, cost per click fell by 25%, and Quality Score rose by 60%.
  • The change let the advertiser get cheaper clicks than competitors who were paying more for weaker ads.

CTR

1 min
  • CTR can be improved through product titles, descriptions, images, bids and negatives (search words that block your ad).
  • After the click, the store experience should keep visitors engaged (2022).
  • A title that is optimised to raise CTR, while keeping the product accurate, reduces wasted clicks.
  • To raise CTR without changing targeting (who sees your ad), spell out why a premium product is worth its price.
  • For example, explain what makes it more premium than cheaper alternatives.
  • Clear reasons for a higher price attract the right clicks.
  • Intentionally decreasing CTR can improve profitability (how much money you keep).
  • Example: if your product costs $900 and competitors charge $800, include the price in ads to reduce clicks from price-focused customers.
  • This lowers CTR but saves money and increases ROAS (return on ad spend: sales earned per dollar spent on ads).

Impressions

1 min
  • Moving a commonly searched term to the front of the title raised CTR for those terms by about 88%.
  • It lifted impressions across all products by about 18%.
  • This comes from a study of thousands of Shopping ads.
  • Title tests in another study reported about a 33% increase in impressions.
  • They also reported about a 320% change in revenue (money from sales).
  • One example reported a 400% increase in impressions after a single word was added to the front of an old title.
  • Adding a converting high-volume generic term (a common, broad search word that leads to sales and is searched a lot) to the front of a title increased impressions in the examples shown.
  • Titles improved to rank higher and earn more clicks in organic search (free results, not paid ads) also flow into the Shopping feed.
  • So one title edit can help both channels.

Title as an Impression Lever

1 min
  • Title is the lever (the control that moves results) that controls search reach, meaning how many people see you.
  • Image controls click.
  • Keep both in mind when running tests.

Competitive Positioning

1 min
  • A product that looks like every other listing gets fewer clicks, so visible differences in photo and title matter.
  • Ads that are cheaper per click than competitors' often come from a stronger ad, not a lower bid.
  • The golf-ball case above shows a CTR-led change (a change driven by click rate) that made clicks cheaper than rivals paying more.

Review Cadence (how often to check)

1 min
  • Review converting search terms (search words that lead to sales) about once a quarter, around four times a year, and add them to titles.
  • Repeat title review regularly, because new search terms appear over time.
  • Review the feed regularly, not only at launch.

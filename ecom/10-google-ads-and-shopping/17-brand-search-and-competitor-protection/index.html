Brand search and competitor protection

Section 10.17 · 17 of 23 in this chapter · 14 min read

In short

  • Make an ad for your store name first. It keeps you at the top when people search for your brand.
  • Start with a small daily budget, about $5 to $10 per day.
  • Keep store-name ads separate from general product words like "dropshipping".
  • Before bidding on another brand's name, check the trademark rules. A trademark is a legal right to a brand name or logo.

Why Brand Search Matters

2 min
  • Competitors bid on your store name. A bid is the money you pay so your ad shows when someone types a word. So you must pay for brand search ads to keep your ads at the top. Brand search means ads that show when people type your store name.
  • One brand spent $10,000 to $30,000 per month on AdWords (the old name of Google's ad tool) and Bing (Microsoft's search engine). It did this because organic results (free, unpaid listings) alone were not enough.
  • A store with existing brand searches tends to appear in the top three or four organic positions for those searches.
  • For a store that drives traffic from other platforms, Google Ads (Google's paid ad tool) works well as a brand search channel.
  • It also works well as a retargeting channel. Retargeting means showing ads again to people who already visited your store.
  • Social posts often lack clickable links. So shoppers who see an influencer post (a person with many followers who promotes products) search for the brand on Google.
  • Branded search ads capture that traffic. Traffic means visitors. The site's pixel (a small tracking code on your site) can then collect their email.
  • A branded search ad should put your store as the first result when people search your store name or domain (your web address).
  • This keeps returning customers from being pulled away by competitors bidding on the store name.
  • Competitor ads threaten returning customers. Your branded campaign protects the store from losing them.
  • Branded campaigns have rarely produced a negative return on ad spend (ROAS, the sales money made for each dollar spent on ads). Negative means you lose money.
  • Brand searches become a growing traffic source as a business matures. This is especially true for high-ticket products (expensive products) with repeat visits.
  • Even when your homepage ranks first organically for your brand name, a competitor's paid ad on that name can push you down.
  • Your own branded search ad keeps the top position. It also reduces the effect of competitor ads.
  • Brand search is worth running even for stores with only about 3 to 5 or 5 to 10 sales per day.
  • It captures shoppers who already know the store but have not bought yet. So you do not lose them to competitors.
  • Brand searches usually deliver a good cost per conversion. Conversion means a sale or sign-up. Cost per conversion means the ad money spent for each one.
  • Brand searches rarely become high-traffic campaigns unless the business is very large. So budget risk (the chance of spending money with no return) is low.
  • Brand search is the simplest first Google Ads campaign for a store. A campaign is one set of ads with one goal and one budget.
  • It shows the store at the top when people search for the brand or product name. It also protects the store from competitors bidding on that name.

When to Start

1 min

Sources do not agree on timing. Pick the rule that fits your store's stage.

  • Brand awareness means people already know your brand name.
  • Meta means Facebook and Instagram ads. TikTok means TikTok ads.
Store stage Guidance Source
Beginner, first Google Ads campaign Start with brand search; simple to create, runs on $5 to $10 per day
Beginner after brand awareness Most effective after brand awareness is built through Meta or TikTok campaigns
Store with real traffic and profit Run branded search targeting store name and domain
Competitors start bidding on your name Run the campaign once competitors begin bidding, since that is when they start taking traffic
  • Brand search campaigns only start after the store is live (open to shoppers) and profitable. Competitors begin bidding on the store name at that point to take traffic (2023).
  • Once a store is profitable, start a Google brand search campaign first. Your brand name then ranks at the top, and competitors cannot take your brand searches.
  • Once profitable, run a search text ad campaign on your store name and domain. A search text ad is a written ad shown in Google search results.
  • Your ad appears first above the organic result for your own name. This keeps competitors from taking brand searches.
  • Once competitors start bidding on your store name, run a search text ad campaign. Cover your company name and all its variations.
  • Early stores can start with brand search at a small budget. They do not need keyword research (finding the words people type). This is because the store name is already known.

Campaign Setup

1 min

Naming and Structure

1 min
  • Name the campaign so it is clearly branded. An example format is "Search - COLD - YourStore.com", which targets searches for the store or brand name.
  • The goal of this name is to capture those searches before competitors start taking customers who look for the brand.
  • Keep brand terms and generic terms in separate campaigns. A generic term is a general word, like "dropshipping" (selling products without holding stock).
  • Generic terms can bring competitors whose ads appear beside yours.
  • Create one ad group containing your business name as keywords. An ad group is a folder of ads that share the same keywords.

Setup A: Source A Setting (no year given)

1 min
  • Terms: Impression share is the share of times your ad shows, out of all times it could show. Absolute Top of Page Impressions means your ad appears at the very top. Max CPC is the most you pay per click. Phrase match means the keyword appears in the search in the same word order. Search network means Google's partner sites. Display network means picture ads on other websites.
  1. Create a brand name Search campaign.
  2. Set the bid strategy to Impression share, with the target Absolute Top of Page Impressions at 100%. A bid strategy is how Google decides how much to bid for you.
  3. Set max CPC to $5.
  4. Use phrase match keywords, with quotes around brand variations, for example "Source A merch" and "Source A merch".
  5. Set the URL to the homepage.
  6. Write headlines (the bold top lines of an ad) led by the store name. Add a call-to-action (a phrase that tells people what to do) and benefits such as free shipping and 30-day returns.
  7. Uncheck the Search network and the Display network.

Setup B: Source A 2022 Procedure

1 min
  1. Create a search text campaign for your store name so you appear above competitors bidding on your brand.
  2. Later, create product-specific phrase-match campaigns for products that convert well in Google Shopping (product picture ads). Convert means turn into a sale.
  3. Do not run generic niche-keyword campaigns. A niche is a small market group. These campaigns do not convert.

Setup C: Source B Procedure

1 min
  1. Create a search campaign named with your business name, for example "Brand + Branded + Search + USA".
  2. Create one ad group containing your business name as keywords.
  3. Add a phrase match and an exact match keyword for the business name. Exact match means the search must be the same words. Add common variations you can think of.
  4. Set a bid high enough to be competitive. The example used a max CPC of about $1.55.
  5. Write the ad with your business name in Headline 1 (the first headline of the ad).
  6. Add a call extension (a phone button on the ad) and up to four site-link extensions (extra links under the ad).
  7. Set a daily budget (the most you spend per day) of around $10. Then review performance (check the results).

Setup Notes

1 min
  • The brand campaign can be set up in one ad group with a small number of brand keywords (2023).
  • Setup A and Setup C differ in bid strategy and budget. See Bidding and Budgets below.

Keywords and Match Types

1 min
  • For the store name, add two keywords. One is the store name written without spaces. The other is the store name as a phrase match in quotation marks, with its normal spacing (2023).
  • Target the store name and domain, not generic product terms.
  • Phrase match keywords with brand variations in quotes are used for the brand name campaign.
  • Add a phrase match and an exact match keyword for the business name. Add variations you can think of.
  • Keyword choice for brand search does not depend on any Google keyword statistic (such as monthly search volume). This is because it targets the store's own name (2023).
  • In the example, the exact match keyword had a 31.01% CTR. CTR means click-through rate: the share of people who saw the ad and clicked it.
  • In the same example, the phrase match keyword had about 13.33% CTR.
  • Conversion rates were roughly 1.28% for exact match and 2.00% for phrase match.
  • Exact match converted at about one third of the cost per conversion of phrase match, in the same example.

Bidding and Bid Strategy

1 min

There are two example settings. They differ, and each fits a different goal.

  • Enhanced CPC means Google can raise or lower your bid per click when a sale looks more or less likely.
  • Ad group default means the bid used for all keywords in one ad group, unless you set a separate bid for a keyword.
Setting Bid strategy Max CPC / bids Use when Source
Impression share Target Absolute Top of Page Impressions at 100% Max CPC $5 You need the top position on your own store name at all times
CPC (enhanced) Enhanced CPC, ad group default Max. CPC US$1.00 Brand keywords bid at US$1.55 You want keyword-level bids that stay competitive on your own brand term
  • Keyword-level bids can be set separately from the ad group default.
  • The bid should be high enough to stay competitive on your own brand term.
  • In the Source B example, the max CPC was 1.55 USD. The observed average CPC was 0.14 USD.
  • The average CPC was low because no competitor bid on the brand term.
  • Cost per click stays low when you are the most relevant result for your own brand name.
  • Bidding on a brand search keyword needs only a small budget (2023).

Budgets

1 min
  • Start with a budget of $5 to $10 per day.
  • Brand search can run for $5 to $10 per day.
  • A trademark is a legal right to a brand name or logo.
  • A daily budget of about $10 is enough for a trademark campaign, meaning a campaign on your brand name.
  • That budget is often not fully spent.
  • The budget can be very small. It should grow or shrink with how many people search for your name.
  • Early brand campaigns reached about 5x to 6x ROAS (return on ad spend).
  • The budget cannot grow much. Only a small number of people search for a specific brand name.
  • The $5 to $10 range is the starting point.
  • The $10 daily figure is the upper end for a trademark campaign.

Ad Copy for Brand Defense

1 min

General Rules

1 min
  • The brand ad should lead with the store name. Follow it with a call-to-action and benefits, such as free shipping and 30-day returns.
  • A branded search ad usually gets the highest Quality Score (Google's rating of how relevant your ad and page are).
  • It also usually gets the first position for your own brand term.
  • This happens because the landing page (the page a visitor reaches after clicking) directly matches the searched keyword.
  • It is hard for competitors to outrank you there. Outrank means appear higher than you.
  • Competitors may target your brand name as a keyword in a search text ad.
  • They may not write your brand name into the ad text, because Google policy (Google's rules) forbids that.

Brand-Focused Responsive Search Ad Template

1 min

A responsive search ad is an ad with several headlines and descriptions. A description is the longer text line under the headlines. Google mixes them together. Use this template:

A brand-focused responsive search ad follows a fixed layout you can adapt to your own store. The first headline (30-character limit) includes your company name, for example in a phrase like 'Browse Our Store: [Company Name]'. The second headline (30-character limit) points to a main advantage, such as free delivery combined with hassle-free returns. The third headline (30-character limit) states a price-match pledge or guarantee, for example 'We'll Undercut Any Price'. Description 1 (90-character limit) reads: 'Looking for the right [product]? Reach out to our staff today.' Description 2 (90-character limit) reads: 'Spot a cheaper offer online? Give us a ring and we will beat it.' Place a call extension under the ad so people on mobile can phone you directly with one tap.

Brand-Defense Ad Pattern

1 min
  • Put your exact business name in Headline 1. The searcher then sees right away that they are in the right place.
  • Use the remaining headlines and descriptions to present an offer, a guarantee, or a call-to-action.
  • Aim these at returning visitors who may still be comparing options.
  • Example pattern:
Shop [Brand] Official Store
Free Returns On All Orders
Questions? Call Our Team Today

Competitor-Conquest Ad Pattern

1 min
  • Target a rival's brand name as a keyword.
  • Avoid writing the rival's name in the copy.
  • Lead with a clear differentiator, which is a clear reason you are different or better.
  • Example pattern:
Looking For A Better Platform?
Start Your Store With Free Trial
Lower Fees, Faster Setup

Extensions

1 min
  • A maximum of four site-link extensions can be shown per ad.
  • Choose the four most common collections (groups of products) or products that visitors want to reach directly.
  • Site-link extensions are especially useful for brand-name searches.
  • Searchers often do not know which part of your site they want.
  • Direct links bring them closer to a conversion (a sale).
  • Add a call extension so mobile users can phone you with one tap.

Competitor Bidding and Trademark Rules

1 min

Two positions exist. Use the one that fits your product's trademark situation and your store's stage.

Position 1: Competitor Bidding Can Be Legitimate

1 min
  • Bidding on competitor brand names is a legitimate (allowed) strategy.
  • Check whether a competitor runs an ad on its own brand name.
  • If it does not, you may run a search text ad targeting that name with a compelling (strong) offer.
  • If competitors are not running Google Ads, bidding on their brand keywords can capture searchers who would otherwise look for alternatives.
  • Google ads appear above organic results, so the advertiser ranks first.
  • Some sellers bid on a large competitor's brand name to take its searchers.
  • So protecting your own brand term with a brand search campaign is necessary.
  • A seller who does not yet run Google ads can capture search traffic for a larger competitor's brand name by bidding on that name as a keyword.
  • The seller's listing then appears when shoppers search for the competitor.
  • Check trademark rules before running brand-name keywords this way.

Position 2: Avoid Competitor Brand Bidding; Use Custom Audiences

1 min

Custom audiences are lists of people you can target with ads, built from data you have.

  • Bidding on competitor brand names gives many clicks but low conversion rates.
  • Brand owners can block that bidding through trademarks.
  • Use competitor names only to build custom audiences, not as bid keywords.
  • Bidding on brand names you are authorized (permitted) to sell usually converts well and is allowed.
  • Bidding on another company's trademarked brand name is often rejected by Google once the owner files a trademark.
  • It tends to bring clicks with low conversion.
  • Use competitor names only to build custom audiences.
  • Do not add competitor brand names as keywords in Google Shopping campaigns (product picture ads). Shopping keywords come from your product pages and feed. A feed is your product list sent to Google.
  • Competitor names belong only in a search text campaign.
  • Use them only for best-selling products, and only after the store is already profitable.

When Each Position Applies

1 min
  • Position 1 applies where the competitor does not advertise on its own name and you have no trademark conflict.
  • Check the trademark situation first.
  • Position 2 applies where the brand is trademarked by someone else, or where the store is not yet profitable.
  • Use custom audiences in that case.
  • Competitor ads on a product search may target a broader term, such as the product type or a platform keyword. They do not have to target your exact product name.
  • Their ad may use dynamic headline insertion, where the headline changes to match the search words. This makes it look more relevant.
  • So their presence does not prove they bid on your exact term.

Competitor Ad Examples

1 min
  • Shopify runs a paid ad on its own brand name.
  • Wix runs a paid ad on the keyword "shopify" without mentioning Shopify in its copy.
  • Shopify's own branded ad keeps it in the first position above Wix's ad.
  • The competitor-conquest pattern is in Ad Copy for Brand Defense above.

Shopping and Brand Restrictions

1 min
  • Google Shopping is not keyword-based. In plain words, it does not run on the search words you pick.
  • A brand that bans bidding on its brand name in search text campaigns does not automatically block Shopping ads.
  • Ask the brand or supplier whether its products may appear on Google Shopping (2023).
  • A Google Shopping keyword brand term with high traffic (many visitors) but no sales should not be bid aggressively (bid high and spend a lot).
  • A brand name often ranks higher than a store for brand-only searches.
  • Product-page keywords can outrank brand sites (2024).

Brand Contamination and Negative Keywords

1 min
  • Brand contamination means branded searches are sneaking into a campaign meant for new customers.
  • In a non-brand campaign, it means branded searches are using the prospecting budget (money meant for finding new customers).
  • Use the brand split (a view that separates brand searches from non-brand searches) to decide one of two things.
  • The first choice: a dedicated (separate) brand campaign is needed.
  • The second choice: branded terms must be excluded from prospecting campaigns.
  • Negative keywords are words that stop your ad from showing.
  • Your own brand and product names should be added as negative keywords only when branded searches are being forced into a separate dedicated brand campaign.
  • Otherwise they stay out of the negative list.

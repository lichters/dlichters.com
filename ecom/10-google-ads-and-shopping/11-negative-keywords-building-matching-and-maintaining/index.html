Negative keywords: building, matching and maintaining

Section 10.11 · 11 of 23 in this chapter · 46 min read

In short

  • Do not block a search word just because it has no clicks yet. Block it only when it is not about what you sell.
  • A negative keyword (a word that stops your ad from showing for that search) can quietly block real buyers. Read every term before you add it.
  • Your own brand, product and product-code searches belong in the Alpha campaign (the campaign meant to show for them). Never add them as negatives there.
  • Find the real searches in the Search terms report (the list of what people typed). Do not use the Keywords section for this.
  • AI lists are a head start, not a final list. Check every term yourself.
More key points
  • Add negative keywords (blocked words that stop your ad from showing) every week, not only at the start.
  • Work hardest in the first 1 to 3 weeks of a new campaign.
  • Never stop the review completely. It gets lighter as the campaign gets older.
  • Wait a few days before blocking a weak word. Block it only if it gets clicks but no sales.
  • If a search brings buyers, keep it. Pay less per click instead of blocking it.

Negative Keywords: Building, Matching and Maintaining

1 min

Why Negatives Matter

1 min
  • Negative keywords stop Google from showing your ads for irrelevant searches, which shifts budget away from low-converting traffic toward profitable keywords over time.
  • Negative keywords stop Google from spending budget on the queries you add, so over the first month or two a Shopping campaign becomes more focused and more spend goes to queries that make sales. Starting with a small budget helps you find non-revenue queries sooner.
  • Roughly 20% of the search terms made on the Google search network each day are unique, meaning they have never been searched before. They cannot be predicted in advance and can only be handled by exclusion.
  • Negative keyword lists substantially reduce the time needed to manage Google Ads campaigns compared with managing exclusions without them, and make it more likely that campaigns run as intended.
  • Google matches products to search terms by reading the product feed in its own way. In the first one to three weeks, many search terms that no buyer would use may appear and need negatives.
  • Google builds Shopping keyword matches automatically from the product feed. It sometimes matches searches unrelated to the store or containing brand names you do not sell, so check the Search terms report regularly and add those queries as negatives.
  • Google chooses which products to show for generic searches based on feed data, including title, SKU, description and keywords. To reduce impressions for a product, add negative keywords or lower its CPC at product level.
  • Many advertisers never review the Search terms report, so they do not add new negatives or exclude non-converting terms. The report is where most wasted spend shows up, so check it regularly.
  • The Search terms report can reveal wasteful searches to exclude, winning searches to scale, comparison or "X versus Y" queries that suggest new keywords, and the split between brand and non-brand searches. Accounts with real spend produce hundreds or thousands of rows.

Shopping search terms and Gmail or YouTube:

  • Shopping ads that also show on Gmail or YouTube can trigger search terms through audiences. To see clean search term data, set the ads to appear only in Google Shopping results.

Shared Library and Negative Keyword Lists

  • Negative keywords can be added to a single ad group, to a single campaign, or to a shared negative keyword list. A shared list is the only option that applies one entry to several campaigns at once.
  • Negative keyword lists are reused across campaigns from the Shared Library. It holds negative keyword lists alongside bid strategies, shared budgets, the audience manager, location groups and placement exclusion lists.
  • Negative keyword lists are found under Tools and Settings > Shared library > Negative keyword lists. Select the list, choose Apply to campaigns, tick the target campaigns and apply.
  • To remove a wrong assignment, open the list, go to the campaign list, tick the campaign and choose remove list from selected campaign.
  • Tools and Settings > Shared Library > Negative keyword lists opens the list overview. It shows each list with its keyword count and number of campaigns, and the plus button creates a new list.
  • The new list dialog has the fields List name and Add negative keywords, with the hint "Enter or paste your negative keywords, one per line", plus SAVE and CANCEL buttons. A list cannot be saved until it contains at least one negative keyword.
  • Inside a negative keyword list, the APPLY TO CAMPAIGNS button attaches the list to selected campaigns. The detail page offers DOWNLOAD, EXPAND and ADD FILTER options. Each keyword in the detail view has a Match type column.
  • The Master Generic Negative Keywords list is meant to grow over time into the largest negative keyword list in the account. It holds every irrelevant search term that your ads should never show for.
  • Create the negative keyword lists before building a Shopping campaign so they are ready to apply at the right moment. Creating a list does not require applying it to a campaign at that point.

Setup procedure for the three lists:

  1. Open Tools and Settings in Google Ads.
  2. Under Shared Library, click Negative keyword lists.
  3. Click the plus button to create a new list.
  4. Enter the list name Master Generic Negative Keywords.
  5. Paste the negative keywords from a ready-made generic starter list into the Add negative keywords field, one per line.
  6. Click SAVE. The list cannot be saved without at least one keyword.
  7. Create a new list named Brand Names.
  8. Enter the names of the brands you sell through your website, using just the brand name, one per line.
  9. Click SAVE.
  10. Create a new list named Product IDs, product identifiers or product names.
  11. Enter the unique identifiers of your products, such as model numbers and model names, exactly as they appear in your product titles or in the manufacturer or supplier documentation, one per line.
  12. Click SAVE.
  13. Do not apply the lists to any campaign yet. They are applied when the Shopping campaign is set up.

Adding Negatives in Shopping Campaigns

Steps for adding negatives in Shopping campaigns:

  1. Log in to Google Ads and click Campaigns in the left navigation, then Campaigns in the dropdown.
  2. Use the toggle to select Shopping campaigns (the options shown depend on your campaign types).
  3. Open the beta campaign first, then repeat the same steps for the alpha campaign.
  4. Do not use the Keywords section for this. It only shows negative keywords already added.
  5. Expand Insights and reports at the top and click Search terms, checking the beta campaign one at a time.
  6. Review the list of search terms the ads appeared for, and check off the ones to exclude.
  7. Click Add to negative keywords. The added terms then appear in the negative keywords list in the Keywords section.
  8. Switch to the alpha campaign in the Search terms report and add only universal negatives there (see the alpha and beta rules).

Negative keywords are added in the campaign under Keywords in the sidebar, using the Negative keywords section and its plus button.

Source A 2025 (Beta location): open the Beta campaign, expand "Audiences, keywords and content" in the sidebar, click "Keywords", then "Negative keywords", and add them to the campaign (Beta Source A merch) rather than to an ad group.

Entering negatives:

  • Enter negative keywords directly in Google Ads line by line. A spreadsheet is not needed.
  • Alpha and beta campaigns each need their own negative keyword list.

Multi-Campaign Setups (High, Medium and Low Priority)

  • Negative keyword work is done in the high priority campaign only, because Google checks that campaign first. If the setup is correct, the medium and low priority campaigns should not show unwanted terms.
  • A negative keyword added to only one campaign pushes the term into the other campaigns, where it costs more per click without a better chance of conversion. Every negative added at the high priority level must also apply to the other two campaigns.
  • Add negative keywords to the shared master generic negative keyword list, which applies to all three campaigns at once. This avoids missed campaigns and copy errors when lists are long.
  • Decide whether to review search terms per campaign or per ad group based on how many brands and product types the store carries. A store with many brands and product types usually needs ad group level review, while a narrow store can be reviewed at campaign level.

Campaign Moves and Keyword Placement

  • Do not move keywords between campaigns. To keep a term out of a campaign, add it there as a negative keyword.
  • Example: a main niche term such as pool table is added as a keyword in beta and as a negative keyword in alpha, so alpha does not spend on it.

Master Negative List

  • Add negative keywords to a master list that applies to all new campaigns. Example list of universal terms that never convert: secondhand, used, wholesale, local, job, training, advice, agency, and similar terms.
  • Merchant Center is used for universal negatives during cleanup of a campaign with no sales.

Universal Negatives

2 min

What Qualifies

  • Universal negatives are terms that should never trigger an ad, such as unrelated products or topics. They belong in both Alpha and Beta. Brand, SKU and product-name negatives belong only in Beta, because Alpha has to be able to show for those searches.
  • Universal negative keywords are terms for other brands you do not sell, and for products or niches unrelated to your store. Add them as negatives in both the alpha and the beta campaign.
  • Universal negatives are only for keywords that never lead to a sale on your store, for example an unrelated product term. Do not use universal negatives for competitor brand names that buyers might search with your product name.
  • Add terms you never want to appear for to the universal negative keyword list, so they block across all campaigns.
  • Example: a chandelier store that shows for tanning beds should block tanning beds in both campaigns so no money is spent on it.
  • If a search term shows another brand you do not carry, add it as a universal negative keyword. Do not exclude generic brand names that could be confused with your own products.

Starting List and Timing

  • Start with a universal list of negative keywords, plus your brand names and SKU numbers. Then review the keyword reports for the active campaigns every two to three days and add any unwanted search terms as negatives.
  • Add universal negative keywords to Alpha and Beta campaigns right away, before data comes in.
  • Universal negative keywords should be added to both alpha and beta campaigns from day one. New irrelevant search terms that appear later should be added as you find them.
  • Add universal negative keywords as irrelevant terms appear in ad reports. Start with a published list, then keep extending it through regular audits.
  • Add generic words such as "free", "cheap" and "local" as universal negative keywords on both alpha and beta campaigns. Broader niche terms should only be blocked as exact-match negatives.
  • Add negative keywords by applying the same universal negatives and campaign-specific negatives from the product feed submission lesson, and update them based on the search terms that bring irrelevant clicks.

Sources disagree on how much to add at the start. Read together: add the obviously irrelevant universal terms from day one, and hold back sweeping broad terms until search term data exists.

Early-phase advice that conflicts with the "add now" rules above:

  • Be selective early in a campaign. If traffic is too new to tell what will convert, only block obviously irrelevant keywords. Broad negatives early risk blocking future sales.
  • Start with a few basic negative keywords, and add more as the search terms that trigger clicks become clear. Adding too many negatives at the start limits reach.
  • Do not add many negative keywords at the start. Begin with basic terms such as free and cheap, then add more negatives from actual search terms that waste money.
  • Start with broad keyword coverage and add negative keywords over time, based on search terms that do not match the products. Do not limit the keyword list at the start, because more impressions give more data.

Match Types

5 min

Match Type Reference

Match type Syntax What it blocks Typical use Sources
Broad term, no symbols Searches containing the term; see the conflict below on single generic words Universal terms irrelevant to every product (e.g. free, retail); brand, product and SKU list in Beta
Phrase "term" Every variation containing the word or phrase Brand names; product-type phrases you never want; long competitor-style product titles in Beta
Exact [term] Only that exact phrase Specific terms that must be blocked exactly; generic words that must not block product-name searches; suppliers; broad niche keywords taking budget

Syntax check: exact match negatives are typed in square brackets, for example [competitor name], and phrase match negatives in quotation marks, for example "competitor name".

Other match-type rules:

  • Negatives in Google Shopping work as broad match, so every variation containing the negative term is blocked, not only the exact phrase. One negative such as course or videos covers many related queries.
  • Negative keyword match types: broad match blocks related searches, while phrase and exact match are narrower and better suited for most negatives.
  • Choose the match type for a negative keyword by how closely the term matches the products you want to keep showing. Broad and phrase negatives also block longer searches that contain the term, so a generic word like chandeliers can be added as an exact match negative if specific product-name searches must still appear.
  • Choose negative keyword match type per keyword. Use exact match for a single term that should always be blocked, and broad match to block variations. In practice, a mix of match types is common.
  • Check the match type before you add any negative keyword.
  • Use exact match negatives for generic terms that you want to block.
  • Exact match negative keyword: enter the term in square brackets in the negative keywords list, for example [table], so only that exact search is blocked.
  • Negative keywords do not automatically cover close variants, plurals or misspellings the way positive keywords do. Add each variant, such as plurals and misspellings of a blocked word, as its own negative.
  • Positive match types for search text ads differ from the negative keyword match types used in Shopping campaigns. Do not transfer the negative keyword rules to search text ads.

Broad Match Pitfalls

  • A broad match negative keyword blocks every search that contains that word, including specific product searches such as dining table with model numbers. Use exact match negatives for generic terms that you want to block.
  • Broad match negatives also block searches that contain the term with extra words before or after it. Use exact match negatives (wrapped in brackets) only when a specific term must be blocked exactly, and not for the brand, product, and SKU list.
  • A broad generic word such as sinks should not be added to alpha or beta manual campaigns. Broad matching would show ads for any search containing that word, so use exact match only if clicks justify it.
  • Negative keywords set as broad match block long-tail variants. Blocking broad "standing desk" also blocks "model 123 standing desk", because the system matches the partial phrase. Use exact or phrase match instead.
  • Do not add negative keywords using broad match. Use exact match to avoid accidentally blocking related keywords.
  • Add negative keywords as exact match by default, because phrase or broad match negatives can block traffic you want. Example: a broad negative "generators" would also block "brand name generators". Use phrase or broad match only for terms you never want to appear for.

Single Generic Words

Sources disagree on how a single generic word behaves:

Source Behaviour described Applies when
Source A Single-word negatives use broad match, which blocks any search containing that word. Single words such as "free" that should be blocked everywhere.
Source A Broad match blocks every variation containing the term. Negatives in Shopping campaigns.

Test the behaviour in your own account before relying on either version.

Match Type Examples

  • Exact match example: [infrared sauna portable] blocks only that exact term but allows "infrared sauna portable for cats". Using just [portable] blocks only the word portable and still allows "portable infrared sauna". Use phrase match "portable" to block all variations containing portable.
  • Exact match example: a negative exact match for blue pool does not block searches for blue pool q.
  • Negative exact match keywords in brackets block only that exact phrase. Negative phrase match keywords in quotes block every search variation containing that word or phrase, which makes them useful for blocking entire categories like "portable".
  • For single-word negative keywords, such as free, use broad match (no quotes or brackets) to block any search containing that word. For brand name negatives, use phrase match (quotes) to block exact brand plus variations. For exact blocking, use exact match (brackets) when only one specific phrase must be blocked.
  • Use broad match for universal negative keywords that are irrelevant to every product, such as free or retail. Use phrase match for brand names and for product-type phrases you do not want, such as an unrelated product term.
  • Use a broad negative only when the term is irrelevant to every product you sell. Exact match suits any term that could still lead to a sale on a specific product.

Over-Blocking Risks

2 min
  • Double-check your negative keyword list early on. A negative keyword is a word that stops your ad from showing for that search. Too many can cut off buyers who search for specific product names.
  • Do not add a broad negative that blocks a model, product or buying term. A broad negative blocks any search containing that word, in any order. Blocking a broad use-case term can also block buyers who search for the same product type.
  • Add a use-case search term as a negative keyword only when it clearly shows no purchase intent (no sign the person wants to buy). Example: a treadmill search for weight loss.
  • Never block model numbers, brand names or clear buying terms.
  • Adding a generic keyword as a broad or phrase negative can block the specific product-name searches that convert best. A phrase negative blocks a search only when the words appear in that order. A "conversion" means a sale or other goal the customer completes.
  • Do not negate ingredient and feature terms that describe your selling points. This applies even when an AI lists them as irrelevant.
  • Check the negative list against your full product range and selling points before you add anything.
  • Some terms that look irrelevant can be real early-stage demand. Examples: "how to" or recipe searches (people still learning), or "wholesale" searches when your brand sells wholesale (in bulk to other businesses).
  • Put these in a review list instead of negating them automatically. Call it "Review before adding."
  • Never negate terms that could come from real buyers.
  • When unsure, place the term in a separate "Review before adding" list, not the main list.
  • A negative keyword added by mistake will quietly block real buyers.
  • Read every term in an AI-generated list before you add it to your account. Treat AI suggestions as a head start, not a final list.
  • Uploading a large list of negative keywords generated by ChatGPT can block searches you want, such as relevant customer queries. Check each proposed negative keyword against your target customers before you add it.
  • Negative keywords reduce spend on low-intent searches (people who are not ready to buy). But a list built too fast can limit your reach. Build negatives gradually.
  • Do not block a search term only because it has no clicks yet. Block it when it is not relevant to what you sell, whether or not it gets clicks.
  • Do not block a term only because it has no clicks yet. But do not wait when it is clearly irrelevant. Waiting before you cut these wastes impressions (times your ad was shown) on unrelated searches.

Testing for Over-Blocking

Test whether broad negatives are blocking profitable traffic (visitors who could make you money):

  1. Copy all universal negative keywords to a Google Doc. A universal negative list is one shared list used across all your campaigns.
  2. Remove most negatives from the campaign. Keep only your core niche keyword (the main word describing what you sell).
  3. Run the campaign for one week.
  4. If results improve, add back only the obviously irrelevant negatives.
  5. If results get worse, restore all negatives.

This test shows whether your negatives are too restrictive.

Competitor Names

1 min
  • Do not block competitor names as negative keywords by default. Let your search term data show which terms lose money, then add those as negatives.
  • Start with exact match on competitor brand names rather than blocking them fully. Exact match blocks only that exact search.
  • Long competitor-style product titles that trigger your ad can be added as negative keywords. Enter them as phrase match negatives in quotation marks, like this: ("term"). This blocks exact searches and pushes them into the other campaign. Source A, Beta campaign.
  • If search terms show a competitor that sells direct to consumers (DTC, straight to shoppers) and does not sell products you carry, add that brand as a broad negative keyword. This stops your ad from showing for it.
  • Competitor brand names belong in the negative list only for prospecting campaigns (campaigns that look for new customers) that should not target competitors.
  • Flag competitor names for review, because the right answer depends on the category.

Own Brand and Product Names

2 min
  • In the Beta campaign (the campaign for finding new searches), add your own brand names, product names and SKU numbers as negative keywords. SKU means a product code. This pushes that traffic to the Alpha campaign.
  • Never add your own brand, product or SKU terms as negatives in Alpha. Alpha has to be able to show for them.
  • Add branded product names and specific SKUs as negative phrase or exact match keywords in your Beta campaign. This sends them automatically to your Alpha campaign.
  • Example: if "Lifestyles" is your brand, add the negative phrase "lifestyles" to Beta. This blocks all related searches there.
  • For Beta campaigns, add the brand names you sell, the product names you sell and your SKU numbers as negative keywords. This stops your ads from showing for your own products in Beta.
  • Beta campaign negative list: add all brand names you are authorized to sell (for example, the five authorized brands), all product names from your store, and all SKU numbers. Enter them as broad match. Searches for these terms are blocked in Beta and handled by Alpha.
  • Update the list over time as new brands are added.
  • Brand names for the Beta list go in the Brand Names list. Use just the brand name, one per line.
  • Block brand names in the Beta campaign, along with vague short-tail keywords (short, general search words) and universal keywords. Set up match types (broad, phrase, exact) later, based on data.

Own-Brand Negatives in a Brand Campaign

  • In a campaign with a brand term, do not add your own brand to the negative keyword list if you carry that brand.
  • Negative keywords should block only terms for product types you do not sell. Example: "commercial" can be added as a universal negative.
  • Use this rule for the brand campaign. The Beta rules above apply to the Beta structure.

Suppliers and Other Brands

1 min
  • Do not add search terms for suppliers you don't carry as negative keywords from day one.
  • Add them only after search term reports show consistent spend without sales. Use exact match, for example a supplier's brand name only.
  • If a search term shows another brand you do not carry, add it as a universal negative keyword.
  • Universal negative keywords are terms for other brands you do not sell.

Building Negatives from the Search Terms Report

6 min

Where to Find the Report

  • To find the search terms that trigger your ads in Google Ads, go to Campaigns → Select your campaign → Insights & Reports → Search Terms. Insights & Reports is the analysis menu inside a campaign.
  • The Search terms report sits inside a campaign under Insights and reports > Search terms. It shows the actual phrases people typed that triggered your ads, with metrics for each.
  • Use Add filter on the Search term column to narrow the list.
  • To see which searches triggered your ads, open the campaign in Google Ads. Expand "Insights and reports" and select the "Search terms" tab.
  • Review each term for relevance. Add irrelevant ones as negative keywords.
  • To see which search terms triggered Google Shopping ads, open the Shopping campaign. Click Insights and reports in the left navigation, then click Search terms. It shows the terms you appeared for and what you spent on each.
  • To see which searches triggered your Google Ads, open the campaign in Google Ads and click Keywords. Review the listed search terms. Mark the irrelevant or overly broad ones as negative keywords.
  • To see which search terms converted (led to a sale or goal), open Google Ads, select the campaign, then click Keywords or Search terms in the left sidebar.
  • Choose the date range. The view shows the terms that triggered ads and the conversions they produced.
  • To see which search terms trigger a Beta campaign, open Google Ads, go to the Beta campaign, click Keywords, and review the triggering search terms.
  • Do not use the Keywords section for this. It only shows negative keywords already added, not the search terms your ads actually appeared for.
  • Use the Search terms report under Insights and reports instead.
  • To find which search terms a Shopping product got impressions for:
    1. Open Google Ads.
    2. Go to your Alpha and Beta campaigns.
    3. Open the keywords view and click the search terms your product appeared for.
    4. Look for keywords with many impressions that go to that product.

Reading the Report

  • The Search terms view shows these columns: Search term, Match type, Added/Excluded, Ad group, Clicks, Impr., CTR, Avg. CPC, Cost and Conversions.
  • Use these columns to spot irrelevant terms, low click-through terms and clicks without sales. Click-through rate (CTR) is the share of people who saw your ad and clicked. Avg. CPC means average cost per click.
  • The Search Terms view can list thousands of terms over 30 days. Example: 6,971 terms in an established account.
  • The view can show up to 500 rows per page. A single ad group view can show 1 to 500 of 2,673 terms.
  • The Google Ads search terms view splits its rows into pages. So only a fraction of a large report is visible on screen at once.
  • Export the complete search terms report to a file before analysis, instead of reading the on-screen list.
  • Use the search terms report within each ad group (a group of ads and keywords inside a campaign) to view the exact searches your ads triggered.
  • Add irrelevant searches as negative keywords at the ad group level. This prevents wasted spend.
  • Shopping campaign search terms: open the campaign, then the ad group. Look at Keywords and then Search terms. Expand the date range if nothing shows.
  • If a product group reads "not set," click it and expand it. A product group is a set of products inside a Shopping campaign.
  • Search terms optimization in Shopping campaigns: sort by clicks to see where most traffic came from.
  • Check that branded terms show up in the campaign that should contain them. Branded terms normally convert very well, so they should not run in the Beta campaign.
  • Compare reported impressions with the impressions the ad group actually received. The difference is search traffic Google does not report. Example: 1,500 shown vs 3,800 received means about 2,000 hidden.
  • Compare the compare-period columns against the previous period to spot new waste. These columns are Clicks, Impr., CTR, Conversions, Conv. rate, Cost / conv. and Cost. Conv. rate means the share of clicks that convert. Cost / conv. means the cost per conversion.
  • Build a negative keyword list from the search terms report. Add a term as a negative only after checking whether it produced clicks or conversions.
  • Build negative keyword lists from the search terms report. Add terms that get impressions or clicks but make no sense for your product. Also add terms that spend money without producing sales.
  • Negative keywords come from the search terms report. Any term that triggers your ads but does not match what you sell should be added as a negative.
  • Example: if you sell only glass dining tables, the word "wood" should be a negative keyword when it triggers searches.
  • Negative keywords come in three match types: exact, phrase and broad. Exact blocks only that exact search. Phrase blocks it when the words appear in that order. Broad blocks it when the words appear in any order.
  • Every irrelevant search term found in the Search terms report becomes a negative. The report decides what to cut.
  • Negative keyword decisions need judgement, not a fixed rule. Combine click-through rate, relevance and conversions.
  • In a profitable account, exclude only a few terms. In an unprofitable account, cut more aggressively.
  • Example: a search term with 13,000 impressions, 19 clicks, a CTR of 0.14% and no tracked conversions is a clear negative keyword candidate.
  • Either the term is not relevant to the product, or shoppers are not looking for it.
  • When low CTR is the problem, exclude search terms with a CTR below 0.5%, unless conversion data shows these terms still produce sales.
  • About three months of search term data is usually enough for a useful search term report. Use a shorter or longer period depending on how much data the account has.

Weekly Review Procedure

  1. Open the high priority campaign. Click into it to see its ad groups, then open Keywords and choose Search Terms.
  2. Set the date range from the day after your last review to today. Example: 2 November to 7 November. Then apply it.
  3. Work through the list from top to bottom. Mark search terms the business does not sell, clearly irrelevant terms, terms with very low click-through rate, and terms with clicks but no conversions.
  4. Select the chosen terms, choose Add as negative keyword, select the master generic negative keyword list and save.
  5. Save the selected negatives before going to the next page. Selections are not kept when you change pages.
  6. Repeat for each page of up to 500 rows until the end of the list.
  7. Note the review date so the next date range starts the day after it.
  • Remove search terms for products or services the business does not sell. Examples: outdoor saunas or Roman tubs.
  • Plural forms and variants of the same term may need their own negative entries.
  • Remove searches for used, rental or repair items when you sell only new products. Words such as used, second-hand, rental or hire do not match your offer. Add them as negative keywords.
  • Image-only or repair-related searches for products you sell are also poor matches. Exclude them when they waste clicks.
  • Broad category searches often come from people who want product images, repair or servicing of a product they already own, used or secondhand items, or rental for an event. A retailer of new goods cannot sell to any of these.
  • Clicks from such terms cost money without sales. Limit them with negative keywords or bid less.
  • Example: if a search term shows an irrelevant category, such as a chandelier store showing for tanning beds, block it in both campaigns.

Search Term Routine by Campaign Type

2 min

Shopping cleanup for a campaign with no sales:

  1. Export the search term report. Review terms that received impressions, not only terms that got clicks.
  2. Mark irrelevant or research-only terms as negatives.
  3. Add negatives at campaign level or as universal negatives. Use Merchant Center (Google's product feed tool) for universal ones.
  4. Leave bids and budgets unchanged while the new negatives take effect.
  5. Set up Meta remarketing (showing ads again to people who already visited) shortly after the negatives are in place.

Finding negative candidates in the Alpha campaign:

  1. Open the Google Ads account.
  2. Click into the Alpha campaign.
  3. Open the Keywords view.
  4. Review the keywords that received impressions, the clicks each one produced, and the sales that came from them.
  5. Add the irrelevant or vague terms as negative keywords.

Reviewing and refining negatives in the Alpha and Beta Shopping campaigns:

  1. Open the Alpha and Beta Shopping campaigns.
  2. Review the search terms that triggered clicks.
  3. Check whether each search term is relevant to buying your products.
  4. Add irrelevant search terms as negative keywords. Keep the list growing over time.

Daily routine for Google Shopping campaigns:

  1. Each morning or evening, open the search terms report for the day.
  2. Mark search terms with no buying intent as negative keywords. Examples: generic terms, video searches, or unrelated topics such as "standup paddle board yoga."
  3. Keep brand names, product names and SKU numbers, because these show purchase intent.
  4. Compare impressions with clicks for each search term. Investigate keywords with many impressions but few clicks.

Steps to find negative keywords:

  1. Let the ads run for a while.
  2. Review the search terms that trigger your ads.
  3. Mark the terms that are irrelevant to your products. Examples: free, retail, or a related but different product type.
  4. Add those terms as negative keywords with the right match type.

Daily checks for irrelevant keywords:

  1. Check Google Shopping ads daily for impressions on irrelevant keywords.
  2. Screenshot or note keywords that do not match your product category or brand.
  3. Add these as negative keywords in your campaign settings before they generate clicks and cost.
  4. Repeat this process every day to prevent budget waste on unqualified traffic.
  • Search terms optimization in Shopping campaigns: use the same five-step routine above. Then add terms you never want to appear for to the universal negative list.
  • Example of a search term to block in a chandelier store: a term that shows the ads for tanning beds. Block it in both campaigns.

Find the Right Scope Before Changing Anything Else

1 min
  • If Google Shopping spend goes to search terms without buying intent, fix keyword targeting and add negative keywords first. Only then change the niche or widen the product range.
  • Buying intent means the searcher is ready to purchase a specific product now.
  • If search impression share stays low for broad category keywords in a Beta campaign set up for brand plus SKU searches, raise the default cost per click (CPC, the amount you pay per click) so those keywords get impressions.
  • Impression share means the share of possible impressions your ads actually received. Add the brand name as a negative keyword to keep category searches out.
  • When a broad niche keyword takes most of the Google Shopping budget, add that keyword as an exact match negative keyword. This shifts spend toward more purchase-ready searches.
  • Negative keywords reduce wasted spend, lift click-through rate and bring more relevant impressions.
  • If search impression share is low, the ads are often showing for irrelevant search terms.
  • If visitors click but do not convert, check which keywords generate those clicks. Add negatives for irrelevant searches. The buying intent of the traffic drives your conversion rate.
  • Negative keywords reduce spend on low-intent searches and raise the share of buyers in your traffic.
  • For a product that is shown too often, add negative keywords or lower its CPC at the product level.

Starter List for a New Campaign

  • For a new campaign with no search history, an AI can build a starter negative keyword list from the store URL alone. This avoids launching blind and spending the first week's budget on irrelevant searches.
  • Starter list prompt for a new Search or Shopping campaign on an eCommerce store. Copy it exactly.
You are a Google Ads strategist building a starter negative keyword list for a new Search or Shopping campaign on an eCommerce store.

Store URL: [paste store URL]
Product overview (optional, use only if the URL gives little detail): [one line]

Review the store to determine what it truly sells: product types, categories, price level and the type of buyer it targets. If the URL cannot be read, request the page contents from me instead of guessing.

Then compile a starter list of negative keywords for searches that would waste budget or indicate the wrong intent for this store. Sort the negatives into these buckets:
- Free / cheap intent (for example 'free', 'cheap', 'discount code', 'coupon')
- Job / career intent (for example 'jobs', 'careers', 'salary', 'hiring')
- Non-buyer intent (for example 'DIY')
- Wholesale / B2B / bulk (only if the store is retail or DTC)
- Wrong product or category (adjacent items the store does not carry)
- Competitor brand names (include only if they are obvious from the category; flag each one for my review)
- My own brand names, such as my actual brand name and product names unique to my brand

Rules:
- Include only negatives that clearly would not lead to a sale for this specific store.
- Never negate terms that could come from real buyers. When uncertain, place the term in a separate 'Review before adding' list rather than the main list.
- Format each bucket as a plain list I can copy directly into Google Ads.
- End with one short note naming any buckets I should double-check against my own product range.

Include a note stating that this list is a starting seed and must be replaced with search terms report data once real data is available.
  • Discount and promo terms such as "discount code" or "promo code" are often added as negatives. Some accounts target them by mistake and end up competing with large discount websites.
  • So these terms are usually worth excluding.

  • Negative keyword work is heaviest in the first weeks of a new Google Shopping campaign. Irrelevant search terms (words people typed that do not fit your product) are most common then.
  • The workload drops as the campaign matures.
  • The work should never be stopped completely, even for established stores.
  • A new account starts with a short master negative keyword list (your main list of blocked words).
  • An established account can grow that list to thousands of entries.
  • Once the main variants (close versions of the same word) are excluded, few new negatives are usually needed.
  • Most new additions are variations of terms already blocked.
  • Example of list growth: an established account had 6,971 search terms (the exact words people typed before clicking) over 30 days.
  • A search term list growing to 20,000 entries is not inherently good or bad.
  • Review which keywords earn money and which waste spend (money paid for clicks that bring no value). Add negatives where needed.
  • Adding negatives at the start limits reach (how many people see your ad).
  • So begin with a few basic negatives. Add more as the search terms that trigger clicks become clear.
  • Between the alpha and beta campaigns, beta carries more negative keywords than alpha.

Intent Mismatches

1 min

Shopping ads often show a product to people searching for the vehicle or item it fits. They are not searching for the accessory itself. This creates irrelevant paid clicks. Check search term reports (Google's lists of what people typed to see your ads) for this mismatch. Those visitors are not in the market (not ready to buy). Paying for them is wasteful. **

Bidding on every keyword a broad search might trigger wastes budget. This is like a billboard that everyone passing sees. The clicks are irrelevant. Examples are searches for generic terms that match a product's name loosely. Limit targeting to keywords and audiences (groups of people you target) that match what you sell. Also match the intent behind the search.

Do not target all available traffic (visitors) on a search network. A search network is a set of partner sites that also show search ads. Build campaigns to attract the paid traffic that best fits your business. Exclude the rest. Use negative targeting to remove traffic that does not match the right customer, time, and price. **

Brand and Product-Name Negatives

2 min

Brand Names list

The Brand Names list should contain the names of the brands sold through your website. Enter just the brand name. This is a separate negative list from the master and product lists. **

Product IDs list

Create a separate Product IDs list. It may also be named product identifiers or product names. It contains the unique identifiers of your products. A unique identifier is a code that tells one product apart from another. These can be model numbers, model names or similar codes. **

A Product IDs list can mix model numbers such as AM156JDTSZ, BO73D-BLK and BT-0502 with model names such as Baldwin, Barcelona, Barrett, Bergen and Bliss. Such a mix is fine. **

Every product with a unique identifier needs its identifier in the Product IDs list. So 1,000 such products produce 1,000 negative search terms. A list of that size is expected and acceptable. **

If a product has both a model number and a model name, enter both as separate negative terms. Customers may search with either one. So both must be covered. **

One Product IDs negative list held 366 terms. It was applied to 2 Shopping campaigns in the example account. **

SKUs, UPCs and product names

A SKU is your own stock code for a product. A UPC is a barcode number. Add SKU numbers and UPCs as broad match negative keywords. Broad match blocks a search that contains all the words, in any order. Broad negatives catch variations of the SKU.

For product names, use phrase or exact match negative keywords with the brand name and product name. Phrase match blocks the words in the same order, with extra words allowed. Exact match blocks only that exact search. Do this so you do not block searches for other products that share generic words.

Which match type for which item

Match Types and Pitfalls

2 min

Match type definitions

Google Ads describes each negative keyword match type as follows. **

Match type Ad does not show when the search
Broad match Contains all keyword terms, even if they are in a different order
Phrase match Contains the exact keyword terms in the same order
Exact match Contains the exact keyword terms, in the same order, without extra words

A broad match negative blocks a search only when it contains all of the keyword terms. A positive keyword (a word that makes your ad show) triggers on any one of the terms. For negative keywords, broad match is often the usual choice. **

A broad match negative keyword blocks any search containing its words in any order. Phrase match blocks searches containing the phrase, with extra words before or after. Exact match blocks only that exact search. Use broad match for SKU numbers. Use phrase or exact match for brand plus product names. This way you do not block searches for other products that share generic words.

Negative exact keywords are written in square brackets. They block only that exact query. Negative phrase keywords block any query that contains the words. Use phrases for brand names. Use exact keywords for generic terms you want to keep out of a campaign.

Use broad match for terms you never want. Use phrase or exact match for terms you might want in some cases.

Use negative keywords to block searches you do not want. A negative phrase keyword blocks every query that contains that phrase. So a negative phrase on a single word blocks all queries with that word.

Common pitfalls

A broad match negative keyword can also block searches that are relevant to your product. It blocks every search containing that word. Check which relevant searches a broad negative is removing. **

Exact match on a single brand or model word, such as Baldwin, would only block searches that consist of that word alone. It would miss most related queries. **

A negative exact keyword blocks only the exact wording you typed. These can still trigger the campaign:

  • Plural forms (more than one, like "shirts" and "shirt")
  • Word-order changes
  • Abbreviations such as "mens"

So add each common variant of a generic term as its own negative exact keyword.

Add irrelevant search terms as negative keywords. Add close variations as broad match modifier (a broad match where marked words must appear), phrase match and exact match negatives. Keep adding negatives over time. They are the main way to stop wasted spend. **

Phrase match blocking can accidentally block searches that already make money. An example is a product name that contains the blocked phrase. Check first whether the term affects profitable search terms. Then choose exact, phrase or broad match. **

Where to find the report

To view search terms that triggered your ads, open the campaign in Google Ads. Click Keywords in the left navigation. The list shows impressions (times your ad was shown), click-through rate (CTR, the share of people who saw the ad and clicked) and other performance data for each term. **

In Google Ads, find search terms under Insights and reporting > Search terms. The report shows every query the ads appeared for. This includes queries without clicks.

Procedure: alpha and beta campaigns (2024)

  1. Log into the Google Ads account.
  2. Open the alpha and beta campaigns.
  3. Go to Insights and reporting.
  4. Click Search terms to see all search terms the ads appeared for.
  5. Add unwanted search terms as negative keywords.

Procedure: Shopping campaigns (2025)

  1. Open the Shopping campaigns in Google Ads.
  2. Go to Campaigns and expand Insights and reports.
  3. Click Search terms.
  4. Check that the search terms match your keywords and that the traffic is relevant.
  5. Check which product pages those terms land on, and what the conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy) is.

Procedure: weekly search terms workflow (2023)

  1. Open the search terms report in Google Ads. It shows which search terms triggered impressions.
  2. Mark every irrelevant search term and add it to the negative keyword list.
  3. Export the list to Excel or Google Sheets (spreadsheet programs) to sort and manage larger lists more easily.

Procedure: general search term review (undated)

  1. Review the search terms report to see which real queries trigger your ads.
  2. Add irrelevant queries as negative keywords to stop wasted impressions.
  3. Build and maintain a universal negative keyword list that applies across campaigns.
  4. Test ad copy (the words in your ad) continuously. Testing is the main way to improve performance.

Actions during review

  • Add negative keywords for search terms that trigger ads without producing sales. This stops spend on irrelevant traffic. **
  • Add negative keywords for broad-match search terms that bring in clicks but do not match the product. This cuts wasted spend as budgets rise.
  • Add non-specific search terms (terms that are too general) that appear in search reports as negative keywords. Then ads stop showing for them. **
  • Check profitability first when a big-box retailer name (a large chain store) appears as a search term that does not make money. Then add it as a phrase-match negative keyword. A retailer name that converts (turns into a sale) should stay in the campaign. **

Bids and Workload Over Time

1 min
  • A bid is the most you will pay for a click. Raising bids on a low-priority keyword to chase more impressions can flood the campaign with generic, non-converting search terms. Non-converting means they do not turn into sales. Watch the search terms report closely after any bid increase. **

More rules worth knowing

  • Add negative keywords over time to clean junk traffic out of Google Ads campaigns. The blueprint (the step-by-step plan) lists universal negative keywords to block first. Then block weaker terms as they appear in the search term report.

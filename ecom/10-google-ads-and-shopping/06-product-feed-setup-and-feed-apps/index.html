Product feed setup and feed apps

Section 10.6 · 6 of 23 in this chapter · 32 min read

In short

  • Start with one product feed. Use several feeds only when the store is growing.
  • Never run two Google product feeds at the same time.
  • If a supplier gives a barcode number, use it. If not, use the MPN (the manufacturer's part number).
  • Products update every 24 hours. Use the manual update button only for urgent changes.
  • If Google says a product has no barcode number (GTIN), enter its manufacturer part number (MPN) instead.
More key points
  • Never make up a SKU to fill a barcode field.
  • Never use the same number for two different products.
  • Never change a product ID after approval or once ads are running.
  • For most products, set gender to unisex, age group to adult and condition to new.

Choosing a Feed Route

4 min
  • Google's official Shopify app manages your product feed (the list of products Google reads) for Google Merchant Center (Google's account that holds your products) and syncs your store with it automatically.
  • In the Google Shopify app, sync either all products or only products from a specific collection (a group of products you pick in Shopify).
  • The Google Shopify app shows each product as published (synced successfully) or warning (has sync problems). This helps you find and fix feed problems.
  • Symprosis Google Shopping Feed is a Shopify app that starts at $4.99/month, with a free trial. It gives granular control (detailed, fine-grained control) over the product data sent to Google. This is far more than Shopify's free Google & YouTube app.
  • Choose Symprosis when you are scaling a store (growing it with more products and ads).
  • Do not use Shopify's free Google & YouTube app for the product feed if you plan to scale. It offers limited manual control and restricted feed customization (changing what the feed contains). It suits only beginners who will not optimize or grow beyond their first listings.
  • The built-in Google & YouTube app is free, but it gives less manual control over the feed.
  • Once a store grows and needs frequent feed edits, a dedicated feed app such as Symprosis is the better choice.
  • As of 2026, the built-in app was judged far less advanced for this purpose.
  • Free plugins (small add-on programs) that push a feed to Merchant Center are a starting point.
  • Feed tools go further. They aim for more traffic through optimized titles, distribution to secondary and tertiary channels (sales places after Google), and product-level performance views across platforms.
  • A Google Shopping feed app gives far more control over feed fields (the individual data boxes in the feed) than the default Shopify sales channels.
  • With a feed app, you can change titles, images and other feed data without editing each product page.
  • A free Google Sheets feed (a spreadsheet used as the feed) is static, meaning it does not update itself. You update it by hand for every new product.
  • A Google Sheets feed suits only a small catalogue that rarely changes. Otherwise, new suppliers and products would never reach the feed.

Single feed or several feeds

  • Starting setup: keep one feed.
  • Do not run two Google product feeds at once, such as the Shopify Google channel and the Feed for Google Shopping app.
  • Disconnect the Shopify Google channel and keep only one feed.
  • Starting setup: use one product feed and one Shopping campaign setup. A Shopping campaign is a paid Google ad that shows product pictures and prices.
  • Make sure product titles, brands and SKUs are complete, because the feed decides what Google displays. A SKU is the store's internal product code.
  • Scaling setup: you can run several feeds at once.
  • Example: one free-listings feed with all products, and one paid feed with only the most profitable products. Free listings are free product results shown in Google.
  • Link the paid feed to the paid campaigns, and link the free feed to free listings.
  • The single-feed rule applies to the initial setup.
  • The multi-feed setup applies once a store is scaling and separates products by profitability (how much money each product makes).
  • Create a separate feed for each channel, such as Instagram Shops.
  • This lets you limit or customize product descriptions for each platform. Do not send identical content everywhere.

Google & YouTube app versus dedicated feed app

  • Install the Google & YouTube app to connect GA4 (Google Analytics 4, the tool that counts visitors and sales) and Google Ads conversion tracking. Google Ads is Google's paid ad tool.
  • Submit the product feed through a separate, dedicated app.
  • In Shopify, Apps > Google & YouTube is used only to connect GA4 and, optionally, to track sales.
  • Disconnect Merchant Center feed submission there.
  • Set up the feed through a separate Merchant Center feed app.
  • If the Google & YouTube app is connected to Merchant Center, it submits a second feed next to the Google Shopping feed app.
  • This causes daily "please review your file format" emails, even when all products are accepted.
  • Do not activate Shopify's built-in Google sales channel for Shopping. It lacks features and gives weaker results.
  • If you set it live after the feed setup, it duplicates the product feed.
  • Do not tick the box that submits products to Merchant Center through the Google sales channel. Ticking it duplicates the feed from the feed app.
  • Keep the Google sales channel installed, but leave the product submission option unchecked.

Procedure: Remove a duplicate feed

  1. Open the Google & YouTube app in Shopify.
  2. Go to Settings.
  3. If it shows it is connected to Merchant Center, click Disconnect.
  4. Keep the Google Shopping feed app as the only product feed sent to Merchant Center.
  5. Keep the Google & YouTube app installed, because it connects Shopify to Google Analytics.

Connecting Shopify to Merchant Center

5 min

Before connecting

  • The Google Merchant Center account must already exist before the Symprosis setup wizard (the step-by-step setup screens) can link it.
  • Create the Google Merchant Center account first. The app's setup wizard links only to an existing account.
  • Do not submit demo or test products to a live Merchant Center account. They create product warnings.
  • For demonstrations, use a store that is already linked to a real Merchant Center account.
  • The tax and shipping details in Merchant Center must exactly match your Shopify settings.

Setting: Shopping ads setup in Merchant Center

  • Adult content: disabled.
  • Country of sale and language: select the country and English.
  • Destinations: Free listings and Shopping ads.
  • Name the feed (for example, "shopify feed") and click Create feed.

Setup with the Google Shopping feed app

Procedure: Symprosis setup, short version

  1. In Shopify Admin, go to Apps.
  2. Search for "Symprosis Google Shopping Feed".
  3. Click Install.
  4. Access the app from your Apps list.
  5. Follow the guided setup wizard.
  6. Connect your Google Merchant Center account (select the account from the previous step).
  7. The app automatically pulls in all Shopify products.
  8. Products initially show as Pending, which is normal.

Procedure: Symprosis setup, detailed version

  1. Create the Google Merchant Center account first, because the app's setup wizard links to an existing account.
  2. Install Symprosis Google Shopping Feed on the Shopify store. Search its name and click Install. It then appears in the store's Shopify apps.
  3. Open the app and follow its setup wizard.
  4. In the wizard, choose the Merchant Center account that was created. Use the Google login that is already active.
  5. Let the app pull in all products automatically.
  6. Check the Google product category of each product and correct any that are wrong.
  7. The category is set inside the product view of the app.
  8. Fill in custom attributes (extra product details) or detailed product characteristics only where needed.
  9. Detailed characteristics are mainly used for apparel (clothing).
  10. Leave custom labels (your own tags for grouping products) empty for now.
  11. Wait 2 to 4 days, up to about four, until every product changes from Pending to approved with a green check.
  12. Only after approval, move on to setting up Google Ads.

Setup with the Google Shopify app

  • Review the app's feed settings: the sync options (which products to sync) and the title and description formats.
  • Most stores can keep the defaults.
  • For products whose variants use different images, enable "Submit additional image". Variants are versions of one product, such as different sizes or colors.
  • Then click sync to generate the feed. Sync means copying the data so both sides match.
  • Clicking sync pulls all products from Shopify into the feed app.
  • For a small store this can take only moments. One example store had 27 products.
  • You can then edit products inside the app, including the Google product category and promotions.
  • After the app is connected, Google Merchant Center creates a feed named "Content API" automatically.
  • You do not need to add or edit this feed manually.
  • Once the feed app is connected, Merchant Center checks the domain (your website address) and shipping setup automatically.
  • Both should show as verified.
  • Run the product sync only after these checks pass.

Sync Frequency and Updates

3 min

Refresh intervals

Sources agree on a 24-hour cycle. Use the manual update button only when a change must go live immediately.

  • Google Merchant Center syncs products from Shopify once every 24 hours.
  • Pricing changes are also synced every 24 hours.
  • The Google Shopping feed refreshes automatically every 24 hours with current product information from the Shopify store.
  • Products sold through the feed app should update automatically every 24 hours.
  • So changes to descriptions or new products appear in Google within 24 hours.
  • In the feed app, set the update frequency (for example, every 24 hours).
  • Use the manual update button when you need changes live.
  • The refresh schedule controls how often products are resubmitted to Merchant Center, often every 24 hours.
  • Find it in the app's sync or Google Shopping settings and change it there.
  • Connect the product feed through an app that refreshes it every 24 hours.
  • Do not rely on manual Excel exports. A price change not updated in the feed causes mismatches.
  • In Google Merchant Center, set the product feed to auto-update, so price and stock changes on the store sync automatically.
  • Feeds connected through the Content API update automatically, so the last upload date stays current.
  • Use the Merchant Center feeds section to confirm updates are running.
  • Any edit made to a product inside the Symprosis app is automatically submitted to Google Merchant Center.
  • After changing categories or attributes, no manual upload is needed.
  • Manual Excel or spreadsheet product feeds are no longer needed for Merchant Center.
  • Use an automated feed solution, usually an app connected to the store.
  • Manually maintained feeds were a major problem. They often missed Google's requirements.
  • Price changes on the website were not reflected in the listings, because every update had to be copied into the feed by hand.
  • Do not upload product data to Merchant Center by hand through spreadsheets. Those files are never updated in real time and will drift from the store.
  • Connect the store through the API.
  • Connect the feed from Shopify to Merchant Center using the Feed for Google Shopping app, through the API connection.

Settings checklist for the Feed for Google Shopping app

  • Feed refreshes every 24 hours.
  • Brand field is mapped to the Shopify brand data.
  • Old or broken product URLs are removed.

Meta fields changed outside Shopify

  • The Feed for Google Shopping app does not pick up custom label changes made in Shopify automatically.
  • Its meta fields (extra product data fields) must be synced manually from Settings > Sync Settings From Shopify. Do this after any custom label update.
  • The same applies to other meta fields, such as SEO title, SEO description, age group and gender, when they are changed via CSV.

Timing after a fix

  • Shopify product data syncs to Merchant Center automatically every 24 hours.
  • Allow 24 to 48 hours for changes to go through.
  • After the first sync, Merchant Center can take up to 10 hours to show products for Google.
  • For Facebook, it can take up to 4 hours.
  • The product section shows "no data" during this time.
  • Wait until active items appear before setting up campaigns.

Standard flow

  • Under the standard setup, new products and changes to existing products flow automatically from Shopify to Google Merchant Center, and then to Google Ads.
  • These changes include price, description and variants.
  • If campaigns are split by brand or price, check that each product is present in the campaigns.
  • The feed decides the data Google reads for each product: price, photo, product link, description and title.
  • Google uses this data to understand what you sell and to show products in Shopping ads.
  • Shopping ad elements come from the Merchant Center feed, not from Google Ads.
  • These elements are the product image, title, price, sale price, compare-at price (the crossed-out original price), seller name, shipping cost and tax information.
  • Merchant Center data must be set up correctly before any Shopping campaign can work.

Barcodes and suspension risk

  • Products that have a barcode (UPC, GTIN or EAN, depending on the country) must include that barcode number in the product feed.
  • If the supplier provides no barcodes, the product does not need one and should not cause suspension.
  • Missing barcodes on products that have them can lead to product suspension (the product is blocked) or full account suspension.
  • If a product has a barcode and it is missing from the feed, Google may find it and suspend the product until the barcode is added.
  • Supplying barcodes where they exist tends to increase impressions (how often your ad is shown). It can also improve ranking.
  • Barcodes are registered in central databases that Google can query. Each barcode links to product information.
  • A valid barcode helps Google match the product more accurately. It also lets Google use the product's historical performance data (its past sales results).
  • UPC is the common term in the US. EAN is the term in Europe. GTIN is the general term.
  • All three describe the same barcode number.
  • Supply a barcode only if the product actually has one.
  • Never buy UPC codes.
  • If a product has no UPC or SKU, use the MPN identifier in Google Merchant Center.
  • **
  • If a supplier does not provide a barcode, a competitor's listing or an online barcode search can reveal the product's GTIN.
  • Use this as a fallback when the supplier does not respond.

Procedure: Fixing missing barcodes

  1. Check the Merchant Center product data diagnostics for missing or invalid GTIN issues.
  2. Contact the supplier. Ask whether they have barcode numbers for the products, and request them.
  3. Enter the barcode in Shopify and make sure the supplier information matches.
  4. Let the feed update Merchant Center automatically.

Custom products

  • Mark a product as custom only if it truly has no identifiers. Examples: a self-made product, a unique bundle, or a product you are the only seller of.
  • Marking a product as custom to avoid competition is not a valid reason.
  • Marking a product as custom while a barcode exists reduces its impressions.
  • Google then ranks it below competitors who supply identifiers, so it rarely helps.
  • Create product bundles (several items sold together as one) as custom products when you sell a combination no other seller offers.
  • This is the main valid use case for custom submission.
  • For a self-made bundle or custom product, Google does not require a barcode.
  • In the Shopping feed you can mark it as a custom product.
  • Bundles built from the same base product still compete for the same clicks. So the cost per click for the same search stays the same.
  • A product with no unique identifiers is submitted with identifier_exists set to FALSE. This is a yes/no field that tells Google the product has no unique code.
  • See the options table below.

Identifier Fallbacks and Errors

1 min

Resolving a GTIN (barcode number) error

1 min
  • A GTIN is a barcode number for a product (Global Trade Item Number).
  • If a product gets a GTIN error, enter the manufacturer part number (MPN) in the MPN column instead. The MPN is the code the maker gives the product.
  • MPN values are usually approved more easily than GTINs. **
  • If a product has no GTIN, use an MPN instead.
  • Google Merchant Center (Google's tool that receives your product list) can accept MPNs in place of GTINs. **
  • Use the MPN as the backup identifier when Merchant Center reports a missing GTIN error.
  • A product flagged for missing GTIN should be listed with an MPN instead.
  • Leave the GTIN field empty.
  • Google documentation explains the required fields when the MPN is flagged as not one of a kind (not unique).
  • To fix invalid GTIN errors in Merchant Center, correct the product page on Shopify (the online store software you use).
  • You can also disable GTIN in the product feed settings.
  • If Google says a GTIN has too few digits, add leading zeros in front of it. Leading zeros are zeros placed before the first digit.
  • Keep adding zeros until the GTIN reaches the full required length.
  • This is the fix Google itself suggests.
  • GTINs must be unique for each product. Unique means no other product uses the same number.
  • Do not use the same value for several products, such as the brand name.
  • Product variations that are multipacks of one item cannot share the same GTIN.
  • Give each variation a unique identifier.
  • Or sell the item only as bundles (sets sold together).

Procedure: Resolving Merchant Center GTIN errors

1 min
  1. Remove the barcode if it duplicates the SKU (the store's own product code, short for stock keeping unit). Keep only the SKU value.
  2. Resubmit the product feed (the product list you send to Google) and check whether the errors clear.
  3. If the SKU does not fix the error, use the MPN instead.
  • You do not need every product identifier.
  • Using the same value in several fields, such as SKU and barcode, can cause a duplicate-value error in Merchant Center.

Creating your own identifiers (MPN)

2 min
  • Create your own MPNs only for suppliers (the companies that make or ship your products) that lack SKUs, UPCs and MPNs.
  • You can also create them when private labeling. Private labeling means putting your own brand on a supplier's product.
  • A UPC is a 12-digit barcode number used in the US.
  • When private labeling, create your own identifier, such as a brand name plus a number.
  • Use it as the MPN instead of the supplier's SKU.
  • This stops Google from matching your products with the supplier's other listings. **
  • To make MPNs, make up a consistent code from the brand's name.
  • For example, use the brand initial, then the product letters, then a sequence number (a number that counts up: 1, 2, 3).
  • Use the same logic for every product. **
  • Use a consistent MPN format per supplier.
  • Take the supplier's product reference number or SKU.
  • Create a prefix (a short code placed in front) from the brand name, such as the first letters of the brand.
  • Join them with an underscore, as in FOV_12345.
  • Apply the same format to every product from that supplier. **
  • Around 99% of suppliers have no UPC. This is based on nearly 1,000 suppliers worked with. **
  • If a supplier does provide a UPC, GTIN or its own MPN, use the supplier's identifier instead.
  • When a product has no GTIN, use the MPN field in Google Merchant Center.
  • If the supplier has no MPN either, create one from the brand initials plus a sequence number.
  • For example: AP-1 and AP-2.
  • A custom identifier can be built from brand initials plus a product descriptor (a short word for the product).
  • For example: AS-blue-one for a blue surfboard.
  • This ensures uniqueness without relying on supplier identifiers. **
  • Use MPN (Manufacturer Part Number) as a unique product identifier when UPC numbers are unavailable or do not exist.
  • You may create MPN numbers if the supplier does not provide them. **
  • Never invent a SKU to fill the identifier field.
  • SKUs are specific to your own store. They are not universal product identifiers.
  • Use a UPC or MPN from the supplier instead.

SKU Rules

1 min
  • Do not invent SKUs.
  • If a brand's products already have SKUs and you sell under that brand, use the supplier's SKU. **
  • Keep SKUs in the product feed as best practice.
  • This is true even if they cause products to appear among competitors in Google Shopping (Google's product ads and price comparison area).
  • Removing them is not the recommended fix.
  • Product SKU numbers from the feed can match buyer searches.
  • So you compete with other sellers on those SKUs in the Google auction (the system where ads bid for each spot).
  • Treat SKUs as part of your competitive exposure. **
  • Listing a product's SKU and barcode in the same identifier fields can trigger duplicate-value errors.
  • See the duplicate-value rule above.
  • The Shopify SKU field (the product code box on a Shopify product page) is for your own management.
  • It has no effect on Google Shopping.
  • Google uses the MPN and UPC instead.
  • These can only be set through a shopping feed app (a tool that builds your product list) or a CSV upload (a spreadsheet file).
  • They cannot be set on the Shopify product page. **

Variants

1 min

A variant is another version of one product, such as another color or size.

  • Sync (copy the data across) all variants in the Shopping feed when variants differ in color or similar attributes.
  • Sync only the first variant when variants do not need separate listings. **
  • When each variant should appear as its own product in Google, set variant submission preference to all variants in the feed app.
  • Choose "first variants only" only if you want one listing per product.
  • Set variant submission preference to all variants if each variant should be its own listing.
  • Upload every product and every variant to Google Merchant Center as its own item.
  • Each variant can match a different specific search term, such as material or size. **
  • Product variants set up in Shopify, such as colors, are picked up by the feed if all variants are included.
  • Each variant can then appear for searches that include its attribute, such as a particular color.
  • For clothing with several sizes or colors, each variant is normally a separate row (one line in the list) in the Google feed.
  • Use the item group ID column (a code that ties variants of one product together).
  • This groups the variants under one product, so Google can show color filters on Google Shopping.

Google Product Category and Shopping Attributes

1 min

Setting the category

1 min
  • Set the Google product category (the type label Google files the product under) for every product in the feed.
  • A default can be set for the whole feed. A default is a setting that applies unless you change it.
  • Per-product assignment is better when the store carries several product types.
  • Google Product Category must be set manually for each product in the feed app.
  • Choose the most specific and closest match from Google's fixed category list, called the taxonomy.
  • Example: Hardware > Plumbing > Plumbing Fixtures > Shower Stalls & Kits.
  • The category also influences US tax calculation and campaign targeting (which searches and places your ads reach).
  • Set a default in the feed app settings.
  • Choose a top-level category (the broadest group) that matches your main niche (your product area).
  • For example, for an apparel (clothing) store, choose a top-level clothing category.
  • This default applies to products without a more specific category.
  • Product categories and other attributes (product details) can be set in bulk by category.
  • This saves time when a catalogue (your full product list) has many products.
  • You do not have to edit each product separately.
  • More product data, such as categories, age group and gender, helps Google match products to more relevant searches.
  • Filling in every detail is not necessary.
  • Titles, descriptions and the keywords shoppers type are the priority.
  • The Shopping tab lets shoppers filter results by price range, category, brand, type and capacity.
  • Which filters a product can appear in depends on the product data sent to Google.
  • So complete and accurate attributes matter. **

Custom attributes

1 min
  • The feed app's Custom Attributes section lets you enter Color, Size, Material, Pattern and COGS.
  • COGS means cost of goods sold, which is what you pay for the product.
  • These values are submitted to connected channels (sales platforms you link).
  • They do not write back to Shopify, so your Shopify store does not change.
  • They apply only when the same attribute is not already defined in Shopify.
  • For clothing, size and color must be entered in the feed.
  • Ads cannot run without them.
  • Standardized size values matter, because customers compare products on size.
  • For non-clothing products, the Size field means a size category such as small, medium or large, not dimensions.
  • Consistent size values help customers compare products.
  • This is true even though variant setups differ across stores.
  • Custom attributes are usually not required for high-ticket products (expensive items), unlike clothing.

Detailed product characteristics

1 min
  • Set Condition to New for all high-ticket dropshipping products.
  • Dropshipping means you sell the product, and the supplier ships it to the buyer.
  • Condition means whether the product is new or used.
  • Set Age Group to Adult unless the product is for children.
  • For children, use Kids, Infant, Toddler or Newborn as appropriate. **

Age, Gender and Adult Attributes

1 min
  • For most high-ticket products, set gender to unisex (for all genders), age group to adult and condition to new. **
  • For unisex products, where any age is not an option, select Adults in the age group setting.
  • Do this per product in the feed app.
  • Age group describes who the product is for.
  • The Adult-oriented checkbox is different. It is only for adult content.
  • Do not confuse the two. **
  • Set Gender only when the product is made specifically for one gender.
  • Examples: men's apparel, footwear, fashion items or gender-specific makeup.
  • For most products, including those used mainly by one gender, choose Unisex. **
  • Check the Adult-oriented box only when the product contains adult merchandise (adult products) or sexually explicit content.
  • Examples include nudity, sexually suggestive content, or products intended to enhance sexual activity. **

Custom Labels, Shipping Labels and Feed Modifiers

1 min

Feed modifiers are rules that change product data in the feed.

  • Ads Label and Ads Grouping have no real use in current Google Ads (Google's paid ad system). **
  • Custom Labels (tags that group products) structure your campaigns.
  • The Shipping Label links a product to a specific shipping rate in Merchant Center.
  • Google Shopping product tags can be generated by the Google Shopping feed app.
  • When using it, take tag problems to the app developer.
  • The Google Ads Shopping feed must include each product's price.
  • Google Ads does not require a separate price field for shipping costs.
  • This is when the cost is already included in the product price.

Procedure: Setting product-level shipping costs in Shopping feeds

1 min

**

  1. Create a shipping label in Merchant Center for each rate you charge. Example: a flat 20 USD rate (one fixed price).
  2. Enter that exact label in the Shipping Label field of each product that uses the rate.
  3. Confirm that the shipping rates charged on the site match the rates advertised in the feed.

Which Products to Include

1 min

Inclusion and exclusion

1 min
  • The feed app's product inclusions and exclusions let you choose which products are in or out.
  • You can select products under $200 and remove them from campaigns.
  • Removed products receive no spend (no ad money).
  • Setup takes about 10 to 15 minutes.
  • In Google Merchant Center, you can set the feed to sync only the products from a specific collection (a group of products).
  • Exclude unwanted categories, such as accessories, by manually removing them from your product feed or campaign product selection.
  • Do not use negative keywords for this. Negative keywords are search words that block ads.
  • Shopping campaigns do not respond to negative keywords the same way Search does. Search means Google's text ads on search results. **
  • Products must have a live product page (a page customers can visit now).
  • Products must also have a valid URL (web address).
  • Remove old or broken product URLs from the feed.

Products that must be in stock

1 min
  • Listing products you do not have in stock can lead to a suspended Google Merchant Center account. Suspended means blocked.
  • A student reported this after selling a product that was not in stock.
  • Keep product availability accurate before running Google Shopping.

Feed preparation before the first sync

1 min
  • Optimize the product titles in your Shopify store.
  • Titles should contain the core keywords and the real search terms customers use and convert with (turn into buyers).
  • Then set up Merchant Center.
  • Install a product feed app for Google Ads.
  • Connect the app to your store.
  • Let the app update the feed daily, so stock, prices, images and titles stay current.
  • Click sync to pull all products into the feed app.
  • Products can then be edited inside the app.
  • Enable "Submit additional image" for products whose variants use different images.
  • Then click sync to generate the feed.
  • Recheck the feed when identifiers are missing or products are flagged.
  • Do this instead of launching new feeds.
  • Google Shopping's standard reach (how many people see your ads) is only as good as the feed.
  • A product feed is the list of product data Google reads.
  • It contains the price, photo, product link, description and title for each product.
  • Fill in custom attributes or detailed product characteristics only where needed.
  • Most fields filled in when creating a product in Shopify are passed to the feed.
  • These include product type and brand.
  • For most stores, the feed needs checking rather than rebuilding.
  • A Google Shopping product feed is the structured product data you send to Google Merchant Center.
  • It is usually sent through a connector (a link between two tools) or an API (a way for programs to talk to each other).
  • Google uses it to fill Shopping ads.
  • Google also uses it to decide which search terms each product matches.
  • Google Shopping product approval can be affected by feed quality.
  • Check the feed when the review takes too long.

Ongoing product feed changes

1 min
  • Changes in product or shipping details in Merchant Center should be checked.
  • Confirm the feed is not duplicated.
  • Deleting and re-adding a feed can create a duplicate.
  • So confirm there is only one feed from the feed app.
  • A product ID is the unique code that names each product in the feed.
  • Changing product IDs after the feed has been approved makes products count as new.
  • Re-approval can take 3 to 5 days.
  • It also wipes the performance history of the feed.
  • Do not change the product ID format after approval.
  • Never change product IDs once ads are running.
  • Google treats a changed ID as a new product. The historical data for the old product is lost.
  • The only exception is when performance is very poor.

Verification and Approval

1 min
  • Verify that products are correctly submitted to Google Merchant Center.
  • Go to Merchant Center, then Settings > Data Sources.
  • Confirm products appear under "Provided by you".
  • The feed source should show as "Content API" (Google's channel for receiving product data), created automatically by Symprosis.
  • No products should appear under "Found by Google".
  • In Google Merchant Center, go to Settings > Data sources.
  • Look at the section "Provided by you".
  • It shows a Content API source with the store's products.
  • This is instead of the "Found by Google" products that appear with the built-in app.
  • For a US-based store, the Content API source under Settings > Data sources > Provided by you usually has the feed label US.
  • A feed label is a tag that names the country the feed is for.
  • The app sets this label automatically when it creates the feed during setup.
  • Wait 2 to 4 days, up to about four.
  • Wait until every product changes from Pending (waiting for review) to approved with a green check.
  • Only after approval, move on to setting up Google Ads.
  • The product section in Merchant Center can show "no data" for up to 10 hours after the first sync for Google.
  • For Facebook, it can take up to 4 hours.
  • Wait until active items appear before setting up campaigns.
  • Once the feed app is connected, Merchant Center checks the domain (your store's web address) and shipping setup automatically.
  • Both should show as verified.
  • Run the product sync only after these checks pass.

Feed Mistakes to Avoid

1 min
  • Do not run two feeds at once without a deliberate plan.
  • Example: the Shopify Google channel (Shopify's built-in Google sales link) and the Feed for Google Shopping app.
  • Do not submit demo or test products (examples, not real items) to a live Merchant Center account.
  • They create product warnings.
  • Do not activate Shopify's built-in Google sales channel for Shopping.
  • It duplicates the product feed if set live after the feed setup.
  • Do not check the box that submits products to Merchant Center through the Google sales channel.
  • Do not connect the Google & YouTube app to Merchant Center alongside the feed app.
  • Do not list products you do not have in stock.
  • Do not change product IDs after approval or once ads are running.
  • Do not mark a product as custom to avoid competition.
  • Do not mark a product as custom when a barcode exists.
  • Do not invent SKUs.
  • Do not use the Shopify SKU field expecting it to reach Google.
  • Do not rely on manual Excel or spreadsheet uploads for a growing store. Excel is a spreadsheet program.
  • Do not use the same GTIN for several products.
  • Do not use negative keywords to remove categories from Shopping campaigns.
  • Remove them from the feed or the campaign product selection instead.

Further details

1 min
  • To remove a brand entirely from Google Product Listing Ads (ads that show product photos and prices), use your Shopify-to-Google feed app.
  • Example apps: EasyTabs or similar.
  • Sort products by brand.
  • Uncheck all products from that brand.
  • Save settings.
  • Google Merchant Center will exclude them on the next feed pull (the next time it reads your product list).

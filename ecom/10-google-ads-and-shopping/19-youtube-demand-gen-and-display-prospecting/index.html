Playbook › 10. Google Ads and Shopping

YouTube, demand gen and display prospecting

Section 10.19 · 19 of 23 in this chapter · 26 min read

In short

  • Turn off "video partners" (other websites that show your video ad) for most campaigns. People often skip these ads, and you still pay.
  • Use YouTube ads mainly to reach people who already visited your store. Use them for new visitors only after your store already sells well.
  • Pay per view with Maximum CPV (you pay only when someone watches). Start at about 10 cents for retargeting and $1 for thank-you videos to buyers.
  • Limit how often one person sees an ad: 1 per day for retargeting, 2 per day for wide audiences, 1 per week for buyers.
  • Do not start with YouTube ads in a new store. Build sales first with search and shopping ads.
More key points
  • Keep the same image and headline when you switch a Display ad between a text link and a button, and only change the call to action (the words that tell people what to do).
  • Product showcase ads need uploaded images, and their description can be at most 120 characters.
  • Demand Gen image ads depend on the picture, so use the right image sizes for each placement.
  • Before you publish, check the conversion and impression estimates in the Channels panel to see where the ad will perform.
  • Judge video ads by cost per view, and flag anything above 0.05 USD.

Video campaign foundations and content controls

1 min
  • Turn off auto-generated video in the ad settings. Auto-generated means Google makes the video for you. Turning it off keeps full control over the quality and format of your ad.
  • Exclude these content groups from the campaign: content not yet labeled (content Google has not rated), all sensitive content categories, and live streaming videos. Exclude means block your ad from showing there.
  • Excluded content options include embedded videos (videos placed inside other websites), live streaming videos, games, and content not yet labeled or aimed at mature audiences. Excluding these usually costs little reach, because the allowed inventory types (kinds of video space where ads can appear) already cover much of it.
  • Google recommends the standard setting for inventory type. It blocks profanity (swear words), sexual content and violence. The alternative, Limited inventory, cuts the available space by about 20% and also blocks videos with repeated strong profanity, strong sexual content and violence. You can change this later.
  • Set the campaign goal to the option that gives full control, not the smart goal recommendations (Google's suggested goals). A goal is the result the campaign aims for. The goal list includes Sales, Leads (people giving you their contact details), Website traffic, Product and brand consideration, Brand awareness and reach (how many people see your name), and App promotion. You can also create a campaign without a goal.
  • Use a clear naming system. For example, name the campaign by funnel stage (the step a visitor is at on the way to buying) and audience, such as bottom-of-funnel (closest to buying) sales video retargeting. Retargeting means showing ads again to people who already visited.

Network and placement settings

2 min
  • Turn off 'Video partners on the display network' in video campaign settings. Video partners are outside websites and apps that show Google video ads. These ads are non-skippable (viewers cannot skip them) and play on random sites. They waste your budget (the money you set to spend) because people don't watch and don't buy.
  • Start the first YouTube campaign with wide network reach: YouTube search results, videos, channel pages (a channel's own page), the homepage and Display Network video partners. The Display Network is Google's group of websites and apps that show ads. Then measure which placements (where ads show) perform, and narrow down based on performance.
  • Campaign build step 3 for a YouTube retargeting campaign lists the network options as YouTube search results, YouTube videos, channel pages, homepage, and Display Network video partners, with languages and countries matching the store.
  • Conflict between sources: and the remarketing settings below say to turn video partners off, while and include them for the first wide-reach campaign. Apply the off setting to remarketing, dynamic and promotion campaigns. Use the wide-reach setting only for the first test campaign that is meant to find where the ads perform.
  • If a site shows Google-served ads in its sidebar (side column) or footer (bottom of the page) and offers no direct ad deal, you can still test ads there. Run Google display ads that appear only on that website. Display ads are picture ads on websites.
  • Ad schedule (the times of day your ads run): start with all 24 hours on every day to cast a wide net (reach as many people as possible). Narrow it later based on performance data.
  • Devices (phones, computers, tablets, TVs): start with all devices for the first campaign, since it is unknown which device or network performs best. Then observe results and apply bid modifiers (extra price adjustments per device) afterwards.
  • Delivery method: use Standard, not Accelerated, which is almost always the right choice. Standard spreads the budget across the day. Accelerated spends it as fast as possible.
  • Set start and end dates for the campaign. With a limited budget, an end date can be added to cap spend (limit how much you spend).
  • Popular video adjustment (raising bids on particularly popular videos) is usually left off, because the goal is a wide net at a controlled cost.
  • Demographic data (age, gender, income and similar group facts) in Google Ads for YouTube often shows many viewers as unknown, since Google does not always know age or income. Review demographic information later in the dimensions report (a table that splits results into groups).

Video remarketing campaigns

1 min

Dynamic Video Remarketing (setup sequence)

1 min

Dynamic Video Remarketing shows past visitors ads for the products they already looked at.

  1. Set the campaign type to 'Custom video campaign'.
  2. Set the bid strategy (how you set the price you pay) to 'Maximum CPV' (cost per view: you pay when someone watches).
  3. Turn off 'Video partners on display network'.
  4. Link the Merchant Center feed (your product list, sent from Google's Merchant Center tool) for dynamic product ads (ads that show your own products).
  5. Select the skippable in-stream format (a video ad viewers can skip after a few seconds, played inside another video).
  6. Target 30-day website visitors (people who visited your site in the last 30 days).
  7. Turn off 'audience expansion' (Google adding similar people on its own).
  8. Exclude recent customers (people who already bought).
  9. Use the video creator tool with preset templates (ready-made designs): 3 images + logo + text.

Retargeting structure

1 min
  • Video - WARM - Website Visitors adds another touchpoint (a place where your ad meets a person) to remarketing. WARM means people who already know your store. This reduces ad fatigue (people getting tired of seeing the same ad) from display, and reaches visitors on the video placements where they spend time.
  • Discovery - WARM - Monthly Promotions uses ads similar to the Google Display Network, but with different aspect ratios (the shape of the picture: width to height). It raises awareness of limited-time offers among warm audiences.
  • Video - WARM - Monthly Promotions uses video ads to raise awareness of limited-time offers among warm audiences.
  • Video - PAST CUSTOMERS - Goodwill targets people who have already bought. Its purpose is to reduce buyer's remorse (doubt after buying).
  • The audience (the group of people your ad targets) sits at the ad group level (a set of ads that share one audience and one bid).

Build order for a YouTube retargeting campaign

1 min
  1. Create the video campaign with a clear name.
  2. Set the start and end dates.
  3. Choose network options, languages and countries matching the store (see Network and placement settings).
  4. Set the bid strategy to Maximum CPV (the most you will pay per view).
  5. Set inventory type and content exclusions.
  6. Set devices, frequency cap (1 per day) (a limit on how many times one person sees the ad) and ad schedule (24 hours).
  7. Create the ad group (a set of ads that share one audience and one bid).
  8. Set the audience, max CPV bid (the highest price you pay per view), and optional popular-video adjustment (usually left off).
  9. Choose the video and format: in-stream (plays before or during another video) or discovery (shown as a suggested video).
  10. Set the destination URL (the page people land on after clicking) and companion banner (a small picture next to the video; auto-generate if available).

Example budgets and targets

1 min
  • A Google Ads Demand Gen remarketing campaign (Google's campaign type that reaches people across its video and feed platforms) was launched with a starting budget of $10/day and a target of more than 10x ROAS (return on ad spend: money earned for each dollar spent).
  • A YouTube remarketing campaign on Google Ads was launched with a starting budget of $10/day and a target of more than 10x ROAS.
  • Bottom-of-funnel sales video retargeting example: campaign budget set to 3x KPI budget (KPI means key performance indicator, the target number you track), at 10 per day.

Introduction video as YouTube ad

1 min
  • If the business owner has a YouTube channel they want to grow, the introduction video can also run as a YouTube ad. The ad is built like a Facebook video ad and needs a link to the target page.

Goodwill and post-purchase video

1 min

A goodwill video is a thank-you video sent to buyers after they purchase.

Goodwill video procedure

1 min
  1. Create or film a 1-minute personal thank-you video in selfie style.
  2. Include your name, thank you for the order, contact information, tracking timeline (the delivery progress steps), and appreciation for customer feedback, with no sales pitch.
  3. Set campaign to 'Custom video campaign' > bid strategy 'Maximum CPV' > audience 'Purchased 7 days' (people who bought in the last 7 days) > frequency cap '1 view per week' (a limit on how often one person sees it) > use budget of $5-10 per day.
  4. People will only see it once after buying, during their first week.

Rules and settings

1 min
  • Do not ask for reviews in goodwill videos. Send review requests in follow-up emails later, not immediately after purchase when the product has not yet been delivered.
  • For goodwill post-purchase video campaigns, set the maximum CPV bid to $1. Actual spend will be much lower, typically cents per view, because recent customers are warm, high-intent audience members (people who want to buy and already know you).
  • Maximum CPV for goodwill versus retargeting: goodwill campaigns start at a $1 maximum CPV bid, while general YouTube retargeting ad groups start at about 10 cents (see Bidding, frequency caps and budgets).

Monthly promotion video and Discovery

1 min

A monthly promotion is a short sale or offer, for example October 29-31.

Video Monthly Promotion campaign

1 min
  1. Choose dates (e.g., October 29-31).
  2. Audience: 180-day website visitors (people who visited your site in the last 180 days).
  3. Bid strategy: 'Maximum CPV'.
  4. Set frequency cap to 2 impressions per day (an impression is one time an ad is shown).
  5. Turn off 'Video partners on display network'.
  6. Turn off 'audience expansion'.
  7. Create the video using the Google video creator template.
  8. Use the /promotions URL (the web address of the sale page).
  9. Call-to-action (the button text viewers see): 'save now'.
  10. Exclude recent customers.

Discovery campaign for monthly promotions

1 min
  1. Campaign type: 'Discovery' (ads shown as suggested videos and in search results).
  2. Choose start and end dates for the promotion.
  3. Audience: 180-day website visitors.
  4. Turn off 'optimized targeting' (Google picking extra audiences on its own).
  5. URL: /promotions.
  6. Static image ads only (still pictures; aspect ratio adjusted from Display ads).
  7. Headlines with the promotion name (e.g., 'Halloween sale').
  8. Description with offer details.
  9. Exclude recent customers.

Promotion reach

1 min
  • Run the monthly promotion across display remarketing, YouTube, Discovery, Facebook, Instagram and email, so the offer appears everywhere the warm audiences are.

Discovery ads and video formats

1 min
  • Discovery ads appear as suggested videos in the sidebar, on the YouTube homepage, or in the search results for a keyword (a word people type into search). They are shown while someone is watching other content or searching.
  • Video discovery ad setup: the thumbnail (preview image) and headline appear in the sidebar and search results. Use a strong headline, since a good headline drives click-through (how often people click). The destination can be the standard video watch page. A descriptive ad name is recommended.
  • For discovery in Google Ads, choose the video to promote. Any YouTube video URL can be entered, including unlisted ones (videos that are not public and only open by link). Then choose the format: in-stream (pre-roll, which plays before a video) or video discovery. Plan to run both formats, and name each ad group by its format, e.g. in-stream.
  • Destination URL for YouTube ads: the YouTube channel, the store website, or a specific product page.
  • Companion banner (a small picture shown next to or below the video): auto-generate if none is provided. It appears next to or below the video during playback.
  • Create a 'Custom video campaign' with the Maximum CPV strategy, a 1-view-per-day frequency cap, and the skippable in-stream format for dynamic video remarketing.

Bidding, frequency caps and budgets

1 min

Bid strategy

1 min
  • Set the bid strategy to Maximum CPV (cost per view), not Maximum CPM (cost per thousand impressions), because you want to pay when a viewer actually watches the ad.
  • Starting max CPV bid for YouTube retargeting ad groups: about 10 cents, then increase gradually to see whether the ad gets traction (people responding). Views on YouTube are generally less valuable than clicks to the website, so do not start high.
  • Goodwill and post-purchase video campaigns start at a maximum CPV of $1 (see Goodwill and post-purchase video).

Frequency caps

1 min

A frequency cap limits how many times one person sees your ad.

Campaign type Frequency cap Source
Large audience video and display campaigns 2 impressions per day, to avoid repeatedly showing the same people the same ad and to control spend
Video Monthly Promotion 2 impressions per day
YouTube retargeting 1 impression per day per campaign, so viewers stay aware of the brand without seeing the ad four or five times a day
Goodwill (purchased 7 days) 1 view per week
  • Frequency caps for large audiences and for retargeting differ. Use 2 per day for broad video and display audiences, 1 per day for retargeting, and 1 per week for goodwill.

Budget reference points

1 min
  • $5-10 per day for goodwill video.
  • $10 per day starting budgets for Demand Gen and YouTube remarketing with a more than 10x ROAS target.

Audience and placement targeting in Google Ads

1 min

Audience and interest visibility

1 min
  • To see the profile Google has built about you, search for Google Ad Settings or open adsettings.google.com. It shows the inferred demographics (guessed age, gender and similar facts) and interests that Google uses for ad personalization (ads tailored to you) and targeting.
  • Google builds a user's interest and demographic profile from their search behavior and from the websites they visit that run Google Analytics (Google's free website tracking tool). Users never enter these details themselves, so the profile reflects what they actually search for and read.
  • Google holds demographic and interest data that is at least as detailed as Meta's (the company behind Facebook and Instagram), and in some respects more granular (more detailed). Its demographic targeting is therefore a strong tool, not just a secondary option.

Targeting methods

1 min
  • Demographic targeting can select audiences by gender, age range, parental status, homeownership and household income. Interest targeting uses browsing history and search behavior to reach people with matching interests.
  • Topic targeting selects content categories, such as health and wellness or swimming pools and spas, as well as specific websites and apps. Google's reach estimate for a topic can run into millions of impressions, so the potential audience is large.
  • Life events targeting reaches people at moments such as moving, getting married, birthdays or college graduation. Custom intent targeting shows display ads on sites to people who have been searching and researching a product category, which makes it an intent-based audience (people showing they want something).
  • Placements let you choose specific websites where your ad appears, for example sites your customers already read. This works as a targeting method alongside search.
  • Demographic segments (groups of people sharing traits) can receive different ads rather than one message for everyone. Examples include households in the top versus bottom income bracket, or senior citizens versus people under 30 who are actively searching.

Launch order

1 min
  1. Start with the targeting methods that convert best (turn visitors into buyers) most often, then move up the funnel to methods that reach earlier-stage audiences.
  2. Do not activate all methods at once.

Finding placements and interest ideas for YouTube

1 min
  1. Browse YouTube channels in the category (e.g. furniture) and check whether their audience matches your customers.
  2. In Google Analytics, use the Interests report (Overview and Affinity categories, which are broad groups of interests) to find audience categories, then search for them in Google Ads.
  3. Use placements only when the audience is large enough. For smaller audiences, do not narrow the targeting too much.

Competitor and top-of-funnel YouTube

1 min
  • YouTube allows targeting competitor audiences by selecting specific channels or videos (e.g. a competitor channel), so that a discovery or in-stream ad appears as a suggested video next to that content. Facebook restricts such page-level targeting, while YouTube does not restrict it in the same way.
  • On YouTube you can target specific channels and specific videos, including videos made by competitors. This lets you reach an audience that already watches content close to your offer.
  • Use YouTube targeting to reach viewers of specific competitor channels or videos only when that content is relevant to your buyers. The goal is to draw part of that audience's attention toward your offer.

Top-of-funnel campaign for competitor fans

1 min
  • Principle: copy the retargeting campaign and modify it. Exclude the website visitors audience (so only new people are reached). Target competitor channels or videos under placements (e.g. competitor YouTube channel), so the ad appears as a suggested video next to their content. Pause the retargeting ad groups if they are not needed.
  • The first touch (the first time someone meets your brand) should use a top-of-funnel video and offer. Users then flow into retargeting campaigns. The purpose of this campaign is to build a list of people to retarget later, so track cost per view against how many enter the funnel.

Build procedure

1 min
  1. Copy the existing campaign and paste it.
  2. Rename it to "top of funnel views".
  3. Replace the visitors-who-haven't-converted audience (people who visited but did not buy yet) with a competitor-channel audience, by adding placements with YouTube channels or videos.
  4. Keep the website visitors audience as an exclusion.
  5. Repeat the same changes for the discovery ad group.
  6. Pick the funnel offer.
  7. Measure cost per view to see whether it pushes visitors into the funnel.

When YouTube makes sense

1 min
  • YouTube ads are not a starting point for a new store. They suit sellers who are already doing well, want to grow further, and are comfortable with video.
  • YouTube ads are not needed early on, because they take considerable effort to get right and easier gains exist elsewhere. Consider them only when a store already does multiple millions of dollars in sales per year and needs more growth.
  • Invest in Google Ads (search, shopping (product ads in Google), Performance Max (Google's automated campaign type across its channels)) for strong returns over 2-3 years. Reserve YouTube ads for retargeting only, because YouTube reaches video viewers rather than search-intent customers (people already searching for a product).
  • The sources differ on cold use. Cold means people who have never seen your store. The retargeting-only rule applies by default. The cold top-of-funnel build applies only once the store has passed the starting stage described above.
  • YouTube ads can serve two purposes: warming up past visitors to raise conversion rate (how many visitors buy), which is retargeting, and introducing the brand to cold users who search for the product but have not seen the store yet. The same setup works for both audiences.
  • YouTube can create ad tools through Google Ads, and a Google support document covers the setup process. It is a separate ad format to consider alongside Shopping and Search campaigns.
  • YouTube ads can target any channel regardless of its size, unlike Facebook, where targeting is limited to certain page types.
  • A YouTube channel that drives zero direct-attributed conversions (sales Google credits straight to it) may still deliver value through brand awareness (people knowing your name), audience building for retargeting, and pixel/cookie placement (a tiny tracking code that remembers visitors). Measure its indirect impact using assisted conversions (sales where the ad helped earlier but was not the last click) in Google Ads.

How a view is counted and billed

1 min
  • The sources describe the counted view in two ways. Under the first, a view counts when the viewer watches 15 seconds, or the whole video if it is shorter. These views are relatively cheap.
  • Under the second, a view counts when the viewer watches a significant portion of the ad or clicks it. Skipping before that point is not billed.
  • On YouTube you pay per view, and a view only counts when the viewer chooses to watch a significant portion of the ad. Facebook can count a mere impression as a view. Skipping an in-stream ad costs nothing.

Format definitions

1 min
  • In-stream ads (pre-roll, which play before the video) are charged only when a viewer watches a significant part of the ad or clicks it.
  • Discovery ads appear as suggested videos in the sidebar, on the YouTube homepage, or in the search results for a keyword.

Display Network: reach and when prospecting makes sense

1 min

Prospecting means reaching people who have never seen your store.

Reach figures (older, 2021-2022 source; verify current values)

1 min
  • The Google Display Network (Google's group of websites and apps that show ads) can reach about 97% of internet users. This is a reach figure for the whole network. It does not mean those users are relevant buyers for a given product.
  • At the time of the source, Google's own reach figure for US internet users on the Display Network was 92%, across millions of sites, videos, and devices. The source said the figure was higher by then. Treat it as a dated figure for the US, and check the current value in Google's tools before using it.
  • Around 2021 to 2022, websites monetized (earning money from ads) through the AdSense publisher network (sites that show Google ads for money) had about 204 million visitors per month combined in the US. This is a dated US figure and should be verified before use.
  • At the time of the source, YouTube had over 1 billion video views per day, and Google properties (Google-owned sites and apps) accounted for about 5% of all online time spent. These are dated global figures that show the scale of Google-owned placements.
  • The DoubleClick Ad Exchange (an online marketplace where ad space is bought and sold) connects hundreds of premium content publishers that offer hundreds of millions of ad placements every day, according to a 2021 to 2022 figure that should be verified before use. This exchange is one of the three groups of properties that make up the Display Network.
  • YouTube was described as the second largest search engine in the world around 2021 to 2022, with over 1 billion video views per day. Video placements on YouTube are therefore a large Display Network inventory (available ad space). The figures are dated, so check them in current Google sources.

Where display ads run

1 min
  • Display ads (picture ads on websites) run on third-party websites and apps monetized through the AdSense publisher network or the DoubleClick Ad Exchange. They also run on Google-owned properties such as YouTube, Gmail, Google Maps, Blogger, and Google Finance. They do not appear in the main Google search results.
  • Search network ads (ads shown in Google search results) reach people who are actively looking for something, so their intent is visible from the search terms. Display ads interrupt people while they browse other content, similar to Facebook ads, so their intent is much weaker.
  • The Display Network contains a large share of irrelevant traffic (visitors who will not buy). The source estimates that roughly 99% of available impressions will not match your market. Relevant audiences can still be reached, but only through careful targeting.

When to use display

1 min
  • For a high-ticket dropshipping store (a store selling expensive products, where a supplier ships the orders), the Display Network is best used for retargeting ads (static and dynamic) and targeted lead generation ads (ads that collect contact details). Using it for cold prospecting is an advanced strategy.
  • Display prospecting should only start once the business is already profitable from other traffic sources, such as Google Shopping, search text ads, and organic search (free results, not paid ads). Retargeting can and should run from the start.
  • Cold display audiences are usually far from the bottom of the sales funnel. A direct offer for an expensive product, such as a $4,000 item, rarely converts from a first impression. People need time and a reason to trust before they buy.
  • Cold display traffic has a longer gap between the first click and the purchase, so ad spend must continue for a long time before returns come in. Only businesses with cash to keep investing should attempt this. Businesses running on thin margins (little profit per sale) should first improve the sources they already have.
  • A lead generation approach to cold display traffic depends on having useful content on the website to send visitors to. Without content pages, the funnel has nowhere to move the visitor next.

Cold display procedure for a high-ticket store

1 min
  1. Run a lead generation ad that sends cold visitors to a content page or lead page on your website.
  2. Retarget those visitors with display ads (static and dynamic) for product pages, content pages, and collection pages.
  3. Get visitors to opt into your email list (sign up to receive emails) and deliver value through content before making a sales pitch for an expensive product.

Mistakes to avoid

1 min
  • Display ads from large brands for expensive products, such as car makers in major newspapers, are usually branding campaigns (ads that build name recognition) with budgets in the millions and no direct tracking. A small store cannot copy this approach. Its own display plan should be built around direct response (ads that ask for an immediate action) and sales within a reasonable time frame.
  • Before copying a competitor's ad strategy, check how much money it can spend and what kind of company it is. Results that work for a different business model or budget are not a reliable guide for your store.
  • One advertiser can fill several ad slots on the same large news page at once, which signals a very large budget. A small store cannot copy this kind of placement. It should judge display placements by the advertiser's size and goal before treating them as a model.

Sidebar and footer testing

1 min
  • If a site shows Google-served ads in its sidebar (side column) or footer (bottom of the page) and offers no direct ad deal, you can still test ads there by running Google display ads that appear only on that website.

Display ad formats and CTA

1 min
  • On the Google Display Network (Google's network of third-party websites and apps that show ads), the same ad can use a plain text-link CTA (call to action, the words that tell people what to do) or a button-style CTA. Keep the same image and headline when you switch between the two styles.

Shopping showcase ads

1 min
  • Showcase ads are Shopping ads (product ads shown in Google search) that show searchers a swipeable carousel (a row of slides they swipe through) of brands and products. Tapping leads to a brand store-style page, then to single products. A very high CTR (click-through rate, the share of people who see an ad and click it) has been reported. The first click is free. Verify that billing claim in current Google Ads terms before relying on it.
  • Product showcase ads require uploaded images: a logo, a header image (the wide banner at the top), and a lifestyle photo (a shot of a person using or wearing the product, such as a model outdoors). The lifestyle photo works best as the top photo of the set. Product images can be added as well.
  • The product showcase description is limited to 120 characters (one letter, digit or space each).

Create a product showcase ad

1 min
  1. Create a new campaign (a container for ads with one goal) and choose the goal Shopping.
  2. Select your merchant feed (the product list you send to Google) and go to the next screen.
  3. Enter a campaign name and click Save and continue.
  4. On the showcase format screen, choose to show a selection of products and put your top products first.
  5. Upload the header image, the logo and, if used, the product images.
  6. Enter the display URLs (the web addresses the ad leads to) the ad should lead to.
  7. Write a headline and a description of up to 120 characters.

Demand Gen

1 min

Why Demand Gen matters

1 min
  • Demand Gen (Google's campaign type for visual ads) matters because a Google Shopping listing shows only an image, a title and a price. The image is one of the main reasons a shopper clicks. Demand Gen native image ads (ads that blend into the look of the app around them) run in browsing places such as YouTube in-feed (ads shown alongside other videos) and Google Discover, where images also drive the ad.
  • Brands scaling through Shopping and Demand Gen (growing fast by raising spend) spend about 10,000 to 40,000 US dollars per day across both channels, according to the figures given here. Demand Gen image placements (the spots where the ad appears) are described as one of the most under-used scaling options.

Assets

1 min
  • A Demand Gen ad can use up to 20 images, up to 5 headlines and up to 5 descriptions. Assets (the separate images and text pieces) are combined by Google into different ad combinations, so each asset should work with any other asset.

Image sizes

1 min

Aspect ratio is the shape of the image, written as width to height. Pixel (px) size is the number of dots that make up the image.

Format Aspect ratio Pixel size Best for
Landscape 1.91:1 1200 x 628 px YouTube in-feed
Square 1:1 1200 x 1200 px Works across both placements
Portrait 4:5 960 x 1200 px Discover

Channels

1 min
  • To choose where a Demand Gen ad runs, open the ad group settings (the settings for one set of ads inside a campaign) and go to Channels. Select All Google channels to run everywhere eligible, or select Let me choose and tick the channels to use, such as YouTube in-stream (ads that play inside a video), YouTube Shorts, Discover, Gmail, Google Display Network or Maps.
  • YouTube in-stream and YouTube Shorts run on YouTube surfaces (places on YouTube). Discover shows ads in personalized content feeds (feeds tailored to each user) on Google mobile apps. Gmail shows ads inside users' Gmail inboxes. Google Display Network runs ads on third-party sites and apps and may modify the ad to fit each publisher's (site owner's) format. Maps shows ads on Google Maps and is labelled NEW in the interface.
  • Before saving a Demand Gen ad group, the Channels panel shows an estimate of conversions (completed goals, such as sales) and impressions (how many times the ad is shown) for the channels currently selected, plus the total available ad group impressions. Check these estimates while ticking channels. This helps you compare which placements will bring volume before you publish.

Ad group page

1 min
  • Above the Channels section, the Demand Gen ad group page has Ad group name, Locations and Languages fields. The example uses United States (country) and All languages.
  • The page is saved with the buttons Save all changes, Duplicate ad group and Discard all changes at the bottom.

Pinterest and secondary formats

1 min
  • Pinterest promoted pins (paid posts that look like normal pins) were in beta (an early test version) when this was written. They were billed on a cost-per-click basis (CPC, you pay for each click). You only pay when a user clicks through from the pin to your website.
  • Pinterest ad targeting uses search keywords, which are displayed as separate keyword segments in search results, plus location, language, device (phone, computer and so on) and gender. Choose the keywords that match what users search for, then select the audience filters before paying.
  • Promoted pins often earned free repins (shares by other users) more easily than they earned paid clicks back to the store in the tests described here. Repins still spread the content and can later lead to purchases or viral reach (sudden, wide sharing).
  • Pinterest promoted pins are new, so there is little public data (shared results) on the best way to use them. Traffic from them tends to be browsing traffic (people looking, not yet buying). The store needs a strong funnel (the path from first visit to sale), with retargeting (showing ads again to people who already visited) and an email opt-in (a sign-up for emails) to capture it.

Measurement across channels

1 min
  • Switching on a Google Ads search campaign raised overall website traffic, even though the campaign running at that time was a YouTube video campaign that sent no visitors directly to the site. Track total site traffic alongside per-campaign clicks to see this kind of cross-channel lift (gain in one channel caused by another).
  • Targeting an interest category with a highly relevant video ad can produce very cheap views, because relevance drives down cost per view (CPV, what you pay each time someone watches). Relevance of message, audience and timing matters more than budget size for this effect.
  • Brand recognition built up by earlier video ads raises the CTR of later search ads, because searchers already recognize the brand. Plan search ads as a follow-up to awareness campaigns (campaigns that build brand familiarity), not only as standalone direct-response buys (ads meant to get a sale right away).

Campaign- and ad-group-level video cost check

1 min
  1. Open Supermetrics (a tool that pulls ad data into a spreadsheet) and choose the last 30 days.
  2. Add cost, clicks and cost per view.
  3. Split the rows by campaign name and ad group (a set of ads inside one campaign).
  4. Apply the cost-per-view KPI (key performance indicator, the target number you track) of 0.05 USD and conditional formatting (rules that color cells automatically) that turns cells yellow when they are greater than 0.05.
  5. Drag the formula (the calculation) down for all campaigns and check for flagged rows (rows marked by the yellow color).
  • In a YouTube awareness campaign, the ad group for retargeting pre-roll (video ads shown before another video) and awareness showed a cost per view above the 0.05 USD threshold. Breaking it down by video title identified the specific video and audience combinations that were not performing, which pointed to exactly which ads to change.

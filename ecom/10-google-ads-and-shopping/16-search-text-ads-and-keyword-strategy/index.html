Playbook › 10. Google Ads and Shopping

Search text ads and keyword strategy

Section 10.16 · 16 of 23 in this chapter · 52 min read

In short

  • Start with Shopping ads (Google product ads with photos and prices). Add search text ads later, only after Shopping makes a profit.
  • Choose search words that already sell your products. Do not choose them by search numbers alone.
  • Use exact, phrase or broad match modifier. Avoid plain broad match, because it wastes money.
  • Until Shopping is profitable, check the account at least once a day.
  • Do not turn off campaigns that are profitable.
More key points
  • Build one separate search campaign for each top product, each with its own budget and bid.
  • Turn off "Search network", "Display network" and "Include Google search partners", so your text ads show only on Google search.
  • Start with Manual CPC and a bid of at least $0.50 per click. Check your bids after 1-2 days.
  • Test one change at a time, for about 2 weeks, and write down every result.
  • Start small, on keywords that already sold in Shopping. Do not start search ads for a product until it sells well.
  • Search text ads are plain text ads on Google's search results. They can make sales fast, but only with tight keywords.
  • Start with exact match keywords, the exact words that already made sales in Shopping.
  • Start each keyword at about $2 per click, taken from your Shopping average.
  • Use the search terms report to find and remove words that get clicks but no sales.

Funnel Position and Intent

2 min
  • Match your search text ads to where the buyer is. Search text ads are short text ads shown in Google search results. The funnel is the path from first interest to purchase. The top of the funnel is early research. The bottom is ready to buy.
  • Search text ads work best on search terms with mid-to-bottom funnel intent. Intent means how strongly the person wants to buy. These searches point to one collection page (a store page with a group of products) or one product page. Rate these keywords as low, medium or high value, the same way as for Shopping ads (product ads with photos and prices). **
  • Broad, top-of-funnel search terms waste budget when these visitors convert poorly or slowly. Convert means make a purchase. The account can run at a loss for a long time with no return inside the planned timeframe. **
  • Low-value or top-of-funnel search terms can be targeted with search text ads only as an advanced technique. Use this only once the account is already profitable. Profitable means earning more than you spend. **
  • More specific keywords (the words a person types into Google) produce more specific clicks. They also bring a higher conversion rate, meaning the share of visitors who buy. **
  • Broad or generic keywords bring visitors who are not yet ready to buy the product.
  • Search targeting means bidding on core commercial keywords. Bidding means offering a price per click. Core commercial keywords are often called money keywords. They are the terms that generate sales. Search targeting is the first targeting method to prioritize.
  • Search ads aimed at research searches, such as "buyer's guide" plus a product category, can put your brand in front of shoppers who are still researching. Brand visibility in these searches can raise the click-through rate (the share of people who see an ad and click it) of the same brand's later ads. Include such queries in the search plan.
  • Buyer-intent keywords (words that show a person is ready to buy) can be found in the search terms of a live campaign. A campaign is a group of ads with its own budget. Impressions means how many times your ad is shown. Start a new store with one alpha and one beta campaign (the two starting test campaigns). Review which keywords bring impressions, clicks and sales. Then push more budget to those keywords.

Role and Placement Alongside Shopping

2 min
  • In a search campaign, you type in the keywords yourself. You also choose a match type. Match types are the rules for how closely a search must match your keyword: broad (wide), phrase (words in order, with other words allowed around them) or exact (only that wording). This makes it easy to get a lot of traffic (visits) that rarely converts.
  • Shopping campaigns remain the main recommendation. Search campaigns are a secondary source.
  • Search campaigns can reach shoppers who never open the Shopping tab. This adds reach, meaning more people seen.
  • Add search campaigns only once Shopping campaigns work and the store gets real traffic.
  • A store can receive traffic from three sources at once.
  • Source 1: organic Google results (free listings that show without paying for clicks).
  • Source 2: Shopping campaigns.
  • Source 3: small search campaigns for best-selling products.
  • Add a branded search campaign (ads for searches using your store's own name) on top, once the store is profitable.
  • Search text ads appear on the Google results page for a keyword. Usually up to three ads appear, and sometimes four. They show above the organic listings. A small block can also appear below the organic results. **
  • The top of the page is the most valuable spot. **
  • Running Shopping ads, search text ads and organic listings together can give a store up to three visible spots on the same results page. **
  • Seeing the brand several times raises the chance that a shopper clicks one of its listings. **
  • Many shoppers avoid clicking ads. Plenty of others do click them. **
  • Search text ads therefore still capture a real share of clicks, even though some users ignore them. **
  • High-ticket dropshipping means selling expensive items that a supplier ships straight to the buyer. For it, search text ads are rarely the main ad format. **
  • Shopping ads usually deliver better returns. Search text ads still have a place alongside them. **
  • In markets with lower Shopping ad usage, such as Australia and the UK, search text ads can outperform Shopping ads for some stores. **
  • Even there, Shopping ads tend to be the stronger performer overall. **
  • Ad stacking means showing several ads for the same product. Use it on your best-selling items for maximum exposure on the search results page.
  • Once one product sells consistently, show it in as many places as possible. **
  • That includes an exact-match search text ad with the product name. The ad then shows only for that exact wording. It can dominate, meaning take most of, the first page for that name. **
  • A strong keyword can also get its own search text campaign, as an exact match. It sends traffic to the same product page that already converts.
  • This is an extra channel on top of the Shopping campaign. It is not a replacement for Shopping.

When to Start Search Text Ads

3 min
  • Google Shopping is the first campaign type to start with. It should take most of the daily ad budget at the beginning. **
  • Search text ads come later, after Shopping campaigns are profitable. **
  • Do not start with Google search text ads. **
  • They become worth considering only once products are proven and the store is highly profitable. **
  • Traditional Google search text ads should not be started until Shopping campaigns are already highly successful. **
  • Search text ads should not be started until Shopping is already profitable. (2023)
  • Do not turn off campaigns that are profitable. **
  • Search text ads should not be used until all other campaigns are already profitable. **
  • Shopping ads must be profitable before you add search text ads. **
  • If Shopping ads do not work yet, search text ads are unlikely to succeed. Only a small number of stores are exceptions. **
  • Start search text ads only after your Shopping ads have revealed medium- and high-value search terms. **
  • These are terms that show buying intent for your products. Examples are product IDs (codes that identify one item), brand names combined with product types, or very specific long-tail terms (long, detailed phrases) for one product or a small group. **
  • Search text ads take longer to set up than Shopping ads. **
  • Many products are never searched by name or product code. **
  • Build search text ads only for search terms that people actually use for your products or brands. **
  • The search term report is a list of the real words people typed before clicking your ad. It comes from your Shopping campaigns. **
  • This report is the data source for finding keywords that should become search text ads. **
  • Shopping ads act as cheap research before you spend setup time on search text ads. **
  • Do not start product-specific search campaigns until Shopping campaigns have shown which keywords sell.
  • Shopping campaigns are expected to keep a better return on ad spend (sales earned for every dollar spent on ads) than search campaigns.
  • So a search campaign adds only a small extra source of traffic.
  • Do not launch Google search text ads until the Shopping campaigns are already profitable and you know your best sellers. **
  • Until then, check the Google Ads account (Google's paid ad platform) at least once a day. **
  • Add irrelevant search terms to the negative keyword list. This is a list of words that stop your ad from showing for them. **
  • Before running search text ads for a high-ticket dropshipping store, answer these questions in order: **
    1. When is the right time to start running search text ads?
    2. Which search terms should you target? Start with terms that have high purchase intent and are likely to convert relatively quickly.
    3. What is the best way to write the ad copy, including the headline, the ad text and the extension details?
  • Ad copy means the words in the ad. Extension details are extra links or information added to the ad. **
  • Search text ads that build on a proven product follow this sequence: (2024)
    1. Identify the best-selling product.
    2. Create a search text ad for it. Use the product name as a phrase match keyword (the words must appear in this order, with other words allowed around them).
    3. Optimize the product page for SEO (search engine optimization, making a page easier for Google to find).
    4. The result is three spots on page one: the Shopping ad, the search text ad above organic results, and an organic listing. (2024)

Keyword Sources from Shopping Data

4 min

Procedure: starting search text ads from Shopping data **

  1. Confirm that your Shopping ads are profitable.
  2. Open your Shopping campaigns.
  3. Find search terms that already get traffic and are specific to one product, one model, one brand or a small product group.
  4. Build search text ads around those terms. Test them with a small budget.

Procedure: search term mining in Google Ads **

  1. Open the Google Ads account and go to Campaigns.
  2. Skip the high-priority Shopping campaign, which holds low-value search terms.
  3. Open the low-priority Shopping campaign, which holds the high-value search terms.
  4. Optionally open one ad group (a folder of keywords and ads inside a campaign) if its products have better margins. Margin means profit left per sale. Use this to test only those products.
  5. Open the Keywords tab in the ad group and select Search Terms.
  6. Set the date range to about three months if the account has run that long.
  7. Look for very specific terms, such as model codes, model names, brand plus model combinations and product-specific wording.
  8. Note close variations of each strong term. You need them to build exact match or phrase match targets.
  9. Download the search term report as Excel, Google Sheets or PDF.
  10. Copy the terms you want to target into a central keyword spreadsheet. Repeat for the other ad groups, and for the medium-priority campaign where brand-level terms can be found.

Setting: Google Ads path for search term analysis ** Campaigns > open the Shopping campaign > open the ad group > Keywords tab > Search Terms. Set the date range (a custom range of about three months is used). Use the Download button to export the list.

Procedure: keywords from the Shopping keyword report (2023)

  1. Open Google Ads and go to the Shopping campaigns.
  2. Pull the keyword report for the Shopping campaigns.
  3. Filter the report to your best-selling product.
  4. Note the keywords that have already led to sales of that product.
  5. Add those keywords to a search text campaign built around that product's page.

Procedure: extracting keywords for a new Search campaign

  1. In Google Ads, open your Shopping campaign.
  2. Navigate to the Keywords section or the report showing where your ads appeared.
  3. Identify all keywords that generated clicks and sales.
  4. Copy the keywords that drove sales for your target product.
  5. Paste them into your new Search campaign ad group as phrase match keywords (enclosed in quotation marks).

Rules for choosing keywords

  • Keyword selection for search text ads is based on what already sells. (2023)
  • It is not based on search volume (how many people search a term each month) or competition scores (how many advertisers want the same term). (2023)
  • Keyword volume and competition figures from Google are not used for choosing these keywords. (2023)
  • Shopping data for individual product pages supports the decision of which products to put on search text campaigns. (2023)
  • Keywords that already drove sales of a product are strong candidates for a search text ad test. **
  • These keywords should also show a high click-through rate on Shopping ads. **
  • Start with an exact match on the core term. Then add the close variations found in the report. **
  • Model names and model codes are high-intent search terms. High-intent means the searcher already knows which product they want. **
  • Keep every variation of a model name or code in your target list. **
  • A brand-level search term (a search using only the brand name) has a lower conversion rate than model-level terms. **
  • In some niches, a low enough cost per click (CPC, the price you pay each time someone clicks) makes brand-level terms viable. **
  • Keep every target keyword in one central spreadsheet. **
  • Record the search term, its match type and its performance figures in the same file. You can then reuse the terms without searching the reports again. **
  • Optimize a Google Search campaign that starts with many keywords and search terms. Some pay off and others do not.
    1. Review which keywords and search terms generated sales or add-to-carts (a shopper adding an item to their cart).
    2. Keep and scale the terms that convert.
    3. Remove or refine the terms that spend without paying off.

Case: model-level starting point ** Starting point: a single-brand ad group in the low-priority Shopping campaign. Its max CPC (the highest price per click you allow) was US$1.20, with enhanced bidding (Google adjusts bids automatically). The three-month search term report showed many searches for specific models and model codes, such as HL400D, HL200SN and HL300SN. These became the candidates for search text ads. The ad group totals for the period were 289 clicks, 4,187 impressions, a cost of US$266.20, a cost per conversion of US$91.36 and a conversion rate of 1.01%.

Case: brand-level benchmark ** Brand-level test: a Shopping ad group for a brand in another campaign had a max CPC of US$0.80 (enhanced) and an analysis window of about three months. It produced 112 clicks, 3,404 impressions, a cost of US$130.92, a cost per conversion of US$65.46 and a conversion rate of 1.79%. This was the benchmark for judging whether brand-level search text ads could pay off.

Brand-level terms ** Brand-level search terms can be tested in the medium-priority campaign. Their conversion rate is usually lower than for model-level terms. In some niches, a low enough cost per click makes them viable.

Keyword Research and Validation

3 min

Setting: Keyword Planner access Keyword Planner is Google's free tool for finding search words and their search volumes. You reach it via Google Ads > Tools > Keyword Planner. Inside it, select the option Discover new keywords.

Procedure: keyword research in Keyword Planner

  1. Open your Google Ads account.
  2. Click Tools.
  3. Click Keyword Planner.
  4. Click Discover new keywords.
  5. Enter your niche (a small market, for example standing desk). Also enter your own URL or a competitor's URL to find related keywords. Doing both gives more and different keyword ideas.
  6. Click Get results.
  7. Close the refined keywords section, since it is not needed for tracking.
  8. Record the average monthly searches, the competition rank and the top-of-page bid range (the price range to appear at the top of the results), low and high.
  9. Use the high range of the top-of-page bid for tracking.

Setting: where to find volumes Keyword Planner is found at Tools > Planning > Keyword Planner. Set the plan location to the target country (for example United States). Set languages to All languages. Use the last 12 months as the date range before reading volumes.

  • Search volume differs widely between closely related terms. Check each keyword's own volume.
  • Keep high-volume terms. Be careful with niche terms that have very low monthly searches. Apply your own judgement to every term that has real volume.
  • A validated keyword set shapes everything downstream, including ad groups, ad copy and campaign structure.
  • Build the set carefully before creating campaigns. It sets the direction for the rest of the account.
  • Keep ad groups tight so the ad copy matches the keywords in the group.
  • Relevant copy protects Quality Score (Google's rating of how relevant your ads and pages are) over the long term.
  • Google Ads shows keyword search volumes in large ranges (for example 10,000-100,000). This lasts until the account is established and has spent more money. (2025)
  • Exact numbers appear only with account maturity and spending history. (2025)
  • Third-party keyword research tools (made by outside companies) give exact monthly search volumes, with country-specific filtering (numbers for one country only). (2025)
  • Google Ads shows broad ranges until the account is established and has spent money. (2025)
  • Keywords Everywhere is a keyword research tool. Use it to compare search volume and competition for product keywords.
  • For a new store, look for keywords with 1,000 to 10,000 searches per month.
  • For a new store, the cost per click should be below $1, with low competition.
  • Golden nugget keywords (valuable terms with moderate search volume and low competition) often deliver better returns for Google Ads.
  • They can deliver better returns than high-volume, highly competitive terms.
  • Keyword Planner cost-per-click estimates are usually higher than what you will actually pay. **
  • Google adjusts the real cost based on click quality and how users behave on the landing page (the page a visitor arrives on). **
  • A low top-of-page bid range does not mean zero impressions. (2023)
  • Better click-through rate and longer time on site (how long visitors stay) can earn cheaper clicks. (2023)
  • So a keyword with a high range can still be worth starting. (2023)
  • Sources differ on AI-generated and outside keywords. One source says keywords for search text ads come from the Shopping keyword report. It says they do not come from outside keyword tools or AI brainstorming. (2023)
  • Another source builds an AI-generated keyword set and validates every term in Keyword Planner before creating campaigns.
  • Both apply to the same decision. The Shopping report sets the list for proven products. A validated set is needed when you build a broader list.

Match Types

4 min

Syntax means how you type the keyword.

Table: match types for search ads

Match Type Syntax Behavior Examples
Exact match [keyword] Appears only for the keyword and close variants such as plurals [car] triggers for 'car' and 'cars'
Phrase match "keyword" Appears when the phrase is present with other words before or after it "car" triggers for 'fast car', 'car parts', 'amphibious car'
Broad match keyword Appears for any search related to the keyword, including synonyms and variations car triggers for 'automobile', 'ferrari paint color', 'car parts', 'truck tires'
Modified broad +keyword +modifier Appears for searches containing the modified terms or close variations +red car triggers for 'reddish car', 'stemming', 'CA'

Table: positive targeting match types **

Match type Syntax Ad shows for Notes
Exact match [keyword] Only the same words in the same order; close variants such as plurals and misspellings are also accepted; no extra words around it Tight control, needs many variations listed
Phrase match 'keyword' in quotation marks The phrase in the same order, with other words allowed before or after it Catches longer searches without predicting every extra word
Broad match modifier +word1 +word2 All words marked with + must appear, in any order, with any other words around them Middle ground between precise and wider reach; recommended for this method
Broad match keyword with no punctuation Any search containing at least one of the words Not useful for e-commerce conversions; only for brand awareness
  • A keyword is what you bid on in Google Ads.
  • A search query is the actual search term that triggers your ad.
  • Your chosen match type determines which search queries will display your ad.
  • Google Ads match types (broad, phrase and exact) determine how closely a user's search must match a keyword before an ad can show.
  • Choosing the match type controls how much irrelevant traffic a campaign receives.
  • Use exact match with square brackets, for example [trail bike X200]. **
  • It triggers only that wording in the same order. Plurals and small misspellings still trigger it. **
  • Use phrase match with quotation marks, for example "trail bike X200". **
  • It also triggers "buy trail bike X200 online". It does not trigger "X200 trail bike", because the word order changes. **
  • Use broad match modifier with a plus sign before each required word, for example +trail +bike +X200. **
  • It triggers any search that contains all three words in any order. So "X200 bike trail shop" triggers it, while "trail hiking boots" does not. **
  • Broad match without punctuation can show ads for unrelated searches. They may share only one word with the target, such as a generic word that appears in many searches. **
  • Forgetting the punctuation when you set keywords is a common cause of ad spend running out before anyone notices. **
  • Prefer exact match, phrase match and broad match modifier for search text ads. Avoid broad match. **
  • Broad match modifier gives extra reach over exact match while keeping the core product term fixed. This protects profitability. **
  • Broad match modifier is the recommended match type for specific product searches. **
  • It captures variations without manual updates. **
  • For product-specific searches, target using exact match on the complete product phrase in square brackets. For example: [Sunray Cayenne Sauna HL400D]. **
  • Exact match typically generates fewer impressions than broad match modifier. It may deliver a higher conversion rate, due to stronger relevance. **
  • Broad match modifier keywords can include more than two required terms. **
  • Start with the most critical product identifiers marked with plus signs, for example '+cayenne +hl400d'. **
  • You may optionally add terms like '+sauna' for higher specificity. **
  • Add extra terms only when the additional precision is needed to exclude irrelevant searches. **
  • In a phrase-match search campaign, ads can also trigger when the searcher adds words before or after the keyword phrase.
  • Limit the keyword list to phrases that have already sold well in Shopping campaigns. This keeps the campaign narrow.
  • Product-level keywords combine the brand with the model number and size. They do not use the broad category name. **
  • A TV seller would bid on a brand plus a size and model, not on the word TV. This keeps the cost per click much lower. **
  • Three keyword types for a high-ticket store should be targeted with search text ads. **
  • The first is product ID keywords. The second is brand plus product type. The third is product type plus specific descriptors that narrow results to one product or a very small number of products. **
  • Non-branded terms (searches with no brand name) are the last option. **
  • Example for a store selling outdoor saunas: a model code plus brand (for example a brand with model code HX400) points straight to one product page. **
  • A brand plus product type (for example a brand plus "infrared sauna") targets a larger group. **
  • A non-branded term with size and type (for example "four person outdoor sauna") needs a well-built collection page with content. **

Bidding and Budget

2 min
  • Choose "Manual CPC" as the bidding strategy when you create the search campaign. A search campaign is a set of ads that show on Google search results. Manual CPC means you set the price you pay per click yourself (CPC = cost per click). **
  • Set the default bid for each ad group to at least $0.50 per click for search text ads. An ad group is a set of keywords and ads inside a campaign. A bid is the most you offer to pay for one click. Search text ads are plain text ads on Google search results. **
  • Google will warn you if your bid is too low to show on the first page of results. **
  • After launching a search ad campaign, check back within 1-2 days to review your bids. **
  • If Google shows a warning that your bid is below the first-page threshold (the minimum bid needed to reach page one), raise the bid. **
  • Starting bids of $0.50 may need to rise to $1.00 or higher. This depends on the auction (the live bidding contest between advertisers for each click). **
  • Case reference: a single-brand ad group in the low-priority Shopping campaign ran at a maximum CPC of US$1.20 (enhanced). Enhanced means Google may adjust your bid up or down. **
  • Case reference: a brand-level Shopping ad group ran at a maximum CPC of US$0.80 (enhanced). **
  • Use these as reference points for the cost per click you can expect. **
  • Source C's new-store target is a cost per click below $1 with low competition.
  • Daily budget: start with $20 per day as a minimum for search text ads. **
  • Budgets below $5 per day will not generate enough data to optimize effectively. "Optimize" means improving results over time. **
  • Test new search text ads with a small budget, for example around US$10 per day. **
  • Let the test run for a while before you judge the ads. **
  • The result to measure is whether the ad's conversion rate supports its cost. Conversion rate is the share of clicks that turn into a sale. **
  • A search text campaign is a new experiment with its own budget, separate from Shopping budgets. Shopping (Alpha/Beta) is one of Google's Shopping campaign types. (2024)
  • Start a search text campaign at about $5 per day. (2024)
  • Use tight phrase match keywords, for example the store name only. Phrase match means the search must contain your keyword phrase in the same word order. (2024)
  • Target CPA (the most you want to pay for each result) is used only for lead generation campaigns. Lead generation campaigns collect contact details, such as buyer guide sign-ups. **
  • For a target CPA, set a maximum cost per lead, for example 4 USD per lead. **
  • Low search volume (few people searching) on specific product keyword match types is normal for high-ticket items (expensive products). **
  • This does not mean the keywords will not deliver. Check your Google Shopping campaign data to confirm that searches exist. **

Campaign and Ad Group Structure

3 min

Networks

  • For Search campaigns, uncheck "Search network" and "Display network" in campaign settings.
  • This limits your ads to Google search only.
  • Search partners are other websites that show Google search ads. Turning them off avoids wasting budget on them.
  • Uncheck both "Include Google search partners" and "Include Google Display Network". **
  • Text-only search ads do not perform well on the Display Network. The Display Network is Google's network of websites that show picture ads. **

Campaign per product

  • Create one separate Search campaign for each top product instead of grouping several products in one campaign.
  • This gives better budget control.
  • It stops high-performing products from being overshadowed (pushed out of view) by weaker ones.
  • Do not put multiple products in one Search campaign with multiple ad groups. Each product needs its own budget and bid control.
  • Conflicting structure (the sources disagree): one source puts each product in its own ad group inside one campaign. **
  • A campaign holds several ad groups. **
  • Each ad group can hold several ads and keywords. **
  • Naming conventions should clearly show the product type or targeting strategy (how you aim the ads at people). **

Ad group size and ad count

  • Build each ad group with about 10 keywords in Google Ads.
  • Always run two text ads at the same time in each ad group.
  • This lets you keep comparing performance and testing.
  • Ad groups should have at least three ads that closely match the theme of their keywords. **
  • Three or more ads let Google test which headlines and descriptions perform better. **
  • This applies to lead-generation ad groups. **

Procedure: standard search text campaign build **

  1. Click "New Campaign" in Google Ads.
  2. Select "Create a campaign without a goal's guidance".
  3. Choose "Search" as the campaign type.
  4. Enter a campaign name that clearly identifies the product type and targeting, e.g., "Product IDs - Search Text - US".
  5. Under Networks, uncheck "Include Google search partners" and "Include Google Display Network".
  6. Select the target location (e.g., United States).
  7. Set the language (default English).
  8. Enter the daily budget (minimum $20).
  9. Select "Manual CPC" as the bidding strategy.
  10. Expand "Show more settings" and find Ad rotation. Ad rotation decides which ad Google shows.
  11. Change "Optimise: Prefer best performing ads" to "Do not optimise: Rotate ads indefinitely".
  12. Add ad extensions: at least 2 callout extensions (short extra text lines) for offers like "Free Shipping" or "Price Match Guarantee", and a call extension (a phone number shown on the ad) with the business phone number.
  13. Skip sitelink extensions (extra links under the ad) for product-specific ads.
  14. Click "Save and continue" to create ad groups.
  15. Name the ad group after the product or product ID (e.g., "Sunray Cayenne Sauna hl400d").
  16. Set the default bid to $0.50 minimum.
  17. Enter keywords with a match type (how closely a search must match your keyword). For specific product searches, use the broad match modifier format (+keyword +keyword). The + sign means that word must appear in the search.
  18. Enter the final URL (the product page URL). This is the page visitors land on after clicking.
  19. Write Headline 1 (30 characters max) with the product name and key identifier.
  20. Write Headline 2 (30 characters max) emphasizing the product's best unique feature.
  21. Write Headline 3 (30 characters max) with the main offer (e.g., "Free Shipping US Wide").
  22. Create the display path (the web address shown under the ad) including keywords (e.g., "bathvault.com/sunray/cayenne-hl400d").
  23. Write Description 1 (90 characters max) that creates curiosity about the product benefit.
  24. Write Description 2 (90 characters max) introducing a secondary benefit or special offer.
  25. Save ad and continue.
  26. Review the campaign and publish.

Procedure: product search campaign setup

  1. Start with the URL of the product page.
  2. Take keywords from your Shopping campaign keyword report (phrases that already convert, meaning they already lead to sales). Enter them as phrase match.
  3. Pin the product name as the first headline. Pinning fixes the text to one position.
  4. Add benefits (in stock, free shipping) in the second headline.
  5. Include the price in the description.
  6. Optionally add image extensions. Image extensions have a 50% approval rate.
  7. Uncheck Search network and Display network.

Naming

  • Build its keyword list from search terms that already convert in Google Shopping campaigns.

  • Build them one product at a time. **

    1. Pull the best-performing products from Google Shopping.

Responsive Search Ads (current format)

  • The expanded text ad option (the older ad style with fixed text fields) was hidden in the interface. **

  • Steps to create expanded text ads in the Google Ads interface of the time: **

  • You enter these elements yourself for every ad you create. **

Ad Extensions and Site Links

3 min

Principles

  • Ad extensions are extra pieces of information added to an ad, such as links or a phone number.
  • They are one of the simplest ways to raise Quality Score (Google's rating of how relevant your ads and pages are).
  • They also raise CTR and the volume of relevant traffic to a site.
  • They can also lower cost per click.
  • Switch on any extension the platform offers.
  • Extensions give the ad more space on the results page and a more distinct look than competitors who do not use them.
  • Callouts and site links take up more space on the results page.
  • This helps even when they do not directly generate clicks.
  • Run an extension whenever the option is available.
  • Ad extensions make the ad more unique. Competitors who skip them look plainer.
  • Using all available extensions together increases CTR and Quality Score.
  • It also lowers cost per click and sends more of the intended traffic to the website.
  • Ad extensions (callout, call, price, structured snippets) can increase CTR by up to 15%. Structured snippets are lists of set categories, such as product types. **
  • Use at least 2 callout extensions to highlight offers. **
  • Phone extensions let mobile users call directly. **
  • Ad extensions can add business information such as a physical location, phone number, product details and reviews. **
  • Google only pulls extensions into the ad some of the time, so the layout varies between searches. **

Site links

  • Site links add four extra links below the main search ad within a campaign. Each one is a separate link.

  • The searcher can jump directly to the page they want.

  • For brand searches, useful site links point to new releases, a specific popular product, another popular product type, or a custom-build option.

  • Choose site link targets from the most visited pages on the site, such as contact, shipping, reviews, or the best-selling product. Do not write generic links.

  • Site links should match what searchers of the brand or product are most likely to want.

  • Procedure to create site links in Google Ads:

    1. Open the search campaign you want to extend.
    2. Click Ads and extensions.
    3. Open the Extensions tab on the ads page.
    4. Click Add a new site link.
    5. Create each site link one by one inside Google Ads.

  • Conflicting guidance on product-specific ads: the 2021-22 build skips sitelink extensions for product-specific ads. **

  • The other sources say to switch on every available extension and use site links for brand searches.

Callouts and other extensions

  • Callout extensions are short extra lines of text shown under the main ad, alongside site links.
  • They state additional facts about the brand that customers should know.
  • For e-commerce stores, this ranks them as the third most important extension.
  • All search-campaign extension types are added from one place.
  • That place includes site links, callouts and promotions.
  • To reach it, open the search campaign, go to Ads and extensions, then the Extensions tab on the ads page.
  • Site links are created one at a time there.
  • The same tab offers the other extension types available for search text campaigns.
  • Use call extensions or call-only ads for local service businesses (plumbers, locksmiths) where customers click to call directly from the ad.
  • Use "Calls on website" to track calls from a phone number displayed on your website.
  • Image extensions have a 50% approval rate (half are accepted by Google).
  • Image extensions can be added optionally.

Ad Testing and Optimization

2 min

Metrics

  • Text ad optimization focuses on improving both CTR and conversions.
  • CTR (click-through rate) shows whether the ad reaches the right audience and appeals enough to click.
  • Conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy) measures whether the landing page persuades visitors to complete the purchase.
  • When split testing search ads, base the winner primarily on CTR. CTR signals relevance to Google. **
  • A higher CTR can lower your average CPC through improved quality score. **
  • Secondary metrics (numbers that measure results) are conversions and cost per click. **
  • Note the conflict: one source treats CTR and conversion rate together, while the 2021-22 material selects winners on CTR first.

Rotation and duration

  • Select "Do not optimise: Rotate ads indefinitely" instead of the default "Optimise: Prefer best performing ads". **
  • This lets you control when split tests end and which ad wins. **
  • It stops Google from deciding too quickly. **
  • Run split tests for about 2 weeks. **
  • After 2 weeks, review metrics to determine the winning ad. **
  • After launching, all ads must be approved before they can generate impressions (times the ad is shown). **
  • Approval typically takes 1-2 days, but may complete within hours. **
  • Monitor the campaign dashboard (the main overview screen). Ads will not run until approved. **

Test design

  • Keep a written log of all text ad variations tested.
  • Record each variation's performance results and whether it succeeded or failed.
  • Use this record to avoid retesting ineffective variations.
  • Use it to build a reference library of proven winning approaches.
  • Iterative testing (changing small things one at a time) modifies only one element, such as the call-to-action phrase (the words that tell the person what to do).
  • It keeps all other headline, body copy and display URL constant (unchanged).
  • This isolates the impact of that single change, so any performance shift reveals what drove it.
  • Evolutionary testing (comparing very different ads) contrasts two substantially different ad variations.
  • These differ in headlines, angles or overall messaging approaches.
  • It helps discover which fundamental frame or emotional angle your audience responds to most.
  • After a split test win, pause (do not delete) the losing ad so you can reference it later. **
  • Create a new ad variation that tries to beat the previous winner. **
  • Then run a new 2-week split test. **
  • Repeat continuously. **
  • Stacking (combining several elements) and extensions are tested the same way.
  • Change one thing at a time.

Monitoring

  • Check back within 1-2 days to review bids and respond to first-page warnings. **
  • Low search volume on a specific product keyword is not a sign of failure. **
  • Check Shopping data before deciding. **
  • Check the account at least once a day. **
  • Add irrelevant search terms to the negative keyword list (words your ads must not show for). **
  • Use the search term report (the list of exact words people typed before clicking) to keep and scale converting terms. Scaling means spending more on them.
  • Use the same report to remove or refine terms that spend money without paying off.

2 min

  • Search text ad lead generation is an advanced strategy. A lead is a person who leaves their contact details. **

  • Set a maximum cost per lead, for example 4 USD per lead. **

Why Relevance Matters More Than in the Past

1 min
  • Many e-commerce stores fail when they add Google Search text ads to an existing Shopping setup. They run them with the mindset of the early 2000s, when search clicks were cheap and competition was low. Search clicks today are expensive, so the same approach wastes budget.
  • Around the year 2000, nearly all Google search clicks cost less than 50 cents. This let advertisers tolerate very low conversion rates on loosely related queries. A query is the text a person types into search. This no longer holds, so search campaigns need tight targeting.
  • When clicks are cheap, a broad or semi-relevant keyword can still pay off with a few conversions. When clicks are expensive, irrelevant keywords drain the budget quickly. Keyword relevance therefore matters far more for search text ads today than in the past.
  • A loosely related keyword can bring around 700 clicks and only one or two sales. When clicks cost pennies, that return looked acceptable. At today's click prices, the same pattern drains the budget quickly. Check the search terms report for such queries and exclude them.

Building a First Search Campaign from Converting Shopping Terms

2 min
  1. Open the Shopping campaign, go to Keywords > Search terms and add the Conversions column.
  2. Download the search terms report and add the product column to see which product received the conversions.
  3. Filter the data to search terms with more than one conversion.
  4. Confirm that all conversions point to the same product, and note its URL as the landing page. The landing page is the page a click opens. A URL is a web address.
  5. In Google Ads, start a new campaign and choose Search as the campaign type.
  6. Select Website visits as the goal and enter the website URL.
  7. Enter a descriptive campaign name.
  8. Set the location to the United States and exclude Hawaii, Alaska and Puerto Rico.
  9. Set the location presence option to target people in the targeted locations and exclude people in the excluded locations.
  10. Set the language to English and choose the daily budget.
  11. Keep delivery method on Standard. Standard spreads the ads evenly through the day.
  12. Set bidding to manual and keep Enhanced CPC off. CPC means cost per click, the price you pay each time someone clicks. Enhanced CPC lets Google change your bids on its own.
  13. Skip dynamic search ads, audiences, sitelinks and call extensions for now. Dynamic search ads are ads Google builds on its own from your site pages. Audiences are groups of people, such as past visitors. Sitelinks are extra links under the ad. Call extensions add a phone number under the ad.
  14. Save and continue.
  15. Create the ad group with a descriptive name that states the theme and source of the terms.
  16. Set the default bid to about $2 CPC. The default bid is your starting price per click. Take it from the average CPC of the Shopping campaign.
  17. Enter the converting terms as keywords, typing exact match keywords inside square brackets. Exact match is explained under Match Types below.
  18. Write the ad: final URL, headline and display path. The display path is the short text shown in the web address line of the ad. Skip tracking templates unless a third-party tool needs them. A tracking template is code added to a web address to measure clicks. Add a mobile URL only if one is used. A mobile URL is a separate web address for phones.
  19. Save, continue to the campaign and launch it.
  20. In the campaign, change every keyword from broad match to exact match in one step using Change match types.

The purpose of this campaign is that it competes on the same results page as the winning Shopping ads. It sends traffic to the same product URL that already converted. Because the terms already converted in Shopping, they have a high chance of converting in Search as well.

Keyword Research and Checks

1 min
  • Ahrefs and SEMrush are keyword research tools. They measure monthly search volume for categories. Monthly search volume is how many times per month people search a term. Both offer free trials that can be used for a basic keyword check.
  • Relevance check before bidding on a keyword. Relevant means the ad matches what the searcher wants.

    2. Note which results show ads.
    3. Read the top ads and check whether the ad text mentions the specific use case or the informational need in the query. A use case is a specific way the product is used.

    6. Repeat the check for each keyword before you start bidding on it.

Match Types

2 min

Match types set how close a search must be to trigger your keyword.

Match type Notation What it matches
Exact match [keyword] in square brackets Only the exact term and very close variants (plurals, misspellings, same meaning in word order)
Phrase match "keyword" in quotation marks Searches that contain the keyword phrase in that order, with other words possibly before or after it
Modified broad match +word1 +word2 with a plus sign before each word Close variants of each marked word individually (e.g. +red also matches reddish), but not unrelated words such as pink for +red
Broad match keyword without brackets, quotes or plus signs Synonyms, related searches, word stems and many unrelated but loosely connected queries
  • Broad match can show ads for a very wide range of queries. The same keyword can trigger on surprising searches, such as a different quantity or a different product type. Exact match is the most controlled option. Phrase match is in between. Modified broad gives more control than broad, because each word only expands to its own close variants.
  • Roughly 25 percent of Google searches each month are completely new and cannot be predicted by a seller. Match types exist so that the ad can still appear for these unknown queries.
  • Phrase match on the keyword "jump rope" lets a Search ad appear for long-tail searches. Long-tail searches are long, specific searches. An example is a jump rope that survives hot summer sun. A seller could never list such a search in advance. This is why phrase match captures traffic that exact match misses.
  • Broad match on "car" can trigger on synonyms and related searches. Synonyms are words with the same meaning. Examples include automobile, Ferrari paint color, truck tires and skyline roadster. This shows how far broad match can drift from the seller's product.
  • Modified broad match with +hike can also match its word stem hiking. A word stem is the root of a word. +CA can match the abbreviation California. Each marked word expands only to its own close variants, which keeps the reach narrower than broad match.
  • Keywords entered without brackets, quotes or plus signs are broad match. A new Search ad group can therefore start with broad keywords, even when you meant exact. Check every match type before the campaign goes live. Convert the keywords to exact match in one step if needed.

Launch Keyword Mix and Bidding by Match Type

1 min

  • Start a new Search campaign with exact match keywords only, using terms that already converted in Shopping. Add phrase and modified broad keywords later at lower bids. Add broad match last, at the lowest bids.

  • Launching a new Search campaign with broad match keywords lets the ads appear for many irrelevant queries. This wastes budget before any data is available. Use only proven converting terms at launch.

  • A long, specific term that already converted in Shopping, such as "infrared sauna for three people", should become the first exact match keyword in a new Search campaign. If it were broad match, the same ad could show for unrelated queries, such as "infrared sauna for 20 people".

  • Bid by match type in this order:

    • Most for exact match
    • Somewhat less for phrase match
    • Less again for modified broad match
    • Much less for broad match

    This keeps the budget on the most reliable queries.

  • Long term, the keyword mix of a Search campaign should be mostly exact match. Add some phrase, some modified broad, and only a small share of broad match. This is a long-term target. It is not the setup for a new launch.

Search Intent and Funnel Stage

1 min
  • Search intent is what the searcher wants. The funnel is the path from "just looking" to "ready to buy". Top of funnel means early research. Bottom of funnel means ready to buy. Meet the searcher at their stage, whether they are early in research or ready to buy. Each keyword's landing page should reflect that stage.
  • Match the landing page to the search intent. A top-of-funnel informational query should lead to an informational page. A bottom-of-funnel commercial query can lead to a product page. Never send top-of-funnel traffic to a bottom-of-funnel landing page.
  • Sending people who search for information, such as how-to or comparison questions, straight to a product page is a costly mistake. They are not ready to buy. Running traffic this way is essentially paying for awareness, and it rarely converts.
  • Classify search keywords by commercial intent. This decides which funnel stage each keyword belongs to:
Group Examples
Commercial "buy product", "product free shipping", product-type terms
Midway "product comparison", "product A versus B"
Non-commercial Keywords that describe a problem or a question
  • The sources place comparisons in the midway group. The list of information-seeking queries that are not ready to buy includes comparison questions. Treat comparison keywords as midway. Do not send them straight to a product page.
  • Build your ad messages from the searcher's exact question or problem. Match the landing page to that stage. A sale depends on the ad, the landing page and the user's readiness working together.

Informational and Lead-Generation Searches

1 min
  • Organic results for informational searches often outperform paid ads. Turning a strong informational organic listing into an ad would likely bring even more traffic. Paid informational ads should therefore point to genuinely helpful content, not a sales page.
  • For an informational query such as asking what makes a good trail running shoe, ads that push free shipping, free returns or a shop-now message miss the searcher's intent. The searcher wants information. A sales-driven ad gets fewer clicks than an informational result.
  • An informational landing page lets you capture a lead. A lead is a name or email from someone interested. You do this through an email opt-in, which is a sign-up form. The page can also set a tracking cookie, a small file that remembers the visitor. Then you can retarget the visitor, which means showing them ads again later. These benefits make top-of-funnel traffic valuable even when the visitor does not buy right away.
  • Keywords that do not fit the simple top, middle and bottom split can still be handled:
    • For someone who knows the problem but not the solution, such as "manage business finances", offer a free software trial as a lead generation step. Sell the paid sign-up later.
    • For a problem such as "improving focus at work", offer a low-commitment product, such as a supplement with the first month free.

Ad Copy Relevance

1 min
  • Ads for a specific use case, such as running shoes for hills, fail when neither the ad text nor the landing page mentions that use case. Without this relevance, the ad performs worse than the organic results for the same query.
  • Showing ads for search queries that differ from what the buyer intended pushes the click-through rate (CTR) very low. CTR is the share of people who see an ad and click it. Keep keyword targeting and ad text tightly aligned with the actual search intent.
  • The display URL path is free text. It can contain a sale word or the target keyword, which signals to the searcher where they will land. It does not have to be the real page address.
  • Putting the price in the ad copy pre-qualifies buyers. This stops people who cannot afford the product from clicking. It may lower CTR, but it improves the cost-to-revenue ratio, which matters more than CTR. Cost-to-revenue ratio is the ad cost divided by the money earned. Shopping campaigns use the same logic by showing prices.
  • Adding buying-intent keywords, such as "buy" or "price", keeps non-buyers from clicking at all. Limiting unlikely buyers before the click usually saves more money than raising CTR.
  • **** The copywriting principles for search text ads apply equally to Bing or any other search engine where the ad is shown. Google is the platform noted for automatically bolding the searched keyword in the ad.

Ad Copy Testing

1 min
  • Routine ad copy testing:
    1. Run at least two ads at the same time.
    2. Track which headlines, descriptions and calls to action get clicks, and which get conversions.
    3. Keep written notes on what works and what does not, so new tests build on past results.
    4. Compare CTR and conversion rate together before deciding a winner.
  • Ad test steps for finding the strongest versions:
    1. Split test your ads to find the versions with the highest CTR. A split test compares two versions to see which one wins.
    2. Use emotion in the ad copy to make people want to click.
    3. Give a clear call to action. A call to action tells people what to do, such as "buy now".
    4. Give people a good reason to click on the ad.
  • Optimize Google text ads for CTR. Always check conversion rate alongside it. Run at least two ads in the same ad group at the same time, so results can be compared.
  • CTR and cost-to-revenue can pull in different directions. Price in the copy may lower CTR, and then the cost-to-revenue ratio takes priority. Where that trade-off applies, judge the ad by revenue per cost, not by CTR alone.
  • **** Saving changes to a live ad creates a new version. That version is sent back for review. Metrics from earlier versions remain visible, so you can compare the old and new ad versions.

Remarketing: audiences, display and dynamic campaigns

Section 10.18 · 18 of 23 in this chapter · 46 min read

In short

  • Remarketing shows ads again to people who already visited your store. It works best alongside Google Shopping ads.
  • Check that Google Analytics data collection is ON before building any audience. If it is off, remarketing will not work.
  • Audience lists need at least 1,000 people before Google uses them. If your list is too small, make the time window longer, such as 180 days.
  • Start with one Display campaign and a small daily budget. Add more campaigns only after the first one makes money.
  • Remarketing (showing ads again to people who already visited your store) needs a small budget: about 10% of what you spend on Shopping.
More key points
  • Turn off optimized targeting and hide your ads from people who already bought, so money goes to the right people.
  • If a campaign shows no ads, first check that its audience says Eligible.
  • Weak early numbers do not prove the plan fails. Give it more time and data before you change everything.
  • Connect GA4 to Google Ads first, and keep both tags installed on your site.
  • Start with a 90-day visitor audience. Widen it to 180 days only if it is too small.
  • An audience needs at least 100 people before ads show.
  • Remove retargeted visitors from normal Shopping campaigns with a -100% bid adjustment.
  • For display ads, use Standard delivery, manual bids, a 0.20 USD starting bid, and a cap of 3 impressions per day per ad group.

Remarketing: Audiences, Display and Dynamic Campaigns

1 min

Audience Basics and Prerequisites

2 min
  • A segment is a group of website visitors. It is built from what they did, such as visits, purchases and page interactions (things people do on a page).
  • Segments are the building blocks of the audiences (saved lists of people) used in retargeting (showing ads again to people who already visited).
  • Traffic temperature means how ready a person is to buy from you. It decides who goes into each audience.
  • Cold audiences are people who may want the store's products. They are built from keyword searches (words people type into Google).
  • Warm audiences are people who have visited the store. They are built from website visitors.
  • Past-customer audiences are people who have bought. They are built from customers.
  • The core Google Ads strategy (Google's paid ad tool) for a dropshipping store (a store where a supplier ships the products) combines Google Shopping ads (product ads with picture and price) with remarketing.
  • Shopping reaches new buyers who are searching for products.
  • Remarketing re-engages people who already know the store.
  • Google remarketing is still worth setting up in countries without Google Shopping.
  • Google Ads works best when traffic already comes from other channels (other sources of visitors).
  • The store should also have tags (small pieces of code on the site that report actions), tracking (recording what visitors do) and analytics (reports on visitor behavior) connected.
  • Google can then retarget visitors across its network, such as YouTube and display placements (ad spots on websites).
  • To set up Google retargeting, follow these steps.
    1. Install the Google tag (a code snippet on the website).
    2. Track user data, such as add to cart and initiate checkout.
    3. Build retargeting campaigns for previous visitors who did not complete a purchase.
  • Before building any audience, check that Google Analytics data collection is on. GA4 means Google Analytics 4, the tool that counts visitors.
    1. Log into Google Analytics 4.
    2. Click the Settings gear icon (bottom left).
    3. Click Data Collection and Modification.
    4. Click Data Collection.
    5. Ensure all toggle switches (on/off buttons) are ON.
    6. Verify that User Data Collection Acknowledgement (a confirmation box) is acknowledged.
    7. If it is not enabled, remarketing will not work.
  • The Google Shopping feed app (an app that sends your product list to Google) provides three tracking events (recorded actions) for Google Ads audiences.
  • The three events are add to cart, begin checkout and purchase.
  • These events must be installed through this app.
  • They are the source for the tag-based segments (segments built from tags).
  • For the purchaser segment, select the purchase event whose name includes the label "purchase in category".
  • This label identifies the correct event when several are listed.

Creating Audiences in Google Ads and Analytics

4 min

Navigation

  • In Google Ads, go to Tools in the left menu. Then go to Shared library (a menu area for shared lists). Then click Audience manager (the screen for building lists).
  • If Shared library is collapsed, expand it with the caret (small arrow) before clicking Audience manager.

Starter Segments in Audience Manager

  1. Log in to Google Ads. Open the Tools menu from the left menu, then select Audience Manager inside Shared library. Expand the section if needed.
  2. Go to Data segments (saved visitor groups) and click + to create a new segment. Select Website visitors.
  3. Configure the first segment:
    • Enter the name [YourStore].com Visitors 30 Days.
    • Set Segment members (who goes in the list) to Anyone who visited a webpage.
    • Set Membership duration (how long a person stays in the list) to 30 days.
    • Leave the Pre-fill option (lets Google fill the list with past visitors) enabled.
  4. Click Create segment and allow the audience size to fill in over the next several hours.
  5. Click + to create another segment. Select Website visitors again.
  6. Configure the second segment:
    • Enter the name [YourStore].com Purchase 30 Days.
    • Change Segment members to Visitors of a page with specific tags.
    • Search for and select the Purchase event tag (a tag that reports sales).
  7. Set Membership duration to 30 days and click Create segment.
  • Website visitor segment sizes take hours to fill in.
  • Purchaser segment sizes fill in only after purchases happen.
  • Purchaser segment sizes may take extra time to show first data.
  • Create dynamic Google audiences (lists that update on their own as people act) early, such as purchase and website visitor audiences. Do this even with no customers yet.
  • These audiences fill automatically as actions occur. So they can be empty on the day they are created.
  • A 180-day website visitor audience or a 30-day purchase audience can be empty on day zero (the day you create it). It can still fill in later.

Analytics Audience Definitions

  1. Create website visitor audiences in Google Analytics admin (the settings area). Use audience definitions (rules that set who is in a list).
    • Website visitors for 180 days, 30 days and 7 days.
    • Plus all customers (people with at least 1 transaction, meaning a completed order) in the past 30 days and the past 7 days.
  2. Link Google Analytics to Google Ads with the same email and linked accounts (connected accounts). Audience data then syncs (updates together) automatically.
  3. In Google Ads, select your campaign. Then go to Audiences > Exclusions (a list of people left out) to block customer lists from remarketing ads.

Missing Audiences

  1. Open the Google Analytics account and click the gear icon (Admin) at bottom left.
  2. Under Audience definitions, choose Audiences. Confirm the audience is listed.
  3. Check that the Google Ads account is linked to Analytics.
  4. Under audience destinations (where lists are sent), click Edit and select the correct Google Ads account.
  5. In Google Ads, open shared audiences and remove any active filters (on-screen filters that hide items).
  6. If the audience still does not appear, create a couple of additional audiences. This shows whether the issue is specific to one audience.
  • A linked Google Ads account can point to the wrong account when several exist. This stops audiences from appearing.
  • Check that the link points to the account you actually run.

Audience Counts

  • An audience imported from GA4 into Google Ads shows far fewer users than GA4 counts.
  • This happens because only visitors eligible (allowed to see ads) for remarketing are included.
  • A drop from about 3,000 GA4 users to about 200 in Google Ads is a large gap.
  • The two numbers are not expected to match one-to-one.
  • If an imported audience is too small, widen its date range (time span) so more people fall into it.
  • The audience itself offers no setting to narrow or change its eligibility.
  • To see users per audience per channel, open Tools > Shared audiences in Google Ads.
  • The list shows the number of eligible users for each channel.
  • Google Ads audience sizes are counted per channel (Search, YouTube, Display, Gmail).
  • Never add them together.
  • Check the number for the channel you plan to run, which is usually Display.

Windows and Minimum Sizes

1 min
  • Website visitor audiences are defined by window length (how many days back), such as 2, 5, 7, 10, 14, 21 and 28 days.
  • A 7-day website visitor audience shows ads only to people who visited the site in the last seven days.
  • Google requires at least 1,000 people in a 30-day website visitor audience before it allows proper targeting.
  • If your traffic is smaller, increase the window to 90 or 180 days.
  • Google remarketing audiences require at least 1,000 people before they can be used.
  • Do not inflate the audience with bot traffic (fake visits from programs) or unique-IP tricks (tricks that fake many separate visitors).
  • Instead, use a bigger window or grow the audience faster with more paid cold traffic (ads to people who do not know the store yet) or SEO (improving your place in free search results).
  • For a single store, a bigger window (for example 180 days) is the right way to reach the 1,000-person minimum.
  • Inflating numbers with bot traffic defeats the purpose of remarketing to qualified visitors.
  • Faster audience growth comes from more cold traffic spend or more SEO traffic.
  • A remarketing campaign with a 7-day audience in a new store can result in no impressions (ad views).
  • This happens because the audience is usually too small.
  • Start with the 30-day audience. Add shorter audiences only when traffic supports them.
  • A remarketing campaign only starts getting impressions once its audience shows as eligible. So budget does not matter until then.
  • If the audience still shows as not eligible after a day, pause the campaign and recreate it.
  • In Audience Manager, check each audience's size.
  • If an audience says "too small to serve", it does not have enough users yet.

Setting Up Dynamic Display Remarketing

8 min

Campaign Order

  • There are eight campaign types across Display, Search, Video and Discovery. They target cold, warm and past customers.
  • Implement one at a time, starting with dynamic display remarketing (ads that show each person the products they viewed).
  • Set up the Shopping campaign before the remarketing campaign.
  • Google Shopping is often the largest traffic source for a new store.
  • Remarketing is built on top of that traffic.
# Campaign name Channel Traffic temperature Audience or focus
1 Display - WARM - Website Visitors Display WARM Website visitors
2 Display - WARM - Monthly Promotions Display WARM Monthly promotions
3 Search - COLD - YourStore.com Search COLD Brand name (store name)
4 Search - COLD - [Top Shopping Product] Search COLD Best-selling product
5 Video - WARM - Website Visitors Video WARM Website visitors
6 Video - WARM - Monthly Promotions Video WARM Monthly promotions
7 Video - PAST CUSTOMERS - Goodwill Video PAST CUSTOMERS Goodwill
8 Discovery - WARM - Monthly Promotions Discovery WARM Monthly promotions
  • Campaign names use the format Channel - Temperature - Audience or goal.
  • Display - WARM - Website Visitors uses dynamic display ads served to the remarketing audiences.
  • Its goal is more exposure to website visitors.
  • Exclude recent customers from this campaign.
  • Display - WARM - Monthly Promotions uses standard display ads to raise awareness of limited-time offers.
  • Static image ads (still pictures) for this campaign can be designed in Canva (an online design tool).
  • Exclude recent customers from this campaign.
  • Implementation order:
    1. Complete Google Ads training first.
    2. Create all audience segments in Google Analytics.
    3. Launch Campaign 1 (Dynamic Display Remarketing) with a small budget.
    4. When it is profitable (making money), add Campaign 2 (Display Monthly Promos).
    5. Then add Search campaigns (3-4).
    6. Then add Video campaigns (5-7).
    7. Finally add the Discovery campaign (8).
  • Each campaign can run independently.
  • After setup, only minimal maintenance is required.
  • Standard display campaigns work well for remarketing and monthly promotions.
  • Dynamic remarketing with Shopping product ads (ads that show the customer's viewed products) also works well.
  • Remarketing is set up for product-specific remarketing with Shopping ads.
  • Start the first paid campaigns with remarketing when a store already has heavy organic traffic (free visitors from search).
  • Remarketing usually shows the highest ROAS (return on ad spend, meaning money earned per dollar spent).
  • Remarketing can be launched quickly, followed by new Shopping campaigns.

Campaign Creation Path

  • Campaign setup for display or search works like this:
    • Select the campaign type.
    • Choose "without goals guidance".
    • Select the campaign category (Display, Search, Video or Discovery).
    • Set the conversion goal (the action that counts as success) to Purchases only.
    • Select "Standard display campaign" or "Search".
    • Link the Google Merchant Center data feed (your product list file) if using dynamic ads.
  • Navigate to Google Ads left navigation > Campaigns > New campaign.
  • Then pick "Create a campaign without goals guidance".
  • This path starts every remarketing campaign build.
  • Choose Display, not Shopping.
  • Display ads run on websites and apps that are not owned by Google, but partner with it to show ads.
  • The Google Display Network (a group of partner websites and apps that show Google ads) is stated to reach about 3 million websites and apps.
  • Remarketing ads use this network to follow visitors across the web.
  • A display campaign can reach customers across 3 million sites and apps, as shown on the campaign type selection screen.
  • Remarketing ads appear on any site or app where Google places ads.
  • Dynamic display ads pulled from the product feed can appear across over 3 million websites and 650,000 apps via the Google Display Network.
  • They show people the specific products they viewed on the site.
  • Dynamic remarketing reduces ad fatigue (people seeing the same ad too often).
  • It shows each person the products they browsed, instead of the same ad repeatedly.
  • This increases relevance and conversion likelihood.

Full Build: Version A (16 steps)

  1. Log into Google Ads and open Campaigns.
  2. Click the blue plus button and choose New campaign.
  3. Choose "Create a campaign without goals guidance" for manual control (you set the rules yourself).
  4. Select the campaign type Display.
  5. Under conversion goals, check that the Purchase goal is listed. If it is missing, click Add goal and add the purchase goal.
  6. Click Continue and enter your store's homepage URL, even though the ads link to product pages.
  7. Name the campaign with the domain, the word display, the purpose and the audience length. For example: domain display campaign remarketing - 30 days.
  8. Select the audience of 30-day website visitors who are not customers.
  9. Click Continue. Set the country where you do business, and set the language.
  10. Open More settings and set the dynamic ads data feed from Google Merchant Center, with no product filters.
  11. Open More settings > Content exclusions and apply the exclusions listed in the content exclusion units.
  12. Set the average daily budget (the most you spend per day) to 10% of your Google Shopping daily spend. Review it over time.
  13. Change the focus from conversions to viewable impressions (ad views that were actually visible on screen).
  14. Enter a viewable CPM bid of $10. CPM means cost per 1,000 views.
  15. Add targeting, select the 30-day remarketing audience, and turn off optimized targeting (letting Google add extra people on its own).
  16. Add headlines, long headlines and descriptions (the text of the ad). Create the ad, give it a name, and click Publish.

Full Build: Version B (18 steps)

  1. Open the Google Ads account and go to Campaigns in the left menu.
  2. Click New campaign, then choose Create a campaign without goals guidance.
  3. Under campaign type, select Display (not Shopping). Keep the existing conversion goals and click Continue.
  4. Enter the homepage as the business website address and click Continue.
  5. Give the campaign a clear name (example pattern: store domain plus remarketing) and click Continue.
  6. Set the targeting location to the country where you do business. Set the language to English.
  7. Under More settings, leave ad rotation (the order ads show in), ad schedule (the times ads run), devices and campaign URL options at their defaults (the standard settings).
  8. Change the dynamic ads setting. Tick "Use dynamic ads feed for personalized ads" and select the data feed from your Google Merchant Center account.
  9. Leave the product filter on no filters so all products are remarketed.
  10. Under More settings > Content exclusions, set the exclusions described in the content exclusion units.
  11. Click Next to go to budget and bidding.
  12. Set the average daily budget (see the budget rule).
  13. Change the focus from conversions to viewable impressions.
  14. Enter a viewable CPM bid of $10 (see the bid units).
  15. Click Next. Then click add targeting and select the 30-day website visitor audience segment. Turn off optimized targeting.
  16. Click Next to open the ad builder and complete the ad fields.
  17. Click Next. If Google reports errors, go back to Ads. Add the required images (one landscape (wide) and one square), click apply changes, then Next.
  18. Click Publish campaign.
  • Versions A and B differ in detail.
  • Version A adds headlines and descriptions as step 16.
  • Version B adds the images check in step 17.
  • Both use the same budget, bid, focus and targeting rules.

Campaign Settings Details

  • In the campaign settings, the Purchase goal must appear under conversion goals.
  • If it does not, use Add goal to add it.
  • For a remarketing display campaign, keep the existing conversion goals already present in the account.
  • Then enter the homepage URL as the business website address.
  • Click Continue after each step.
  • A remarketing display campaign needs the Purchase goal in its conversion goals list.
  • If it is missing, add it. Then Google treats any purchase signal as a success for performance optimization (adjusting ads for better results).
  • Set the targeting location to the country where the business operates. Leave the language on English.
  • Google's EU political ads declaration (a separate Google declaration about political ads in the EU) is a separate requirement.
  • It applies only if political ads are run.
  • Under More settings, leave ad rotation, ad schedule, devices and campaign URL options unchanged.
  • Only the dynamic ads option changes in this section.
  • Dynamic ads option, Version A: in campaign settings under More settings, the field "dynamic ads, no data feed" must be changed.
  • Click the icon that appears on hover (when the mouse passes over it). Choose "use dynamic ads feed for personalized ads", and select the data feed from Google Merchant Center.
  • Keep filters off so that every product a visitor viewed can appear in the ads.
  • Dynamic ads option, Version B: tick "Use dynamic ads feed for personalized ads".
  • Select the data feed from the Google Merchant Center account.
  • The dynamic feed is what makes each ad show the products the visitor actually viewed.
  • The dynamic ads feed must already exist as a data feed in Google Merchant Center before the remarketing campaign is built.
  • The feed is selected there during campaign setup.
  • A dynamic Display remarketing campaign needs a Google Merchant Center product feed already connected to the Google Ads account.
  • This is usually left over from an earlier Shopping setup.
  • Connect Merchant Center before building the remarketing campaign.
  • Product filter: leave on no filters so all products are included in remarketing.
  • Add a filter only if a subset of products should be remarketed.
  • The filter option can exclude specific products from remarketing ads.
  • For a general remarketing campaign, keep filters off.
  • Turn off native formats (ads that blend into the look of a site) in ad settings.
  • Once live, the dynamic remarketing campaign updates its products and audience automatically.
  • The only ongoing change needed is raising the campaign's daily budget as shopping spend grows.

  • Google may still ask for extra images during its error check. Add them only when the check requires it.
  • Skip video for this campaign type.
  • If Google reports errors on the review screen, add the required images (one landscape and one square).
  • Then click apply changes, and click Next.
  • Complete the headlines, long headlines and descriptions.
  • Create the ad, give it a name, and click Publish.

Approval means Google checks your ads and accepts them.

Situation Approval time Source
New Google Ads account with a new remarketing campaign 1 to 2 days
Account already running other campaigns About 1 hour
Established account with previously approved ads Usually under 30 minutes
First-time remarketing setup A couple of days

  • Clicks and sales come after impressions.

Budget Rules

1 min
  • Set the average daily budget (the most you plan to spend per day, on average) of a remarketing display campaign (ads on websites and apps that reach people who already visited your store; also called retargeting) to 10% of the daily spend on your Google Shopping campaigns (ads with product pictures and prices in Google search). For example, $30 per day on Shopping means $3 per day on remarketing, and $50 per day means $5. Adjust the number up or down over time based on results.
  • Set the remarketing campaign's average daily budget to about 10% of the total daily budget spent on Shopping campaigns. If Shopping and remarketing share one budget, use 10% of the combined Shopping total.
  • Budget examples: with $10 per day on Shopping, the remarketing budget is $1 per day. With a shared $10 per day across Shopping and remarketing, the remarketing budget is $2 per day.
  • The 10% rule scales as Shopping spend grows, so keep it proportional when profitable Shopping campaigns get more money. At $50 per day on Shopping, the remarketing budget becomes $5 per day.
  • Core-spend example: a store spending 100 per day on Google Shopping can start remarketing at about 10 per day.
  • Google remarketing budgets are usually smaller than Facebook budgets, around $5 to $50 per day, and take a similar amount of time to set up.
  • Budget and delivery method (the rule for how fast the budget is spent) are set for retargeting Shopping campaigns during setup.

Bidding Focus and CPM

2 min
  • For remarketing, change the campaign focus (the goal Google aims for) from conversions (sales or sign-ups) to viewable impressions (ads a person can actually see on screen; an impression is one showing of an ad). Viewable impressions appear at the bottom of the focus list. The bid is then entered as a viewable CPM for the ad group (a set of ads inside a campaign).
  • Configure the bidding focus to viewable impressions so the campaign optimizes for ads actually seen by users rather than counting all impressions.
  • Set Viewable CPM to $10 when using viewable impressions as the bidding focus in Google Display remarketing.
  • Set CPM (the cost to reach 1,000 views) to $10 for display campaigns. Actual spend typically ranges $3-$4 per 1,000 impressions, which lets you outbid competitors (pay more than they do) and ensures the warm audience (people who already know your brand) sees your ads.
  • CPM is the cost to reach 1,000 people. For remarketing, a viewable CPM bid of $10 is a suggested starting value. The full bid is rarely spent, but a high bid helps the ads win impressions in a small, highly qualified audience (a small group of people very likely to buy).
  • Recommended viewable CPM bid for remarketing is $10. CPM means the cost for every 1,000 people who see the ad, and actual spend is typically far below the bid.
  • A high CPM bid makes Google show the remarketing ads to more people, since it ranks inventory (the available ad spaces) by the bid it receives. The bid does not mean spending the full amount per 1,000 impressions.
  • Bid adjustment for low delivery (few ads shown): if the viewable impressions bid does not produce enough impressions, raise the maximum viewable CPM bid. If that still gives few clicks, duplicate (copy) the campaign, change its objective to conversions and let it run alongside the original. The original can be re-enabled if needed.
  • For retargeting Shopping campaigns, Manual CPC (you set the price per click yourself; CPC means cost per click) is the bidding strategy at setup. The campaign starts with a bid and is adjusted from there.

Targeting and Optimized Targeting

1 min
  • Targeting (limiting ads to a chosen group of people) for a remarketing display campaign: under Add targeting, open Audience segments (groups of people you can pick) and search for the customer exclusion audience. Scroll to the bottom to find the most recently created audiences, then select the 30-day website visitor audience. Turn off optimized targeting (Google's setting that lets it show ads to people outside your chosen group) before clicking Next.
  • Turn off optimized targeting for all remarketing campaigns. It spends more money on people outside the audience, despite promising 20% more conversions.
  • If optimized targeting stays on, Google may show the ads to people outside the chosen audience. Turn it off so only the qualified remarketing audience sees the ads.
  • Retargeting audiences can receive their own offers, such as a coupon, instead of the message shown to cold traffic (people who do not know your brand yet). Because these visitors already know the brand, bids for them can be set more aggressively (higher).
  • Targeting methods can be stacked (combined), so an audience must meet several conditions at once. For example, show a retargeting ad only to people who visited the site and are also browsing hotel or travel websites.

Exclusions: Customer Exclusions

1 min
  • Always exclude customer lists (lists of people who already bought) from remarketing campaigns after publishing (making the campaign live). Go to Audiences > Exclusions (hiding ads from chosen people) and select the customer audience that matches the campaign timeframe (for example, 30-day customers for a 30-day remarketing campaign). This avoids wasting budget on people who have already bought.
  • Build a 30-day purchase audience and exclude it from remarketing campaigns. This keeps remarketing ads from reaching people who have already bought.
  • For monthly promotion campaigns (special offers that run each month) on Google, add recent purchasers as exclusion audiences. All other warm audiences see the promotion ads.
  • Promotion Google ads should match the email message and be shown to all website visitors from the last 180 days, excluding customers from the last 30 days. Use Display ads (ads on websites and apps) and Demand Gen ads (a Google ad type shown on YouTube, Discover and Gmail) for this audience.
  • Exclude recent customers from both Display - WARM campaigns (campaigns aimed at warm audiences) (Website Visitors and Monthly Promotions).
  • Two remarketing audiences of different lengths, such as 30-day and 14-day, must exclude each other, so the same user does not see ads in both.

Exclusions: Content Exclusions

1 min
  • Content exclusions (blocking the types of pages and videos where ads can show) under More settings (version A): exclude content that is not yet labeled and mature audience content (content for adults). Exclude the sensitive content group in the middle of the list. Under content type, exclude live streaming YouTube videos and parked domains (web addresses with no real site).
  • Content exclusions under More settings > Content exclusions (version B): exclude content not yet labeled (Google has not rated it yet), the entire sensitive content category, and live streaming videos. These keep ads off unlabeled, sensitive and live-stream placements (the sites, apps or videos where an ad appears).
  • Google review extension (extra information shown under an ad, such as reviews): exclusions for reviews are not tested in the material. Turn off the Google review extension if it appears, and ask Google Ads live chat to confirm the setting.

Exclusions: Placement Exclusions

1 min
  • Account-level placement exclusions: open the display network placements section (the Display Network is Google's network of websites and apps) and add exclusions for sites or apps that do not convert (turn visitors into sales or sign-ups), such as app traffic. The exclusion applies to the whole account (your entire Google Ads login), not one campaign.
  • Excluding unwanted placements in a display remarketing campaign:
    1. Open the Google Ads account and go to Display campaigns.
    2. Open the current remarketing campaign.
    3. Go to Insights and reports > Where ads showed.
    4. Sort the placements by Cost to see where the most money goes.
    5. Tick (check) the checkbox next to each placement you do not want, then choose the exclude action.
    6. Check the placement exclusions under Audiences, keywords and content > Content > Placement exclusions to confirm them.
  • The placement exclusion view lists all apps and websites excluded from the display campaign. After excluding a placement, a small black bar at the bottom of the screen confirms how many placements were excluded. With many placements at once, that message can take a while to appear.

Exclusions: App Placement Exclusions (information from 2021-2022, older)

4 min
  • Display ads can run inside free mobile and tablet apps (mobile apps), usually as a small banner (a thin strip ad) along the bottom of the screen. That spot sits close to the phone's own navigation controls (the buttons used to move around), so many clicks there are accidental.
  • App placements often show a high click-through rate (CTR, the share of people who click after seeing an ad) that is largely accidental. This spends budget without bringing in genuinely interested visitors, so excluding app placements from display campaigns is worth doing.
  • Google Ads does not let you exclude apps during display campaign setup. The exclusion must be built beforehand as a placement exclusion list (a saved list of places to block), then applied to the campaign.
  • Excluding app placements matters most for cold traffic, lead generation (collecting contact details from people) and content promotion on the display network. For retargeting it matters less because the traffic volume is much lower, but it can still be applied.
  • Placement exclusion lists are found at Tools and Settings > Shared Library (saved items you can reuse across campaigns) > Placement exclusion lists. Click the blue button at the top to create a new list.
  • A suitable name for the list is Display Network App Placement Exclusions. Once saved, it appears in the Placement exclusion lists table of the shared library.
  • Create the app exclusion list:
    1. Open Tools and Settings in the Google Ads account.
    2. Under Shared Library, click Placement exclusion lists.
    3. Click the blue button at the top to create a new list.
    4. Enter a list name, for example Display Network App Placement Exclusions.
    5. In the placement selector, open the App categories option (groups of similar apps). The selector offers YouTube channels, YouTube videos, Websites, Apps and App categories (140). Individual apps can be typed into the search field, but to exclude all apps use the categories.
    6. Expand (open up) Apple App Store (84) and tick every category, including the subcategories under Games (19) such as Action, Arcade and Board. Visible Apple categories include Book, Business, Catalogs, Education, Entertainment, Food & Drink, Finance, Music, Navigation, News, Photo & Video, Productivity and Reference.
    7. Expand Google Play (56) and tick every category, including Places & Objects, Social, Sports, Sports & Activities, Tools, Travel, Travel & Local, Utilities, Video Players & Editors and Weather.
    8. Check that the counter shows 140 selected, which is 84 Apple categories plus 56 Google Play categories.
    9. Click Save.
    10. Confirm that the new list appears in the placement exclusion lists table.
  • Every app category checkbox must be ticked by hand, because there is no select-all option. Ticking a whole category excludes all apps inside it, so there is no need to go through individual apps.
  • The App categories list contains 140 categories in total: 84 under Apple App Store and 56 under Google Play. All 140 must be selected to exclude all apps.
  • Individual app names can be added through the search field if only specific apps should be excluded. App suggestions only appear after a search term has been entered.
  • Placement exclusion lists sit in the shared library, so one list can be applied to several display campaigns. The same list can also be used in YouTube campaigns (ads shown on YouTube).
  • Excluding app categories pushes Google to show display ads mostly on websites. Visitors then arrive from a website, instead of being pulled out of a game or other app by an accidental click.
  • Apply the list to campaigns:
    1. Select the checkbox of the new placement exclusion list in the table.
    2. Click Apply to campaigns.
    3. Select the display campaigns the list should apply to, including both retargeting and cold traffic campaigns, and confirm.
  • Apply the list to one campaign:
    1. Open the display campaign, for example a retargeting campaign.
    2. Open the Placements menu and go to Exclusions.
    3. Under Exclude placements, choose Use placement exclusion list.
    4. In the field Search placement exclusions lists, select the list. The field shows how many lists are selected.
    5. Click Save.
  • The campaign-level (inside one single campaign) Exclusions page has an "Exclude placements on Display Network and YouTube" section with a Use placement exclusion list option. A list selected there applies to both the Display Network and YouTube placements of that campaign.
  • Creating the list does nothing by itself. It has to be applied to every display campaign that runs ads, otherwise apps remain eligible (allowed to show your ads) for those campaigns.
  • After the list is applied, the campaign's Exclusions view lists the excluded mobile applications with the type Mobile application and the level Ad Group. Entries show the excluded apps by name and store, such as iTunes App Store.
  • Display ads inside mobile apps usually appear in the free versions of those apps. Users who pay to upgrade often get an ad-free version, so ads in apps mostly reach users who have not paid.
  • For products that are themselves related to an app, a different approach may make sense. High-ticket products (expensive products) generally do not need app placements excluded this way.
  • Demo example: a retargeting display campaign with a budget of US$1,500 per day and an enhanced default max CPC (a click-price setting that Google can adjust) of US$0.20. These figures only illustrate the example and are not benchmarks (standard numbers to compare against).

Static and Promotional Remarketing

1 min
  • Use static remarketing ads (fixed ads, not linked to the product feed; the product feed is your product list sent to Google) to run promotional remarketing when dynamic product-feed remarketing (ads that automatically show each person the products they looked at) is not possible or needed.
  • Monthly promotions run across all channels (places you advertise) at once: Google Shopping, Display Network, Search, Video, Discovery (a Google ad format), Email and Meta (the company behind Facebook and Instagram ads). This keeps the offer top-of-mind (easy to remember) and gives warm audiences several touchpoints (moments when people meet your brand) to see and act on a limited-time offer.
  • A specific offer can also run as a static Google Display Network banner with the same deal message and a Shop Now call to action (the button text that tells people what to do), shown based on the person's journey stage (where they are on the path to buying).
  • Holiday promotions on Google:
    • Use static Display ads (not dynamic display ads) and static Demand Gen ads.
    • Audience: all website visitors in the past 180 days.
    • Exclusion: all customers who purchased in the past 30 days.
    • The ads should use the same message as the emails.
  • Promotion Google ads should match the email message and be shown to all website visitors from the last 180 days, excluding customers from the last 30 days. Use Display ads and Demand Gen ads for this audience.
  • Running both Meta and Google accounts with remarketing audiences gives more reach for holiday promotions. Having both is not mandatory, but it increases traction (strong results and momentum) considerably.
  • Google Display remarketing for promotions: the Display - WARM - Monthly Promotions campaign is the standard approach.
  • The same Google remarketing setup can run while a policy violation warning (a notice that you broke a Google rule) is active. Reach is limited, but the ads still show.

Troubleshooting: No Impressions: Checks in Order

2 min
  1. Go to Google Ads > Tools > Shared Library > Audience manager (the place where your audience lists are kept).
  2. Check whether the audience is marked as Eligible (ready to show ads).
  3. If it is not eligible, go to Google Analytics 4 (Google's website tracking tool) > Admin (gear) > Data Collection.
  4. Ensure all data collection toggles (on/off switches) are ON.
  5. If the audience is eligible in Audience Manager, delete the remarketing campaign and recreate it from scratch.
  6. Give it a few days to accumulate impressions (collect ad views).
  7. If it still does not work, post in the Discord community (an online chat group) or contact support with screenshots.
  • Eligible audiences can run remarketing ads. If an audience is not eligible, check Google Analytics settings.
  • If an audience says "too small to serve", it does not have enough users yet. If the audience is not labeled too small and the campaign still does not run, recreate the campaign.
  • A remarketing campaign only starts receiving impressions once its audience shows as eligible. If still not eligible after a day, pause and recreate it.
  • Creating a 7-day remarketing campaign in a new store can produce no impressions, because the audience is too small. Start with 30 days.
  • Policy disapprovals: Google Merchant Center (Google's product data tool) can disapprove (reject) products for dynamic remarketing when ads target personalized advertising around health content or are built to exploit customers' struggles or difficulties. Such products can still be retargeted on Meta.
  • Some niches (narrow market areas) may not be allowed to run Google remarketing due to Google policy. In that case contact Google support to check whether an extra terms form (a form to accept extra rules) or step is needed.
  • Low results are not yet a verdict. A remarketing display campaign that ran three months with 123 clicks at an average CPC of $3.50, CTR 0.11%, zero conversions and total spend of roughly $400-450 was too small to judge. Some clicks are repeat visitors, so the unique audience (the number of different people) may be under 100 people. It needed more time, and the CPC needed to come down.
  • With only 65 clicks over two months, the sample (the set of data you judge from) is too small to judge conversions. The priority at that level is to increase traffic (more impressions and higher CTR), not to optimize who the ads target.
  • Old guidance on dynamic remarketing was updated in 2024 because Google changed how dynamic remarketing works, not because the method stopped working. A paused legacy campaign (an older campaign type, stopped for now) may give different results or show an error message when re-enabled.
  • Excluding placements by name requires a current view of the campaign. Earlier advice on review placements remains untested.

Audience Creation Pitfalls

1 min
  • Create dynamic Google audiences early, before customers exist. They fill as people take the action, so a 180-day visitor audience or a 30-day purchase audience can be empty on day zero (the first day).
  • Creating a remarketing list and copying a search campaign with an added audience took about 19 to 20 seconds in Google Ads Editor (a desktop tool for editing many ads at once) in the demonstration.
  • Google Shopping ads convert best when they reach people who already engaged through an earlier touchpoint.

Performance Expectations and Strategy

1 min
  • Remarketing campaigns aimed at your best customers should exceed 10X ROAS (return on ad spend: 10X means $10 of sales for every $1 spent) within the first two to five days, because those buyers already know the brand. Higher ROAS is better, and there is no fixed upper limit.
  • Display ads should be remarketed to recent visitors. For the hottest audience (the group most likely to buy), such as seven-day website visitors who did not buy, expect a click-through rate around 5 percent. Broader audiences often reach around 1 percent. Responsive display ads (Google ads that adjust themselves to fit each space) have performed well.
  • Google works as a strong retargeting platform because people search the brand after seeing a Facebook ad. The seller reports about 50% of Google visitors search the brand after a Facebook ad.
  • Prospects (possible buyers) who joined an email list with a coupon convert more often on Shopping ads, because the earlier brand contact and incentive (reward) build trust. Use email signup offers as a warm-up step (a step that prepares people) before Shopping campaigns.
  • Shopping ads convert best when they target people who have already engaged with the brand through an email signup, a guide download or a coupon. Use these warmed prospects as the audience for Shopping campaigns, and keep cold traffic on upper-funnel channels (early-stage channels that make people aware of you).
  • Excluding placements and audiences is a cost-control step; it does not replace growing the audience.

Customer Match and Audience Lists

1 min
  • Customer Match lets you upload a list of your customers' email addresses to Google. You can then show ads to those people or to lookalike audiences (people who resemble your customers).
  • Customer lists from previous customers can be uploaded as audiences on Facebook and Google. These audiences typically perform better than cold campaigns.

Search and Shopping Retargeting: Search Audiences

1 min
  • Search audience campaigns are an underused tactic. Fewer than 10 percent of advertisers ran them in AdWords (the old name of Google Ads) and Bing Ads (Microsoft's ad platform). They allow targeting on Google search by audience lists and demographics (age, gender and similar groups), not only by keywords (the words people type into search).
  • Google's ad preferences page (google.com/ads/preferences) shows the gender, age range and interests Google has inferred (guessed) from a user's activity, even without profile data (details the person typed in). Check it to see what Google expects about your audience.
  • Google search campaigns can be targeted by audience in two main ways: retargeting lists built from past site visitors, and demographic groups such as gender, age range and household income. Layering (adding) these onto search campaigns allows bid adjustments by segment (paying more or less for each group), which goes beyond keyword targeting alone.

Search and Shopping Retargeting: Retargeting Shopping Campaign

2 min
  1. Create a new Shopping campaign with the same steps as a standard Shopping campaign: select merchant and goal.
  2. Give it a clear name, for example "Retargeting on search Shopping".
  3. Set bidding to Manual CPC.
  4. Set the budget and delivery method.
  5. Set campaign priority to High so it is checked first.
  6. Skip the alpha-beta structure (a setup that splits products into a proven group and a test group) and use one campaign for the whole retargeting audience.
  7. Include the Search Network (the Google search results and partner sites where search ads show) and show ads on all devices.
  8. Set the location to the United States.
  9. Add an ad group name and a starting bid.
  10. Open the Audiences section, apply the audience to the campaign, and choose Targeting.
  11. Select the 540-day retargeting list.
  12. Make the starting bids in the waterfall more aggressive (the waterfall is the order in which campaigns are checked) than in normal campaigns so retargeting traffic is funneled into this campaign.
  • Campaign settings: Bidding = Manual CPC; Campaign priority = High; Networks = Search Network included; Devices = all devices; Location = United States; Audiences: Apply to campaign > Targeting > 540-day retargeting list for search.
  • Set campaign priority to High for a retargeting Shopping campaign so it is evaluated before other campaigns that may target the same searches.
  • In the experience behind this method, an alpha-beta split is not needed when monthly spend is below 10,000 USD. A single retargeting campaign can serve the whole audience at that budget level.
  • The retargeting campaign should be set to Targeting, not Observation. Targeting narrows delivery to the selected audience and allows bid adjustments. Observation (it only reports on the audience and does not limit who sees the ads) keeps the broad reach.
  • Choosing Observation instead of Targeting means the campaign still reaches all searchers, so the retargeting list does not limit spend. Always apply the audience as Targeting when the campaign should serve only that audience.

Cross-Platform Notes

1 min
  • Remarketing, also called retargeting, means showing ads again to people who already visited your store. A campaign is one set of ads with its own budget and settings. Facebook remarketing campaigns can be set up right after the first Google campaign is live. They are small-budget campaigns that boost your Google Shopping results. Google Shopping shows product photos and prices in search. Bing (Microsoft's search engine) can be added later.
  • Match the Google video to the Facebook thank-you video, so the message is the same on both platforms. A thank-you video is a video shown on a thank-you page.

Setting up audiences in GA4 and Google Ads

3 min

An audience is a saved list of people, such as past visitors. GA4 (Google Analytics 4) is Google's tool for tracking what visitors do on your site. Google Ads is Google's advertising tool.

Use this order when you connect GA4 to Google Ads for the first time:

  1. Link Google Ads to GA4 under Admin > Product links > Google Ad Links > Link. Product links are the connection settings between Google tools.
  2. Turn on all options under Data settings > Data collection, and accept the user data collection acknowledgement. This acknowledgement confirms you may collect visitor data.
  3. Change Data settings > Data retention > Event data retention from 2 months to 14 months, then click Save. Event data retention is how long Google keeps visitor actions, such as clicks and page views.
  4. Create the three non-purchaser audiences (website visitors who exclude customers, 7, 30 and 180 days) under Audiences. A non-purchaser is someone who visited but did not buy.

The Google Ads remarketing tag and the Google Analytics tag both need to be installed. A tag is a small piece of code on your website that sends visitor data to Google. Add both as audience sources. Google shows a warning when you enable the second source. Confirm it, because the two tags feed different audiences.

The Google Ads remarketing tag builds audiences only from pages a visitor viewed. Google Analytics can also segment by session duration, technology, location and other behaviour. Segmenting means splitting visitors into groups. A session is one visit to your site, and session duration is how long that visit lasted. This makes Google Analytics the main audience source. The Google Ads tag still helps with dynamic product ads, where it is easier to set up. Dynamic product ads show the exact products a visitor looked at.

Google Analytics Audience Builder, the GA4 screen for creating audiences, offers these options:

  • Traffic Sources: filter by Campaign, Medium, Source or Keyword. These labels show where a visitor came from. They work with UTM parameters, which are small tags added to your links. This lets you target visitors from one specific ad campaign, such as a bottom-of-funnel shopping campaign. Bottom-of-funnel means shoppers who are close to buying.
  • Advanced Google Ads conditions: Ad Content (what the ad showed), a specific Google Ads campaign and ad group, Targeting Type (how the ad was aimed), and Ad Slot (where the ad appeared). Sequences are advanced and not needed for basic audiences. A sequence is a set of steps a visitor must take in order.
  • Audiences built in Google Analytics appear in Google Ads as remarketing lists for Search Ads and the Google Display Network. Search Ads are text ads on Google search results. The Google Display Network is a group of websites and apps where Google shows image and video ads. A remarketing list is a saved audience used for remarketing. These audiences show up as targeting options only after the Google Ads account is selected as an audience destination. An audience destination is the place an audience is sent to.

Audience rule example: the rule Visitors of a page who did not visit another page builds a list of people who viewed a product page but never reached the checkout page. Checkout is the page where people pay. You can limit this rule to chosen dates or tags. Tags here are labels used to group pages. This is a narrower alternative to a plain page-visit audience when the goal is to target shoppers who stalled before buying.

Audience windows and minimum sizes

1 min

A window is the number of past days an audience covers.

Google Ads remarketing audiences need at least 100 unique people before ads start serving. Serving means ads are being shown. Unique people means each person counts once, so repeat visitors count only once. Check eligibility in Google Ads under shared audiences by opening the audiences you created. Shared audiences are audiences kept in the Google Ads library. Check Meta Business Manager under Audiences. Meta Business Manager is Facebook's tool for managing ads.

Example audience sizes for reference: a website visitor list of about 1,000 to 1,500 people, built from all site traffic in the last 30 days, and a retargeting list of about 7,100 people used for search retargeting. Actual sizes depend on your site traffic.

Recommended windows by stage:

Remarketing audience Window
All website visitors, 90 days Start here if traffic is low
All website visitors, 180 days Use if the 90-day audience is still too small
Visitors, 7 days Build once traffic is high
Visitors, 30 days Build once traffic is high
Visitors, 60 days Build once traffic is high
Visitors, 90 days Build once traffic is high

Window procedure:

  1. Create a remarketing audience of all website visitors covering 90 days.
  2. Widen the window to 180 days if Google says the audience is too small.
  3. As traffic grows, create more recent audiences with 7, 30, 60 and 90-day windows, and adjust spend accordingly.

Shopping remarketing structure

2 min

A bid is the amount you offer for one click. Retargeting bids should be higher than normal bids for the same audience. This lets the retargeting campaign win the auction for returning searchers. An auction is the instant contest where Google picks which ads show. Lower bids in normal campaigns keep that traffic from triggering there.

The alpha-beta structure is a way of organizing Shopping campaigns into groups. It first filters the search population to past site visitors, then to those searching again, and only then splits that group further by performance. The search population means everyone searching for your products. Performance means how well each group sells. At monthly Shopping spend below 10,000 USD, this extra split is usually unnecessary, so one retargeting campaign can serve the full retargeting list.

Keep retargeted visitors out of normal Shopping campaigns (procedure):

  1. Go to the normal Shopping campaign (for example, the alpha-beta campaign).
  2. Open the Audiences section and add an audience to the ad group. An ad group is a set of ads inside a campaign that share the same audience and settings.
  3. Select the ad group and the campaign, then select the 540-day retargeting list. This is a list of past visitors from the last 540 days.
  4. Set the bid adjustment to minus 100%. A bid adjustment changes your bid by a set percentage for one audience.
  5. Save. Retargeted visitors then get a near-zero bid in normal campaigns and the higher bid in the retargeting campaign.

Setting path: Normal Shopping campaign > Ad group > Audiences > add the 540-day retargeting list > Bid adjustment: -100%.

A -100% bid adjustment on the retargeting audience in normal Shopping campaigns is the method for keeping that traffic out of them, so the retargeting campaign receives it.

Once a website visitor audience is applied to a Shopping campaign with Targeting selected, the audience appears in the campaign's audience list. Targeting is the setting that limits who sees the ads. The bid adjustment is set there, directly below the audience entry. Observation mode only adds reporting for that audience. It does not change bids or limit reach. Reach is how many people see your ads.

Free traffic from Google product listings and organic results can be used alongside a small remarketing budget. Free product listings are unpaid product pages in Google Shopping. Organic results are unpaid search results. This way paid spend goes to visitors who already found the store.

Display retargeting campaign setup

2 min

Static ads are fixed image files you design and upload yourself.

Standard display retargeting campaign with static ads:

  1. Open Campaigns in Google Ads, go to the Display campaigns view, and click to add a new campaign.
  2. Choose Create a campaign without a goal's guidance. This skips Google's goal-based setup help.
  3. Select campaign type Display (use Video only for YouTube retargeting). Video campaigns run on YouTube.
  4. Select subtype Standard display campaign.
  5. Skip the website URL step and click Continue.
  6. Name the campaign, then set locations and language.
  7. Choose Manually set bids under bidding, and pay for Clicks, with Enhanced CPC turned off. Manually set bids means you set the price yourself. CPC means cost per click. Enhanced CPC means Google changes your bids automatically.
  8. Set the daily budget (test budget: 20 USD). Daily budget is the most you plan to spend each day.
  9. Set the delivery method to Standard. Delivery method controls how your spend is spread across the day.
  10. Choose Do not optimize: Rotate ads indefinitely under ad rotation. Ad rotation decides how the ads take turns showing.
  11. Set a limit of 3 impressions per day per ad group under frequency capping. An impression is one showing of an ad. Frequency capping limits how often one person sees an ad.
  12. Leave dynamic ads without a data feed, and keep the account-level conversions setting. A data feed is a product list file. Conversions are actions you count as success, such as sales.
  13. Set content exclusions as needed for brand safety. Brand safety means keeping ads away from unsafe content.
  14. Create the ad group: give it a name, add the audience Product Viewers 14 Days, turn targeting expansion Off, and set the ad group bid to 0.20 USD CPC.
  15. Create the ads: choose Upload display ads (not responsive), enter the final URL, and upload the image files. The final URL is the page the ad sends people to. Responsive ads are ads Google builds from parts you give it.
  16. Click Create campaign.
  17. Open the new campaign, go to Audiences, then the Exclusions tab, and add an exclusion for Product Viewers 7 Days at ad group level. An exclusion blocks an audience from seeing the ads. Save.

You can skip creating an ad during campaign setup and add one later. The campaign will not run until it has at least one ad.

Bidding, budget and delivery

1 min
  • Bidding for a new retargeting test: Settings > Campaign > Bidding. Choose Manually set bids, pay for Clicks, with Enhanced CPC turned off. Audience-based bidding on conversions is not recommended at low volume. Low volume means few sales or actions.
  • The starting ad group bid for Google display retargeting is 0.20 USD CPC, kept low at the start. Manual CPC bids are the starting point for new campaigns.
  • For a new Google Ads display campaign, set Delivery method to Standard rather than Accelerated. Accelerated spends the budget as fast as possible. Monthly spend cannot exceed the daily budget multiplied by the average number of days in a month, although individual days may spend slightly more or less.
  • When Smart Bidding is active, Google Ads optimizes ad rotation toward the best-performing ads, even if rotation is set to rotate indefinitely. Smart Bidding means Google sets your bids by itself. The rotate-indefinitely setting only keeps ads evenly rotated in campaigns that use manual bidding.

Static ad creative and split testing

1 min

Split testing means comparing ads to see which one works better. Creative means the ad's design and content.

  • Insert a real review text and star rating directly into static Google Display ads. A review text is a real customer comment. A star rating is the score a customer gave.
  • Use image ads for the display set. Text-only display ads tend to underperform image ads for retargeting.
  • Standard uploaded display ads are the recommended base. Responsive display ads let Google decide formats, placements and sizes. Placements are the websites and spots where ads appear. Responsive ads can include text-only and weaker ad sizes, so you have less control over the result. Responsive ads can be tested as an optional extra.
  • Ad rotation for split tests: choose Do not optimize: Rotate ads indefinitely. Avoid Optimize: Prefer best performing ads. Google picks a winner very early using data that is not statistically meaningful. Statistically meaningful means the sample is big enough to trust.

Split test procedure on static ads (rotation set to rotate indefinitely):

  1. Let the ads run until each one gets a large, even number of impressions.
  2. Compare the results once the sample is large enough.
  3. Pick the best-performing ad, and replace the weaker ones as needed.

Frequency capping

1 min

Under Settings > Campaign > Additional settings > Frequency capping, you can limit impressions for the whole campaign, per ad, or per ad group. For retargeting, the test limit is 3 impressions per day per ad group.

Exclusions

1 min

Content exclusions

Setting path: Settings > Campaign > Content exclusions. Content exclusions stop your ads from showing next to certain content.

  • Digital content labels: DL-G General audiences, DL-PG Most audiences with parental guidance, DL-MA Mature audiences, and Content not yet labeled. Digital content labels are Google's ratings for how mature content is.
  • Sensitive content categories: Tragedy and conflict, Sensitive social issues, Profanity and rough language, Sexually suggestive, and Sensational and shocking. These are topics you can block.
  • Content types that can be excluded: Games, Inactive, Live streaming YouTube video, Embedded videos, Below-the-fold, Mobile app non-interstitial, Parked domains and In-video. Below-the-fold means content lower on a page, out of view. Embedded videos are videos placed on other sites. Mobile app non-interstitial means ads inside apps that are not full-screen. Parked domains are web addresses with no real website. In-video means ads inside a video. Google states that exclusions are applied as well as possible but may not catch all related content.

Audience exclusions

Audience exclusions in Google Ads can be applied at campaign level or ad group level. Campaign level covers the whole campaign. Ad group level covers one group of ads inside it. Use the ad group level when different ad groups in the same campaign target different audiences, because a campaign-level exclusion applies to all of them.

Targeting expansion

1 min

Keep targeting expansion Off at ad group level. Targeting expansion lets Google show ads to users similar to your audience. This ensures the ad group reaches only the selected audience. Otherwise, Google may expand reach to similar users, which defeats retargeting. Defeats means ruins the purpose.

Search remarketing

1 min

Google search campaigns accept audience and demographic targeting, similar to Facebook targeting. Demographic targeting means aiming ads by traits such as age and gender. You can narrow who sees a search ad beyond the keywords. Keywords are the words people type into search. Audience-based search ad clicks were reported to have grown 34% year over year in 2018. Year over year means compared with the same period one year earlier.

Remarketing lists for search ads is an advanced Google Ads option that applies retargeting audiences to search campaigns. It is not needed for a standard display retargeting setup. It is usually added after display campaigns are running.

Troubleshooting: no impressions and narrow audiences

1 min
  • If remarketing shows zero impressions after the audiences are built, check the audience size first. Raising bids is a secondary option if the audience is eligible but still not serving.
  • Google's weekly estimates can show "Estimates aren't available because your targeting is too narrow" for small retargeting audiences. Weekly estimates are Google's guesses of reach each week. This message is normal for narrow lists. It does not by itself mean the campaign is broken.

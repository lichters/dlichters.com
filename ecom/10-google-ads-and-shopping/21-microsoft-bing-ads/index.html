Microsoft/Bing Ads

Section 10.21 · 21 of 23 in this chapter · 24 min read

In short

  • Bing ads (Microsoft Advertising) are a second ad platform. Add them after Google makes a profit.
  • Before importing Google campaigns, put your products in the Merchant Center (Microsoft's product list store).
  • When you import from Google, cut the budget by 80% first, then check that Bing shows ads.
  • Use the same email as Google Ads, and choose post-pay (you pay after ads run).
  • Start Bing ads small: about 25% of your Google budget. Add more only when the numbers show profit.
More key points
  • Bing often shuts new accounts for vague reasons. This is common, so appeal it and wait for a person to review it.
  • If Bing spends money but makes no sales, check the location and other settings before adding budget.
  • Keep Bing paused until Google Shopping makes a profit.

Role of Microsoft Ads in the account

2 min
  • Microsoft Advertising, formerly Bing Ads, is Microsoft's paid search platform (a service where you pay to show ads in search results). It is an alternative to Google Ads (Google's own paid ad platform). Its ads mainly appear on bing.com.
  • About one in three web searches happens on Bing. So it is a sizeable extra source of visitors next to a Google Ads account.
  • Microsoft Ads is an advanced paid search channel (one place where ads run). Add it later. It is not part of the core setup. It only makes sense once Google Ads is maxed out (pushed to its full potential).
  • Pause Bing Ads until Google Shopping (product ads on Google) is profitable. Then move the proven campaigns over to Bing.
  • Microsoft and Bing ads should not take part of the starting budget (the money you set aside at first). Add them once campaigns are profitable and profits are being reinvested (put back into ads).
  • Bing produces real sales with a good return on ad spend (ROAS: money earned for each money spent on ads). But it is weaker than Google. Focus on Google first and add Bing once Google performs.
  • Much of Bing's search volume (number of searches) likely comes from people who kept the default search engine preinstalled on a new PC and never changed it. Paid reach on Bing (how many people see your ads) is therefore smaller but still profitable.
  • Bing works almost like a copy of Google Ads. Account structure (how the account is organized), campaign logic and optimization habits (ways to improve results) transfer directly. So little new learning is needed.
  • A product feed (your product list sent to the ad platform), conversion tracking (counting sales from ads) and campaigns move to Bing as one set with the same logic. The existing Google setup does not need to be rebuilt from zero.
  • Google and Microsoft do not share data. Past results on one platform do not show what Google will do.
  • If an account that sold well on one platform gets very few conversions (sales) on Google from thousands of clicks, the setup or traffic type is likely wrong.

Account setup

1 min

Sign-up (detailed route, ads.microsoft.com)

1 min
  1. Open ads.microsoft.com and click Sign up now. If you already have an account, log in. If not, click the option to create one.
  2. Enter an email address. Use the same email address as your Google Ads account.
  3. Set up the account, then click switch to expert mode, as in the Google Ads setup.
  4. Enter your business information. Set the primary use of the account to promote this business (your own business).
  5. Accept the Terms and Conditions (the rules you agree to; a link to read them is provided) and click create account.
  6. On the next screen, do not import from Google yet. Click skip campaign creation. It sits at the top of the page.
  7. Enter your business location (address) and tax information, then enter your billing information.
  8. In the billing options, choose Post-pay. Post-pay means you pay after you have spent money on ads.
  9. Select Credit or debit card as the payment method and enter your card details.
  10. Confirm. The new account screen opens. It is empty at this stage, which is expected.

Sign-up (short route)

1 min
  1. Search for Bing Ads and go directly to the Bing Ads site.
  2. Click to sign up and create an account.
  3. Verify the email address, then complete the captcha (a check that proves you are human).
  4. Enter the company name.
  5. Enter a business phone number.
  6. Select the option to manage your own accounts.

Setup rules

1 min
  • Use the same email address for Microsoft Advertising as for Google Ads, so both accounts stay linked to one identity (one owner profile).
  • Entering business address, tax and billing details does not charge anything, because no ads are running yet.
  • Microsoft Advertising offers Post-pay or Prepay (paying before ads run). Choose Post-pay as described above.
  • The Bing Ads campaign screens can still look like the old AdWords layout (Google's old ad system look) after an import. This is cosmetic (only how it looks) and does not mean the import failed.
  • Bing may ask for a credit card during account setup. It can be skipped for now while the imported campaigns are still set up.
  • Setup order: sign up for Bing Ads, Bing Webmaster Tools (Bing's tool for website owners) and Merchant Center (Microsoft's product list store), then create the first Bing Shopping campaign (product ads on Bing).
  • Bing Webmaster Tools and Merchant Center are set up alongside Bing Ads. Merchant Center supplies the product data that Bing Shopping campaigns use.
  • Next step after the account exists: create the Microsoft Merchant Center account. Products are submitted there before any Google Ads campaigns are imported.

Sign-up import option: sources differ

1 min
  • Source A says that during Bing account setup you should use the import-from-Google option so the existing Google campaigns appear inside the Bing account.
  • Source B says to skip campaign creation at first setup, because importing from Google requires logging in to Google and only works correctly after Microsoft Merchant Center is set up and products are submitted.
  • When each applies: use Source B if Merchant Center is not yet set up. Use Source A's import option once Merchant Center is in place and you are importing.

Vouchers and credits

1 min
  • Microsoft Advertising sometimes offers voucher codes (discount codes) that give new accounts ad credits (free ad money). Availability changes, so search for current Microsoft advertising vouchers on bing.com.
  • Microsoft Advertising offered a $100 credit for new accounts. Search for the current version of the offer, since the URLs (web addresses) change often.

Microsoft Merchant Center

1 min

What it is

1 min
  • Microsoft Merchant Center is the Microsoft counterpart to Google Merchant Center. It holds store information and the product feed used by Microsoft Shopping ads on Bing.
  • Microsoft Merchant Center is where products must be set up and submitted before importing Google Ads campaigns into Microsoft Advertising for Shopping.
  • The Merchant Center setup follows the same logic as Google Merchant Center: store details and shipping first, products after. The product feed is only submitted once the account is complete.
  • A Microsoft Advertising account must exist first, before a Merchant Center store can be created, because the Merchant Center link is opened from inside that account. The new store is attached to the main Microsoft Advertising account.

Store creation procedure

1 min
  1. Log in to the Microsoft Advertising account and click the Merchant Center link in the header menu (the top menu bar).
  2. On the empty welcome screen (no stores yet), click Create store.
  3. Enter the store name.
  4. For domain validation (proof that you own your web address), select the Bing Webmaster Tools account that already holds the store's destination URL (the web address customers land on).
  5. Add a store description.
  6. Answer yes to secure checkout (a protected payment page) and click Next.
  7. Select Block for aggregators (sites that collect offers from many shops) and click Next.
  8. Choose the language and enter the store's contact email.
  9. Answer no to best-practice emails if the main account email already receives them, then click Create store.
  10. Open the store's settings from the main account, go to shipping settings, turn on free shipping, enter a minimum purchase price (0 if there is none), and click Submit. Products are not uploaded in this step.

Store settings details

1 min
  • The store description works like a short About Us page (a page that tells visitors who you are). It tells shoppers what the store is and who runs it. The field shows a remaining character count (how many letters are left). A real business should use more of it to explain what it sells and what makes it different.
  • Turn on secure checkout when creating the store.
  • Aggregators collect product offers from many, often unrelated, businesses. By default they can include your product feed in their ads. Blocking aggregators keeps your products out of their Bing ads.
  • Block aggregators during store setup. The extra explanation is available by hovering your mouse over the question mark next to the option.
  • Use a dedicated support email address on the store's own domain (your own web address) as the contact email.
  • Set the minimum purchase price for free shipping to 0 if there is no minimum order value. Free shipping is switched on in the store's shipping settings.
  • Do not upload products during Merchant Center setup. The account must be complete first, and the feed is submitted afterwards.
  • Right after the store is created, it appears under the main account with no items listed. The list stays empty until a feed is submitted, so an empty store at this stage is normal.

Product feed for Bing

1 min
  • Sources differ on whether the Bing merchant account carries over from Google. Source A says it does not and must be created again from scratch, because assuming it comes across leaves Shopping ads without a working feed.
  • Source B says one feed app (an add-on for your online shop that builds the product list) connection can supply products to both Google and Bing. The connection screen lets you pick an existing Merchant Center store or create a new one from inside the app.
  • When each applies: if you have no Microsoft Merchant Center store, create one. If you do, pick it in the app's connection screen.
  • Before connecting a Shopify store (an online shop platform) to Bing Shopping through the feed app, a Microsoft Advertising account and a Merchant Center store should already exist.
  • Feed procedure:
    1. Use the same process that was used to create the Google Shopping feed.
    2. Open a Shopify app that builds the feed inside the store. The same feed app serves Google Shopping and Bing Shopping.
    3. Log in to the app and set up the feed.
    4. In the app, go to Settings > Bing Shopping Settings. The only option that needs changing is the sale price with price checkbox (a box you tick), which must be checked. Leave the other Bing Shopping options at their defaults (the standard settings).
    5. Sync the feed with Bing Ads (make both copies match).
    6. Check that the product feed is now available in Bing Ads.
  • After a successful import, products show the status pending (waiting for review). This alone is not an error. It confirms that the feed was received.

Store verification

1 min

Procedure for verifying the store for Bing (proving the store is yours):

  1. When Bing asks for the store name, first set up the store in Bing Webmaster Tools.
  2. Return to Bing Ads once the store is set up there. The remaining fields fill in automatically.
  3. When Bing asks for the product feed, select the feed that was already created.
  • Domain validation in Merchant Center uses the Bing Webmaster Tools account that already holds the destination URL.
  • Bing Webmaster Tools and Microsoft Merchant Center support the Bing Shopping campaign, which runs alongside Google.
  • Bing Webmaster Tools also shows organic Bing search terms (words people type to find you without paid ads).

Import from Google Ads

1 min

Why import, and what not to do

1 min
  • Existing Google Ads campaigns can be imported into Microsoft Advertising. This takes much less work than building them from scratch. Budgets (the money you allow for ads) are then set lower than on Google.
  • Do not use Auto Import (a setting that copies everything automatically) when importing campaigns from Google Ads to Microsoft Advertising. Auto Import copies everything in your Google account and becomes unmanageable (too messy to control) once you have many campaigns.
  • Use Import Specific Campaigns and Ad Groups to choose exactly which campaigns to copy. Ad groups are the smaller sets inside a campaign.
  • Sources differ on ongoing sync. Source A says Auto Import should not be used (above). Source B says below about $5,000 monthly ad spend, export the Google Ads campaigns straight into Bing Ads, because later changes in Google Ads are then updated automatically in Bing Ads.
  • When each applies: the import settings in the procedure below cover the choice of campaigns. Source B's automatic updates depend on apply updates to existing items being checked (the import option in step 12). The sources do not give a threshold for Auto Import itself.
  • Above about $5,000 monthly ad spend, do the initial import and then optimize (improve) Bing Ads using its own performance data. Repeated imports from Google Ads become less useful once Bing has enough data of its own.
  • Example signal that Bing has enough own data: a Bing conversion rate (share of clicks that become sales) of around 1% combined with regularly around 100 clicks. Then optimizing on Bing's own numbers can replace constant importing from Google Ads.

Import procedure

1 min
  1. Log in to Microsoft Advertising.
  2. Click Import in the header menu.
  3. Select Import from Google Ads.
  4. Sign in to the Google account linked to your Google Ads account.
  5. Click Continue.
  6. Change Schedule from Auto to Once and set the time to Right Now (your timezone).
  7. Click Customize Import.
  8. Select Import Specific Campaigns and Ad Groups, not import all existing and new campaigns.
  9. Search for and select the campaigns you want to copy (for example, your Alpha and Beta campaigns).
  10. Click Next.
  11. Uncheck account level URL options.
  12. Check the three sync options: sync items not previously added to your Microsoft account, apply updates to existing items, and remove items from Microsoft Advertising if they were deleted from Google Ads.
  13. Select your UET tag (a small code that counts sales) from the Google Shopping app, so conversion data from Bing Shopping feeds into your Microsoft Advertising account.
  14. Uncheck the bid (the price you offer per click) and budget minimums options (Increase any of our bids to reach minimums for Microsoft Advertising, Update Bid Strategies, Increase Campaign Budgets to the minimum).
  15. Select Customize Budgets for Microsoft Advertising and reduce by 80%.
  16. Click Next.
  17. Name the import (for example, Alpha Beta Import).
  18. Set it to email on errors.
  19. Click Run Import Now.
  20. Wait for product feed approval in Microsoft Merchant Center. It may show pending (waiting) while images are crawled (scanned automatically).
  21. Once approved, check the account weekly to review numbers, add negative keywords (words your ads must not show for) and adjust bids.

Import settings explained

1 min
  • Uncheck account level URL options. Leaving it checked transfers URL tracking from Google to Bing, which incorrectly attributes Bing sales to your Google Ads account (credits the wrong platform for the sale).
  • The UET tag must be connected to the Google Shopping app in the Microsoft Advertising Conversion section. Select this tag during import setup.

Pending approval

1 min
  • Do not expect imported Bing campaigns to run immediately while the feed is pending approval in Merchant Center. The feed may show pending according to image crawling until Microsoft approves all product images and data.
  • Campaigns show Delivery errors (errors that stop ads from showing) until approval completes. Wait for the feed status to turn green before expecting ad delivery.

Other import routes

1 min
  • To set up Bing ads from an existing Google setup:
    1. Import your campaigns from your Google account to Bing.
    2. Lower the budget for the Bing campaigns.
    3. Keep using the product images from your product pages. No separate assets (extra files) are needed.
  • To launch Bing Shopping from an existing Google feed:
    1. Submit the same product feed you use for Google to Bing Ads. This can be done within about an hour if it is already clean.
    2. Import your Google Shopping campaigns into Bing Ads. The platform supports this with one action.
    3. Review the imported bids and segmentation (splitting ads into groups) before turning the campaigns on.
  • Remarketing audiences (lists of past visitors to show ads to again) must be created inside Microsoft Ads. Shopping campaigns can be transferred from Google Ads without creating new audiences.
  • if import errors occur:
    1. Open the Microsoft Merchant Center account in Microsoft Ads.
    2. Delete the existing Google Ads integrations, but do not delete the accounts themselves.
    3. Re-add the integrations and confirm the correct Merchant Center accounts are selected, since linking the wrong account causes data errors.

Examples:

  • $100 per day on Google becomes $25 per day on Bing.
  • $100 per day on Google Shopping gives $15 to $30 per day on Bing.
  • Setting Bing to $400 with a $100 Google budget would skew results (throw the numbers out of balance).

Rules for picking a value:

  • For a first import, start with the 80% reduction. The procedure's test is whether Bing delivers any impressions (times your ad is shown) and clicks before committing larger spend (spending more).
  • The 80% reduction leaves 20% of the Google budget, which is the top of the 15 to 20% range given for Shopping.
  • The 25% figures (2026, and the 2021 volume ratio) and the 15 to 20% figure (2023) use different bases. Use the one that matches how your budget is set.
  • If the budget is set as a share of total spend, start at about 10%.
  • If Microsoft spends more than Google despite higher Google budgets, the Google daily budget is not being spent. See Bids and CPC (cost per click: what you pay for each click) below.

Cuts and pauses:

  • If Microsoft Ads costs are high while its sales are low compared with Google, cut its budget sharply or turn it off. Focus on Google Shopping first.
  • Restart Microsoft Ads only once the Google campaigns are profitable. Size its budget in proportion to the traffic it brings, which is typically about 25% to 30% of Google's.
  • Traffic relation: Microsoft Ads usually brings about 25% to 30% of the traffic that Google brings. Its spend should be proportional to that, and Google should get the larger share because it typically has the better ROAS.
  • Share of searches: roughly one in three web searches takes place on Bing. This is a share of all searches, not a share of Google's traffic.

Bids and CPC

1 min
  • Start Microsoft Ads (the ad system for Bing) with 25% of your Google Ads daily budget. A daily budget is the most you spend per day.
  • Set the CPC (cost per click, the price you pay each time someone clicks your ad) to match your Google Ads CPC. Adjust it as data comes in.
  • a Bing account was told to raise every CPC to $2. The better approach was a small increase of about 5 cents, because Google campaigns already got clicks at 60 cents.
  • If Microsoft Ads spends more than Google Ads, even with higher Google budgets, the Google daily budget is not being spent.
  • This usually means the default CPC (the starting bid used when you set none) is too low.
  • Raise the Google Ads default CPC by about 10 cents.
  • if Google impressions (how many times your ad is shown) are low while Bing shows many impressions for the same products, check the keyword triggers (search words that make your ad appear) and product matches (rules that link your products to searches) in both accounts.
  • Bing clicks are often cheaper, so raising the Google cost per click can increase impressions.
  • Bing clicks being cheaper than Google is part of why Bing can add impressions at low cost.
  • Keep the bid adjustments small, and check results after each change.

Targeting

1 min
  • Bing Ads supports the same geo-targeting (choosing where your ads show) options as Google Ads and Meta (Facebook and Instagram ads): individual zip codes, whole states, or a radius (a circle around a city).
  • Use these instead of a country-wide target when a supplier's shipping cost makes distant customers unprofitable. A supplier is the company that ships the product for you.
  • Before adding budget to a campaign (a group of ads with its own budget and settings) that has traffic (visitors) but no sales, check its location and other settings.
  • A campaign with about 5,944 clicks from very low spend and zero sales points to the wrong traffic.
  • This often comes from targeting many countries where clicks are cheap.

Suspensions and reinstatement

1 min
  • A suspension means the platform shuts your account off. Reinstatement means it is turned back on.

Why it happens

1 min
  • New Microsoft Ads accounts are often suspended automatically within a day or two of creation, sometimes on the same day.
  • The reason given is vague, with no specific details.
  • This happens even when the store is compliant (it follows the rules).
  • A suspension alone does not mean the advertiser did something wrong.
  • A new account may be suspended the next day with a notice citing an egregious policy violation (a serious rule break) and a list of possible causes, without saying which one applies.
  • Treat this as a common platform issue and appeal it (formally ask for a review), rather than assuming the store broke a rule.
  • Microsoft Ads commonly suspends new accounts. Resolve any Google issues first, then pursue the Microsoft suspension.

Appeal procedures

1 min

Procedure A (if a new account is suspended):

  1. Check the store against a platform compliance checklist (a list of the platform's rules).
  2. Submit an appeal through the account.
  3. Wait for a human reviewer to visit the site, which usually leads to reinstatement.

Procedure B (reinstatement after an immediate suspension, 2024):

  1. Visit the Microsoft support page every two to three days.
  2. Request help, say that the store follows all policies, and ask for a human review of the store.
  3. Keep repeating until a reviewer who can look at the store is reached (about one week in one case).

Procedure C (new account closed automatically without a clear reason, 2023):

  1. Set a daily reminder for the same time each day.
  2. Send a message to Bing support every day.
  3. Try live chat (typing with support in real time) and open support tickets (formal help requests) as well.
  4. Keep following up until a human reviews the account, because new accounts are often flagged by automated checks (computer checks with no person looking).
  • For repeated suspensions, request live chat again and ask for a different representative (support person).

Merchant Center flags

1 min
  • Merchant Center is Microsoft's tool that checks your product data (the details about each product).
  • Microsoft Merchant Center can flag a store (put a warning mark on it) for professional services or unallowed characters (symbols the system does not accept) in product data.
  • Tobacco or e-cigarette ads are also flagged.
  • Some flags cannot be appealed from inside the account. Contact Microsoft support directly and ask for a manual review (a review done by a person).

Using Microsoft as a backup

1 min
  • if Google Shopping (product ads on Google) is blocked by a suspension, use Microsoft as a backup traffic source (another source of visitors):
    1. Set up a Microsoft store.
    2. Create a Microsoft Shopping campaign (ads that show your product pictures and prices).
    • The traffic volume is smaller, but the campaign can still be profitable.
  • Case (Google account suspended): a Shopify store (online shop software) open since January with over 100 orders sold only through Microsoft Ads.
  • Net profit (money left after all costs) was about 10 to 15%, around $2,000 in September.
  • Case (Merchant Center and Google Ads suspended): a store with about 200 products from seven brands ran Alpha and Beta campaigns (two named campaign groups) on its full catalog (all its products) across Google and Microsoft.
  • Microsoft received about twice the spend of Google.
  • See Optimization for the outcome and advice.

Ad formats and tracking

1 min
  • Microsoft Advertising offers search text ads (text ads shown when people search), the Bing counterpart to Google search text ads.
  • They carry a small Ad label above the listing.
  • Microsoft Advertising offers Shopping ads (product ads with a picture and price), the Bing counterpart to Google Shopping.
  • They show when a user searches for a product, or a keyword related to a product sold in the store.
  • Microsoft Shopping ads work the same way as Google Shopping. Set up Shopping ads after the search text campaigns and the organic webmaster setup (a free search listing set up in Bing's webmaster tools).
  • Bing Ads offers search text ads and other formats that copy Google's types.
  • Import the Google campaigns into Bing, and check Google first if Bing shows errors.
  • Conversion tracking (measuring sales from ads) is set up by adding Microsoft tags (small pieces of tracking code) to the site, as described in the Microsoft setup steps.
  • Connect the UET tag (Microsoft's tracking code) to the Google Shopping app in the Microsoft Advertising Conversion section.
  • Bing search terms appear in the Microsoft Advertising dashboard (the main control page).

Optimization

2 min

Optimization means changing your settings to get better results.

Core rule

  • Optimize Bing Shopping campaigns weekly when first starting.
  • Review performance numbers, identify negative keywords to add (search words that block your ad from showing), and adjust bids up or down based on results.
  • Treat Bing management the same as Google Ads optimization after import.
  • Microsoft Ads is optimized with the same actions as Google Ads: search term review (checking which searches triggered your ads), negative keywords, and budget changes.
  • Only the interface (the screens you click through) differs, so the Google Ads workflow can be applied directly.
  • The same optimization rules used for Google Ads also apply to Microsoft Ads (Bing).
  • The same Google Ads campaign optimization steps also apply to Microsoft Ads (Bing).
  • The same tweaks can be made on both platforms before raising bids.
  • Leave Microsoft Ads out of the starting budget until campaigns are profitable.
  • Microsoft Ads responds to the same optimization as Google Ads, so apply the same Google Ads optimization process to Microsoft campaigns.

Negative keywords

  • On Microsoft Ads, removing negative keywords has a different impact than on Google Ads.
  • If you have high impression share (how often your ads show out of all the chances you had) but low CTR (click-through rate, the share of people who see your ad and click it), and want to improve visibility, remove some negative keywords on Microsoft specifically before increasing bid.

Troubleshooting an unprofitable campaign

  • A Bing campaign that sends unprofitable traffic (visitors who cost more than they earn) should be cut to about $10 in total until the settings are fixed.
  • Check the campaign's location and other settings before adding budget.

Case: lowering spend on a losing account

  • Case (2025): the owner of a store with over 100 orders feared paying too much per click and had not raised spend.
  • The account had about 1,100 clicks from about 75,000 impressions on $650 of spend.
  • It ran on a shared $20 daily budget (one budget used by several campaigns) that was always maxed out (used in full every day).
  • Advice given: do not raise the budget yet.
  • Review which products and keywords convert profitably (turn visitors into sales that make money) and which do not.
  • Move budget toward the profitable ones.
  • Let the account run for a couple of weeks.
  • Confirm that return on ad spend (ROAS, the sales you get back for each dollar of ads) rises, and only then increase the budget.
  • No final result was reported in the call.

Case: Microsoft spend exceeded Google's return

  • Case: Microsoft received about twice the spend of Google, yet Google had the better return (more money back for each dollar spent).
  • Google had four conversions (completed sales or goals) from 566 pounds of spend.
  • Total sales were reported around 4,400 pounds from 10 conversions.
  • Advice given: keep Microsoft off until extra profit covers it.
  • Run Google only.
  • Review which products already sold.
  • Lower CPC on products that spent at least 50 without converting.

Scaling

1 min

Scaling means growing your spend once it works.

Scale only when proven

  • Bing Ads should be added after the core setup (the basic account and campaign build) and only when Google performs.
  • Pause Bing Ads until Google Shopping is profitable, then move the proven campaigns to Bing.
  • restart Microsoft Ads only once the Google campaigns are profitable.

Stepwise scaling procedure

  1. Check which products and keywords convert profitably and which do not.
  2. Move budget away from non-converting or unprofitable keywords and products, toward the most profitable ones.
  3. Let the adjusted account run for a couple of weeks.
  4. Confirm that return on ad spend improved.
  5. Only then increase the daily budget.
  • Increase budgets gradually.
  • If results are positive after the 80% test (a first test check), raise budgets step by step.
  • Start with about 10% of the total ad budget, optimize there, and only then scale.

Scaling by account spend

  • Below about $5,000 monthly ad spend, export (copy out) Google Ads campaigns straight into Bing Ads.
  • Later changes in Google Ads update automatically.
  • Above about $5,000 monthly ad spend, do the initial import and then optimize Bing on its own data.
  • Signal that Bing has enough own data: around 1% conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy) with regularly around 100 clicks.

Case: restarting after losses

  • Case: a store with about 200 products restarts at about 30 per day on five to ten proven products.
  • It improves product pages, expands the product list later, and adds Microsoft back at a much smaller budget.

Secondary platform budget

  • A secondary search platform (a second search ad system, such as Bing) can run at $15 a day.
  • If it does not pay off, drop it to about $5 a day to keep visibility until profits can support more spend.

Market background data

1 min

Dated figures from older sources, to be used as background only:

  • As of 2015: about one in three US searches happened on Bing.
  • In the same period, Google had about 65% of US search share (the share of all searches).
  • Google and Bing together reached about 95% of the US market, so skipping Bing meant missing a large share of buyers.
  • For the period around 2018: Bing Ads ad spend grew 32% year over year (compared with the same time last year) in Q4 (the last three months of the year).
  • Bing handled about one in three US searches.
  • For the same period: audience-based Google search ad clicks grew 34% year over year.
  • This means audience targeting (showing ads to chosen groups of people) became a larger part of search campaigns.

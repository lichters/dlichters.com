Playbook › 10. Google Ads and Shopping

Pre-Launch compliance and store readiness

Section 10.3 · 3 of 23 in this chapter · 11 min read

In short

  • Google checks your store with automated bots before it approves your ad accounts, so finish the store first.
  • Leftover placeholder text, demo products and broken links must be gone before you submit.
  • Prices, shipping fees and business details must match exactly across your store, Shopify and Google.
  • Check that your product area is allowed on Google before you build anything.
  • Start paid ads only after at least two suppliers are on the site and about 20 products are uploaded.
This section has 20 parts. Tap one to open it.

Why Compliance Comes First

1 min
  • Google uses automated bots, called crawlers, not human reviewers. They scan your site and flag anything missing or incomplete.
  • Make the store fully compliant before you create your Google Merchant Center and Google Ads accounts. Compliant means it follows Google's rules. Google Merchant Center is Google's tool that holds your product list. Google Ads is Google's paid ad system.
  • Fixing these issues first helps you avoid:
    • suspensions (your account is shut off)
    • review loops (Google checks again and again)
    • back-and-forth (long rounds of messages with Google)
  • The crawler checks the store before your Merchant Center and Google Ads accounts are approved. So the site must be complete at submission. It must have no errors and no placeholders. A placeholder is dummy text left in a template.
  • Google crawls the site during setup. Do not proceed if the store has:
    • demo products (sample items that came with the template)
    • policies with placeholder text
    • broken links or pages (links that lead nowhere)

Compliance Checklist Sequence

1 min

Work in this order before any account or campaign is created. A campaign is a set of ads.

  1. Add every required policy and store element to the site.
  2. Confirm the checklist is complete.
  3. Only then set up and launch the campaigns. This lowers the risk of a Merchant Center or account suspension.

Timing rules that apply to the checklist:

  • Complete the Compliance Checklist before creating a Google Merchant Center account.
  • Before setting up Google Merchant Center, Google Ads or a Facebook account, complete the compliance checklist. Following it lowers the chance that the store gets flagged for misrepresentation (showing false or misleading information).
  • Run through the store compliance checklist before submitting products to Google Merchant Center. It covers the required pages and elements to add to the store. (2023)
  • Do not start paid traffic (paid visitors from Shopping or Facebook ads) before the compliance checklist is completed.
  • The Compliance Checklist outlines all requirements to complete before you turn on Google Ads live. Follow it to reduce the risk of account suspension.
  • Complete the compliance checklist before launching Google Ads. Run Google Ads alone until sales come in before adding other traffic sources. (2023)

Required Store Elements

1 min

Contact Page

1 min
  • Your contact page must offer at least three ways to reach you: an email address, a contact form and a phone number.
  • Keep prices, contact details and page content on your site consistent with your ads. Do not let them disagree. Inconsistencies between ads and webpages are a common cause of account reviews (Google's checks of your account).

Policies

2 min
  • Shopify's policy generators (free tools that write legal pages) insert placeholder text into refund, privacy, terms and shipping policies. Shopify is the store software you build on.
  • Google treats leftover placeholders as a sign of an incomplete store.
  • Read each generated policy line by line and replace every placeholder before submission.
  • Merchant Center policy details, such as return and privacy policies, can start with the default text (built-in text the software provides) and be edited later. The store and Merchant Center must eventually match.
  • Before submission, the policies must be final, not default text.
  • Review the compliance section several times before launch. Merchant Center suspensions can come from:
    • broken links on the store
    • filler text such as lorem ipsum (dummy Latin text used as a placeholder)
    • a missing shipping or return policy
    • policies entered in Merchant Center that do not match the site
  • Check that all legal pages are in place and that shipping times are stated correctly before starting Google Ads. New merchant accounts (seller accounts on Google) are often suspended for missing legal pages or incorrect shipping times.

Policy requirements at a glance:

Policy or element Requirement Source
Privacy policy Must disclose that the store uses retargeting (showing ads to people who already visited the store). A policy that omits retargeting does not meet the Google Ads requirement.
Shipping policy Must state the exact shipping cost, so customers know what they will pay before checkout (the buying steps). If the header or site promises free shipping, the shipping policy must also state free shipping.
Shipping policy, freight Freight charges (fees for large or bulky shipments) must be disclosed, even if competitors do not show them.
Return policy Place a summary table at the top. Google crawlers can pull shipping and return data easily from tables, so it helps Merchant Center read the terms. Use clear, specific language. Vague wording commonly triggers Merchant Center flags.
Refund, privacy, terms, shipping Every placeholder replaced.
All legal pages In place before Google Ads starts.

Shipping, Prices and Checkout Consistency

1 min
  • Shipping fees, business name, phone number and tax information in Merchant Center must exactly match the store's website and Shopify settings.
  • Google's bots test the checkout (the steps where a customer pays).
  • A mismatch leads to suspension. Example: the store charges for shipping, but Merchant Center shows free shipping.
  • Do not mark products as free shipping on Google Shopping while shipping is charged at checkout. This is a compliance problem. (2024)
  • Website prices must match exactly the prices submitted to Google Merchant Center. A mismatch is one of the most common causes of a suspension.
  • If prices, taxes or shipping shown at checkout differ from those in the ads and feed, Google can suspend the entire product feed. The feed is your full product list sent to Google. Google compares these during checkout.
  • Set up Shopify Markets per country so checkout runs in each buyer's local currency. Shopify Markets is Shopify's tool for selling in several countries.
  • A shopper from the UK or Australia will not easily buy when prices show in USD.
  • Create shipping profiles per country, or one international profile with free tracked shipping (shipping with a tracking number). A shipping profile is a set of shipping rules.
  • Showing estimated delivery days in the shipping text is optional. Store owners are split on it. It works for some stores and not for others, so test it rather than assume.

Demo Products, Dummy Stores and Broken Links

1 min
  • Do not start the Merchant Center setup until the store's compliance requirements are fixed, including removing demo products. Unresolved compliance issues will cause problems with the account.
  • Do not link Google Ads or other ad accounts while the store has only dummy products (fake test items), because it can hurt account approval. Add real products first.
  • Do not proceed if the store has demo products, placeholder policies or broken links or pages.

Business Information and Addresses

1 min
  • Do not hide business information, such as an address, with text that matches the background colour.
  • Google can treat this as misrepresentation (false information), which can lead to account bans. (2026)
  • Some sellers report that Google flags virtual mailbox addresses (mail-forwarding addresses) as business addresses in Merchant Center.
  • This is anecdotal (based on single reports, not proof) and not confirmed. Check your Merchant Center before relying on a virtual address. (2026)

Reviews and Ratings

1 min
  • Customer reviews and product ratings in Google Merchant Center are fine when they come from Judge.me, a review app for Shopify.
  • Judge.me transfers them automatically.
  • Do not use reviews copied from other websites on a new store. Google can detect this, and it can lead to account problems.
  • Reviews without dates still have impact for the product types sold. But showing dates can help customers. (2023)
  • Google Trusted Store badge (a Google seal that shows a store is trusted):
    • It requires Google user ratings, about 50 reviews.
    • It must be requested from Google.
    • Reviews alone are not enough. (2023)

Live Store, Products and Stock

1 min
  • Do not set up Google Ads or other paid ad accounts until live products are on the store. Running ads before that is a red flag (a warning sign) for Google. (2023)
  • Do not run ads on a store that is not live. Merchant Center accounts and ads are set up only after real products are on the store and it is ready to take orders.
  • A site that does not take orders is not eligible for Google Shopping (Google's product ads).
  • The product feed links to product pages where a customer can check out. Approval would not last.
  • Google Shopping ads must link to live product pages that are in stock (available to sell). Pause a product when stock is short. This prevents wasted spend (money spent with no sale) and poor customer experience.

Products That Are Not Ready

1 min

Keep products that are not ready out of the ads and store:

  1. Open the product in Shopify.
  2. Change its status to draft and save. Draft means saved but hidden. It will not show in the store or in the ads.
  3. When images and descriptions are complete, set the status to active and save. Active means visible. The product is then pushed (sent) to the Google ad account.
  • Products that are ready for sale but not fully optimised (not fully polished for search) can stay in the shopping campaigns.
  • Products missing images or descriptions should be set to draft.

Launch Structure for Large Supplier Sets

1 min
  • If many suppliers are approved, upload all their products.
  • Set up alpha and beta (the names of two campaign setups in this method) with all products included once the product pages are complete.
  • Turn ads on after the products are ready. (2025)

Supplier and Product Thresholds Before Paid Traffic

1 min

Sources give different supplier minimums. A supplier is a company that ships products for your store. A niche is your product area. Use the values below and apply the most recent one as the floor. The floor is the lowest number you must reach.

Source Year Minimum before paid Google ads Notes Id
Source A 2024 At least two supplier approvals with uploaded products Keep uploading in the background afterwards.
Source A 2022 At least two suppliers approved and their products uploaded Keep getting approvals with other suppliers in the background.
Source A 2020 At least two suppliers on the site After the first campaign runs, use time to get more suppliers approved and add their products.
Source A 2021 One or two approved suppliers can start Do not start with a supplier count below the target. Keep getting approvals with the remaining suppliers in the niche.
  • The 2021 source allows a start with one or two suppliers.
  • The 2020, 2022 and 2024 sources set two as the minimum.
  • Treat two as the floor and the target supplier count as the goal.
  • Ads should start once at least two suppliers are on the site.
  • Have roughly 20 products uploaded to the store before enabling Google Shopping ads.
  • Both thresholds were stated in the sources. Apply the supplier minimum and the product count together.

One Store, One Google Ads Account

1 min
  • Each unrelated store needs its own Google Ads account. Register it with a sales email address on that store's domain (the store's own web address) and a separate phone number. (2025)
  • Stores that can cross-promote (promote each other's products to their customers) can share one account. Example: a bounce house store and a trampoline store. (2025)

Prohibited Products and Restricted Categories

1 min
  • Google Merchant Center prohibits certain product categories from Google Shopping. These include adult products, weapons, vehicles and smoking-related implements (tools).
  • Listing a prohibited product usually leads to suspension, and recovery chances are very low.
  • Check a niche against Google's prohibited products list before building the store and campaigns.
  • A product category on that list can get the Google Ads account suspended, even when the business is otherwise legitimate.
  • Google Ads does not allow advertising for some product categories. A niche in a prohibited category cannot run paid Google ads.
  • If the core products (main products) of a niche cannot be advertised on Google, such as paintball guns, the niche is a strikeout (a niche that cannot work).
  • Google Shopping cannot be used to sell those core products. Other products in the same niche do not change this result.
  • For restricted or specialty product categories, enable the extra restriction boxes in the Google Ads setup. These are settings that limit which audiences see the ads. Check Google's policy first.
  • Google's policy on pop-ups and large sticky ads (ads that stay on screen while the visitor scrolls) is aimed at elements that block a visitor from using the page.
  • A sticky top announcement bar or homepage banner that does not block content was not expected to break this rule.

Information Products

1 min
  • Information products, such as courses (digital teaching material), can only be advertised through Google Shopping and product listing ads if a physical item is shipped with them. For example, a workbook.
  • Without a shipped item, the ad account risks being suspended.

Health and Diet Products

1 min
  • Use factual product attributes (plain product facts), such as low-calorie, gluten-free and seed-oil-free.
  • Use lifestyle benefits, such as macro-friendly (fits diets that track protein, carbs and fat), diet-friendly and supports low-sugar diets.
  • Avoid absolute health claims or medical assertions (statements that a product treats or cures).
  • Be transparent about ingredient variations. For example, some product flavours may use sucralose (an artificial sweetener), while others are naturally sweetened.
  • Google's health policies are strict. Ads making unsupported health claims (claims with no proof) will be disapproved or suspended. Disapproved means rejected.

Organic Fallback

1 min
  • Plan organic traffic from the start. Organic traffic means free visitors from search results.
  • Build it with SEO (search engine optimisation, making your pages rank higher) and content.
  • A store can grow through organic search even after losing ad access.

Ad Consistency

1 min
  • Do not let the price, contact details or page content on your site disagree with your ads.
  • Merchant Center and the store must eventually match on policies and business details.

Readiness Before Any Ad Account Is Opened

1 min

Complete these steps in order before creating a Google Merchant Center, Google Ads or other paid ad account (an account that runs paid ads):

  1. Complete the compliance checklist, including every required page and store element.
  2. Make the store live with real products and the ability to take orders.
  3. Remove demo products, dummy data (fake sample data) and placeholder text, and fix broken links.
  4. Publish the contact page with at least three contact methods.
  5. Publish the privacy policy with retargeting disclosed, and the shipping and return policies with exact terms.
  6. Confirm that prices, shipping fees, tax information and business details match in the store, Shopify and Merchant Center.
  7. Confirm that the niche and its core products are not prohibited or restricted, and enable the extra restriction boxes where required.
  8. Confirm the supplier count and product count thresholds are met.
  9. Set products that are not ready to draft, and keep only in-stock, live product pages in the feed.
  10. Link Google Ads only after the store is ready. Run Google Ads alone until sales come in before adding other traffic sources.

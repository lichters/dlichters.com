Playbook › 10. Google Ads and Shopping

Setting up Google accounts: ads, analytics, search console, tag tracking

Section 10.4 · 4 of 23 in this chapter · 26 min read

In short

  • Set up the free tracking accounts (Google Analytics, Merchant Center, Google Ads) first, and use one email for all of them.
  • Create the Google Ads account without a campaign, using Expert Mode.
  • Do not let the sign-up steps create a campaign or AI ads. Ignore the campaign prompts for now.
  • Check time zone, currency and billing country before you submit. You cannot change them later.
  • Connect Google Analytics and Merchant Center in Data Manager, and check that both say "Linked".
More key points
  • Set up tracking with the Google Shopping feed app, the app that sends your product list to Google. Do not add a second tracking app.
  • Keep Purchases as the only goal Google uses to improve your ads.
  • Use a separate Google Ads account for each business. Never share one account between businesses.
  • The status Unverified is normal after setup. It changes once the first real sale comes in.
  • Add your store to Search Console, Google's free tool that shows how your products appear in search.

Setup Order and Measurement First

3 min
  • Build the measurement setup first, before spending on Google Ads. Measurement setup means the tools that count visits and sales. Sales can only be traced to specific campaigns (one group of ads with its own budget), and results can only be improved, if this setup exists.
  • Complete this setup before spending on Google Ads:
    1. Link your Google Ads account to Google Analytics. Google Ads is Google's paid ad tool. Google Analytics is Google's free tool that tracks what visitors do on your site.
    2. Configure basic retargeting audiences, and check that they capture visitor data. Retargeting means showing ads again to people who already visited. An audience is a saved group of visitors.
    3. Activate conversion tracking, and confirm it records purchases. Conversion tracking records when a visitor buys.
  • Set up Google Ads, Google Shopping, Google Analytics and Google Merchant Center together, from the first day of the store. Google Shopping shows your products as ads with pictures and prices. Google Merchant Center is where your product list is sent to Google.
  • Three parts are needed before running Google Ads in this approach:
    1. Google Analytics, to track website activity and build audiences of visitors who interacted in certain ways, for remarketing.
    2. Google Merchant Center, where all products are submitted to Google.
    3. Google Ads account, where the actual ad campaigns are created.
  • All three accounts are free to set up.
  • The same accounts that enable paid campaigns also provide free Google traffic (visitors who arrive without paid ads). Correct setup supports both paid and free Google visibility.
  • Google traffic setup, in order:
    1. Sign up for Google Ads, Google Search Console and Google Merchant Center. Google Search Console is Google's tool that shows how your site appears in search.
    2. Create your first Google Shopping campaign and your first Google remarketing campaign.
  • Set up Google Search Console and Google Merchant Center alongside Google Ads.
  • Merchant Center supplies the product feed (your product list in a form Google can read) that Google Shopping campaigns use.
  • Merchant Center feeds product data to Google Shopping campaigns.
  • Search Console covers the search visibility of the store site. Search visibility means how easily people find your site in search results.
  • Traffic setup on Google and Bing, in order. Bing is Microsoft's search engine. Bing steps are not covered in this text:
    1. Sign up for Google Ads, Google Search Console and Google Merchant Center.
    2. Create the first Google Shopping and remarketing campaigns.
    3. Sign up for Bing Ads, Bing Webmaster Tools and Microsoft Merchant Center.
    4. Create the first Bing Shopping campaign.
  • The sources disagree on how many accounts a store needs:
    • A new store usually needs four Google accounts: Google Ads, Google Merchant Center, Google Search Console and Google Analytics.
    • A Shopify store (Shopify is the software that runs the online shop) launched with Google Shopping is described as needing four accounts or connections.
    • But the same source lists only three: Shopify with Google Analytics, Google Merchant Center and Google Ads.
    • The Google Ads account is the first marketing channel (the first way you advertise) and focuses on Google Shopping.
  • Use the setup steps for Google Ads from the build phase (the early stage of setting up a store) even for an established business, if existing campaigns are not performing as expected. This lets you check and correct the account setup.

One Google Identity and One Email

2 min
  • One Google identity means one login email for all your Google tools.
  • Create a dedicated business Google account email. Do not use your personal main Gmail (Google's email service).
  • Use that same email for Gmail, Google Analytics, Google Search Console (formerly Webmaster Tools), Google Merchant Center and Google Ads. This lets the properties link and share data. A property is one site's record inside a Google tool.
  • Using the same email for Google Ads, Google Analytics and Google Merchant Center lets the accounts detect each other. This removes manual permissions (access settings you set by hand) and reduces setup errors.
  • Google links the accounts with one click only when they share one owner email.
  • Use the same email for all three accounts (Analytics, Merchant Center, Google Ads), in this order:
    1. Create a Google Analytics account to track website activity and to build visitor audiences for remarketing.
    2. Create a Google Merchant Center account and submit all store products to it.
    3. Create a Google Ads account to build the ad campaigns.
  • Use the same email address for all three accounts, so Google can link them with one click and let data pass between them.
  • Use the same email for Google Ads, Merchant Center and Google Analytics. Data Manager (a page in Google Ads that connects your other tools) then lists the matching accounts automatically when you connect products.
  • Use the same Google Workspace email for Merchant Center, Google Analytics and Google Search Console, so the platforms can connect. Google Workspace is Google's paid business email and office suite. Domain verification (proof that you own the web address) is then completed through Search Console automatically.
  • Log in to Google Ads with the same Google email used for Search Console and Analytics, so all Google properties stay under one identity.
  • A different address splits the properties across separate identities and makes the setup inconsistent.
  • The Google Ads "Linked accounts" screen and the Analytics "Google Ad Links" screen only list Google Ads accounts tied to the same Google email as the Analytics account. Use one email for Google Analytics, Search Console and Google Ads.
  • Create the Google Ads account by logging in with your Google Workspace email address.
  • Create the Merchant Center and Google Ads accounts with the owner's company email as admin. An admin is the person with full control of the account.
  • Customer-facing contact details (what shoppers see) use the store's customer service email.
  • If the Google Ads account is created with a different email than Google Analytics, either create a new Google Analytics account or create a new Google Ads account using the Analytics email. Choose based on how much setup is already done.
  • The main email of an existing Google Ads account cannot be changed.
  • You can add another user as admin with full access.

Creating the Google Ads Account in Expert Mode

5 min
  • Create the account at ads.google.com.
  • Create the Google Ads account first, without building any campaign. Campaign creation comes later, after the account is configured.
  • Enter the business name and website, then click Next.
  • Select Skip wherever possible.
  • Choose Leave campaign creation to avoid Google's forced campaign setup attempt.
  • Use the standard sign-up, not AdWords Express, if you plan to optimise (improve) campaigns. AdWords is Google Ads' old name. Express is a simpler version. Express removes most manual control features.
  • Google has renamed AdWords to Google Ads and changes its sign-up flow often, so screens may differ. Follow the logic (standard sign-up, control over settings, billing setup) rather than exact button positions.
  • Both names refer to the same product today.
  • Avoid the automated goal-based options offered during sign-up. They reduce manual control over targeting (who sees your ads) and websites.
  • Do not let the wizard generate AI ads or accept automated AI features during setup. A wizard is a series of guided setup screens.
  • Generated ads are low quality.
  • Automation tends to use up the budget quickly.
  • Skip the AI-generated ad questions, skip linking additional accounts, skip the goal selection, and choose the option to leave campaign creation.
  • Do not build any campaign at account creation. Google will keep prompting you to create one after the account exists.
  • Ignore these prompts until your planning, research and campaign setup are ready.
  • Do not use the simple guided Google Ads setup for a high-ticket dropshipping business. High-ticket dropshipping means selling expensive products that a supplier ships directly to the buyer.
  • Google earns most of its money from ads. Its recommendations may favour Google over your margins (the profit you keep per sale).
  • Goals such as getting more calls do not reflect a sound business objective.
  • Enter the business address in the Google account used for Google Ads.
  • Create a separate Google Ads account for each business. If the login already shows an existing account, choose Create new Google Ads account.
  • A new business needs a new Google Ads account.
  • An old account can be left alone or have its campaigns deleted.
  • The historical data (past records) stays.
  • If you already have a working Google Ads account, do not repeat the account setup.
  • The account only needs to be created once per business.
  • After you submit business information, Google shows a completion message and opens an account with no campaigns. This empty state (a blank account) is expected. Campaigns are added later.
  • Once the account is created, leave it without a campaign and mark the setup as complete.
  • Log in and start from the Expert Mode wizard. Expert Mode is the advanced setup with full control.
  • Click Switch to expert mode instead of continuing in easy mode. Easy mode asks about the main advertising goal.
  • Then click Create an account without a campaign.
  • Standard account creation, step by step:
    1. Search for Google Ads (or AdWords) and open the sign-up page.
    2. Select the standard sign-up, not AdWords Express.
    3. Skip the first campaign by choosing the option to create a campaign later.
    4. Enter the billing information and select the currency. The currency cannot be changed later.
    5. Enter the customer info: Individual or Business. For a business, enter the business name and address.
    6. Choose automatic payments and enter the credit card details.
    7. Submit the payment details.
    8. If the page is stuck on "start reaching people", refresh the page and continue the billing setup.
    9. Open the billing settings. Confirm that the account, the payment method and the billing details are saved.
  • Expert-mode account creation with the coupon, as one run:
    1. Go to ads.google.com and open the coupon page for your country.
    2. Enter your email and click Get the offer code. Wait up to one minute for the code by email.
    3. Sign in to Google Ads with the same email used for Google Search Console and Google Analytics.
    4. Click Switch to expert mode in the setup wizard.
    5. Click Create an account without a campaign.
    6. Enter the billing country, time zone (keep it consistent with your other Google properties) and currency. The time zone is the clock your account runs on.
    7. Click Submit and confirm the account setup is complete.
    8. Later, to redeem the coupon (use it for credit): click the wrench icon (Tools and settings), open Billing and payments, add a payment method, then open Manage promotional codes and enter the code.
  • Full wizard run for a new store before Google Shopping ads:
    1. Open the account sign-up page via the link provided. Check which promotional offer is shown, because offers change over time.
    2. Pick the offer that needs the smallest spend. Offer A gives $500 credit after $500 spend in 60 days (credit is free ad money). Claim it. If the spend threshold (the amount you must spend) is not reached, nothing is lost apart from the missing credit.
    3. Sign in with the Google Workspace account that will be used for the whole setup.
    4. Enter the business name and the store URL in the wizard, then continue.
    5. Skip the AI-generated ad questions, skip linking additional accounts, skip the goal selection, and choose the option to leave campaign creation, so no campaign is created.
    6. Confirm billing country, time zone and currency, then continue.
    7. Set up the payments profile. Choose Individual (or Organization if a company exists, or a proprietor name for sole traders). A sole trader is a self-employed single owner. Then add a payment method.
    8. A temporary $50 authorisation (a temporary hold on the card) appears. It is usually released within a week or sooner.
    9. Answer no to the offer of personalised guidance from a Google Ads account strategist (a Google sales contact). Then submit to create the account.
  • Account sign-up can force a first campaign. Do not use it.
  • Skipping through the wizard and leaving campaign creation avoids an unwanted campaign. Otherwise you would have to switch that campaign off.
  • After payment, the new interface may stay stuck on a "start reaching people" screen with an error. Refresh the page and open billing settings.
  • In billing settings, check whether the account and payment method were created. The account can exist despite the error.
  • Verify in billing settings that the account, payment method and billing details are saved.

Coupons and Promotional Offers

1 min
  • A coupon is a code that gives you free ad credit. Use the official coupon page Google publishes for promotional offers.
  • Select the coupon page for the country where the billing address is located.
  • Outside the US, search google.com for "Google Ads Voucher" plus the country name. Then open the link that leads to the Google Ads page for that country.
  • The coupon page shows offers for the country the visitor (the person viewing the page) is located in. A US visitor sees the US offer.
  • Visitors outside the US should search for "Google Ads Voucher" plus their country name.
  • Enter an email address on the coupon page and click Get the offer code.
  • The code is sent by email within one minute.
  • You can apply it after the account has started.
  • To redeem a coupon (use it), click the wrench icon at the top (Tools and settings), then Billing and payments.
  • Set up a payment method first. The promotional code can only be entered after that.
  • Manage promotional codes appears under the payment setup.
  • The coupon can only be applied to the Google Ads account that is linked to the payment method.
  • Do not add billing information to an account you will never use. It only creates unnecessary setup.
  • Pick the smallest-spend offer when several are shown. Offer A in the source gives $500 credit after $500 spend in 60 days.
  • Offers change, so check the sign-up page each time.

Payment Method and First Charges

1 min
  • No money is spent during account setup.
  • The account is created and connected so Google Shopping ads can start once products are approved in free listings (product entries Google shows at no cost).
  • The payment method is only charged after ads run.
  • Choose automatic payments and enter the card details.
  • A temporary $50 authorisation (a temporary hold on the card) appears during payment setup.
  • It is usually released within a week or sooner.
  • In a new Google Ads account, Data Manager only becomes available after a payment method has been entered.
  • Until then the connected-products list is empty. This is normal for a brand-new account.

Account Approval and Billing Threshold

1 min
  • Google Ads usually bills once the account reaches its billing threshold (the amount owed before a bill is sent).
  • The threshold is set in the Google Ads settings. It was stated as $500 by default in 2019.
  • Accounts can differ, so check the threshold.
  • Approval for a new account usually takes 1 to 3 days.
  • It can sometimes take 24 hours, and occasionally longer.
  • Google's sign-up and the first campaign build are described in the Route C steps above, which were written in 2019.

Linking Google Analytics (GA4) to Google Ads

1 min
  • GA4 is the newer version of Google Analytics.
  • Linking Google Ads to a GA4 property lets the two platforms share data, including audiences.
  • Once linked, audiences built in Google Analytics can be used for Google Ads campaigns.
  • Link Google Ads to Google Analytics (GA4):
    1. Log in to Google Analytics.
    2. Click the gear icon in the bottom left corner to open Admin.
    3. In the right-hand column, scroll down to Product links and click Google Ad Links.
    4. Click Link.
    5. Select the Google Ads account that is listed. It must use the same email address.
    6. Click Link again.
    7. Check the new link. It shows the Google Ads account name, the account ID (its number), whether personalised advertising is enabled, and who created the link.
  • Older Universal Analytics route (labelled UA, the older version of Google Analytics; source from 2021-22):
    1. In Google Ads, open Tools and settings.
    2. Go to Linked accounts.
    3. Click Google Analytics (UA).
    4. Turn on Auto-tagging by clicking Enable.
    5. Check the Analytics property shown.
    6. Click Link, then switch the toggles to green.
    7. Click Save.
  • Before linking, make sure the login email in Analytics matches the login email in Google Ads.
  • That user must have Edit access (permission to change) to the property.
  • Otherwise no Analytics property appears for linking.
  • An audience built in Google Analytics does not reach Google Ads unless the Google Ads account is selected as an audience destination (where the audience is sent) before saving.
  • After linking, data only flows between the two platforms once ads have actually been running.
  • A newly linked account shows little or no shared data until the first campaigns go live.

Auto-Tagging

1 min
  • Auto-tagging means Google adds tracking tags to your ad links automatically.
  • Turn on auto-tagging in Google Ads before linking to Analytics.
  • It makes click data flow into Google Analytics, with better detail about ad performance.
  • Auto-tagging is switched on with the Enable button in the Google Analytics linked-accounts section of Google Ads. This is the older Universal Analytics route (2021-22).

Data Manager and Merchant Center Link

1 min
  • Connect Google Analytics and Merchant Center in Google Ads:
    1. In Google Ads, navigate to Tools, then Data Manager.
    2. Click Connect product.
    3. Select your existing Google Analytics account and Google Merchant Center account.
    4. Confirm both display as Linked when complete.
  • The click path is: Google Ads, then Tools (left menu), then Data Manager, then Connect product (blue button), then select Google Analytics and Google Merchant Center.
  • Both must show as linked before you move on.
  • Before the Google Ads setup counts as finished, Data Manager must list Google Analytics and Merchant Center as connected, both showing linked.
  • Accounts signed in with the same email appear as selectable options.
  • Data Manager is empty in a new account until a payment method is entered.
  • Three-step foundation summary before running Google Shopping ads:
    1. Create the Google Ads account.
    2. Connect Google Analytics and Google Merchant Center to Google Ads via Data Manager. Both must show as linked.
    3. In the Shopping feed app (a tool that sends your product list to Google), open Tracking Tags. Choose Google dynamic remarketing (ads that show visitors the products they looked at) and conversion tracking. Link the Google Ads account, and let the app create all tags. Tags are small pieces of code that record visits and sales.

Conversion Tracking and Tags via the Google Shopping Feed App

3 min
  • The app is spelled Symprosis in and, and Simprosys in. It is the same app in each case.
  • Set up Google tracking for Shopping through the feed app:
    1. Open the Symprosis Google Shopping Feed app in Shopify. (Shopify is the software your online shop runs on.)
    2. Hover over Tracking Tags. (Tracking tags are small pieces of code that report visits and actions to Google.)
    3. Click Google Dynamic Remarketing and Conversion Tracking. (Dynamic remarketing shows people again the exact products they viewed. Conversion tracking counts the sales that come from your ads.)
    4. Connect your newly created Google Ads account and approve permissions. (Google Ads is Google's paid advertising system. Permissions are the access you allow.)
    5. Enable Conversion tracking and Dynamic remarketing.
    6. The app automatically creates, installs and configures tracking tags and Google Ads events. (Events are named actions reported to Google.)
  • In the Shopify product feed app, open tracking tags, hover over it, then click Google dynamic remarketing and conversion tracking.
  • If the setup was made years ago, an update is probably needed, because tracking has changed.
  • A Merchant Center message about a conversion measurement problem usually means conversion tracking is missing or outdated. (Merchant Center is Google's account for your product data.)
  • Check the tracking tags in the product feed app.
  • Re-run the Google setup before changing campaigns. (Campaigns are groups of ads.)
  • Tracking events (Add to cart, Begin checkout, Purchase) must be pre-installed through the feed app. (Events are the named actions Google records.)
  • The Purchase event may appear as Purchase or as Purchase in category.
  • Duplicate tags from reinstalling are normal.
  • Select the correct one instead of excluding the duplicates.
  • Use the Google Shopping feed app (the one used to set up the Google integration) to create conversion tracking events automatically.
  • Do not install a separate Google Ads pixel app. (A pixel is a small piece of code that reports sales.)
  • Conversion tags are created through the feed app.
  • Disconnect any tags added by someone else through Google Tag Manager (a tool that holds and places tags on a site) before creating new campaigns.
  • If a previous third party paused old campaigns (a third party is someone outside your team, such as a former agency), re-enable them after the correct tags are reinstalled.
  • If they were deleted, rebuild them from scratch.
  • Only one purchase conversion tag should be primary. (Primary means the main tag Google counts.)
  • All the others should be secondary.
  • If the purchase tag set up by the feed app is in use, keep it primary and set the others to secondary.
  • Avoid running two conflicting tag sets from different apps.
  • Conversion tracking for Google is set up through the store's Google sales channel process. (A sales channel is a built-in connection between Shopify and another platform.)
  • A separate tracking tag is not needed if that process already sends conversions.
  • Feed-app tracking tags may first show as Unverified. (Unverified means no real data has confirmed the tag yet.)
  • This is normal. The tags become active once traffic and purchases occur.
  • Google's removal of third-party cookies (small files that track visitors across different sites) and iOS tracking limits (Apple's limits on tracking users across apps and sites) changes how tracking is set up.
  • It does not change whether Google Ads works.
  • Expect the setup to change, but Google Ads to keep working.

Shopify Without the Feed App: Conversion Pixel Route

1 min

Two routes exist in the sources. Use the pixel app route only when no feed app handles the tags. (2023) advises against installing a separate pixel app when the feed app is used.

  • Conversion tracking on Shopify no longer requires editing theme code. (Theme code controls how the shop looks.)
  • A conversion-pixel app from the Shopify App Store (Shopify's store of add-on apps) installs the latest Google Ads pixel automatically.
  • Coding the pixel by hand means editing Liquid files (Shopify's theme code files). This is an advanced, optional route that is harder to maintain.
  • Install Google Ads conversion tracking on Shopify without code:
    1. Open the Shopify App Store and search for Google Ads conversion pixel (listed under apps.shopify.com).
    2. Install the app and log in to it.
    3. Link your Google Ads account inside the app. This takes a minute or two.
    4. Confirm in Google Ads under Settings, Measurement, Conversions that a conversion action (the named goal Google counts) for the store (purchases) has been created automatically.
    5. Expect the status unverified until the first real conversion arrives. The status then changes to active.
  • In Google Ads, under Settings, Measurement, Conversions, the automatically created Shopify conversion action shows status unverified until the first real purchase is tracked.
  • After that, it becomes active.
  • It then tracks each conversion with a 30-day click-through conversion window. (This is the number of days after a click during which a sale still counts.)
  • The default conversion window is 30 days.
  • A click on day 1 still counts if the purchase happens within the following 30 days.
  • The conversion is attributed to (credited to) the original click date.
  • A Shopify store without a developer (a person who writes code) can get the following working in roughly 10 minutes:
    • Google Ads conversion tracking
    • The Google Analytics link (Google's free visitor statistics tool)
    • enhanced ecommerce (detailed shop data reporting)
    • remarketing tags
  • It uses the app and the Shopify integration instead of manual code.

Verifying Tags

1 min
  • Verify Google Ads and Google Analytics tracking on a Shopify store with Google Tag Assistant (a free browser tool that checks tags):
    1. Install the Google Tag Assistant browser tool.
    2. Open the live Shopify store in the browser.
    3. Check that the Google Ads remarketing tag is active on the site.
    4. Check that the global site tag (Google's universal tracking code, which carries other tags) is live.
    5. Refresh the page and confirm that the Google Analytics code loads.
    6. If any tag is missing, recheck the linked IDs in the app or in the Shopify Google Analytics settings before relying on the data.
  • Check tracking status in Google Ads.
  • Optionally use Google Tag Assistant to confirm tracking is working.

Purchase-Only Conversion Signal

1 min
  • Keep only Purchases as the conversion signal in Google Ads. (The conversion signal is the goal Google learns from.)
  • Remove page views, lead form submissions (people filling in a contact form) and all other actions.
  • This way Google optimises for buying, not browsing.

Search Console and Indexing

1 min

Search Console is Google's free tool that shows how your site appears in Google search.

  • Google Search Console is free to set up.
  • It shows impressions (how often your pages appear in search), clicks and keywords (the search words people use) for your store.
  • Add the store there to see detailed search data.
  • Google Search Central (formerly Google Webmaster Tools, the old name) gives detailed reporting on impressions, clicks and keywords.
  • Link your store there to see which search terms bring traffic (visitors).
  • Search Console shows whether your website and product pages are indexed (stored by Google so they can appear in results).
  • It shows this for Google organic results (free search results, not ads).
  • It also flags errors that stop Google from reading the site.
  • After setup and verification (proving you own the website), Search Console lets products rank organically in Google Search results.
  • It brings buyer-intent traffic (visitors ready to buy) that grows over time.
  • Google Webmaster Tools tells Google to crawl (visit and read) the website regularly and detect new content, including star reviews.
  • Configure this so rich snippets (extra details in search results, such as star ratings) appear once products receive reviews.
  • Request indexing of new product pages and blog pages in Search Console. This gives faster crawling and indexing.
  • Indexing check:
    • If Merchant Center shows 2,000+ non-indexed pages with odd URLs while about 250 indexed pages are normal, the non-indexed pages are variants or images. (Variants are versions of the same product, such as different colours or sizes.)
    • If 250 normal URLs are indexed, the store is fine. No action is needed.
  • Use the same Google Workspace email (your business Google account) for Search Console, Merchant Center and Google Analytics.
  • Domain verification (proof that you own the web address) is then completed through Search Console automatically.

Call Tracking

2 min
  • Google Ads provides free call tracking, limited to AdWords traffic. (AdWords is the old name of Google Ads.)
  • Paid call tracking tools (such as CallRail, PhoneWagon and Call Tracking Metrics) track calls from all traffic sources.
  • They integrate with Google Analytics.
  • They offer more complete attribution (credit for which ad led to the call), but at a cost.
  • The global site tag must be installed on your website for call conversion tracking to work.
  • If Google Analytics or other Google conversion tracking is already set up, the tag is present and no new code is needed.
  • If you enter your actual website phone number during setup, Google scans your website and replaces all instances with the forwarding number (a Google number that passes calls to your real phone).
  • If you skip this step, you must update the HTML code manually. (HTML is the code behind your web pages.)
  • Set up call conversion tracking:
    1. In Google Ads, navigate to Conversions (search for conversions in the top menu).
    2. Click New Conversion.
    3. Select Track phone calls, then choose Calls on website (not call extensions or call-only ads). (Call extensions show a phone number under an ad. Call-only ads are ads that only lead to a call.)
    4. Enter a conversion name such as Lead. (A lead is a contact request.)
    5. Optionally set a revenue value. Leaving this blank is acceptable.
    6. Choose whether multiple calls from the same person count as 1 conversion or as multiple conversions (typically 1 in total).
    7. Set the minimum call duration to count as a conversion. 20 seconds is standard.
    8. Confirm the lookback window (how many days back a click can still count) is set to 30 days.
    9. Confirm the attribution model (the rule that decides which click gets credit) is set to Last click (the last click before the call gets the credit).
    10. For the conversion tracking tag, confirm or select Global site tag if Google Analytics or other conversions are already set up.
    11. Enter your current website phone number in the phone number field.
    12. Google will automatically find and replace this number with a forwarding number on your website.
    13. Click Next to complete setup.
    14. Verify tracking is working by checking the tracking status and optionally using Google Tag Assistant.

Account Separation per Store

2 min
  • Each store should have its own separate Google accounts and its own email address on all Google properties (Google services such as Ads, Analytics and Merchant Center).
  • This makes it easier to sell one store later, because its data and ad history are not mixed with the others.
  • Use a separate Google Ads account for each business.
  • Each business needs its own separate set of Google accounts.
  • Do not send product feeds (product lists) from multiple businesses into one Merchant Center account.
  • Do not run ads for multiple businesses from one Google Ads account.
  • Each unrelated store needs its own Google Ads account.
  • Stores in the same network that can cross-promote to each other's customers (promote each other to the same buyers), such as a bounce house store, a trampoline store and a swing set store, can share one account.
  • Do not run two separate Google Ads accounts for the same store, because they compete with each other.
  • Splitting the data across accounts also slows the learning of each account. (Learning is the period when Google's system adjusts to your data.)
  • Ads for the same store from two accounts are only less harmful if one account runs video ads and the other runs Shopping ads (product ads) on non-overlapping products (products that do not overlap).
  • Even then, this setup is not recommended, because both accounts learn slowly.
  • Google and Facebook ad systems learn from data concentrated in a single account.
  • Spreading data across several accounts makes each one learn more slowly.
  • Two stores in the same market selling the same products can compete against each other in Google Ads.
  • If you run two stores in one market, keep their product ranges different.
  • Multiple Google Ads accounts under different business entities (separate registered companies), with different email addresses and billing information, are allowed.
  • Each account is managed separately, and issues with one do not typically affect the others.
  • For a new business, create a new Google Ads account.

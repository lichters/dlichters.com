Playbook › 10. Google Ads and Shopping

Account suspensions, appeals and Google support

Section 10.22 · 22 of 23 in this chapter · 25 min read

In short

  • Google can block your store account (a suspension), but a block is not a deletion, and most blocks can be fixed.
  • Most blocks come from mismatches: your store's shipping, tax, phone, prices or policies differ from what Google has on file.
  • Never make up a product barcode number (GTIN), and never open a new account to get around a block.
  • Fix the cause first, then send a calm, factual appeal (a formal request for review).
  • Keep calling or chatting with Google until a person who can help answers. Follow up on the same case with proof.
More key points
  • Do not open a new Google Ads account while the old one is suspended. Work on the old account first.
  • Do not hand your ads to an agency until your own store makes a profit and you know your starting results.
  • Sources disagree on some routes. Check the year of each piece of advice before you act on it.

Why Accounts Get Suspended

1 min

Merchant Center: Misrepresentation and Data Mismatches

3 min
  • Google suspends Merchant Center accounts mainly for five reasons. Merchant Center is Google's tool that holds your product data.
    • Business information does not match the website.
    • Product data is incomplete.
    • Required business information is missing from the website.
    • Merchant Center settings are incomplete.
    • Products are in prohibited categories (product types Google bans).
  • A suspension is not a deletion. The account is only blocked from use. **
  • Suspensions often carry the reason misrepresentation. This means Google believes some information is missing or wrong. Suspension notices arrive by email to the business email address set up for the account. **
  • A suspension is a legitimacy check (a test of whether your store is real and trustworthy), not a final verdict. Google suspends to protect searchers and to screen out scam or low-quality stores. **
  • New accounts are checked most strictly and are often suspended almost immediately. Established accounts can also be suspended after two years or more of trading. **
  • Google tightened account security after the e-commerce boom in 2020, so suspensions can also hit legitimate (real, honest) sellers. A suspension can be reversed, so fix the cause and request reinstatement (getting the account turned back on) rather than panic. **
  • Common causes of suspension are shipping or tax information in Merchant Center that does not match the Shopify store (your store software), product prices in the catalog (your product list) that do not match the store, missing contact information, and a missing privacy policy. **
  • Missing or inconsistent contact details can cause a contact-information suspension. The contact page should offer at least two contact methods, such as a phone number, an email address or a contact form, plus a privacy policy. **
  • A currency mismatch (the money type does not match), missing contact details or a missing privacy policy can flag an account. Fix these and then appeal. **
  • A missing or unverified (not confirmed) business phone number in Merchant Center is a recurring trigger for misrepresentation. **
  • Shipping costs, the shipping policy and the privacy policy URLs (web addresses) in Merchant Center must match what appears in the Shopify store, because Google bots (automatic visitors) add products to cart to check this.
  • Footer social links (links at the bottom of a page) that point to Shopify social pages or to platform home pages, instead of your own profiles, were a known cause of a Merchant Center suspension. Change them to your own profiles or remove them, then resubmit (send again). (Advice from 2023.)
  • Misrepresentation often comes from missing policy pages and content. Make sure the store has all required policies and pages. **
  • Vague or missing wording in shipping and return policies is a common cause of a suspension without a clear reason. Check that both policies are clear and complete.
  • Never invent a GTIN (a product's barcode number) or enter a third-party GTIN (a barcode that belongs to someone else) that does not belong to the product. Google treats a mismatched GTIN as misrepresentation, which can lead to suspension.
  • A missing GTIN alone does not cause a misrepresentation suspension. The problem arises only when a wrong identifier is placed in the GTIN field (the box where the barcode number goes).
  • A contact detail or policy that differs even slightly from the Merchant Center entry can trigger misrepresentation. Example: a suite number (an office or apartment number) written differently. Compare address, phone, email and every policy character by character with the store.
  • A Merchant Center suspension can have many causes. Check the GTIN, verified phone number, shipping, return and privacy policies, and product detail content, and use the full checklist before escalating to support (asking a higher level of help). **
  • Installed dropshipping apps alone were not considered a cause of account rejection. The likely trigger is a red flag (a warning sign) on the store that a Google bot detects as dropshipping (selling products that a supplier ships for you).
  • Blocking Google's checkout crawler (an automatic visitor that checks the payment page) to hide abandoned checkouts (carts customers left before paying) also leads to automatic feed suspension. The feed is your product data file. Do not try to block it. **
  • Test ads with fake URLs or non-existent websites can get a Google Ads account suspended. Google Ads is Google's paid ad tool. Ads must point to the business website linked to the account. **

Google Ads: Account-Level Reasons

1 min
  • Google Ads accounts are suspended for specific reasons:
    • Incorrect business information.
    • Multiple accounts tied to the same business or personal information, including old dormant (unused) accounts after a prior suspension.
    • A payment method flagged as fraudulent (possible fraud) or previously bounced (refused).
    • Ads that point to a URL (web address) other than the business website, or to a website that does not exist. **
  • A Google Ads suspension applies to the whole advertiser account and every related account. While suspended, you cannot open new accounts, and ads stop running on Search (Google's results page), search partner sites and the Display Network (Google's network of other websites that show ads) at the same time. A store that relies on one paid channel can lose all paid traffic (visitors from ads) at once.
  • Google can flag an ad account as restricted in a country where the advertiser does not sell or advertise. This is usually an automated error (a computer mistake). **
  • For suspected bot clicks (fake clicks from automatic programs), contact Google support by live chat (chatting with a person on the support site) for the recommended course of action before relying on a click-blocking tool alone. **
  • A Google Ads suspension for suspicious payment activity is usually caused by billing details (the name and address on the payment method) that do not match the account. This is covered in the appeal steps below.

Circumventing Systems and Linked Accounts

1 min
  • The circumventing systems suspension (trying to get around a platform's rules) usually occurs when Google believes a new account was opened after a previous account was closed.
  • The circumventing flag often appears when a previously suspended account used the same billing address, credit card or website as a new account. Creating a new account to get around a policy triggers this flag.
  • Google can link new ad accounts to earlier ones through IP address (your internet address), cookies (small files a website saves in your browser), name, address and card details. Creating a new ad account after a suspension therefore usually fails. **
  • A suspension on one platform does not automatically affect other platforms. Accounts on Microsoft, Facebook, TikTok, Pinterest and Instagram can keep running. **
  • Using agency accounts (accounts run by an outside ad company) to circumvent bans on Google Merchant Center is not recommended. It can lead to a cycle of banned accounts and costs a lot of money.
  • Only use .com domains (web addresses ending in.com) for the store. Do not switch to a.store or.us domain to fix a Google Ads policy problem, because changing domains to get around a flag can look like circumventing systems.
  • The circumventing flag can be a false flag (a wrong warning) when a new account was opened after a previously closed account. If you never had an earlier account, appeal the suspension instead of creating a new one.

Response Procedure After a Suspension

1 min

First Steps

1 min
  1. Do not reply immediately, and do not reply in anger.
  2. Read the suspension reason in Google's email. It may be a warning giving 7 days to fix the issue, or an immediate suspension.
  3. Check that the business information in the account is complete.
  4. Make sure the website follows the business launch checklist (a list of what a store needs before it goes live).
  5. Check product data in the feed for disapproved products (products Google rejected) and missing attributes (missing product detail fields).
  6. Make sure shipping, tax, phone number and other account information match the website exactly.
  7. Delete old, unused Google Ads accounts.
  8. Submit a detailed, neutral response through the form or avenue in Google's email, explaining what was checked and changed and asking for a review.
  9. Expect a review within one to two days, sometimes up to a week. **
  10. Replies asking for unsuspension must be neutral and factual. An angry or terse (short and curt) reply makes the problem worse and delays the review. **
  11. When a Merchant Center issue is unclear, respond calmly, check the official Google policy pages, and ask an expert or coach rather than attempting a risky fix alone. **

Merchant Center Recovery (Current Method)

1 min
  1. Read the compliance checklist (a list of rules to follow) and confirm every recommended setting is in place. **
  2. Log in to Merchant Center and click through all settings, making sure each is complete. **
  3. Check the phone number under contact info in Merchant Center settings.
  4. Check whether the business phone number is verified (confirmed by Google). **
  5. Verify the phone number. Depending on what Merchant Center offers, use the verification by text message, the automated call, or the PIN (a short code) sent by phone call. **
  6. Reply to Google's suspension email and request a review. In the source example, reinstatement came the same day. **
  7. Follow up with Google until the account is reinstated (turned back on).

Merchant Center: Full Review Request

1 min
  1. Log in to Google Merchant Center.
  2. Add the return policy and make the other changes Google asked for.
  3. Find the request review option, which may appear in the first email Google sent.
  4. Submit the request. After the first few reviews, Google may impose a waiting period of about one week before the next submission.
  5. Before requesting review, correct all mismatches. The first suspension round is usually handled by an algorithm (automatic computer rules), and a human reviews the store only after a manual review request (a request for a person to check it).
  6. Read the reason Google gives and fix the mismatched item.
  7. Check that every setting and policy in Merchant Center matches the store exactly.
  8. Submit for a review. If suspended again, request a manual review.
  9. In the manual review request, list each correction you made and state that you checked all Google policies.
  10. Do not request a review again until the problems are fixed.
  11. After a Merchant Center suspension is fixed, wait for Google's review result before changing other platforms. Once confirmed, make sure the same errors do not repeat on Microsoft, Bing or Meta.
  12. Prepare the fix before appealing, so the first appeal succeeds. A failed appeal can be resubmitted.
  13. Manual review requests for suspended products are typically decided within 24 to 48 hours. Use them when a flag appears to be an algorithm misreading rather than a true violation. **

[List each change you made to meet compliance.]

Google Ads: Response Routine

1 min
  1. Identify the suspension reason first by contacting support. Do not create a new Google Ads account with the same email, card on file or URL (web address).
  2. Fix whatever caused the suspension.
  3. Contact Google Ads support by phone first, or by live chat if phone support is unavailable.
  4. Ask the support team to reinstate the suspended account.
  5. Use the Google Ads support page to find the contact options.
  6. Only if reinstatement fails, create another Google account. (Note: the 2025 rule in step 1 and the rule against new accounts in the Avoiding New Accounts section both apply before this step.)
  7. Keep records of every change made. **

Appeal Rules

1 min
  • Google allows a limited number of suspension appeals through the review form. Reaching a person through live chat or phone support is a separate route that can be used in addition to appeals.
  • Google bots (automatic visitors) crawl stores and can flag a store wrongly, which leads to a back-and-forth until a human reviews it. A suspension is often resolved by getting a human to look at the store rather than by repeating automated appeals.
  • Reinstatement sometimes happens when you reach the right person at the right time, so keep following up. **
  • A Google Ads account and a Merchant Center account can be reinstated separately. A Google Ads account may stay suspended for months after Merchant Center is reinstated.
  • A Google Ads appeal for an ad that was not in compliance is a valid route. Do not run ads before the store has real products and is open for business. **
  • After a suspension, first resolve the issues on the existing account before creating a new business account.

Avoiding New Accounts

1 min

Rules Against Opening a Replacement Account

1 min
  • Do not start a new Google Ads account while an existing one is suspended (switched off by Google). Creating a second account to get around a suspension is treated as circumventing the system (dodging the rules) and can lead to a permanent block. **
  • After a Google Ads account suspension, do not create a new Google Ads account. A new one is usually suspended as well, and Google may cite circumvention of policy. **
  • Opening a new ad account right after a suspension can get it blocked or suspended again within minutes. Work through the appeal (the formal request to reverse the decision) on the existing account rather than repeatedly creating replacement accounts.
  • A new account with a different email and card may still be linked if it is opened from the same computer or IP address (the number that identifies your internet connection).
  • Creating a second Merchant Center account to get around a disapproval causes more problems. Fix the disapproval and request review instead. (Advice from 2023.)
  • Do not delete the account and create a new one with the same email, because that is flagged as circumventing policies. **
  • Do not run a new account with the same card or domain (your website address) as the suspended one. (See the conditional route below.)

Conditional New-Account Routes (Conflicting Sources)

To open a new account, use a completely different business entity, new email address, new billing information and new domain URL. Reps, Scams and Unsolicited Offers

1 min
  • Callers who say they are Google ad representatives and offer paid ad management are often third-party sellers (outside companies, not Google). Even genuine Google reps tend to push higher ad spend, so decline such offers and manage the account yourself. **
  • Google reps who call or email about account reviews are sales staff who earn money from higher ad spend, so their advice should not be followed blindly. Google's own sales advice is not a reliable guide for account setup.
  • If the form route gets no answer after an appeal, ask your Google account rep (your named Google contact person) for help, since phone support pointed to a form. Google ad accounts can be suspended without a clear reason, such as an alleged policy circumvention.
  • When a Facebook account is blocked, a direct contact at the platform can help recover funds or restore access. Keep an agency or platform rep contact available for these cases.
  • Be skeptical of agencies offering negative keyword management services (managing the search words your ads should NOT show for), since such services often do not involve the owner's own oversight of campaigns. Hands-on management of campaigns in early stages is a better approach.
  • If any Google account shows errors or problems, contact the platform support or the community for help instead of making ad hoc changes (random one-off edits). Report the error so it can be resolved before the learning phase (the first days when Google's system is still figuring out your ads) ends.
  • Keep requesting live chat with Google through the Ad support center whenever that option appears, and do not give up on the appeal.

When Not to Outsource

1 min
  • Do not outsource Google Ads until the store is already profitable with your own optimization (improving your own ads). Only hire for management or scaling (growing your spend) once you have proven what works. **
  • Do not outsource Google Ads management until the store has a track record of profitable ads and known winning products. **
  • Before raising ad spend, verify the account is profitable. Outsourcing ads to an agency is not recommended until the store is profitable and the baseline performance (your starting results, the benchmark to measure against) is known, since agencies rarely take a brand-new store. **
  • Do not outsource Google Ads early. First prove a baseline on your own account, such as a store live for six months with about 13X return on ad spend (ROAS, the money earned for each dollar spent on ads) over the past four months, before hiring an agency. (Advice from 2023.)
  • Do not outsource Google Ads before you have a documented baseline on your own account. An example baseline is a store live for six months with about 13X return on ad spend over four months, which gives an agency a measurable target. (Advice from 2023.)
  • Do not start ads before the store has real products and is open for business. **

Handling a Google Merchant Center suspension:

  1. Read the email from Google, which usually names a broad reason for the suspension.
  2. Send that reason and a link to the store to support for a review.
  3. Look for a clear pattern in the flagged stores (stores marked as a problem) before changing anything.

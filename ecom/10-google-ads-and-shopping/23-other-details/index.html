Playbook › 10. Google Ads and Shopping

Other details

Section 10.23 · 23 of 23 in this chapter · 19 min read

In short

  • Start paid ads with Google Shopping (product ads showing a photo, price and store name). They reach people who are already searching for your products.
  • Get Shopping working well before you add retargeting (ads shown again to people who visited your store).
  • Your first target: 100 unique products (different items, not copies), about 60 visitors a day, and about one sale every other day.
  • Improve your Google Ads before you change niche (your narrow product market).
  • Google often changes its screens. If a setting moved, look in its new place.
More key points
  • Big, heavy products usually ship with the supplier's own carrier, the shipping company the supplier uses.
  • Do not target ads on the word Shopify. Those ads can be taken down.
  • Send only Shopify's standard order emails. Do not send a separate invoice.
  • Your results depend on your own market, product and niche. Do not copy a strategy exactly.

Launch order and first targets

3 min

Channel priority

  • Use Google Shopping ads as the first paid channel for a high-ticket store. Google Shopping ads are product ads with a photo, price and store name. High-ticket means expensive products.
  • Google Shopping ads show your products to people who are already searching for them.
  • Add Bing Shopping ads as a second search channel. Bing Shopping ads are the same kind of product ads on Bing, Microsoft's search engine.
  • A search-based funnel (the path from first click to purchase) can sell high-ticket products.
  • Customers already search for the product, so social media ads are optional, not required.
  • Google Shopping is the largest source of traffic (visitors) and conversions (sales) for these stores.
  • Get Shopping campaigns working well before adding search, display or discovery ads. Display ads are picture ads on websites.
  • In the first months of a new high-ticket store, most traffic is paid, meaning bought with ads.
  • Spend most of your checking time (diagnostics) on Google Ads traffic.
  • Organic traffic (free visitors from normal search results) usually takes longer to build.
  • The balance shifts toward organic only as the store matures.
  • Going live sooner is better, even if training is still in progress.
  • Learn by working in your ad account (the Google Ads account where your campaigns live) while you study.
  • Then the setup is ready when you finish.

Launch sequence A: Shopping first, then remarketing

  1. Launch the alpha beta Google Shopping campaigns first and keep them live. Alpha beta is the source's name for its Shopping campaign setup.
  2. Then add Facebook remarketing ads. Remarketing shows ads again to people who already visited your store. Retargeting means the same thing.
  3. Then add Google remarketing ads, the same idea on Google.
  4. Keep 90% of the budget on alpha beta.
  5. Split 10% between Facebook and Google remarketing.

Launch sequence B: Shopping, then brand search, then product search (2023)

  1. Launch Alpha and Beta Shopping campaigns with a combined $5 to $30 per day. Then optimize them (improve them) using the data.
  2. Once Shopping sales are consistently profitable, launch a brand search campaign. This is an ad that shows when people search your store name. Use a small budget.
  3. Once specific products are proven sellers in Shopping, build search text campaigns (plain text ads in Google search results) around those product pages.
  4. Use the keywords (the search words) that already drive those products' Shopping sales.

Launch sequence C: New dropshipping store (Dropshipping means you sell products that a supplier ships to customers for you. Undated.)

  1. Launch the store and get Google Shopping ads running as soon as possible.
  2. Test the full checkout (the steps a buyer takes to pay) with your own credit card. Then cancel the order.
  3. Upload at least 100 unique products to act as a broad product test.
  4. Aim for 60 unique visitors per day (different people, each counted once) from Google Shopping only. Hold off on retargeting and Facebook ads for now.
  5. Expect about one sale every other day, even at a low conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy). This is the baseline for further growth.

Baseline targets for a new store

  • The baseline is 100 unique products.
  • It also means 60 unique visitors per day through Google Shopping, and about one sale every other day.
  • The source presents this as the minimum before outside help makes sense.

Conflict: when retargeting starts

  • Sequence A adds retargeting right after the Shopping campaigns are live.
  • Sequence C holds retargeting and Facebook ads back until Shopping alone reaches the baseline above.

Launch on Google and Bing (2019)

  1. On day one, set up Google Shopping product listing ads. This is another name for Google Shopping ads. It takes about 20 minutes.
  2. Set up Bing right after. Bing can transfer the Google setup with little extra work and syncs with it.
  3. Hold off on Google search text ads until the store is making money and the best sellers are known.

Funnel and channel mix

2 min

High-ticket launch funnel

  1. Run ads on Google Shopping, where customers first find the products.
  2. Make the site available to search engines so it appears in free organic listings (normal, unpaid search results).
  3. Capture visitors' email addresses (collect them into an email list) while they browse. Then email them to bring them back to purchase.
  4. Show retargeting ads to visitors who viewed products but did not buy.
  5. After the first sale, use email and retargeting to bring the same customer back for a second and third purchase.

Four channels to run the store

  • Master four channels to run the store. They are Google Shopping ads, organic search engine optimization (SEO, getting your pages to rank in free search results), email marketing (messages sent to your list) and retargeting.
  • These channels are enough to generate large revenue in a good market.
  • Keep Google Ads running if they are profitable. Do not treat them as the only source.
  • The framework's minimum set of four traffic channels is Google Ads, organic search, social media and YouTube.

Adding social and other ad types

  • Run a brand search campaign, a retargeting campaign and a Performance Max campaign alongside Facebook ads. Performance Max is one Google campaign type that runs across many Google places.
  • The combination adds traffic from several sources.
  • Google's Performance Max shows ads on places such as YouTube and websites.
  • Once a brand has consistent results, adding Google and Facebook ads gives omni-channel presence (being present wherever customers browse).
  • Visitors who leave without buying can be reached again through Google ads and retargeting.
  • Traffic sources beyond Google Shopping include Google Discovery (ads in Google's feeds), Display (picture ads on websites), YouTube and search text ads.
  • Facebook dynamic remarketing (ads showing the exact products a visitor viewed) is the next strongest source after Google.
  • Banner ads (picture ads on a website) or sponsored articles (paid articles on a niche blog) have been consistently profitable for drop-shipping stores. A niche blog is a blog on one narrow topic.
  • Shopping and search can be complemented by other traffic sources. Transfer-of-influence blog banner ads borrow a blog's trust.
  • Use them for products with color or design searches that do not match a SKU (the product's own stock code) or brand queries (searches for your brand name).

Conflict: social ads

  • In a search-only high-ticket funnel, social ads are optional.
  • Where Google Shopping and a brand are already running, Facebook ads are added alongside them. No stage condition is given.

Platform comparison and print-on-demand

  • Google Ads had the highest return of the platforms compared at the time. Return means sales earned per dollar spent on ads.
  • YouTube ads, TikTok ads and Pinterest ads followed.
  • Instagram placements (ad spots inside Instagram) performed worse. Snapchat burned money quickly.
  • For a print-on-demand store (products printed only after an order) that needs its first 1,000 in sales, paid ads are needed.
  • Google Ads was described as the most reliable and consistent channel for this model.
  • Ads with product images are the entry route, meaning the first way in.

Scaling into advanced Google Ads types

1 min

When to add more types

  • Stores that reach roughly $50,000 to $100,000 per month in revenue are at the stage where mixing in additional Google Ads types makes sense.
  • These stores can no longer grow Google Ads spend (money put into ads) beyond about 10% extra ad spend.
  • The source wording on the 10% figure is unclear.
  • Advanced Google Ads types are mostly a testing ground for typical stores.
  • They can get good results, but they are less proven and less profitable than the core Google Ads setup.
  • So run them as tests, not as your main plan.

Optimize before changing niche

  • Google Ads optimization (improving it) comes before a niche change.
  • A new niche is not the answer when a store is making sales.
  • First reduce the budget. Reintroduce spend as campaigns become more efficient.

Budget, bidding and cost signals

1 min

Spend and cost reference

  • Example Google Ads spend over 90 days: $100,000.
  • Search volume cannot be increased by spending more money on Google Ads. It reflects what the market searches for.
  • Focus on keyword choice and product listing quality instead.

Competition and cost signals

  • A product with no paid competitors in Google Shopping typically means cheap clicks. (2023)
  • Third-party click protection services (paid tools from outside companies that claim to block fake clicks) are not worth paying for.
  • Google already blocks invalid clicks (fake or useless clicks).
  • In one test, a click protector did not reduce traffic or improve return on ad spend (ROAS, sales per dollar of ads).

Time in the ad account

  • When hours in the ad account are cut back to about 30 minutes for routine data checks and small tweaks, the freed time should go to more important work such as SEO.
  • Extra time in the ad account is justified only for setting up a new account, building new campaigns, or making major structural changes.

Campaign setup and account settings

1 min

Campaign list filters

  • In Google Ads, open the Campaigns view. Set the Campaign status filter to 'All but removed', so paused and limited campaigns appear alongside active ones.

  • Without this filter, you can miss shopping campaigns that hold useful search term data (the search words that led people to your ads).

  • Hide removed campaigns:

    1. Open the campaigns view.
    2. Use the filter that controls campaign status or campaign type.
    3. Select All but removed.

    Removed campaigns still exist in the account and cannot be deleted. The red X entries disappear from view.

Account availability

  • Google Ads may show that Buy on Google (a Google program for selling products in Google) is unavailable for businesses registered in Canada. (2023)
  • Before concluding that you cannot list products, search Google's documentation for your country.
  • Then open a live chat or support ticket (a written request for help) with Google.

Niche research on Google Shopping

2 min

Validation thresholds (validation means proof that a niche is worth it)

  • 10,000 monthly search volume in Google Ads is the minimum required for niche validation. Search volume means how many people search a term each month. (2023)
  • The niche's own research should decide the niche.
  • The ranking of competitor stores in organic search results does not decide whether a niche is viable.
  • Competitor stores to target as future competitors should rank between 1 and 100,000 on SimilarWeb (a site that estimates website traffic).
  • Use the rank within the country where the business plans to sell, not the global rank. (2024)
  • Niche research tool: the source's own site It provides a keyword research tool and a competitor research tool.
  • The competitor research tool shows Alexa traffic data (website traffic estimates) for niche validation.

Reading Google Shopping results

  • If searches show mostly cheap items for a niche you think is luxury, raise the price filter in the left sidebar of Google Shopping.
  • Then check whether the brands and volume still appear in your target price range.
  • Use Standard Shopping data for keyword and product research only.
  • Sponsored results (paid spots) at the top of Google Shopping show one store per ad, not the full competitor set. (2024)
  • Standard Shopping data for a product shows up to 20 reviews only if the scraping limit (how many results the tool collects) and pagination (moving to the next page of results) are set. (2024)
  • Check the review count before analyzing product demand.

Pricing outliers and sellers

  • Prices on Google Shopping can be skewed by outliers (prices far above or below the rest). (2023)
  • Include high-priced items only if they are relevant to what you would sell.
  • Exclude them when they are unrelated or obvious pricing errors.
  • If a niche's average price is distorted by low-priced results, add a qualifying keyword to the search, such as family size or luxury. (2023)
  • The keyword should match the products you actually want to sell.
  • Refining the keyword is better than excluding products one by one.
  • Exclude Etsy listings from supplier-based calculations, because they are unique sellers. (2023)
  • Include Etsy results when judging what customers actually see.
  • Include evergreen eBay stores (steady, long-running sellers) in price averages.
  • Exclude one-off auction listings.
  • A niche's first-page Google Shopping competition is healthy when more than half of the top results come from ten or fewer stores. (2023)
  • If more than half come from many stores, refine the niche before launching.

Competitor tracking

1 min
  • The Search Monitor (a tool that tracks competitors' Google Shopping ads) shows which sellers appear for your product searches and at what price. (2023)
  • The Search Monitor is a dedicated monitoring tool for Shopping campaigns.
  • It tracks how Shopping ads appear and perform in search results. (2023)

Search terms, content and SEO

2 min

Using search terms to find content

  • To find content ideas, review the search term report (the list of what people typed before clicking your ad) in a Google Shopping campaign. Look for informational queries (questions people ask to learn) that do not fit a product page, such as beginner-level questions.
  • Exclude those terms from the shopping campaign, so they stop spending on product ads.
  • Then create a content page for each one. Promote it with a Google Search text ad.
  • Move the visitors from that page into the email list and retargeting audiences (groups of past visitors for ads).

Anchor content for organic traffic

  1. Identify top traffic products in Google Analytics (a free tool that counts visitors and their pages). Go to Content > Pages and sort high to low.
  2. Customize the top 20% of product pages by traffic. Add unique descriptions, additional photos, and embedded videos (videos placed on the page).
  3. Build 3-5 'anchor blog posts' (large, valuable articles that form the core of your content). They should provide massive value and incorporate quotes/contributions from authority figures in your niche.
  4. Email anchor posts to your list and share them on social media.
  5. Reach out to contributing authorities to request they share the content, expanding reach.

Tools for the content work

  • Google Page Speed Test measures website loading speed and identifies specific technical errors affecting performance.
  • A score under 75% indicates optimization is needed. This directly affects Google organic search rankings.
  • Google Analytics (GA) Content > Pages shows which product pages receive the most traffic.
  • Sort high to low for the last quarter to identify your top 20% of performers for optimization focus.

SEO rules

  • A topic called 'Search Engine Domination' (2.5 hours) covers site speed optimization, SEO implementation, and compliance.
  • Follow this instead of hiring outside SEO companies.
  • Do not outsource backlinks (links from other websites to yours) or backlink services.
  • Google's algorithms (the rules that rank pages) advanced significantly.
  • There is a risk of a sandbox penalty (Google holding back a new site's rankings). There is also a risk of losing organic traffic.
  • Instead, focus on content and product pages that naturally earn links.
  • Content pages attract backlinks much more easily than product or collection pages.
  • Terms that need links to reach page one (the first page of results) should therefore be targeted with content pages.
  • Shopping ads still show products above AI-generated results (answers written by AI) on Google. (2023)
  • So product searches are less affected by AI tools than content searches.
  • The main impact of AI results is on content and blog visibility.

Other channels and marketplaces

3 min

Fallback when Shopping is unavailable

  • Where Google Shopping is not available, run Google search text ads using your supplier names.
  • Also research local shopping comparison engines (sites that list products from many stores). Many sellers use them as an alternative channel.
  • For products Google does not allow, use secondary ad networks (smaller ad platforms).
  • BuySellAds (a marketplace where website owners sell ad space) lists websites where ads can be bought.
  • Bing is generally less strict than Google.

Google Search and Bing text ads

  • Google and Bing ads (text and shopping ads) can drive traffic to your Amazon listing (your product page on Amazon).
  • Conversion rates are significantly lower than Amazon-native ads (ads inside Amazon). Google shoppers are researching, not buying.
  • Bing sometimes outperforms Google, depending on niche.
  • These channels warrant minimal budget unless you discover an unusually profitable angle.

Amazon

  • Google Ads to Amazon product listing means direct traffic to an ASIN (Amazon's product ID number).
  • In the supplement category, this was tested but did not become profitable. It broke even initially, then lost money.
  • Not recommended for all products. Results vary by category and competition level.
  • As of 2015, Amazon product ads were being discontinued. Amazon's shoppers could still be reached through the Google Display Network (Google's network of websites that show image ads).
  • Do not plan around Amazon product ads as an acquisition channel (a way to win new customers).

eBay

  • eBay Promoted Listings uses a percentage slider. Set an ad rate such as 1 percent. You only pay that percentage when the item sells.
  • Promoted items are placed higher in search rankings.

Direct site and blog advertising

  • Craigslist (a free classified ads site) is a quick, short-term way to get early sales without ads. Use it in the top two cities where you operate.

    1. Post a photo of your bestseller.
    2. State that the products are in stock and can be delivered within the city (for example Manhattan, within 3-5 business days).
    3. Answer the emails that come in to start conversations.

    This is only a very early tactic and does not replace long-term traffic sources.

  • When a site sells ads directly, check its advertising section for separate listing types. Examples are single product listings, multi-product listings and banner advertising.

  • Note the advertising email in your spreadsheet.

  • Banner ads are usually the easiest format to get as a free trial, so start with a trial there.

  • Outreach template for blog owners (a ready message for contacting site owners):

Hey, we came across your website and love what you publish. We would love to write a guest post for your blog. Or alternatively, we would like to take out a banner ad in your sidebar, since I noticed you are not currently running any ads on the site. If you're open to either, I'd be happy to share more details and discuss pricing.

Geo-targeting

  • Geo-targeted ads (ads limited to chosen places) will not be shown to users outside the target country.
  • Check how your ads appear in the target market using a VPN (a tool that makes your connection look like it comes from another country) when you are abroad.
  • Do this only in a separate browser. Logging into your Shopify admin (the back office of your Shopify store) through the VPN can freeze a payout (the money the platform pays you).

Ad costs, seasonality and market data

2 min

Downturns

  • During the April 2020 COVID-19 lockdowns, Google ad costs decreased by 20%.
  • In the same period, Facebook ad costs decreased by 15%. The drop came from reduced advertiser competition and increased user search and social activity.
  • The same fall in ad costs appeared on Google too. The decrease was smaller than on Meta (the company behind Facebook).
  • During economic downturns with reduced advertising competition, maintain current ad budgets.
  • Shift strategy toward building remarketing audiences for when consumer confidence returns.
  • Higher lead volume (more potential customers) at the same ROAS compounds future revenue, meaning it builds on itself over time.

Reported market figures (dated)

  • Reported for Q1 2018 (first quarter of 2018): paid search ad spend grew 21% year over year (compared with the same period a year earlier).
  • Q4 (fourth quarter) saw seasonal spend increases in heavy e-commerce periods.
  • Shopping ads typically grow by double digits each year. They drive most non-branded revenue in paid search, meaning revenue not from searches for your brand name.
  • Reported for Q4 2017 compared with Q4 2016: desktop ad spend in e-commerce search grew about 20% year over year.
  • Mobile shopping was described as rising too.
  • Treat these as 2017 to 2018 figures. Check current data before using them for planning.

Google Ads interface and changes

  • Google moved its older AdWords interface toward retirement after the 2018 rename. AdWords was the old name of Google Ads.
  • So new campaign work should be done in the current Google Ads interface (the screen you work in).
  • Older tutorials and screenshots may show menus and layouts that no longer exist.
  • Google often runs several interface versions at once. It tests changes on subsets of users.
  • So the Google Ads screen may look different from one day or one account to the next.
  • Do not assume a setting has disappeared just because its location or appearance differs.
  • Google changes the look of the Google Ads interface often, sometimes weekly to monthly.
  • Changes to how the ads actually work are much rarer.
  • When a setting seems missing, look for it in its new location. The underlying option is usually still there.
  • When the Google Ads screen differs from a guide, focus on the outcome or function you need, not the exact menu position.
  • The option usually still exists under a new label or placement.
  • The underlying mechanics of how ads run change far less often than the layout.

Store operations and shipping

1 min

Heavy items

1 min
  • A supplier is the company that sells you the products. For large, heavy products, the supplier's own carrier usually does the shipping. A carrier is the shipping company that moves the goods.
  • About 70 percent of suppliers ship on their own. (2023)
  • About 30 percent require the store to use a carrier.
  • Such a carrier can be Freight Center (freightcenter.com), a freight company that ships large loads.
  • The store then needs a bill of lading. This is the shipping paper that proves the carrier took the goods. (2023)
  • Freight Center (freightcenter.com) gives shipping cost quotes. A quote is a price estimate.
  • The quotes run from the supplier's location to several US cities.
  • They are used for pricing calculations, which means working out your product prices.

Order and product rules

1 min
  • For products without a clear default price, use the default quantity. A default quantity is the amount a buyer starts with.
  • Use the amount that the top competitors (your biggest rival stores) use on their own product pages.
  • Offering cards, invoices, or tracking: use only Shopify's default order confirmations. Shopify is online store software. Default order confirmations are the automatic messages Shopify sends after an order. Tracking is the code that shows where a parcel is.
  • A separate invoice is not sent.
  • Do not use ad targeting for the word Shopify. Ad targeting means choosing who sees your ad.
  • Ads that target this word can be taken down. (2023)

Team and hiring

1 min
  • Loom is a tool that records your screen, your voice and your webcam (the camera on your computer). It is used for team training.
  • Save recordings into folders such as team training, order processing or supplier training.
  • You can share recordings with chosen email addresses.
  • A marketing hire is a new person who works on marketing.
  • When choosing which marketing hire to add first, PPC can be prioritized (put first) over SEO.
  • PPC means pay-per-click: you pay each time someone clicks your ad. SEO means search engine optimization: getting visitors from free search results.
  • Choose PPC if the owner wants a quicker return on investment (ROI). ROI is the money you earn back from the money you spend.
  • PPC needs a budget that covers ad spend (the money paid for ads) within the operating expenses allocation. Operating expenses are the monthly running costs of the business.

Strategy adaptation

1 min
  • Your account results depend on your specific market, product and niche. A niche is the group of customers you sell to.
  • Adapt the strategy to your own situation. Do not copy it exactly.
  • Early results may be weaker than expected. The setup is still being fine-tuned on both the ads and the website.

Habits

1 min
  • Keep learning all the time.
  • Use blogs, podcasts (audio shows), videos and news about paid advertising.
  • Staying curious about how to push campaigns further is part of keeping them profitable. Campaigns are your paid ad runs.

  • When stuck on a problem, such as Google Ads, book a coaching call. A coaching call is a session with a coach, a person who guides you.
  • Solve that problem. Repeat the practice until it feels comfortable. Then move on to the next problem.

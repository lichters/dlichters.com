Playbook › 6. Conversion optimization

Urgency, scarcity and promotions

Section 6.12 · 12 of 22 in this chapter · 21 min read

In short

  • Expiring codes push people to decide now. Use a three-day countdown, the recommended setting.
  • Only show deadlines and stock numbers that are true. Fake ones lose trust and can get you in trouble.
  • Show the same code and offer everywhere: the top bar, homepage, product pages and emails.
  • The code in Shopify must match what the site says, or buyers won't get the price.
  • Fake urgency (false pressure, like fake timers or fake stock counts) destroys trust. Do not use it.
More key points
  • Use scarcity (having only a few left) only when it is real.
  • For expensive products, an application step makes buyers feel chosen. Only state rules you truly enforce.
  • Pop-ups distract buyers. Use an exit box only as a last chance, and only if your profit allows it.

Why urgency and expiring offers work

1 min
  • Urgency means pressure to act now.
  • Expiring coupon codes (discount codes that stop on a set date) create urgency. They force the visitor to decide. They convert better than permanent discount offers. To convert means a visitor becomes a buyer.
  • Expiration forces a yes-or-no choice: claim the discount now or risk missing it. This reduces hesitation and speeds up purchase decisions.
  • In one store, expiring coupon codes doubled sales compared with codes that never expire. Introducing coupons without expiration first raised sales by 30%.
  • Most potential customers decide within two to three days. An expiring coupon speeds that decision up.
  • Estimated delivery dates reduce hesitation and support questions. Both increase the conversion rate, meaning the share of visitors who buy.
  • Most visitors decide within two to three days of first finding a store. Some buy right away and others take much longer.
  • Visitors get distracted by other tabs, devices, messages and competitor sites. So they put off buying.
  • A visible deadline pushes visitors who are close to buying to decide during the visit.
  • Visitors who search for the exact products you sell are already near a decision.
  • A time-limited offer shown at that moment shortens the time between interest and buying.
  • An expiring coupon on the product page shows a deadline. It pushes visitors to decide now instead of later.
  • Urgency should come from the link between the customer's problem and your solution. It should not come from invented scarcity (fake rarity).
  • Discounts, limited stock and time-limited offers create urgency only up to a point.
  • The strongest urgency comes from the customer's problem. Show that the product solves it now and that buying is the only step left.
  • Stores with products people truly want usually need no countdown timers or fake scarcity.
  • twice-weekly coupon codes that expire in 2 to 3 days create urgency on product pages. Most first-time visitors decide within that time.

Which products suit expiring codes

1 min
  • Expiring codes suit high-ticket products. High-ticket means expensive items. Customers buy them only every few years.
  • About 90% of site visitors are first-time visitors. They have never seen the code.
  • For low-ticket recurring products, meaning cheap items bought again and again, the same code is a problem. Repeat visitors see it again and again, so it likely works less well.
  • Use scarcity only when it is genuine, and use it sparingly. Scarcity means making something look rare or limited.
  • If a product is always on sale, or sales run every week, repeat visitors stop reacting to urgency.
  • The average e-commerce customer has about seven touchpoints before buying. A touchpoint is any contact with the brand, such as an ad, an email or a visit.
  • So many shoppers return several times. A scarcity message that stays on the page for weeks loses credibility.

  • Keep the coupon text limited to what the code actually grants.
  • Benefits that apply to every order anyway, such as free shipping on all orders, should be removed from the coupon message.
  • Test different discount percentages over time to optimize conversion. Optimize means improve.

Shopify coupon rules

1 min
  • Coupon codes cannot be combined. If a 5% and a 10% code both exist, the customer picks one. They do not get 15% off.
  • A coupon discount is applied in the cart when the code is entered, not on the product page.
  • Product prices shown on the store stay at MAP. MAP is the compare-at price, the higher price shown next to the real price. The discount appears only at checkout.
  • Shopify's coupon settings can exclude specific products from a promotion.
  • Set the exclusion in the Shopify promotion settings where coupon codes are configured.
  • In those same settings, exclude the products that are not eligible.
  • The discount then applies only to eligible products in the cart.
  • Coupon codes can be set to show only on eligible product pages. The discount is then not shown on non-eligible products.
  • The code shown in the bar must match a discount code that already exists in Shopify.
  • The bar only displays the code. Shopify applies it at checkout.

Announcement bar: placement and behaviour

2 min
  • The announcement bar is a thin strip at the top of the page.
  • Show the discount code prominently on every page. Use a bar or banner that stands out, so every visitor sees it on arrival.
  • The announcement bar appears on every page, including the visitor's first view.
  • Use it for the most important site-wide offer or information.
  • The bar is used for urgency and conversion. It only makes sense once the store has live products and real traffic.
  • Before that, hide it in the Announcement bar section.
  • The sticky top bar is a bar that stays at the top while the visitor scrolls.
  • Show it on every page, including product pages, on both desktop and mobile.
  • After saving, refresh the storefront (the public shop view). Check that the bar stays visible while scrolling.
  • Keep the bar non-clickable when it applies sitewide. Visitors should see the code and remember it for the cart, rather than leave the page.
  • Do not add a close button, so the offer stays visible.
  • A promotion bar with light text can get lost on the background.
  • Test a higher-contrast text colour, such as black, to make the sale message stand out.
  • Test the bar on all pages and for all visitors, mobile first.
  • The bar can also hold a second short text at the start, such as a highlight word like Hot.
  • When a discount is running, show the discount code in the bar so visitors see it immediately.
  • A sample bar could read: a weekend sitewide sale of 25% off everything, paired with a code for a free gift on orders over a set amount, running for a limited time.
  • Enter both messages as separate announcements in the same bar.
  • Hello Bar, or a similar dynamic notification app (an app that changes its message automatically), can display warehouse status above the Add to Cart button.
  • The text can change based on product collection or supplier. It tells customers the shipping status before they buy.

Announcement bar message patterns

1 min
  • Pattern: state the discount amount and its scope, then the coupon code, then the expiry day, time and time zone.
  • This works because it gives a concrete saving, a clear action (enter the code) and a visible deadline in one line.
    • Example 1: Take 7% off all orders with code SPRING7. Offer ends Wednesday 11:59 p.m. PST.
    • Example 2: Get 5% off your whole order with code HOME5 until Sunday 11:59 p.m. EST.
  • Express the deadline with the day, the time and the time zone, for example Sunday 11:59 p.m. EST.
  • Any time zone works, as long as the deadline is clear and consistent.
  • Announcement bar messages work best when they combine three parts.
  • The three parts are: an offer or benefit, a condition or threshold, and a credibility signal or icon.
  • This keeps the message quick to scan and gives a reason to act.
    • Examples: Free delivery on orders over $60 | 20% off your first order and Trusted by 8,000+ happy customers | Free returns within 30 days.
  • Template for a holiday promotion line. Use it in the announcement bar.
  • While the promotion runs, keep the same wording and code in the homepage slider (the rotating image banner) and in the expiring discount code section on product pages.
[HOLIDAY] promotion live now: [OFFER] with code [CODE], guaranteed delivery before [DELIVERY DEADLINE].
  • If the bar still shows an expired code or an old expiry date after a cycle change, the urgency is lost. Visitors may distrust the offer.
  • Update the bar at every Wednesday and Sunday change.

Header space and scarcity lines

1 min
  • The header bar is the most visible space on the site.
  • Reserve it for the strongest current offer, such as an expiring coupon code.
  • Do not spend it on a generic message that competitors already show, such as a shipping threshold.
  • A header with a scarcity line, such as buy now before we run out of stock, can increase conversion.
  • A limited stock or free shipping today message works too.
  • The reviewer reported that customers wrote in asking for the bundle not to be removed after this header was added.
  • Scarcity and call-to-action messages (CTA, the line that asks for the next step) in the header are a proven conversion tool.
  • They should be used on a store, not left out.
  • Scarcity text is less effective now than before, because many shoppers know the tactic.
  • Use scarcity sparingly and test it rather than relying on it.

Cart top bar and checkout banners

1 min
  • Pattern for an urgency warning in the cart top bar: a warning label, a specific sold-out count over a period, and a call to order now before the product runs out again.
  • The specific count makes the scarcity credible. The deadline phrasing pushes the decision.
    • Example 1: Stock alert: this item has sold out 6 times this year. Secure yours now before it runs out again.
    • Example 2: Almost gone: we sold out twice in the last 30 days. Check out today before the next batch sells.
  • Pattern for a low-stock banner at checkout: a short stock-level statement that the product is running low.
  • Follow it with a clear action to finish the order before the stock is gone.
  • It works best in the checkout banner rather than the cart.
    • Example 1: Units are running low on this bundle. Finish checkout now to lock in today's price.
    • Example 2: Only a few units remain. Place your order today to avoid a backorder wait. (A backorder is an order that waits until new stock arrives.)

Promotion consistency across the site

2 min
  • Promote every offer across the whole website, not only in emails and ads. Then all visitors know the offer exists.
  • Use the notification bar and the exit-intent overlay (a pop-up that appears when a visitor is about to leave) for this.
  • The website must show the current promotion's offer in three places: the homepage slider, the announcement bar and an expiring discount code.
  • These must match the offers in the emails.
  • Use the same promotion message in every place: the announcement bar, the homepage image slider, the discount code section on product pages, and the email and ads.
  • Mismatched codes or percentages, such as 5 percent in one place and 7 percent in another, confuse shoppers and reduce trust.
  • Keep the discount code and offer identical across the announcement bar, the homepage, product pages and the discount code section.
  • Never show 5% in one place and 7% or free shipping in another. Mismatched offers confuse buyers and reduce sales.
  • Show active coupons and brand deals with their expiry dates directly on the homepage and category pages.
  • Burying promotions behind a small link such as "see all sales" hides them from shoppers who are not looking for a sale.
  • Create a permanent collection page for rotating promotions. A collection page is a shop page that groups products.
  • Example addresses: yourstore.com/promotions or yourstore.com/sales.
  • Add and swap products into it as seasonal sales or supplier promotions become available.
  • Customers can bookmark it and return during sales. You manage the included products without creating new collection pages each time. (2023.)
  • Promo bars on product pages must match the product.
  • A generic outdoor sale banner on a chandelier page makes no sense to the shopper. Tailor on-page messages to the product category.
  • Remove banners for past events. A Black Friday banner still showing in May makes the store look neglected.
  • Stale promotions look neglected.
  • use a header banner with an expiring coupon to boost early conversions.
  • Once real traffic arrives, replace an earlier free shipping banner with a 10% off banner.
  • But not before suppliers are on board.
  • The discount is applied at checkout, so product prices stay at MAP (see the Shopify coupon rules above).

Discount size and margin

1 min
  • A discount should only be offered if your margin still covers it. Margin is the profit left after costs.
  • Test whether the discount raises conversion, and base the size on current margins.
  • For high-priced items, fixed-amount offers work better than a percentage with a cap.
  • Example of a fixed-amount offer: spend 1,000 USD and save 100 USD.
  • Percentage discounts on expensive products can still be profitable when margins are large.
  • A 10% discount on a high-ticket sale can still leave thousands of dollars in profit.
  • If the margin does not allow a discount in the announcement bar or exit-intent popup, use a free shipping offer for those locations instead. (2023.)
  • Traffic from TikTok expects strong discounts.
  • Small offers such as 5 or 10 percent off rarely work.
  • So test discounts of at least 20 to 50 percent.
  • A small discount such as 5% off a $3,000 product with a six-hour validity will not change the buying decision.
  • Expensive products need a clear value message, not a token discount.

Countdown timers on product pages

1 min
  • A countdown timer is a clock that counts down to a deadline.
  • On the product page, it shows a short validity window for a special offer, for example 10 minutes. After that the offer disappears.
  • It works when no fixed calendar date is used. It pushes visitors to act much faster than usual.
  • A countdown timer set to about 10 minutes worked well for one seller. That seller had also tested 5 minutes.
  • Configure the countdown timer app so the timer disappears after it expires. Ultimate Sales Boost was the app named.
  • Configure it so the timer does not repeat for the same visitor.
  • A repeating timer can make the offer look fake and hurt sales.
  • A countdown timer next to a limited-time discount for new customers reinforces the deadline. It supports the urgency of the offer.
  • Place it where the offer is visible, such as on the product or landing page.
  • Show the remaining stock count on the product page with a countdown. Buyers then have a reason to decide now instead of later.
  • The author says this works well, using custom code in his Shopify theme.
  • A countdown timer that restarts with every visit looks fake.
  • A timer that shows three hours and resets on every visit removes trust. A returning visitor who sees the same three-hour timer will stop trusting the store.
  • a countdown timer that restarts each day is fake urgency.
  • When a visitor returns and sees the same offer again, trust drops. This is especially true for expensive purchases, where people visit several times.
  • Product pages must show the exact shipping time, warranty and full product details.
  • Do not use fake urgency, such as a countdown timer that constantly resets.
  • Clear information supports the sale without misleading the buyer. (2025.)

Limited quantity and application-based scarcity

1 min

Scarcity means making something look rare, so people want it more. These rules come from direct-response sales letters (long written ads that ask the reader to reply and buy). They apply to high-priced or limited offers only.

  • Scarcity can be used to intimidate (scare buyers into acting) in five ways:
    • limited-quantity wording (saying only a few are left)
    • a bandwagon message (a "most people are buying this" message)
    • a message that only qualified people will be accepted (qualified means they meet your rules)
    • a qualification process before buying
    • a select-group message (saying only a chosen group is invited)
  • Scarcity works best when the offer is honest. A limited number or application process has to be real.
  • Limit the offer to a quantity that is actually limited.
  • State that a response arriving after the supply is gone will be returned, uncashed if needed (the payment is not cashed).
  • Use this only when the stock or spot (place) is truly limited.
  • For high-priced (expensive) products, use an application or qualification step. This is a check that the buyer meets your rules.
  • Examples: a questionnaire (a list of questions), reference letters (letters from people who vouch for the buyer), a required listening period, or an interview.
  • It makes the buyer feel selected.
  • Only state qualification rules you actually enforce.
  • Most of a sales letter's losses come from the later group. These are readers who want the offer but set the letter aside.
  • Most real response (actual replies and orders) arrives in the first days after mailing (after the letters are sent).
  • So the letter must create urgency (a reason to act) now, not tomorrow.
  • Use limited availability to create urgency only if the product, premium (a free extra gift) or discount is truly limited.
  • Say so specifically. For example: the race is on for a limited number of items.

Rules against fake urgency

2 min
  • Fake urgency is dishonest. It can be a stock count (number of items left) or a deadline that resets every day or week. It erodes (wears away) trust (the customer's belief that you are honest). Customers are a business's most important asset, so urgency built on lies damages it.
  • Do not use scarcity messages (messages saying items are rare), such as Limited stock on hand, unless they are true and change over time. A stock message that never changes signals fake scarcity.
  • Use urgency only when it is true. Real limited stock can be stated. Example: a product that is really down to its last 10 units. A blanket claim (one claim for everything) that every product is low on stock is not believable.
  • Fake urgency (false pressure), such as countdown timers (clocks that count down to a deadline) that reset, fake stock levels, or small token discounts (tiny discounts that mean little), is not a recommended way to build urgency.
  • Visitors notice it. It damages trust once discovered.
  • Fake urgency tactics (tricks that make people feel rushed), such as false countdown timers, claimed low stock, or offers that never actually expire, usually give small, temporary conversion gains (more visitors who buy).
  • They are often misleading, so they should not replace a stronger offer.
  • The consideration period (the time a customer takes to decide) cannot be shortened by pressure.
  • People who truly want the product buy it regardless of a small discount. So do not rely on urgency tricks to close the sale (get the purchase made).
  • A strikethrough reference price (a crossed-out "was" price used for comparison), combined with a large You save percentage, shown on a sale that never ends, reads as a fake discount and lowers trust.
  • Show a reference price only when it is a real list or previous price (the official price, or the price before).
  • Make the saving amount match the numbers shown.
  • Case: a Shopify (online store software) reseller (a shop that sells products from other makers) of high-ticket bathroom products (expensive bathroom items) ran a 48 hour sale banner (a strip across the top of the page) for at least three months.
    • The countdown date changed by one day each day.
    • The offer stayed at 5% off with code FLASH5 (a discount code the buyer types in).
    • The page still said Limited Stock On Hand.
    • A $2,999.99 product was shown next to a $4,838.69 reference price, with You Save: $1,838.70 (38%).
    • The store also had Some exclusions apply on its tax statements (notes about taxes).
    • No Shipping to California Addresses, with no explanation.
    • A financing line (a line about paying in parts over time) with no link.
    • Reviews hidden at the bottom.
    • An empty review widget (a review box with nothing in it).
    • Non-working tabs (clickable page sections that do not open).
    • Its USP (unique selling point, the main reason to buy) sat in small header text that did not show on mobile (phones).
    • The verdict: this combination makes the store look like a commodity (an item that looks the same everywhere and is sold on price), and reduces trust.

Pop-ups and overlays

1 min
  • Avoid pop-ups (boxes that appear over the page) to present offers. They distract customers and interrupt the buying decision.
  • Use the notification bar (a thin bar at the top of the site) and the exit-intent overlay (a box that appears when a visitor tries to leave) to make every offer known across the site.
  • If the margin (profit left after costs) does not allow a discount in the announcement bar or exit-intent popup, use free shipping for those locations. (2023.)
  • Source conflict (sources that disagree): one source advises avoiding pop-ups entirely, while two others use exit-intent overlays for offers.
  • Avoid general pop-ups that interrupt the buying decision.
  • Use an exit-intent overlay only as a last-chance offer, and only where the margin supports it.

Playbook › 6. Conversion optimization

Checkout, payments and shipping friction

Section 6.13 · 13 of 22 in this chapter · 23 min read

In short

  • Keep checkout to one or two steps. Every extra step lowers sales.
  • Turn on Apple Pay and Google Pay early. They let buyers pay with one tap.
  • Show all costs, especially shipping, before the cart, not at the last step.
  • Offer several payment methods. Each missing one can make buyers leave.
  • Check the buttons on your live pages. Make sure Apple Pay or Shop Now comes first.
More key points
  • Show when the product arrives on the product page and in the cart.
  • Tell buyers about backorder dates (items not yet in stock) before they pay.
  • Make the checkout (the payment page) look like your store, with trust signals.
  • Test the whole buy flow on a computer and a phone before you send more visitors.

Checkout steps and customization

1 min

  • Keep checkout to one or two steps. Every extra click before payment lowers conversion (the share of visitors who buy).

  • Where possible, use an express payment method, such as Apple Pay. Express means a fast option that skips typing. Apple Pay allows a one-click purchase, because the delivery address is already linked to it.

  • On Shopify (the platform your store runs on), you cannot match express wallets such as Apple Pay or Amazon Pay. Express wallets are payment services that already hold the buyer's card and address.

  • The realistic lever is fewer steps and less friction (anything that makes the buyer stop or think) before checkout.

  • Shopify offers two ways to change the checkout. Checkout is the payment page.

    • A third-party checkout app that fully replaces the Shopify checkout. (Third-party means made by another company.)
    • A theme or app that adds features on top of it. (A theme is a store design template.)

    Some themes already include a faster checkout option.

  • Fast Checkout and Instant Checkout apps skip the add-to-cart page and send users straight to checkout. This reduces friction and can improve conversion rate.

  • ** Do not pay for a one-page checkout tool such as CartHook. Add every payment option available to your customers instead, because such a tool is not necessary for these store models.

  • To change the checkout, open Settings > Checkout and click Customize checkout. Newer Shopify stores may show only a one-page checkout option by default.

  • Simplify the checkout as much as possible. Avoid extra requests that distract or overwhelm the buyer before the main goal is reached.

  • In the checkout customer information section, only last name and company name are required fields (fields the buyer must fill in). Marketing options such as SMS (text messages) stay disabled by default in all regions.

  • Under Customer accounts in Checkout, customer accounts are turned on, and Show sign in links is turned on. No other changes are made to that section.

Express wallets and button order

1 min

Apple Pay and Google Pay

1 min
  • Apple Pay is Apple's phone payment service. It is a top priority for conversion, because customers tap once and skip manual address and payment entry. Enable it before anything else in the checkout setup.
  • Enable Apple Pay and Google Pay alongside Shop Pay Installments (paying in parts over time) in Shopify Payments (Shopify's own payment service) to improve checkout speed and conversions.
  • ** Enable Apple Pay and Google Pay on Shopify when you accept Shopify Payments. A shopper on an iPhone can then complete a purchase in about 30 seconds.
  • Set up Apple Pay in the Shopify store for any new store. If it does not work, contact Shopify support. A domain-related fault (a problem tied to your web address) was fixed within half a day.
  • Keep Apple Pay, Google Pay, and Visa and Mastercard credit and debit cards as the main payment options, since most customers pay with these. Apple Pay gives one-click checkout, which speeds up the transaction.
  • Keep Apple Pay as the visible option. One seller reported that disabling Shop Pay helped, because it can hide Apple Pay at checkout. Most of that seller's US customers had Apple Pay.
  • Reduce checkout effort by offering fast payment options such as Apple Pay or PayPal (an online payment service). At the time it was written, Shopify was working on a one-page checkout to replace a multi-click checkout.

Button order and visibility

2 min
  • Check the payment buttons shown on the product page under Shopify > Settings > Payments. Make sure Apple Pay or a Shop Now button is shown before the Buy with Shop Pay option. Shop Now is a plain button that sends the buyer straight to checkout. Shop Pay is Shopify's fast checkout.
  • Buy with Shop Pay as the first and main checkout button confuses most shoppers who do not have Shop Pay. They hesitate and click add to cart instead. The reviewer advised replacing it with Apple Pay or Shop Now as the first option.
  • If a store's payment methods are limited, contact Shopify support as a worst-case step to get help configuring the checkout buttons.
  • Shop Pay is toggled on (switched on) by default in the checkout area. Turn it off in markets where Shopify Payments and Shop Pay are not available. A market is a country or region you sell to.
  • Accelerated checkout buttons include PayPal, Amazon Pay, Google Pay and Apple Pay, depending on what is integrated in your Shopify admin (the back-office panel where you manage the store). The layout can be set to a number of columns so the buttons take up little space, for example three buttons in one row.
  • Older advice: Turn on "Show dynamic checkout button" on product pages. A dynamic button changes to show the payment method a customer usually uses. It is based on device, shopping history and the options enabled in the store, such as PayPal, Apple Pay or Google Pay. The customer can pay directly without going through the full cart and checkout. The dynamic button only appears for methods that are enabled, so offer these payment options in the store.
  • Express checkout buttons such as PayPal or Venmo (a US payment app) depend on the visitor's geolocation (the country the visitor is in). So you may see a button that other visitors do not. A payment option customers do not recognize may be skipped, so make sure the common options are clear.
  • Shop Pay is Shopify's accelerated checkout, provided through Shopify Payments. You can offer it as an extra payment option next to cards and PayPal. It gives buyers a faster way to pay that can support conversion.
  • Use a direct Shop Now button as the main purchase path (the route the buyer takes to pay) instead of a PayPal cart button. The hosts said they no longer used PayPal much and found it a headache to manage.

PayPal Express buttons

1 min
  • PayPal Express checkout buttons are buttons on the product or cart page that send buyers to PayPal. They can break e-commerce tracking, which means you lose sight of what happens to each order.
  • Buyers leave the site to pay on PayPal and do not always return, so orders go untracked.
  • These buttons also bypass your own checkout, your email system and abandoned-cart sequences. An abandoned-cart sequence is an automatic email sent to buyers who left without paying.
  • Remove these buttons and send all buyers through your checkout, offering the standard PayPal option there.

Payment icons and visibility

1 min
  • Show accepted payment icons, a guaranteed safe checkout note and an accelerated checkout option such as Shop Pay near the buy button. Competitor stores use these to reduce hesitation at checkout.
  • Show payment icons close to Add to Cart so buyers see trusted payment methods before they commit. Include Apple Pay, Google Pay, Shop Pay, PayPal and major cards in the icon row when they are enabled in the store.
  • Show the accepted payment options early, for example in the cart, not only after the shopper fills in the first checkout step. Hiding payment information likely increases abandoned carts.

Payment method coverage

2 min

  • ** Offer several payment methods, including Shopify Payments, Amazon Pay, Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal and PayPal Credit. Amazon Pay accepts credit cards, debit cards, and personal and business cards. Missing options give buyers a reason to leave.

  • ** Offer as many payment methods as possible, including Apple Pay and Google Pay through Shopify Payments, plus Amazon Pay and PayPal. More choices improve conversion.

  • ** The following setup was described for accepting payments:

    • Shopify Payments (cards).
    • PayPal, for buyers who only use PayPal.
    • Amazon Pay, set up in the Shopify back end (the admin side of the store) and paid directly to your bank account.
    • Google Pay and Apple Pay, both enabled in Shopify Payments in the Shopify admin.

  • Accepted payment methods are a basic conversion fix. Each missing method can lose sales from buyers who do not trust the site or use a different wallet (a digital payment account). Check the payment methods before changing the offer or the product page.

  • Payment options such as Apple Pay, Amazon Pay, PayPal and a payment plan can increase conversion, because shoppers have different payment preferences. A missing preferred option can lower conversion, so add the options the store can support.

  • A strong offer can produce sales with only a card processor (the company that handles card payments). So PayPal is not strictly needed to start testing a product.

  • Missing well-known payment methods, however, can lower checkout completion and limit scaling (growing ad spend and sales).

  • Run at least two payment methods: one card processor, such as Shopify Payments or Stripe (a payment company), plus PayPal. This helps avoid conversion drops caused by a missing option.

  • PayPal can accept guest checkout (buying without making an account), but the store landing page (the first page a visitor sees) should explain how to do it.

  • Address payment concerns by clearly showing payment security and the payment options available at checkout.

  • Buy-now-pay-later (BNPL) providers such as Klarna, Zip or Afterpay let price-sensitive buyers spread costs. Mention them when cost is the likely blocker.

Financing and buy-now-pay-later

1 min

Why financing helps

2 min
  • Removing payment friction through financing options increases conversion by making purchases accessible to cash-constrained buyers (buyers short of cash now) who would otherwise abandon.
  • Shop Pay Installments significantly increases conversion rates on high-ticket products (expensive items). It does this by reducing the upfront cost (the price paid right away) and the perceived financial risk customers face. This results in higher average order completion rates.
  • Instalment payment options, such as four interest-free payments, can overcome price objections (buyer doubts about price) by making the purchase feel affordable in smaller payments.
  • Offering pay-over-time financing at checkout can raise conversion. Shoppers who would hesitate at a high price are more willing to buy when they see a small first payment.
  • Pay-over-time financing is especially useful for products priced from several hundred dollars upward.
  • The effect is strongest when the option is visible on product pages, not only at checkout.
  • Financing can raise average order value (the average amount spent per order). Shoppers may choose a more expensive upgrade when they can spread the cost over time.
  • This is most relevant for high-ticket stores, where customers would otherwise trade down to a cheaper option.
  • Offering installments lowers the amount a buyer must pay upfront. This removes a purchase barrier and raises conversion rates.
  • The effect matters most for high-ticket products, where one large upfront payment is most likely to stop a sale.
  • ** Offer financing such as Klarna or Affirm (companies that split a price into payments), or PayPal Credit, as long as the store does not finance the purchase itself. Financing helps with high-ticket items.
  • ** Financing options can help conversion for high-ticket products, especially when customers can pay in four installments. Klarna and Affirm are the main options cited for orders from $500 to $5,000.
  • ** Klarna and Affirm charge no fee to apply or to be approved, so applying to both is free. Application approvals were reported as slow in 2020, so applying early is recommended.
  • Shop Pay Installments is recommended as a payment plan option when using Shopify Payments. Its limits (how much can be financed) have grown over time.
  • Affirm or Klarna can be used if Shopify Payments is not in use.
  • Shop Pay Installments also appears as a selectable payment method at checkout, next to cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay.
  • Shoppers who choose it complete the purchase through the same flow, so no extra checkout step is needed for the merchant (the store owner).

Installment messages on low-priced items: a conflict

1 min
  • ** An installment message (a line showing the price in parts) under a low-priced product does more harm than good. It distracts from the purchase. For example, four payments of about $20 under a $70 item.
  • Shop Pay Installments shows its own message on product pages, with two payments for lower-priced items and four for higher-priced items.
  • Where both apply, check the live product pages (the pages as customers see them now) to see what customers actually see. Then compare with the thresholds in the table above.

Product page messaging

1 min
  • ** Customers who choose financing on high-priced products can be offered a financing option. It is shown on the product page with the installment amount, using the financing provider's built-in Shopify integration. Set the messaging so that it asks whether the customer wants financing and only appears on expensive products.
Customers who choose financing on high-priced products can be offered a financing option that is shown on the product page with the installment amount, using the financing provider's built-in Shopify integration. Set the messaging so that it asks whether the customer wants financing and only appears on expensive products.
  • After activation (turning it on), product pages display a small installment message. Lower-priced items show two interest-free payments. Higher-priced items typically show four interest-free payments of the price divided by four.
  • Formula (a fixed rule you fill in) for the product-page installment message:
pay in [N] interest-free payments of [price divided by N] with Shop Pay

N is 2 for low-priced items and 4 for higher-priced items. The shopper sees a small per-payment amount instead of the full price, which makes the purchase feel affordable.

  • Example for a $30 product: "pay in two interest-free payments of $15.00 with Shop Pay".

  • Example for a $200 product: "pay in four interest-free payments of $50.00 with Shop Pay".

  • Installment messaging on product pages is part of the offer itself. After enabling it, check the live product pages to verify that customers actually see the options. Only then consider the task complete.

Local payment methods

1 min

  • Local payment methods (options popular in one country) can be enabled on Stripe and Shopify Payments:

    • For Stripe, check the payment methods in your Stripe account and turn on the needed option, or ask Stripe chat support to enable it.
    • For Shopify Payments, ask Shopify support to turn the method on for you.

  • Locally preferred checkout methods named for European markets (verify before use, as preferences change):

Country Methods named
Germany Klarna, PayPal
Switzerland Twint
Spain Google Pay, Apple Pay, credit card
Several European countries Bancontact (direct bank transfer)
  • Running ads in a European market with only cards limits both conversion and how far ads can scale. Local methods such as Klarna in Germany or PayPal are important in those markets.
  • Adding a local payment method increased sales noticeably. Customers in the local market trust payment providers that they already know.
  • Show the logo of a local payment method, such as Twint in Switzerland, on the store to build trust.
  • Twint is limited to Swiss companies. The customer scans a QR code (a square code read by a phone camera) to pay.

Currency and price display

1 min
  • Set the store's main currency to the currency of the target market.
  • Buyers who first see USD (US dollars) and then a local currency at checkout lose trust and leave. This happens even if a currency converter (a tool that changes prices into another currency) is available.
  • If most visitors come from a country other than the one shown by default, show prices in that visitor's local currency via geo-targeting (showing content based on the visitor's location).
  • At minimum, display prices in USD when the main ad traffic is from the United States.
  • Check the traffic origin in Google Analytics (a free tool that shows where visitors come from) to decide which currency should be the default.
  • Which rule applies: use the single target-market currency when the store is built for one market. Use geo-targeting when traffic comes mainly from a different country, and USD when the main ad traffic is from the US.
  • Converting prices from a supplier's currency (the currency your supplier, the company that sells you products, uses) with an app's automatic conversion produces odd, uneven price points. Examples are 42.95, 41.36 and 46.13.
  • These make the store look less trustworthy, so align them to clean USD price points.
  • Plain sale prices do not show the original price at checkout.
  • Sale prices show the original price in the cart or checkout only when a coupon code (a discount code the buyer types in) is applied. This is true unless custom code (code written specially for your store) or an app adds it.

Shipping costs and fees

1 min

Show costs early

1 min
  • Show all costs, including shipping, fees and taxes, early in the buying process. Do not reveal them at the final checkout step.
  • Unexpected extra charges at the last step lead to abandoned carts.
  • Show shipping costs and the final price before the cart, for example on the product page.
  • When shoppers add to cart but do not buy, a frequent cause is an unexpected total, often shipping. Visitors should not need the cart to discover the total.
  • Shipping costs and fees should be shown before checkout to avoid shock.
  • Customers who return a product because of an unexpected extra payment are a sign of hidden fees.
  • The most common reason for checkout abandonment (buyers leaving the checkout without buying) is a shipping fee that the buyer did not see before reaching the checkout page.
  • Show shipping costs before checkout to prevent this.
  • A shipping fee of around $5 at checkout can stop buyers who expected free shipping.
  • Offering free shipping or showing shipping costs earlier can prevent drop-off (buyers leaving) at checkout.
  • Older advice: For buyers who are anxious, state the shipping method and all shipping costs clearly before checkout.
  • Do not use fine print or asterisks whose meaning is hidden. Do not leave any charge undisclosed, because hidden costs create doubt.
  • Before sending paid traffic (visitors from paid ads), check at checkout that the shipping cost matches what the store advertises.
  • Also check that any "free shipping" claim holds for the customer's cart. A mismatch at checkout reduces trust and conversion.

Free shipping and offer elements

1 min
  • Show free shipping together with an estimated delivery date on the product page.
  • A competitor that displays free shipping without a delivery estimate is at a disadvantage.
  • Free shipping, including free worldwide shipping, is a common offer element (a part of the deal) that removes a purchase barrier.
  • A free-shipping message placed near the price lowers hesitation.
  • Shipping language matters. Compare "free tracked shipping" with "standard shipping" when you write the offer.

Shipping protection

1 min
  • Offer shipping protection at checkout only if it is real insurance (paid cover for lost or damaged parcels). It must not be an upsell (an extra paid add-on pushed on buyers).
  • Check the wording before you enable it.

Answering shipping cost questions directly

1 min
  • Formula (a fixed rule to follow): answer the buyer's direct question right away, for example about the shipping cost.
  • Then add a true scarcity note (a true note that stock is limited), such as low remaining stock.
  • The direct answer removes friction. The honest scarcity note pushes the buyer to act.
  • Reported results: around 20 to 30 percent of such conversations ended in a sale.
    • Example 1: "Standard delivery is $7.95 for this item, and there are only four left at this price, so order today."
    • Example 2: "Shipping to your address is $5 with 5-7 business days, and just two units remain in stock."

Delivery estimates and backorders

3 min

Estimated delivery dates

  • Estimated delivery dates are the expected arrival days you show buyers.
  • Clear fulfillment expectations (how and when you ship orders) reduce purchase hesitation (buyers stopping because they feel unsure) on the product page (the page for one item).
  • They also reduce inbound support requests (messages customers send you) about shipping time.
  • Show a delivery date range (a first day and a last day) on the product page.
  • This removes doubt about when the product will arrive.
  • It raises conversion (the share of visitors who buy).
  • Fewer customers message you to ask when it arrives.
  • The arrival range is counted from the day the visitor views the page.
  • A shopper who orders today sees the earliest and latest delivery days counted from today.
  • A shopper who visits on a later day sees the same day counts moved forward.
  • Show the delivery estimate on the product page and in the cart (the shopping basket).
  • Use a date window, such as X to Y.
  • Buyers who need an item by a fixed date, such as a gift or an event, feel more sure when the window fits their deadline.
  • Show estimated arrival dates on the product page, so the buyer knows when the product will arrive.
  • Unknown delivery times add doubt, especially for visitors who do not yet trust the store.
  • Show the ETA (estimated time of arrival, the expected arrival day) for each product on the product page, before checkout (the payment step).
  • Shoppers want to know this before they buy.
  • Few stores show it.
  • Estimated delivery answers "when will I receive it?" before the visitor asks.
  • This removes an objection (a reason not to buy).
  • Show it on a live store (a store that takes real orders).
  • It can be hidden on a demo store (a practice store where nothing is really shipped).
  • A long shipping time can scare buyers away.
  • Show a long time, for example two to three weeks, on the product page and in the email. Do not hide it.
  • This sets the expectation before purchase, so customers are not surprised later.
  • Being open about shipping time and where the product comes from helped close a sale.
  • In one case, the store had not stated it clearly.
  • When the buyer asked, they were told 10 to 20 days from China.

Lead-time confirmation

  • Set correct expectations before purchase. This reduces refunds (money given back), complaints and angry emails after the order.
  • A lead time is how long the item takes to reach the buyer.
  • A clear lead-time message on the product page is a cheap way to prevent many service problems.
  • Template for a lead-time confirmation before checkout:
We are working hard to get your {product_name} to you, but this item currently takes about {lead_time} to arrive. Please confirm you are happy with this timeline before you continue to checkout.

[Yes, continue to checkout]   [No, keep browsing]

Backorders

  • A backorder is an item you sell before it is in stock.
  • ** Do not close your store when your supplier (the company that sends you products) has uncertain inventory (stock on hand).
  • Tell customers about delays clearly before they buy.
  • This helps them decide well and avoids surprises after payment.
  • Show a backorder notice in a highlighted block (a box that stands out) above the Add to Cart button (the button that puts an item in the cart) on every backordered product page.
  • Include the expected ship date, which is the day the order leaves your side.
  • Use this exact wording style, so customers see the date before they buy.
  • Template for the highlighted block above the Add to Cart button on backordered product pages:
This product is currently backordered and is on its way to our warehouse. Expected shipping date: [EXACT SHIP DATE]. [OPTIONAL: This delay is due to REASON.] All orders will ship first come, first served, starting [SHIP START DATE].
  • Replace the placeholders in square brackets (the marked blanks) before use.
  • [EXACT SHIP DATE] is the exact date from the supplier, not a vague month.
  • [SHIP START DATE] is the first date shipments begin.
  • The reason line is optional.

Payment and inventory check

  1. Authorize the payment at checkout without capturing funds. (Authorize means reserving the money on the card. Capture means taking it.)
  2. Check that the product is in stock.
  3. If it is in stock, capture the payment (take the money) and proceed with fulfillment.

Checkout trust and branding

2 min

Branding the checkout

  • An unbranded checkout is one that does not look like your store.
  • It can make customers abandon (leave without buying) at the last step.
  • This can happen even after your ads and content have built trust.
  • Apply your branding (logo, colors and look) to every step of the purchase process, including checkout.
  • The checkout can look like a different site, so it may feel untrusted.
  • A branded checkout banner (a top strip with your look) with badges (small marks) for secure checkout, privacy protection and satisfaction guarantee can raise conversion.
  • In one case, conversion rose from 0.29 percent to 2.15 percent after these changes.
  • The checkout page should look trustworthy.
  • Add a checkout banner with secure checkout and satisfaction guarantee messages.
  • Add payment processor logos (the logos of companies that handle payments), such as PayPal, Visa and Mastercard.
  • Optionally, add a 30-day money-back guarantee (a promise to return the money within 30 days).
  • Add these because the checkout is otherwise unbranded.
  • Add the store logo and store information to the checkout.
  • Optionally, add a background image in the theme settings (the design controls) under Checkout.
  • This keeps the checkout looking like part of the store.
  • Customers then do not feel they moved to a different site after adding items to the cart.
  • Showing your logo on the checkout reassures customers that they are still on your store.
  • Shopify (the store platform you use) controls the checkout for security (safety).
  • So you can add a logo and colors, but you cannot fully redesign it.

Trust signals

  • Shoppers feel more unsure at checkout because they must enter their email, address and payment details.
  • Add trust icons (small symbols that signal safety), such as privacy protected, secure checkout and satisfaction guaranteed.
  • Also show a contact number, so customers feel supported.
  • Add a trust badge (a small mark that shows the store can be trusted) or logo at checkout.
  • This raises credibility (how believable the store seems).
  • Show customer review counts or ratings (star scores) on the checkout page as well as on product pages.
  • Buyers close to paying benefit from seeing that other customers completed their purchase successfully.
  • Payment technical problems (errors in the payment system) and worries about payment security can make buyers leave checkout quickly.
  • Make security signals and the accepted payment methods clearly visible during checkout.
  • Add payment plan options (paying in parts over time) for buyers who hesitate because of cost.
  • A PayPal study found that adding a padlock icon (a small lock symbol) to the checkout button raised mobile conversion (conversion on phones) by 17 percent.
  • Small, thoughtful design details at checkout can have a measurable effect.

Case

  • A store began with a 0.9 percent checkout conversion rate (the share of checkout visitors who buy).
  • Its TikTok visitors (people coming from a video app) often added items to the cart, which was a high add-to-cart rate (the share of visitors who add an item).
  • The owner added trust badges and icons to the checkout page.
  • Conversion rose to 2.05 percent within one day.
  • Only two or three changes were made, all on that page.

Checkout completion checklist

1 min

Steps to get more buyers to finish checkout (complete the purchase):

  1. Make sure the shipping language is attractive, for example "free tracked shipping" (free shipping with a tracking number) rather than "standard shipping".
  2. Add a trust badge or logo at checkout to increase credibility.
  3. Consider a limited-time free gift offer or an extra product at checkout to give buyers a reason to buy now.
  4. Make sure the cart display is not minimized (made small or hidden) on mobile. Shopify now minimizes cart contents on mobile checkout.

Testing checkout

1 min

Desktop and mobile

  • Test the add-to-cart and checkout flow (the steps from adding an item to paying) on both desktop (computer) and mobile (phone).
  • Look for problems such as an unexpected extra fee, a cut-off logo, or a button that does not work.
  • Test the complete purchase path after every change, including add-to-cart, subscription selection (choosing a repeat delivery plan), upsells (extra offers at checkout) and the cart.
  • A cart that stays empty after items were added will stop almost all sales.
  • Verify the checkout on the live store.

Test purchase with no conversions

A test buy to check that the checkout works.

  1. Add a cheap product priced around 1.50 USD to the cart.
  2. Buy it on a desktop device (computer).
  3. Buy it on a mobile phone.
  4. If either purchase fails, fix the checkout before sending more traffic (visitors) to the store.

Usability test with a tester

A usability test is a test where a person (the tester) tries your store while you watch.

  1. Set up the store and add a specific product to the cart.
  2. Find the add-to-cart button and confirm it is easy to locate.
  3. Start and complete the checkout process.
  4. Watch the tester without giving any guidance (no hints).
  5. Note every place they hesitate or get stuck.
  6. Ask them why they got stuck.
  7. Improve each problem point and repeat the test.

More rules worth knowing

1 min
  • A fast-delivery promise reassures the buyer.
  • The order will arrive quickly, so the purchase feels safer.

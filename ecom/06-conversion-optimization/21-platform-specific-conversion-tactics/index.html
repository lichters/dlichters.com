Playbook › 6. Conversion optimization

Platform-specific conversion tactics

Section 6.21 · 21 of 22 in this chapter · 22 min read

In short

  • Check your conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy) every week, not just once.
  • On Amazon, stop changing a listing once it reaches 18% conversion.
  • Run the automatic Amazon ad campaign for one week first. Never run automatic and manual campaigns at the same time.
  • Keep website text short. Visitors rarely read long blocks.
  • Never copy a competitor's page, video or ad exactly.
More key points
  • Test two landing page designs (themes): PageFly and Source F. Run each for two days, then compare results.
  • Do not raise ad spend on TikTok until your conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy) is above 1.5% to 2%.
  • Pick the right goal for each TikTok ad. Viral views alone do not mean sales.
  • Never copy a competitor's page, video or ad. Change the images, text and layout.
  • Keep your test store simple. Remove features you do not need yet.

Amazon conversion benchmarks

2 min
  • Check your conversion rate every week, not just once. Conversion rate means the share of visitors who buy.
  • Even small changes to images, text or product features can move it.
  • Once conversion is well optimized, stop changing things. This avoids regression (getting worse).
  • The sources set the "exceptional" line differently.
  • puts exceptional at 18% and above. It says to stop further changes once a listing reaches it.
  • uses a fuller scale: 5% is poor, 10-18% is good, 20% and above is excellent, and 30% and above is exceptional.
  • Use the scale for grading. Use the 18% point from as the freeze point.
  • A supplement product (a health or nutrition product) averaged 38% conversion on Amazon, with some days above 50%. Other platforms typically reach 2-3%.
  • Amazon shoppers are in a buying mindset. Baseline conversion is 15-20%. It rises to 33% on some days, and above 50% in supplement categories. Dropshipping stores (shops that sell products bought from a supplier, who ships them) typically convert around 2%.
  • So Amazon ads are the best traffic source for FBA products (Fulfilled by Amazon, where Amazon stores and ships your product). This holds despite a high cost-per-click (CPC: what you pay each time someone clicks your ad).
  • Amazon conversion is much higher than on a website. But the sources set different working bars.
    • treats 3% as good on a store. On Amazon, it treats a rate under 15% as a bad sign. It cites an 8% rate as a poor week.
    • counts 10-18% as good on Amazon.
    • Use as the general benchmark. Use as the stricter alarm level.
  • Amazon shoppers already want to buy. That is why their conversion is high, compared with traffic from Google, Bing or Facebook.
  • Items under $50 need little thought from the buyer. This supports fast purchases.

Amazon listing optimization

1 min
  • The parts of the product page that affect Amazon conversion are the title, images, bullet points (short feature lines) and description (the long text). Optimize each one. Match it to the problems and benefits buyers mention in competitor reviews.
  • Research competitor reviews in this order:
    1. Open the product pages of competitors. Read the positive and negative reviews.
    2. Note the recurring problems buyers mention.
    3. Use these problems in your title, bullet points and description. Show how your product solves them.
    4. Make small product improvements from these insights. Do not build a new product.

Amazon advertising sequence

1 min

Run these steps in order. A campaign is a group of ads with its own budget and settings.

  1. Set up an automatic Amazon ad campaign for one week. Amazon picks the keywords (search words that trigger your ad) and shows the product.
  2. Review which keywords brought sales (conversions) and which wasted money (spend).
  3. Build a manual campaign that targets only the keywords that convert. This gives you control over cost-per-click and bid amounts (the price you offer per click).
  4. Do not run automatic and manual campaigns at the same time. You will outbid yourself and waste budget competing against your own ads.
  • In the short term, losing money or breaking even on Amazon ads is acceptable. You do this to collect sales and conversions. These improve your organic ranking (where your product shows in search results without paying).
  • In the long term, you need profit. Your margin (the money left after costs) must be high enough that each sale can pay for more ads and still make a profit. Higher price points make this strategy possible.
  • If Amazon ads are already profitable (for example, $10 spent makes $20), increase the budget. Keep scaling until conversion rates plateau (stop rising). More sales improve organic ranking, which brings extra unpaid traffic.

Dynamic product ads

1 min
  • Dynamic product ads (ads that show products automatically from your catalogue), optimized for CTR (click-through rate: the share of people who click the ad), are the target for a 26% lift in conversion rate.

Service-business websites

1 min
  • Use shorter text on a service website. Visitors rarely read long blocks. Services must be explained quickly and clearly.
  • On mobile layouts, page builders (apps that let you design pages without coding), such as Gem Pages or PageFly, can create side-scrolling carousels (slides you swipe sideways) and drop-down menus (accordion menus that open and close). Use them to compress feature lists and benefit copy while keeping detail accessible.

High-ticket and bulky items

1 min
  • For high-ticket products (expensive items), a phone call to action (a line that asks the visitor to act) can convert better than a buy button. End the copy with an instruction to call today on a visible phone number. Offer to discuss purchase options.
  • White glove delivery (professional delivery and setup) can raise conversion rate for large or bulky items.
  • Mobile hesitance (visitors hesitate to buy on their phones) is common for high-ticket products. Many mobile visitors will convert later on desktop. Measure performance with that in mind.
  • There is no fixed rule that conversion falls as price rises. Traffic quality (how well targeted visitors are) matters more than the price point.
  • Conversion rates for high-priced products can be higher, lower or similar to standard products. Measure each high-priced product after launch. Do not assume a figure.
  • The social pages support high-ticket orders. Buyers place orders of roughly $500, $1,000, $2,000 or $5,000. Credibility matters more than viral reach for purchases at this level.
  • A search bar in the header is important for high-ticket dropshipping stores. The catalogue grows over time, and visitors need a fast way to find the product they want. Present it as a conversion measure that helps more people find the right product.

Products that require assembly

  • For products that require assembly, request photos from the supplier. The photos should show the product as delivered, in boxes or on pallets (wooden platforms that hold stacked boxes). Show them on the product page with assembly time and instruction videos. Customers then understand the unboxing and assembly reality.
  • Example. Outdoor swing sets for backyards are sold for $5,000, with photos of children playing and climbing. Delivery arrives as three pallets holding 1,000 pounds of lumber (cut wood). Customers expect an assembled product. The fix: show assembly time in videos, include delivery photos, and list assembly requirements prominently.

Dropshipping-specific rules

1 min
  • Do not copy a competitor's landing page (the page a visitor arrives on), video or ad creative (the ad's images and text) exactly. If the original advertiser is banned, your account and store can be banned too. Change images, text, layout and creatives.
  • Hide stock levels (the number of items left) on a high-ticket dropshipping store. Showing stock gives no advantage in that model.
  • Where stock is genuinely low on a standard store, a low-stock message can help conversion. See the inventory settings below.
  • Hide the expiring coupon block (a box showing a discount that ends soon) until the store receives real traffic. Demo products do not need urgency messages. You can re-enable it at any time with the same settings.
  • Enable the out-of-stock alert for items that are on backorder (sold out, restocked later) for a long time. This lets you build a waiting list instead of taking orders.
  • Test landing page themes before anything else. The procedure is in the page builder section below.

Shopify setup order

1 min

Shopify is the online store software. Work through these conversion tasks (steps that raise sales) in this order:

  1. Install an urgency app (an app that shows time pressure) and a review app on Shopify.
  2. Modify the content page templates (the page layout files).
  3. Create Facebook and Instagram business pages.
  4. Install Klaviyo (an email app) on Shopify.
  5. Set up automated actions and emails for bounced visits (visitors who leave right away) and abandoned carts (items left in the cart without buying).
  6. Apply for a financing app (lets customers pay in parts).
  7. Complete all action tasks of the conversion step.

Theme-native conversion features

5 min

A theme is the design template of the store. "Theme-native" means built into the theme, with no extra app.

  • Theme-native features aim to raise conversion rate and average order value (the average money spent per order). For most of these needs, the theme covers the function without a paid third-party app (an app from another company that costs extra).
  • Exit pop-up (a box that appears as a visitor leaves): enable it in Theme settings. Optionally turn on an image, and offer a discount code. It appears to visitors who are leaving the site. It displays text only and captures no email.
  • Social proof pop-up (a small box showing other people's purchases): enable it in Theme settings. It shows recent store orders in the bottom corner of the website. The format is "[Customer name] recently purchased [product name]". A frequency setting controls how often it appears.
  • Social proof pop-ups are optional. The built-in version avoids a monthly fee for a separate app.
  • Sticky content on desktop (the image stays in place while you scroll): turn it on in Theme settings and save. The image stays in place while the text column scrolls. This helps on monitors where the page does not fill the screen. The option is recommended on.
  • Price colors: under Theme settings, you can change the color of the list-price savings text (red by default). You can also change the color scheme of the sales badge (the sale label). Both can be set to any colors.
  • Sticky add to cart (the buy button stays at the bottom while the visitor scrolls): it is optional and can be tested. Icon, colors and font size are set under customize add to cart button.
  • Share button: keep it on. Visitors can copy the product link or share it on social media or by email. The link updates to the product page URL automatically. You can change the button text.
  • Image block: add it at any position on the product page, and add several if needed. It suits a promotion banner, such as a sale graphic showing the discount and the checkout code. Prepare the image in advance in a design tool such as Canva (an easy online design app).
  • Countdown timer (a clock counting down to the end of an offer): it needs a tag (a label) on the individual product in the Shopify admin, not in the theme settings. The tag begins with the word sale and carries the offer's end date. The date must lie in the future, because the timer counts down to that date.

Inventory display

  • The low inventory threshold (the stock number that triggers a warning) defaults to 10 units. When stock falls below that number, a low stock message appears. It can be dragged to sit directly below the buy button.
  • With show inventory count turned on, the page displays the exact number left next to the low stock message. For example: "low stock with 8 left". This can help conversion when real stock is actually low.

Trust badges

  • The trust badges block (small logos that build confidence) sits across the bottom of the page by default. This is unlike the shipping text block, which sits on the side. Its title can be changed and aligned center, left or right. Each badge can have an image, a link and optional text under the icon.
  • The block allows a maximum of seven badges. Turn on display trust badge text to add a caption under each icon. The caption text comes from the badge boxes.
  • The trust badges block shows reasons to buy below or beside the product. It is an advanced option. You can turn it off for a basic demo store.
  • Switch a feature such as trust badges on temporarily in the theme editor (the screen where you change the look). See how it looks on the product page before deciding to keep it. If it is not needed yet, switch it off again. Do not build it out at the demo stage.

Estimated delivery block

  • The block shows text such as "estimated arrival between", followed by the earliest and latest delivery days. Days are counted from the day the order is placed. It also shows the shipping country. That should be the country you ship to. Its text and color can be changed. The block is recommended once the store goes live.
  • In the theme used in this example, the block sits in the Product information section of the theme editor. Click its eye icon to make it visible. It then appears under the discount code, with the label "estimated arrival" and a date range.
  • The date range is built from the earliest and longest delivery days entered in the estimated delivery settings. Example: 4 days as the earliest and 7 days as the longest, shown as two calendar dates.
  • The dates update automatically every night at midnight based on the day count. The range stays current without manual edits.

Setup procedure for coupon and delivery on the product template

The product template is the page layout file for product pages.

  1. Create the discount code in the Shopify Discounts section first.
  2. Turn on and configure the expiring coupon on the product template. Match the code name exactly.
  3. Turn on and configure the estimated delivery section. Set the delivery day range.
  4. Save the template and check the product page in preview.

Review widget and hero slide

  • The hero slide is the large banner image at the top of the homepage. To edit its text in the Shopify theme editor:
    1. Open the slideshow section and click the first slide.
    2. Select the image, upload it, and check how it looks.
    3. If the image is too dark, remove the overlay (the dark layer on top of the image) so it looks brighter.
    4. Enter the Heading and Subheading. Set Text alignment to Left.
    5. Remove the button by clearing its Label field, if no button is needed.
    6. Click Save.
  • To place the review widget (the box showing customer star ratings), go to Online store > Themes in Shopify. Click Edit theme. Switch from the homepage to a product page template. The review widget appears in the apps section of the sidebar. You can drag it to a different position. By default it sits directly under the product description. Save with the theme save button.

Visually A/B tests (Shopify)

3 min

An A/B test shows two versions of a page to split visitors, so you can see which one sells more.

Running and reviewing experiments

  • The Pages setting "All Pages" covers the homepage, product pages, collection pages (category pages) and the rest of the store. To test only one template, choose a specific page type, such as Product Pages or Cart page. Then the change does not affect unrelated pages.
  • A running experience (a live test) appears in a live list. The list shows its start date, last edit date, pages, audience, device and allocation (the share of visitors who see each version, for example 50%). From there you can Edit, Pause or Preview it, without creating a new one.

No-code A/B test setup

  1. Hover over the element on the live page and click Edit Visual. Change the text, font, size, spacing or color. Then click Save.
  2. Enter an Experience Name (the name of the test).
  3. Set the Audience (for example All Visitors), Pages (for example All Pages or Product Pages), Devices (for example Mobile) and Trigger (what starts the test) (PAGE LOAD).
  4. Click Next. Set the experience as an A/B test with a 50-50 allocation (half the visitors see each version).
  5. Select the goals: Conversion Rate as the primary goal. Per Session Value and Profit as secondary goals. A session is one visit.
  6. Tick the box that sends the events to Google Analytics (a free tool that tracks visits).
  7. Publish.

Social proof widget below Add to Cart

  1. Add the Social Proof element through Content Blocks. Place it directly under Add to Cart.
  2. Choose Bought instead of Viewed.
  3. Set the General Customization to BOUGHT IN THE PAST X TIME, with Timeframe WEEK and Rounding (50's) for rounded numbers.
  4. Set the threshold to a minimum of 150 purchases.
  5. Style the text block: font Commons Sans-serif, 16px, bold, black to match the brand, with reduced padding (the space around the text).
  6. Save. Then set Audience All Visitors, Pages Product Pages, Device Mobile. Name the experience, for example "mobile BDP social proof".
  7. Set the A/B test to 50-50 with the primary goal. Send the events to Google Analytics. Publish.
  • Choose Bought over Viewed, because bought counts show stronger proof. Timeframe options are day, week, month and year. Rounding (50's) shows a rounded count instead of the exact number. The Set threshold field hides the widget until the purchase count exceeds the chosen number.

Trust elements (USPs) in the cart

A USP is a unique selling point, a reason to buy.

  1. Copy the USP block from the product page. USPs include shipping, returns policy, lifetime warranty and active promotion.
  2. Paste it into the cart page directly below the upsells (extra product offers) and above the shipping line.
  3. Set Audience All Visitors, Pages Cart page only, Device Mobile. Name the test, for example "mobile cart USPs".
  4. Set the A/B test to 50-50.
  5. Add returns rate and profit as goals. A visible return policy can raise the return rate.
  6. Tick the Google Analytics box and publish.

Opening a feature dropdown on page load (Fake Click)

A Fake Click is an element that clicks for the visitor when the page loads.

  1. Add a Fake Click element from the element list.
  2. Hover over the dropdown (for example Features) and click it, so the interaction is recorded.
  3. Preview the page to confirm the dropdown opens on load.
  4. Set Audience All Visitors, Pages Product Pages, Device Mobile.
  5. Name the test, set it as an A/B test, set the goals, send the events to Google Analytics and publish.

Cart, announcement and progress elements (Shopify)

2 min

Announcement bar

  • Header > Add > Announcement Bar: use it to promote sitewide sales or discount codes inside the cart. The header is the top of the page. With several messages, they slide across the top of the bar. A single message stays static, without the sliding animation.
  • An announcement bar can hold several announcements that scroll as a marquee (text sliding across like a ticker). The default display time is roughly five seconds per message. The character limit (the maximum text length) was not stated. So keep messages short and watch for unwanted line breaks.

Tiered progress bar (free shipping goal)

  • Goal Box > Add > Tiered Progress Bar shows customers their progress toward offers. Examples: free shipping, free products, an order-wide discount, or a decorative tier with no reward. Up to four tiers can be configured.
  • Tiered Progress Bar > Configure Tiers > Free Shipping: enter the unlock price as a dollar amount. The example uses $50. The free shipping options are: Free Shipping on All Products with no minimum, Free Shipping on Subscription Product, and Enable Presentment Currencies (showing prices in other currencies).
  • Example: with a $50 unlock price, a cart holding $29 in products shows the customer is $21 away from free shipping. The bar updates as items are added or removed.

Cart footer components

  • Footer > Cart Subtotal controls how the subtotal (the sum of the items, before shipping) appears in the cart.
  • Footer > Checkout Area > Express Checkout Buttons: Shop Pay, Apple Pay and Google Pay (one-tap payment buttons) can be shown in the cart through Accelerated Checkout (a faster checkout).
  • Pre-checkout options (Terms and Conditions, Pre-Purchase Pop-Up), standard buttons (Checkout Button, View Cart Button, Continue Shopping Button) and Installments (paying in parts) are configured in the same area.
  • Footer > Add > Discount Code Input lets customers enter a discount code inside the cart. The saving is visible before the checkout page. The field can be dragged to any position in the footer.

Section settings (Shopify themes)

1 min
  • The Instagram stories section shows images and testimonials (customer quotes) as a stories-style feed. Add user post blocks inside the section. The section fills with stories automatically once content is added.
  • Instagram stories section settings in the theme editor:
    • Display section ID: the ID (a label that names the section) can merge two sections in the Sections group. It can be placed in any button link, so the button scrolls to this section.
    • Display on: All devices.
    • Heading.
    • Heading words highlight 1: words in bold get the highlight style (for example with Text color #6D388B).
    • Heading words highlight 2: words in italic get the highlight style. Choose None to keep the default italic style.
    • Heading size, Margin and Remove section.
  • Highlights banner settings: Section background color, Desktop section spacing (the empty space around the section; set to 60px, pixels, in the tested version), Mobile section spacing, and Banner text alignment set to Center. Colors: Banner headings, Banner text, Icon, Dividers and Background.
  • Product information section in the Shrine theme: Display section ID, Enable sticky content on desktop, Color scheme (Background 1), Media settings, Display first variant image as featured image, Disable prepend of the new active media (stop adding new images to the front), Hide other variants' media after selecting a variant, and Active variant images filtering. A variant is one version of a product, such as a size or color. Use the sticky option to keep the purchase block visible while the visitor scrolls.
  • Product template block order in the Shrine theme: Title, Rating stars, Price, Variant picker, Quantity selector, Buy buttons (with Add to cart), Shipping checkpoints, Sticky Add To Cart, Description, Reviews, then Image with text sections and an Icon Bar. Remove blocks you do not need so the page does not get busy.

Demo-store configuration

1 min
  • Hide the expiring coupon block (a discount box with a countdown timer) until the store has real traffic (visitors).
  • Turn off the trust badges block (small logos that show security or guarantees) for a basic demo store. A demo store is a test version of your shop.
  • Keep the demo store simple: test features temporarily and remove them if they are not needed yet.

Landing page builders and theme testing

2 min

A landing page is a single sales page that an ad sends people to. A theme is the design template a store uses.

Theme split test

Split-test (A/B test: two versions compared side by side) landing page themes before anything else:

  1. If you do not use PageFly yet, build the landing page with PageFly (a tool that builds pages by dragging blocks) and test it.
  2. If you already use PageFly, switch to the Source F theme for the test.
  3. Run ads for two days on one theme, then two days on the other, and compare results.
  • Some products convert (turn visitors into buyers) better on PageFly and others on Source F, so do not commit to one design.
  • Both PageFly and Source F are free to use.
  • Older source (from 2024, no date mark needed): a store with a low conversion rate (for example 0.03%) on an older theme should be rebuilt on the current theme, Manhattan.
  • A fresh build on a newer theme should show a much higher conversion rate.

Page builders

  • Gem Pages is a page builder, and it has carousel (a slider of images) and drop-down features.
  • PageFly is a similar builder with duplicate-and-edit functionality (copy a page, then change the copy) for creating variant landing pages per audience.
  • Instant is a page builder used for pre-landers (short pages before the main sales page), listicles (numbered list articles) and advertorials (ad-style articles).
  • GemPages has better templates, but Instant is easier to work with.
  • PageDeck is another builder option.
  • The choice of builder matters less than the flow: clean copy (clear, simple sales text), then pre-lander, then funnel (the path a visitor follows from ad to purchase).

Button styling in PageFly

  • To build a button, add a button element to the page.
  • Enter the button text.
  • Set the background color and text color, for example white text on a dark background.
  • Set the text to bold.
  • Add inside padding (empty space between the edge and the label) so the button has room around its label.
  • Set a button's border radius (how round its corners are) to 50 px to round its corners into a pill shape.
  • Increase the value until the corners look fully round for the size of the button.
  • A fixed-position button (it stays in one place on screen while the page scrolls) floats over the page.
  • Set its position to bottom 30 px and left 30 px.
  • This works as a sticky add-to-cart button (one that stays visible and adds the item to the basket) on mobile product pages.

Pinterest conversion setup

1 min
  • Pinterest conversion campaigns (ads aimed at sales) need a checkout event (a signal sent to Pinterest when a purchase-style trigger happens).
  • Use this snippet (a short piece of code). It has a sample value of 80 and a placeholder product (a stand-in product).
  • Replace the product name, ID (product number), price and value with the real trigger product's details before use.
pintrk('track', 'checkout', {
  value: 80,
  order_quantity: 1,
  currency: 'USD',
  line_items: [
    {
      product_name: 'Trigger Product',
      product_id: '6789',
      product_price: 80,
      product_quantity: 1
    }
  ]
});

Activating conversion events

  1. Open a product page on your store.
  2. Open the browser developer tools (a built-in panel for inspecting web pages): right-click the page and choose Inspect, or go to More tools > Developer tools if inspect is blocked.
  3. Open the Console tab (the panel where you can run code).
  4. Paste the Pinterest conversion event code into the console and run it 10 to 20 times.
  5. Wait five to 10 minutes.
  6. Return to Pinterest. The conversion campaign option should now be active.

Pinterest audience growth

  • Add a pop-up (a box that appears over the page) on the store. It invites visitors to follow the store on Pinterest or check out new boards.
  • This increases the number of people who follow the store and share its content automatically.
  • Place a Pinterest follow button in the website header (the top part of every page).
  • Add links to your boards (collections of pins) and your profile in the sidebar or footer (the side or bottom strip of a page).
  • This helps visitors who want to interact on Pinterest find the profile easily.

TikTok conversion setup

1 min
  • Set the TikTok pixel's (tracking code) Data Sharing to Maximum during the Shopify connection (Shopify is the online store platform). The pixel then receives the most event data for optimization (TikTok's way of improving ads).
  • Do not scale (raise the budget a lot) a TikTok campaign until the store conversion rate is above 1.5% to 2%.
  • Set the call to action (the button text on the ad) to standard Shop now, or to Dynamic, which lets TikTok choose the most suitable button.
  • Both options are valid. Test which converts better.
  • If a video goes viral (many views very fast) but produces no sales, the audience is probably wrong, even if the product is good.
  • Switch the coins (TikTok's paid boost for a video) or Spark Ads (an ad that boosts a real post) objective (the goal you pick for the ad) to conversions to reach buyers.
  • Views versus conversions, in one example: $10 with a conversion objective produced 1 to 2 conversions (the goal you set, such as a purchase) and about 500 views.
  • The same $10 on a views objective could double the views but produce no conversions.
  • Conversion objectives cost more per result.
  • Conversion objectives also give fewer total views.
  • Choose between sales and reach (how many people see the ad) based on your goal.
  • If conversion targeting produces no sales, go back to views, because TikTok may still find the audience either way.

Mixed coins strategy by stage

  1. Spend about $10 on coins for views in the first stage.
  2. Once the account reaches about 1,000 followers, switch the remaining about $20 to a conversion objective.
  3. If growth slows, use views and conversions again to re-balance.

Common mistakes

1 min
  • Do not copy a competitor's (rival shop's) landing page, video or ad creative (the images and videos of an ad) exactly.
  • Change images, text, layout and creatives to protect the account and store.

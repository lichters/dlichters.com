Playbook › 6. Conversion optimization

When to start: readiness and the pre-traffic launch checklist

Section 6.2 · 2 of 22 in this chapter · 9 min read

In short

  • Do not spend money on ads until the store is ready: suppliers approved, real products live, and checkout working.
  • You need at least five supplier approvals and live products before you start conversion work.
  • Before ads, buy your own $1 test product on desktop and mobile to make sure checkout completes.
  • Check profit first: supplier cost plus shipping plus ad spend must leave real margin.
  • Finish the 11 setup tasks before traffic. Design polish and announcement bars can wait until real visitors arrive.
This section has 13 parts. Tap one to open it.

Readiness gate: what must exist before paid traffic

1 min
  • Start conversion optimization (changing the store so more visitors buy) only after at least five supplier approvals exist (suppliers have accepted your store), with products from those suppliers live on the store. Without live products, there is nothing to measure or improve.
  • Conversion optimization comes after suppliers approve and real products are on the store. Optimize the site before any money is spent on paid traffic (ads that send visitors to the store).
  • Complete the store's conversion setup before launching paid traffic campaigns, because starting early wastes ad spend on visitors you cannot convert.
  • Complete all store optimization before driving traffic to the store, so it converts from the first visitor onward.
  • Finish conversion setup before sending paid traffic, so every paid click lands on a store with urgency (pressure to buy now, such as a deadline), reviews, email capture (collecting visitors' email addresses) and recovery flows (automatic follow-up messages) already in place. Traffic setup comes after the store is ready for visitors.
  • Before paying for traffic, confirm the store already converts visitors into buyers, because ads and other traffic sources only pay off when the site can close the sale.
  • Conversion optimization should be done before paid or organic traffic (free visitors, for example from search or social media) is pushed to the store. A store that converts poorly wastes the visitors that traffic work brings in.
  • A store is never fully optimized, but the core conversion steps should be done before ads go live. Launching with the basics in place reduces the chance of wasted spend.
  • Before launch, the store should be optimized for conversions using a structured conversion-optimization process. This is the condition under which an early launch is recommended.
  • Improve product pages (the pages for single items) and checkout (the steps where the buyer pays) before increasing ad spend, because traffic is wasted if product pages do not convert.

Money check before ads

1 min
  • Calculate total profitability (whether you make money overall) before launching ads: supplier cost + shipping cost + allocated ad spend (the share of the ad budget assigned to each sale). This confirms margins (money left per sale after costs) can support scaling (spending more).
  • A supplier margin of about 15 percent is too thin to fund ads, order processing and a future virtual assistant (a helper you pay for tasks), so it does not justify paid traffic. Use organic traffic for low-margin products and spend paid traffic only on products with real margin.

The 11 action tasks before setting up traffic

1 min

Complete these 11 tasks before setting up traffic.

  1. Enable expiring coupon codes (discounts that stop working after a set time).
  2. Add and modify all provided content templates (ready-made page and text layouts).
  3. Install a review app (a tool that shows customer ratings on the store) and collect initial reviews from suppliers, family, or friends.
  4. Set up social media accounts.
  5. Install and configure Klaviyo (an email marketing tool) for email marketing.
  6. Set up an exit intent overlay (a pop-up that appears as a visitor is about to leave) to capture bouncing visitors (visitors who leave quickly).
  7. Create an abandoned cart flow (automatic messages to people who left items in their cart) for automatic follow-up with visitors who do not complete checkout.
  8. Brainstorm and plan the first bonus offer.
  9. Identify competitor weaknesses (where rival stores fall short) and areas for improvement.
  10. Apply for and set up a financing app (lets customers pay in installments, small parts over time).
  11. Install a live chat app (lets you talk with visitors in real time).
  • These 11 tasks are the same process used for personal store launches before paid traffic. This shows the full sequence is treated as a validated best practice (a method proven to work) for conversion optimization.

Reviews and trust: where the sources differ

1 min
  • The task list asks for initial reviews from suppliers, family, or friends.
  • The Google Merchant Center note (Google's tool for listing stores and products) says store reviews are not required before approval, and stores without any customer reviews have been approved (2023). Trust should come from a clean, complete store rather than fake or added reviews.
  • Apply both: treat the review app and initial reviews as part of the 11-task build, but do not treat reviews as an approval condition, and never substitute fake reviews for real trust-building.

Store build for supplier approval (demo store)

1 min

Setup for supplier approval on a demo store (a practice store used to build the page layout before going live):

  1. Keep breadcrumbs on (the path links, such as Home > Shirts, that show where a page sits).
  2. Turn on sticky content (content that stays on screen while you scroll) on desktop.
  3. Set desktop media width to medium (the size of product pictures) and keep constrain media to screen height (stops pictures from being taller than the screen) on.
  4. Set media fit (how a picture fills its frame) to original or fill, whichever looks better.
  5. Keep desktop layout set to thumbnail carousel (a row of small pictures you click through).
  6. Set image behavior (what happens when a picture is clicked) to none, or to zoom only for high-resolution images.
  7. Set mobile image behavior to none and mobile media to show thumbnails.
  8. Keep on the share button, SKU (the item's internal product code), vendor name, quantity selector, dynamic checkout buttons (fast-pay buttons that skip the usual form), and description.
  9. Hide the coupon block, estimated delivery, low stock message, inventory count (how many items are left), sticky add to cart (a buy button that follows the screen), and countdown timer (a clock counting down to a deadline).
  10. Save and reload the product page.
  • Many conversion elements, such as low stock messages, coupon blocks, estimated delivery, countdown timers and extra trust badges (small logos that signal safety), are not needed for supplier approval. Add them once the store has live products and traffic. Use the demo store to build the page structure first.

Test purchase and checkout check

1 min

Current procedure (2023):

  1. Put a $1 test product on the store.
  2. Attempt a purchase on desktop.
  3. Attempt a purchase on mobile.
  4. Check whether checkout completes both ways. If there are zero conversions (sales) after clicks, checkout failure is the likely cause.

Earlier procedure:

  1. Add a test product priced at $1 to the store.
  2. Go through checkout as a customer and pay with a real credit card.
  3. Confirm that every step works, including the order confirmation (the message shown after buying).
  4. Install a session recording tool such as Lucky Orange (software that records how real visitors move around the site) and watch real visitor sessions to find where they drop off (leave).
  • Both versions test the same thing, a real purchase through checkout. The 2023 version adds the desktop and mobile comparison, and the 2019 version adds session recordings. Use both.

Pre-traffic checklist in order

1 min

Before sending paid traffic to the store, check these items in order:

  1. Payment processors (the services that handle card payments)
  2. Markets served (the countries you sell to)
  3. Shipping prices
  4. Legal pages (terms, privacy and similar rules pages)
  5. Checkout page

Store pages to check before scaling:

  • Product pages should be fast on mobile.
  • The contact page should offer multiple ways to contact you.
  • The store should show a clear return and shipping policy.

Do not run ads to a page that still has missing photos, broken navigation links (menu links that lead nowhere) or unfinished sections, because visitors will leave before the offer is understood.

Store review gate with Elixir

1 min

Store review workflow before paid traffic:

  1. Build the store with the program's tool, Elixir.
  2. Do not go live directly. Submit the store for review.
  3. The reviewer checks the product page, copy (the sales text), offer, trust signals (proof that the store is safe, such as reviews or security badges) and checkout flow (the path from cart to payment).
  4. Fix the issues found.
  5. Only then spend money on ads sending traffic to the store.
  • A store that does not convert is a store problem, not a traffic problem, so an ad budget spent on an unreviewed store can be lost quickly. One example figure: $500 in ads sent to a store that never converted.

Product page and homepage: what to finish first

1 min
  • Make the product page presentable and easy to buy from before focusing on the rest of the store. The home page can stay simple, since many visitors go straight to the product page.
  • A product page in testing does not need to be perfect, but it needs the core sections. Get the structure right first, then decide how much more time to spend on it.
  • Beginners often spend months polishing design while the site still has problems. A beautiful site with a scattered layout, weak trust signals, or a banner that does not show the product will not convert paid traffic.

Design refinement: when to delay

1 min
  • Leave out web design refinement and conversion rate optimization (CRO, improving the share of visitors who buy) from the initial store build. Schedule them after the store is live and receiving real visitors, because their decisions depend on actual traffic and behavior data.
  • If the store looks fine to you, do not change the design before paid traffic arrives. Start paid ads, then compare bounce rate (the share of visitors who leave without doing anything else) by paid traffic source to judge the store.
  • Apply both: during the initial build, defer refinement until live traffic exists. Once the store is acceptable to you, let paid-traffic bounce rates decide what to change.

Pre-landers: when to build

1 min
  • Send paid traffic directly to the product page at the start. Add pre-landers (separate pages that warm up visitors before the product page) only when scaling (spending more to grow), and choose which pre-lander to use based on the market angle (the selling message that fits a group of buyers).
  • Building many pre-landers before knowing which marketing angles work wastes time. First identify the winning angles through direct product page tests, then build pre-landers.

Steps to build a new pre-lander test from an existing one:

  1. Copy a basic pre-lander that already exists.
  2. Swap in the new copy and new images.
  3. Launch it to test a new marketing angle.

Urgency elements: coupons, timers and announcement bars

1 min
  • An expiring coupon (a discount code with an end date) is a day-one requirement for all stores. It belongs to the 11 action tasks and is set up before traffic.
  • Announcement bars (thin banners at the top of the site) create urgency (pressure to buy now) and lift conversion rates (the share of visitors who buy), so they only make sense once real traffic and live products exist. Hide the bar until then.
  • While the store is still a demo, hide the announcement bar with the eye icon in the customizer (the store's design editor). Its text and colors can be set later.
  • Countdown timers are hidden on the demo store and added once the store has live products and traffic.

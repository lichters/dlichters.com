Recovering lost visitors and carts

Section 6.16 · 16 of 22 in this chapter · 10 min read

In short

  • A customer who started checkout and left is the most likely to buy later, so win them back first.
  • Send the first follow-up email about 4 hours after they leave, and the second about 24 hours after.
  • Reach out fast. The first hour gets the best results.
  • Turn the automation on and check that its button says Turn off workflow.
This section has 13 parts. Tap one to open it.

Abandoned checkout versus abandoned cart

1 min
  • An abandoned cart means items were added to the cart (the shopping basket), but checkout (the payment step) was never started.
  • An abandoned checkout means the customer started checkout and did not finish.
  • Reasons a checkout is abandoned: a declined payment, missing information, or an interruption.
  • Abandoned checkout recovery (winning back these sales) works better than abandoned cart recovery.
  • Why it works better: these customers went further in the buying process.
  • An automation (a set of steps the store runs by itself) follows up with customers who add products, start checkout, and leave without buying.
  • It recovers sales that would otherwise be lost while the customer still wants to buy (high purchase intent, meaning strong readiness to buy).
  • It runs without daily work from you.
  • A workflow (another word for an automation) starts, called a trigger, when checkout is abandoned with a total greater than $0.
  • Customers who complete their purchase leave the workflow.
  • An abandoned cart flow is an automatic email sequence (a series of emails sent in order).
  • It follows visitors who entered checkout but did not finish. These are people with high intent, meaning they are very ready to buy.
  • An exit-intent overlay is a pop-up shown when a visitor is about to leave the site.
  • Exit-intent overlays and abandoned cart flows fix two different leaks: visitors who leave without acting, and prospects who quit checkout.
  • Each leak needs a separate strategy.

Setting up the abandoned checkout automation

2 min
  1. Open Marketing > Automations in the Shopify admin (Shopify is the store software, and the admin is its back office).
  2. Automations are created in the Messaging app but edited in the Automations section.
  3. Build the workflow using the settings in the table below.
  4. Check that the workflow is on.
  5. When it is on, the button shows Turn off workflow.
  6. If it shows Turn on workflow, the automation is not yet active.
  7. Once set up, the automation keeps running with no end date. It fires each time a customer abandons a checkout.
  8. You can change or pause it at any time without setting it up again.

Speed of follow-up

1 min
  • Customers who open the abandoned cart email within the first hour are 400% more likely to buy.
  • Speed matters because customers stay focused on the purchase for only minutes.
  • After 30 minutes, distractions take over, memory of the purchase fades, and competitors can take the sale.
  • Where possible, call the customer as soon as possible after they abandon, because speed matters most.

Manual follow-up of abandoned checkouts

1 min

Procedure A (2024):

  1. Open the Shopify admin, go to Orders, then Abandoned checkouts.
  2. Count how many abandoned checkouts contain an email address or phone number.
  3. Send each of those customers a manual follow-up email (written and sent by hand, not automatically).
  4. Where possible, call them as soon as possible after they abandon, because speed matters most.

Procedure B (2023):

  1. Go to Shopify > Orders > Abandoned Carts.
  2. Follow up with all abandoned cart customers via email, phone, or SMS (text message).
  3. Message: Noticed you started checkout, happy to help if you have questions.
  4. This can recover 1-3 quick sales.
  • The Abandoned checkouts section lists customers who started checkout but did not finish the order.
  • Check this list often and follow up. This is a way to win back lost sales.

Phone and SMS follow-up

1 min
  • When calling an abandoned checkout customer, use this template.
  • The template treats the stop as a checkout problem, not a lack of interest.
  • This raises answer rates (how many people pick up or reply) and conversion (how many visitors buy).
Hi [customer name], I saw you were looking at a [product name] that would ship to [city/state]. You didn't complete your checkout-how can I help you finish the order?
  • Follow-up can run by email, phone, or SMS (text message).
  • Checkout contact setting: go to Settings > Checkout > Customer contact.
  • This field is where the shopper types their contact details.
  • Set the field to email address only if SMS retargeting (sending text reminders to people who left) is not working.
  • Then customers can only enter an email at checkout.
  • The store can then retarget them (reach them again) with Klaviyo, an email tool.
  • During a paid testing phase (when you spend money on ads to test), always keep SMS Bump, or an equivalent SMS flow, active.
  • SMS Bump is a text message app.
  • Checkout drop-offs with a phone number can be retargeted by SMS.
  • Without SMS Bump, collect email addresses at checkout so email retargeting can be used instead.

Real-time alerts and tracking

1 min

Procedure:

  1. Send automatic follow-up emails to customers with abandoned carts.
  2. Display targeted Facebook ads to the customer (ads shown to a chosen group).
  3. Send a real-time notification (an instant alert) including customer name, cart value, phone number, item details, and cart link.
  4. Log customer information to a Google Sheet (an online spreadsheet) for tracking.
  • Abandoned cart notification messages must include: customer name, total cart value, customer contact phone number, specific items in the cart, and a direct link to the abandoned cart.
  • Real-time Slack alerts (instant messages in Slack, a team chat app) add to email follow-ups and Facebook retargeting.
  • Slack alerts do not replace email or Facebook.
  • Using all three together maximizes recovery.
  • Recovery tracking: create a second Zapier automation (Zapier is a tool that links apps and moves data between them).
  • This Zapier automation checks incoming paid orders against the abandoned cart sheet in Google Sheets.
  • If the customer appears in both, update the sheet with the recovery date and method (phone call, email, etc.).

Diagnosing clicks without abandoned checkouts

1 min

Procedure (2025):

  1. In Shopify, open Orders and click Abandoned checkouts.
  2. If abandoned checkouts exist, email each customer personally from the owner's address, referencing the product and offering help.
  3. If there are none despite many clicks, check which keywords and search terms triggered the clicks in Google Ads (the ad tool where you pay per click).
  4. Keywords are the words your ads target. Search terms are the words people actually typed.
  5. Adjust ad spend based on what the search terms show.

Why visitors leave

1 min
  • Abandoned checkout visitors show high purchase intent but did not buy.
  • The usual reasons are the overall offer (not only price, but any discount, bonus item, or ebook).
  • Other usual reasons are the product price and trust.
  • First-time visitors may doubt the store or be unsure whether to buy now.
  • Common reasons for cart abandonment are high or unexpected shipping costs.
  • Other common reasons are complicated shipping rates or policies.
  • Distractions such as a doorbell, a phone call, or a notification also cause abandonment.
  • Distraction cases are solved mainly by reminders.
  • Visitors who do not buy right away are unlikely to come back on their own.
  • The speaker estimated that about 90% will not bookmark the store.
  • Instead, they search again and click a competitor or a paid ad, which costs more money.
  • Close the sale on the first visit.

Reminders, retargeting, and second chances

1 min
  • Visitors who leave without buying, and abandoned carts, should be followed up later with reminders.
  • Reminders let you reach them once they are ready to buy.
  • Pop-up offers, flow emails, and SMS reminders are each a second chance to convert.
  • Traffic (visitors) from paid ads or organic content (free posts) without retargeting messages is largely wasted when visitors leave without buying.
  • Conversion rates increase when customers interact with the website multiple times.
  • Retargeting (showing messages again to people who visited) and multi-touch strategies (several contacts, one after another) are therefore essential.
  • They move visitors closer to purchase.
  • Set up remarketing (the same as retargeting) from the start with a small budget, around a few dollars per day.
  • This lets you reach visitors who did not buy again.

Exit-intent visitors

1 min

Procedure:

  1. Check how many visitors entered their email on the exit-intent overlay (the pop-up shown as they leave).
  2. Email each person personally with an introduction as the owner.
  3. Invite a reply or call, and offer help choosing the right product.

Abandoned cart email content

2 min
  • Test changes to live flow emails through split tests (sending two versions to see which works better) whenever traffic (visitors) allows.
  • A low-traffic store cannot test reliably.
  • So keep changes small and treat results as weak evidence.
  • Subject line (the email's title line) effectiveness varies by audience and product type.
  • Test formulas across successive sends: question, how-to, scarcity, declaration, numbers, curiosity, personalized.
  • Compare open rates (the share of people who open the email).
  • Open the first abandoned cart email with the logo.
  • The message must build trust before it asks the shopper to return to checkout.
  • Place the company logo at the top of every email, in the same form it uses on the website.
  • This way readers know they are in the right place.
  • Generous spacing between blocks makes the email easier to scan.
  • Avoid crowding sections.
  • Changing the email width matters little compared with clean spacing.
  • Show third-party ratings (scores from outside sites) as trust icons (small badges).
  • For example, a 4.9-star rating on Google and Trustpilot, and a 5-star rating on a review site.
  • Shoppers check reviews before buying, so placing the proof in the email saves them the search.
  • Test one to three customer testimonials (written praise) as social proof (other people's approval).
  • Import reviews automatically with Klaviyo Reviews (Klaviyo's review feature) where the account is connected.
  • The testimonial test is planned as the next iteration (next version) of the email.
  • Answer the "why" with specific relevance to this customer, rather than generic benefits.
  • Specificity makes the email feel personally relevant rather than broadcast (sent to everyone).
  • Example: We understand keeping your gear comfortable during long rides is what matters to you. instead of This product is great.
  • A USP (unique selling point, the main reason to buy) pattern can also handle an objection (a doubt).
  • Choose the selling point that answers a common doubt, so one line does two jobs.
  • Shipping speed answers "when will it arrive".
  • Own example: Order before 2pm, shipped the same day answers the delivery-time doubt.
  • A risk-reduction email pattern makes buying feel safe.
  • Step 1: give a review count and star rating as proof.
  • Step 2: invite the shopper to buy with confidence.
  • Step 3: restate the return promise.
  • Own examples: Over 2,000 five-star reviews from riders across the country. Buy with confidence. and Rated 4.8 by 900 home cooks: try it risk-free.
  • The risk-reduction email can show secure payment options as payment badges (small logos of payment methods).
  • It can also point to social proof such as Instagram stories and customer reviews.
  • Together these reassure a shopper worried about scams before they return to checkout.

Welcome series and personal outreach

1 min
  • Manually email every new subscriber (someone who joined your email list) within one business day, as the store owner.
  • Personal outreach converts better than automation alone.
  • Structure each email in four parts:
    • (1) Acknowledge their signup.
    • (2) Introduce yourself as owner and briefly state your store's stage or focus.
    • (3) Ask what they need or whether they have questions.
    • (4) Invite them to reply directly.
  • Example 1: Hi, just saw your signup. I'm [name], founder here. We focus on [category]-what brought you in?
  • Example 2: I'm [name], owner of [store]. Noticed you joined our list. What got your attention-any specific products or questions?
  • Each email must feel personal and vary by subscriber.
  • Do not include standard unsubscribe links (the "stop emails" link) or brand signatures (the company's standard sign-off).
  • The goal is to start real conversations and learn what visitors want.
  • Add urgency and scarcity (a limited-time feeling) in the final emails of a welcome series.
  • For example, a countdown to offer expiry.
  • This pushes subscribers who have not bought yet to act before the deadline.
  • Abandoned cart emails should run as a sequence in Klaviyo to recover checkouts that were not finished.

Reorder pages

1 min
  • Reorder landing pages (a page made for buying again) make it easy for existing customers to buy the same products again.
  • The vendor presents them as a way to build repeat purchases and loyalty after the first order.

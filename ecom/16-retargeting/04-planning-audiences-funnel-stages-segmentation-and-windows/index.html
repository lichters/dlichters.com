Playbook › 16. Retargeting

Planning audiences: funnel stages, segmentation and windows

Section 16.4 · 4 of 24 in this chapter · 12 min read

In short

  • An audience is a group of people who did something on your store or social pages. Build audiences first, because every ad and email uses them.
  • Retarget only people who already showed interest, such as viewing products. Never retarget people who have not visited your store.
  • Set the lookback window (how many days back an audience looks) by product price. Cheap products: about 7 days. Products over $1,000: 60-90 days.
  • Exclude past buyers from retargeting, unless you promote another product to them. Also exclude recent customers from every campaign.
  • Remove visitors who stayed only about 10 seconds. They rarely buy.

Audience Foundations

2 min

  • An audience is a group of people who took a specific action on your website or social media accounts. Examples of actions are visiting a page or engaging with a post. Engaging means liking, commenting or watching.

  • Build audiences so you can promote current and future offers to them. Aim for the highest possible ROAS (return on ad spend: money earned for each dollar spent on ads). The same audiences are used for search ads and audience ads.

  • A remarketing audience is a group of people who visited your website. The site recognises them through tracking cookies (small files that let ad tools know a browser has visited before). Showing Google or Facebook ads to those visitors is called remarketing. Remarketing builds the audience for later promotions. **

  • Remarketing audiences grow from paid ads, organic search (free results from search engines) and direct visits (people typing your address). Each visitor who lands on the store adds to the audience for future campaigns. **

  • Retargeting (showing ads again to people who already interacted with your brand) needs no prior sales. People who viewed products on the site without buying are enough to fill the remarketing audience. **

  • Do not target people who have never visited the store or do not know the brand with Meta remarketing ads. Meta is the company that runs Facebook and Instagram. Remarketing is only for visitors who already showed interest by viewing products on the store.

  • Audiences make retargeting work. They turn the cookie pool (everyone whose browser holds your tracking cookie) into groups that receive more personal and relevant ads. Without segmenting (splitting people into smaller groups), most of the benefit of retargeting is lost. **

  • Retention is the number of days a person stays in an audience after the triggering action. The triggering action is the event that put them there, such as a visit. Visitor and purchase audiences are dynamic, meaning they refresh by themselves as people act. A person who visited within the last 7 days is in the 7-day audience. They drop out on day 8 if they do not return.

  • Build dynamic audiences in Meta and Google before launch, such as 180-day website visitor audiences. Launch means starting your ads. These audiences start empty. They fill automatically as people visit, and each person drops out after the window ends.

  • The three main audience types are:

    • retargeting and customer match (people who already interacted with the brand or are on your customer list). Customer match means uploading your customer email list to an ad tool;
    • search intent (people actively searching for relevant terms). Search intent means they type words that show they want to buy;
    • lookalikes (people who resemble existing customers). A lookalike audience is built from people similar to your buyers.

    Cold traffic means people who do not know your brand yet. Lookalike and search-intent audiences are central to cold traffic campaigns. The text gives the most attention to the first two types.

Audience Types by Funnel Stage

1 min
  • A funnel is the path from first interest to purchase. Top of funnel (TOF) is first interest. Middle of funnel (MOF) is active consideration. Bottom of funnel (BOF) is ready to buy.
  • This text defines MOF and BOF audiences only. It does not define top-of-funnel retargeting audiences. For cold traffic, it points to search-intent and lookalike audiences.
  • Facebook remarketing can follow visitors who viewed low-margin product pages. Low margin means little profit per sale. This works even when those products are excluded from cold Google traffic. It builds brand association (people link your brand with your products) without buying cold traffic. **

Create them in the custom audiences section of Business Manager. Business Manager is Meta's tool for running ads. Custom audiences are lists you build from your own data.
  • Sources differ on engagement and video windows. A window is the period of time that counts, such as 7 days.

Bottom of Funnel (BOF)

1 min
  • BOF audiences are people who added to cart (put items in a shopping basket), started checkout, or purchased.
  • Always exclude purchasers (people who already bought) from retargeting campaigns. The exception is when you promote another product to them. A campaign is one set of ads aimed at one audience.
  • Past buyers and non-buyers should have separate remarketing audiences. Their date ranges (how far back each audience looks) can vary across campaigns. The lists of people who bought and people who did not buy are the basis for this. **

Segmentation by Pages and Actions

1 min
  • Segment retargeting audiences by how each visitor interacted with the store. Segmenting means splitting people into smaller groups. Examples are which pages they viewed, or whether they added to cart. Each segment should receive ads matched to that behaviour. **
  • Putting all visitors into one or very few retargeting segments gives generic ads that convert poorly (few visitors become buyers). Split the cookie pool into several audiences with different intent levels. Intent level means how close someone is to buying. **
  • Using one broad audience, such as everyone who viewed a product in the last 30 days, misses opportunities. Segment audiences by funnel stage so each group receives more relevant content. **
  • Most people start by retargeting everyone who viewed a product page. That can be enough at very low traffic. As traffic grows, segment the cookie pool further so ads match each group's stage and interest. **

Procedure: Building Segmented Audiences and Ads

1 min
  1. Build a range of retargeting audiences tailored to specific pages and actions on the website. **
  2. Run a set of retargeting ads that remind, build trust, educate, recommend and show why to buy from this business. **
  3. Vary the angle to reduce ad fatigue (people tire of seeing the same ad) and match each prospect's stage. A prospect is a visitor who might buy. An angle is the message or viewpoint of an ad.

Procedure: Starting from High-Value Visits

1 min
  1. Start by retargeting the highest-value visits, such as longer visits and product or content engagement. **
  2. Use Analytics Audiences to build highly segmented retargeting audiences. Analytics Audiences are lists built from Google Analytics, a Google tool that measures site visits. **
  3. Deliver a different message to each segment based on its stage in the funnel. **

Quality Filters: Time on Site

1 min
  • A standard retargeting pixel (a tiny tracking code on your website) adds every visitor to the audience. This includes people who stay only 10 seconds on a content or product page. These visitors are very unlikely to buy. Yet they are shown ads for 30 to 60 days. This wastes spend (money put into ads) and lowers click-through rate (the share of people who click an ad after seeing it). **
  • Focus retargeting on your most valuable potential customers. These are visitors who spend more time on the site and engage seriously with products and content. This tightens ad spend and improves conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy). **
  • A narrower product page audience can be limited to visitors who stayed on the product page for longer than 30 seconds. These visitors are further down the funnel (closer to buying) than people who only glanced at the page. **
  • Visitors who stay about 10 seconds are rarely serious buyers. People who spend minutes on the site or on one page are considering a purchase. Filtering by duration focuses the budget on that group and raises click-through rate. **
  • Time on site is one of the audience types in the segmentation list ("Users by time on site"). **

Lookback Windows by Price and Traffic

1 min
  • A lookback window is how many days back an audience looks for visitors or buyers.

Standard Windows for Website Visitors

1 min
  • Website-visitor audiences are commonly built at 7, 30, 60, 90 and 180 days. The right window depends on traffic volume (how many visitors you get). A store with heavy traffic can use a short 7-day audience as its main pool. A smaller store may need a 30- or 60-day window.
  • One source sets the remarketing audience window at 30 days: target only website visitors from the last 30 days.
  • For each audience, create time-based versions covering the last 7, 14 and 30 days. **

Recency Groups

1 min
  • Create separate audiences for people who visited in the last 7 days, 7-30 days, 30-60 days, and 60+ days. 60+ days means more than 60 days ago. This lets you adjust messaging frequency (how often ads show) and offer intensity (how strong the deal is) for each recency group.
  • Three segment names to set up for remarketing: Website Visitors exc. Customers, 7 Days, Website Visitors exc. Customers, 30 Days, and Website Visitors exc. Customers, 180 Days. "exc." means excluding, which means leaving out.

Price-Based Duration

1 min
  • Retargeting audience duration by product price (a guideline from this source):
Product price Retargeting audience duration
Under $100 7 days
$100-$500 14 days
$500-$1,000 30 days
Over $1,000 60-90 days
  • Higher-priced products get a longer audience window, because buyers usually take longer to decide on a larger purchase. Lower-priced products get a shorter window.
  • Shoppers typically make their purchase decision within two to five days of first visiting the store. Plan remarketing windows around that period.

Customer Lists

1 min
  • Customer lists can use longer windows, such as three to four months. **
  • The uploaded email list has no expiration. An uploaded email list is a list of customer emails you send to an ad tool. Use the three-to-four-month window for time-based customer audiences and the no-expiration setting for an uploaded custom audience.

Shorter or Longer Windows

1 min
  • Shorter remarketing windows usually have a better return on ad spend. A 60-day window is still better than none if the 30-day audience is too small.

Narrow-Window Audiences

1 min
  • Longer audience minus shorter audience = a narrow window audience. "Minus" means leave out. Example: 14-day product viewers minus 7-day product viewers equals people who entered the audience between day 7 and day 14. Ad networks (the platforms that run ads, such as Google and Meta) often cannot set this day range directly, so exclusion rules create it. Exclusion rules tell the ad tool to leave out a group. **
  • A retargeting ad can be limited to visitors who viewed a product 7 to 14 days ago. Do this by targeting the 14-day product viewer audience and excluding the 7-day product viewer audience. This keeps the ads away from people who just visited and stops them once the visit is older than 14 days. **
  • A 7 to 14 day window reserves the ads for people who are still considering the product. Reaching them too early risks ads that feel pushy. This is a starting setup for the Google and Facebook examples and can be changed based on results. **

Procedure: Building the Remarketing Window Structure

1 min
  1. Create website visitor audiences for 180, 90, 60, 30, 14, 7 and 2 days, if each audience is big enough.
  2. Create one remarketing campaign per audience.
  3. In each campaign, target recent website visitors for that window and exclude visitors from the next shorter window: 180 excludes 90, 90 excludes 60, 60 excludes 30, 30 excludes 14, 14 excludes 7, 7 excludes 2, and 2 excludes nobody.
  4. Exclude recent customers in every campaign.
  5. Set the budget for each campaign separately. A budget is the money you allow for a campaign.

Stacking

1 min
  • Retargeting audience stacking layers a second targeting method on top of the website visitor audience. Stacking means adding another filter on top. This reaches people actively researching. Example: YouTube content in the same category as the business, such as furniture for an office furniture store.
  • Audience size rule for stacking targeting filters:
    • under 5,000 users: do not add an extra topic or placement filter. A topic filter targets a subject. A placement filter targets where the ad appears;
    • over 5,000 users: add a filter to target people actively searching the topic.

Retargeting Only Valuable Visitors

1 min
  • Two sources give different defaults for the audience pool (everyone you could target):
    • retarget every visitor who leaves the store without buying, using Google and Facebook remarketing from the start. This applies to all visitors, not only those who added items to the cart.
    • Newer (2021-22): focus on the most valuable visitors, meaning those who spend more time and engage seriously. This tightens spend and raises conversion rate. **
  • Use the focused approach as the default. Reach every product viewer only when traffic is too low to segment, as the 2021-22 guidance allows. **
  • Filter by duration to keep the budget on visitors who are considering a purchase. **
  • Do not target people who have never visited the store.

Why retargeting matters and what to expect

Section 16.1 · 1 of 24 in this chapter · 9 min read

In short

  • Most visitors leave a store without buying on their first visit.
  • Retargeting means showing ads again to people who already visited. It brings them back.
  • Run retargeting next to ads for new visitors, not instead of them.
  • Retargeting visitors usually buys more than showing ads to strangers.
  • Do not judge retargeting only by direct sales, especially for expensive products.

Why Most Visitors Leave Without Buying

1 min
  • A conversion rate is the share of visitors who buy. It is never 100 percent, so most visitors leave without buying. Remarketing ads bring those visitors back.
  • Plan for most visitors not buying on their first visit. This is true even when the store is well designed, converts well and has strong products. Remarketing aims to get them to take the first step toward buying.
  • Most first-time visitors leave without buying. Retargeting ads bring them back to the products they already viewed. The brand stays in front of the people closest to ordering.
  • About 97.7 percent of visitors leave without buying on their first visit. Retargeting and Facebook dynamic product ads (DPA, ads that automatically show the exact products a visitor viewed) bring these visitors back.
  • Typical e-commerce conversion rates are around 1 percent, 2 percent or 0.5 percent. So most visitors leave without buying on that visit. Retargeting keeps these visitors engaged so they can return and complete a purchase.
  • In an example store with about 500,000 dollars of monthly sales, the conversion rate was below 1 percent. No site reaches 100 percent. So most visitors leave without buying, and retargeting them has real value.
  • Visitors who buy on their first visit are a minority. Retargeting covers the rest.
  • Shoppers forget a store quickly, even with a strong domain name (your web address). So remarketing is needed to bring them back. **
  • Skipping retargeting means losing sales. Many visitors leave without buying and need repeated exposure (seeing the brand again and again) to convert. Treat retargeting as a standard part of the setup, not an optional extra.

The Case for Retargeting

1 min

Reaching People Who Already Showed Interest

1 min
  • Retargeting works because it reaches people who already showed interest in the product. It works best alongside cold prospecting ads (ads aimed at people who have never visited), not instead of them.
  • Remarketing targets people who already showed interest. It brings previous website visitors back to complete their orders.
  • Remarketing recaptures sales from first-visit non-buyers. It re-engages visitors who showed interest in specific products.
  • Remarketing shows ads for the exact products a visitor viewed, to people who left without buying. Clicking the ad returns them to those product pages so they can complete the purchase.
  • Retargeting, also called remarketing, shows ads to people who have already visited your website.
  • Retargeting ads show specific ads to people who already viewed products on the store. The goal is to bring them back to finish a purchase. **
  • Retargeting shows ads to people who already visited the website and took an action. Examples are adding to cart (putting an item in the shopping basket), viewing a product page, viewing a collection page (a page listing several products) or viewing a content page. Its purpose is to bring those visitors back to complete a purchase. **
  • Retargeting means showing specific ads to people who already interacted with your store. For example, they added a product to cart, viewed a product page, a collection page or a content page. The audience is built from these on-site actions (things people did on your website). **

Higher Conversion Than Cold Traffic

1 min
  • Retargeting conversion rates are 2 to 10 times higher than cold traffic conversion rates. Cold traffic means visitors who have never seen your store.
  • People who already visited the store are expected to convert at a higher rate than cold traffic. So retargeting is usually the stronger bottom-of-funnel layer (the last step before buying). The source claims this holds for about 99 percent of e-commerce stores. This is a rule of thumb, not a verified statistic. Check it against your own account data.
  • Retargeting lists convert better than cold traffic. So you can bid more aggressively (pay more per click) for these users, or offer them a coupon (a discount code).
  • Retargeting ads lifted conversion rate by about 26 percent on average. For example, a 1 percent conversion rate becomes 1.26 percent after adding Facebook dynamic retargeting ads.
  • A cited figure claims retargeting raises the chance that a visitor converts by 70 percent. This only holds when the original traffic was well targeted. Do not apply the figure to poorly targeted campaigns.

Lower Cost of Reach

1 min
  • Converting existing buyers or visitors is said to cost about seven times less than acquiring new customers. So retargeting should get real budget and attention. This is a general claim, not a verified benchmark (a typical number to compare against).
  • Retargeting ads show to people who already visited the store. This lets a small budget bring back visitors who are likely to buy. This is the main way to spend little on paid ads early. **

Several Touchpoints Before Purchase

1 min
  • On average across e-commerce, a customer needs about 7 touchpoints (each time a customer sees or interacts with the brand) before feeling ready to buy. This is an average. Some customers buy after fewer, others after more. **
  • Across the e-commerce industry, a customer needs on average about seven touchpoints with a business before buying. Some need fewer and some need more. So seven is a benchmark, not a fixed rule. **
  • Because customers need several brand interactions before buying, reach them as quickly as possible. Keep the brand within their attention until they are ready to purchase. **
  • Because customers need several brand interactions before buying, fit those touchpoints into as short a time as possible. Staying within the customer's attention until they are ready to buy is the purpose of retargeting. **
  • Retargeting keeps the brand top of mind (the first brand people think of) during the period before purchase. So when the customer is ready to buy, they think of this store, not competitors who also target them. **
  • Retargeting also keeps your brand top of mind. So when a visitor is ready to buy, they choose your store, not a competitor. Direct conversions (sales traced straight back to the ad) are a welcome bonus, not the only goal. **

Competition and Brand Choice

1 min
  • The main remarketing goal is to make sure no lead or customer is lost. A lead is someone who showed interest in buying. Many competitors sell similar products, so the customer should choose your brand when ready to buy. Abandoned cart emails (emails sent to people who left items in their cart) and dynamic product ads are the basic must-have tactics.

Platform Advantage

1 min
  • A Shopify store (a store-building platform) lets the seller run retargeting for visitors who did not buy. This is hard to do on Amazon. Retargeting works best when the traffic is sent to a store focused on the seller's own products.

Retargeting Setup and Process

1 min

Audiences and Ad Creative

1 min
  • Remarketing targets website visitors and past buyers who did not complete a purchase or made a one-time purchase. It uses abandoned cart emails, custom audiences (lists of people built from your data, such as site visitors) on Facebook and Google, and dynamic (changing by itself) or static (fixed) ad creative (the ad images, text and video) to re-engage them.
  • Facebook dynamic product ads (DPA) are used to bring back visitors who left without buying.
  • The ideal return point is the product page the visitor was already viewing. That is where the first purchase action is most likely.
  • Missing follow-up, such as retargeting or email, lets visitors who leave without buying disappear. This pulls the conversion rate down. Retargeting must be planned as part of the traffic review (checking where visitors come from), because it runs on the same traffic platforms. **

Audience Window and Budget

1 min
  • Facebook retargeting ROAS (return on ad spend: sales earned for each dollar spent on ads) depends on the audience window (how many days back you count visitors). A 4-day audience usually performs much better than a 30- or 60-day audience. With small budgets and a good traffic source, a 30 to 50 times return on ad spend is realistic.

Example Sequence

1 min

**

  1. Send visitors to the store from Google Shopping ads (product ads in Google search results) and search traffic.
  2. Track the visitors who did not buy on their first visit.
  3. Retarget those visitors with email marketing, Facebook ads and Google ads.
  4. Keep showing products until the visitor is ready to buy, then move them to purchase.
  5. Repeat the cycle with new first-time search traffic.

Example From Source C

1 min
  • Retargeting drove most sales for this store. Visitors often leave on their first visit, then return after retargeting. A compelling offer (a strong deal) pushes them to buy. Plan retargeting ads for visitors who did not purchase.

Do Not Judge Retargeting Only by Direct Conversions

1 min

Direct Conversion Varies by Ticket Size

1 min

Ticket size means the price of one order. High-ticket products are expensive. Low-ticket products are cheap.

  • Retargeting should be seen as more than a direct conversion tool (a tool that only creates sales right away). Many visitors do not convert immediately but still benefit from repeated exposure to the brand. **
  • Retargeting ads should not be judged only by direct conversions. Low-ticket products often show high conversion rates from retargeting. High-ticket products often show no directly attributable conversions (sales clearly traced back to the ad), even though the ads still influence buyers. **
  • On low-ticket products, retargeting often shows a high, directly measurable conversion rate. On high-ticket products, it often has no clearly attributable conversion rate. Yet it still serves a purpose and should not be judged only by direct sales. **

Check the Role Before Cutting

1 min
  • Retargeting campaigns (groups of ads) that generate clicks and cost money but no attributed sales are not automatically failing, especially for higher-ticket products. Before cutting them, check their role in keeping the brand in front of the customer. **

Further details

1 min
  • At any time only about 10% of the total available market is ready to buy. This is the bottom-of-funnel segment (the last step before buying) that needs little extra effort to convert. Growing traffic increases the share of audience that needs education and support.

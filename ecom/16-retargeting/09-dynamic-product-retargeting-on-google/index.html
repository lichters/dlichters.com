Dynamic product retargeting on Google

Section 16.9 · 9 of 24 in this chapter · 4 min read

In short

  • Dynamic remarketing shows past visitors the exact products they looked at, which usually makes it the best Google retargeting.
  • Your product feed (your product list sent to Google) must contain products before the ads show anything.
  • For beginners, the ROI Hunter app sets up the ads with no code. Run its quick sync, then keep the default audience settings at first launch.
  • Make all later campaign changes in Google Ads, and as the store grows, only raise the daily budget.

Concept and Scope

  • Remarketing means showing ads again to people who already visited your store. Dynamic remarketing on Google targets people who viewed products on your store but did not buy. The ads show those exact products and link to the same product pages.
  • Dynamic ads are the most important form of Google retargeting (showing ads again to past visitors) because they perform best. Standard display ads (ads with a fixed image) and YouTube remarketing (video ads for past visitors) are also possible, but test them for profitability first (check that they make money before you spend more).
  • Dynamic ads are the fifth element of the Power Five (a named list of five key parts) and drive dynamic remarketing.
  • Dynamic ads are the form of Google remarketing to use with Shopping ads (product ads shown in Google search results) so customers see the products they viewed.
  • Dynamic ads pull images and links from the product feed (your product list file), so each viewer sees their own products. Each image links to its own product page. A static display ad (one fixed image) shows the same image and destination to everyone.
  • Dynamic ads pull products from the Merchant Center feed (the product list stored in Google Merchant Center) based on each visitor's views. Clicking an ad opens the exact product, so no product-level setup is needed in the ad itself.
  • Dynamic display retargeting follows visitors across the Google Display Network (a group of websites and apps that show Google ads), including when they browse other websites.
  • Dynamic ads automatically show products the visitor already looked at, including items added to the cart (the shopping basket).
  • Google remarketing ads also appear on any website monetised by Google AdSense (Google's program that lets websites show ads and earn money), including eBay, without special configuration. (Older advice, 2021. The original heading names Facebook, but its rule describes Google remarketing.)

Feed and Product Requirements

  • Dynamic ads show products from your product feed. A new store with an empty feed shows no products, so the feed must contain your products before the ads are useful.
  • Google Display remarketing campaigns update automatically when the product feed changes in Merchant Center (Google's tool for managing your product data).
  • Products used in dynamic display ads must pass Shopping ads policy review (Google's check that products follow its rules). Until they pass, they may show as disapproved (rejected) or invalid for that destination. **

Visitor Tagging and Tracking

  • Dynamic retargeting requires each visitor to be tagged (marked with a small piece of code that records what they viewed) with the products they viewed. This is what makes product-specific ads possible.
  • Custom code (code you write yourself) for this tagging often breaks after Shopify platform updates. For non-developers (people who do not write code), a maintained app (a ready-made tool its makers keep up to date) is the lower-risk option.

ROI Hunter Setup

Use ROI Hunter (a Shopify app that sets up dynamic ads for you) as the standard route for non-developers. Installation and sync take a couple of minutes, and no custom code is required.

  1. Search the Shopify App Store (Shopify's shop for apps) for ROI Hunter and open the app by ROI Hunter.
  2. Click Get to install the app.
  3. Run the quick sync (a fast link between your store and Google Ads). It asks for your starting budget (the money limit you set at the start) and other initial settings.
  4. Optionally preview the ads and upload your logo.
  5. Confirm that the three display campaigns and their audiences (groups of visitors Google tracks) were created automatically.
  6. Manage all further campaign changes in Google Ads (Google's ad control panel), not inside Shopify.

ROI Hunter Campaigns and Audiences

  • ROI Hunter creates three separate display campaigns (groups of ads with their own settings):
Campaign Audience it targets
Dynamic Remarketing for Product Viewers Product Viewers
Dynamic Remarketing for Category Viewers Category Viewers
Dynamic Remarketing for Cart Abandoners Cart Abandoners
  • ROI Hunter creates these smart audiences (groups of visitors that Google builds automatically) in Google Ads. The default lookback window (how many past days of visitors are counted) is 7 days:
    • Cart Abandoners (past 7 days)
    • Category Viewers (past 7 days)
    • Product Viewers (past 7 days)
  • At first launch, keep the default audience conditions (the rules that decide who is in an audience) instead of editing them.
  • Editing conditions is optional and only worth doing once you have data to test against.
  • To edit conditions, open the audience in Google Ads Audience Manager (the Google Ads page where audiences are kept) and use the edit list option. The audiences appear under their names, for example Cart Abandoners, Category Viewers and Product Viewers.

Management and Optimisation

  • The only ongoing adjustment is increasing the daily budget (the amount you spend per day) as the store scales (grows).
  • You do not optimise dynamic ads directly. Improvement comes from increasing the volume of qualified visitors (people likely to buy) to your store.

Expected Clicks, Conversions and Cost

  • Dynamic retargeting on the Display Network usually produces cheaper clicks and higher conversion rates (the share of visitors who buy) than standard retargeting, because the ads show the products the visitor already looked at.

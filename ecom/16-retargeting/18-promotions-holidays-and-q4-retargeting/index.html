Promotions, holidays and Q4 retargeting

Section 16.18 · 18 of 24 in this chapter · 14 min read

In short

  • Use retargeting (showing ads again to people who already visited the store) for holiday sales. Run those ads only during the sale dates.
  • For a specific offer, aim at people who viewed a product about 30 days ago. Do not discount just because someone looked.
  • Leave out recent buyers, so they do not see the same discount again.
  • Build ad text from the email text, so the offer, code and deadline match.

Offer-based retargeting

1 min

Two types of deal

1 min
  • Irresistible deal ads (ads with an offer people find hard to say no to) come in two types. One type is aimed at one person based on where they are in the customer journey (the steps a buyer takes before buying). The other type is tied to a holiday or season, such as Black Friday (the sale day after Thanksgiving), Christmas, Thanksgiving, Veterans Day or Easter. Event deals can be niche-specific (for one market) or global (for all markets).
  • For a holiday deal, retarget everyone, including previous customers, with a campaign that runs only for the deal period. Where a person is in the funnel (the path from first visit to purchase) does not matter here.
  • For a specific offer, target product viewers who are near the point of no return (the moment a buyer is almost decided). This is generally around 30 days after the product view, so a 14 to 30 day viewer window is the usual target.
  • If your funnel moves fast, shorten the specific-offer window, for example to 7 days after the product view. High-ticket products (expensive products) usually have longer buyer journeys, so their offers should come later.
  • Do not offer a discount just because someone viewed a product. A buyer who needs more time may not need a discount to convert (to buy). Do not rush the deal. Protect profit by timing it to the customer journey.

Deal timing

1 min
  • Schedule the Christmas promotion in early December. Running it on Christmas Day misses buyers, who have spent their budgets and are not shopping online.
  • Publish holiday content weeks ahead of the event. A holiday blog post published just before the sale is wasted effort, because it will not have time to rank (appear high in search results) and few people will see it.
  • Run promotion-specific Google and Meta ads only during that promotion's dates. Each holiday then gets its own creative (ad image or video) and a short exposure window (time the ad is shown).
  • Run retargeting ads from November 24 through the end of Cyber Monday (the online sale day after Thanksgiving).
  • High-CPM periods (times when ads cost more, measured as cost per 1,000 views) are ideal for retargeting. The warm audience (people who already know the store) has high conversion intent (how likely they are to buy), despite higher CPM.
  • Build the visitor audience early. Then spend on remarketing when ad costs rise in Q4 (October to December).

Monthly promotions

1 min
  • Retargeting ads work alongside email to capture extra revenue during holidays and promotions. They are run as a standard part of the promotion plan.
  • During a short seasonal sale, point paid ads at remarketing audiences (saved lists of people who already engaged). This pushes visitors toward the discount. Use the same offer, coupon code and dates across the emails and the ads, so the message stays consistent.
  • Promotion ads are not product-specific like dynamic ads (ads that automatically show the product a person viewed). They push one store-wide deal, bundle (several products sold together) or bonus to lapsed contacts (people who stopped engaging). The goal is to re-ignite the reason the shopper searched for the product in the first place.
  • Remarketing to warm audiences costs less than reaching cold traffic (strangers who do not know the store). Promotions can run on a small budget if the offer is strong. Good offers in these audiences can produce a very high ROAS (return on ad spend, meaning money earned for each dollar spent on ads).
  • Static remarketing ads (fixed image ads) aimed at older website-visitor lists are not limited to holidays. They also run during regular monthly promotions. They show store products with the current coupon code and link to the store.
  • During the promotion window, run Facebook and Google remarketing ads to the same audience that receives the promotion emails. This maximizes reach and increases sales from people who have already visited but not bought.

Promotion audience and sequence

1 min
  • The promotion audience is the catch-all group of people who engaged with the store but have not bought. This includes website visitors, email opt-ins (people who signed up for emails) and Facebook and Google remarketing lists. Promotions are sent to them only after they have gone through abandoned cart emails (emails to people who left items in their cart) and dynamic product remarketing ads without purchasing.

Procedure:

  1. Visitor views a product and leaves without buying. Add them to Facebook and Instagram remarketing and Google remarketing.
  2. Send them through the email flows (automatic email series), starting with the abandoned cart sequence. Show dynamic product remarketing ads that return them to the product they viewed.
  3. Anyone who still has not purchased after these automations (automatic systems) is added to the promotion audience and receives the promotional offers.

BFCM and holiday campaigns

1 min

Standard BFCM campaign

1 min

BFCM means Black Friday and Cyber Monday.

Procedure:

  1. Use 5-6 different creatives (ad images or videos) per campaign.
  2. Prioritize image ads. They perform best during holidays.
  3. Highlight the discount (for example, "60-70% Off") and show a price comparison (for example, "Was 159, Now 89.99, Limited Time Only").
  4. Run this on the warm audience (recent visitors and cart abandoners, meaning people who left items in their cart).
  5. High-CPM periods are ideal for retargeting, because the warm audience has high conversion intent despite CPM increases.

Procedure, audience and traffic routing (for the Black Friday to Cyber Monday window):

  1. Primary audience (prioritize this one): people who entered the email sequence, meaning newsletter subscribers.
  2. If the primary audience is too small for a viable campaign (one that can work), expand to hot audiences. Hot audiences are anyone who engaged with your brand:
    • Visited your website
    • Watched 50%, 75%, or 90% of your videos
    • Engaged with your Facebook page (likes, comments, shares)
    • Engaged with your Instagram page
  3. Run retargeting ads from November 24 through the end of Cyber Monday.
  4. Send all retargeting traffic to the hidden category page (a store page not shown in the main menu), not to product pages.

Stacked campaign for a short, high-demand event

1 min
  • For a short high-demand event such as Black Friday or Christmas, one approach was to stack all retargeting audiences into one ad set (a group of ads that share one audience and budget). Use at least 10 creatives that combine testimonials (customer reviews) with offer images. Add roughly 10 to 30 dollars to the budget.
  • The retargeting audience is small, so it needs fresh proof and new offers. Do not show the same ad over and over.

Holiday-specific Facebook audiences

1 min
  • For holiday Facebook remarketing, target all visitors from the past 180 days instead of only the recently viewed-product carousel (the row of recently viewed products) used normally. Use creative that is specific to the holiday, such as gift boxes with the offer shown on the image.
  • Run holiday-specific Facebook ads to a custom audience (a saved list you build) of website visitors from up to 180 days ago. Run them alongside dynamic product ads. Example offers: 10 percent off the entire store for Cyber Monday, or a free water bottle with every purchase.
  • Each visitor can see several different holiday offers within about three months when the same offer and creatives as the holiday email campaign are used.

Static holiday ads

1 min
  • Standard remarketing uses dynamic ads. Holiday promotions use static image ads (fixed images you upload). Each holiday gets its own campaign on Meta (the company behind Facebook and Instagram) and its own display and demand gen campaigns on Google.
  • For Q4 remarketing on Meta, use static remarketing ads, one for each holiday, uploaded as images. Do not use dynamic product ads for these holiday campaigns.
  • For Q4 remarketing on Google, use static display ads (image ads on partner websites) and static demand gen ads (ads shown in video and feed placements). Target the same audience of website visitors from the last 180 days, and exclude customers from the last 30 days.
  • Dynamic remarketing ads that show the exact products a visitor viewed should target recent website visitors who have not bought. Examples are 7-, 30- or 60-day audiences. These audiences form the main group used to bring buyers back during regular promotions. [L623.008]
  • Visitors who were last active 30, 60 or 90+ days ago may no longer remember which products they viewed. Dynamic product ads are a weak fit for them. Static ads that show store products and the holiday offer work better for these older audiences. [L623.009]
  • Example: A static holiday remarketing ad shows several store products with a holiday logo placed over them. The ad text states that a promotion is running, names the saving and the coupon code, and links to the store. [L623.010]

Conflict on dynamic ads during holidays:

  • Use dynamic product ads alongside holiday static ads on a 180-day custom audience.
  • Do not use dynamic product ads for the holiday campaigns. Use static ads only. (2024-2025 advice)
  • Both approaches appear in the source. The 2019 advice pairs holiday ads with dynamic product ads on the 180-day audience. The 2024-2025 advice drops dynamic ads from holiday campaigns entirely.

180-day audiences and Q4 remarketing

1 min
  • For Q4 remarketing, target all website visitors from the last 180 days. Exclude all customers who purchased in the last 30 days. Do this on both Meta and Google. (2024)
  • Meta (Facebook) ads for the promotion should carry the same message as the emails. Show them to all website visitors from the last 180 days. Exclude all customers from the last 30 days. (2024)
  • Google Ads for the promotion should carry the same message as the emails. Show them to all website visitors from the last 180 days. Exclude all customers from the last 30 days. Run them as Display Ads and Demand-Gen Ads. (2024)
  • Meta remarketing targets all website visitors from the last 180 days, excluding customers from the last 30 days. Use the same offer and creatives as the holiday email campaign. Each visitor can then see several different holiday offers within about three months.
  • Google remarketing uses the same audience as Meta: site visitors from the last 180 days, excluding customers from the last 30 days. Run both Display and Demand Gen ads on this audience. The two formats add placements (places where ads appear) for short promotion windows.
  • For Google display and demand gen retargeting, use an audience of all website visitors from the last 180 days. Exclude customers from the last 30 days. Run both ad types on the same audience to get more placements for short promotion windows. (2025)
  • Matching the ad message to the email message keeps the offer consistent across channels during the same promotion window. Excluding recent buyers stops the promotion from following customers who just purchased. (2024)

Email and ad exclusions

1 min
  • Email promotions go to all contacts except those who purchased in the last 30 days. Also leave out those currently in an evergreen automation (an always-on email series), such as an abandoned cart sequence. The same segment applies whether you use Shopify Email or Klaviyo.
  • Do not run holiday promotions as cold campaigns to people who have never visited the store or joined the list. Promote only to email subscribers and recent site visitors. Exclude recent buyers so they do not see the same discount again.

Ad copy and message consistency

1 min
  • Remarketing ads on Meta and the Google Display Network (Google's network of partner websites that show ads) can reuse the offer, code and deadline from the promotion's email copy. This keeps the message consistent across channels. Build the ad copy from the emails rather than writing it from scratch. [L620.005]
  • Prompt for writing remarketing ad copy from the email copy:
Using the email copy provided above, write copy for the remarketing ads I will run on Meta and the Google Display Network. Please write a separate version for each platform, and for each include a headline, primary text, and a call to action. Keep the discount code, the 10% discount, the promotion dates, and the delivery-before-Christmas guarantee identical to the emails.

Budget math per promotion

1 min

Small-budget principles

1 min
  • Holiday remarketing ads can run on a very small budget and still reach many people. Budget is therefore rarely the reason to skip this step. [L623.012]
  • Remarketing budget for a small holiday audience is about $5 per day. This makes the ad spend a small cost compared with the email sales it supports. The budget can be scaled with the audience size.
  • Remarketing to warm audiences costs less than reaching cold traffic. Promotions can run on a small budget if the offer is strong.
  • Remarketing to people who have already visited the website is typically the lowest-cost advertising. Those visitors are cheaper to reach than new traffic.

Budget procedure

1 min
  1. Count how many people are in each Facebook and Google audience. [L757.033]
  2. Decide how many times you want each person to see your ads. This is called frequency (how many times one person sees the ad). [L757.033]
  3. Find the current remarketing CPM (cost per 1,000 ad views) for each platform from your historic data (your past results). [L757.033]
  4. Calculate the budget for each audience and total it per platform. [L757.033]
  5. Divide each platform total by the number of promotion days to get the daily budget. [L757.033]

Formula

1 min

Daily budget = total platform budget / number of promotion days. For a 3-day promotion, divide the Google total by 3 and the Facebook total by 3. [L757.035]

Worked example

1 min

Small-audience example: seven-day website visitors, 2,000 people each on Google and Facebook, frequency 6. At a $1.50 Google CPM the total is $18 (about $6/day over 3 days). At a $5.00 Facebook CPM the total is $60 (about $20/day over 3 days). [L757.037]

Ending promotions cleanly

1 min
  • Leaving bundle pages, bonus gifts or progress trackers live after a promotion ends sends visitors to dead ends and rewards that no longer exist. Revert every change in the closing procedure.
  • Run promotion-specific ads only during that promotion's dates, so each holiday has its own creative and a short exposure window. Stop them when the window closes.

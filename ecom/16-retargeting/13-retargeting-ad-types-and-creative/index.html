Retargeting ad types and creative

Section 16.13 · 13 of 24 in this chapter · 8 min read

In short

  • Pick the ad type first. Then make only the pictures and text that type needs.
  • Show product reminder and trusted advisor ads to people who already looked at your products but did not buy.
  • Do not mention past browsing in an ad. Give value and the next step, and let visitors connect the dots.
  • Test many ads with different messages. Keep the ones that sell best.
  • Reuse your best past offers for new groups of visitors. This saves time and money.
This section has 12 parts. Tap one to open it.

Retargeting ad types and networks

1 min
  • Every retargeting ad fits one of four basic types. Retargeting means showing ads again to people who already visited your store. The type decides which files and text you must make.
  • The four types are:
    • Static image ad: one fixed picture. It may have text on the picture. It has no separate ad copy (the written words of the ad).
    • Responsive ad: fixed pictures plus several separate ad copy versions. The network mixes them and tests them on its own.
    • Static image and text ad: a fixed picture plus separate ad copy. The copy can be a headline (the big title line), a call to action (words that tell people what to do, such as "Shop now"), or long body copy (the main text).
    • Video ad: a video alone, as on YouTube, with optional overlays (text or buttons placed on top of the video). Or a video with separate ad copy, mainly on Facebook.
  • Each type runs mainly on certain networks. An ad network is a company that shows ads on many websites and apps.
    • Static image ads: mostly on the Google Display Network (Google's group of websites and apps that show image ads) and smaller display networks.
    • Responsive ads: mostly on the Google Display Network.
    • Static image and text ads: mainly on Facebook, Taboola and Twitter.
    • Video ads: on YouTube (video alone, with optional overlays) or Facebook (video with separate ad copy).
  • Choose the ad type first. Then prepare the assets (the files and text the ad needs) that type requires.
  • Static image ads are the simpler format to set up for retargeting on the Google Display Network and Facebook. The same setup logic carries over to most other ad networks.

Image sizes and asset preparation

1 min
  • Image-based retargeting ads need good-quality images.
  • Dynamic product ads are not part of this work. They are ads that pull products from your store automatically, so they reuse existing product ads.
  • Across several networks, the same ad must be made again quickly in many sizes. So you need a fast resizing tool (a tool that changes image size quickly).
  • The Google Display Network offers about 20 different image and ad sizes for static retargeting ads.
  • Make only the most-used sizes first. Start with the sizes that have the highest inventory (the number of places an ad can show) and the best performance.
  • Facebook retargeting ads of every type use one image size: 1200 x 628 pixels. A pixel is one tiny dot in an image.
  • One resized image set covers all Facebook placements (spots on Facebook where ads appear) in retargeting.

Dynamic and site-wide static ads

1 min
  • Use dynamic remarketing for product-specific ads on Google display and Meta. Meta is the company behind Facebook and Instagram. Dynamic remarketing shows the actual products the visitor viewed.
  • Use static site-wide ads only for promotions that cover the whole store.
  • Keep dynamic product ads in the retargeting plan.
  • Pair them with other ad types that approach the visitor from different angles (different ways of making the point).
  • Use these combinations to address the specific reason each visitor has not bought.

Product reminder ads

1 min
  • A product reminder ad reminds people of a specific product they already know.
  • It is meant for audiences that have already seen the product. It is not for cold traffic (people who have never heard of you).
  • Build the audience from visitors who viewed a specific product page.
  • Show the ad 1 to 7 days after that view.
  • The goal is to keep the product top of mind (the first thing they think of) while the shopper decides.
  • Use product reminder ads only for high-volume products, such as best sellers. High-volume means many people buy or view them. The audience has to be big enough to fill.
    • If the store has hundreds or thousands of products, do not build this for every product.
    • If the store has only a handful of products (a few), it can be used for all of them.

Trusted advisor ads

1 min
  • Trusted advisor ads show social proof (other people's reviews or results) to people in retargeting audiences who visited the site without buying.
  • They address doubt. The doubt may be whether the product lives up to its promises. Or whether the business can be trusted.
  • Audiences and windows (how long after the visit the ad can show) for the trusted advisor ad:
Audience Window
Viewers of specific product pages (generally high-volume product pages) 7 to 14 days
Viewers of specific content pages 7 to 14 days
Viewers of any product page 7 to 14 days
  • Run several variations of the trusted advisor ad.
  • Use different reviews in each variation. Then the audience does not see the same ad again and again.

Introduction ads

1 min
  • An introduction ad is a brand ad. A brand ad builds awareness of your business name.
  • This ad shows the business owner's face and invites contact.
  • It works best at roughly 14 to 30 days after the first contact with the visitor.
  • It is not the first retargeting ad a person sees.
  • It can be shown to anyone who interacted with the business. For example, people who viewed products or all-visitor sequences (a set of ads shown one after another).

Informant ads

1 min
  • An informant ad (an ad that teaches something useful) must be genuinely valuable.
  • It may link to products inside the content. Or it may mention them. There is no hard sell (no pushy selling).
  • Products should come up naturally. They should not dominate the content.

Promotion ads

1 min
  • Retargeting image offers can push a viewer who already saw a video to buy.
  • Examples: 50% off with an end-today deadline, a free gift, or a testimonial (a customer's praise of the product).
  • Run these offers in separate image campaigns in the same ad account (the account where you run ads).
  • Video ads bring in colder customers (people who know you less). They work like a sales letter.
  • Image ads in a separate campaign for the same product can act as retargeting for people who watched a video but did not buy.
  • Pattern: a caption that leads with a discount and a coupon code (a code the buyer types at checkout) for a specific percentage off.
  • Then add two real customer review images. For example, the product in hand or worn.
  • The offer addresses price doubts. The reviews address trust doubts. Both sit in one ad.
    • Example: Still thinking about it? Use code SAVE15 for 15 percent off today, plus two photos of customers with the product.
    • Example: Your cart is waiting. Take an extra 10 percent off with CART10, with two real reviews from buyers in your region.

Testimonial and proof videos

1 min
  • Testimonial and proof videos work especially well in retargeting.
  • Warm visitors (people who already came to your store) saw the brand for the first time. They need trust, not just an offer.
  • Trust is the bigger barrier for people who do not know the store. So show real reviews and people using the product.
  • Classic dropshipping-style videos also work well in retargeting. Dropshipping is selling where the supplier ships each order straight to the buyer.
  • These videos convert less in cold traffic. (Convert means turn a visitor into a buyer.)
  • Use them as retargeting creative (the ad itself) alongside UGC (user-generated content, made by real customers) and testimonial videos.
  • Static trust ads matter.
  • Visitors leave for reasons other than lack of interest. For example, they need to check their card, ask someone, or do not know the brand.
  • Showing videos, testimonials and product photos helps them come back.

Creative that works

1 min
  • Retargeting should be subtle.
  • Use each ad to deliver value, state product benefits and offer the next step in the sales funnel (the steps from first look to buying).
  • Do not mention past browsing. Visitors connect the dots themselves, and the ads stay effective.
  • Using one retargeting message for all audiences fails.
  • Visitors have different pain points (problems that hold them back):
    • trust
    • not understanding the product's value
    • not understanding why the business is better than competitors
    • doubting that the product fits their problem
  • One ad cannot address all of these. So the same person should see different ads across the retargeting window.
  • Procedure for building the message set:
    1. Create several ad creatives, messages, styles and angles (the reason or approach used) for retargeting.
    2. Address each likely pain point with its own ad, such as trust, product value, competitor comparison or product fit.
    3. Match each ad as closely as possible to the pages the visitor previously viewed on your site.
    4. Test different combinations and keep the ones that perform best.
  • Customer research makes retargeting ads more personal and relevant.
  • Retargeting ads should hit the points customers need to hear before they buy.
  • A first ad must contain the full case (the complete reason to buy).
  • Do not depend on a later reminder. Readers who see an ad once may never see it again.
  • A repeat exposure does not replace a complete message.

Copy rules

1 min
  • The goal of retargeting ad copy is to get the viewer to click through to the website. Click through means click to go to your site.
  • The goal is not to make the sale.
  • The conversion happens on the website, where the selling takes place.
  • On some placements, such as many Google Display image-only ads, no ad copy is shown.
  • The product image alone must do the persuading.
  • On Facebook, ad copy always appears.
  • So weak copy hurts dynamic product ads there.

Recycling best offers

1 min
  • Identify your best-performing promotional offers and ad creatives from past campaigns. A campaign is a group of ads run for one goal.
  • Then systematically recycle and repurpose them across different remarketing audience segments (groups of people sorted by what they did).
  • The segments are: abandoned carts (people who put items in the cart and left), one-time buyers, inactive visitors and email subscribers.
  • This maximizes ROI (return on investment: money earned compared to money spent).
  • It also reduces the time and cost of creating new creative assets.

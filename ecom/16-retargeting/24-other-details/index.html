Retargeting Principles

  • Retargeting (showing ads again to people who already visited your store) covers several ad types. Each type needs its own setup.
  • Split your audiences (saved groups of people in your ad tools) into smaller segments. Each group must see the message that fits where it is in the buying process.

Retargeting Setup Sequence

Do these nine steps in this order:

  1. Set up all required pixels (small tracking codes that record visitor actions) and tracking codes from Google Ads, Facebook and Google Analytics.
  2. Build the relevant retargeting audiences in Google Ads and Facebook.
  3. Plan the retargeting and email strategy before building anything.
  4. Identify email opt-in opportunities across the site. An opt-in is a visitor agreeing to get your emails.
  5. Review the email list (your list of addresses) and its segmentation structure (how the list is split into groups).
  6. Plan the email sequences (series of automatic emails) for post-purchase (after a buyer orders) and for each opt-in.
  7. Review and update the abandoned cart sequence (automatic emails sent to people who left items in their cart).
  8. Build the first 30-day master flow (your main automatic email path for the first 30 days).
  9. Replicate the master flow for each new entry point (each new way people join your list).

Meta Account Setup

Meta is the company behind Facebook and Instagram. Set up a new Meta account in this order:

  1. Create a free Meta Business Manager (a free tool to manage your ads and pages) at business.facebook.com.
  2. Create a Facebook page for the business.
  3. Create an Instagram page for the business.
  4. Install the Meta pixel (Meta's tracking code) on the Shopify store (your online shop software).
  5. Turn on auto advanced matching (a setting that sends extra visitor details, such as email, so Meta can match people better) in the Meta account.
  6. Create the audiences listed below in Business Manager.
  7. Create the campaigns (groups of ads with one goal). If no lead generation (collecting contact details) or testing is running yet, start with hot dynamic, hot static, customers goodwill and promotions. Dynamic means ads that change to show each person's products.
  8. Fill each ad set (a group of ads sharing one audience and budget) with 3-4 ads that look similar to the creative examples (sample ad designs).

Landing Pages by Audience

  • Speak to different target audiences (groups of people you want to sell to) with separate landing pages (single web pages built for one goal) when they have different needs or experience levels, for example beginner, intermediate and advanced.
  • Match each landing page to the wording of the ad that brings that audience in.
  • This increases relevance (how well the page fits what the visitor wants) and conversion (how many visitors buy).

Traffic Sources and Affiliate Offers

An affiliate offer is a product from another company. You earn a fee when a buyer purchases it through your link.

Amazon Associate stores (Amazon Associate is Amazon's affiliate program)

  • Do not send traffic from Google Shopping (Google's product ads), Google Performance Max (Google's automatic ads across channels) or search ads to an Amazon Associate store.
  • This is not allowed. It can get your account banned.
  • Use Amazon Associate stores only for retargeting people who already know your brand.
  • Batch-imported products (uploaded all at once) get almost no organic traffic (free visits from search results, not ads).

Raw affiliate links (plain long links that send buyers to an affiliate product)

  • Never run paid traffic to a raw affiliate tracking link, such as a long ClickBank hop link (an affiliate link from the ClickBank marketplace).
  • Facebook dislikes affiliate promotion, and the account can be lost.
  • Instead, write a review or informative blog post on your Shopify store.
  • Add the affiliate link there, then retarget the blog post visitors.

Display Placements

  • Remarketing display ads (banner ads shown on other websites to past visitors) can appear on unrelated websites, because they follow the visitor to the next site they visit.
  • Unrelated placements (the websites where your ad shows) are normal. They are not an error on their own.

Video Production

  • Video editing is not a skill a store owner needs to learn.
  • Freelance editors (people you hire per job) can cut a video cheaply.
  • At a rate of one or two videos per month, the cost stays low.

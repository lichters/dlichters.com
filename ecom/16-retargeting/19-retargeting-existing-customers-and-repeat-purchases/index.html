Retargeting existing customers and repeat purchases

Section 16.19 · 19 of 24 in this chapter · 6 min read

In short

  • Past buyers are your easiest sales. Selling to them costs less than finding new customers.
  • Put past buyers in their own group, and do not show them first-purchase ads.
  • Start follow-up messages after delivery, not after the order is placed.
  • Repeat buyers convert far better than new visitors, so keep offering them new products and better deals.

Why Existing Customers Are Worth Targeting

  • Ignoring existing customers is a big missed chance. Selling to an existing customer is much easier than finding a new one.
  • Show relevant upsells (a bigger or better version of a product) and resells (the same product again) through ads to past buyers.
  • After a first sale, retargeting brings the same customer back for second and third purchases. Retargeting means showing ads again to people who already know your store.
  • Repeat email campaigns also bring customers back. An email campaign is a set of emails sent to a group over time.
  • Repeat sales are cheaper to win than new ones, so their profit margins are usually higher. Profit margin is the share of each sale you keep as profit.
  • Second and third purchases from existing customers usually bring in more profit than the first sale.
  • This makes retargeting existing customers a key driver of customer lifetime value, which is the total profit one customer brings over all their purchases.
  • Combine it with email marketing for the best effect.
  • Do not assume existing customers will never buy again. Not all of them will, but some will.
  • So test repeat offers instead of dropping existing customers from campaigns.
  • People who already use the product have already decided. They rarely read ads meant to get new customers. Acquisition means getting new customers. Repeated acquisition ads for current customers waste budget.

Building Customer Audiences

  • Leaving existing customers out of retargeting means missing repeat sales. Build a separate customer audience and give it its own campaigns. A customer audience is a saved group of customers used for ads.
  • Build a customer list from customer email addresses and names.
  • Upload the list to the ad network, which is the company that shows ads, such as Google or Meta. The network matches the list to users on its platform.
  • Wait until the list reaches the network's minimum size before using it. Google Display needs 100 people. Google Display is Google's network of ad spaces on websites and apps.
  • Show ads to this audience only when you have a relevant offer or content for these customers.

Customer audience procedure:

  1. Build a customer list from customer email addresses and names.
  2. Upload the list to the ad network, which matches it to users on its platform.
  3. Wait until the list reaches the network's minimum size before using it. Google Display needs 100 people.
  4. Show ads only when you have a relevant offer or content for these customers.

Excluding Customers from Acquisition

  • If you have enough volume (enough customers in the list), build a customer audience and exclude it from new-customer acquisition campaigns. Exclude means leave out.
  • Customers also need their own retargeting campaigns matched to their stage in the customer journey, which is the steps a person takes from first look to purchase. Do not use first-purchase offers for them.
  • Showing first-purchase retargeting ads to existing customers wastes spend, because they rarely buy the same product right away. Spend means money spent on ads.
  • Serve customers separate messages based on where they are in the journey.
  • Target only first-time buyers by excluding customers with a purchase frequency above one. Purchase frequency is how many times someone has bought. Above one means they bought more than once.
  • Use the same exclusion for the thank-you video so repeat buyers do not receive it again.

Timing After Delivery

  • Timing for existing-customer ads can range from soon after the first purchase to six months later or longer.
  • The right timing depends on the offer, such as an upsell or a reorder, which means buying the same product again.
  • The repeat-purchase funnel, which is a set of steps that aims to get people to buy again, must start only after the customer has received the product. It must not start when the order is placed.
  • If products have different shipping times, separate the audiences by brand or product. Each one is then triggered by its own delivery.

Goodwill and Thank-You Videos

  • Goodwill means building a good feeling without trying to sell.
  • Recent buyers get a goodwill video ad set on reach and frequency. Reach is how many people see it. Frequency is how often each person sees it.
  • Set it at around 2 impressions per day for 7 days. An impression is one showing of the ad.
  • The goal is to be seen, not to sell, so conversion objectives are the wrong choice here. A conversion objective is an ad goal aimed at sales.
  • The thank-you video ad for customers in the first 7 days is set to low frequency, roughly twice a day per person.
  • Customers from the last 180 days see the same content and promotions over time.
  • Goodwill thank-you ad pattern: a short personal video that thanks the buyer, names the store, and says what to expect next. It works because it builds the relationship after purchase and gets high engagement, meaning people react, watch, or click.
    • Example one: "Thank you for ordering from us, here is what happens next and how we will look after you."
    • Example two: "Welcome to the family, here is what to expect from your first week with us."

Thank-you video script for first-time buyers. The store owner records it on camera and it is posted as a Facebook ad after the product has been delivered. Target only people who have bought once, by excluding purchase frequency above one, so repeat buyers do not receive the thank-you message again.

Hi {first name}, I wanted to take a minute to thank you for your order of {product name}. Customers like you are the reason this store exists, and I hope you love using it. Keep an eye on your inbox, because I have a discount for your next order.

Repeat-Purchase Funnel

  • Repeat buyers were cited as converting at 60% to 70%. Converting means a visitor who buys.
  • New customers have an industry average of 2% to 3%. The industry average is the typical number for this business.
  • So targeting past buyers is presented as the main source of repeat sales.

Repeat-purchase funnel procedure:

  1. Step 1, after delivery: Have the owner send a short thank-you video through Facebook. Then follow up with an email containing a coupon code, which is a discount code, for the next purchase. Target only first-time buyers by excluding customers with a purchase frequency above one.
  2. Step 2, 7 to 14 days after delivery: Run a cross-sell campaign, which means offering a different, related product, using dynamic product ads. Dynamic product ads are ads that automatically show products from your catalog. Set up pairings in the product catalog, which is your list of products in the ad platform, beforehand. For example, when a customer buys product X, show product Y. Then send an email that recalls the coupon code from step 1.

Cross-Sell and Upgrade Ads

  • Set up cross-sell pairings in the product catalog before launch, so that a purchase of product X triggers product Y. The pairing logic is the same as in the current funnel.
  • Example: customers who bought a product a year ago are shown an ad for a newer model. It is presented as an upgrade to the product they already like.
  • Some of them will buy again, so the offer is worth testing.

Channel Split: Acquisition vs. Retention

  • Use paid ads to acquire new customers, and use SMS (text messages) and email to retarget existing buyers with new products or better offers.
  • Retargeting existing customers avoids paying again to reach them on Facebook.
  • Use retention (keeping current customers) and reactivation (bringing back customers who stopped buying) messages separately from the acquisition campaign.

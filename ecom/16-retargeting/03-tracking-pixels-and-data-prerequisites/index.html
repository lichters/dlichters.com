Playbook › 16. Retargeting

Tracking, pixels and data prerequisites

Section 16.3 · 3 of 24 in this chapter · 4 min read

In short

  • Put tracking codes on every content page, and install each one only once.
  • Check that the codes really record visits.
  • Ad blockers hide part of your visitors, so your audiences are smaller than your clicks.
  • Most ad platforms need a minimum number of visitors before an audience can be used. Quick leavers can be kept out of it with a 30-second delay or a minimum visit length.

Prerequisites for Tracking and Data

Setting Up Pixels and Tags

  • A pixel is a tiny piece of code that records who visits a page. A tag is a small piece of code that sends visitor data to a tool.
  • Install retargeting pixels and tracking codes from Google Ads, Facebook and Google Analytics on all content pages of the site. Content pages are the pages visitors read. Retargeting means showing ads again to people who already visited your site.
  • Retargeting only works after tracking is set up on each ad network you plan to use. An ad network is an advertising platform, such as Google or Facebook.
  • Cookies are small files a browser saves to remember a visitor. The cookies placed on people who view pages or take actions on your site become the basis for your audiences. An audience is a saved group of people you show ads to.
  • The Google Ads retargeting pixel must be installed on the Shopify store before a website visitor audience can fill with visitors. Shopify is the software that runs your online store.
  • Without the pixel, the audience stays empty.
  • Add the remarketing tag through the Feeds for Google Shopping app before launch, so visitors are tracked from their first visit.
  • The remarketing campaigns themselves are set up after the store goes live. Going live means the store is open to real visitors.
  • Put a pixel on every store you own, so you can retarget visitors later on Facebook and Google.
  • You control the code of your own stores, so they can carry the pixel.
  • You cannot track visitors who come through affiliate links. An affiliate link is a link from a partner site that sends visitors to you.

Avoiding Duplicate Installs

  • Install each tag or pixel only once. Duplicate installs of the same tag make your tracking numbers wrong.
  • Installing the Google Shopping feed app's tracking events twice creates duplicate events with the same names in the tag list. An event is one recorded action, such as a purchase.
  • Choose the one labeled 'purchase in category'. Keep only one copy of each event in the account.

Verifying Firing

  • Firing means a tag sends its data when it should.
  • Pixels and tracking codes often exist in an existing store but are set up incorrectly or not firing. Verify that they register site visits.

Data Sharing Settings

  • Since 2023, GA4 remarketing audiences require data sharing settings to be enabled. GA4 is the newest version of Google Analytics, Google's visitor-tracking tool.
  • Without these settings, audiences show a 'not eligible' status. This means they cannot be used yet.
  • Enable the following settings:
    1. Granular location and device data
    2. Google insights
    3. Ads personalization
    4. Acknowledgement of user data collection

Privacy and Ad-Blocker Losses

  • A remarketing audience can show zero impressions even with daily clicks. An impression is one showing of an ad.
  • The cause is ad blockers (software that hides ads) and browser privacy (built-in limits on tracking). Together they stop tracking part of your visitors.
  • Estimate the trackable share first. For example, about 40 of 50 daily clicks may be trackable.
  • Visitors who block tracking in their browser are excluded from Google and Meta remarketing audiences. Meta is the company behind Facebook.
  • Repeat visits from the same person count only once.
  • Remarketing audiences built on advanced privacy blocking (stronger hiding of visitor data) still work. They use platform-owned data, such as link clicks, comments and shares. Platform-owned data is data the ad platform already holds.
  • Platforms with their own data, such as Google-owned YouTube, can still target viewers.

Traffic Minimums

  • Each advertising platform requires a minimum amount of traffic before a retargeting audience can be built and activated. Traffic means visitors to your site.
  • The minimum usually counts within a 30-day window, a set period of time.
  • A low-traffic store can keep its audiences below the 100-person minimum for weeks.
  • Check the audience size before changing bids or budgets. A bid is the price you offer for an ad click. A budget is the money you set aside for ads.
  • A new store with low traffic can still create audiences in Google Ads and Facebook ahead of time.
  • Such an audience stays inactive until enough visitors fill the pool, meaning the group of collected visitors.
  • Then it can be used straight away, and ads can run on it automatically.

Filtering Low-Quality Visits

  • Add a minimum session duration rule to each audience. A session is one visit, and the duration is how long it lasts.
  • Or delay pixel firing with a trigger rule in Google Tag Manager. Google Tag Manager is a tool that manages tracking codes in one place. A trigger rule tells a tag when to fire.
  • Set the delay to 30 seconds so bouncing visitors stay out of the cookie pool. Bouncing visitors leave a page right away.

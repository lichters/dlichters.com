Content-Based Retargeting

Section 16.15 · 15 of 24 in this chapter · 5 min read

In short

  • Content (articles, guides, videos and posts that teach or help) gives you something to show people who left without buying.
  • Build one content library and use it for both new visitors and retargeting.
  • Match each ad to the problem the visitor already had.
  • Show several pieces of content before any expensive offer.

Why content matters for retargeting

  • Retargeting means showing ads again to people who already visited your store.
  • A store with only product pages has little useful material for those visitors. Without content, good retargeting is very hard.
  • ** Visitors who have not bought yet often lack enough information to feel sure. Useful content can help them move past that doubt and back into the sales funnel (the steps a person takes from first visit to buying).
  • ** Content pieces on your website can also be the base for retargeting ads that reach people who already visited the site. Plan the content so it can be reused in those ads, not only in organic posts (unpaid posts on social media).

Building the content library

  • Develop content continuously and promote it to cold traffic (people who do not know your brand yet).
  • Use the same content to fill out remarketing plans (plans for showing ads to people who already engaged with you), so one content library serves both acquisition (finding new customers) and retargeting. **
  • Not all content must sell directly. ** Top-of-funnel content (broad content that first attracts a viewer) can hook them.
  • A follow-up piece on a more specific problem can then lead them to products. **
  • Base content on customer feedback, such as questions asked by customers or by virtual assistants (remote helpers who answer messages for a store).
  • Also base content on the hesitations customers mention. Turn these into emails and Facebook ads sent to the full list (everyone on your email list).
  • Every piece of content should work toward making buyers feel safer and trust the brand.

Matching content to pain points

  • ** Choose the content piece for a retargeting ad by matching its main problem to a pain point (a problem that bothers the person) the visitor already linked to the products or pages they viewed.
  • ** Answer that problem directly in the content.
  • The content should link naturally to relevant products, without a hard sell (pushy sales talk). **
  • Do not show generic content (content that is not specific) to specific product viewers. **
  • The content must be relevant to the exact product audience the viewer belongs to. Otherwise the ad does not answer their reason for not buying. **

Sequencing content before offers

  • Do not move people straight from a meme (a funny image or post) or an engagement post (a post built to get likes, comments or shares) to an expensive product offer. They do not know or trust the brand yet.
  • Show several pieces of content first, such as recipes, reviews and videos, before presenting any offer.

Content audiences

  • Create a separate retargeting audience (a saved group of people you can show ads to) for each content piece used in these ads.
  • ** Viewers who click that content enter the new audience.
  • They then move through a product sales funnel built around the products in that content.
  • A second audience for a product reminder ad (an ad that reminds people about a product) is people who read a content page that recommends the product.
  • Examples of such pages are a buyer's guide (a guide to choosing a product) or a best-of article (a list of top picks).
  • Retarget these readers with the ad whether or not they clicked through to the product page. **
  • Content-based reminders work because the reader has already received a recommendation. **
  • The reminder moves them to the next funnel step, which is looking at the product itself.
  • The ad is also simple to set up and keeps the product in front of the reader. **
  • The content-page audience uses the same 1 to 7 day window after the visit as the product-page audience. **
  • Ads are shown only within that window, so the product stays in mind while the reader decides.
  • Add visitors who arrive through a content piece to a retargeting audience. They can later be served retargeting ads that move them into the sales funnel. **

Social engagement audiences

  • People who engage (like, comment or click) with a Facebook post about a piece of content can be added to a retargeting audience. **
  • Those people can be shown retargeting ads later in the funnel. **
  • So a small content campaign (a short run of posts or ads on one topic) can feed the retargeting pool at low cost. **

Content viewer sequence

**

  1. Build the audience only from people who stayed on the content page for a minimum of about 30 seconds, ideally 1 to 2 minutes. They are the most worth retargeting. Include people who arrive at the content from a retargeting ad, as well as cold visitors.
  2. Days 1 to 7: show a Product Reminder Ad for the product that the content recommends. This is needed because the visitor may have read the content without clicking through to the product page. If they did visit the product page and did not buy, the reminder is still relevant.
  3. After that, run the same sequence as the product viewer sequence (the usual ad steps for people who viewed a product) for that specific product: Trusted Advisor Ad, Introduction Ad and Irresistible Deal Ad.
  4. After 30 or more days, show an Informant Ad (an ad that points to a different piece of content or a different angle on the problem that started the sequence).

Timing and re-engagement

  • Product viewers who have not bought after all other ads have run for 30 days can be re-engaged (won back) with content. **
  • Use content that approaches the topic differently from what they saw before. **

Advertorial retargeting logic

An advertorial is an ad that looks like an article.

  1. Identify visitors who reached the advertorial but did not click through to the offer page.
  2. Show them an ad that educates them further about the product and explains the benefits.
  3. Include a discount such as 40 percent off or 10 dollars off.
  4. Send the ad click directly to the offer page instead of the advertorial.

Site-wide and dynamic retargeting with content

  • Use a site-wide retargeting pixel (a small tracking code placed on every page) to remind visitors of your store.
  • Use article or content retargeting to drive readers of your content back to your website.
  • Dynamic multi-product ads (ads that automatically show several products) show several products from your store.
  • The best-converting products (the ones that most often lead to a sale) appear at the front.

Email capture from readers

  • A content visit does not need to produce a sale. **
  • An opt-in form (a sign-up box that collects email addresses) should be present on the content page to collect leads (people who give you their contact details).

Dynamic product ads on Meta

Section 16.10 · 10 of 24 in this chapter · 3 min read

In short

  • Retargeting shows ads again to people who already visited your store. Start with a 30-day window for most products.
  • Adjust the window to the price and the sales cycle. Test it, because longer buying decisions need longer windows.
  • Before you start, install the Facebook pixel and upload your product feed.
  • Send each product ad to its own product page, not your homepage.
  • Review your dynamic product ads every two weeks.

Retargeting Window by Product Price

  • Retargeting shows ads again to people who already visited your store. The retargeting window is how many days after a visit the ads keep running.
  • The retargeting window depends on the product price. Rule of thumb (a simple guideline):
Product price Retargeting window
Under $100 7 days
$100 to $500 14 days
$500 to $1,000 30 days
Over $1,000 Longer window
  • For most advertisers, 30 days is a suitable starting point.
  • Test the windows. The longer the sales cycle (the time from first look to purchase), the longer the retargeting window should be.

Setup Requirements

  • Dynamic retargeting shows people the exact products they viewed on your site.
  • It requires the Facebook pixel (a small tracking code on your site that records visits).
  • It also requires an uploaded product feed (a list of your products sent to Facebook).
  • It is the fourth campaign type to launch. A campaign is one ad project with its own goal and budget.

Format and Creative

  • The usual dynamic retargeting format is a carousel (a row of swipeable images in one ad). It shows the viewed product and related accessories.
  • The product and price are visible. People recognize what they looked at, which makes them more likely to click.
  • Dynamic product ads are the main Meta remarketing format. Meta is the company behind Facebook, Instagram and Messenger.
  • They show the products a visitor actually viewed on your store. Product images, descriptions and text fill in automatically for each visitor.
  • They run across Facebook, Instagram and Messenger.
  • Example: a jewelry store visitor views three specific rings or necklaces on the site, then leaves.
  • Later, on Instagram, the visitor sees those three exact products in an ad. A Shop now button leads back to them.

Improving Dynamic Product Ads

  1. Review the dynamic product ads every two weeks and ask what can be improved.
  2. Set the audience (the group of people your ad targets) duration by product price and sales cycle. Usually start at 30 days.
  3. Use dynamic text (text that changes to match each product) in the primary text. The primary text is the main message above the image. It should show the product name and price.
  4. Add a price overlay (the price written on top of the image) to make the price visible.
  5. If the audience is small, use link clicks as the optimization event instead of website conversions. The optimization event is the action Meta tries to get more of. Website conversions are completed actions on your site, such as sales.

Landing Pages

A landing page is the page a visitor sees after clicking an ad.

  • Mistake: sending paid traffic (visitors who came from paid ads) to the homepage loses buyers.
  • A multi-product ad shows several products in one ad. Each product in it should link to its own product sales page, where the customer can enter payment.
  • Do not send them to the store homepage.
  • Dynamic multi-product ads were a fairly new format, about six to twelve months old at the time.
  • Facebook's automatic ordering of products by best sales was being phased out. Do not rely on it to show your top seller first.

Cross-Device Behavior

Cross-device means across phones, laptops and desktops.

  • Facebook can recognize the same shopper across devices.
  • A visit on a work desktop can lead to a retargeting ad on a personal phone later.
  • Results are less predictable for B2B (business-to-business, selling to companies) products with longer sales cycles.
  • The reason: the ad may be seen at a different time and on a different device than the original visit.

Remarketing Workflow

Remarketing means the same as retargeting.

  1. A qualified visitor (a person likely to buy) arrives through a targeted search such as Google or Bing and views a product page.
  2. The visitor leaves the store without buying.
  3. Meta Ads Manager (Meta's tool for running ads) shows the visitor an ad for the product or products they looked at.
  4. The visitor clicks the ad and is sent back to the exact page they were viewing.

Combining with Other Ad Types

  • Older advice from 2019 is outdated. It said to use Facebook remarketing with a simple dynamic product ad group that retargets visitors who came from Google. An ad group is a set of ads that share one audience and budget.
  • Keep the daily budget (the amount you spend per day) small, such as $5 per day.

Retargeting campaign structure and setup

Section 16.11 · 11 of 24 in this chapter · 7 min read

In short

  • Use one campaign for retargeting where you set the budget per ad set (ABO). Use a CBO campaign (one budget for the whole campaign) only if it does better in your account.
  • On Google, set the remarketing daily budget to about 10% of your Shopping budget.
  • On Meta, pause the ad set, not the whole campaign. Pausing the campaign resets Meta's learning phase.
  • Do not use Performance Max for retargeting. Use standard display campaigns.
  • At the start, aim remarketing at reach (how many people see your ads), not at sales.

Campaign Architecture on Meta

1 min
  • Use one ABO campaign for retargeting. ABO (Ad Set Budget Optimization) means you set the budget on each ad set.
  • A CBO campaign (Campaign Budget Optimization, one budget for the whole campaign) can replace it if it performs better in the account.
Retargeting campaign structure:
1 ABO campaign (a CBO campaign can be used if it works better in the account).
Ad sets with MOF (middle of funnel) and BOF (bottom of funnel) audiences:
- 1 ad set for 75% video views and 95% video views.
- 1 ad set for website visitors.
- 1 ad set for FB/IG engagements.
  • The structure is one ABO campaign (CBO is an option if it works for the account) with ad sets for MOF and BOF audiences.
  • An ad set is one group of ads with its own audience and budget.
  • MOF (middle of funnel) means people who have shown interest. BOF (bottom of funnel) means people close to buying. The funnel is the path from first interest to purchase.
  • Build these ad sets:
    • Video views at 75% and 95% (combined)
    • Website visitors
    • FB/IG engagers (people who engaged with your posts or ads on Facebook or Instagram)

Campaign Architecture on Google

1 min
  • Build the Google remarketing campaign as a separate campaign from Shopping. Shopping is Google's product ads campaign type.
  • Its purpose is to bring back visitors who viewed the store but did not buy, show them the products they looked at, and win the sale against competitors.
  • Use standard display campaigns for remarketing and for monthly promotions to existing audiences. Display campaigns show banner ads on websites and apps. (2023)
  • Older advice: build each retargeting ad group around one audience or one message. An ad group is a set of ads that share one audience and settings.
  • Name the ad group after its target audience so the campaign list reads at a glance.
  • Older advice: the same Google display and Facebook setup used for retargeting also works for cold traffic (people who have never visited your store).
  • Only the targeting changes. Campaign structure, budgets and ad formats can be reused.

Step-by-Step Setup on Google Display

2 min
  1. Log in to Google Ads.
    • Click Campaigns in the left navigation (the menu on the left).
    • Click New Campaign.
    • Select "Create a campaign without guidance."
  2. Select Display as the campaign type and click Continue.
  3. Enter your store's homepage URL as the Website.
    • Name the campaign in this format: [YourStore].com Remarketing.
    • Choose the Country where you sell.
    • Set Language to English (or your store's primary language).
  4. Scroll to More settings.
    • Turn on Dynamic ads. Dynamic ads pull products from your product feed automatically.
    • Check "Use dynamic ads feed for personalized ads."
    • Select your Google Merchant Center product feed. Merchant Center is Google's tool that holds your product list. The feed is that list.
    • Leave Product filter on "No filters" (include all products).
  5. Under Content exclusions, exclude: Content not yet labeled, All sensitive content categories, and Live streaming videos. Content exclusions block your ads from showing in certain places.
    • Click Next.
  6. Set Daily budget to about 10% of your total Shopping Ads budget.
    • Example: $10/day Shopping = $2/day Remarketing.
    • Change Bidding focus to "Viewable impressions." Bidding is how you tell Google what to pay for.
    • Set Viewable CPM to $10. CPM means cost per 1,000 views. Viewable CPM counts only views where the ad was actually seen.
    • Click Next.
  7. Click "Edit Audience segments."
    • Select your [YourStore].com Visitors, 30 Days segment. A segment is a saved group of people, here visitors from the last 30 days.
    • Disable "Optimized Targeting" (critical; do not skip). Optimized Targeting lets Google widen your audience beyond the group you chose.
    • Click Next.
  8. Set Final URL to your homepage and Business name to your store name. Products are pulled automatically from Merchant Center. The Final URL is the page people land on after clicking. For the ad:
    • Headline: "Shop [Store Name]"
    • Long headline: "Come back and complete your order"
    • Description: "The official [Store Name] store"
    • Turn OFF auto-generated video (Google making a video from your images).
    • Turn OFF native formats (ads styled to blend into the site).
    • Add 1 landscape image and 1 square image.
  9. Review for errors and click "Publish campaign."
  10. Navigate to the new campaign.
    • Go to Audiences, keywords, and content → Audiences.
    • Scroll to Exclusions.
    • Add the [YourStore].com Purchase, 30 Days exclusion. An exclusion keeps a group of people from seeing the ads. This stops ads from showing to people who already bought.
    • Save changes.
  • In step 7, add the audience under targeting. Use the audience segments option to add the 30-day website visitor segment created earlier, and select it so it appears in the targeted audience list.

Static vs. Dynamic Setup

1 min
  • Static and dynamic remarketing differ only at campaign creation. (2024)
    • Static remarketing uses fixed banner ads.
    • Dynamic remarketing uses ads that pull products from your product feed.
    • For dynamic remarketing, link the product feed at the first setup step.
    • For static remarketing, skip the option to link the product feed.
  • A static Google Display Network banner for a specific offer can be set up as a Repeat audience campaign aimed at product viewers. The Google Display Network is Google's group of websites and apps where display ads show. A Repeat audience is a group of people who have visited before.
    • Use a Shop Now call to action (the button text).
    • Show the same deal stated in the banner as in the matching Facebook ad.

Bidding Choice

1 min
  • Google Display, as set up in the procedure, uses Bidding focus "Viewable impressions" with a Viewable CPM of $10.
  • In the early stage, the goal of remarketing is reach, not conversions. Reach means how many people see your ads. A conversion is a sale or another action you want.
  • Do not optimize remarketing campaigns for add-to-cart events. An add-to-cart event is when a visitor puts a product in their basket.
  • Focus on getting ads in front of people, especially when starting out. (2024)
  • Conversion bidding (paying for the action you want, such as a sale) often underperforms for retargeting. This is especially true at low volume (few visitors and sales).
  • Retargeting often aims to bring visitors back rather than force an immediate sale.
  • Click-based or traffic-based bidding is the more practical default for these campaigns. Click-based means you pay per click. Traffic-based means you aim for visits to your store.
  • A higher-ticket store (one with expensive products) may not reach the number of conversions a Facebook conversion campaign needs within its optimization window. The optimization window is the time Facebook uses to learn and get results.
  • In that case, a traffic campaign is the practical choice.
  • Test conversion campaigns once the store has shown sales and steady volume.
  • How the sources fit together:
    • The Viewable CPM setup applies to Google Display.
    • The click/traffic default and the conversion-later rule describe Facebook campaigns.
    • On Facebook, the store's volume decides between traffic and conversion bidding.

Budgets per Ad Set and Campaign

1 min
  • A retargeting ad set can run on a low budget of $10 to $15 per day each.
  • Adjust the remarketing ad budget per campaign rather than with one shared budget.
  • Each window should still reach an acceptable return on ad spend. Return on ad spend (ROAS) is the money you earn back for each dollar spent on ads. (2023)
  • On Google, set the Daily budget at about 10% of the Shopping Ads budget.
  • Example: $10/day Shopping = $2/day Remarketing.

Meta Learning Phase and Changes

1 min

The learning phase is the period when Meta tests who responds to your ads and finds the best people to show them to.

Procedure:

  1. When the campaign launches, Facebook shows the ad to a random sample of the audience to set costs.
  2. Once costs stabilize at the desired level, don't pause the campaign, because that resets the learning phase.
  3. If you modify a campaign, pause the ad set (not the campaign).
    • Unpause it after 5 days.
    • Let it run 1 day.
    • Pause again if needed.
  4. Budget changes over 20% also reset the learning phase.
    • Make large budget changes in small increments (small steps).
  5. If the campaign is paused for 5-7 days and restarted, the learning phase resets and costs will likely increase.

Pausing strategy (summary of steps 3 and 5):

  • When an established campaign (one already running) needs modification, pause the ad set, not the campaign.
  • Unpause within 5-7 days if needed. This preserves learning phase costs.
  • If the campaign itself is paused for 5-7 days and restarted, the learning phase resets and costs likely increase.

Performance Max Caveats

1 min
  • Performance Max (Google's automated campaign type that runs across all Google channels) is not recommended for remarketing.
  • It can work for a few months. But return on ad spend drops once remarketing audiences saturate, meaning the same people have seen your ads so often there are few new people left to show them to. (2025)
  • For remarketing and monthly promotions to existing audiences, use standard display campaigns instead. (2023)

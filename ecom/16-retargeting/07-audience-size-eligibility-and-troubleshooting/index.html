Audience size, eligibility and troubleshooting

Section 16.7 · 7 of 24 in this chapter · 7 min read

In short

  • Ads won't show to a group of visitors until the group is big enough. Each ad platform sets its own minimum.
  • If your group is too small, keep it set up. It fills as more visitors arrive.
  • If the group stays too small, make the time window longer, for example from 30 days to 60 days.
  • Check the size in Google Ads before expecting ads to show.

Minimum Audience Sizes per Platform

Ad platforms (companies that show ads, such as Google and Facebook) do not show ads to an audience (a saved group of website visitors) until it reaches a minimum size. Each platform sets its own minimum. The minimum depends on the platform, the ad type (the kind of ad you run) and where the audience came from. Check the number that applies to your setup.

Google Ads and the Display Network

The Display Network is Google's group of websites and apps where ads appear.

Threshold Source Applies to Ids
100 unique visitors within the time window (a unique visitor is one person, counted once, even after several visits) Source A Below this, ads won't show even if the audience is set up correctly
100 active users in the last 30 days Source A Display remarketing (showing ads again to past visitors); ads can run only at this level
100 people Source B Custom audiences on the Google Display Network
1,000 active members in a network Source A Remarketing audience; the source specifies the network
1,000 unique visitors Source A Audience error means the list is too small
  • The roughly 100-person minimum for a 7-day audience applies only to some Google Ads ad types, not to every Google option. Check the audience size for each ad type before you choose step-by-step sequencing.

Facebook (Meta)

Meta is the company that owns Facebook.

  • Facebook remarketing audiences need at least about 100 unique people before ads can show. Facebook counts fewer visitors than Google Analytics (Google's visitor-counting tool), so ads usually start reliably at around 150 to 200 visitors.
  • The roughly 1,000-person minimum for a 7-day Facebook audience is a general guideline, not an exact limit. Newer stores often reach it only with the all-product-viewers audience. Single-product audiences stay below it.
  • Facebook's minimum is considerably higher than Google's 100-person minimum for custom audiences.

General Rules for Small Audiences

  • Most networks set minimum audience sizes. If a group is too small, keep it set up anyway. It will fill as traffic grows. Start with the largest group that is still big enough, such as 14 days and up.
  • A retargeting audience of around 50 people who visited over two weeks is too small to run alone. Combine several related audiences, such as video viewers, engagers and page visitors, into one larger audience.

Lookback Windows

A lookback window is the number of past days a visitor must have been on your site to count in an audience.

Lengthening a Small Audience

Starting window Extended window Source Ids
30 days 60 days Source A
7 or 30 days 45 or 60 days Source A
14 days Up to 180 days Source A
  • If a remarketing audience is too small, lengthen its window so more people fall into it. A larger audience makes remarketing more effective.
  • If a remarketing audience is too small, lengthen its window, for example from 7 or 30 days to 45 or 60 days, so more people fall into the audience.
  • If a remarketing audience shows as Not Eligible (it cannot receive ads) because it is too small, expand the window from 30 days to 60 days. This reaches the size needed for ads to show.
  • Check both the audience size and the membership time frame in days for each remarketing audience. If the audience is too small or the window is too short, it will not fill with enough people to show ads.

Choosing the Starting Window

  • Create the audience with the longest lookback window, 540 days, and pre-fill it (fill it at creation) with the past 30 days of visitors. Google remarketing needs at least 1,000 active members in a network.
  • Use a 30-day audience for display remarketing. A 7-day audience usually has too few people to serve ads. A larger audience is needed before ads (called impressions, meaning each time an ad is shown) can start.
  • A new store's 7-day website visitor audience usually holds about 30 to 90 people. That is often below the 100 unique visitor minimum.
    1. Use a 30-day audience to start. A new store can usually build more than 100 unique visitors within 30 days.
    2. Create the 7-day audience anyway, so it builds in the background.

Checking Audience Size in Google Ads

Where to Look

  • To check whether remarketing audiences are big enough, go to Tools, then Shared Library, then Audience Manager. The Shared Library stores audiences you can use across campaigns. The Audience Manager is the list of them. Audiences that are too small show the message "too small to advertise to". Wait until they grow.
  • Menu names differ between sources. The 2019 path was Tools, then Audiences.

Checking Audience Sizes

  1. In Google Ads, open Shared library.
  2. Open Audiences.
  3. Click an audience. It shows the total number of people and how many are eligible (able to receive ads) on each Google platform.
  • An audience built from GA4 (Google Analytics 4, Google's current visitor-tracking tool) and linked to Google Ads can show a smaller eligible count than the total. Google can only match some visitors. This gap is normal and cannot be fixed.

Estimated Sizes in the Audience Form

  • The estimated audience size in the audience form is based on past data. It is not always accurate, and it changes over time. It becomes more reliable as tracking history (the record of past visits) builds up and data retention (how long Google keeps visitor data) is extended.
  • New audiences often show fewer than 10 users at first. This is normal. They grow as site traffic increases and the audience fills with data.

Not Eligible Status

  • A remarketing audience that shows as Not Eligible because it is too small is normal early in a store's life. Wait for more traffic and check the audience size before expecting impressions.
  • If remarketing ads do not show and there are no errors, the audience is most likely too small. Create a larger audience, such as 60-day website visitors, and add it to the remarketing campaign (a group of ads with the same settings).
  • Google can flag an audience as too small to target on the Display Network, for example a 30-day product viewer list. Check the audience status before launching. A flagged audience cannot be used for display targeting until it is large enough.

Audience Counts That Differ from Analytics

  • A gap between GA4 or Universal Analytics (Google's older visitor-counting tool) counts and the audience counts in Google and Meta is normal. iOS opt-out settings (Apple iPhone privacy settings that let people block tracking) add to the gap. Keep all tracking and data-sharing settings maximized to get the largest audience possible.
  • A retargeting audience includes all visitors to the website, not only traffic from Google Ads. Organic visitors (people who arrived without clicking an ad) therefore count toward audience size.

No Impressions and Identical Counts

First Checks

  • If the remarketing campaign shows no impressions, check that the audience size is enough for each platform under the audience tools. Then recreate the campaign. If it gets a few hundred impressions but no clicks over a month, raise the bids (the amount you pay for each click).
  • If Google remarketing campaigns serve no impressions and audience counts look identical, check the audiences' creation dates and settings against the process before recreating them. Recreating audiences or campaigns alone may not fix the issue.

When Display Remarketing Still Shows Zero Impressions

  1. Confirm the audience is a 30-day audience.
  2. Confirm there are enough visitors in the audience, such as the thousands of clicks seen over several months.
  3. If it still shows zero impressions, delete the remarketing campaign and recreate it from scratch.

Policy Flags

  • Google remarketing can run despite a policy-violation warning (a notice that ad content breaks the platform's rules). However, reach is limited until the flagged content is fixed.

Shrinking Audiences After Pauses

  • Remarketing audiences shrink when ads are paused. Restarting ads can therefore produce very few impressions. Recovery can take two to four weeks before remarketing becomes profitable again.

Fixing a Failing Pixel Setup

A pixel is a small piece of tracking code on your website. It sends visitor data to the ad platform.

Checking for an Empty Audience

  • If a remarketing audience is empty or shows zero, the pixel or code is not firing (sending data) and must be reinstalled.

Google Ads Pixel Procedure

  1. Repeat the setup steps exactly, one more time.
  2. Note the exact error message each time it appears.
  3. If the same error keeps appearing, contact Google Ads live chat (support in a chat window) and share the error message.

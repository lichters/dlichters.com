Playbook › 16. Retargeting

Channel selection and priorities

Section 16.21 · 21 of 24 in this chapter · 7 min read

In short

  • Run search ads first: Google Search, Google Shopping and Bing. People search there for the product.
  • Retargeting, meaning ads shown again to people who already visited your store, comes second. Start with Google.
  • Facebook and Instagram come after search is fully used. They find new people, so they are not the first choice.
  • TikTok comes last. Test it only after your Meta and Google retargeting budgets are fully spent.

Start with search, then retarget

1 min
  • Run search-based campaigns first. Search-based campaigns are ads that show when someone types a search. They come before retargeting.
  • Google Search, Google Shopping and Bing are the preferred sources of store visitors. Google Shopping shows product ads with a photo and price in Google results. Bing is a second search engine.
  • People actively look for the product on these platforms.
  • Start Facebook retargeting only after Google Search, Google Shopping and Bing are maxed out.
  • Facebook is a discovery platform, not a search platform. People find products there while scrolling, without searching. So Facebook is the next channel to add, not the first.
  • Cold Google campaigns target people who do not know your store yet and are searching for the product. Remarketing (the same as retargeting) runs on both Google and Meta.
  • This does not change based on whether your audience is more active on Google or Facebook.
  • Run retargeting on several networks with the same core messages. Using only one channel loses customers whose attention is split across platforms.
  • Aim for a subtle, always-present ad presence across relevant networks. Visitors then meet your ads wherever they go, without feeling followed.
  • Reach cold traffic this way. Retarget warm and hot audiences across the same networks, so competitors do not dominate attention. Warm and hot audiences are people who already know your brand.
  • Scale retargeting to your traffic volume.

Channel order

1 min
  1. Start with Google retargeting. It has the highest priority. Set up dynamic remarketing first. Dynamic remarketing shows visitors the exact products they looked at. Then set up standard remarketing, which shows general ads to past visitors. Then set up video/YouTube remarketing, which shows video ads to past visitors.
  2. Build these audiences before any other retargeting channel.
  3. Add Facebook, Instagram and Bing next. Use the same audience logic on each platform, not separate strategies.
  4. Add the next bottom-of-funnel campaigns. Bottom-of-funnel campaigns target people close to buying. Use website retargeting on the Google Display Network, which shows ads on partner websites and apps. Also use retargeting on Shopping. Both target people who have already visited the store.
  5. Keep YouTube low priority until other channels are fully used.
  6. Test TikTok last, and only after the Meta and Google remarketing budgets are fully used. TikTok is not a main revenue source. (2025)

Conflicting advice on Facebook dynamic remarketing versus Google remarketing. The 2021 advice gives Facebook dynamic remarketing ads the remarketing budget before Google remarketing or YouTube placement ads. It says Google remarketing is not worth the added complexity until monthly sales reach the high five or six figures. High five figures means tens of thousands. Six figures means hundreds of thousands. The undated Source A advice puts Google first, without a sales threshold.

Google retargeting

1 min
  • Google Ads offers several retargeting options. Facebook (Meta) is the second main retargeting platform.
  • Run retargeting natively inside Google Ads. Do not pay for third-party retargeting apps.

  • Remarketing ads bring back visitors from all traffic sources. This helps convert them into sales.

Meta: Facebook and Instagram

1 min
  • Meta owns Facebook and Instagram. Use Meta for remarketing to people who have already visited the store.
  • Use Facebook for remarketing, not for prospecting. Prospecting means finding new people who never visited. Remarketing is the part of the Facebook budget that works best.
  • Use Facebook ads to re-engage visitors after they leave the store. Most of the profit comes from this once Google brings in the first traffic.
  • Facebook retargeting showed a slightly higher return on ad spend than Google retargeting in 2020. Return on ad spend (ROAS) is the money earned for each dollar spent on ads. The gap was small. Run both rather than choosing one.
  • Remarketing is a high priority. Run it on both Google and Meta. A good audience can help conversion, meaning visitors who buy, and return on ad spend. This is especially true for high-ticket products, which are expensive items. (2023)
  • Use Google remarketing alongside Meta, because some visitors never use Facebook or Instagram. Audience Network is Meta's network of ads on partner websites and apps.
  • Give Facebook dynamic remarketing ads the remarketing budget first when monthly sales are below the high five or six figures. Google remarketing comes after.
  • Use carousel ads for the product in Facebook remarketing. Carousel ads show several swipeable images in one ad.
  • Remarketing to non-buyers runs through Google Ads, Facebook, Instagram and email. Each channel can restart the funnel from the store visit. A funnel is the path from first visit to purchase.

If your target audience has low Facebook usage, use retargeting campaigns on Google, Instagram or other advertising networks. Do not rely solely on Facebook.

YouTube and video

1 min
  • YouTube ads work well for remarketing. Target website visitors from the last seven days and exclude customers. Excluding customers means hiding the ads from people who already bought.
  • Video production is a barrier. YouTube is generally lower priority than Google Shopping.
  • A short video can remarket to visitors who viewed the store in the past seven days without buying. The video can be filmed as a selfie or recorded as a screen share. It works well on small budgets.
  • Keep YouTube ads low priority until other channels are fully used.
  • Video/YouTube remarketing comes third in the Google priority order. It comes after dynamic and standard remarketing.

TikTok

1 min
  • Test TikTok remarketing only after the Meta and Google remarketing budgets are fully used. It is not a main source of revenue. (2025)
  • A separate TikTok retargeting campaign is generally unnecessary. Prospecting already reaches people who saw the product.
  • Retarget only with a specific ad built for existing brand audiences, such as a discount-code offer.
  • Adding Google ads to Facebook and TikTok campaigns lets you retarget visitors from those channels who did not buy.
  • This recovers customers who would otherwise never return.

Bing

1 min
  • Retarget on Bing after Google. Use the same audience logic as on Facebook and Instagram.
  • Bing search campaigns belong with Google Search and Google Shopping. Max them out before Facebook retargeting starts.
  • Bing is a preferred search source of store visitors.

Pinterest

1 min
  • Pinterest remarketing can add some revenue if your target audience uses Pinterest.
  • It is unlikely to double a business on its own.

Running Google and Meta as one sequence

1 min
  1. A visitor clicks a Google Shopping product listing ad and visits the store.
  2. The visitor leaves without buying.
  3. The visitor sees Facebook retargeting ads for those products.
  4. Plan Google and Facebook campaigns together as one sequence, not as separate projects.
  • Use Google ads after TikTok or Facebook ads to reach people who visited the site but did not buy. Google reaches them across channels such as YouTube and Google. This creates an omnichannel presence, meaning you are present on many channels at once.

Tools not worth using

Third-party retargeting apps (outside tools that add retargeting) Do not pay for them. Run retargeting natively in Google Ads and Facebook Ads.

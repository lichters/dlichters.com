Brand foundation: vision, mission, values and the brand document

Section 8.5 · 5 of 24 in this chapter · 11 min read

In short

  • Start with two plans: your vision (the big goal far in the future) and your mission (why you exist and who you help today).
  • Know your customer on four points: who they are, what they value, what they do, and what they want.
  • Pick one clear reason you are different from other sellers, your USP, and use the same look, tone and words everywhere.
  • Fill in the whole short brand document before you write any ads or product pages.

Build order and layout of the minimum viable brand document

4 min

A "minimum viable brand document" is the smallest brand plan you need to start. Fill it in step by step.

  1. Define the business: write one sentence about what the business does. Then write a brand vision (a long-term, big, future goal) and a brand mission (why the brand exists, what it does, and who it serves).
  2. Identify the target audience (the group you sell to) using the four pillars (four main parts to describe them): demographics (age, gender, income), psychographics (values, interests, personality), behaviours (habits and how they buy), and goals, needs and motivations (what they want and why). Base this on the customer research from earlier work.
  3. Develop the unique selling proposition (USP: the one clear reason you are different from others).
  4. List the brand values (the core beliefs the brand stands for).
  5. Choose the brand name.
  6. Create the logo concept (the idea for your logo), the colour palette (your set of colours) and the typography (your fonts). Put them together in a brand kit (one place that holds all of them).
  7. Define the brand voice keywords (adjectives that describe how the brand talks) and how the brand should be perceived (how people should feel about it).
  8. Write the basic brand messaging: the tagline (a short slogan), the product descriptions, and the About Us section (the page that tells your story).
  9. (Optional) Build a mood board (a collage of pictures that shows the look and feel you want).

Steps to fill out the minimum viable brand document:

  1. Define the business: write the business goals, vision, mission and reason for existing.
  2. Identify the target audience: describe demographics, psychographics, and goals, needs and motivations.
  3. Develop the unique selling proposition: state what makes the brand different from competitors.
  4. Define the brand values: list what the company values publicly, and make them internal as well.
  5. Choose the brand name: pick a name that represents the niche (your small market), or a general name for a general store.
  6. Create the logo, color palette and typography: select the logo concept, colors and typeface that reinforce the brand.
  7. Establish the brand voice and tone: choose adjectives that describe how the brand is perceived.
  8. Write the brand messaging: define the tagline, copy style (the way your written words sound) and product description approach.
  9. Develop a mood board (optional): collect colors, images and graphics that represent the brand.

Vision and mission: the reason the brand exists

2 min
  • A brand needs a reason to exist. Two statements show it: the vision (the long-term future the company wants to help create) and the mission (why the business exists, what it does, who it serves, and what guides daily work).
  • The brand vision is a long-term, ambitious, future-looking statement. It is the driving force (the main push) of the company. The mission is shorter. It explains why the brand exists, what it does and who it serves, and it guides daily work. Keep the vision for the future and the mission for the present.
  • Vision statement formula (a fill-in pattern): To [lead or transform a specific field] by [ambitious, long-term action]. It works because it gives the company a direction that is ambitious and outlasts any single product. Example in this pattern: To make healthy eating the easiest choice for busy families. Second example: To turn every home into a place where people learn to repair what they own.
  • Vision and mission formula: the vision describes the long-term future state the company wants to help create. The mission uses the present tense to say why the business exists, what it does, who it serves and how. Example vision: A world where every kitchen is stocked with affordable, healthy food. Example mission: We sell affordable plant-based meal kits that help busy families eat well in under twenty minutes.
  • Mission statement formula: To [accelerate or deliver a specific outcome] by [what the brand actually does] for [who it serves]. It works because it links daily work to a concrete result and a named audience. Example in this pattern: To make outdoor gear affordable by selling durable designs direct to hikers. Second example: To help small cafés keep more profit by offering simple inventory tools for independent owners.
  • Write a mission statement that says what the company stands for beyond its product, for example the personal benefit the customer gets. Write it before producing content, because a clear mission makes every later content decision easier.
  • Older advice: in the vision, describe how well known the brand is, who knows it, what people think of it, and how many reviews it has. Also note whether people talk about the business without being asked.

Brand brief: answer before writing any copy

1 min

A brief is a short list of answers. Answer every question before you write any copy (the words for ads, pages and emails).

What is the product or niche?
What community does it serve? (describe who they are and what they want)
Is there a play on words that fits the product or brand?
What does the product do?
What are the benefits? (list each one as a short point)
What is the brand tone? (list three or more adjectives)
  • The play-on-words question (a pun, a joke using words) is optional. If no wordplay fits the product or brand name, leave it blank and move on to the benefits and tone questions.
  • Completed brief, golf swing trainer brand. Product: a golf swing trainer in the golf niche. Community: amateur men who want to improve their game and skills. Product function: the trainer leaves a mark where the golf club hits the floor, showing how accurate the swing was and where the golfer can improve their direction. Benefits: a better golf swing, taking strokes off their game, beating their friends, and becoming a better golfer. Brand tone: playful, manly and competitive. The brand copy and visuals should reflect these benefits and tone throughout.

Personality, values and voice

1 min
  • A product or store has a personality made of several traits (parts of its character). One trait usually sums it up best. For a luxury car, this is quality. Build headlines around that dominant (main) trait, and blend in other traits only to add variety, always linking them back to the dominant one.
  • A brand should behave like a consistent person. Its public image should match how it acts in private. It should have its own values, personality and tone (the way it sounds). Authentic (real and honest) brands make these two sides as close as possible.
  • Choose brand values on purpose, for example quality, empowerment (helping customers feel capable), customer focus, fast shipping or a great customer experience. Public values should also be lived inside the company, so the team actually follows them.
  • Brand values formula: choose four to five single-word values that reflect how the company acts, such as performance, honesty, innovation, responsibility. It works because short, consistent values can guide product choices, customer service and marketing alike. Example in this pattern: Our values are clarity, speed, reliability and care for customers. Second example: Our values are craft, transparency, comfort and fair pricing.
  • Older advice: core values can be any traits, such as honesty, compassion or having fun. A value is right when reading it makes the owner feel motivated to start the day.

Brand promise and unique selling proposition

1 min
  • The unique selling proposition (USP) is the specific way a brand differs from competitors (other sellers) who sell similar products. The brand promise is what the brand consistently delivers to customers, and that is what sets it apart from the rest.
  • Brand promise formula: state the concrete result you deliver, the feature that delivers it, and the frustration (annoyance) it replaces. Example: We deliver back support for eight-hour workdays through ergonomic seating (chairs shaped to protect the body) that suits a modern living room, instead of clinical office chairs. This works because a shopper can judge in seconds whether the store solves their specific problem.

Brand name and identity

1 min
  • Elements that build a clothing brand identity (the look that makes a brand easy to recognise):
    1. Choose a logo and brand name that represent the meaning of the brand.
    2. Define a photography style.
    3. Define a website style.
    4. Define a typography.
    5. Define the portrayal of a specific lifestyle or emotion the brand stands for.

Mood board and visual direction

1 min

  • Steps to create a mood board when starting from scratch:

    1. Collect colors, images and graphics that represent the brand, for example from Pinterest or mood board software.
    2. Place these items together in one document.
    3. Review the combined look to see how the brand could look and feel.
    4. Adjust the selection until the style is clear, for example vintage with muted colors.

  • A mood board collects images and graphics that show the look and feel the brand should have. It is optional, but it helps align the designer, the copywriter and the owner on one visual direction before creating the logo and content.

Retro and premium style direction

1 min
  • A retro (old-style) or vintage look can be described by combining classic, traditional style imagery with muted, low-saturation (soft, faded) color schemes. Collecting such images in a mood board before briefing a designer gives a clear visual direction. It is optional, and it makes starting from a blank page much easier.
  • Brand perception is chosen as a set of adjectives, for example luxury, premium, wholesome or witty. Every visual and written element should match that choice. Pick the perception you want visitors to form before you produce ads.
  • Brand perception is a single descriptive tone chosen on purpose, such as luxury, premium, wholesome or witty. Choosing one tone keeps product pages, ads and copy consistent, so visitors form the same impression at every touchpoint (every place they meet the brand). Example for a pet accessories store: wholesome suggests warm, family-focused language, while premium suggests refined photography and restrained wording. Example for a fitness apparel brand: witty suggests playful captions and light humor in ads.
  • Brand image should match the company's real story. A company positioned as a small underdog should look and feel small through its materials and a folksy (simple, homey) tone, because the image itself carries the concept.

Tagline and messaging

1 min
  • Tagline formula: use one to three words built around a strong action verb (a doing word) that signals determination and movement. It works because it is short enough to repeat across ads, packaging, emails and the about page. Example taglines in the same pattern for an outdoor gear store: Stretch further. Own the trail.
  • Tagline formula: a short phrase of a few words that is memorable and encourages the customer to act or feel determined. It works because short, action-oriented phrases stick in memory and reinforce the brand's promise. Example in this pattern: Start Faster for a productivity app. Second example: Go Further Today for an outdoor clothing brand.

Keeping the document up to date

1 min
  • Hold a brand workshop once per year (a team meeting that reviews the brand) as part of an annual company conference (e.g., UDI Conference). Include additional team connection activities, like lunch and learns (short talks over lunch), throughout the year.
  • Use brand workshops to reconnect the team with the company vision, review market trends and the content created that year, and check alignment to brand objectives.
  • Rolling out a brand to the store:
    1. Write a brand message and a tagline before switching the store to a brand.
    2. Stick to the same brand colors so customers recognize the brand easily.
    3. Do not change the brand look or direction too quickly; keep to the plan.

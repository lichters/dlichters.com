Social media accounts and profile setup

Section 8.16 · 16 of 24 in this chapter · 17 min read

In short

  • Set up Facebook and Instagram before you run paid ads. Visitors check a brand before they buy.
  • Use the same name, profile photo and short bio on every account.
  • Post about 2 to 3 times per week when the store is new.
  • Check Instagram's rules before using a VPN or a new phone number to make an account. Breaking them can get the account banned.
  • Make your social pages real and active before you run ads.
More key points
  • A real page gets likes and comments from about 10 percent of its followers.
  • Don't connect an inactive Instagram profile to ads. Post first.
  • Publish a few real posts on your Facebook page before you test.
This section has 10 parts. Tap one to open it.

Strategy and platform priority

2 min
  • Get your social accounts ready before you start paid traffic. Paid traffic means visitors who come from paid ads.
  • Visitors often check a brand before they buy. A complete social presence helps them trust you.
  • Business accounts (Facebook and Instagram business pages) take about 30 minutes to set up. Most suppliers need them before they approve you.
  • Social accounts mainly bring indirect sales. People who search for your store and find active social pages trust it more. **
  • Organic social means free posts, with no ads. With few followers, organic posts are rarely worth the effort. **
  • A basic paid campaign can build a few hundred likes. These likes act as social proof, which means signs that others trust the brand. **
  • Expensive products are usually bought after research. So do not pay for social management to grow a large free following.
  • Put your effort into Facebook and Instagram, your two main accounts. Do not spread effort across Twitter, Pinterest or YouTube. This is the 80-20 approach: spend most of your effort on the few places that matter most.
  • Facebook organic reach (how many people see free posts) has been low for years. Even people who like your page often do not see your posts.
  • Instagram gives more chance of virality (posts spreading fast). It suits experimental, meme-style content. Meme-style means funny, joke-based posts.
  • Meme-style content is an advanced step. You do not need it at launch.
  • Start with plain business updates, such as products, promotions and restocks (new stock arriving).
  • Instagram Reels are short vertical videos. A few strong Reels can gain a few hundred followers quickly for a new brand.
  • Do not rely on Reels alone for sales. In the owner's tests, Reels sold less than TikTok, and the audience may be more skeptical (doubtful).
  • Snapchat's audience covers a very wide age range, not only young users. It suits brands led by their founder, who want to connect personally with people.
  • Pinterest has its own section below.

Profile photo, name, username and bio (all platforms)

3 min

Profile photo

  • Use the same profile photo on every social platform. Users then recognise your account in every app.
  • The photo should clearly show the brand or person. It should be eye-catching and colourful.
  • Profile photos appear as a round circle called an avatar. If the photo shows a face, zoom in so the face fills the circle.
  • On a phone the photo is tiny. A far-away shot, an off-centre shot or a group photo is not recognisable.
  • A photo you cannot identify at a glance while scrolling is a red flag (a clear warning sign).
  • Use a clean, single-colour background with the subject clearly visible.
  • For a product or brand profile, use a high-quality product image, the product logo or the brand name. Do not use a selfie.
  • A profile video is optional.
  • Keep logos, colours and the profile photo the same on all pages. Then people who find the brand on one platform recognise it on another.

Name and username

  • The display name (the name shown on the profile, not the username) can be your store domain, such as brand.com. People who tag the account then see where to buy.
  • The Facebook page name should match your store name exactly. This includes the domain-style name you use. Customers then find the page when they search.
  • Use the store name as the Facebook page name. Do not use the full website URL.
  • The Instagram name should match the store name.
  • Usernames (the @name) should match the store name, for example shakasurfboards.
  • If the username is taken, try a close variation, for example shakasurfboard.

Bio: content

  • A bio is the short text about your account. It should include your main offer or where to shop.
  • Also include your location if the business has a physical location, your niche (the topic or market you serve), a short catchy tagline, and a contact email for brands or press.
  • Location strongly affects discoverability (how easily people find the account) when they search for profiles. Include it when it fits.
  • A bio that only says "follow me" is weak. It gives no information and pushes people off the platform.
  • Give people a reason to follow instead of asking them to.
  • The platform reads the bio to decide what the page is about and which viewers to show it to. This is the algorithm (the platform's rule for who sees what).
  • A bio that states your niche clearly helps the algorithm target your account. It helps the machine, not only the human reader.

Bio: formula and examples

  • Use this bio formula: niche, then location (if you have a physical base), then a short tagline that states the benefit, then a contact email for brand deals or press.
  • A relevant emoji makes the bio stand out when people scan it on a phone.
  • Example: Vegan skincare for sensitive skin | Berlin 🇩🇪 | Routines that take 3 minutes ✨ | Brands: collab@yourbrand.com
  • Second example, same pattern: Pet grooming tips for Labradors | Denver, CO 🐾 | New tip every Tuesday | Press: press@yourbrand.com

Facebook page setup

4 min

Overview

  • Quick path: log in, click the plus (+) symbol at the top, select Page, complete the setup wizard. The page is then ready.
  • Steps in order: create a Facebook business page. Upload the business information: profile photo, cover image and about-us section.
  • Then post 2 to 3 times per week on Facebook.
  • Create an Instagram business account. Upload its business information.
  • Then post 2 to 3 times per week on Instagram.

Create the page (other path)

  • Open the Facebook profile switcher. Choose See all profiles, then select Create Page.
  • On the Create a Page form, the Page name is required. Use the brand name.
  • The Category field is also required. Choose the category that best describes the business.
  • The Bio field is optional. You can fill it in later.

Create the page (full step list)

  1. Go to Facebook, click See more in the left menu, then click Pages.
  2. Click Create page.
  3. Choose Public page, then click Next.
  4. Click Get started.
  5. Enter the store name as the page name. Not the full URL.
  6. Choose a relevant category, for example Sporting goods store.
  7. Paste a short bio based on your About Us page. The bio field appears under the profile picture and header.
  8. Click Next. Enter the live store URL as the website.
  9. Enter the business phone number.
  10. Enter the store email address, for example sales@ followed by the store domain.
  11. Leave the address empty. It is not necessary.
  12. Set the hours to Always open, because the store is online only.
  13. Upload the logo as the profile picture. Upload the wide homepage header image as the cover photo.
  14. Crop the symbol out of the logo if the logo does not fit the profile picture. Place it in the middle.
  15. Click the action button, choose Learn more, enter the homepage URL as the link, then click Save.
  16. Skip the WhatsApp number. Skip inviting friends.
  17. Click Next. Turn on emails about updates to the page, then click Done.

Steps 1 to 17

Finish the page

  • After you create the page, upload the brand profile picture and the banner (cover photo).
  • Add a short store description and the website link.
  • The Links section of a new Facebook Page accepts up to 10 links, such as websites, blogs or portfolios.
  • Edit the page info through the sidebar option Edit Page Info.
  • Use the store's logo as the profile photo. Upload a cover image as the background.
  • Facebook image sizes change over time. Check the current recommended sizes on a current source before you upload.
  • On the page, the account switcher at the top right switches between your personal profile and your pages.
  • To post as the business page, switch to the page. Then type in the post box while acting as that page.

Page fields

  • Name: must match the store name.
  • Username.
  • Description: a short summary of the company and its mission statement (its purpose).
  • Phone: optional. Only add it if the business is live.
  • Best contact email address.
  • Best website.
  • Address: only if you have a physical address.
  • Skip local-business fields, such as service areas and opening hours, for an online store.
  • Privacy policy and product information are not needed at the beginning.
  • The bio should be a shorter version of your About Us page.
  • The bio must be short enough to show fully under the profile picture and header.

Page category (sources differ)

  • For an online store, select Product or service as the page category. Do not choose a local business type.
  • Local-business fields can then be left empty.
  • Example category in the setup walkthrough: Sporting goods store.
  • The category is not the most important setting. But it is shown on the page, so pick one that matches the business.
  • Facebook does not show every option in a complete list. AI can help you find a suitable category.

Prompts for the page bio and category

Bio prompt:

I am creating the bio for a Facebook page for [your store name]. Here is the URL of the live store: [your store URL]. Please read the store and help me write a bio for the page. Please help me write this, and keep the bio short enough to fit in the bio section of the page without being cut off.

Category prompt:

What category should I use on a Facebook page for my store that sells [your product or niche]? Please suggest the most relevant categories for the page and briefly explain why each one fits.

Facebook username

Steps while logged in as the page:

  1. Click the three dots on the page.
  2. Click Settings and privacy.
  3. Scroll down the left menu and click Page setup.
  4. Click the top link, Name.
  5. Click Edit next to the username.
  6. Enter the store name as the username, for example shakasurfboards.
  7. If the username is not free, try a close variation, for example shakasurfboard.
  8. Click Save. Enter your password if asked.
  9. Check the page URL. It should now be facebook.com/ followed by the username.

Path: Settings and privacy > Page setup > Name > Username > Edit > Save.

  • The username appears in the page URL, so it should match the store name.
  • A username can be taken if someone already used it. Then use a close variation.

Instagram account setup

2 min

Overview and fields

  • Quick path: go to instagram.com while logged out. Select Sign up, go through the signup, and choose the business account option.
  • Then upload your business info and post 2 to 3 times per week.
  • Profile fields: Name, Username, Category, About us (short bio) and Website (link).
  • Name should match the store name.
  • Edit these fields through Edit profile.
  • Profile category, as listed in the fields: Brand for a store.
  • Category chosen when you convert to a professional account: Shopping and Retail.
  • The Brand value is the profile category. Shopping and Retail is the category you select during conversion. Both are reported.
  • Instagram is mainly a mobile app. Most posts are published from a phone.
  • You can still post from a desktop. Use the create button, which accepts image uploads.
  • The website field can only be added in the mobile app, not on desktop.
  • Log in on a mobile device to enter the store website.

Create and convert the account

  1. Open instagram.com on a desktop, or use the mobile app.
  2. Click Create new account. Do not use your personal account. This is a brand-new account for the store.
  3. Enter the store's official sales email address. If you cannot access its verification email, use another email address that you can access.
  4. Enter a password and a date of birth.
  5. Enter the business name as the full name.
  6. Enter the store name as the username. If it is taken, add a variation.
  7. Click Submit. Enter the verification code sent to your email.
  8. Click More, then Settings.
  9. Scroll down the left menu and click Account type and tools.
  10. Click Switch to professional account and select Business.
  11. Click Next twice.
  12. Choose the category Shopping and Retail, then click Done.
  13. Confirm that you want to switch to a professional account.

Path: More > Settings > Account type and tools > Switch to professional account > Business > category Shopping and Retail > Done.

Profile photo and bio on Instagram

  • Use the same profile photo as on Facebook.
  • The Instagram bio must be even shorter than the Facebook bio.

Pinterest profile

2 min

Setup

  1. Set the username to the store name. For a general store, use the store name. If the store sells one product, use the product name.
  2. Replace the profile picture with the store logo or a product image.
  3. Write an About section of five to six lines describing the store. The suggested method is to use ChatGPT to draft it.
  4. Add a cover image, such as a store banner or a product-related image.

Treat the profile like a small website

  • Treat your Pinterest profile like a small website. Include the same types of content you would show in your store.
  • Every board and pin should link back to the store. A profile without links sends no traffic.
  • A board is a folder of pins. Each board works like its own niche category.
  • The board a visitor saves a pin to decides where that pin sits on your profile.
  • Organise boards by category, so each one fits a specific audience segment (a defined group of buyers).

Board types

  • Product boards cover products, new releases and best sellers. They are not enough on their own.
  • Storytelling or inspiration boards perform better. They show the result a customer wants and place the products inside that story.
  • Build a board around an outcome, such as better sleep. Fill it with relevant images and tips.
  • Place the product as one piece of that routine. It then becomes the means to the outcome, not just an item for sale.
  • Resource boards collect useful how-to and tip content about your products. The content can come from your blog or from other good sources.
  • Resource boards add value and build trust. Trust makes visitors more likely to buy.
  • Behind-the-scenes boards show real people, such as founders, the team or daily life behind the brand.
  • Behind-the-scenes boards build trust, because people prefer to buy from people.
  • A testimonial pin combines a customer quote with a photo of the customer. It is designed as an image.
  • Place testimonial pins on a dedicated testimonial board. Do it the same way testimonials appear on your website.

Reach

  • A single viral Pinterest pin can drive enough traffic to build a business. Some brands built most of their marketing on Pinterest.
  • Still, connect Pinterest to places your audience already visits. These include your site, email and other social networks.
  • Do not make Pinterest the main focus of your homepage.

Organic content ideas

2 min
  • Organic posts (free posts) for a small store follow a simple pattern.
  • The pattern: show a product, announce a seasonal or holiday promotion, announce a newly approved supplier, announce a product back in stock, or announce a product in a new colour.
  • No large content production is needed.
  • Example: a product photo with a short lead line for a winter sale.
  • Example: a post about a new supplier that was just approved.
  • Example: a restock notice for a bestseller.
  • Example: a note that a product now comes in a new colour.
  • Look at the Facebook pages of large dropshipping stores. Dropshipping means you sell and a supplier ships. Their pages give ideas for what to post.
  • Instagram feed: fill the normal feed with images, not Reels. Use lifestyle images, offers and product details.
  • Use Instagram Highlights (saved story groups) for social proof and offers.
  • Keep a Reviews highlight with supplier reviews.
  • Keep an Offers highlight that gets a new announcement each week.

Instagram setup procedure for high click-through rate

Click-through rate means the share of viewers who click. This is the goal of the steps below.

  1. Connect to a USA VPN before creating the account. A VPN is a tool that changes your apparent location. Keep it on at all times while using the account.
  2. Create a brand-new Instagram account. Use a unique phone number and email address that have not been used before.
  3. Convert the account to a business page.
  4. Write a clear offer in the bio. It should state the product or brand, the shipping promise, social proof, scarcity and a call to action that points to the link.
  5. Create two highlights. Reviews: post the supplier's reviews to build trust. Offers: post the new offer each week as an announcement.
  6. Fill the normal feed with images, not Reels. Use lifestyle images, offers and product details.

Reels

  • Before you run any Reels on a new Instagram page, the feed should already hold about 20 images.
  • After that, add 1 to 3 posts per week.

Engagement shows a real page

1 min
  • A page with many followers but very few likes and comments looks fake. "Engagement" means likes, comments and shares. A healthy page gets engagement on about 10 percent of its posts' reach. So 100,000 followers should bring roughly 6,000 to 10,000 likes and comments.
  • A new page with only 5 to 10 followers did not cause problems in practice. Ad viewers mainly decide based on the ad and the website. More followers only help later.

Accounts must be active before paid traffic

1 min
  • "Paid traffic" means visitors you pay for through ads.
  • Accounts must be active before paid traffic is launched.

Linking social accounts to ads

1 min
  • "Business Manager" is Meta's tool that holds your ad accounts and pages.
  • When launching test ads, do not connect an Instagram profile to Business Manager. Clicks from Instagram ads then go straight to the website instead of an inactive profile.
  • An Instagram profile that has not posted for a long time loses trust once it is linked to running ads. Keep it unlinked until you have a plan to grow a long-term audience.
  • Before testing, publish a few posts on the Facebook page so it looks legitimate.
  • Ad sign: enter the store name and upload the store logo in the ad sign field.

Two sources differ on linking. requires active accounts before paid traffic for a complete brand presence. and advise keeping an inactive Instagram profile unlinked during testing. Both apply: the first to a finished brand presence, the second to test campaigns on a profile that is not yet active.

More details

  • Buying a few thousand followers for an Instagram or Facebook page to build trust was reported to cost under $50, often around $10. This is a trust factor, not a sales driver, and it does not affect the ad account.

  • Build brand name search signals for a store name that resembles a well-known store: 1. Create profiles on Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest, YouTube, TikTok and Snapchat, even if you post little. 2. Write a 100-character description of the store with a link to the homepage on each profile. 3. Get listed in shopping directories with the store name as link text pointing to the homepage.

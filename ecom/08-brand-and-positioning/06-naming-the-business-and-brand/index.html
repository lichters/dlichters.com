Naming the business and brand

Section 8.6 · 6 of 24 in this chapter · 14 min read

In short

  • Keep the name to three words or fewer. Short and easy to say wins.
  • Test it: someone hears the name once and can spell it correctly.
  • Make the store name match the web address (domain) exactly, and check the address is free.
  • Search the name for trademarks before you buy anything. A trademark is legal protection for a name.
This section has 22 parts. Tap one to open it.

Core criteria for the store name

2 min
  • Keep the name to three words or fewer. Short names are easier to remember and better for your brand.
  • For a general store, aim for 5 to 10 letters that are easy to say and spell. Run the radio test: a listener should hear the name once and be able to spell it correctly.
  • Make the name short, memorable and easy to say. Do not use hyphens (the small dash between words). Including the niche (the small market you sell to) helps, but it is not required. **
  • Make the name short, memorable and easy to recall, so customers can find you again. Avoid random letters or numbers. Make the name sensible. **
  • Say the name out loud in normal talk. Imagine someone repeating it to a friend. A good name is one people naturally use when they talk about your brand.
  • Keep the name neutral or positive. Only avoid names that are clearly negative or offensive.
  • Your actions, service and behaviour shape how people see the brand far more than the name does.
  • Do not use a deliberate misspelling, such as "Kandi" instead of "Candy". Suppliers and customers see it as unprofessional. People who search from memory type the correct spelling and miss your store.
  • Spell every word the way the dictionary does. In a non-English market, use the correct spelling in the local language.
  • Choose a name that is clear and simple, not clever. It does not need a logical link to the products. Many successful companies have names unrelated to what they sell.
  • Avoid frivolous or overly unique names for a serious product. They damage how serious the product looks. Pick a name that suits how serious the purchase is.
  • Keep the store name neutral and not vague. **
  • Do not tie the store name to the owner's personal name. These stores are not meant to be built around one person's personality. **
  • Set a time limit for the naming decision. Overthinking delays the launch and has only a small effect on success. **

Sound, meaning and tone

1 min
  • If the brand should mean something, the sound matters. Sounds are the way a word is spoken (phonetics). Soft, rounded sounds suggest friendly, bubbly or feminine brands.
  • Sharp, hard sounds suggest aggressive, extreme or masculine brands. Pick the sounds on purpose to match the tone you want.
  • Most people link sounds to shapes in the same way. In a known test, people paired a round shape with the made-up word "bouba" and a spiky shape with "kiki".
  • Soft-sounding names feel round and gentle. Sharp-sounding names feel angular and intense.
  • Write down specific adjectives for the brand tone before you brainstorm.
  • Tone words such as playful, manly, competitive, moody, exciting, sweet, bubbly, feminine and aggressive each need different sounds and words.
  • Choose the name only after the niche and first product range are settled. Then the name matches the market you really serve. **
  • Avoid a word many competitors already use, such as "pro" in golf. Common niche words make you less distinct.
  • Check how often a candidate word appears in your niche's product names.
  • Check that the name has neutral or positive meaning in major languages.
  • A name that describes a benefit or tells a short story can be part of the advertising itself. Names that tell a story are usually shown prominently.
  • Names that describe a benefit or product in a memorable way can be shown prominently.

Brandable names and word construction

2 min

A brandable name (a name made to build a brand, not just to describe a product) is easy to grow with.

  • Make a brand name from two simple words: one for the product, one for what makes it different. This is easy to remember and can grow into a family of products.
  • Keep product formulas simple, for example 4 to 5 ingredients each. Customers can then mix several products into one routine.
  • Store names work best when they include the niche keyword (the word people type into Google) plus a brandable word.
  • The name must be free to use. Descriptive words about the product, without the keyword, can also work.
  • A good brandable word can be a term people in the niche already know, such as a local gesture or slang word.
  • This makes the name feel real while staying short, unique and easy to remember.
  • Use a brandable name and domain, not an exact-match name. An exact-match name copies the product keyword. **
  • Exact-match web addresses are the outdated approach. **
  • Brandable names are easy to remember. They do not lock you to one product, so you can widen the niche later without renaming. **
  • A name that shows the product's main benefit, such as speed, victory or care, works well. Customers link the name to the product without extra explanation.
  • Example pattern: "swift" for a fast-delivery product, or "haven" for a home-comfort product.
  • Names that only describe an ingredient or category invite copying. Rivals can use the same name. This makes it hard to hold your price.
  • Avoid names competitors can claim as their own.
  • A coined name (a made-up word with no meaning yet) is exclusive and can be protected.
  • A coined name needs long, steady advertising before it carries meaning. Most coined names never get there, so choose them with care and budget.
  • A pioneer (the first company in a new product type) should give the product an exclusive name.
  • That name can become the common word for the category, so rivals cannot use it. A generic brand (a name anyone could use) lets rivals take the demand.
  • A name tied to a patent (a legal right to sell an invention alone for some years) may become common property when the patent ends.
  • Plan names and brand protection separately from patent protection.
  • When a product must be sold under a common name, add a person's name as a second identifier.
  • A named creator shows pride and authority. It gives a generic product its own identity.

Generic versus niche-signaling names

1 min

A generic name is not tied to one product type. A niche-signaling name shows the market it serves.

Generic names (not tied to one category)

1 min
  • Brand names for dropshipping must be generic. Dropshipping means you sell products that a supplier ships to the customer, so you keep no stock.
  • Do not tie the name to one product category. A pet store called "Paw Paw Friend" blocks growth into other categories.
  • A generic, catchy brand name with a simple font logo is a flexible start. You can use it for any product.
  • Generic brand names are easier to come up with. They are a valid choice.
  • Choose a flexible name that is not tied to one product category. A category name limits your store once you add more products.

Niche-signaling names

1 min
  • The business name should show the market it serves. A buyer who reads it should see right away that the business is about their interest. **
  • Pick a name tied to the market's category, not a single item. **
  • Niche-relevant names make it clear right away which market the brand belongs to.
  • A name built on a product description, such as "golf swing trainer", suits niche or product-led stores. The name itself explains what you sell.
  • A niche-specific name and logo must be replaced when the product changes.

When the sources differ

1 min
  • Generic is the rule for a store with many categories.
  • For a niche or single-product store, a niche name is fine.
  • Replace the niche name if the product changes. It fits best when the product description is the main selling point.
  • Both generic and niche names can work with either a generic or a niche store.
  • The store name should reflect the niche. It does not need to name every product category. **
  • Buyers still click and buy a product they searched for. **

Structure of market names

1 min
  • Formula: [local marker, such as a country nickname] + [market keyword] + optional [broad category word]. **
  • The local marker shows a national or local business, which many buyers prefer. The market keyword tells the target customer what the store is about. **
  • Examples: "Nordic Pet Supply Co." for a Nordic pet market, and "Texas Grill Depot" for Texas grilling products. ("Co." means company.) **
  • Country names, mountain ranges and region names make generic brand names with no product link. That lets you expand into any category.
  • Examples: Tatran (Tatra Mountains), Karpatia (Carpathian Mountains), Orava (region), Danubia (Danube River), Liptov, Spis.
  • Any country works as inspiration. It can be a big nation, a small one, a region or a geographic feature.
  • Names from Slovenia, Peru or Estonia work as well as names from Slovakia for multi-category stores.
  • A name built on one product, such as Logsplitters.com, makes little sense once you add snowblowers or generators. **
  • A name built on the market word, such as Power Pros or Power Superstore, works across the whole range. **
  • A name built on one product type, such as surfboards, locks the store into that product. New categories then feel unnatural. **
  • A broader market name, such as one that implies water sports, leaves room to grow. **
  • A single-product name can work if you must. It is not ideal. **

Case: Oz Off-Grid

1 min
  • An Australian store sold off-grid living products. Off-grid means living without public power or water connections.
  • The store's approved niche covered solar energy, waste disposal, water treatment and off-grid food alternatives.
  • The owner considered several names and chose Oz Off-Grid.
  • Off-Grid names the market. Oz signals an Australian business, which local buyers often prefer.
  • The alternative, Off Grid Warehouse, was also considered.
  • The domain osoffgrid.com.au was already owned, so the business name and domain matched exactly.
  • The store was planned to serve the Australian market first. The name leaves room for many more product categories.

Generating names with AI

1 min
  • Use ChatGPT or Gemini (AI chat tools from OpenAI and Google) to brainstorm brand names for dropshipping stores.
  • An AI assistant such as ChatGPT can help create unique brand and product names when naming is hard.

Basic prompt

1 min 
Give me 10 brand names for my branded drop shipping store

For a different quantity or geographic inspiration:
Give me 15 brand names similar to [country name]

Procedure: basic brainstorming

1 min
  1. Open ChatGPT or Gemini.
  2. Submit a prompt asking for brand names. You can add a number and a country for geographic ideas.
  3. Keep testing with different settings to get more options.
  4. Select and use your chosen brand name.

Deep research prompt (system role)

1 min

A system role is the hidden instruction that tells the AI who it is.

You work as a senior brand namer and strategist.

Deep research procedure (AI research model)

1 min

Deep research is an AI mode that searches and writes a long report.

  1. Open a ChatGPT thinking model (a version that reasons longer) with deep research switched on.
  2. Paste the brand name prompt. Fill in the product description and the product list.
  3. Run it. Then review the 12-name longlist (a long list of names), the 4 positioning routes (4 ways to place the brand in customers' minds) and the Top 3 shortlist (the 3 best picks).
  4. Search each candidate's.com in a registrar's domain search. A registrar is a company that sells web addresses. GoDaddy was used. Confirm the address is available.
  5. Add the chosen domain to the cart to confirm it is still free.
  6. Search the name on Trademarkia (a website for searching trademarks) and on Google for conflicts or unwanted meanings.
  7. Choose the brand name and its matching.com domain.

Input and output rules for AI research

1 min
  • Deep research often asks what exactly you sell. Put the actual product list into the prompt, not a general category.
  • Add a short description of the quality and the feeling the customer should get. The names are built from those benefits.
  • Ask for two groups in one run: about six premium single-word names.
  • Also ask for about six names that are likely free. Examples are two-word combinations of a brand word and a descriptive word.
  • This gives you aspirational options and realistic backups in one list.
  • If the positioning routes all sound alike, the prompt is too vague.
  • Ask again for bolder positioning and a more specific target audience.
  • For a broad niche store, the first name and positioning are temporary.
  • Launch a basic store and test products. Sharpen the positioning later, based on what sells and what the ads say.

Framework for a meaningful name

1 min
  1. Define the niche, since it can shape the name.
  2. Identify the community the product serves and what that audience likes to do.
  3. Look for puns or wordplay (word jokes) that fit those interests.
  4. Describe what the product does and how it helps people.
  5. List the product's benefits. Pull some of them into the name.
  6. Define the brand tone or values, for example moody or exciting. This strongly affects the name.

Worked example for a golf swing aid:

  • The product is a swing trainer in the golf niche.
  • The community is amateur men who want to improve their game.
  • The product leaves a mark on a pad. The mark shows swing accuracy and direction.
  • The benefits are a better swing, fewer strokes per hole and beating friends.
  • The desired brand tone was playful, manly and competitive.

Product-specific name pattern

1 min
  • Combine a word for the core benefit (such as "pro", "swing" or "precision") with the product type word ("trainer", "pad", "aid", "mate").
  • Optionally add a word that signals the tone you want.
  • Examples: "Putt Rhythm Pro" for a putting-rhythm trainer, and "Iron Line Coach" for an iron-shot alignment guide.
  • Swap the product word or reorder the words. This quickly produces a large list of names.

Iterating on AI output

1 min
  1. Review the list. Mark the names you like or dislike.
  2. Tell the AI what you dislike about the rejected names.
  3. Ask for another batch based on that feedback.
  4. Repeat until the list is big enough.
  • Do not accept the first suggestion.
  • Pick the final name yourself from the AI's list. Choose the most memorable one that fits the niche best. Check the others first.

Checks before you commit

2 min

Domain match

  • The business name must match the domain exactly. A domain is your web address. **
  • Customers remember the name and type it in with an ending such as.com,.com.au or another country ending. **
  • If the matching domain is taken, choose a different name. **
  • Brandable names beat keyword-copy domains. **
  • A matching domain should be brandable, not an exact keyword match. **
  • In high-ticket dropshipping (selling expensive products you do not keep in stock), buyers visit the site several times before buying. **
  • The first visit often comes from an ad. Later visits usually come from typing the name you remember. **
  • So the name must be easy to recall and type. **

Competitor similarity

  • The name must be fairly unique in your market. **
  • Search your product keywords on Google and Google Shopping (Google's product results page). Review the seller names in the results. **
  • Avoid names or web addresses that are close copies of a competitor's. **
  • Avoid names that look like or closely copy a competitor's name. Rivals can use the same name and make it hard to hold price.
  • A store name that matches an unrelated local business, such as a gym with the same name, is not a major concern. **
  • Clarity and memorability matter more. **

Trademark and languages

  • A quick trademark check should be part of the naming step.
  • Search the name on Trademarkia and on Google for conflicts or unwanted meanings.
  • Choose the name carefully. It appears on the store, invoices (bills), ads and domain.
  • Check domain availability and possible trademark conflicts before launch.
  • A trademark-style name is a unique invented name combined with a product word.
  • It gives a unique domain for your Shopify store (online store software) and blocks rivals. This is called ring-fencing competition.
  • Searches for your brand face less competition than searches for the generic product name.
  • Add the TM symbol (a small mark showing the name is claimed as a trademark) to the end of such a name. It is trademarked.

Multi-store and second-store naming

1 min
  • Several websites selling the same products in the same niche split the effort. **
  • Put all products under one brand and one domain. **
  • In the example given, one site with the same total work builds about ten times the authority (how much Google trusts a site) compared to ten separate sites. **
  • When one owner runs several related services or product lines, one brand usually gets a higher response rate (how often people reply or buy). **
  • Customers see one clear company. Separate brands are an option. **
  • One brand is easier to run and tends to convert better (turn more visitors into buyers). **
  • A second store in a related niche can be named after its category, with a "plus" ending. **
  • Example: a dining room furniture store paired with a home lighting store. **
  • Both stores can share one theme. Only the logo and colour scheme change. **

Test stores that become brands

  1. Start with a general, catchy brand name on one store to test many products.
  2. When a product proves to be a winner, keep the same website. Change the domain, name and logo to build a real brand.

Company and LLC names

1 min

An LLC (limited liability company) is a common US company type.

  • Choose the company name carefully. It appears on the store, invoices, ads and domain.
  • A company name does not have to match the website name. **
  • Many businesses run one or more stores under a different name. That is no problem for approvals or operations. **
  • Choose a generic company name for the LLC. **
  • Then several related stores can share it later. Do not name the company after one store or domain. **
  • Avoid the word "consulting" in a company name chosen from the start. It does not help the business. **
  • If an existing company already has it, keeping the name is generally harmless. **
  • Create one legal business entity (a separate legal company) for each group of related businesses. **
  • For example, one for fitness equipment and one for office furniture. **
  • This keeps accounting (money records) separate and makes selling a group easier. **

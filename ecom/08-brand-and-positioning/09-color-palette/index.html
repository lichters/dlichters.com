Playbook › 8. Brand and positioning

Color palette

Section 8.9 · 9 of 24 in this chapter · 14 min read

In short

  • Use 2 to 4 brand colors, and use the same ones everywhere on the store.
  • Keep most of the store white, black, or gray. Use one bright accent color only to highlight products and buttons.
  • Copy each color as an exact hex code (a six-character color code, such as #540863), and paste that same code into every tool.
  • Make your button color clearly different from the background, so the button that asks visitors to act stands out.

Why a tight palette builds trust

1 min
  • Your global theme colors (the main color settings for the whole site) control buttons, text, backgrounds, and UI (user interface, the parts visitors see and click) across the entire site.
  • A tight palette (a small set of colors that work well together) uses 2 to 4 brand colors.
  • Use those colors the same way everywhere. The store then looks professionally designed.
  • Suppliers find that store trustworthy.
  • Mixed or inconsistent color use makes a store look untrustworthy.
  • Going over the color limit creates visual confusion.
  • Visual confusion lowers how trustworthy the store looks to people.
  • Many colors and fonts (letter styles) quickly make a store look messy.
  • Reuse the same 2 to 4 brand colors everywhere.
  • a multicolor website that looks like a rainbow looks unprofessional.
  • It confuses visitors who cannot focus on key information.
  • Limit yourself to three carefully chosen colors plus white.
  • for startup brands (brand-new businesses), design uniqueness matters more than design quality.
  • Your logo and colors must visually set you apart from competitors (other businesses in your market). Do not copy their approach.
  • Before creating a brand, research the branding of your niche (the small, focused market you sell in).
  • Check the colors, hex codes (six-character color codes, such as #540863), fonts, and font sizes that large brands in the same niche use.
  • Those big brands have already tested these choices.

Choosing the number of colors

1 min

Sources give different numbers of colors. Use the count that matches your situation. Three words are used below: primary (the main color), accent (an extra color used sparingly to highlight things), and contrast (a clear difference between two colors).

Situation Color count Source
Quick product test Black, white, and one brand color, such as the product color
Standard store Maximum of three to four colors: black, white, and one brand color (recommended palette)
Standard store, strict limit At most four colors: white, black, and one brand color or brand color palette
Brand identity, main design Three colors plus white
Main colors plus supporting colors Three main colors, with two or three supporting colors used sparingly
Brand palette reused across the store Two to four brand colors
Brand colors with contrast button Three to four colors: one primary, one contrasting button color, and one minor accent
  • Keep the total to no more than four colors when you ask for a palette.
  • Use one dominant (the main one, taking the most space) neutral color (black, white, or gray).
  • Add occasional bright accent colors to highlight products.
  • Use only one bright accent color (a strong color that draws the eye).
  • Reserve it for highlighting products or calls to action (buttons or links that ask people to act).
  • Keep the base (the main background colors) mostly neutral, such as black and white.
  • Keep one clearly separate brand color, so people recognize the brand right away.
  • Do not use many accent colors across the site.
  • Otherwise, the brand color stops standing out.
  • Colors matter less before the store sells.
  • At this stage, the goal is a reasonable-looking site, not perfection.

Choosing colors from emotion and market

1 min
  • Choose colors by considering what they mean to the brand, the customer, and the niche.
  • Color carries emotion and signals, meaning what a color tells people.
  • Color choices should match the mood the brand wants to create.
  • In color psychology (the study of how colors affect feelings), purple represents luxury, royalty, rest, and sleep.
  • Purple reads as feminine but more mature than pink.
  • For brands that stress comfort and trust, pair purple with warm, grounded, calm accent colors.
  • Avoid high-contrast or vibrant colors (very strong, bright colors) with purple.
  • Choose the three brand colors to match what your target market (the customers you aim at) expects and feels.
  • Water-based products benefit from blues and greens.
  • Luxury goods work with blacks, golds, and silvers.
  • Color selection should reflect the market you sell in.
  • For water sports, use oceanic (ocean-like) blues and greens, with a contrasting button color like red.
  • For luxury goods, use sophisticated blacks, golds, and silvers.
  • This creates emotional alignment (colors that feel right to customers) between your visual identity (how the brand looks) and customer expectations.
  • Older example (2021-22): Oz Off Grid uses three colors: green (primary), burnt orange (accent), and yellow.
  • They were chosen because they represent nature, survival, and off-grid living (living without public power or utilities).
  • The tagline Prepare. Survive. Prosper reinforces the target market of survivalists and environmentally conscious customers.
  • Mute a strong red (make it softer) by shifting it slightly toward pink.
  • Or mute it by blending it into a softer tone. This makes it less aggressive on a page.
  • A gradient (a color that fades smoothly into another) that moves into a softer shade of the same red has the same effect.
  • Take the accent color from the product photo to keep the store palette consistent.
  • A very saturated accent (a very bright, strong color) often reads better on web pages.
  • Reduce its intensity (strength) and move it slightly toward gray.

Creating a palette with tools and AI prompts

1 min

Procedure: selecting a brand color palette with ChatGPT (an AI chat tool) and Color Hunt (a website of ready-made color palettes)

1 min
  1. Use ChatGPT to get color recommendations based on brand emotion and psychology.
  2. Visit Color Hunt and search for palettes that match those color names or emotions.
  3. Scroll through options to find a palette that resonates.
  4. Hover over the chosen palette (move your mouse over it) to reveal all hex codes.
  5. Copy the hex codes for immediate use in design and website tools.

AI prompts

1 min

A prompt is the text you type into an AI tool. Words in [square brackets] are placeholders (spots to replace with your own words).

Use ChatGPT as a color psychologist (an expert in how colors affect feelings) and brand strategist (a person who plans how a brand looks and speaks):

As a color psychologist and brand strategist, what primary, secondary, and accent colors would best represent a brand called [name] that should feel [emotional goal, e.g., cozy, trustworthy, luxurious]?
  • ChatGPT returns color names with reasoning for each choice.
Suggest a color combination for an online [NICHE] store. Give me a palette of three colors (main color, accent color and background color), each with its HEX code, and describe the mood each color creates for the customer. Keep the palette consistent with the [NICHE] audience and the brand tone [TONE, e.g. playful or premium].
Please provide a color combination or color palette for my [NICHE] store. Include one main brand color, a lighter tint and a darker, bolder shade of that color for call-to-action buttons, plus white and black as base colors. Keep the total to no more than four colors and explain briefly where each color should be used.
Please provide me a color combination or color palette for my [niche] store. Give me one main brand color with a lighter and a darker variation, plus white and black as base colors. Explain which shade should be used for the call-to-action button and why.

Hex, RGB, and exact codes

1 min
  • Hex codes (six-character color codes, such as #540863 or #FFD3D5) ensure exact color consistency across all brand materials.
  • Brand materials include the website, print, video, social media, and design work.
  • The same hex code always produces the same color in Shopify (online store software), Kittl (a design tool), web browsers, and print services (companies that print on paper).
  • Hex codes are the universal format (one standard way) to name online colors.
  • A hex code is a hashtag symbol (#) followed by six letters or numbers.
  • It gives the exact same color in any design software or website builder.
  • The example #0DJ34K contains letters outside the hex range.
  • Valid hex digits are only 0 to 9 and A to F.
  • Color codes are six characters long.
  • They mix digits and letters in hex format.
  • Enter the exact code from the brand file (the saved record of your logo and brand colors).
  • This makes the theme match the logo and images.
  • Hex codes and RGB values describe the same color in two notations (two ways to write one color).
  • RGB means three numbers from 0 to 255 for red, green, and blue.
  • List both for each palette color.
  • This lets the color be set in the store and reused consistently in creatives (images and ads you make).
  • Store the brand's hex color codes in a shared document.
  • This way, anyone creating social media graphics uses the correct colors.
  • Use the exact hex codes from the design tool that created your logo and images.
  • This makes the store colors match the brand.

Roles of colors

1 min
  • your brand identity (how the brand looks) uses a primary color for main design elements.
  • Use an accent color for subtle highlights.
  • Reserve a contrasting button color for clickable elements.
  • Clickable elements must stand out from the rest of the page.
  • choose one primary color used most often.
  • Choose one contrasting color for buttons.
  • Choose a third color for minor accents.
  • The palette needs a dark color and a light color that work with white.
  • This keeps the whole store from ending up in one color.
  • The darker colors are used for buttons and contrast.
  • keep most of the website background white.
  • Use color only on small elements, such as the menu, the footer (the bottom strip of a page) background, buttons, and link hover states (how a link looks when the mouse moves over it).
  • Do not use color on the whole page.
  • Set the color scheme (your full set of colors) before designing the site.
  • Brand colors are part of the palette you apply to headings, backgrounds, and buttons.

Contrast and button colors

1 min
  • Complementary colors sit opposite each other on the color wheel (a circle that shows how colors relate).
  • They create strong visual contrast.
  • Use this principle to select your button color.
  • Blue contrasts with orange, and purple with green.
  • Use a darker, bolder shade of the brand color for the call-to-action button so it stands out.
  • Use a contrasting color, such as orange, for buttons so the call to action stands out.
  • Keep the main text dark, such as black, so it stays easy to read.
  • On a dark section, build text hierarchy (the order of importance in text) with tint (a lighter version of a color).
  • The title should have the strongest contrast, the subtitle slightly less, and the body text (the main paragraphs) the least prominence while staying readable.
  • This keeps the title as the first thing the eye reads.
  • Dark backgrounds with light text can look jarring (harsh and clashing) in a layout (how things are placed on the page).
  • Check colors together in a realistic website layout to avoid this problem.
  • Test the palette on a sample website layout before finalizing it.
  • A very dark brand color, such as deep purple, can look almost identical to black.
  • Check before finalizing. Place the color version next to the black version and confirm the difference is visible.
  • If it is not visible, the color variant adds little.

White space (the empty space around content)

1 min
  • white space is your most important color on a website.
  • It allows the human eye to process information clearly.
  • Solid color blocks everywhere make pages hard to read.
  • They cause visitors to disengage (stop paying attention) and look elsewhere.
  • Keep most of the website background white to create white space and make the site easy to read.

Applying exact codes in the theme

1 min
  • Global theme colors control buttons, text, backgrounds, and UI across the entire site.
  • Use the exact hex codes from the brand file.
  • Copy the codes from the design tool used for the logo, favicon variants (the small icons shown in browser tabs), color-matched images, and banner visuals into the theme colors.
  • Theme settings > Colors: set the solid button label (the text on a button) to white.
  • Accent 1 is the primary brand color (pink in the example).
  • Accent 2 is the secondary color (teal in the example).
  • Gradients are not used.
  • Theme settings > Colors > Secondary: text black.
  • Set an outline button (a button with only a border line, no fill) in the brand blue.
  • Background: a light gray, slightly lighter than the default.
  • The price for you save red stays at default and appears on product pages later.
  • Header navigation menu (the top menu bar of the site) colors in the example: background black.
  • First level link (a top-level menu link) white.
  • Sub menu (the drop-down menu) background white.
  • Sub menu link the brand blue.
  • Sub menu border the brand pink.
  • Active link color in main menu white. An active link is the link for the page you are on.
  • The same brand colors are reused across these fields rather than new ones.

Applying exact codes in email, chat, and apps

1 min
  • Set the button fill color in Shopify Messaging template branding (the brand settings for the automatic emails Shopify sends).
  • Copy your store's hex color code from Shopify or a browser color picker (a tool that reads the code of any color on screen).
  • Paste it into the button fill color field (the setting for the color inside a button).
  • The chat button brand color can be changed in the Shopify Inbox settings (Shopify's built-in chat tool).
  • This lets it match the store's color scheme.
  • Take the exact hex code from the store's button color, for example via a free Chrome color picker extension (a small add-on for the Chrome web browser).
  • Paste it into the brand color field.

Applying exact codes in design tools

1 min
  • Set the color scheme before designing.
  • In Canva (an online design tool), go to the home page.
  • Open Brand kit (a saved place for your brand colors, fonts, and logos), and scroll to Brand colors.
  • The free plan allows three brand colors.
  • Black and white can be used alongside them.
  • Brand colors saved in Canva's Brand kit appear as swatches (small color samples) in the text and element color pickers.
  • Later designs can reuse the same palette without re-entering codes.
  • The free plan allows three brand colors, and black and white can be used alongside them.
  • In Adobe Illustrator (a design program), a palette saved as a color scheme in Adobe Color (a color tool) can be opened from the Libraries panel (a side panel that holds saved items).
  • It can be applied directly to shapes and text.
  • Save the palette there first so it is ready for the layout.
  • Use the same hex codes in Kittl, Shopify, and all other design tools.

Ebooks (digital books) and documents

1 min
  • An ebook palette example uses deep forest-ink, sage green, warm coral, and soft gold.
  • It uses Fraunces for headings, Inter for body text, and Space Mono for small labels.
  • Pick one palette and two or three typefaces (font families, which are letter styles) that match the store brand.
  • Then use the same colors for headings, highlights, and dividers (thin lines that split sections) across all pages.
  • A calm health or wellness ebook can use the same deep forest-ink, sage green, warm coral, and soft gold palette.
  • It can use Fraunces for headings, Inter for body text, and Space Mono for small labels and eyebrows (short labels above a heading).
  • This pairing looks editorial (like a magazine) and slightly apothecary (like an old pharmacy).
  • It avoids a generic wellness cream look.
  • In an ebook that reframes a myth into a fact (rewrites a false belief as a true fact), give the myth and the fix two different colors.
  • For example, use coral for the myth and sage for the fix.
  • The reader sees the reframe structure at a glance before reading the text.

Common mistakes

1 min
  • Using one color for the whole store makes it look flat (dull, with no depth).
  • Using one color for the whole store gives buttons no contrast.
  • Always add darker colors for buttons and text on top of the light tones.
  • Too many bright colors weaken the brand's identity.
  • Do not use many accent colors across the site.
  • Otherwise, the brand color stops standing out.

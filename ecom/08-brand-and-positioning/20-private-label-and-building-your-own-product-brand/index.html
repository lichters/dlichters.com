Private label and building your own product brand

Section 8.20 · 20 of 24 in this chapter · 14 min read

In short

  • White label means many brands sell the same product. Private label means the product is made to your own plan, so it is unique to you.
  • Putting only your own label on a common product is not enough. Competitors can sell the same item.
  • Test the market with existing products first. Build your own product only after that.
  • Keep product names people already search for. Rename only generic products that nobody searches for.
  • Start your own brand only after you have dropshipping experience and cash.

Private label versus white label

2 min
  • White label: A manufacturer already makes the product and sells it to many buyers. The buyer adds their own label. Sometimes they change the packaging or small details to sell it as their own.
  • Private label: The product is made to the buyer's specification (the detailed plan the buyer gives the maker). The buyer decides on ingredients or design parts, packaging and label design.
  • So the product is unique to their brand.
  • A white label product is sold by many competing brands at once.
  • In the example, one product was sold by up to 90 sellers.
  • Competition came mainly through price.
  • Differentiation (standing out from competitors) depends on having the best version of a product.
  • Private label is the path to a unique product.
  • White label works best when the product is a commodity (an item where sellers look the same) with little room for change.
  • Putting your own label on a white label product does not make it a distinct brand.
  • If many sellers offer the same item, you compete mainly on price.
  • So the product itself must change to stand out.
  • A private label product must be clearly different from what already exists in its market.
  • The difference can be small.
  • It should be brandable (easy to build a brand around), so customers quickly see why it is better or different from alternatives in the niche.
  • A private-label copy of a product that every competitor already sells does not differentiate you.
  • A manufacturer will simply stamp your name onto the same item.
  • Offer a distinct feature, design or bundle.
  • This makes customers seek out your brand specifically.
  • A private-label version should stay recognizable as the same product category.
  • It needs one clear advantage over existing options, such as a missing feature or a fixed weakness.
  • Then customers ask for it by your brand name.
  • Selling an unbranded product is a failure pattern.
  • A well-funded unbranded e-commerce company (a company that sells online) sold everything without a brand.
  • It later shut down.
  • So a product needs a recognizable brand identity (the name, look and feel that make a brand recognizable) to survive.

Deciding between a brand name and private label

2 min
  • About 10% of the brands the expert worked with allow private labeling.
  • These are usually lesser-known brands.
  • Start private label only after the store has data and sales.
  • Before choosing private label, check whether you can sell under the brand name at current market prices.
  • Private labeling makes sense only when competitors undercut each other (sell cheaper) so much that margins disappear.
  • A margin is the money you keep from each sale after costs.
  • Do not remove a searchable brand name, product name or SKU (a stock code that identifies one exact product) just to attract higher-end suppliers.
  • Private labeling only makes sense for vague, generic product names that nobody searches for.
  • Keep the supplier name and original product names where customers search for them.
  • Renaming products to be more appealing can reduce search traffic (visitors who find you through search engines).
  • Older (2020) procedure: Deciding between brand names and private label for suppliers:
    1. Check whether each supplier requires private label.
    2. If it does, focus on building a strong private label brand around those products.
    3. Where the supplier does not enforce MAP (minimum advertised price, the lowest price a seller may advertise), check supplier by supplier whether competitors have pushed prices so low that margins are gone.
    4. Where margins are still intact, list the products under the supplier's brand name and run ads as usual.
    5. Where margins are cannibalized (eaten away by price cuts), add bonuses or offers, or consider private labeling those products.
  • Private labeling as an alternative: When a product has a good market but its price is too low for the 2x rule (the sell price should be about double what you pay), or it is being undercut, add your own brand name to the unbranded supplier product.
  • You then no longer compete on the brand's search terms or on price.
  • This works when the product is unique or high quality and enough markup (the extra added on top of cost) is possible for a healthy margin.
  • Options for products that fail the margin test: Either do not list them, or check whether they can be private labeled.
  • Private labeling requires the supplier to offer something unique or high quality.
  • The market must support a higher markup.
  • Brand-less suppliers (2023): Private labeling for brand-less suppliers (suppliers with no brand of their own) is a later step, not one from day one.
    1. Get a small batch of products from a manufacturer who has no brand names.
    2. Create your own brand name for the products.
    3. Sell them as one of the brands on your store.

Testing the market before building your own brand

1 min
  • Test the market with existing products first.
  • Learn which products, prices, features and benefits the market responds to.
  • Only then create your own product.
  • Ask your target customers what they want before producing a custom product.
  • This validates demand (proves people want it) and avoids producing a product that will not sell.
  • Choose products based on your own sales data: what sells, what customers ask about, what they complain about and which products have profit.
  • Using this data reduces the risk of a new line.
  • Branded testing carries high risk.
  • A large upfront investment (money spent before any sales) and emotional attachment to a product can lead sellers to keep spending on a product without good market fit (customers actually want it).
  • Branded testing requires strong product-selection skill and a large budget.
  • Starting a brand from zero without dropshipping experience and cash flow usually wastes a lot of money.
  • Dropshipping means you sell products that the supplier ships straight to the customer, so you do not stock them yourself.
  • Cash flow means the money coming in and going out.
  • Gain experience and cash flow from dropshipping first.
  • After several successful dropshipping stores, the next step is building your own brand with custom branding (your own name, logo and look on the product) and packaging.
  • Early on, you may fulfill orders yourself (pack and ship them) to keep control of the customer experience.
  • Some high-ticket operators (sellers of expensive products) add their own manufactured products next to dropshipped items to build a brand.
  • This is a growth step after the store is already profitable.

Preparing a private label product line

1 min
  • Definition: Private label means taking a product that already sells well in your drop shipping stores.
  • Make minor changes, add your own label and sell it as a new product line (a group of related products sold under one brand).
  • You do not need to invent a new product.
  • The sales data shows what customers already buy.
  • Size of the first line: Start with about 3 to 5 products.
  • Each product needs investment and, when importing, warehousing (paying for storage space).
  • Carrying many SKUs dilutes the line (weakens it) and adds cost without proportional results.
  • Growth lever: Private label is a major lever (a big factor that moves results) for growing a drop shipping business from about $50,000 in monthly sales to several hundred thousand.
  • It requires a real investment in branded products and their stock.
  • Market fit in branding: Branded products need strong market fit.
  • Invented brand names have no search demand (people typing the name into Google) at launch.
  • So private label dropshipping only makes sense when margins are strong and buyers can find the brand another way.

Branding levels and the path to a unique product

1 min
  • Three levels of branding:
    1. Apply your logo to an existing sourced product (bought from a supplier).
    2. Partner with a manufacturer to modify an existing product, creating a unique version competitors cannot replicate.
    3. Design a completely original product.
    4. This offers maximum differentiation but requires substantial upfront investment.
  • Stages of private labeling: First add your own labels and logos to a product.
  • Then create your own variant (a changed version of the product).
  • The variant stage is where most of the money can be made when selling the store or brand.
  • Generic products as a stepping stone: A generic product (a plain, unbranded item) may not be brandable.
  • It can still earn profit and teach dropshipping skills.
  • Treat early generic winners (generic products that sell well early) as a cash source and learning step.

Manufacturing a private label product with a supplier

1 min
  • Branding cost (Unify): Unify branding typically costs 1 to 3 dollars extra per product.
  • The extra cost depends on the size of the product.
  • Minimum order quantity (Unify): The minimum order quantity (MOQ, the smallest amount you can buy) is roughly 20 to 100 units (single items).
  • It can sometimes be pushed to about 20 to 50 units when the request comes through a mentor or course referral (a recommendation from someone).
  • Procedure: private labeling with Unify:
    1. Contact the supplier (Unify) with a link to the product and request a price list for the relevant countries.
    2. Ask the supplier to send the product's package size, product price, shipping fee and total cost per country and shipping times.
    3. Check the numbers.
    4. Only once the numbers work, ask about branding.
    5. Ask for a packaging template (a ready outline to design on).
    6. Design the packaging in Canva, Photoshop or Placeit using the template dimensions (Placeit's packaging mockups, which are preview pictures of the product, provide the size).
    7. Upload the finished design to the supplier.
    8. Get the sizes and requirements back from the supplier.
  • Customization: Private suppliers can add customization such as logos and better packaging.
  • This improves branding and can increase profitability (how much profit you make) as the store scales (grows).
  • Own name or color: Put your own name or a custom color on the product through your manufacturer to create a brand.
  • Customers trust and return to brands they recognize.
  • So unbranded goods are hard to differentiate.

Brand names and product naming

1 min
  • Own brand name, not the store name: A brand created for an imported generic product must have its own name.
  • It must be separate from your company or main store name.
  • Competitors can then sell the same product under your brand.
  • Store brand name on each store (2024): Use a store brand name you own on each store.
  • Write unique product names and descriptions for them.
  • Do not copy the supplier brand or product name when you white-label products for Google Shopping (Google's product ads).
  • Unique names: Private label products need a unique brand name and a unique product name, such as brand plus product name.
  • A purely descriptive title, such as material and size, makes the product look identical to the same item sold elsewhere.
  • Brand on the page: Put the brand name on the product page and in the product descriptions.
  • Not only on the physical product.
  • So the seller brands the product.
  • Procedure: creating a private label listing (2025). A listing is the sales page for one product.
    1. Create your own brand name, not the store name.
    2. Create unique product names.
    3. Use the supplier's images.
    4. Add the products to the store like products from any other brand.
  • Customers then see a normal brand.
  • The brand can later be sold by competitors.
  • Conflict on renaming and names: The 2021 rule keeps original product names where they carry search traffic.
  • The 2024 and 2025 approach replaces generic names with your own unique product names and brand.
  • Use original names where customers already search for them.
  • Use your own unique names where the product name is generic and nobody searches for it.
  • Private label products need unique product descriptions, reviews and specifications (the product's details, such as size and materials).
  • Do not copy text from other sellers.
  • Only the photos can be the same, and ideally you use unique images too.

Making product images unique

1 min
  • Conflict on images: The 2025 procedure says to use the supplier's images.
  • The 2020 guidance says only the photos can be the same, and ideally you use unique images.
  • Reverse image search lets shoppers find identical products on other sites.
  • Only a tiny fraction of buyers use it.
  • For private label products, customizing the supplementary images (extra photos besides the main one) is still worthwhile to make the product page look unique.
  • Procedure: unique supplementary images in Canva:
    1. Keep the main product image with a white background, since Google Shopping requires it and it cannot be customized.
    2. Upload a supplementary product image to Canva.
    3. Use the Magic eraser tool (Canva's tool that cuts out the background) to remove the white background so the product is transparent (see-through).
    4. Search for a backdrop image (a background picture) that fits the product, such as modern or minimalist office space for a standing desk.
    5. Drag the backdrop behind the transparent product.
    6. Export the result and upload it to the product page as an additional image.

Launching and selling private label products

1 min
  • Launch inside the existing store: Launch privately labeled products as another brand inside the store.
  • Use your own logo, brand name, product names and descriptions.
  • Their traffic (visitors) comes from collections (groups of products on one page), the homepage, email and social.
  • Nobody searches for a new brand yet.
  • Build a separate brand store only once that brand has grown.
  • To build demand for private label products, use the traffic from your other brands, remarketing audiences (people who already visited your store, shown ads again) and the email list.
  • Promote the private label products through promotions such as a 20 percent discount.
  • Unbranded supplier products can be turned into your own brand through private labeling.
  • Early traffic for such products comes mostly from remarketing, email and monthly promotions rather than Google Shopping.
  • Value of a brand: A store built around its own brand is worth far more than a store selling generic, non-branded products.
  • A brand also gives you a more loyal audience to upsell (sell a bigger or more expensive option to people who already buy).

Faceless brands in supplement and digital niches

1 min
  • A faceless brand has no person's face or name in front. A niche is a small, focused market. A supplement is a health product, such as a vitamin.
  • Build faceless supplement and digital product brands focused on specific emotional or performance benefits.
  • Examples: energy, focus, confidence, testosterone (a hormone) or libido (sex drive).
  • These brands do not need a personal brand or founder visibility (the founder being seen).
  • Use white-label suppliers and position (present) the product around solving a specific problem through benefits and proof.
  • This is not personal authority.
  • Problem-based marketing replaces personal brand.
  • This works well in supplement, e-learning (online learning products) and digital product niches.
  • In these niches, solution effectiveness matters more than the founder story.

Upgrading and expanding the product line

1 min
  • Annual upgrades: Once you manufacture a branded product, plan to improve and upgrade it once a year.
  • Example: a hearing aid manufacturer continuously upgraded its product each year after initial manufacturing through an Alibaba supplier (a supplier found on Alibaba, a large online marketplace for suppliers).
  • Collections: Each collection should carry its own campaign and theme.
  • Add new ideas each time, so the brand stays fresh.
  • Similar products in different colourways (colour versions) can still work when they suit the brand.
  • But the collection should still change.
  • Differentiation through quality and variants: Many mass-market accessories (products sold cheaply to everyone) in a niche are of low quality.
  • So a higher-quality version of the same product is a clear way to differentiate.
  • Differentiation can also come from new flavors for rubs (spice mixes for meat) or humorous sayings on products such as grill covers.
  • Licensing (paying to use someone else's name or design) is another differentiation route for accessories.
  • For example, licensed sports-team designs or licensed logos.

Niche brand examples: coloring books

1 min
  • Case: The coloring book seller saw that competitor books offered around 30 designs, sometimes up to 40.
  • They also saw that their paper was too thin.
  • Markers bled through (ink soaked to the other side) and ruined two or three designs.
  • The seller hired designers and kept the best design work for the second book.
  • The seller built each book on thicker paper.
  • Each book had a larger design count of over 120 designs.
  • Books were also split into beginner, intermediate and advanced designs.
  • The set was offered as a three-pack.
  • The result was an improving product line across the nine-book plan.
  • Paper quality: Paper quality is a visible pain point (a problem customers clearly feel) in the coloring book niche.
  • Thin paper lets markers and gel pens bleed through.
  • Thicker paper reduces complaints and reviews that hurt conversion (the share of visitors who buy).
  • Difficulty levels: Offer a range of difficulty levels for the same niche, such as beginner, intermediate and advanced designs.
  • Buyers want quick sessions as well as detailed work.
  • So one difficulty level does not satisfy everyone.
  • Theme range: Offering a variety of themes, such as animals, mandalas (circular patterns) and landmarks, helps buyers who choose books quickly in a store.
  • Carrying a wide range of themes lets each customer find something they want to color.

Niche brand examples: clothing

1 min
  • Case: A dropshipping seller who already had revenue (money coming in) from a dropshipping store started a first fashion brand in Switzerland.
  • Instead of only reselling, the seller sourced the product directly (bought it from the maker).
  • The seller ran a self-funded photo shoot, paid for from their own money.
  • The seller packed every order by hand from a local base.
  • The next step was to order stock (products held in storage) in bulk (large quantities) into their own warehouse.
  • Focus on a strong customer experience.
  • Rely on word of mouth and referrals.
  • The stated goal was to sell the brand for seven figures (a million or more) within the Swiss market in a few years.
  • The outcome was not yet known at that point.

Building a brand from a proven product

1 min
  • Conversion threshold: A generic product that proves itself, reaching roughly $50,000 to $100,000 in revenue, is then worth turning into a brand.
  • Procedure: turning a proven product into a brand:
    1. Confirm the product has real revenue, around $50,000 to $100,000.
    2. Add a brand identity to the product.
    3. Create custom packaging.
    4. Build a separate website for the product.
  • Timeline: Building a proven product into a recognized brand can take around one and a half years.

Further details

1 min
  • Launch a private label brand in this order:
    1. Build a simple brand website separate from your store. For example, a basic WordPress site (a common website builder) on the brand's own domain (its own web address). It describes the company and where to buy the product. It is not an e-commerce store.
    2. List the brand on your drop shipping store alongside your other suppliers.
    3. Contact your competitors and offer them the branded product to list on their own sites. They then act as another supplier.

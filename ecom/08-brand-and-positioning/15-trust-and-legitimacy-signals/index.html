Playbook › 8. Brand and positioning

Trust and legitimacy signals

Section 8.15 · 15 of 24 in this chapter · 12 min read

In short

  • Trust comes from doing things well: fewer bugs, fast replies, clean design and good service. Quick tricks do not last.
  • Show real contact details: a business email, a visible phone number and a clear About page make buyers feel safe.
  • Show a badge only for a brand you really sell. Fake badges are a false claim.
  • Ask permission first before you use a supplier's reviews or logos, or name a charity on your store.
  • Be honest about donations, delivery delays and what support you really offer.
This section has 12 parts. Tap one to open it.

Trust foundations

1 min
  • Trust comes mostly from professionalism. Buyers see it in bugs, speed, design and service. Quick tricks and low-effort shortcuts do not build lasting trust.
  • Trust grows when you remove the reasons customers would write or call before buying. Professional design, correct grammar, a visible phone number, a strong About page, a clear contact page, personal product notes and trust badges (small logos that show a promise, such as a guarantee) all serve this goal.
  • Visitors trust a new store more when it is visibly linked to brands, companies or associations they already know. This is called authority by association: trust passes from a known name to you. A brand with no recognition can borrow credibility this way.
  • Reviews, video reviews and user-generated content (photos or posts made by customers) lower perceived risk, because buyers see happy customers. They work as social proof (other people's good experiences shown to buyers) and as risk reversal (a promise that takes the risk off the buyer). So they belong in the offer.
  • With a cold audience (people who do not know you yet), educate people on the problem and the solution before you sell. Deep product knowledge and expert-backed proof make customers feel loyal to the brand.

Business identity: email, phone and address

1 min

Business email

  • Use a professional business email address, not a personal account, for supplier communication. This shows legitimacy and supports long-term partnerships.
  • Business buyers can be spotted by their email domain (the part after the @ sign). Orders from company addresses, rather than free providers such as gmail.com, suggest a B2B customer (business-to-business, selling to companies). Track these companies so you can list them as customers once you have enough of them.

Virtual address

  • Register a virtual business address (a mailing address that is not your home) to meet supplier approval requirements and look legitimate. Do not display it prominently everywhere on the website. Use it for approvals while keeping your privacy.
  • Do not post a physical location everywhere on the site.
  • If a customer asks for a showroom (a place to see products in person), answer: "We do not have a physical location; we ship direct from the factory." If the product needs touch to decide, ask suppliers for swatches or samples (small fabric or material pieces, about 2 x 2 inches) to send to customers.

Phone numbers and voicemail

2 min

Showing a phone number

  • Display a phone number on your website to increase trust, even if you cannot answer every call. A visible number signals credibility.
  • Put a dedicated business phone number on the website and give it to suppliers, instead of a personal mobile. Buyers expect a business to have a separate contact line, and a personal number lowers trust. You can still answer business calls on your own mobile, since the business number only needs to look professional.
  • Show the phone number on the website so visitors can call before buying.
  • Show the phone number prominently in the header (the top strip of every page) for expensive products.
  • For B2B sales, show your phone number prominently on the trade page (a page for business buyers) and on the quote request form (a form where companies ask for a price).
  • Open Phone (open phone.com) provides US virtual phone numbers (phone numbers that ring on your device) for non-US residents doing business in the United States. It is the alternative to Mighty Call if you cannot get a domestic number.
  • Mighty Call is a US virtual phone number service. Source A members get a discount code in the bonus section of the members area.

Voicemail

  • Use this voicemail greeting template (a ready-made message that callers hear):
Welcome, this is [Your Name] at [store name]. We're currently unable to answer your call because we're helping other customers. Please leave a message with your name, email address, phone number, and the purpose of your call-we'll get back to you as quickly as possible.
  • Set up voicemail to be delivered as an email transcript (written text of the message) or text message. You see the request right away and can handle urgent calls, such as large orders, during business hours.

Outsourcing phone support

  • Outsource phone support to a virtual call center (a company that answers calls for you), billed per call or per minute. Do not pay for fixed full-day staff. This gives 24/7 coverage (answers any hour, every day) while you work another job.
  • If your core buyers are older and prefer personal contact, design support around calls, texts and direct answers rather than only automated channels. Know your buyer profile before you choose your service style.

Presence beyond the website

1 min
  • To look legitimate to customers who doubt a new store, build a presence beyond the website.
    1. Create and keep active a Facebook page, an Instagram account and a Pinterest profile.
    2. Post a couple of times per week on each. You do not need daily posts in large communities.
    3. Show a phone number on the website so visitors can call before buying.
  • A store with no social media links looks like a burner store (a short-lived store that may vanish) to visitors who check whether a real business stands behind it. Missing social profiles weaken trust.

Support and service promises

1 min
  • State the support you actually provide, such as phone hours, email response time and shipping time. Competitors who sell at a loss tend to fall behind on service, so accurate statements build credibility.
  • Respond to positive reviews sent by email. Do not respond to public positive reviews on judge.me (a review app for Shopify, the online store software).
  • Respond publicly to negative reviews only when the business really failed. Ignore abusive reviewers.

Supplier logos and authorized retailer status

2 min

Authorized retailer statements

  • Display that you are an authorized retailer (an approved seller) for the brands you sell. This builds your authority by association and tells customers the products are genuine.
  • Show an authorized retailer badge only for a brand you are actually approved to sell. A badge on a product page for a brand you do not represent is a false claim.
  • Place the authorized retailer statement on that brand's product pages, in the product description, for example: "We are an authorized retailer for [brand]".
  • Show the authorized retailer badge on each brand's collection page in Shopify, with a statement that you are an authorized retailer for that brand.
  • Use this statement near the product title or price, not only in the footer (the bottom strip of every page):
We are an authorized [official brand name] retailer.
  • When a brand approves you as a reseller (a seller who resells its products), it may send an "authorized retailer" image. If it sends only its logo, create the badge yourself by adding the words "authorized retailer" under the logo.
  • When you request reseller approval, be ready to explain how you plan to position the supplier's products on your site. Brand owners want to see the intended positioning before they agree.

Creating the badge

Creating an authorized retailer badge (the 2021 version gives the same four steps):

  1. Get the brand logo.
  2. Open Canva (canva.com, a free online design tool; the free version is enough) and load the logo. Add the supplier logo.
  3. Add the words "authorized retailer" under the logo.
  4. Save or export the result as your badge image and place it on the store.
  • Keep logos simple. Overloaded logos with cartoon characters, large circles and taglines underneath look messy and reduce trust.

Logo placement

  • Show supplier logos on product pages, not in the footer on every page. The logo matters most where the buying decision happens. Footer logos add many images that slow the site without helping sales.
  • Show the brand names and logos of your suppliers prominently, and add a "Shop by Brand" navigation section (a menu that lists brands). This catches customers searching for those brand names directly and builds your authority as an authorized retailer.
  • Supplier trust badges can go on product pages, with a different badge for each brand.

Trade associations, B2B and customer logos

1 min

Trade associations

  • If you or your business belong to a trade association (a membership group for one industry), show that membership on the About Us page. For example, show an NRA membership (National Rifle Association, a gun-rights group) for a store selling gun safes, or membership in the International Interior Design Association for a store selling curtains. New stores usually have no such membership unless the owner has one personally.

B2B trade page

  • For B2B drop shipping (you sell, and the supplier ships directly to the buyer, to companies), add a trade page that shows companies you have worked with and offers bulk discounts.
  • Include a quote request form that asks for name, email, phone and products of interest.

Customer logos

  • Once businesses start buying, add their logos to a "Past Clients" or "To the Trade" section to build social proof. Add a logo only after the client has ordered, and build the section over time.
  • Create an "Our Customers" page on Shopify only once you have business customers, not from day one. List the companies that bought from you. Visible business clients show that you sell to other businesses and raise your authority.

Reviews, testimonials and supplier reviews

1 min

Supplier reviews

  • Use supplier reviews on your store only with permission.
    1. Ask the supplier for permission to use its reviews.
    2. Once they agree, copy the approved reviews into your review app.
    3. Never import reviews from other websites without permission.
  • To get early reviews without waiting for sales, first ask the supplier to share its existing review database (its stored list of customer reviews) for its products. Then ask customers who buy from you for their own reviews.

Testimonials

  • Quoted testimonials (written praise from customers) can serve as a starting point before real reviews exist. Replace them with real customer testimonials once sales come in.

Press mentions

1 min
  • If a brand has been featured in the press, mention it in the brand's collection page content, not on product pages. Add it in the collection SEO section (the text box that helps search engines such as Google understand a page).

Charity partnerships

3 min

Choosing a partner

  • Charity Navigator is a free website for checking charities. Use it to confirm that a partner is a registered 501(c)(3) (a US charity that is tax-exempt, meaning it pays no income tax) and to review its finances before you donate.
  • When choosing a charity partner, pick three candidates and list the pros and cons of each. Make sure the partner has no downside that could harm your brand or the local economy.
  • Start with a domestic or local charity, not an international one, when you are new. Local partners are easier to verify and can strengthen trust with domestic customers.
  • A good benchmark is about 5% of donations going to administration and executive salaries. Check this before donating and review the partner's past use of funds.

Permission and wording

  • Mention a charity you support only with the charity's explicit permission (clear, direct approval). This applies whether you give a fixed percentage of sales, a fixed amount per month or ad hoc donations (one-off gifts). Smaller charities, or ones you already have a relationship with, are more likely to agree.
  • Naming a charity on your store without its explicit permission is a mistake. Charities are careful about who they are linked to because of charity scams. Ask for approval before you show the association.
  • A holiday charity pledge should state the percentage of sales, the exact holiday period it applies to, and the named beneficiary with its logo. Limiting the pledge to a defined period makes it verifiable and avoids an open-ended claim.
  • Humorous or off-brand copy next to a trust element, such as a charity donation message, weakens its seriousness. Keep copy near trust signals consistent with the store's tone.

Donation amounts

  • Two values appear for the per-sale donation (money given for each sale). They apply to different situations:
    • The minimum donation per sale was 50 cents in the campaign described.
    • A start at 25 cents is enough to make a charitable claim credible.
  • Use 25 cents as a credible starting point, and treat the 50-cent minimum as the value set in the campaign described.
  • One sales day that donated 100% of revenue, split across three charities after a hurricane, totaled $75,000.

Transparency and campaigns

  • Be open about your donations and tell customers how much is given, because this drives conversions (visitors who buy). The message must be genuine: it should describe the product and the donation accurately and never misrepresent the brand.
  • A collaboration framed around social good, such as a promised donation match within a 24-hour window, earned coverage from major publishers. You do not need to announce final donation totals up front, but be ready to be open about them when asked.
  • Brands with a real cause tend to attract customers, especially when the cause is authentic. Profits can be donated, or customers can be offered an optional extra payment that goes to a specific charity or cause.
  • Reading glasses donations were first routed through Vision Partners. A later distribution partner (a group that delivers goods to people) could handle about 200,000 pairs globally, shipped in batches of roughly 1,000 units.

Badges, claims and honesty

1 min

Fake and purchased badges

  • Many dropshipping stores show fake or bought trust badges, such as Google Trusted Store, Forbes, FDA approval (US food and drug agency approval) or a TM trademark symbol. These badges can be bought cheaply, so they do not prove legitimacy. Do not copy them.
  • Paid placements (paid features on news sites such as Fox or Forbes) can be bought through Brand Push Up. The source quoted about 195 to 200 USD for one featured placement. This is an optional, pricier way to gain authority.
  • Big-name brands can create brand association even when margins (profit left after costs) are low. Including them can strengthen brand recognition and a larger catalog (list of products).

"Not sold on Amazon or eBay" badge

  • Some competitor product pages add a "not sold on Amazon or eBay" badge to show the brand sells only through its own store. Use this badge only if it is true for your supply, and check the marketplaces before publishing it.

Niche and generic trust badges

  • Trust badges that speak to the buyer's own world show that the store understands its customers. Generic badges, such as free shipping or a guarantee, fit any store. A niche-specific badge builds credibility faster. Examples of the pattern: "Built by hikers for hikers" for a trail gear store, or "Tested by dog owners" for a pet bed store.
  • Show trust badges for shipping, product sourcing and a guarantee, such as free shipping, direct-from-manufacturer delivery and a 30-day guarantee. These help new visitors who do not know the brand yet.

Communicating about delays

1 min
  • Send proactive customer communication (messages sent before the customer asks). If delivery takes more than 2 weeks, email customers every 3 days with shipping status updates, explain the delay and confirm that a free gift is included.
    1. Check shipment tracking (the record of where a parcel is) daily.
    2. If expected delivery is more than 2 weeks away, email the customer within the first week.
    3. Explain the reason for the delay.
    4. Confirm that a free gift is included in the parcel.
    5. Send a follow-up email 3 days later with a tracking update.

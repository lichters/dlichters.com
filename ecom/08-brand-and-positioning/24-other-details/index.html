Playbook › 8. Brand and positioning

Other details

Section 8.24 · 24 of 24 in this chapter · 8 min read

In short

  • Test one product at a time. If you test several, give each one its own campaign.
  • A failed product still teaches you something. Improve it or try a new angle or market.
  • Build the brand in order: name, look, domain, then demo website.
  • Make the sale path simple: a clear storefront, clear offers, and an email for abandoned carts.

Product testing

1 min
  • Test one product at a time.
  • If you must test several products, give each product its own campaign. A campaign is one set of ads with its own budget. Never share one campaign across products. This keeps the spending, traffic and results of each product easy to read.
  • A product that fails can still teach you something.
  • Pick products you can test quickly, with limited spend (small amounts of money).
  • After a test, improve the product or try a new angle (a different way of selling it) or a new market.
  • Not every product needs to win in its first market.
  • To find the best-selling product in a new or existing store:
    1. Open the store's sales data (the report of what was sold) and sort or filter sales by product.
    2. Find the product with the highest sales.
    3. If you are not sure, copy the URL (web address) of that product page and record it with your onboarding answers (the first setup questions).
    4. If the store has just launched and has no sales, skip this step.

Brand foundations

2 min
  • Older advice. Set up the brand and website in this order:
    1. Choose and settle the business name.
    2. Develop the branding (the look, colors and style of your brand).
    3. Register and secure the domain (your web address).
    4. Build the demo website (a first version of the site) on Shopify (a store-building platform).
  • Advanced branding and social media strategies come later.
  • They build on a basic brand and on channels (platforms where you sell or post) that already work.
  • Once basic needs are covered, make the business bolder in every area, not only in branding.
  • Hiring, sourcing (finding products and suppliers) and warehousing (storing stock) decisions should also aim bigger.
  • Every bold move carries risk. Choose the risk deliberately.
  • Case: a supplement brand (a brand selling vitamins or health products) focused on cognitive health (brain health), with open-source formulas (recipes shared publicly) and full supplier disclosure (showing where ingredients come from).
    • The founders named it Natural Stacks.
    • "Natural" means all ingredients are natural.
    • "Stacks" means the simple formulas are meant to be combined.
    • The homepage was not the most visited page.
    • The ingredient traceability (proof of where each ingredient came from) and label pages were among the most visited pages on the site.
    • Results: over $100,000 in revenue (money from sales) in the first 100 days.
    • Over $1,000,000 in the first year.
    • More than $1,000,000 per year on Amazon (an online marketplace) US and UK as a separate channel.
  • Frame a purchase as an asset (something that keeps producing value after you pay).
  • Examples: a training course that keeps raising earnings, or an investment that keeps growing.
  • Buyers who see the cost as an investment resist it less than buyers who see it as spending.
  • Describe the ongoing return in terms the buyer can measure.
  • Build an idea file of mailings and ads you receive.
  • Build a swipe file of strong samples.
  • With them you can show a designer or copywriter (the person who writes the words) the exact look and copy (written text) you want.
  • Reusing proven patterns is faster than inventing new formats.

Storefront and content

1 min
  • Required content for a static affiliate site. A static site is a simple set of fixed pages. An affiliate site earns a commission (a share of each sale) from links to other stores.
    1. Static pages, such as an About Me page and any disclaimer pages.
    2. A product page for the main product, with a personal experience, a case study and proof screenshots (pictures showing real results) from your own or a friend's store.
    3. Three to five blog posts that show authority (prove you know the subject).
    4. Affiliate links placed in the pages and posts.
    5. Opt-in forms (sign-up boxes) to collect emails from visitors.
  • An affiliate store built on imported products (products you list from another source) looks and acts like an e-commerce store.
  • But it is not one.
  • It should not receive paid traffic (visitors you pay for through ads) from the main traffic sources.
  • Its useful role is retargeting (showing ads again to people who already visited) visitors who already came to your dropship store (a store where suppliers ship orders for you).
  • If no recognized brand exists, you can still build search traffic (visitors who find you through search engines).
  • Get approved for brands in similar categories and list their products.
  • Then guide shoppers toward your own products or the artisan (handmade by small makers) products you offer.

Traffic

1 min
  • To test a new traffic source with a paid banner (picture ad) on a relevant niche website (a website about one small topic):
    1. Find sites that already have the target audience and show banner ads.
    2. Ask about buying ad space.
    3. Negotiate a free trial of about one week.
    4. Send the banner image files and a UTM-tagged link (a link with tags that show where each visit came from).
    5. If sales come in, pay the ongoing rate.

Email and post-purchase

1 min
  • Shoppers check the order tracking page (where buyers see where their parcel is) more than any other post-purchase touchpoint (contact point after buying).
  • That makes it a high-value place for cross-sells (offers for a related product) and upsells (offers for a better or bigger version).
  • To build the first abandoned cart email (a reminder sent to someone who left items in their cart without buying), use this order:
    1. Put the logo at the top to build trust.
    2. Add the big desire or USP (unique selling point, what makes you different) headline.
    3. Add the product block with each abandoned item's title, quantity and line total (price times quantity).
    4. Add a short urgency or scarcity line (a sentence saying time or stock is limited).
    5. Add the CTA (call to action, the button asking for a click) buttons "Complete your order" and "Return to cart".
    6. Add a "Why choose us" section with three objection blocks (boxes that answer doubts): a quality claim, local or family ownership, and the 30-day money-back guarantee.
    7. Add a help line with the contact email.
    8. Close with a final CTA and the brand's unique value points, such as personal service, free shipping and quality.
    9. Remove the header (top menu bar) and announcement bar (a strip at the very top of the page) if the logo and products already appear.
      • Delete filler lines that add no value, such as lines that repeat that the cart is waiting.

Business-to-business sales

1 min
  • To set up B2B (business to business, selling to other companies) for a store with business customers:
    1. Create a page called "To the Trade".
    2. Link it in the header menu and the footer (the bottom strip of the page).
    3. Mention on the page that the store sells business to business.
    4. Add a contact form (a question form) asking which products and what quantity the business is interested in.
    5. Follow up on large volume orders (orders of many units) with special pricing or a bulk incentive (a deal for buying in bulk).
    6. Add logos (brand pictures) of past business customers to the trade page and the home page to build trust.
  • B2B customers search the same way as consumers.
  • So the main marketing does not need to change.
  • For business customers, only the trade page, contact form and logos are added.
  • Targeted B2B ads through industry publications (trade magazines) or associations (groups for one trade), such as banner or print ads, reach the right audience.
  • But they convert less (turn fewer viewers into buyers).
  • Treat them as a later scaling tactic (a method for growing once the basics work).

Team structure

1 min
  • Build the brand team starting with three core roles (main jobs):
    • Senior Creative: develops brand strategy (the long-term plan for the brand) and documents.
    • Content Creator: produces narratives (stories) across mediums (ways of sharing, such as video or text).
    • Social Media Manager: handles campaigns (organized series of posts or ads), analyzes trends and closes the audience feedback loop (uses audience responses to improve).
  • Hire a Senior Creative (a senior graphic designer, art director or creative director).
  • This role can align the brand across the entire business.
  • It can assemble specialized team members as needed.
  • It can develop brand cues (signals of the brand's style) and documents that guide market positioning (how the brand is placed compared with others).
  • A Content Creator must produce narratives across different mediums.
  • While maintaining specialist-level quality (expert quality) in execution (the actual work).
  • A Social Media Manager quickly identifies and acts on platform trends (what is popular on each app or site).
  • They leverage audience reach (how many people see the posts).
  • They feed insights back to the Senior Creative team so the feedback loop closes.

Seasonal planning

1 min
  • For a seasonal brand (a brand whose sales rise and fall with the seasons), plan the next season's photo shoot, creatives (ad images and videos) and designs in the off-season (the quiet months). Do this before the selling season begins.
  • Reinvest in that off-season period so the new content is ready when demand (customer interest) returns.
  • For example, a winter brand reinvests in summer.
  • Before running a Black Friday campaign (a big US shopping sale day), you need:
    • a proof of concept (proof that the idea works): the product is selling;
    • profitable upsells in place, ideally with profitability;
    • ready-made unique offers (deals prepared in advance), such as free gifts.
  • These offers ring-fence (protect) the campaign from competition.

AI and process

1 min
  • Some dropshipping programs add AI as a side tool, such as using ChatGPT (an AI chat tool) for product descriptions.
  • This is not the same as building the business process around AI.
  • A process-level AI approach uses AI in product research, store building and creative production (making ads and images).

Learning resources

1 min
  • Recommended learning resources for brand strategy (the plan for how your brand is built):
    • StoryBrand, a book on the hero's journey framework (a story structure where a hero faces a challenge and wins).
    • A Coursera (an online learning site) brand course covering psychographics (what people think, value and feel) and customer behavior.
    • 100 Million Offers by Alex Hormozi.
  • Courses on branding and strategy from universities can teach brand fundamentals (the basic building blocks).
  • For example, the Coursera branding course costs roughly 30 GBP per month (British pounds).
  • The first part is often free.

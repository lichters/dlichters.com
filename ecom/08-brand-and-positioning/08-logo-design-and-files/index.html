Logo design and files

Section 8.8 · 8 of 24 in this chapter · 27 min read

In short

  • Keep the logo simple: text plus one small picture.
  • Decide the brand name and its spelling first. A changed name means redoing everything.
  • Start with a text-only logo. The name matters more than the logo.
  • Test the logo on small spots, like a profile circle, and on light and dark backgrounds.
  • When unsure, pick the simpler design. Less is more.
More key points
  • Make a light logo version for light backgrounds and a dark version for dark backgrounds.
  • Use a transparent PNG (no background) for the website. Use a vector file for big signs.
  • Keep the logo simple. Busy or childish designs lose trust.
  • Test the logo on every background before you finish.
This section has 14 parts. Tap one to open it.

Logo requirements

4 min
  • Define your brand first: decide its values (what it believes in), what it offers, the customer problems it solves and its tone of voice (how it talks, such as friendly or formal). These answers guide the logo's shape and style.
  • The logo is the face of your brand (the picture or name that stands for it). It is often the first thing customers link to your brand. It supports the feelings, beliefs and attitudes people already hold, which is why it matters for trust (customers feeling safe buying from you).
  • A good logo passes four tests (called criteria, the rules a good logo must meet): simplicity, memorability, versatility and relevance. Use each one to judge a design.
  • Simplicity: the logo is easy to recognize and remember. Simplified versions of objects work well, as the Apple and Twitter bird examples show. A simple mark (a symbol in the logo) is recognized at a glance and recalled later.
  • Memorability: the logo makes a strong impression at first glance and stands out enough that a viewer instantly recognizes it on later encounters.
  • Versatility (works in many places): the logo must work in many formats (social media, websites, letterheads, which are printed pages with your business details) and on both light and dark backgrounds. Test it in each context before finalizing.
  • Relevance: the logo should visually match the niche (a small, specific market group). For a dropshipping brand (a store where a supplier ships the products for you), use concepts from the product's niche. For example, use pet symbols for a pet brand or baby symbols for a baby brand, so the brand looks like it belongs to that niche.
  • A logo should be as simple as possible, because simpler logos produce better results. Minimal designs look more professional and more luxurious than busy ones.
  • A logo should be simple: text plus one small graphic, nothing showy, so it is noticed but does not compete with the page. Stores whose products are already colorful and busy benefit most from a simpler logo.
  • When unsure about a logo design, choose the simpler option and avoid over-engineering (adding too many parts) or overcomplicating it. Less is more.
  • A logo does not need to be professional at the start. It should be clean, the right size and not pixelated (blurry or blocky at the edges). A simple typed wordmark (a logo made only of letters) is enough and cheap to make. **
  • The logo should use the same words as the domain name (your web address, such as brand.com), so the store name is the most visible element. Check that the spelling matches the domain exactly. This keeps the brand name consistent across the site.
  • Logo design should be primarily text-based, showing the business name clearly and legibly (easy to read) as the dominant element (the biggest, most noticed part). Decorative logo marks (symbols added for looks) are optional and not necessary for success. **
  • The logo must be rectangular (wider than tall) to fit into website headers (the top strip of a web page) without consuming excessive vertical space. Circular logos are difficult to size and display properly. **
  • Your website logo appears in the header, the footer (the bottom strip of a web page) and a few other pages. Rectangular orientation (the wide shape) is critical because it fits naturally in the header space without consuming excessive height. **
  • A logo should be rectangular and no more than about 200 px high, with a width of around 400 to 500 px. px means pixels, the tiny dots that make up a picture. This size loads quickly and displays well in multiple places on the site. **
  • Logo text style should match the target customer base (the people you want to buy from you). Male-dominated markets such as power equipment benefit from bold, chunky, thick fonts. Water sports and movement-focused markets suit softer, flowing typography (the design of the letters). Luxury segments need elegant, refined letterforms (the shape of each letter). **
  • Logo design quality comes from a brand colour palette (your set of brand colours) and a chosen typeface (a font design), defined before the logo is built. This way colours can be swapped quickly to test how the logo looks.
  • Build logos from core basic shapes (circles, squares, ellipses or ovals, and rectangles) instead of drawing everything freehand (by hand, without tools). Complex icons can be reduced to a few simple shapes that fit together.
  • Taglines (short slogans) in the logo are optional and make no measurable difference to business success. Include one only if it genuinely resonates with the target market's values or positioning (how the brand is placed in the customer's mind). **
  • Branding is not permanent. The logo design and colours can evolve over time without changing the business name or domain. **

Wordmark or icon: where to start

2 min
  • A simple typed wordmark in a nice font is enough as a logo at the start. Logos matter less than the brand name, so do not spend much time on logo design.
  • At the early stage, use text-only wordmarks with no icon or symbol, because a mark is not needed for versatility yet. Each wordmark option should differ only in its letter case (capital or small letters) or type style.
  • Paying for a logo is optional during setup. A logo can cost about USD 20 or up to USD 100, and the choice adds to the USD 100 to 500 launch budget. **
  • A consistent symbol paired with the brand name builds recognition (customers knowing your brand) over time. When later products launch, customers who saw the icon before connect it with past purchases and expect to like the new product.
  • A brand logo must work across many surfaces, such as a website, packing slips (the paper inside a parcel that lists its items), social media ads and other materials. It needs to be versatile and adaptable, not a single image that only fits one place.
  • Test a logo by mocking it up (making a test picture of it) on the real surfaces where it will appear, such as an Instagram profile, before deciding. A wordmark can fit poorly in a small profile circle while a compact icon fits well.
  • There are two ways to build an icon. The automated route uses AI to brainstorm concepts. The manual route has you sketch the icon yourself and have AI refine it. Use the AI brainstorm when you need options, and use the sketch when you already have a specific idea in mind.
  • Logo workflow in four steps:
    1. Develop an icon that pairs with the brand name.
    2. Export (save) the logo as a transparent PNG (an image file with a see-through background).
    3. Convert the PNG into a vector file (a logo file that stays sharp at any size).
    4. Finalize the logo into the variations you actually need.
  • A logo concept should express the product's core meaning in a simple visual idea, such as motion for speed or growth for progress. A simple symbol is easier to remember and connects to the brand story. Example: a leaf-shaped mark for an eco-friendly cleaning product. Second example: an arrow shape forming a 'U' for an upgrade-focused electronics store.
  • Logo design pattern: a simple mark made of two or three basic shapes, where one shape is cut out of another (negative space, the empty part that forms a picture) to suggest a recognizable object. The viewer completes the image in their mind, which makes the mark memorable and easy to recognize. Example of the same pattern: a coffee cup whose handle is formed by a cut-out circle in a solid square, and a leaf built from a single cut-out ellipse on a circle.
  • A pet-friendly brand logo can pair a rounded display typeface (a font made for large headings) with a lighter, softer secondary typeface. Choose the typefaces together with the brand colours before building the mark, so the text and symbol share one visual tone.

Choose the route (the way you make the logo) by budget and time to market (how fast you can launch).

Option Best when How it is used

Adobe Illustrator or Photoshop (professional design apps) Owner has design skills Build the logo from scratch

  • Logo, colour and typography can be made in Canva, through a graphic design class, or by hiring a freelancer (an independent worker) on a third-party service such as Fiverr.
  • Canva is a free design tool that can produce a simple logo. A professional designer is an option when more polished branding is needed. **
  • Canva can hold a brand kit (Canva's saved place for your logo, colours and fonts) that combines the logo, colour palette and typefaces in one place.
  • You do not need a finished logo concept before contacting a Fiverr designer. Provide three colour hex codes (colour codes made of letters and numbers), the business name, what the business sells and the target market feel you want. Then let the designer create options from that foundation. **
  • A logo can be built from overlapping shapes in Illustrator (Adobe Illustrator, a professional drawing app):
    1. Set each new shape to about 50 percent transparency (how see-through a shape is) so overlapping shapes stay visible.
    2. Draw the basic shapes with the Shape tool (draws simple shapes) and the Pen tool (draws lines point by point), and shape anchor points (the dots that control a line's bend) with the Direct Selection tool.
    3. Group the shapes you want to combine and open the Shape Builder tool (merges or cuts shapes).
    4. Drag across the areas you want to keep to merge them into one shape, and hold Alt or Option (keyboard keys) and drag across the areas you want to remove.
    5. Lock finished layers using the lock icon (a padlock picture) in the Layers panel (the list of all parts of your drawing) so they cannot be moved by accident.
    6. Polish the outline with the Direct Selection tool (moves single points), then set the transparency back to 100 percent.
    7. Collect several reference logos for inspiration and iterate (repeat and improve) until the shape feels balanced.

Canva: store logo for the Source A theme

1 min

Canva procedure with the 800 x 300 px template (a ready-made design of that width and height):

  1. Open the logo template for the Source A theme (800 x 300 px) and click Use Template.
  2. Click Text, then Add a heading, and type the store name exactly as the domain name.
  3. Save the brand colors in Brand Kit first, then select the text and apply a brand color with the text color (A) icon.
  4. Enlarge the text until it fills the template, choose a bold or extra-bold font and optionally capitalize it.
  5. Adjust letter spacing (space between letters) and line spacing (space between lines) so the text fills the frame and the lines sit close together.
  6. Open Elements (Canva's menu of shapes and icons), search Graphics for a simple icon such as a letter S, drag it in, resize it to fill the template and recolor it to match the brand colors.
  7. Keep the logo to text plus one small graphic.
  8. Download the logo as a PNG. On the free plan (Canva's no-cost version) it keeps a white background.

A second version of the same Canva procedure:

  1. Open the logo template (800 x 300 pixels) through the linked template and click Use template.
  2. Click Text, then Add a heading, and type the store's domain name.
  3. Select the text and use the A text color button to pick one of the brand colors from the Brand kit.
  4. Change the font, make it bold or extra bold, capitalize it if wanted, and enlarge it to fill the template. Adjust letter spacing and line spacing as needed.
  5. Go to Elements, then Graphics, search for a simple letter or symbol and pick a free item (items with a crown are paid).
  6. Drag the icon into the template, enlarge it to nearly the full template size and change its color to the brand palette.
  7. Click Share or the download button, choose PNG and download.

Canva: square template

1 min
  • The Canva logo template used in this method is a square 500 × 500 px design. Keep the logo on a square canvas (the working area) of this size.
  • Procedure in Canva Premium (Canva's paid version):
    1. Decide the brand name first.
    2. In Canva Premium, open Designs in the top-left area.
    3. Search "Logo" in the "Search Logo templates" field and scroll through the results.
    4. Choose a simple, minimalist template that matches a professional look.
    5. Replace the template text with your brand name.
    6. Edit the template's letters to fit the brand name, for example by changing the first and last letter of the template text into an O-shape.
    7. Adjust the position of the logo elements (move up or down) until they are balanced.
    8. Adjust the size of the logo so it is clearly readable.
    9. Check the result against the simplicity rule and finish if it is simple and professional.
  • Pick a template whose style is black and white, minimalist and simple, because that style matches the professional look a logo should have. Reject templates with many elements or strong decoration, even if they catch the eye first.

Wordmarks generated with ChatGPT

5 min
  • For logo generation in ChatGPT (an AI chat tool), select the ChatGPT 5 Thinking model (the AI version, set to reason longer before answering) and the Heavy thinking mode in the composer (the text box where you type) before pasting the logo prompt (the instruction text you give the AI). The full set of four wordmarks, four favicons (the tiny icons shown in browser tabs) and a palette (your set of brand colours) took about one minute of thinking in this setup.
  • Before generating, finalise the brand name and its spelling. The wordmarks and favicons are built from the exact spelling. If the name changes later, all four wordmark options, favicons and the palette must be generated again with the new name.
  • Keep the wordmark to the brand name only and leave out the domain extension (the ending, such as.com), even if the domain is pasted into the prompt. The domain is an input for the brand positioning, not part of the logo text.
  • Steps to create a logo set and palette with ChatGPT:
    1. Build a master brand document (one file that holds all your brand facts) with the product list, the chosen brand name, the domain name and one brand positioning (three short pillars, meaning main ideas, each a label with one sentence, plus a short mini story paragraph).
    2. Open a new chat in ChatGPT using the thinking model.
    3. Paste the logo generator prompt and replace the placeholders (blank spots to fill in) with the brand name, the domain and the positioning text.
    4. Wait until the generation is finished.
    5. Click download and download all assets (all the files): the 4 wordmark PNGs, the 4 favicon PNGs and the palette swatch PNG (a colour sample image).
    6. Open the downloaded files in Finder (the Mac file browser) to check that the wordmarks, favicons and palette swatch are all there.
    7. Use the logo, favicon and text options in the Shopify theme (Shopify: the software that runs your online store; theme: its design template).
  • Logo generator prompt, to be pasted with placeholders filled in:
Act as a senior logo designer. Produce 4 clean wordmarks for the brand [BRAND NAME] that use type alone: no icon, symbol, mascot or other mark, only the name set in lettering. Leave out drop shadows, gradients, 3D effects and ornaments. The background must be transparent.

Mandatory baseline rules:
- Every letter sits on one shared baseline. No letter may be moved vertically on its own.
- Reject any bouncy or playful baseline. Switch off any stylistic set that offsets glyphs.
- Keep the font's default kerning. Adjust tracking evenly across the whole word, never letter by letter.
- Fonts may overshoot for optical reasons, but the baseline must still look straight, with no stair-stepping.
- Run a baseline check before export and regenerate any option that shows vertical jitter.

Four style options:
- A: lowercase
- B: Title Case
- C: UPPERCASE, bold
- D: soft serif (small caps, expanded)

Inspiration, not imitation: draw on recent, high-quality brand aesthetics from the last 10 years. Choose new, free fonts that suit the brand positioning and the products sold.

Deliverables: PNG wordmarks on a transparent background, 3000 x 1200 px, wordmark centred with even padding on all sides. Also create 4 PNG favicons, one per option, each showing the first letter of the wordmark.

Spelling must match the input exactly. Use consistent tracking with optical kerning turned on.

Output: download links for the 4 wordmark PNGs and 1 palette swatch PNG. Under a 'Palette' heading, list 5 colours with HEX and RGB values. Include at least one dark and one light colour with strong contrast for buttons, and make sure the colours work with white. Under each option (A, B, C, D), give a short spec line.

Inputs (paste below):
Brand name: [BRAND NAME]
Domain: [DOMAIN]
Brand positioning: [POSITIONING 1: label and one sentence] [POSITIONING 2: label and one sentence] [POSITIONING 3: label and one sentence]
Mini story: [SHORT MINI STORY PARAGRAPH DESCRIBING THE PRODUCTS, THE CUSTOMER FEELING AND THE BRAND PROMISE]
  • The four wordmark style options in the logo prompt:
Option Style
A lowercase (small letters)
B Title Case (a capital at the start of each word)
C UPPERCASE, bold

Any generated option that shows vertical jitter (letters bouncing up and down) or per-letter nudging (moving single letters by hand) must be regenerated before export.
  • Wordmark export specification: PNG (a picture file), transparent background (no colour behind the logo), 3000 x 1200 px (pixels), text centred with even padding (equal empty space) on all sides.

  • Logo export checklist for wordmarks and favicons:
    1. Use a single straight baseline with no per-letter vertical shifts.
    2. Keep default kerning and even tracking, with no manual glyph (single letter shape) nudging.
    3. Make the canvas 3000 x 1200 px, centered, with at least 12% clear margin (empty space) on all sides.
    4. Check that no letters overlap, there are no unwanted ligatures (two letters joined into one shape), and the mark stays clean at small sizes.
    5. Export transparent PNGs for wordmarks and favicons.

Icon development with AI

4 min
  • Run the icon or logo brainstorm (a session where you generate ideas) in a chat that already holds the brand details, such as the name, target customer and style words. The AI then has the brand context and returns concepts that fit the brand instead of generic symbols.
  • Suggest icon ideas with this prompt:
Suggest several icon ideas that could stand for [BRAND NAME]. For each one, explain its shape, the feeling it conveys, and where it would appear, such as hang tags, waistbands, packaging, an app icon, or embroidered details. Keep the look consistent with [BRAND STYLE WORDS, e.g. soft, minimal, luxe, neutral, sporty, playful].
  • If you want a full logo rather than a separate symbol, ask for logo concepts instead of icon concepts in the brainstorm prompt. This produces a cohesive logo design (one design that works as a unit) that already includes the brand name.
  • Choose the concept whose shape and mood match the target customer and the brand direction. A refined, luxury-leaning silhouette (outline shape) can fit a women's brand better than a more generic symbol, even if the generic one looks more striking at first.
  • Pick icons that match brand maturity (how grown-up and polished the brand looks). A sophisticated, techy or luxury brand fits abstract marks (not a picture of a real thing) or geometric marks (built from simple shapes), while cutesy, cartoon-like graphics suit a playful, girly brand. Compare each candidate with competitor logos (rival businesses' logos) in the same niche to see where it lands.
  • An abstract symbol built from three flowing lines can carry several meanings at once, such as breathability, movement and luxury. This gives the brand a story it can reference in marketing and product messaging.
  • The icon must fit the existing brand elements, such as the font and existing icons. A geometric symbol pairs well with a squared-off typeface, while a playful rounded font suits a more cartoonish mark.
  • Judge an icon by how it looks at small sizes, such as a profile picture circle or an app icon, not only at full size. These are the places where the symbol appears alone, so it must stay recognizable there.
  • Manual icon method:
    1. Decide on the exact icon idea you have in mind before you open the chat.
    2. Draw the logo on a sheet of paper. You do not need artistic skill, but expect several tries.
    3. Take a clear photo of the sketch.
    4. Drag and drop the photo into the ChatGPT chat.
    5. Ask ChatGPT to turn the sketch into the icon.
    6. Compare the AI result with the sketch and refine it if needed.
  • Sketch-to-icon prompt:
Can you turn this sketch into the icon? Keep the exact outline, proportions and key details of my drawing, and turn it into a clean, simple shape that works as a logo.
  • AI image generation can ignore details described in the text, such as a leg silhouette, and return a plain letter shape instead. For a specific shape, upload a sketch so the AI follows your drawing rather than a written description.
  • Smoothing prompt for a sketch-based icon:
Smooth this icon so the edges are clean and even. Then make it even smoother and more minimalistic, keeping the core shape and proportions unchanged.
  • Logo pattern in use, from a case:
    • Starting point: a women's luxury-leaning fleece (warm, soft clothing) and activewear (workout clothing) brand had an original stylized logo that was judged not versatile enough for everyday brand use. A mockup (test picture) on an Instagram profile showed the wordmark fit poorly where a compact icon would fit well.
    • Step 1: ChatGPT brainstormed icon concepts, including a curved C with a leg silhouette, a spiral C, a flame C, a soft mountain, a crescent moon, a monogram (letters combined into one symbol) and a wave C. The curved C with leg silhouette was the first favorite, and it was generated as concept 1.
    • Step 2: Concept 1 ignored the leg shape and came out as a plain C. The team drew the leg by hand, photographed the sketch, and uploaded it so ChatGPT could turn it into an icon. The sketched leg icon was more recognizable but its first AI version was rough.
    • Step 3: The team went back to an earlier message and branched the conversation (started a new side path from that message) to generate the mountain concept as concept 6. The mountain concept was judged fine but without much personality. The crescent moon concept 9 was rejected, and the monogram concept 8 looked like a copyright symbol.
    • Step 4: The leg sketch was refined by asking for smoothing and a more minimalistic look, which made it cleaner. The wave C concept 4 was judged abstract, luxurious and suitable as a profile icon, with three lines that could suggest breathability, movement and luxury.
    • Step 5: The wave C was chosen over the leg icon. The leg icon was seen as more cutesy and playful, while the brand was judged more mature and techy, and the geometric wave C paired well with the existing geometric icon and squared-off font.
    • Step 6: ChatGPT regenerated the chosen icon as a transparent PNG, which was then vectorized (turned into a vector file) in Kittl (an online design tool) and tested by scaling up (making it bigger).
    • Step 7: Variations (versions) were built in a grid of icon, wordmark, stacked (text one above another) and left-aligned logos, each in black, white and color, plus black and white text over a color icon. The stacked logo used center alignment and a larger text than symbol.
    • Result: all variations were exported (saved) at 3x size (three times normal size) as PNG and SVG files, and screenshots were collected on a FigJam board (an online whiteboard). The next planned step was a branding deck (a slide file of your brand rules).

Icon shape and style patterns

1 min
  • Alignment means how the parts line up, such as centered, left or right. A curved C icon (the symbol alone) looks strongest when centered or left-aligned. Other alignments, such as right-aligned, are possible.
  • Choose the alignment that suits the shape of the icon.

Variations

1 min
  • Build a logo variation grid in Kittl (an online design tool). Rows are layouts. Columns are colour versions.
  • A wordmark is a logo made of text only. A stacked logo has the symbol and text one above the other. A left-aligned logo has them side by side, with the symbol on the left.
Layout Black White Color Black/Color White/Color
Icon black icon white icon color icon not used not used
Wordmark (text only) black text white text color text not used not used
Stacked (center aligned) black white color black text, color icon white text, color icon
Left aligned black white color black text, color icon white text, color icon

Black/Color means black text with a color icon, and White/Color means white text with a color icon.

  • Plan colour versions from the start. A colour icon with black text, or with white text, is very common. Many backgrounds need it. You will not see that need until you try to use the logo.
  • Choose a logo colour version for each background type. Use a light version for light backgrounds and a dark version for dark backgrounds. A colour that works on white often needs to be lightened to work on black.
  • The minimum set of logo versions to launch a brand is one light, one dark and one social media profile version.
  • In a stacked logo, make the text larger than the symbol for a more balanced look.
  • Align the stacked and left-aligned versions by eye. Optical alignment means judging how it looks, not following strict alignment tricks. This usually gives the best result.
  • Test versatility, meaning how well the logo works in many places. Check it on each background and in each context before finalizing.

Formats and files

2 min
  • A transparent PNG (a common picture file type) has no background. It can be placed on any colour or photo. A normal image with a white box cannot be used on top of other designs or surfaces.
  • A raster file, such as PNG, is made of tiny dots called pixels. When enlarged, it becomes pixelated (blocky) and blurry. So it fails on large uses such as building signs, vehicle graphics and billboards. Any logo used at large sizes needs a vector version, which is made of lines and curves and scales cleanly.
  • Use PNG files with a transparent background for logos and favicons. JPG files do not support transparency. They show a white box around logos placed on a dark background. Only PNG and JPG files are generally needed for a Shopify site. Shopify is the online store software. **
  • Export the logo variations:
    1. Click each variation in the group.
    2. Choose Quick Export (the fast save option).
    3. Pick the format: PNG for the transparent raster version, SVG for the vector version. SVG is a vector file type.
    4. Set the size to 3x, meaning three times the normal size.
    5. Click Export.
    6. Repeat for every variation in every group.
    7. Take a screenshot (a picture of the screen) of the finished variations and add them to a FigJam board for review. FigJam is an online whiteboard.
  • Name the downloaded logo files as [brandname]wordmark[A-D].png for the wordmarks, [brandname]favicon[A-D].png for the favicons and [brandname]_palette_swatch.png for the palette, all in lowercase. A palette swatch is a colour sample. On a Mac, these files land in the Downloads folder, where they can be checked in Finder (the Mac file browser) before uploading.
  • Prepare the store logo for Shopify:
    1. Open the transparent PNG logo in PicMonkey (a simple online photo editor).
    2. Use Crop canvas (cut the image edge) to remove the extra white space around the logo.
    3. Use Resize to set the width to about 500 px, keeping the logo rectangular. px means pixels.
    4. Download as PNG.
    5. Upload it under the header logo and checkout logo in Shopify. **
  • In the Shopify admin (the store's back office), the logo and the wide homepage header image can be downloaded from Content > Files. Open each file and click its download button. The wide header image is a separate file from a square image, so pick the wide version for the Facebook cover photo.

Favicons

1 min
  • A favicon is the small icon shown in the browser tab. A store without one looks unfinished. Create one favicon per wordmark option: a PNG showing the first letter of the brand name.
  • A site without a favicon shows a default blank globe icon. Use an element of the logo so the browser tab looks polished and recognizable. **
  • Favicon image size: the Shopify Favicon field asks for a 32 x 32 px PNG. The older method resized the favicon to 40 x 40 px. Use 32 x 32 px where the Shopify field specifies it. The favicon should be a square PNG with a transparent background that represents the brand. **
  • Create a favicon in Shopify with PicMonkey: **
    1. Open the logo PNG with a transparent background.
    2. Use Crop canvas to make a square around one element of the logo, such as an icon, and click Apply.
    3. Click Resize, enter 40 in both width and height in pixels, and click Apply.
    4. Click Download, keep the PNG file type, and save the file.
    5. In Shopify Theme settings (where store design options are) under Favicon, click Select Image, upload the file, and select it.
  • Theme settings > Favicon provides several colour variants of the favicon image in the Files list. Pick the variant that matches the logo colours, so the browser tab icon stays consistent with the brand.

Social profile versions

1 min
  • For social media profile pictures, the logo should be square with a lot of empty space around it. This lets it fit the circular or square crop (the shape the app cuts the picture into) without being cut off. A simpler, centered version is usually better for this use.
  • A square profile version and a wide header version serve different places. A header is the top banner. The profile uses the square version. The Facebook cover photo uses the wide header version from Content > Files.
  • Mock up the logo on the real profile before deciding. A mock-up is a preview placed on the real thing. A wordmark can fit poorly in a small profile circle, while a compact icon fits well.
  • Judge the profile version at small sizes, such as a profile picture circle. The symbol appears alone there, so it must stay recognizable.

Logo don'ts

1 min
  • Avoid busy, cartoonish or sports-team style logos. They look childish and unprofessional. A logo that looks amateur undermines trust in the store from the first impression.
  • Do not use a busy or heavily decorated template, even if it catches the eye first.
  • Do not over-engineer the logo. When in doubt, choose the simpler option.
  • Do not spend much time on logo design at the start.
  • Do not write the logo in lowercase where the brand expects capital letters. A logo written in lowercase can look careless. (Lowercase is a valid option where it is the brand's chosen style, such as option A in the wordmark prompt.)
  • Do not repeat the same element inside a logo. It is distracting and weakens the mark. A monogram (a logo made from brand initials) can also look like an existing copyright sign. Check how it reads before using it.
  • Do not make the symbol larger than the text in a stacked logo. It often looks wonky (uneven).
  • Do not leave colour versions until later. Plan them from the start.
  • Do not expect AI to follow a written description of a specific shape. Upload a sketch instead.
  • Do not include the domain extension, such as .com, in the wordmark text. A domain extension is the ending of a web address.
  • Do not use a circular logo in a header-bound layout, meaning a layout built around a top banner. It is difficult to size and display properly. **
  • Do not add a tagline (a short slogan) unless it genuinely resonates with the target market's values or positioning. Positioning is how the brand is placed against others. **
  • Do not use a raster PNG at large sizes such as signs, vehicle graphics or billboards. Use a vector version.
  • Do not place a normal image with a white box on top of other designs or surfaces. Use a transparent PNG.

Further details

1 min
  • Premium brands often use a single clean mark or wordmark with no extra decoration. This signals luxury. A serif is a letter style with small strokes at the ends of the letters.
  • To apply this, pick one clear element, such as a monogram or a single elegant serif wordmark. Leave out ornaments.
  • Example 1: a black serif wordmark with one small letter-based symbol on a plain background.
  • Example 2: a white minimal monogram of the brand's initials in a thin circle.

Playbook › 8. Brand and positioning

Localization, expansion and exit value

Section 8.23 · 23 of 24 in this chapter · 9 min read

In short

  • Test a product with English pages in your current store first. Move to a new language only if it makes profit.
  • For each new language, use a separate store on a matching domain, such as.de for Germany.
  • Keep each store focused on one niche (one narrow product type), and build a real brand.
  • Selling the store for $1 to $1.5 million is the big money, not monthly profit.

Localizing (adapting to the local language and culture) for a new language market

1 min

Recommended procedure for entering a market with a different language:

  1. Test the product with English-language pages in the existing store (your current online shop).
  2. If results are profitable (you make money), create a separate new store on a country-specific domain (a web address for one country, such as.de for Germany).
  3. Set the shop's default language to the target language, so the checkout (the payment steps), header (top of every page), footer (bottom of every page), and all pages display natively (in the local language, as locals expect).
  4. Use a Shopify store (an online shop platform) at 1 dollar (new account) with a domain like yourdomain.de.
  5. Separate stores enable full localization and typically yield higher conversion (the share of visitors who buy) than single-store multi-language pages (one store showing several languages).
  • Many Germans speak English.
  • If a product sells profitably with English pages, it will sell better with German pages.
  • Why: localization builds confidence and more finished checkouts (buyers who complete payment).
  • For German-speaking markets (Germany, Austria, German-speaking Switzerland), offer the website in German.
  • German customers prefer shopping in their native language (their mother tongue).
  • They have more confidence buying on German-language sites than on English ones.
  • You have two options: a language converter (a tool that translates the same store) or a separate store for each language.
  • Separate stores allow language-specific optimization (tuning each store for its language) and better conversion rates.
  • Shoot product photos in recognizable local places.
  • Use the words and spelling locals use on the store.
  • Both build trust with buyers in the target market.
  • Copying a proven competitor's product page and creatives (the ads, pictures and videos used to sell) in a new market rarely works.
  • In one France test, a copied page brought only one sale.
  • Instead, research local brands.
  • Then build your own images, your own creatives, and a full native translation.

Expanding to a second country

1 min
  • Expand a store to a second country (for example Canada) only after the first market is working well.
  • Duplicating ad campaigns (groups of paid ads) for each market is a lot of extra work.
  • The US market alone has room for growth.
  • A store running as a niche store (a store focused on one narrow type of product) with a strong brand can still be tested with international markets.
  • Review the brand each time a new market is added.
  • Branding a product with a logo adds cost and requires bulk inventory (buying large amounts of stock up front).
  • Brand only once the product is proven.

Domains and separate stores per market

1 min
  • A second store domain should be nearly identical to the first.
  • Add a small extra part, such as the word "shop" or the suffix "co" (an ending added to a name), so customers recognize it as the same brand.
  • This makes the backup store easy to switch to.
  • A winning product should get a domain name that reflects its niche, such as a pet name for a pet product.
  • Do not use a generic store name.
  • Once it shows consistent profitability (steady profit) for one to one-and-a-half weeks, turn the store into a niche store with more products in the same niche.
  • When a product works, buy a domain name that matches the product's niche.
  • For example, use a pet-related name for a pet product.
  • Then build a niche store.
  • Add more products in the same niche over time.
  • Rebrand (give the store a new name and look) to a niche store only after the product shows consistent profitability for about one to one and a half weeks.

Multi-store branding

1 min
  • Each store can have its own brand name and domain.
  • Localize the branding to the market language, such as German or English.
  • Keep products in the same store when they are related to the niche. **
  • Put unrelated product categories into a separate store. **
  • A store can be pivoted (changed) to a different product type within the same niche without renaming it or building a new store. **

Niche expansion and product sets

1 min
  • Build 3-5 complementary products (items that go well together) in the same niche or category.
  • Do not jump to unrelated niches.
  • This enables cross-promotion (selling one product to people who bought another).
  • Example: customers who buy your first product (e.g., resistance bands, stretch bands for exercise) see "frequently bought together" suggestions (a box showing items others often buy with it) for your second product (e.g., an ab wheel, an exercise tool for the stomach).
  • This speeds up organic ranking (showing higher in search results for free) without additional ad spend (extra money on ads).
  • Adding similar niche categories builds a broader niche and authority (trust as an expert) in one theme, such as silk products.
  • The brand then attracts a wider set of customers than a single product would.
  • After one product gains traction (starts selling well), add similar products from the same category that fit the same audience.
  • Building a set of related items in one niche is a practical path from a single product toward a brand.
  • Consider a niche where current products are not enough and average order value (AOV, the average amount one buyer spends per order) is about $300. **
  • Add higher-priced products within the niche rather than starting a new store. **

Procedure for building a product set:

  1. Pick products that belong to one niche, not random categories.
  2. Choose a set of items that a single audience buys together, for example resistance bands, yoga mats and ab wheels in Sports and Outdoors (a product category on Amazon, a large online marketplace).
  3. Use this shared audience to cross-promote, for example by emailing a customer who bought resistance bands about an ab wheel.

Building a brand across platforms

1 min
  • Build a brand across multiple platforms, not only on Amazon.
  • A brand is a recognizable experience.
  • So the seller should also run, in the same niche: a Shopify store, a Facebook group, an email list (a list of people's email addresses for messages), and subscription upsells (extra offers that turn buyers into repeat subscribers).
  • A recognizable brand protects the business from new competitors who try to enter the market.
  • It also diversifies revenue (spreads income over several sources), because income from Shopify, eBay (another online marketplace) or subscriptions adds to Amazon sales.
  • A community is valued more than a count of social followers.
  • A closed group (a private group for members only) of about 10,000 engaged members (active people who interact) adds value at sale.
  • An email list with open rates above 70 percent (more than 70 out of 100 emails are opened) adds value at sale.
  • Both add more value at sale than cheap likes, which can be bought in bulk (in large amounts).
  • Paid social likes are cheap, for example about 1,000 likes for about $5 on freelance marketplaces (websites where you hire freelancers).
  • Buyers acquiring a brand do not pay for them.
  • Engaged email subscribers and members of a closed community carry much more weight in a brand's sale value.
  • Specialist stores can build assets buyers value.
  • Examples: a Facebook group, an email list, and a push notification list (messages sent straight to a phone or browser).
  • These assets raise valuation (the price a buyer would pay) because they show an existing audience.
  • Local agency positioning (how you present a service business that works for other companies in one place) is built on being the best-known helper in one city.
  • This makes the brand recognized by many businesses there before scaling abroad (growing into other countries).

Brand value at sale

1 min
  • A store brand has value beyond its net profit (profit after all costs are paid).
  • A store brand is worth more when the site is sold.
  • It must be built as a brand, not just a logo, a domain name or a tagline (a short slogan).
  • Start by defining the unique selling proposition (USP, the one reason customers should pick you).
  • It explains why your store is different from competitors.
  • A brand built for resale or long-term value is run differently from a dropshipping store (a store where a supplier ships products directly to the buyer, so you hold no stock).
  • Its priority is audience and brand awareness (how many people know the name).
  • So it may run at break-even (no profit and no loss) for the first year or two while building customers.
  • Buyers of online stores often avoid pure dropshipping stores because their revenue is seen as volatile (jumping up and down).
  • The store has no unique product of its own.
  • To make a store more sellable, add private-label elements (your own brand put on a product) such as your own logo and labels.
  • Ideally, create your own product variant (a changed version that only you sell).

Exit value (the price you get when you sell) and the long-term goal

1 min
  • Build your store and choose products with the long-term goal of transitioning to a branded store and selling the business.
  • A branded store has significantly higher exit value than a commodity dropshipping operation (a store selling generic products anyone can sell).
  • A successful branded Shopify store typically sells for $1 to $1.5 million.
  • Example: a store with $2.6 million in total revenue (all sales money together) had an estimated sale value of $1 to $1.5 million.
  • Monthly profit from actively running a branded Shopify store is typically around $10,000.
  • Life-changing money comes from selling the store for $1 to $1.5 million, not from monthly operations.

Marketplace listings and broker sales (a broker is a middleman who sells a business for you)

1 min
  • On auction platforms (websites where items go to the highest bidder), your listing (your advert for the store) appears among many scam or low-quality sites.
  • That positioning pushes the multiple (the number a buyer multiplies your profit by to set a price) down.
  • Public listings reveal your domain, niche, keywords (words people type into search) and traffic (how many visitors you get).
  • This invites copycats (people who copy your store) who can rebuild the site.
  • Sites without a strong moat (an advantage that is hard to copy), such as a fledgling dropshipping store (a new, small one), are the most exposed.
  • Brokers keep the URL (web address) and keywords private.
  • Brokers share them only with buyers who pay a deposit (money paid up front).
  • This reduces copycats and keeps the marketplace premium (high-value).

Monetizing the brand through an ecosystem (a set of connected stores and offers)

1 min
  • Use tracking pixels (small pieces of code that record what visitors do) and email to keep visitors inside your own ecosystem.
  • This lets each visitor be sent to related stores and offers.
  • Without this, a visitor who leaves after one purchase is lost to the store.
  • This is the web ring idea (a group of linked websites that send visitors to each other).
  • Diversified revenue (income from several sources), from Shopify, eBay or subscriptions, adds to the income from a single marketplace.
  • This strengthens the brand's value as an asset.

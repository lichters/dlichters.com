Playbook › 8. Brand and positioning

Packaging, inserts and customization levels

Section 8.19 · 19 of 24 in this chapter · 9 min read

In short

  • A plain box is fine for a short test, but a long-term brand needs a planned unboxing.
  • Wait two weeks of steady profit before you pay for custom boxes.
  • Start with cheap steps: thank-you cards and fake mockup pictures. Move to real boxes once a product wins.
  • Put your logo on the product itself only after two to three months of steady profit.
  • Never show a branded box in pictures while you ship a plain one. That mismatch is risky.

Why the unboxing matters

2 min
  • The unboxing is the moment the customer opens the parcel.
  • It is the customer's first physical contact with the brand.
  • A generic supplier box makes a store look like a temporary dropshipping operation.
  • Dropshipping means a supplier (the company that makes and ships the product) sends the order for you, so you hold no stock.
  • For a short test of a few weeks, plain packaging is acceptable.
  • For a long-term brand, design the customer experience on purpose.
  • Customers pay for the whole experience, not only the product.
  • Better packaging can raise the repeat purchase rate, meaning how often customers buy again.
  • It can also build a loyal customer base at relatively low cost.
  • Packaging is the physical touchpoint, meaning each point where the customer meets the brand.
  • Continue the look and voice of your website and photos on the packaging.
  • A branded unboxing strengthens the brand.
  • Use the same seasonal or emotional message on the packaging as on the website.
  • Then the physical product and the online store feel like one brand.
  • Small surprises make customers more likely to enjoy the purchase and return.
  • Examples: an unexpected extra item, or a thank-you gift in the parcel.
  • Build surprise into the unboxing experience.
  • A full packaged presentation has four parts: a box, a personalized message, a unique product insert (a card or item placed inside the box), and possibly a free gift.
  • At unboxing, the customer first connects with the brand emotionally.
  • This can lead to higher brand loyalty.
  • Personalized packaging, inserts and notes are small changes that matter for customer loyalty.
  • A store that launches new products to a loyal base can slowly become a brand.
  • Brand presentation matters more than price for differentiating a dropshipping product.
  • A custom box can remove most generic competitors from the comparison.
  • Marketing angles that set your store apart from competitors help build the brand early.
  • Small brand elements count too, such as custom boxes and thank-you cards.
  • Every touchpoint, from store to delivered parcel, shapes the customer experience.
  • Custom branded packaging, such as a bag with your logo, is a low-cost way to make unboxing feel more premium than a plain cardboard box.

When to start customizing

1 min
  • Branding has four levels.
  • Level one is front-end branding: the look customers see first, such as custom images, videos and ads.
  • Level two is store customization.
  • Level three is packaging, such as custom boxes and thank-you cards.
  • Level four is an omnichannel presence, meaning your brand on several platforms at once.
  • Add each level as the product proves itself.
  • The mentor raises prices after branding.
  • For dropshipping, wait at least two weeks of consistent, profitable results for a product.
  • Only then invest in custom packaging.
  • Work on one clear winner product at a time.
  • Do not customize many products at once.
  • A brand that manufactures or owns its own product should start customization from day one to build trust.
  • Do not ship a branded product as dropshipping.
  • Doing so removes the chance to make the customer feel the owner's brand at unboxing.
  • That reduces word of mouth, meaning customers recommending you to others.
  • Early on, plain unbranded packaging caused no customer complaints.
  • So the priority is to secure the first sales.
  • Once a supplier is in place, move to branded packaging with your logo.
  • Full branded customization, with the logo printed on the product itself, needs at least two to three months of consistent profitable sales before you invest.

The customization ladder by effort and budget

1 min

The order below runs from least to most effort and budget.

  1. Thank-you cards and tags: minimal cost and effort.
  2. Custom box designs shown on the website as mockups (fake pictures of the finished product), without building real boxes.
  3. Real custom boxes, once a product is a clear winner.
  4. Product customization with the brand logo on the product itself. This requires at least two to three months of consistent profitable sales before investing.

Once thank-you cards or custom boxes are in place and the product is profitable, the next level is a custom manual, branded cards and better-quality boxes.

Thank-you notes and branded packaging

1 min
  • Brand the packaging with the logo.
  • Include a thank-you note from the owner.
  • This creates a better experience and drives word of mouth.
  • Use Placeit, an online mockup tool, or a similar tool, to find packaging that matches the product's size.
  • Create the design on that template.
  • Choose a box that fits the product. An oversized box for a small product makes customers doubt that the product is real.
  • Unify branding can be done on a supplier's packaging template.
  • Create the design in Canva (an online design tool) or Photoshop (an image editing program).
  • The cost is 1 to 3 dollars per product.
  • A custom box design from Fiverr (a marketplace where you hire freelancers) costs $5 to $10.
  • A supplier can print it for about $0.50 per box.
  • So 100 boxes cost about $50.

Custom manuals

1 min
  • Supplier manuals often have poor English and no branding.
  • A custom manual teaches the customer about product features and about the brand.
  • It stays with the customer for a long time, which builds lasting trust.
  • A custom manual costs about the same as thank-you cards or tags.

Mockups and synthetic branding

1 min

Minimal-investment branding procedure

1 min
  1. Create design mockups or design images of custom boxes.
  2. Show them on the website.
  3. Ship in simple plain boxes at the start.

This increases the store's credibility without the cost of real custom packaging.

Synthetic branding risk

1 min
  • Synthetic branding means showing a product in a branded box while the delivered item is unbranded.
  • This is risky.
  • A stated disclaimer (a note saying the picture may differ) does not remove the mismatch.
  • The approach described here was to start with synthetic branding and switch to real packaging once sales succeed.

Reconciling the two positions

1 min
  • The mockup-only stage (level 2 of the ladder) is presented as a credible early step.
  • It is a bridge: start with mockups, ship plain, then switch to real packaging once sales succeed.
  • The risk flagged for synthetic branding applies when the delivered item looks different from what the customer was shown.
  • Keep this stage short.
  • Move to real packaging once sales succeed.

Packaging copy and inserts

1 min
  • Pair two opposite ideas across the package, such as how it feels inside and how it looks outside.
  • This creates a clever twist that stays on brand.
  • Two examples: "Warm to open, sharp to look at" and "Plain from the door, cozy once it is open."
  • Place the main playful line on the front of the packaging.
  • Place a second line on the side or back. This keeps the front clean and gives the packaging more depth.

Influencer shipping with branded packaging

1 min

An influencer is a social media creator with many followers. Procedure:

  1. Ask the supplier or agent for the size of the box.
  2. Hire a designer on Fiverr to design a custom box and obtain a printable file.
  3. Print one branded box at a local print shop.
  4. Pack the product in the box, and add a handwritten card and a small gift such as chocolate.
  5. Ship it quickly by post, since fast delivery matters to influencers.
  • Use the custom box and branded packaging in creator content (videos or posts made by creators).
  • Viewers then imagine the brand and the service, not only the product.

Authorized retailers and brand-shipped orders

1 min
  • An authorized retailer is a store officially allowed to sell a brand's products.
  • You do not need your store name on the shipping box.
  • The brand ships the order, and the customer expects the brand's name.
  • Do not include your own invoice in the package, since the brand does not either.
  • A branded postcard with your store name or marketing message is optional.
  • It is only worth doing at scale, when you are one of the brand's top sellers.
  • Its purpose should be marketing, such as a mailing list signup, a coupon, a free gift, or a refer-a-friend offer (a reward for recommending you to others).
  • Its purpose is not just brand name visibility.

Customizing books and other products

1 min

Deciding whether to customize

1 min
  • For book products, customizing the cover and inner content is strongly recommended once the product has been validated (proven to sell).
  • It gives the book a distinct, personal identity.
  • Customization is optional if the goal is only quick testing.
  • Customize a product only when it has a specific pain point, a problem its buyers care about.
  • Most books without a clear pain point are weak candidates for building a brand.
  • Before investing, check whether the product can support a brand.
  • Also check whether the pain point is real.
  • Customizing a product that only sells on a short spike (a sudden burst of sales) does not build a lasting store.
  • Customized versions build longer-lasting brand equity, meaning the value of your name in customers' minds.
  • This is a long-term positioning approach, not a pump-and-dump (a quick sales spike followed by a fall).
  • The benefit depends on actually customizing the product.

Why customizing separates you from competitors

1 min
  • A customized book separates you from the many sellers who run the same generic version.
  • This is the main competitive advantage of customizing.
  • Unchanged copies compete head-to-head with every other seller of that version.
  • Customizing turns a found product into your own version instead of a repackaged template.
  • A customized listing is harder for competitors to copy directly.

Case: from proof of concept to custom brand

1 min
  • A proof of concept is a first test that shows a product can sell.
  • The source's own book product started with a proof of concept found in Kalodata data (a tool that tracks product sales data).
  • The product was tested first.
  • It was only rebranded after it showed it could sell.

Cover and content changes

1 min
  • Changing only the cover colour and one title phrase can leave a customized book looking almost identical to the original listing.
  • Add further differences before judging whether the cover stands apart, such as wording variations and decorative elements.
  • Keep an accent element in a colour that contrasts with the cover. An accent is a small detail that stands out.
  • Do not match a colour that is already on the cover, such as red accents on a red cover.
  • Matching colours in that way can look odd on the finished product.
  • Decorative additions look more authentic when they are hand-drawn and imperfect rather than professionally polished.
  • A raw look makes the book seem personally written.
  • Example: a gut-health book with a cure-focused title can be retitled toward a beginner audience, such as mothers, using a "101" style introduction (a simple beginner-level opening).
  • This shifts the angle while keeping the same underlying book.
  • Example: shifting a blue cover to a darker blue shade creates a visible variation.
  • A full recolour of the cover is also an option if a bigger change is wanted.
  • AI image edits (pictures changed by artificial intelligence) can change parts of the image that were not requested, such as the colour of an unrelated element.
  • Check every returned image against the original.
  • Send a narrower follow-up request to restore the unwanted change.

Upselling and cross-selling on calls

Section 21.12 · 12 of 26 in this chapter · 2 min read

In short

  • Phone upselling is optional. A store can run well without it.
  • Give every agent the upsell planner and a written script.
  • Answer the customer's question first, then offer the related product.
  • Offer only when the product fits the buyer. Skip the offer when it does not.

Where phone upselling fits

  • Phone upselling and cross-selling can raise store profit a lot. Upselling means offering a better or bigger version of a product. Cross-selling means offering a related product.
  • It is optional. It is a different style of selling from the core approach (the main way the store sells).
  • A store with a net margin of about 30% can run without it. Net margin is the share of each sale left as profit after all costs.

Upsell planner for agents

  • Give every customer service agent the full upsell planner. Customer service agents are the people who answer customer questions.
  • The planner is the list of core products and their related upsells. A core product is the main product a customer buys.
  • The planner tells agents which products to offer with which core product.
  • If you handle service alone, the planner applies to you too.

Answer first, then offer

  • Answer the customer's question first.
  • Then offer the related upsell product.
  • Example: answer a shipping question, then mention the related product that goes with the item.

Relevance over frequency

  • Offer upsells only where they fit the customer's purchase.
  • Relevance (how well the offer fits) matters more than offering every time.
  • Skip the offer when it does not fit.

Phone and chat upsell steps

Steps for customer service agents:

  1. Answer the customer's question first.
  2. Mention the related upsell product that pairs with the core product the customer is buying.
  3. Ask whether the customer wants to add it to the order.
  4. If the customer agrees on the phone, create the checkout (the page where the customer pays) and send it. Or guide them step by step to find the product on the site, especially when a deal applies.
  5. If the customer is in online chat (live text talk on the website), send a direct link to the product page or checkout.
  6. If a discount or special offer applies, give the customer the discount code (a code that lowers the price) or apply the offer.

Agent scripts and training

  • Provide agents with a written script and instructions. The script covers when and how to offer upsells.
  • Build the upsell step into the customer service process or SOPs. SOP means standard operating procedure: the written, step-by-step routine the team follows.
  • Agents need practice before the upsell becomes a normal habit.
  • Upsells in customer service fail when agents have not been trained, or when the step is not part of the service process.
  • Practice the script with agents. Make the upsell step a standard part of their workflow.

Post-purchase calls and offers

  • A post-purchase call (a phone call after the buyer has paid) should add a helpful offer only when a better product or benefit makes sense for the buyer.
  • Calling a customer just to tell them they should have bought something else damages trust.
  • Case: a business owner with a sales background called every buyer after purchase for a few months, after selling the store.
    • The owner offered a different product, extended warranties (longer guarantees) and faster shipping over the phone.
    • The owner had previously offered these on the site.
    • The owner saw sales climb steadily during that time.
    • The approach needs a team member or owner who is comfortable on the phone. It takes little time.

Post-purchase, confirmation and review calls

Section 21.11 · 11 of 26 in this chapter · 4 min read

In short

  • Call a customer before shipping only when an order looks suspicious or needs checking.
  • Make the thank-you call 7 to 14 days after the order, and ask for a review only at the end.
  • Ask whether the customer is happy first, so unhappy customers are found before any review request.
  • If a customer is unhappy, fix the problem on the call.

Order confirmation calls

When to use an order confirmation call

  • Use a confirmation call only for orders that look suspicious, meaning they may be fake or a scam, or orders that need checking before shipping.
  • The call compares three details: the shipping destination (where the order will be sent), the billing address (the address on the customer's payment card), and the connection location (where the customer's internet came from, shown by their IP address, a number that identifies a device online). The caller then asks the customer to confirm or correct these details.

Order confirmation script

Use this script word for word. Replace each blank in square brackets with the real detail.

Hi, this is [Your name] from [Store name]. We received your order for [product] and want to confirm it is yours before we ship. Our records show it is going to [shipping state or city], the billing address is in [billing state or city], and the connection came from [IP location]. Could you confirm these details and tell me whether anything looks different from what you expected? Thank you, and we will get your order on its way once we have confirmed it.

Thank-you and satisfaction calls

Purpose and timing of the post-sale call

  • The first call is a post-sale support call, meaning help after the sale. It is not a review request. Customers find this natural. Most businesses never follow up, so the call builds goodwill (a good feeling and trust) before any request is made.
  • Schedule a post-purchase call (a call after the order) for every customer, at the same point the automated review email would go out. An automated review email is an email sent by software that asks for a review. The call usually happens 7 to 14 days after the order or after the usual delivery time. For example, if most orders arrive within 7 days, call at day 7. If the review email goes out at 2 weeks, call at 2 weeks.
  • Call new customers after purchase to thank them and confirm the order. Calls cost nothing if you do them with your own time or on commission (pay for each sale, not a fixed wage). Each call takes about 10 to 20 minutes.
  • Make an upsell (an offer of a bigger or extra product) only when a product truly fits the customer's purchase.

Goals of the review call

  • A personal thank-you.
  • Measuring how happy the customer is with the business and product.
  • Resolving any problems.
  • Securing at least a text review (a written review).
  • Requesting a photo of the customer with the product.
  • Requesting a video review via VideoPeel (a tool for collecting video reviews).

Structure of a post-sale call

  1. Open by asking questions about whether the customer is getting the value they expected from the product and whether anything is wrong.
  2. Answer any questions the customer has.
  3. At the end of the call, ask the customer to leave a review.

Post-purchase call script

Use this script. Replace each placeholder (a blank in square brackets) with the real detail.

Hello [first name], this is [your name] from [store name]. Thank you for your business. I wanted to let you know that we have your order [order number] confirmed, and it usually ships within 48 hours. Here is what to expect: you will get tracking updates by email as soon as it ships. If you have any questions, you can call us at [phone number], email [email address], or use live chat on our site between 9 and 5. We appreciate you, and we are here to support you.
  • If the customer does not answer, leave a voicemail (a recorded message on their phone) with the same thank-you message and the same contact details. Do not pitch a product (try to sell one) in the voicemail.

Use the script as a guide

  • Use a call script as a guide to the content only. Callers should speak in their own natural words instead of reading the script word for word.

Review call procedure

Step-by-step review call

  1. Call the customer at the scheduled point after the order (usually 7 to 14 days).
  2. Open with a personal thank-you for the order. Say the call is a check-in to make sure everything arrived fine.
  3. Ask whether the customer is happy with the product.
  4. If the customer is satisfied, explain that reviews help the business serve customers better. Then ask whether they would leave a quick two-minute review.
  5. If the customer is unhappy, listen and try to resolve the problem on the call. A resolved issue often produces a stronger review.
  6. Request a text review at minimum.
  7. Offer a video review (via VideoPeel) and/or a photo of the customer with the product.
  8. Close the call and follow up with the email sequence (a series of emails sent one after another) that goes with the script.

Finding unhappy customers

  • Callers must find out when a customer is unhappy, because customers often will not say so themselves.
  • A problem that gets resolved on the call can turn into a more credible five-star review (the top rating) that mentions the good service.
  • Ask whether the customer is happy with the product before asking for a review, so unhappy customers are identified first.

Starting new staff on calls

  • Do not start new customer service staff on live calls straight away if the product is complex or technical.
  • Start new staff with outbound scripted calls (calls the business makes, following a script) to existing customers. This lets them learn the product and the customer's language first.

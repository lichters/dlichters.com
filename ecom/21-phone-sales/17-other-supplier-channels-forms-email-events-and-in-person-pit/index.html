Other supplier channels: forms, email, events and in-person pitches

Section 21.17 · 17 of 26 in this chapter · 3 min read

In short

  • If a supplier has an online dealer form, submit it first. Then wait 24 hours and call if you hear nothing.
  • If you have a phone number, call first. Talking directly works better than email.
  • Be honest that your store is new. Explain how you will bring sales, and say that customer service comes first.
  • Keep the talk about how you will bring the supplier more sales, not about the money you expect to make.
  • This advice is old (2018 to 2022). Check it against current supplier practice before you use it.

Online dealer forms and follow-up

Older advice, 2021-2022 and 2022. Check it against current supplier practice before use.

  1. Submit the online form on the supplier's website. An online dealer form is the sign-up form a supplier uses to approve shops that resell their goods.
  2. Wait 24 hours.
  3. If you get no response, make a phone call.

When you call to check on a form you sent (this is called following up), use this opening:

I'm John from Oz Off Grid. I submitted your online application form about a day ago and wanted to reach out to follow up and discuss it with your team.

Start supplier contact with the online dealer application form when it is available.

  • Call during free time, when you are not working.
  • The online form takes less time and can be filled in during the evening.

Form or phone first: when each applies

These two sources give different first steps. Both are older (2021-2022). Use the phone when a number is available. Use the form when calls are not possible.

Situation First step Source
Online form available, you can call in free time Form first, then call after 24 hours if no response
Phone number available Call first, because talking directly works better than email
Calls not possible Fill out the online form
  • Call a supplier. Do not only send the online form.
  • A call lets you explain your store, ask for the dealer application (the approval request for shops), and get things moving quickly.

Introduction emails

When you first contact a supplier by email, a short introduction is enough. It can get you passed to the right contact. Being passed on like this is called a referral. Your email works if it:

  • Names your store.
  • Says you want to sell their products.
  • Asks for the person who opens accounts for internet retailers.

Pitching in person or on a call

A pitch is your short sales talk. Older source, 2018. Follow these steps:

  1. Think about two things first. What does the supplier care about? How can they trust you?
  2. Be honest about where your store is. You can say you are learning from a training program and are just getting started.
  3. Explain the marketing you will use to bring them sales:
    • Google Product Listing Ads (paid shopping ads that show your product photo and price).
    • Retargeting (showing ads again to people who already visited your store).
    • Exit-intent capture (a pop-up that asks for an email as a visitor is about to leave).
    • A follow-up email sequence (a set of automatic emails sent over several days).
    • YouTube and industry-specific platforms (websites made for one business field).
  4. Make clear that customer service is your first priority. Say you are building a long-term business.
  5. Keep the focus on how you will bring the supplier more sales, not on the money you expect to make.

Use this opening for a first meeting, adapted to your situation:

I appreciate you meeting with me. I am [your name] and I am starting an online store focused on [niche]. I am still early in the process and I want to be honest about that. I am learning e-commerce from [training program or mentor name], and I plan to bring you more sales by using [traffic channel 1] and [traffic channel 2] to reach new customers. Customer service is my first priority, and I want to build a business that customers come back to for years.

Trade events

A trade event is a fair where suppliers and shops meet. Older source, 2020. Follow these steps:

  1. Attend supplier trade events and meet the representatives (people who speak for the supplier) in person.
  2. Explain why your store is a good fit for what they sell.
  3. Ask questions about their products.
  4. Find the person who can make the final yes or no (the approval decision).
  5. Follow up with a short email after the conversation.

Competitor calls

A competitor is a rival business that sells similar things. Calling one by phone is a quick way to check whether it has a showroom (a place where people can see products in person) or a store.

  • Ask for the opening hours.
  • Ask whether a visit is possible.

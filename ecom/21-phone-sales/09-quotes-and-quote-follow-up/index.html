Quotes and quote follow-up

Section 21.9 · 9 of 26 in this chapter · 5 min read

In short

  • Call a new quote request within three minutes. Speed makes a strong first impression.
  • Use the same five steps on every expensive custom order call.
  • Ask each product type its own questions, such as space for installation.
  • Put the customer name and item in every card title.
  • Follow up on every quote on a set date. One team let $70,000 in quotes go cold.

Handling quote requests

A call within three minutes of the form submission (the moment the customer sends the online form) makes a strong impression. Speed and a personal touch set you apart from slower competitors.

Use this opening script as an example:

Hi, this is [name] from [company]. I saw your request for [specific need-e.g., playground setup]. We're working on a quote and will follow up later today. Let me confirm your contact details.

For high-value custom orders (expensive products made to the customer's specification), follow this call sequence:

  1. Introduce yourself and your company source.
  2. Acknowledge (say you saw) the specific need they asked for, showing you read their request.
  3. Confirm the action you are taking for them.
  4. Verify their contact information (how to reach them) for follow-up.
  5. Express enthusiasm about working together.

This builds credibility (trust that you know your job), separates you from generic outreach (mass contact that is not personal), and increases conversion (the share of people who buy) on B2B (business-to-business) and custom product quotes.

B2B prospects (possible business buyers) often send requests to several vendors (sellers) at once to compare pricing. Set up Zapier (a tool that links apps so they act automatically) to send immediate Slack alerts (messages in the Slack team chat app) when Wufoo or Typeform quote requests arrive. Wufoo and Typeform are online form tools. Then call the prospect within minutes. The first vendor to respond with a qualified quote (an offer sent to a checked, serious buyer) typically wins the order.

Product-specific questions before quoting

Before quoting or selling certain products, ask for the details that the product category (a group of similar products) requires. Do not use one generic set. Examples include:

  • Whether the customer has space to install the item.
  • A photo of that space.
  • The customer's zip code (postal code).
  • Any regulation (legal rule) that applies to the product.

Keep a separate question list for each product category or brand, so your staff (team) do not need to remember them.

Quote task cards

When a call or chat asks for a quote, handle the pending task (the job not yet done) as follows. A card is a task note on your board.

  1. Create a card with the customer name and the item, both together, so cards are easy to find.
  2. Record the contact information, the product, any shipping needs (such as inside delivery, where the item is carried into the house, or installation), and the shipping address.
  3. Send the request to the manufacturer (the company that makes the product).
  4. When the manufacturer replies, move the card to Quote Received and note the extra cost, for example that inside delivery costs $100.
  5. Send the customer the quote, move the card to Quote Sent, and attach a screenshot (a picture of your screen) or copy of the invoice (the bill) or email.

Creating several cards for the same item made it hard to see which customer each card belonged to. Always record both the customer name and the item name in the card title. Without this, staff must open each card to find the right customer.

Follow-up sequence after every quote

Run this sequence after every quote, with a due date set on each step:

When Action Units
Next day Check that the customer received the quote email.
After 3 days Send the warranty information for the product (the guarantee that it will be fixed or replaced for a set time).
After 1 week Send a message explaining why to buy from you, such as financing (paying in parts over time), price matching (matching a cheaper competitor's price) and other benefits.
After 1 month Offer a free consultation with an expert to check whether the product fits the customer's needs.

Give each follow-up message a real benefit, such as warranty details, reasons to buy, or a free expert consultation. Do not only ask whether the customer has decided. Many quotes stay open because the customer is missing information or budget options, so each new message should add value to the conversation.

Follow-up email templates

Day after the quote. Adjust the wording as needed:

Hi [customer's title and last name], this is [your name] from [company name]. I wanted to check whether you got the quote request you made for [item name]. If there is anything else we can help you with, just let us know. Best regards, [your name].

Three days after the quote. Send this so the customer receives useful information rather than just a request for a reply:

Hi [customer's title and last name], we are attaching the warranty details for your [product name]. You will find them [attached or listed below]. If there is anything further we can do for you, please tell us. Best regards, [your name].

Change the warranty details to match each product or brand.

One week after the quote. Use this to outline why buying from your company makes sense:

Hello [customer name], this is [your name] from [company name]. Many customers pick us for [product name] for these reasons: [list your financing options, price match policy, support, and other advantages]. Let us know if there is anything else we can assist with. Warm regards, [your name].

One month after the quote. Use this to offer help rather than pressure:

Hi [customer name], this is [your name] from [company name]. Would you like a consultation with an expert to see whether [product name] fits your needs? If you prefer a lower-priced option that fits your budget, we can also look at alternatives with you. Kind regards, [your name].

Recovering open quotes

Following up by hand and now and then (manually and sporadically) left a large amount of quoted business unclosed (offers never accepted or finished). At one point, there were about $70,000 in open quotes. Quotes were sent once and then forgotten when staff did not remember to call back. A dated follow-up system fixes this, because each card shows its next due date.

Prospect sheets and short codes

Record every prospect (possible buyer) who does not buy right away in a prospect sheet (a list of possible buyers, such as a spreadsheet). This way, no lead (possible customer) slips through. In the notes, use short codes (short letter labels) such as PH for phone and EM for email. Also note the product sought and the reason for not buying.

Playbook › 21. Phone sales

Supplier follow-up cadence and record keeping

Section 21.16 · 16 of 26 in this chapter · 5 min read

In short

  • Call first if the supplier has a phone number. A call gets an answer faster than email.
  • Keep following up. Many suppliers only reply after several tries. Do not stop after one missed call or email.
  • Write down every call. Note the date, the person you spoke to, and what they said.
  • Keep going until you get a clear yes or no.

Day-by-day contact plan

  • Start on day one with a phone call, if the supplier has a phone number. A supplier is the company that sells the products you want.
  • Calls got a yes faster than waiting for email replies.
  • The day one call has two goals.
  • First goal: find out the supplier's application process, meaning the steps to be approved as a seller.
  • Second goal: identify the decision maker, meaning the person who makes the final yes or no.
  • Day three is another phone call.
  • The full sequence runs to day seven. It is a one-week follow-up plan that mixes calls and emails.
  • Keep following up with the plan until you get a clear yes or no.
  • You can stop early if you reach the right person on the first call.
  • Do not stop after one unanswered call or email.
  • Suppliers often respond only after repeated follow-ups, so continue through the full sequence.

Call first, then email: sequencing

  • Call the phone number listed for the supplier first.
  • Then send a follow-up email that confirms what you discussed.
  • When a supplier does not answer your emails, call instead. A phone call is the most reliable way to be heard and to get a point of contact, meaning the person you talk to from now on.
  • Older advice (2019).
  • Procedure for an email that went unanswered:
    1. Call the supplier.
    2. Mention the earlier email and roughly when you sent it.
    3. Follow the call script in the Follow-up scripts section below.
  • Keep following up by phone and leave voicemails. A voicemail is a recorded message left when nobody picks up. Many brands return calls later.
  • If a pitch script gets no results, make it specific. A pitch script is the set of words you use to sell your idea on a call.
  • Explain how you will drive sales for the brand, meaning how you will bring buyers to its products.
  • Explain how you will handle its customer service, meaning how you will answer buyer questions and problems.

Repeated calls for unanswered suppliers

  • Procedure for a supplier that has not replied:
    1. Keep calling if nobody answers. A persistent caller usually reaches someone.
    2. If there is no reply after a few days, send a follow-up in the same email thread. An email thread is the chain of messages in one conversation, so the messages stack up in one place.
    3. If there is still no response, find supplier relations staff on LinkedIn. LinkedIn is a professional social network. Supplier relations staff are the people who handle supplier contacts. Contact them there.
  • Older advice (2022).
  • When suppliers do not answer calls, keep following up.
  • Suppliers with limited stock, meaning the products they have available, may later accept new dealers. A dealer is an approved reseller of the brand.
  • So upload approved products to your store while contact continues. Upload means putting the product listings online.
  • Older advice (2022).
  • Supplier approvals often need repeated calls, because companies get busy.
  • Keep following up by phone.
  • Log each conversation's notes in a tracking sheet. A tracking sheet is a spreadsheet where you record your contacts.
  • Older advice (2021).

Follow-up scripts

Follow-up call for a supplier that has not replied

Hi, this is [your name] from [your store name]. I sent an email yesterday to the dealer contact at [supplier domain] about becoming a retailer for [brand name], and I just wanted to make sure you received it. Would you have a few minutes to talk about how we could work together and what the next steps are for approval?

Follow-up call for a supplier that said it is too busy

Hi [Supplier contact name], this is [your name] from [store name]. We spoke [timeframe] ago and you asked me to follow up. Our store launched on [date] and we are now showing up for the main keywords in [niche] on Google Shopping. We are selling for [brand A], [brand B] and [brand C], and business is going well. We would like to add you to our lineup so you get this exposure too. When would be a good time to talk?

Older advice (2022).

Follow-up call on an application

Hi [supplier contact name], this is [your name] from [store name] calling about your application. I wanted to follow up on [previous step, e.g. the application sent on date]. We have [new progress, e.g. grown traffic and a larger email list] and are excited to list your products. What do I need to do next to move forward, and when can we get started?

Older advice (2021).

Hesitant suppliers and callback dates

  • Procedure for a supplier who is hesitant but has not said no:
    1. Do not push for an immediate decision.
    2. Ask them to think it over for a few days.
    3. Agree on a specific callback day and say you will call then.
    4. Send a follow-up email that confirms the conversation and includes your contact details.
    5. Note the callback date in your supplier sheet, a list of your suppliers, and in your calendar, your date planner.
    6. Call back on that date. If they ask you to end the call, end it respectfully and move on.
  • Older advice (2021-22).
  • Persistence pays.
  • A supplier who believes you will call back is more likely to decide.
  • A follow-up email keeps you in front of them.
  • Always make the callback you promised.
  • Older advice (2021-22).

Final offers and follow-up dates

  • When a supplier declines, make a polite final offer and ask for a follow-up date.
  • Recording that date in your notes gives you a reason to return with results.
  • Older advice (2021).

Notes and tracking sheets

  • Keep notes during or right after every supplier call.
  • You will make many calls, and you cannot remember what each supplier said.
  • Calling back to ask again makes you look unprofessional.
  • Older advice (2021-22).
  • Procedure for each supplier call:
    1. Record the date of your request call.
    2. Record what they said about supplying their products, their pricing and anything else specific to them, during or right after the call.
    3. Record the name of the person you spoke to. This person becomes your contact.
    4. Record any direct email, direct line or mobile number they give you for follow-up.
    5. Mark the supplier as Approved (Yes) or not (No).
    6. Move on to the next supplier.
  • Older advice (2021-22).
  • Log each conversation's notes in a tracking sheet.
  • Older advice (2021).

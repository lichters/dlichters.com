Playbook › 21. Phone sales

Staffing, hiring and delegating phone coverage

Section 21.23 · 23 of 26 in this chapter · 4 min read

In short

  • Answer the phone yourself first, so you learn how calls work before you pay anyone else.
  • Missed calls mean lost sales. Pay for phone cover only if calls bring in money.
  • Hire your first helper once the business is profitable (it earns more than it spends), not before.
  • Test every new phone hire by calling your own business at least a couple of times per week.

Decide when to answer the phone yourself and when to hire

  • Answer calls yourself first so you learn them. Then reinvest profits (money left after costs) into hiring someone to take over. Do not outsource (hand work to outside people) phone sales before the business is profitable.
  • Missed calls from customers mean lost sales. Whoever answers needs a set schedule (fixed working hours) and a quiet place to take calls. If that person is running errands, they carry a notebook to capture leads (potential customers' names and contact details).
  • Customer service calls can interrupt the owner about every 10 minutes, which makes focused work (uninterrupted concentration) almost impossible.
  • Whether you need coverage (someone to answer for you) depends on whether the calls make money:
Situation Action
Calls from customers turn into revenue (money coming in) Have someone answer the phone while you are away.
Calls do not generate revenue You do not need to cover them.

Timing the first hire

Sources give different triggers (events that tell you when to hire). Use the one that matches your situation:

Trigger Source Applies when
The site has traffic (visitors come to your website) Older, 2019 You need someone to cover live chat (a chat box where visitors type to you in real time) and phone calls during hours you cannot. The cost should be recovered quickly from the sales they handle.
The business is profitable Older, 2018; older, 2020 You are handing phone sales over to someone else, with profits funding the hire.
  • Phone support must still be answered while you travel. If you work from another time zone (a different clock time than your customers), hire a virtual assistant (a remote helper who works from their own location) rather than leave calls unanswered, because sales drop when calls go unanswered.
  • The first hire is usually a customer service assistant (a person who answers customers' questions and problems).

Virtual assistants and remote coverage

  • Once the business is profitable, a virtual assistant (remote helper) can handle customer phone calls so the owner is not tied to the phone while travelling.
  • For short absences, such as a week away, have a friend or family member answer by forwarding calls (sending incoming calls to their phone).
  • For ongoing phone coverage, hire a full-time person through onlinejobs.ph, a job website for workers in the Philippines.

Who to hire for phone work

  • Phone support staff should sound natural to the local market (the customers' area). When selling in the United States, for example, they should have no accent, because an accent can lower the conversion rate (the share of callers who actually buy). Email and live chat do not carry this accent problem, so the person's location matters less for those channels.
  • Phone staff are usually more expensive than staff who handle email, live chat and order processing (preparing orders to ship), especially at the start. That is why the two roles are often separated when you start out, although one person can do both.

Vetting and interview tests

Vetting means checking candidates before you hire them.

Hiring and vetting a phone-support virtual assistant:

  1. Post a job on onlinejobs.ph for someone with phone and email sales or support experience.
  2. Review resumes (CVs) and prefer candidates with experience at large, well-run companies who are still employed there and looking for a better opportunity.
  3. Test the hire by calling your own business at least a couple of times per week, from a withheld number (a hidden caller ID) and at unusual hours. Confirm they answer and handle the call correctly.

Interview checks for the first hire:

  • Use a five-minute interview test. A call that ends in five minutes or less shows no rapport (a friendly, trusting connection), and the candidate is unlikely to build rapport with customers.
  • Reject candidates who are hard to understand on a call. They will be the voice of the business to customers every day, so poor spoken English or unclear speech is a reason to remove them from the list.

Trial and pay structure

Pay structure for new phone or chat staff:

Stage Pay structure
Month 1 One-month trial.
After the trial Add commission on sales (a share of each sale they make).
Once they prove reliable Base salary (fixed pay) plus incentives (bonus pay for good results).

Daily performance tracking

Tracking a phone and live chat team:

  1. Log in each night and check how many calls came in and how many were answered.
  2. Check the average response time (how long a customer waits for a reply) for live chats.
  3. Review the call logs (the records of every call) in Mighty Call (a business phone app) to confirm calls are answered and handled properly.
  4. Confirm the quality of the work matches the standard you expect from the hire.

Outsourcing outbound work

  • For B2B (business-to-business, selling to other companies) businesses, outbound sales calls (calls you start to other companies) and LinkedIn lead generation (finding potential client companies on LinkedIn, a professional networking site) can be outsourced. Delegate them once the business can afford it.

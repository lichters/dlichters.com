Running the supplier call

Section 21.14 · 14 of 26 in this chapter · 8 min read

In short

  • Write a short script and plan the main points. Do not memorize exact words.
  • Call the supplier and ask for the person who handles online retailers.
  • Never say "dropshipping" to a supplier. Say "direct-to-customer" instead.
  • Give a launch date about one month away. Never go past two months.
  • Ask for the product data first. Accept any way they send it.
This section has 13 parts. Tap one to open it.

Before the call

1 min
  • Prepare a short script before your first call.
  • A script is a few planned notes you can follow. Having one makes the call feel less scary.
  • Keep the first call simple.
    • Pick up the phone.
    • Introduce yourself and your business.
    • Build a little rapport. Rapport means a friendly connection.
    • Ask whether you can sell their products through your website.
  • Plan the framework, not the exact words. A framework is the set of parts your call follows.
  • Know the purpose of each part: introduction, planned launch, reason for choosing them, and request for the application process (how you apply to sell their products).
  • Say each part in your own words on every call.
  • Suppliers are busy.
  • E-commerce means selling online. Suppliers get more requests as e-commerce grows.
  • Your call must be ready before you dial.

Reaching the right person

2 min
  • Call the supplier using the phone number on their website. This starts the approval conversation.
  • Use this first-contact procedure:
    1. Introduce yourself briefly. Say you want the person who handles working with online retailers.
    2. Wait for their reply.
    3. If they are not that person, ask who you should speak to. In very small companies, the owner often answers and is the right contact.
  • When calling a supplier for an account or referral, mention your existing supplier relationship. An account is a business profile that lets you buy from them. A referral is when someone recommends you.
  • Ask for the specific contact who handles approvals.
  • Use this procedure for account requests:
    1. Call the supplier's phone line. Ask for the person who handles new retail or dealer accounts. A dealer is a seller who resells their products.
    2. Introduce yourself and your business. Say you have a website built for their products.
    3. Ask whether you can sell their products through your website. Ask whether they drop-ship to customers. Drop-ship means they ship each order straight to the buyer, not to you.
    4. Ask for the online dealer application or product information. Note the name and the instructions they give.
    5. Send a follow-up email after the call to confirm the conversation.
    6. Record the date and outcome in your tracker. A tracker is your list of contacts.
  • If you do not reach a decision maker (the person who can say yes), ask for a sales representative (a salesperson).
  • Keep the call short.
  • Make sure your message is clear each time.
  • Handle a gatekeeper (the person who screens calls) this way:
    1. State briefly that you want to talk about becoming a retailer or reseller for their products. A reseller buys and sells their products.
    2. Let them direct you to the right person, such as the sales contact who handles new retailer requests.
    3. If that person is not available, ask for their mobile number.
    4. If you reach the right person, let them tell you what they need next, and respond to that.
    5. Do not give a long pitch to a gatekeeper. A pitch is your sales speech. They cannot approve you.
  • Supplier approval calls should explain why your niche fits their brand. A niche is a small group of buyers with a shared interest.
  • Ask about their products.
  • Ask who handles approvals.

Opening the call

1 min

Use this opening script. A placeholder is a word in square brackets that you replace with your own detail. Fill in the placeholders:

Hi, it's [Your name] calling from [Your company name]. How are you?
[Wait for their reply, then continue:] We are an online retailer of [your niche or product category]. We have looked at your products, we like them, and we would like to talk with you about becoming a retailer for them.
[Stop talking and let them respond. Do not fill the silence.]

Explaining the model without the word dropshipping

1 min
  • Avoid the word dropshipping on calls. Some people have negative views of it.
  • Describe the model (how your business works) in plain terms. Use direct-to-customer or warehouse-direct-to-customer.
  • Some suppliers avoid working with dropshippers because of bad experiences.
  • Do not use the term in the first contact.
  • Present yourself as an additional sales channel (a way to sell products) that brings new customers.
  • Use this model explanation. Fill in the placeholders:
Say: Look, we're an online-only retailer and we work on a direct-to-customer basis. If you are at your computer, you can have a quick look at [your website] while we talk. Here is how it works: we build and maintain our website, upload your products to it, handle all the marketing and customer service, and generate the sales. When we make a sale, we send you an order for that product, we pay for it upfront, and you ship it directly from your warehouse to the customer.
[Stop talking after this and let them respond.]
  • Spelling out the model prevents confusion.
  • It shows you will not buy in bulk. Buying in bulk means buying many units at once to store them.
  • It sets the basis for your request.
  • Mention upfront payment in that explanation. Upfront payment means you pay before they ship.
  • This lowers the supplier's risk right away. Risk means the chance of loss.
  • A zero-risk offer (one where the supplier loses nothing) is the easiest way to win agreement.
  • The same call approach works in any country. It is not specific to one market.

Stating the launch date

1 min

Sources give slightly different windows. A window is the time range. Use the table to choose:

Source Recommended window (time range to aim for) Upper limit (latest allowed) Note
Source A, pitching a new store Roughly two to four weeks away, or up to about one month Never more than two months A longer timeline makes the supplier treat the store as not relevant right now
Source B, opening call About one month away None stated Launching a few days earlier or later is fine
Source A, script guidance A few weeks to about one month ahead Never more than two months Applies to the window the supplier should expect
  • Use a real launch date, not a vague one. A launch is the day the store opens to customers.
  • Use the window in the table above.
  • The shorter windows fit most calls.
  • The two-month ceiling is the hard limit. Never go past it.
  • If the store is almost ready, say it is in its final touches before launch. Final touches means the last small fixes.
  • Do not say it is in beta testing. Beta testing means early trial use by real people.
  • Present the store's future potential, not its current results. Potential means what it could become.
  • Do not present sales or achievements the store does not yet have.

Positioning a store with no business history

1 min
  • If this is your first store, say so honestly.
  • Then explain the niche opportunity, the search demand (how many people search for the product online), the brand quality, and why you want to sell that supplier's products.
  • Use the pronoun I when you run the business alone. A pronoun is a word like I or we.
  • Instead of "we," you can use whatever you are comfortable with.
  • Do not claim a team you do not have.

Checking US distribution

1 min

US distribution means how the supplier gets their products to buyers in the United States. Use this script for a supplier with unknown US distribution. Replace the brackets and keep the question about shipping:

Hello, my name is [name] from [store name]. I noticed your products are listed on many US websites. Do you have distribution in the United States, a warehouse, or a fulfillment partner, and how does your shipping to US customers work?

Building rapport

1 min
  • Ask casual questions about the brand's history, goals, and best products.
  • These questions build a relationship and give you data (facts and numbers) for your marketing.
  • Build a relationship rather than pitching only price.

Making the first request

1 min
  • Your only request at this point is the product data.
  • Product data means the price list, product images, and any useful technical specifications. Technical specifications are detailed facts, such as size or weight.
  • Ask how soon they can send it to you.
  • Accept any delivery method for the product data. Examples: a shared cloud folder link (online storage you open with a link), emailed files, a login to a dealer portal (their website for sellers), or downloading images from their website.
  • Confirm you will get access.
  • Do not insist on a format (a specific file type).

Tone

1 min
  • Sound genuinely excited.
  • A flat or disinterested tone makes the supplier less likely to approve you.
  • Do not overdo it.
  • Stay calm, friendly, and respectful even if the supplier does not say what you hoped.
  • Treat them with the same courtesy you would expect yourself.

Brevity

1 min
  • Get to the point quickly. Say as little as needed.
  • Long rambling calls and long emails waste the supplier's time and weaken your chances.
  • Keep the sales call short, concise, and tight.
  • Follow the framework: introduce yourself and your company, then state which brand you want to carry. To carry means to sell in your store.
  • When calling suppliers, keep the call short.
  • Make sure your message is clear each time.

Using scripts as guides

1 min
  • Scripts are guidelines, not text to read aloud.
  • Use the same key points on every call, phrased naturally.
  • Reading a supplier script word for word sounds unnatural and makes the call feel scripted.
  • Use the same framework in your own words instead of reading a call script word for word. Word-for-word reading sounds robotic to suppliers.
  • Use the script as a guide, not word for word.
  • Cover the same key points concisely.
  • Let the supplier do most of the talking, hear what they need, and then respond to it.
  • Use the supplier call script as a guide and speak naturally.
  • Keep the questions consistent across calls.

After a yes

1 min

Follow this procedure:

  1. Thank them and confirm when they will send the product data.
  2. Exchange contact details (name, phone, email) if not already recorded.
  3. Record the outcome in your supplier sheet (your spreadsheet of suppliers).
  4. Follow up if the data does not arrive by the date they promised.
  5. Move on to the next call.

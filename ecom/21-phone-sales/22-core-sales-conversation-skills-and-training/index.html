Playbook › 21. Phone sales

Core sales conversation skills and training

Section 21.22 · 22 of 26 in this chapter · 6 min read

In short

  • Ask questions first, then present your offer, then close by doing the math for the buyer.
  • Show the highest price first, then the cheaper options. Going the other way lets the first low number set the buyer's sense of value.
  • Build trust before you make an offer. Trust makes buying far more likely.
  • Stay calm and do not look desperate. Buyers feel pressure when you seem to need the sale.
  • Practice out loud until your prices and close come out automatically.

Conversation flow and qualification

Run the sales conversation in this order

  1. Get the prospect's attention first (a prospect is a possible buyer). Nothing else works without it.
  2. Find out about the prospect. Ask these questions:
    • Which provider or solution do they use now (the company or product they use today)?
    • Do they like it?
    • What is missing?
    • Do they care most about price, quality, or having the best option?
  3. Present a custom offer (a deal shaped to their needs) that matches what you learned. Walk through the options from highest to lowest.
  4. Close (get the buyer to say yes) by doing the math for them. Start with the highest figure that fits the argument.

Qualify prospects (check who is a good fit to buy)

  • Start with people who are unhappy with their current solution.
  • If they are satisfied, look for one missing feature that could matter to them.
  • If nothing matters to them, move on.

Build know, like and trust first (the buyer must know you, like you and trust you)

  • Build know, like and trust before presenting an offer.
  • Help people first.
  • Learn about them, then move to the offer.
  • Trust makes buyers far more likely to purchase.
  • Move the prospect to a second location during the conversation.
  • This makes the talk feel longer and builds trust faster.
  • Use this when it fits the context.
  • A sales call or customer chat should always end with a clear offer or next step.

Price reveal order

Reveal prices top-down (from the highest option to the cheapest)

  • Reveal prices in a fixed order, from the top option down, one step at a time.
  • Showing the cheapest option first removes the chance to anchor on the top price.
  • To anchor means letting the first number you see set your sense of value.
  • Showing the highest price first creates a moment of concern that the buyer cannot afford it.
  • Showing cheaper options afterward releases that tension.
  • This makes the buyer more willing to commit (agree to buy).

Doing the math for the buyer

  • Do the math for the buyer instead of expecting them to calculate.
  • Lay out logically why the purchase makes sense.
  • Use figures that are believable and tied to their situation.
  • Use the following closing template (a ready-made sentence pattern). Fill in the placeholders (the blanks in square brackets) with real figures:
So you pay [ONE-TIME COST] once and add [MONTHLY GAIN] to your bottom line every month. Is that worth it for you?

An extra [MONTHLY GAIN] a month adds up to [ANNUAL GAIN] over a year. If someone offered you [ONE-TIME COST] today in exchange for [ANNUAL GAIN] at the end of the year, what would you say?

Objections (reasons a buyer gives for saying no)

Anticipate objections (raise them yourself before the buyer does)

  • Top closers (the best sellers at getting a yes) anticipate objections before they are raised.
  • They address them openly.
  • Name the real weaknesses of a product, framed honestly.
  • This keeps trust and prevents later doubt.

Handle objections in person

  1. List every objection the prospect raises on a pad (a notepad) before answering any.
  2. Keep asking if there is anything else, until the prospect has no more objections.
  3. Answer each objection.
  4. Ask a conditional closing question (a question where the yes depends on solving their concerns). An example is asking whether the prospect will go ahead if all these concerns are resolved.
  5. Once the prospect agrees, the close is in place.
  • A printed sales letter (print copy) cannot copy live feedback, the back-and-forth of a real talk.
  • So the letter must answer the full list in advance.

Emotional buyers

  • Some buyers, such as passionate sports fans, are much less sensitive to price for products tied to their team or interests.
  • Identify the emotional attachment (a strong feeling for a team, cause or interest) first.
  • Build the offer around it.
  • Sales psychology (how people feel and decide when they buy) matters.
  • Understanding emotions and impulses (sudden urges to buy) is the core of online selling.
  • Studying human behaviour helps a store owner predict how a customer will respond.
  • Customers, not payment processors (the companies that handle card payments) or platforms, pay the business.
  • So the owner must understand how people think and decide to buy.
  • Understanding persuasion principles (rules of how people can be convinced) helps predict what people will spend money on.

Composure and not appearing desperate

  • Sales conversations work best when the seller is not desperate for the deal.
  • The hardest deal is the one you visibly need.
  • Taking a position of not needing the deal removes pressure.
  • This keeps buyers engaged.

Training and role-play drills

Train sales skills in this order

  1. Role-play different customer types (act out a sale with a partner), each with a different objection.
  2. Drill the pricing sequence and numbers (repeat them until they come out in the right order without thinking).
  3. Keep a fixed close and offer block on printed cards for field use (cards you carry when selling in person).
  4. Improve the opening and the middle of each pitch (your sales talk). This is where creativity matters most.

Reference case: a commission-based field seller (paid per sale, selling in person)

  • Starting point: a new salesperson sold TV subscriptions face to face.
  • He earned about $125 per installation (the TV service set up in the home).
  • Commission (pay per sale) was paid only when the service was installed.
  • The first week was used to learn a four-step process.
  • In week two, he moved from a busy discount warehouse to a quieter electronics store.
  • There, meeting buyers in aisles allowed him to build a relationship before pitching.
  • He asked about current providers.
  • He identified what buyers wanted.
  • He presented options from the highest price down.
  • He closed with a clear calculation.
  • Results in one week (units are items sold; installed means set up):
Salesperson Units sold Units installed
Colleague 10 5
Case salesperson 7 7
  • By week three he was making more sales and more installs than everyone else in the office.
  • He sold only what matched his promises.
  • This kept customers from buyer's remorse (regret after buying).
  • He later left after four weeks because the schedule was too demanding.

Books and learning by doing

  • Read recommended books on sales and persuasion.
  • Dale Carnegie's How to Win Friends and Influence People is recommended for handling clients.
  • Alex Hormozi's 100 Million Offers is recommended for building offers.
  • Robert Cialdini's Influence is recommended for learning persuasion principles.
  • Time spent in calls and real-world conversations is the most effective way to learn sales.
  • The mistakes you make in calls show what to do better next time.

Written sales presentations (sales letters)

  • Sell in print the way you sell in person.
  • A sales letter is a sales presentation on paper.
  • Write it as if speaking to one prospect, sitting at a kitchen table or on a living room floor.
  • Invite an immediate phone call (a call right away).
  • Tie it to a specific time the reader can act on before the deadline (the last date to act).
  • A time-limited request removes the delay of a letter's slow reply.
  • Formula (a pattern to copy): invite an immediate phone call tied to a specific time that the reader can act on before the deadline.
  • Examples:
    • "Call me before noon today, and I can have your order confirmed and the first shipment booked for tomorrow."
    • "Call the office before 5 this afternoon, and we will hold the discounted rate for your team until Friday."

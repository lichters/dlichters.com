Support resources and miscellaneous notes

Section 21.25 · 25 of 26 in this chapter · 3 min read

In short

  • A failed test does not mean a product is dead. Check the competitor first.
  • Start small. A basic Facebook ad can test for 5 pounds a day.
  • Shipping, supplier quality and customer service decide success. Broken deliveries can lose sales.
  • For beginners, native ads are a helper, not a main channel.
  • Pick partners with different skills and real experience with failure. Avoid anyone who wants a quick win.

Testing products and offers

  • Do not conclude that a product is dead just because a competitor is growing sales with it while your test fails. A test is a small trial to see if something sells.
  • Before you conclude anything, check the competitor's pixel data (tracking data from a small code on their store that shows what visitors do), when they first entered the product, their targeting (who they show ads to), their product page and their creatives (the ad images and videos).
  • Clothing brand and product tests should use cost-effective approaches first, meaning methods that give the most result for the money.
  • A basic Facebook ad (a paid post on Facebook) can start as low as five pounds a day to test quickly.

Case: baby gyro ball

  • Starting point: A dropshipper (a seller who does not stock products, and the supplier ships each order) found a baby gyro ball (a spinning ball toy for babies) through Facebook keyword research (finding the words people type into search).
  • Starting point: The product solved a problem and sat in the evergreen baby niche (a small market that stays in demand all year).
  • Starting point: It sold for about 25 USD.
  • Action: The product was marketed on Facebook.
  • Action: Sales began arriving in a steady stream once the ad worked.
  • Result: The store generated 50,000 USD in just over a month.
  • Lesson: Logistics (shipping and delivery), supplier quality and customer service were critical.
  • Lesson: Fragile items arrived broken, so the suppliers (the companies that send the products) had to be changed.

Native ad networks

  • Native ad networks (ad platforms that style ads to look like the content around them), such as Taboola, have few public tutorials.
  • Agencies (outside firms that run ads for clients) often claim the platform is very hard to master.
  • Some brands do well with native ads.
  • Beginners without a tested process should treat native ads as a supplement (an extra source), not a main channel.

Creative formats and attention metrics

  • Meme-style video ads (funny, viral-style clips) reached a 60 percent attention rate (the share of viewers who kept watching).
  • The same ads had a 4 percent click-through rate (the share of viewers who clicked).
  • They showed no direct sales.
  • They were kept running because Google Ads (Google's paid ads) results improved after they went live.
  • This suggested they fed other channels, meaning they helped other ads work better.

Presenter-led video

  • A presenter-led product tutorial (a how-to video with a person on screen) can raise conversion (the share of visitors who buy).
  • The same tutorial can also be a course built from AI-generated videos (videos made by AI tools).
  • The buyer sees the product explained by a face and voice.
  • The same approach works for a niche product (a small market) such as pet health.
  • A how-to video shows how to use the product before purchase.

Coaching calls and private mentorship

  • Coaching calls (live calls with a coach) are held on the 1st Wednesday of every month.
  • Start time: 2 PM Eastern time.

Booking a coaching call:

  1. In the members area (the private area for members), move your mouse over Activate.
  2. Select Activate Coaching.
  3. Book one of the available times.

Private mentorship (one-on-one guidance from a mentor):

  • Private mentorship is available through the members area.
  • If mentorship is not included with your membership, click Get Help and select Private Mentorship to enroll (sign up).

Partner choice

  • Do not partner with someone who has the same skills as you.
  • Do not partner with someone who lacks experience with failure.
  • Do not partner with someone who wants a quick win (a fast, easy result).
  • Each of these mismatches tends to end the partnership when sales fall short.

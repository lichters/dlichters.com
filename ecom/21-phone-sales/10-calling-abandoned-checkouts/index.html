Calling abandoned checkouts

Section 21.10 · 10 of 26 in this chapter · 5 min read

In short

  • Call a lost customer within minutes, not 30 minutes later. Speed makes the sale far more likely.
  • Ask for the customer's phone number at checkout. Without it, you cannot call.
  • Record every call in the sheet and color each row by the result.
  • Call every abandoned checkout as soon as possible, except on Sundays.
  • After each call, leave a voicemail and send a text. Keep following up for about a week.

Why speed decides the outcome

  • Reach out within minutes after a shopper stops an order, not 30 minutes later. A shopper who stops before paying is an abandoned checkout.
  • This makes contact about 100 times more likely. A lead is a possible customer. Memory and focus fade quickly.
  • Reaching out within 5 minutes, not 30, makes lead qualification about 21 times more likely. Qualification means checking whether the lead is a real buyer.
  • Fast contact lets the customer assume there was a normal checkout problem. They feel less pushed. They are more likely to finish the purchase.

Catching abandoned checkouts in real time

  • Mistake: checking abandoned carts by hand in Shopify Admin (Shopify's back office, at Orders > Abandoned Orders) every few hours or days.
  • Compared with real-time notification (an alert that arrives instantly), this delay cuts contact chance by 100 times.
  • This delay also cuts qualification chance by 21 times.
  • Principle: orders worth $2,000-$3,000 or more almost always need a phone call to close.
  • Use automation (software that runs by itself) to find these abandoned carts.
  • Send immediate alerts to the sales team.
  • Slack alert: Slack is a team chat app. A message goes to the phone team as soon as a new abandoned cart arrives, so calls can start quickly.
  • Slack alert content: customer name, products left in the cart, and phone number. **
  • Sheet: the same data also lands in the tracking sheet, a spreadsheet where the team logs results.

Capturing the phone number at checkout

  • Roughly half or more of abandoned checkouts can give a phone number. **
  • So the checkout should ask for a phone number. Capturing it is what makes the follow-up call possible. **
  • Handle abandoned checkouts in this order: **
    1. Ask for and capture (save) the shopper's phone number during checkout.
    2. Call every abandoned checkout with a phone number as soon as possible.
    3. Send the abandoned checkout email flow (an automatic series of emails) to all abandoned checkouts, including those without a phone number.
  • When you have the customer's phone number, combine email follow-up with a call or text. (2024)
  • Many carts can still be turned into sales this way. (2024)

Timing the call

  • Contact every abandoned checkout as soon as possible, ideally straight away, by phone. **
  • The call aims to find and fix the problem that stopped the sale. **
  • Call customers who abandoned checkout to ask why they did not finish the order, except on Sundays. **

Working each row (phone team routine)

For each abandoned cart row (one line in the sheet, for one abandoned cart):

  1. Call the customer. In the Notes column, record whether the person picked up, whether it was a wrong number, whether they bought, and what was said on the call.
  2. Set the row color by the call outcome, so results can be read at a glance (a quick look).
  3. If a paid order later comes in from the same email, the Recovered column fills in automatically with the purchase date.

Alternative call procedure (2022): **

  1. Receive a Slack notification showing the customer's name, the products left in the cart, and the phone number.
  2. Call from a business number, such as Mighty Call or Google Voice (phone apps that give you a second number for business calls).
  3. Say that you saw they were on the store, did not finish the order, and ask whether you can help.

Call scripts

Phone script for an unfinished checkout. Adjust it to your store, and stay warm (friendly and kind) and never pushy. **

When a customer leaves a checkout unfinished, use this phone script, adjusted to your store, and stay warm and never pushy. Greet the customer by first name, introduce yourself and your store, and say you noticed they started an order but did not complete it. Ask whether anything went wrong during checkout and whether there is something you can fix or help with. Say you would be glad to have them as a customer. If a problem came up, write down exactly what it was, then either fix it or escalate it (pass it to a manager), and confirm the result back to the customer. If no problem came up, thank them, ask whether they have questions about the product or shipping, and end the call politely. Log the outcome and the reason for the abandonment in your notes.

Opening script (2021). Two identical copies exist, and the text is unchanged. **

Hi, this is [your name] from [store name].com. We noticed you were just about to complete your order for [product name] but did not finish checking out. I just wanted to follow up quickly to see if you ran into any trouble or if there is anything I can help you with. If they are ready to buy, offer to send them a direct link back to checkout.

Message script (2024). (2024)

Hi, this is [Your name], the owner of [Store name]. I noticed you started the checkout for [product name] but did not complete your order. If you have any questions about this product, I am happy to help, so just text me back and I will take care of you.

Voicemail and text follow-up

  • Calls after abandoned checkouts are often unanswered. So pair each call with a voicemail (a recorded message left for the customer) and a later text. (2024)
  • Plan the follow-up over about a week to measure whether it recovers sales. (2024)
  • The call comes first. The voicemail and text follow after it, as the second step of the same sequence. (2024)

Tracking sheet setup

  • Color-code the rows by call result, so outcomes are visible at a glance.
  • Place the color key (the legend that explains each color) at the top of the sheet.
  • Notes column: record pickup, wrong number, purchase, and call content for each row.
  • Recovered column: fills in automatically with the purchase date when a paid order arrives from the same email.

Using the answers as feedback

  • Use customers' answers about why they left as qualitative feedback (opinions in words, not numbers) to fix the store. **
  • Log the outcome and the reason for the abandonment (leaving without buying) in the notes, so the reasons can be reviewed later. **

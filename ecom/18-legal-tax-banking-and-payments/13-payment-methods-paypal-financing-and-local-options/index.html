Payment methods, PayPal, financing and local options

Section 18.13 · 13 of 24 in this chapter · 25 min read

In short

  • Offer many payment methods, not only card payment, so fewer buyers leave at checkout.
  • Do not make PayPal your only payment account. PayPal can pay you out slowly, and buyers can ask for their money back.
  • With financing (letting buyers pay in parts), you get the full amount right away. The customer repays the financing company.
  • Test Apple Pay on a phone after setup, including through a TikTok link.
  • Turn on Shop Pay Installments only when Shopify Payments is active. Otherwise, apply directly with Klarna or Affirm.
More key points
  • Start with PayPal and credit cards. Add Klarna or Afterpay when the product costs more than $60.
  • Klarna can cost you money through disputes. Avoid it in the UK, be careful in Sweden, and start in the Netherlands or Germany.
  • Local payment methods build trust. Examples: PayPal in Germany, TWINT in Switzerland.
  • Never store customer card details. Do not give business customers 30-day payment terms. Use a finance company instead.

Payment setup and checkout options

2 min
  • Offer every common payment method, not card payment alone. More options mean fewer drop-offs (buyers who leave before paying) at checkout (the page where the buyer pays).
  • In Shopify Payments (Shopify's own built-in payment system), turn on Apple Pay (pay with a card saved on an Apple device), Google Pay (the same idea for Android phones), PayPal, PayPal Credit (a PayPal loan option for buyers) and Amazon Pay (buyers pay with their Amazon account). This lets shoppers pay with the method they prefer.
  • Show the full range of accepted methods: credit card, debit card, and express checkout (a fast button that skips typing details) with Shop Pay (Shopify's fast checkout that saves buyer details), Google Pay or PayPal.
  • Express wallets (saved payment accounts on a phone), such as Apple Pay and Google Pay, are especially useful on phones, where one tap can complete the order.
  • Amazon Pay and Shopify Payments add options for customers. They do not produce many sales on their own, but they are worth turning on.
  • A checkout guarantee (a promise shown at checkout that buying is safe) listed these accepted payment methods: Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Discover and PayPal.
  • "Accept Multiple Payment Options" was named as a resource in a December 2020 coaching call (a live advice session). This source gave no setup steps.
  • Additional providers (companies that handle payments), such as PayPal, are added under Settings > Payments > Payment configuration.
  • Fee examples differ by store. A transaction fee is the cut the payment company takes per sale.
    • In one example, PayPal charged no transaction fee, but PayPal processing fees still applied.
    • In another example store, PayPal was added with a 2% transaction fee. Its account setup had to be completed before PayPal payouts (money sent from a payment account to your bank) could be received.
  • Installment providers (companies that split a price into payments) can be added at the bottom of the checkout area, with a link to the provider's terms. Showing the option in the cart warns customers in advance that they can pay in installments at checkout.
  • Confirm that the local payment methods buyers expect are enabled before launching ads in a country. A missing payment option can lose sales even when traffic (visitors to the shop) and ad results are good.
  • One store with a single payment processor (the company that moves the money) saw conversion (the share of visitors who buy) fall to about half of its previous level. Adding PayPal raised conversion again.
  • A missing PayPal option once halved conversion for one seller.
  • Shopify Payments and Stripe (another payment company) mainly process credit and debit cards. PayPal is a separate system that customers widely recognise and trust.
  • PayPal and Shop Pay redirect customers away from the site during checkout, as Bread and Klarna do. Bread and Klarna are buy-now-pay-later (BNPL) companies, which pay the shop now and let buyers pay later. Sales then appear as referral traffic (visits sent from another site) to the payment provider, instead of being credited to the traffic source that brought the customer.

Apple Pay

1 min
  • Apple Pay usually comes with Shopify Payments once payments are set up in the store.
  • Shopify Payments or Stripe bring Apple Pay as a checkout option. If it is missing, check the payment provider settings and test a payment to confirm it works.
  • Apple Pay can still fail to appear on a TikTok link (the shop link inside TikTok) even when it is enabled, so check the link on a phone after setup.
  • Apple Pay must appear on the link itself to work for TikTok buyers. If it does not show, follow these steps:
    1. Open Shopify Settings > Payments.
    2. Switch Apple Pay off and on again.
    3. Return to the TikTok link, search the link again if needed, and place it once more.

PayPal

4 min

Role and timing

Advice on PayPal conflicts across sources, so the conditions for each view are given.

  • Most buyers pay with credit cards or Apple Pay, so PayPal is optional for a new store.
  • Avoid PayPal as a payment or payout method at the foundation stage. The reasoning given is that PayPal approves transactions slowly and can limit the account after returns or chargebacks (buyer requests to reverse a payment). Shopify Payments is the stable in-house option (run by Shopify itself).
  • Offer PayPal Express Checkout alongside Shopify Payments from the start, because some shoppers will not buy from a store without PayPal. If the business is registered in one country and sells into another, PayPal may refuse to work, so this option may not be available.
  • Offer PayPal as an additional payment option, because a share of online buyers prefer it and it adds trust.
  • PayPal can raise trust and conversion, but payouts can take weeks, funds can be held, and buyers can request chargebacks more easily than with credit cards. Weigh these risks against the conversion gain before enabling it.
  • One seller disabled PayPal because payouts were slow and chargebacks were harder to contest than card chargebacks. A few PayPal sales may be lost when deciding whether to offer it.
  • Credit card chargebacks are easier to contest than PayPal disputes (buyer complaints about a payment).
  • Some buyers will only pay through PayPal. PayPal may hold a new seller's funds for several days, and the length of that hold is not known (2025).
  • Germany requires a PayPal account for payments, since most German customers pay with PayPal.

Account structure

  • PayPal should never be the only merchant account (the account that receives card money).
  • Relying on PayPal alone is risky because accounts can be problematic. PayPal can be used as an additional payment option alongside another processor.
  • PayPal should not be the primary payment method.
  • Withdraw PayPal funds to the bank every one to two days so money is not held up.
  • A single PayPal business account can handle payments for several Shopify stores. Stripe allows separate profiles (separate setups) for different stores.
  • One PayPal account under a company name can handle payments for several Shopify stores. Stripe can use separate profiles for different stores.
  • A business PayPal account is required because customers commonly pay through PayPal, even if the owner dislikes the platform.
  • Getting a PayPal account is harder without a registered company. PayPal asks business accounts for business details once sales start. This is a common reason why beginners need a company before they can use PayPal fully.
  • Non-US owners of a US company generally struggle to get a US PayPal account. It requires a US tax ID (EIN, a US tax number for businesses) that can take about 2 to 3 months. A PayPal account from the owner's home country is the more practical choice.
  • PayPal states on a dedicated Hong Kong page that it supports dropshipping (selling products that suppliers ship directly to buyers) for businesses there. Check this policy directly on PayPal before relying on it, as rules can change.
  • PayPal financing should only be used in countries where it is available. For split payments that combine PayPal with other cards, ask support for custom payment links (links that charge one order in a set way).

Setup

  • Activating PayPal Express Checkout creates a PayPal account automatically, using the email address from the Shopify signup. PayPal emails instructions for setting up a PayPal business account and claiming funds after the first sale.
  • Complete PayPal business account setup:
    1. In Settings > Payments, click Complete account setup under PayPal Express Checkout.
    2. Enter the primary business email, country (Australia) and create a password.
    3. Select the business type.
    4. Enter business details: the business type or trust (a legal way to hold assets), the primary authorised user and the people associated with the business. Have this information ready; it takes about 5-10 minutes.
    5. Confirm the email address and any identity documents PayPal requests, such as a driver's licence or passport photo.
    6. Log in to the PayPal business back end (the account's control area).
    7. Go to Pay & get paid and link your business bank account (Banks > Link a new bank account).
  • To withdraw money from PayPal, you need to link a business bank account. A card only needs to be linked if you want PayPal to charge it when paying on other websites, and it cannot receive payouts.
  • If PayPal is missing in the payment settings, check Settings > Payments, where it should appear as an additional payment option. If it does not, use the live chat (a typed chat with a support agent) on the Shopify help page. Ask to speak to a human, who can walk you through adding it.

Payout sweeps and balance risk

  • Set up a business PayPal account under the LLC (a US company type) or EIN, and connect it to the business bank account. Then contact PayPal support via live chat and ask them to enable auto sweep. Auto sweep transfers incoming funds to the bank account daily, automatically.
  • Limiting PayPal balance risk (2023):
    1. When an order ships, mark it as shipped in PayPal and enter the tracking number (the code that follows the parcel).
    2. Do this for every order as soon as tracking numbers are available.
    3. Every night, withdraw the PayPal balance to the bank account.

Financing: how it pays the merchant

5 min

Mechanics

  • The merchant (the shop owner) receives the full payment immediately as a standard credit card transaction. Shopify handles all customer financing, installment scheduling and payment collection.
  • With third-party financing (a separate company lends to the buyer), the merchant gets paid in full right away, while the customer repays the financing provider.
  • Payments through financing options such as PayPal Pay Later (PayPal's buy-now-pay-later option) or Affirm (a buy-now-pay-later company) pay the store in full right away. The customer repays the financing provider. (2024)
  • Even when a customer pays in installments, the merchant receives the full order amount immediately, the same as with a card or Apple Pay. The customer repays the financing to Shopify, so the store does not collect or manage the installment payments.
  • With Shop Pay Installments, the merchant receives the full order amount immediately, just like a normal sale. Repayment schedule and billing are handled between the customer and Shopify, so the merchant does not chase payments or manage loans.
  • With third-party financing, the financing company approves the customer and pays the store from day one, as with a credit card. If the customer does not pay the financier (the financing company) in time, the financier handles collection, not the store.
  • Affirm, Klarna and PayPal Credit are free to set up, with no setup fee. They pay the merchant upfront and take on the customer's debt, so the merchant has no collection work.
  • Financing options such as Shop Pay Installments, Afterpay (another buy-now-pay-later company) or Klarna let customers spread payments, while the store is paid right away. This helps during inflation (prices rising fast) or a recession (an economic slowdown).
  • Customers can pay in installments through Shop Pay Installments, Klarna or Affirm. The merchant receives the full amount right away, as if the customer paid in full, while the customer pays the financing company. (2023)

When to offer financing

  • Financing can be offered on high-ticket products (expensive items).
  • Financing is not needed for low-priced products such as t-shirts. Offer it only where order values justify it. An order value is the total of one order.
  • Offering installment payments on expensive products is a good idea because it gives customers more options to pay, even though financing is not a large share of revenue.
  • Accepting financing through a credit option such as PayPal Credit can be offered on higher-priced items. It does not require a separate medical or insurance billing setup.
  • Installment and credit terms can increase sales, but they are most effective when offered to people whose good standing has been vouched for by a trusted person. Open accounts without investigation only for referred customers.
  • Apply for a financing app as part of store setup, so customers have payment-over-time options at checkout.
  • A buy-now-pay-later provider application, such as Klarna or Affirm, can be slow. Follow up by email or live chat with the business name you applied under. Check the application status in your provider control panel (the provider's account page). Apply to a second provider as a backup.
  • Shop Pay is approved instantly through Shopify Payments. It can be turned off in Settings > Payments in the Shopify admin (your store's control panel) if you do not want it. Buy-now-pay-later options such as Klarna, Afterpay or Affirm usually take about two weeks up to one to three months for approval. They can be swapped in later for higher credit limits (the most a buyer can borrow).
  • Payment expectations by price: Klarna or Afterpay works well for products above 60 dollars.
  • Show a monthly payment message on product pages with Shop Pay enabled. In the demo, orders over $50.00 can be paid in 4 interest-free installments (payments with no extra charge). A sale price of $22.50 with a compare-at price (the old price, shown crossed out) of $25.00 showed this message below the price. A shoppable video (a video with a buy button) that leads to a native checkout (built into Shopify) therefore also gets these Shop Pay installment options.

Financing and fee economics

  • Most PayPal Credit approvals are around $2,000 or less, so financing decisions should be based on the average order value (the average amount per order).
  • Affirm and Klarna perform better when the average order value is about $2,000 or more, so test them for expensive products.
  • Klarna fee on a 3,000 order at 3.5% plus a 1.00 static fee (a fixed amount per order): 3,000 × 3.5% = 105, plus 1.00 gives 106.00. With a 2,000 supplier total (the total you pay your supplier) and 30 fraud insurance (a cost that covers losses from fraud), switching that order from Shopify Payments (87.30 fee) to Klarna lowers net profit (what you keep after costs) from 882.70 to 864.00.

Insurance as payment

  • Accepting insurance as payment (the buyer's insurance pays the bill) is only worth exploring if many competitors do it, because it requires an approval process. Selling without insurance acceptance can still perform well.
  • Insurance payments are optional for some product categories.

Promotion and content

  • Financing and installments are promoted with a how-to infographic (a picture that explains steps). A prompt for building one follows. Use it only as written.
Please create an info graphic showing how to use [payment option, e.g. Shop Pay Installments] when checking out on a Shopify store. Before creating the infographic, check the current steps for using this payment option online, and show each step in order.
  • Checkout steps to show in the infographic:
    1. After adding items to the cart, proceed to checkout.
    2. Select Shop Pay at checkout.
    3. Complete the purchase using the Shop Pay installment plan.
  • A how-to infographic for a payment option such as Shop Pay Installments can be placed on the financing page, embedded in the standard email that answers customer questions about financing, and used in customer communication. Adding the store's logo and colours makes it the store's own asset.
  • Infographics about payment steps are built from steps ChatGPT (an AI chat tool) finds online. Verify each step against the store's actual checkout before publishing the infographic.

Shop Pay Installments

3 min

Basics and requirements

  • Shop Pay Installments is a native Shopify payment option (built into Shopify itself). It lets shoppers split a purchase into interest-free installments, usually two to four payments. It requires Shopify Payments and does not need a separate app.
  • Shop Pay Installments is a Shopify payment option that lets customers split a purchase into several payments at checkout. Check its eligibility rules (who and what qualifies) for your country and order value before enabling it.
  • Shop Pay Installments lets customers split a checkout total into several payments. It is available in supported regions and can help sell higher-priced products.
  • Availability depends on Shopify Payments. The installment limits have been increased over the last few years, so check the current limits in the Shopify settings.
  • Shopify Payments must already be active before Shop Pay Installments can be turned on. Without it, the payment method cannot be configured.
  • Shop Pay Installments requires Shopify Payments as the payment processor. If a store does not use Shopify Payments, the feature cannot be used. The alternative is applying directly with Klarna or Affirm, which offer similar financing.
  • Shop Pay Installments is powered by Klarna for the financing part, while the integration is built into Shopify. Once enabled, the option appears on the store and in checkout without custom code (no programming needed).
  • Shop Pay Installments is the recommended installment option instead of Klarna, which received many negative reviews. Choose it for financing options in the store. (2025)
  • Use Shop Pay Installments for financing when Shopify Payments is active, instead of adding Klarna. Without Shopify Payments, contact Klarna support directly if an application is denied for a functioning URL (a working web address). (2024)
  • Shop Pay Installments can replace Klarna for buy-now-pay-later. (2025)

Setup

  • Settings path: Shopify Admin > Settings (bottom-left corner) > Payments > Shopify Payments > Manage > Manage payment methods > Shop Pay Installments: toggle on (switch it to on).
  • Apple Pay and Google Pay should be enabled alongside Shop Pay Installments, so that shoppers have all the payment options available at checkout.
  • Setup procedure:
    1. Open the Shopify admin.
    2. Click Settings in the bottom-left corner.
    3. Click Payments.
    4. In the Shopify Payments section at the top, click Manage.
    5. Click Manage payment methods at the top of the page.
    6. Find Shop Pay Installments in the list of enabled payment methods (it was the second entry in the demo).
    7. If its toggle is off, switch it on.
    8. Confirm that Apple Pay and Google Pay are also turned on.
    9. No separate save click is needed, because the page saves automatically.
    10. Go back to a product page to confirm the installment messages appear.

Klarna

1 min
  • Klarna is a financing option suited to expensive products, with approval depending on the customer's credit. Klarna's average merchant fee was estimated at about 4.5 percent (2021). This is unverified and should be checked on the current pricing page.

  • A Canadian business selling to US customers on Klarna financing should most likely apply with the Klarna US entity (the Klarna company for the US), because the customers applying for credit are in the US. This is an informed guess rather than confirmed guidance (2022).
  • Klarna's Shopify setup, if the option does not show:
    1. Confirm that your Klarna approval is complete and the Klarna app is linked to your store.
    2. Follow Klarna's Shopify setup documentation step by step.
    3. If it still does not show in the store, contact Klarna support first.
    4. If the problem remains, use Shopify live chat and ask their team to check the Klarna setup on your store.
  • Klarna and Afterpay applications were not approved for one seller, so Affirm was recommended.
  • Klarna is expected in Germany, so the store should offer it.

Afterpay

1 min

  • Adding Afterpay to a Shopify store (Shopify is online store software):

Affirm

1 min
  • Affirm is an alternative to Klarna (a pay-later service) and Afterpay for buy-now-pay-later options (plans that let buyers pay in parts). Klarna and Afterpay applications were not approved for one seller, so Affirm was recommended.
  • Affirm, Klarna and PayPal Credit (PayPal's pay-later plan) are free to set up, with no setup fee.
  • Affirm and Klarna perform better when the average order value (the average amount one buyer spends per order) is about $2,000 or more.
  • PayPal Credit is a steady choice in the US. Affirm and Klarna are alternatives with deeper Shopify integrations (connections built into the Shopify software).

PayPal Credit

1 min
  • Offer PayPal Credit on big-ticket items (expensive products), for example above about 1,000 USD.
  • Qualifying buyers pay no interest for 6 months. The merchant receives the money at once.
  • Setup takes about 5 minutes from the PayPal account. The option should be shown clearly on product pages.
  • When PayPal is accepted, PayPal Credit can be offered as a payment option. Customers can pay over six months without interest or payments.
  • PayPal Credit was described as a US-only financing option. Confirm its availability in your country before offering it at checkout (the payment step).
  • PayPal Credit was believed to work only in the US and UK. Check whether any financing provider works in your country before promising installments (payments split over time).
  • Most PayPal Credit approvals are around $2,000 or less. Base financing decisions on the average order value.
  • In 2019 the stores offered only PayPal Credit as a buy-on-credit option. Klarna was being evaluated but was not yet in use.
  • PayPal Credit is a steady choice in the US. Keeping payment volume through PayPal consistent reduces the risk of account issues caused by revenue spikes (sudden jumps in sales).

Klarna dispute risk by market

1 min
  • Klarna disputes (a buyer's claim that reverses a payment) can lead to full refunds even when tracking (the online record of where a parcel is) shows delivery.
  • Disputes are especially likely if delivery takes around 10 days.
  • Klarna can also hold the store's money. This creates cash flow problems (the store has less money on hand to pay bills).
  • Klarna is the most used payment method in Sweden, but it is risky for beginners.
  • Only use Klarna in Sweden if you can ship within 4 to 5 days, and ideally one day.
  • A beginner should avoid Sweden and start in the Netherlands or Germany instead. The risk of Klarna disputes is high in Sweden.
  • The UK was described as the worst market for Klarna disputes, so Klarna should be avoided there.
  • Stores that do not offer Klarna at all avoid this risk.
  • Offering only credit card and PayPal at checkout lets a store avoid Klarna exposure.
  • Some stores have run this way since 2017 without issues.

Local payment methods: general principles

1 min
  • Know the main payment methods of each target market (the countries you sell to).
  • For the top markets, offer PayPal and credit cards through Shopify Payments or Stripe (a service that processes card payments). Add Klarna or Afterpay when the product price is more than $60.
  • Install payment providers that make sense in each specific target country.
  • Local payment options build trust and conversion (the share of visitors who buy).
  • Buyers expect the payment methods they know.
  • Payment methods matter for expanding to a new country.
  • Before expanding into a European country, check which local payment methods buyers use. Conversion depends on them.
  • In Germany, Klarna and PayPal are widely used.
  • In the Netherlands, Bancontact (a bank payment method) is a major method alongside credit cards.
  • Targeting European countries can produce some sales in English, but usually at lower conversion.
  • Facebook's cheaper CPMs (cost per 1,000 ad views) there attract low-intent traffic (visitors unlikely to buy). Check the local payment methods first.
  • Target countries where card payments through Stripe or PayPal are available. The lack of these options in some markets limits sales.
  • Offer a local payment provider in the target market.
  • Buyers trust a store more when they can pay with a method they know. This was the most important local advantage in the example.
  • Trust can be built when the business is visibly local.
  • A registered local company, local payment methods and visible local products in the content all reduce buying hesitation.

Local payment methods: Switzerland

1 min
  • Install a local payment provider that Swiss customers recognise. Choose one that accepts QR-code payments (you scan a square code with your phone) and is registered only in Switzerland.
  • This builds trust and raised sales.
  • Local payment providers require a company registered in the country with a local bank account.
  • Adding TWINT (a Swiss payment app) in Switzerland raised the conversion rate of ads targeting Switzerland.
  • TWINT builds trust because buyers recognise the provider and use it daily. Payment is quick by scanning a QR code.
  • Show the local payment method logo (TWINT in Switzerland) on the store. This builds trust with local customers who already use it daily.
  • TWINT can only be offered on a store run by a Swiss company, since it is a payment provider used only in Switzerland.
  • A Swiss merchant account is required before the logo can be shown.
  • Swiss customers who pay in euros instead of Swiss francs face a conversion fee of about 3 percent.
  • This matters when pricing a Swiss-facing store.

B2B payment terms: terms and third-party financing

1 min
  • B2B means business-to-business: you sell to companies. Do not offer net 30 payment terms (the buyer pays 30 days after receiving the order) to customers.
  • Net 30 means you carry the collection risk (the risk of not getting paid), and many businesses struggle to collect.
  • Instead, offer a third-party financing option (a finance company that pays you and handles the buyer's payments), such as Klarna.
  • With third-party financing, the financing company approves the customer and pays the store from day one, as with a credit card.
  • If the customer does not pay the financier in time, the financier handles collection, not the store.

B2B payment terms: wires, deposits and checks

1 min
  • A B2B buyer placed an order of about 66,000 USD and asked to pay by check (a paper payment) after delivery.
  • The seller refused this and accepted a deposit (part payment in advance) of 30 percent, roughly 20,000 USD plus, wired (sent by bank transfer) before shipping.
  • The remaining balance was wired on delivery the same day. The seller never accepts payment by check after receipt.

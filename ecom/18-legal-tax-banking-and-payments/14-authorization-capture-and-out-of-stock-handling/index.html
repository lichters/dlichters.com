Authorization, capture and out-of-Stock handling

Section 18.14 · 14 of 24 in this chapter · 5 min read

In short

  • "Authorize" means the bank holds the money. "Capture" means you take it. Only capture after the product is confirmed in stock.
  • Use manual capture (you take the money yourself) while your supplier's stock is unreliable.
  • A payment hold lasts only 7 days. If you don't capture within 7 days, it cancels by itself.
  • If the item is out of stock, don't charge the customer. Cancel the order or ask the customer first.

Authorize-only versus automatic capture

  • Authorize-only confirms the order and approves the customer's payment method, but charges nothing. "Authorize" means the bank checks the card and reserves the money. You capture the payment manually, meaning you take the money yourself, to complete the charge once the product is confirmed in stock.
  • After checkout, the money is authorised (reserved) on the customer's card or PayPal balance, but not yet captured. PayPal is an online payment service. Capturing transfers the money to you and sends it to your bank account.
  • Manual capture authorizes the card without charging it. A new store that is still learning which supplier stock is reliable should use manual capture. Automatic capture is acceptable once stock is consistently available.

Setting up manual capture in Shopify

Shopify is the software that runs your online store. Follow these steps to turn on manual capture.

  1. Go to Settings > Payments
  2. Scroll to Payment capture
  3. Select Manually capture payment for orders
  4. Keep the box Send a warning email when authorizations are close to expiring checked. This warns you before an authorization runs out.
  5. Click Save

Capture only after stock is confirmed

  • Use manual capture, especially in high-ticket dropshipping. Dropshipping means your supplier ships the product straight to the customer, so you never hold stock yourself. High-ticket means expensive products. Products may be out of stock in this model.
  • Capture only after you have confirmed stock with the supplier and placed the official order. Refunding a captured payment to the customer can take several days.
  • Capture the customer's payment only after you have confirmed the item will ship. This avoids charging customers for orders you cannot fulfill.
  • Do not capture payment in Shopify until stock is confirmed and the order has been checked for risk warnings, which are alerts that an order may be fraud (a scam or stolen card). Medium- and high-risk orders must be escalated, meaning passed up, to the business owner first.
  • Authorize the customer's card at checkout, but charge it only after the supplier confirms stock. This way the customer is not billed for an item that cannot be delivered. The VA (virtual assistant, a helper who works for you remotely) then captures the charge in Shopify.

Core out-of-stock workflows

Protecting against out-of-stock orders

  1. Set your payment settings to authorize the customer's card without capturing the charge
  2. When an order comes in, confirm with the supplier that the item is in stock
  3. Capture the payment in the Shopify admin (the back office where you manage orders)
  4. Ship the order. This is when the customer is charged.

Payment capture when stock is uncertain (Shopify payment settings)

  1. In the Shopify payment settings, set the payment mode to authorize. The card is checked for the order total without taking the money.
  2. When the customer orders, the order goes through and you receive the usual notification
  3. Contact the supplier and confirm the product is in stock and can ship right away
  4. If it is in stock, open the order in the Shopify admin and capture the payment, which sends the money to you
  5. If it is not in stock, email the customer and ask whether they want to wait or cancel

Pre-authorization workflow

Using pre-authorization instead of charging immediately

"Pre-authorization" means checking that the card has the money and holding it, without charging it yet.

  1. When an order is placed, pre-authorize the card so the funds are confirmed as available, without charging the customer
  2. Check with the supplier that the product is in stock
  3. If it is in stock, capture the payment, which charges the customer, and process the order
  4. If it is out of stock, cancel the order so the customer is never charged and no refund is needed

Order confirmation and pre-authorization rule

  • When an order is placed, the customer gets an order confirmation email, and the card is only pre-authorised, not charged
  • The store checks stock and charges the card only when the item is available for immediate shipment, within 5 business days. A business day is a working day, not a weekend or holiday.
  • If the item is on back-order, meaning it is sold but not yet in stock, the pre-authorisation is voided (cancelled, as if it never happened). The customer is then contacted by email about alternatives.

What to do when stock is not available

Different sources give different actions for an out-of-stock or back-ordered item. Choose the row that matches your situation.

Situation Action Source
Out of stock within the pre-authorization workflow Cancel the order. The customer is never charged and no refund is needed
Stock not confirmed, item not in stock Email the customer and ask whether they want to wait or cancel
Item on back-order under the 5-business-day rule Void the pre-authorisation and email the customer about alternatives

Seven-day authorization window

  • A payment authorization (the hold on the money) is valid for 7 days. If it is not captured within 7 days, it is cancelled automatically, and the customer must re-enter their payment details
  • Some credit cards authorize a payment for only about seven days, so the authorization may not last through a long delay (no date given)
Situation Consequence Source
Not captured within 7 days Authorization cancelled automatically; customer must re-enter payment details
Some credit cards Authorize a payment for only about seven days

Backorders

  • For a backorder (an item sold before it is in stock), check the supplier's ETA (estimated arrival date). If the supplier has a reliable ETA of about two to three weeks, confirm the backorder with the customer. Capture the payment right away once they confirm they are willing to wait.
  • Do not charge the customer's card for a backorder that could last months with no reliable date.
Supplier situation Payment action Source
Reliable ETA of about two to three weeks Confirm with the customer, then capture once they agree to wait
No reliable date, could last months Do not charge the card
Item on back-order under the 5-business-day rule Void the pre-authorisation (see pre-authorization section)

Split payment for backordered items

Splitting a payment through PayPal

A split payment means the customer pays in parts. Here, the customer pays part of the total now and the rest later.

  1. Confirm the customer's email address
  2. Send a PayPal invoice (a payment request sent by email) to the customer's email, asking for the partial amount
  • A basic Shopify plan at 29 per month did not allow partial payments natively (built in). On that plan, the PayPal invoice route was needed.

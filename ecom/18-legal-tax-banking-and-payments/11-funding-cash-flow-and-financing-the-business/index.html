Funding, cash flow and financing the business

Section 18.11 · 11 of 24 in this chapter · 7 min read

In short

  • Avoid debt where you can. Do not run the business on borrowed money.
  • Shopify usually puts customer money in your bank within 48 hours, so plan supplier payments around that timing.
  • Do not count on the Shopify balance. You can withdraw only 5,000 dollars per payout.
  • Short-term loans can be cheap, but only if you repay within the set time.
This section has 9 parts. Tap one to open it.

Debt principles

1 min
  • Avoid debt where possible. Pay for growth from margins, which means the profit left after costs. **
  • Shopify Capital, a loan from Shopify, and similar financing take a share of your sales. They can be expensive. **
  • Credit, meaning borrowed money on a card or loan, can help when used carefully. Do not run a business on borrowed money. **
  • If you cannot say what a loan is for, and the business does not need it, treat paying it back as a risk to deal with. **
  • You do not need large upfront capital, meaning money needed before any sales, to pay for inventory, meaning the products you hold to sell. **
  • Paying suppliers with a credit card and paying the card off quickly needs only about the wholesale cost of one order in credit. Wholesale cost is the price the supplier charges you. **

Limits on the Shopify balance

1 min
  • Do not rely on the Shopify balance, meaning money held in Shopify and not yet paid to your bank, for working capital. Working capital is the cash used for daily costs like suppliers and ads.
  • Withdrawals are limited to 5,000 dollars per payout. This can block funds you need for orders.
  • Shopify Balance can be a short-term source of funds for scaling ad spend. Scaling means spending more on ads to grow sales.
  • Shopify Balance works only for stores on Shopify Payments and only for US residents.
  • Treat it as a stopgap, meaning a short fix. Focus on getting a business credit card as the longer-term funding source.

Matching supplier and ad payments to money coming in

1 min
  • Scaling needs cash to pay for the MOQ, the minimum order quantity (the smallest order a supplier accepts), up front, before sales arrive. **
  • Shopify payouts may take longer than supplier and ad payments. Cash flow, the money moving in and out over time, must cover that gap. **
  • Suppliers with net terms, meaning time to pay after ordering, such as about 14 to 20 days, can help. **
  • Credit cards with a 30-day billing window, meaning the time before the card bill is due, can help match money going out with money coming in. **
  • When stock must be paid before sales come in, some established sellers cover the gap with business lending. Business lending means loans for businesses.
  • Some sellers use other financing instead of relying only on cash.
  • Plan for month-end inventory bills so that a slow month does not force a sudden sale of assets. Assets are things you own, such as stock.
  • When a store sells, the customer's money goes into your own account.
  • You pay the supplier separately for each order, using the payment method you agree on with them. **
  • Plan cash flow before peak sales, which are your busiest selling times.
  • Ad spend on credit cards and fast budget increases can quickly use up all your cash.
  • So arrange extra funding in advance.

Funding stock with credit cards (routine)

1 min

Use this routine when the business credit card limit, meaning the most you can charge on the card, is too low to fund orders.

Sources: and are the same routine, merged here. **

  1. Max out the business credit card every day to pay supplier orders as they come in. Max out means use up all of the limit.
  2. Pay the card off in full every night.
  3. Call the card issuer, the bank that gave you the card, every day. Request a higher limit, and repeat until the limit rises gradually.
  4. Apply early for an American Express charge card, a card that must be paid in full each month, as a second card.
  5. Optionally open a second credit card from another issuer. Rotate, meaning take turns, between both cards while continuing the nightly payoff and limit requests.
  6. When no credit is left, wait for Shopify payouts, about 48 hours, before paying the supplier. Or use a debit card, a card that takes money straight from your bank, as a backup, even though it earns no points.
  7. Spend the first one to two months building credit limits and cash reserves this way.

Checkout financing

1 min
  • When a customer pays through a financing provider, a company that lets shoppers pay in parts, the store receives the full order amount right away. **
  • So the merchant, meaning the store owner, does not finance the purchase. **
  • If cash is short before the first payout arrives, a credit card can cover the supplier order and be paid off later. **
  • Shopify Payments payouts typically arrive within about 48 hours. **

Peak season and reserves

1 min
  • Around the end of the year, banks and suppliers can slow down because many people are on holiday. At the same time, order volume, meaning the number of orders, peaks.
  • Keep reserve cash, meaning money set aside, or a backup payment source. This covers fulfillment, which means packing and shipping orders, during the peak period.
  • This way, payout delays do not stall orders.
  • Treat cash as profit only after the money is in the bank, all expenses are paid, and the return window has closed. The return window is the time customers can send items back. **
  • Keep a cash reserve for returns and unexpected costs. **

Currency conversion

1 min
  • Revenue, meaning money earned from sales, earned in US dollars and converted into another currency is valued at the exchange rate, the price of one currency in another. This rate is taken on the day the money is transferred.
  • A stronger US dollar adds value only if the transfer happens at a favorable rate. Compare the transfer-day rate with the sale-day rate before counting any gain.

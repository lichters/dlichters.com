Contracts, partners, contractors and agencies

Section 18.22 · 22 of 24 in this chapter · 8 min read

In short

  • Always get a written contract, even with a casual helper or agency.
  • In the US, pay freelancers as 1099 contractors. If you control their hours fully, they may need to become employees.
  • Never give freelancers access to your main ad or payment accounts. Use separate logins and payment methods.
  • Real staff never ask for money in a private message. Ignore the request and send nothing.
This section has 8 parts. Tap one to open it.

Written contracts and agency agreements

1 min
  • Use a written contract even when the relationship feels casual or informal. A written contract is a signed document that says who must do what. It protects both sides legally, makes expectations clear, and gives you recourse (a way to take action) if problems arise.
  • Every contract should specify:
    • payment structure (how and how much you pay)
    • timeline of work
    • cancellation terms (how either side can end the deal)
    • performance expectations
    • communication protocols (how and where you talk)
    • penalties for unlawful behavior, such as deleting accounts without consent
  • Get a written contract even if the agency offers performance guarantees, refund guarantees, or payment-only-if-successful terms. A performance guarantee is a promise about results. A refund guarantee promises your money back. Payment-only-if-successful means you pay only when a result is reached. Legal protection matters regardless of how confident either party feels.
  • Written contracts with offshore freelancers and staff (people working in another country) are often seen as unnecessary for simple jobs, because cross-border legal issues (problems that cross national borders) are complicated. The main value of a contract is making each party's position and obligations (what each side must do) clear.

Contractors, freelancers and employee classification

2 min

Classification means deciding whether a worker counts as a contractor or an employee.

United States: 1099 contractor or employee

  • In the US, pay independent contractors (self-employed people who work for you on their own terms), including family members who work as freelancers, as 1099 contractors. A 1099 is a US tax form for contractors. Agree on this and on the expectations in writing before the work starts.
  • The team in the Source A example was hired as 1099 independent contractors, not employees. If the owner sets fixed hours and full-time control over the work, the contractor may need to be converted to an employee. That brings insurance and benefits obligations (duties to pay for insurance and benefits). Check local labor and tax laws before deciding.
  • Where the two rules meet: a 1099 setup assumes the contractor controls their own working pattern (their own way and timing of work). If you set fixed hours and control the work full-time, the arrangement may need to become employment, as described above.

Paying hourly contractors

Pay hourly contractors every two weeks:

  1. Each contractor logs their hours on their personal board in Trello (an online board for tasks; logging hours means writing down time worked).
  2. Every other Thursday the owner checks the logged hours.
  3. Pay US contractors through QuickBooks (accounting software that sends payments).
  4. Pay Philippines-based contractors through Wise (a money transfer service) with direct deposit (money sent straight into a bank account).

Virtual assistants

  • Sign a VA contract before training starts. A VA is a virtual assistant, a remote helper. Include:
    • pay
    • weekly work hours (for example Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 17:00 Eastern Standard Time, the US East Coast clock)
    • The holidays observed (here US holidays)
    • vacation time and how much notice is required for it
    • how much notice is required to quit
    • The training obligations
  • Sign a short agreement letter with the assistant that repeats the job post terms in writing. Include an exclusivity clause (a rule that the helper works only for you) stating the assistant will not work for other businesses.
  • Classify the virtual assistant as an independent contractor (self-employed, not an employee) rather than an employee in the signed agreement. Confirm the tax reporting rules for your country with a qualified adviser before paying.

Non-disclosure agreements

  • For an NDA (non-disclosure agreement, a promise to keep information secret), search Google for an NDA template (a ready-made draft) in Google Docs (a free online document editor). Then adapt it to your needs before sharing it with a contractor.

Data and account ownership

1 min
  • Clarify data ownership (who owns the information) in the contract. Verify that ad accounts (accounts used to run paid ads), creative files, and account configurations (account settings) remain your property. Verify that the agency cannot delete or withhold access if the relationship ends.
  • Store all supplier, shipping and app logins in one restricted accounts sheet (a spreadsheet only certain people can open), so the assistant has what they need without searching. Keep the owner's social media logins there too when the assistant manages those accounts.
  • Do not give Fiverr freelancers access to Google Ads or Google Merchant Center accounts. Fiverr is an online marketplace for freelancers. Google Ads is Google's paid advertising tool. Google Merchant Center holds your product data for Google shopping ads.
  • If you need help unsuspending an account (getting a shut-down account turned back on), use a reputable PPC agency (a firm that runs pay-per-click ads, where you pay for each click) with a public reputation. Logins from unfamiliar IP addresses (internet addresses your account has not used before) can flag an account.

Payment providers and bank access

1 min
  • Give a team member access to your payment providers (services that take customer money) only after you have built enough trust with them, and handle this step with great care. They can install the payment providers in the store so it goes live (ready to take real orders).
  • If an assistant pays for orders, give them a dedicated payment method (a separate card or account used only for this), such as a PayPal account or a credit card, rather than the owner's main account.
  • While delegating everything else, the owner kept control of the bank accounts and of supplier payments that needed transfers (sending money directly to a bank). This kept financial control in one place during a seven-week absence.

Business partnerships

1 min
  • Two partners can own a store through a new corporation (a company separate from its owners) split 50/50 or 51/49 (the percentage of ownership each partner holds). They can also own it through their existing LLCs (limited liability companies, a type of business entity) that own the corporation. Whichever structure is chosen, a clear written partnership agreement is required.
  • Any business partnership needs a written agreement covering:
    • The responsibilities of each partner
    • The money split
    • every foreseeable event (everything you can reasonably expect might happen)
  • Typical clauses (standard written rules) cover one partner wanting to buy out the other (pay them to take over their share), the death of a partner, and a partner failing to meet their obligations.
  • AI tools can produce a useful first draft, but a lawyer should review the final agreement before anyone signs.
  • Prompt for drafting a first partnership agreement (a ready-to-copy message for an AI tool):
I run a dropshipping store called [STORE NAME] on Shopify with my business partner [PARTNER NAME]. We have no written partnership agreement yet. Please help me draft a one-page partnership agreement. First, list everything a standard business partnership agreement should contain. Then include clauses for: what happens if one partner wants to buy the other out; what happens if one partner dies; what happens if one partner does not meet their obligations. Add a responsibilities section that splits tasks between us, for example: [PARTNER A] handles website design and maintenance, product uploads and supplier relationships; [PARTNER B] handles customer service and marketing. Also add a section on how profits, costs and finances are split and paid out. Ask me for any details you need before drafting, and mark which clauses should be reviewed by a lawyer before signing.

Influencers and ambassadors

1 min
  • A simple one-page, three-paragraph agreement with each influencer (a social media creator who promotes products) formalizes the arrangement and confirms the posting obligation (their duty to post). It acts as a digital handshake (an informal online promise) and does not create liability (legal responsibility), but it helps set expectations.
  • A simple written influencer agreement with the creator's name and key terms creates a record, even if it is not a strict legal contract. Enforceability (whether a court can make it stick) across countries varies. Get local legal advice before relying on agreements abroad.
  • Keep ambassadors (people who speak for your brand) unpaid and in the ambassador role. According to general counsel's advice (the company's lawyer), unpaid ambassadors who sell or refer products but are not paid can be treated as non-employees. This helps avoid employment liability (legal duties of an employer).

Community rules

1 min

  • Members must not post or offer paid services to other members of the program. They must not use the course community or direct messages (private one-to-one messages) to sell anything to other members. Anyone who wants to sell a service must build a separate business rather than use the program's audience.

  • Members may not copy or share program content, such as program materials, attachments, and spreadsheets, with anyone who is not a member. The only exception is express permission (clear, explicit approval) from the program owners. The content is copyrighted (legally owned by its creator) and owned by the company behind the program. Sharing it outside the membership can lead to legal action.

  • Permitted sharing with a non-member, such as a virtual assistant helping build the business:

    1. Message the program owners before sharing.
    2. Name the specific materials you want to share and explain why.
    3. Share only after receiving written approval.

    Sharing without approval is not allowed.

Scam avoidance

2 min

Unsolicited offers and messages

  • Shopify (a store-building platform) and Google never send unsolicited emails (emails nobody asked for) to a store asking for money to optimize the store. Emails from agencies offering store optimization are usually cold outreach (unrequested sales contact) and should be ignored.
  • Legitimate mentors, coaches, and staff of an online community never send unsolicited direct messages first. Any direct message that arrives right after joining, claiming to be from the community's team, is a scam until proven otherwise.
  • Anyone asking for money in a direct message, including for store setup help, course access, or a service fee, is a scammer. Real staff do not request payment through private messages.
  • Requests for payment in cryptocurrency (digital money such as Bitcoin) made by someone posing as a community coach or support person are a clear fraud signal (a clear sign of fraud). Refuse and do not send any funds.
  • New members often trust the first private message they receive after joining a server (a chat group). This is exactly what impersonators count on. Treat every unsolicited message from a community member as untrusted until verified in an official channel (a public place run by the real team).

Spotting impersonators

  • Impersonation scams (scams where someone pretends to be a real person) copy a real person's display name, and sometimes profile picture. Then they add a small change such as an extra word, a letter, or trailing punctuation (marks at the end, like a dot). Check the exact spelling of every name against the official member list before trusting a message. Example pattern: a real handle Alex_Store (a username) becomes Alex_Store vs or Alex_Store. in a fake account.
  • Official announcements, pinned messages (messages stuck to the top of a chat), and public channels are the reliable source for who the real staff and coaches are. Compare any suspicious account against these before replying. If a fake account is confirmed, block it and do not engage further.

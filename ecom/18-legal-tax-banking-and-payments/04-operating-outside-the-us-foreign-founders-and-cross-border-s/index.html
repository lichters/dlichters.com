Operating outside the US: foreign founders and cross-Border setup

Section 18.4 · 4 of 24 in this chapter · 20 min read

In short

  • You can often sell in another country without living there or registering a company in that country at first.
  • Stripe Atlas is one paid service that sets up a US company for you. You do not have to use it.
  • Before you pay for a setup, check the choice with people who did the same thing and with a local accountant.
  • Pick one company and one country setup and keep it. Switching between setups caused account bans in one reported case.
  • Before you assume a deadline, check whether the US reporting rule applies to your LLC.
More key points
  • Foreign companies usually need a yearly filing. An accountant can usually handle it.
  • The coach's tax setup applies only to him. Confirm any setup like it with a local tax advisor first.
  • Selling abroad usually needs no company there at the start. Registration becomes necessary once you pass the local sales limit.
  • Keep the company's country, currency and bank details the same when you open a payment account.

Decide first whether you need a foreign company

2 min
  • A business does not have to be based in the country it sells to. Owners living abroad have run stores successfully.
  • A seller outside the United States does not need a US company to run a store. Both options have pros and cons, so ask people in forums who have done it in their own country before you decide.
  • Sellers living outside the US can work under their home-country business details. They can also form a US company, for example through Stripe Atlas (a paid service that sets up US companies). Neither route is confirmed as required for every supplier, so check your choice with sellers in the same situation.
  • Not being a US citizen does not block you from selling in the US. Legal obstacles are mostly about setup, not citizenship.
  • Non-residents who are US citizens can run US stores from abroad. One source ran stores from Southeast Asia and South America for years without problems. Check your own tax and legal situation.
  • In some cases, a business with no US entity (a registered business unit) can still sell to US suppliers. The team advises confirming this in the community first and not relying on a general statement.
  • Sellers based outside the US with no US physical presence (no office, staff or property there) were reported to run US stores without a US business entity or sales permit. This is an informal view, not legal advice.
  • Do not form a new US business entity if your current foreign entity already sells successfully to US suppliers and customers. Register in the US only when there is a clear benefit.
  • A US entity is only worth forming when your current setup is not already winning supplier approvals and sales. (2024)
  • Selling in other countries does not require registering a company there at the start. Registration becomes relevant when sales in a country approach the local tax threshold (the sales level that triggers tax duties), about 80,000 as of 2020. Then you must start collecting and reporting tax.
  • Expanding to a country where your company is not registered may require a local business partner or moving the company. Check whether your payment provider allows it before you plan the expansion.
  • Opening a US business is comparatively simple even for people who are not US citizens. It still has challenges that need separate research before you commit.

Routes to a US company for a non-US owner

5 min

Route A: keep your home-country business details

  • Sellers living outside the US can operate under their home-country details.
  • A Canadian business selling to US customers can keep operating under its Canadian details. Both approaches are used in practice, so the choice depends on whether the owner wants a US entity.

Route B: form a US company through a formation service (a company that sets up companies for you)

  • Stripe Atlas (stripe.com/atlas) sets up a US company, a US bank account, a Stripe account (Stripe is a payment company) and a legal entity together. It is one option and is not required.
  • Stripe Atlas lets non-US residents set up US business formation and operations without being physically present in the United States.
  • Stripe Atlas lets non-US residents set up a US company and US bank account without travelling to the United States. This was described for 2018, so check current pricing on the website before you commit.
  • For a store owner who is not based in the US, Stripe Atlas can set up the US company and the related business bank setup. It is the recommended route for that situation. (2024)
  • A non-US seller can set up a US company and US bank account through Stripe Atlas. This gives a US business presence without living in the US.
  • Stripe Atlas can set up a US business entity for a founder living abroad and handles the setup steps. Use it as one option when you need a US business for suppliers and banking.
  • Stripe Atlas is a paid service that incorporates (legally creates) a company, commonly a Delaware entity (a company registered in the US state of Delaware). It also helps set up a tax ID and banking. It suits founders who want a US company without doing the paperwork alone. (2025)
  • Stripe Atlas incorporates a business in Delaware, opens a bank account and obtains an LLC (a limited liability company, a US company type that keeps your personal money separate from the business) for a founder, including those outside the US. It charges a fee for these services. (2025)
  • Stripe Atlas helps non-US residents form a US LLC and set up a Stripe payment account. It is a common route for international students or founders who cannot otherwise form a US company.
  • Stripe Atlas is a company-formation service. The US company it creates can then be used to open a Stripe payment account. It is useful for founders who need a US entity to accept card payments. (2024)
  • Stripe Atlas is a service for incorporating a US company and setting up its banking and Stripe payments account. Founders use it when they want a US entity without doing the paperwork alone. (2024)
  • Stripe Atlas can help with an EIN (a US tax ID number for businesses), a sales tax certificate (a permit to collect sales tax) and nexus setup (the link to a US state that triggers tax duties). Confirm its included services directly with Stripe support.
  • Stripe Atlas was cited as an option for forming a company and opening a bank setup as a non-US founder. Requirements such as a social security number should be checked.
  • Stripe Atlas is an optional incorporation service that some companies offer during setup. You do not need it to form an LLC, get an EIN, open a bank account or connect Stripe. (2026)
  • Other companies also offer formation services for non-US residents. Several providers offer similar packages, so compare offers before paying if the flat fee (a fixed price) is a concern. Check community forums for cheaper, trusted options.
  • Other countries may have comparable services. Check them before you choose.

Procedure: flat-fee US LLC through Stripe Atlas

  1. Go to stripe.com/atlas.
  2. Pay the one-time flat fee of $500 (current pricing at time of recording).
  3. The service sets up a Delaware LLC as a legal US business entity.
  4. The service connects the entity to Stripe for accepting customer payments. Stripe connects to Shopify (an online store platform).
  5. The service also opens a US bank account for the business.
  6. Once set up, the business is ready to operate without further formation steps.

  • Wyoming and Delaware are the two most popular states for forming an LLC. Stripe Atlas can form a Delaware company.
  • Delaware was named as the default state in Stripe Atlas.
  • For non-US nationals forming a US LLC, Stripe Atlas is one option that sets up the entity.
  • A non-US founder can set up a US company (an S corp or C corp, two types of US corporation), obtain an EIN, and get a US business address and phone number through a formation service. Choose the provider by checking reviews and experiences of people who have used it, not a single recommendation. Confirm that the entity type and state fit your plan before you pay.

Choosing a provider and checking advice

  • Stripe is a trusted payment company that has been used for a decade. For an unanswered question, try the Stripe phone line and support forum. Stripe Atlas-specific advice should come from community members who have used it, not from a coach. (2024)
  • A Stripe Atlas or formation service may ask for a social security number (a US personal ID number). This is a common hurdle for sellers abroad who need a US bank account. (2024)
  • For sellers abroad who need a US bank account, Mercury (a US online bank) and Stripe Atlas (which provides a US bank account, with a Delaware entity mentioned) are options. (2024)

Choosing the company location

1 min

Step-by-step framework for any country

  1. Look up the business types your country offers. There is usually a choice between operating as yourself (a sole proprietor, meaning no separate company) and operating as a legal entity.
  2. Search community forums and Facebook groups for your country name to see which entity others have used.
  3. Post a specific question in the community if no answer exists.
  4. Consider a free consultation with a local accountant about which structure fits your business and whether they can handle the books (your financial records).
  5. Register the chosen entity directly through the official government website.

Principles

  • Business entity types differ by country, and there is no universal recommendation. The same framework usually offers doing business as yourself or as a legal entity. The legal entity is generally preferred for tax and liability reasons (liability means your legal responsibility for debts and problems).
  • International tax law is complex and depends on the region, and there is no universal playbook. Hire a lawyer in each target region to give written advice. This makes them responsible for that advice (professional liability) and gives binding guidance.
  • Advice on tax for a seller located in another country, selling into the US, should come from an accountant who knows that situation. General store rules are not enough. (2023)
  • Check current account approval rules and tax treatment with a local accountant before choosing a location.

Hong Kong and UK as first steps

1 min
  • A UK company was described as cheaper to start than a Hong Kong business, so a UK company is the suggested first step. A Hong Kong company with a PayPal account that accepts dropshipping (selling products that a supplier ships directly to the customer) is the fallback if UK restrictions continue.
  • A UK LTD was used as a cheap start, but the cheap option later caused account problems. Compare the total cost of the setup you will need before you choose.
  • Using a Singapore or Hong Kong company for PayPal gave stronger payment processing with few holds, at a dispute rate (the share of customers who challenge a charge) of about 4 to 5%.

Canada

2 min

Options, by source date

  • Registering in Canada is often simpler for a Canadian seller who wants to sell into the US. A US entity does not automatically unlock more supplier approvals. Shopify can show prices in the customer's currency.
  • A Canadian business selling to US customers can keep operating under its Canadian details or form a US entity. Both are used in practice.
  • Canadian sellers in the US often form a US LLC. A Canadian sole proprietor can register a DBA (a business name for a sole proprietor) to show a business name to suppliers. Confirm the best Canadian entity setup with an accountant. (2024)
  • A Canadian founder can register the business in Canada, and Canadian banks may open accounts for it. An EIN is optional at the start. The business still needs a US address and entity setup if it sells into the US. (2025)
  • You can run a US-based dropshipping business from Canada, selling to US customers, if the business is registered in the US and suppliers ship from US warehouses. As of 2025, this should not be affected by current regulations. Tariff and trade laws (import taxes and trade rules) may change. Confirm with legal or tax advice if you are unsure. (2025)
  • A Canadian resident can form a US business, such as in Delaware, and operate it in the US. Whether the reverse arrangement works (running a US company from Canada) should be checked with an accountant.
  • A Canadian sole proprietor selling only to US customers may be accepted by suppliers with an EIN. Start as a sole proprietor with a business name and an EIN. Check with an accountant before converting to a corporation. (2025)
  • Shopify can run Canada and US sales on one store, with currency conversion through Shopify Payments (Shopify's built-in card payments). A US entity is optional until the US business grows.
  • A business bank account and address for Canadians selling to US customers can be discussed in the Discord North America channel (an online community chat). (2023)

Selling in the US from outside it

2 min

Payment and tax-ID requirements

  • For a foreign owner operating in the US, a US EIN (also called FEIN, the federal version of the tax ID) is needed. Resale certificates (tax forms that let a seller buy goods to resell without paying sales tax on them) are needed in some cases. Ask a US-based accountant or the Discord channel about setup rather than relying on general advice. (2025)
  • Non-US residents who are unsure about US paperwork, such as resale certificates, should get specialist advice. Forming a US LLC was suggested as a route that may simplify that paperwork.
  • A business can be run from Europe with a US LLC and Shopify, using a credit card or debit card linked to a European bank or a US bank account. A US credit card is not strictly required.
  • For a non-US founder who wants a US bank account, a service such as Stripe Atlas can set up the US company and bank account. This is 2020 information, so check current requirements.
  • A US PayPal account for a non-resident business can face long holds of 21 to 60 days, sometimes 50,000 to 60,000 USD. Getting an ITIN (a US tax number for people without a social security number) or SSN to remove these holds took about two to three months. Plan for that delay before you scale payments.
  • A traditional US bank account requires opening in person at a US branch. That was impractical in 2021 because of travel restrictions. Mercury Bank was then the one option offering a virtual US bank account (an account with no branch visit) for non-residents.
  • A US company can get a bank account as a non-resident through banks or fintechs (financial technology firms) such as Mercury or Brex. This helps with payment processing.

US address and the card rate

  • The card rate can be lowered by running the store as a US LLC on the Shopify Basic plan (a Shopify subscription tier), which the source gives as 2.9% per transaction. To get that rate you need a US address. This can be a residential address of a contact or the address of a US 3PL fulfillment center (a third-party warehouse that ships orders for you). You also need a connection willing to lend it. (2026)
  • To set up a US LLC for banking, you need LLC registration and a physical US address. You may also need a representative (a real person) to appear in person at the bank if you are not physically in the US.

Registration address and proof of address

1 min
  • The Shopify account country and the business address must match the country where the business operates, not the country where the owner lives. For example, an owner living in Japan who runs a US store must enter a US business address and select United States as the country.
  • Mixing up the owner's residence country and the business country can cause problems with Shopify Payments, checkout functionality (the payment page customers use) and payouts (money sent to your bank). Set the account country correctly from the start.
  • A formation service can provide a US business address and phone number for a non-US founder.
  • Canadian founders need a US address and entity setup if they sell into the US, even when the business is registered in Canada. (2025)

Banking, payment accounts and the owner's identity

1 min

Multi-currency accounts

  • For payment setup, use multi-currency accounts (accounts that hold several currencies) such as Wise or Airwallex. These give you virtual cards (card numbers used only online) and USD or EUR accounts. Personal bank cards may reveal your location to the ad platform.
  • Airwallex is an online business account used to pay for advertising. It sends international transfers in about 1 day and converts currency at real-time rates (the current exchange rate). It can be used alongside a Hong Kong company. Its currency conversion was reported as more accurate than Wise.
  • Airwallex transfers from a Hong Kong-linked account were reported to reach a US account in about one day and an Indian account in about 2 to 3 hours. Conversion rates were reported as real-time with low transfer fees. These figures are reported by sellers and should be verified.

Owner identity on supplier and platform accounts

  • A non-US citizen running a business as a sole proprietor gives suppliers personal details. A US LLC with a US bank account is generally easier for suppliers to approve.
  • A supplier's request for a social security number is a common hurdle. (2024)

Funding ads from a non-US card

Procedure: funding Facebook ads from a non-US card with a USD account

  1. Open a US LLC.
  2. Open a Mercury account, which works with Facebook.
  3. Transfer money from the bank to Wise.
  4. Convert the money there before paying Facebook.

Payment processors and payout chain

1 min

Merchant account

  • A merchant account is the payment-processing account a business needs to accept customer card payments. Stripe provides these accounts and connects to Shopify. A foreign owner can use it to take payments from US customers once a US entity is set up.

Payout chain for a non-US seller

Procedure: receiving Stripe payouts as a non-US seller

  1. Open a Stripe account linked to a US LLC.
  2. Move the Stripe balance to a US business bank account, such as Mercury or RelayFi (a US business bank). This requires a US LLC.
  3. Transfer the funds from that account to a Wise account.
  4. Convert USD to the local currency in Wise with minimal fees.

PayPal

  • Opening PayPal in one country while the company is registered in another, then switching to a different account type, led to repeated permanent bans in one reported case. Pick a single, consistent company and country setup before you open PayPal. (undated)
  • PayPal from India can be held for 21 to 60 days, sometimes with holds of 50,000 to 60,000 USD.
  • A Hong Kong business account for PayPal requires paying taxes. The first tax payment is due after 18 months.

Compliance: Corporate Transparency Act and annual filings

1 min
  • Check whether the Corporate Transparency Act applies to your US LLC before you assume any deadline. The Corporate Transparency Act is a US law that asks companies to report their owners. An LLC (limited liability company) is a common type of US company.
  • The law asks for "beneficial ownership" reporting. Beneficial owners are the real people who own or control a company.
  • A reporting window of 30 to 90 days was mentioned for LLCs formed in 2024. This was not confirmed. (2024)
  • Verify the current rule with an accountant or an official source.
  • Each foreign company usually needs an annual filing, which is a yearly report to the government. An accountant or a CPA (a licensed certified public accountant) can handle it.
  • Plan for filing deadlines. The coach noted they fall in March in his case.
  • If a second company is set up in another country, confirm its tax obligations first. Tax obligations are the taxes you must pay and report.
  • Check whether the local advisors understand the dropshipping model, since some do not. Dropshipping means selling products that a supplier ships straight to the customer.
  • A US LLC is usually formed quickly.
  • Expanding into a country with a local company may need a local business partner or a move there.
  • The US resale certificate and sales-tax setup can be checked with a US-based accountant. A resale certificate is a document that lets a store buy goods without paying sales tax, because the store resells them. Sales tax is a tax added to the price in some US states.

Structure and tax cautions

1 min
  • The coach's company setup treated dropshipping as an export of marketing services to avoid VAT. An export of services means selling a service to a customer in another country. VAT (value added tax) is a sales tax charged in many countries.
  • This applies only to the coach's own India and US setup.
  • Confirm any such structure with a local tax advisor before use. This is especially important in Germany, where invoices must show the company address.
  • In India, a withholding of 18% applies to foreign advertising payments. Withholding means tax is taken off a payment before the money is paid out.
  • So 1,000 USD of ads costs 180 USD extra.
  • This made the setup unsuitable for the long term, so a US company was set up instead.

Selling into a country without registering there

1 min
  • Selling in other countries does not require registering a company there at the start. Registering means officially signing up the company with the local authorities.
  • Registration becomes relevant at the local tax threshold, which is the sales amount that triggers the duty to register. The source gives about 80,000 as of 2020.
  • A business does not need to live in the country it sells to.
  • For selling into the US without a US company, the source notes conflicting positions.
  • Some sellers operate that way (2020, informal, meaning not an official rule).
  • Others say it is possible only in some cases and should be confirmed in the community (2022).
  • Consider both, and check the current position with a local advisor.

Living abroad while running the store

1 min

Procedure: planning before moving abroad for long-term travel

  1. Find a tenant to rent your home. A tenant is the person who rents your home.
  2. Research the visa requirements for each country. A visa is official permission to enter or stay in a country.
  3. Clarify your tax situation with your home government, which may not be set up for long-term travelers.
  4. Book flights only after the above steps are complete.
  • Non-residents, meaning people who do not live in the country, who are US citizens can run US stores from abroad. Check your own tax and legal situation.

Common mistakes

1 min
  • A UK LTD (a private limited company from the United Kingdom) was opened with USD as its currency and with US bank details.
  • This led Stripe (an online payment service) to ask for tax residency documents before activating the account. Tax residency documents prove the country where you count as living for tax purposes.
  • Keep the company jurisdiction (the country whose laws apply), currency and bank details consistent when opening a payment account.
  • Mixing up the owner's residence country and the business country can cause problems with Shopify Payments (Shopify's built-in payment service), checkout functionality and payouts (money sent from the payment service to your bank).
  • Opening PayPal in one country while the company is registered in another led to repeated permanent bans in one reported case. A permanent ban means the account is closed for good.
  • A cheap UK company later caused account problems, so compare the total setup cost before choosing. Total setup cost means all costs of opening it, added together.
  • Presenting dropshipping as a marketing-services export to avoid VAT is only the coach's own setup. It needs local tax advice before use.

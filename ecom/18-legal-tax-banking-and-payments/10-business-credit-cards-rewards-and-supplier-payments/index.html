Business credit cards, rewards and supplier payments

Section 18.10 · 10 of 24 in this chapter · 23 min read

In short

  • You do not need a business credit card to start. A debit card is enough to pay suppliers.
  • A business credit card is still worth getting, because it earns points or cash back on business spending.
  • Pay the full card balance every month so you never pay interest.
  • Start with a bank debit card, then build credit slowly with a secured card and small limit increases.
  • Pick a card for a reward you will really use, and check current terms before you apply.
More key points
  • Transfer points to an airline, not a hotel. Airline transfers give the most value per point.
  • Before you move points, compare the airline's own points price with the card's travel website.
  • Pay suppliers with a credit card and pay it off every day or two, so no interest builds up.
  • New business cards usually need your name too: expect to co-sign at first.

Whether a business card is required

2 min
  • A business credit card is not required to run a dropshipping business (a store where the supplier ships each product straight to the customer).
  • A debit card is enough to pay suppliers.
  • A card is still recommended because business spending earns points quickly.
  • Cash back, travel rewards and other perks are big enough to make getting and keeping a card worth it.
  • Pay as many business expenses as possible with a business credit card. Include advertising ("ad spend"), Shopify fees (Shopify is the software for your online store) and cost of goods sold (COGS, what the products cost you).
  • Points, miles or cash back build up fast this way.
  • Many suppliers only accept payment by card, not bank transfer, for orders. So you need at least one business card (credit or debit) before you contact suppliers.
  • Suppliers typically accept ACH transfers (direct bank-to-bank payments in the US), checks and credit cards.
  • Suppliers generally do not care whether a personal or business credit card pays, as long as it is accepted.
  • Suppliers generally accept debit card payment, so you can pay orders with a debit card as well as a credit card.
  • With suppliers, credit card terms are preferred. The supplier keeps the card on file (saved on their system), charges it on each order, ships right away, and the business earns points.
  • Credit card versus debit card for supplier payments: both work. A credit card is preferred because it earns points or miles and helps you ask for a limit increase (a higher spending cap) based on your daily payoff history.
  • An alternative to credit is to order from the supplier only after the customer's payment has arrived.
  • Points-earning credit cards are a useful option for supplier payments.
  • Prepaying suppliers by credit card (paying before the goods arrive) earns card points on those purchases. This is an extra benefit of a dropshipping store.
  • This benefit only applies when the card is in your name.
  • Use a personal credit card to buy inventory (stock you hold) until the business is formed. Then use a business credit card for purchases.
  • With good or decent credit, a rewards credit card can be used from the start to build points and miles for the business.
  • If you have no credit yet, give the supplier a debit card number to start with.
  • Once you have built up credit, replace it with a credit card on file.

Starting with a checking account and debit card

1 min
  • Open a business checking account (a bank account for daily business money). Use its debit card for early expenses before you apply for a business credit card.
  • This keeps business and personal money separate. It also delays annual fees (yearly charges) on a credit card until the business is profitable.
  • A business debit card should run on the Visa or Mastercard network (the companies that handle card payments).
  • If a business credit card is not possible because of personal credit history or finances, a debit card linked to the business bank account can be used to buy products.
  • A debit card linked to the business bank account can serve as your card when credit is unavailable.
  • You need it when a supplier requires a card, or when a card must be linked to a Google Ads account (your Google advertising account).
  • Keep cash flow positive (more money coming in than going out). Do this rather than taking loans or funding programs.
  • Pay ad spend and cost of goods on a business credit card.
  • Pay it off in full each month to earn rewards without carrying debt.

Scaling with multiple cards

1 min
  • Use business credit cards for scaling (growing ad spend). AmEx offers a near-limitless limit (very high spending room).
  • The balance must be paid off within the month, or you face high interest (extra cost on unpaid balance).
  • Build a business credit rating (a score showing how reliably the business pays) by using cards consistently.
  • Build it by paying on time.
  • When ad spend outgrows one card's limit, combine several cards.
  • Use personal credit cards at different banks, a business card and partner cards.
  • Example from the Black Friday run: personal cards at about 5,000 GBP, a business card at about 10,000 GBP and a partner's card at about 10,000 GBP.
  • Total leverage was roughly 25,000 to 35,000 GBP combined.
  • Ask American Express for additional business cards under the same company once the 4X category limit has been reached.
  • Each card can earn 4X on its own first 150,000 USD of ad spend. Cards can be used for separate businesses to keep books apart (keep records separate).
  • Rotating American Express cards over time, for example moving ad spend to a new card every few months, allows 4X earning to continue beyond the first 150,000 USD of spend under one company.
  • The author reports no known limit on the number of cards.
  • Ask the card issuer for a credit limit increase while paying the balance off every day.
  • Repeated increases over a few months can take a small starting limit to tens of thousands of dollars per card.

Building credit from a low limit

4 min

Credit path for a new store

  1. Start with the debit card linked to the business bank account.
  2. Accept that the first orders wait for the 48-hour payout (the money the payment processor, the service that collects customer payments, sends to your bank) before they go to suppliers.
  3. Once profits build up in the account, open a secured credit card (a card backed by your own cash deposit).
  4. Secure the card with a certificate of deposit (CD, a savings deposit locked for a set term) equal to the wanted credit limit. For example, a 5,000 USD CD for a 5,000 USD limit.
  5. Use the card and pay it off as orders come in. The credit score (a number showing how trustworthy you are with credit) improves quickly.
  6. Once credit is strong, close the secured card and cash out the CD.

Building business credit without a credit history

  1. Open a business checking account, which comes with a business debit card.
  2. Pay suppliers with the business debit card while cash on hand (money you have right now) is small.
  3. Build a cash reserve (savings kept for slow months) from sales over time.
  4. Once the reserve is large enough, open a secured credit card.
  5. Use the secured card and pay the full balance each cycle so credit builds.
  6. As credit improves, move to cards with better rewards, benefits and higher credit limits.
  • A secured credit card needs a certificate of deposit (CD) opened at the issuing bank.
  • The bank holds the deposit as collateral (security it keeps if you do not pay). A 5,000 USD limit needs a 5,000 USD CD.
  • The bank pays interest on the CD for its term.
  • Using a secured card and paying it off in full each cycle builds credit quickly.
  • Strong credit can then qualify a business for cards with more rewards, benefits and higher limits.
  • If the card is not paid, the bank can collect the balance from the CD.
  • Building credit with a new card: use a low-limit card, pay it in full each night, and request a limit increase every few weeks.
  • A secured card can help if credit is poor.
  • Paying card balances down promptly as sales come in can raise credit limits quickly.
  • With a low credit limit, suppliers can still be paid by debit card or wire transfer (direct bank transfer).
  • A card with a credit limit of about 2,500 USD allowed several orders a day when paid off regularly.
  • Requesting limit increases every few months grew that capacity over time.
  • A store owner can build card credit by paying balances off promptly and asking the card issuer for periodic limit increases.
  • This helps cover supplier orders before customer payments settle, which typically takes about 48 hours.

Daily payoff and limit increases

  • Build available credit limits by paying the balance every couple of days instead of waiting for the full billing cycle (the period between card statements).
  • This renews the credit line frequently. It keeps credit available for continuous business purchases without waiting 30 days.
  • If the personal credit limit is low (300 to 1,000 USD), provide the credit card to suppliers.
  • If the limit is insufficient for orders, offer a debit card instead or request multiple charges.
  • Build credit over time. Eventually upgrade to a secured credit card with a limit of 5,000 USD or more.

Daily payoff strategy (small limit)

  1. Put business charges on the card throughout the day.
  2. Each morning, check that the day's store deposits have reached the business bank account.
  3. Pay off the full card balance.
  4. Repeat every day until the credit limit has grown.

Requesting a credit limit increase

  1. Set a recurring reminder to call the card company every week, every two weeks or every month, depending on sales volume.
  2. Call the card company.
  3. Explain that the card balance is paid off every night or morning.
  4. Ask whether the credit limit can be increased.

Case: daily payoff and recurring calls

  • Starting point: a small Chase credit card with a very low limit, paid off every morning after store deposits arrived.
  • Actions: full payoff every day, plus recurring calls to the card company asking for limit increases.
  • Results: the limit rose from about 5,000 USD to 10,000 USD, then 25,000 USD, then 100,000 USD.
  • Later it reached spending of up to 1,000,000 USD per month without problems.
  • Repeated payoffs and limit requests over a few months grow the limit.
  • Ask the supplier to split charges, pay in full daily, and request increases monthly, as set out in the procedure below.

Paying a supplier when the limit is below the order

  1. Ask the supplier to split the charge into several smaller charges, for example 500 USD today and 500 USD tomorrow.

  2. Pay the card balance in full every day, not at the end of the month.

  3. Alternatively, use a debit card linked to your checking account.

  4. Call the card company every month and request a credit limit increase, pointing to the daily payments as proof. The same approach, in short:

  5. Use the business credit card to pay suppliers.

  6. Pay the card balance off every day while the credit limit is small.

  7. Build the limit over a few months by repeating the daily payoff.

  8. Consider a rewards card once the limit is larger.

Rewards math

1 min
  • Card reward points from ad spend equal the spend in the capped bracket (the spend level the reward limit applies to) multiplied by the earn rate.
  • Example: 100,000 USD of annual ad spend at 4x on the first 100,000 USD earns 400,000 points.
  • Check current terms, since reward rates and caps change.
  • At 1 to 2 reward points per dollar, a card used for 100,000 USD in monthly supplier and ad spend would earn about 100,000 to 200,000 points per month.
  • An AmEx Business Gold card used for 150,000 USD of ad spend earns 600,000 points.
  • Those points transfer one-to-one to 600,000 Delta SkyMiles (airline miles).
  • At 100,000 USD monthly cost of goods sold on the Capital One Spark card, 2% cash back equals 2,000 USD per month.
  • At 1,000,000 USD monthly spend across all cards, the reward equals 20,000 USD per month.
  • Break-even (the point where rewards cover the cost) on the Capital One Spark card's 95 USD annual fee from year two: 95 USD divided by 2% equals 4,750 USD of annual spend.
  • Spending 4,750 USD on the card in a year covers the fee.
  • Example: spending 5,000 USD in the first three months on a Capital One Spark Business card meets the 4,500 USD threshold for the 500 USD cash bonus.
  • The same spending also earns 100 USD of 2% cash back.
  • These figures assume the offer is still active.
  • Choose cash-out or spending on rewards according to a tax adviser's advice. The reward can be used as cash, to cover purchases or in other ways.
  • Confirm the tax treatment with an accountant before choosing how to use it.

Card split and bookkeeping

2 min

Keeping business charges on one or two cards

  • Use a business credit card only for business expenses.
  • Keep all business charges on one or two cards so month-end bookkeeping (recording money in and out) and reconciliation (matching card charges to your records) stay simple.
  • Choosing a separate card for each expense category also helps avoid hunting for charges during reconciliation.
  • Use the American Express card for advertising spend (Google, Bing, remarketing (ads shown again to people who visited your store), Facebook lead ads, Snapchat, Pinterest, TikTok and similar) and for shipping costs.
  • These categories earn 4X points.
  • Use the Capital One card for cost of goods sold and all other business expenses such as Shopify, email and virtual assistant tools.

Two-card setup

  1. Apply for an American Express Business Gold card. Use it only for advertising spend and shipping costs.
  2. Apply for a Capital One Spark Business card. Use it for cost of goods sold (supplier orders) and all other operating expenses such as Shopify, apps and virtual assistants.
  3. Keep spending mostly on each card as assigned, so that the monthly statements (the card's bill for the month) map directly to the books.
  4. Pay off both cards in full each month.

Separate cards per allocation account

  1. Get a separate credit card for each expense account (a bookkeeping category for one kind of cost), or a card with a different number if the issuer allows it.
  2. Pay supplier invoices with the supplier card.
  3. Pay the supplier card off from the COGS account.
  4. Pay operating expenses with a separate operating card.
  5. Pay the operating card off from the operating expenses account.

Statements, payoff and balances

  • A business credit card is usually backed personally, so the balance appears on your personal credit file (your personal record of borrowing). This is true even when the card is issued for a business.
  • Never carry a balance month to month.
  • Pay the statement in full every month to avoid interest and protect your credit.
  • Pay credit card balances off in full every month.
  • Any balance carried reduces the cash needed for other business costs and creates interest.
  • As sales grow, the balance rises faster, so repayment must keep pace with spending.
  • Use the business credit card only for product purchases and marketing spend, never for personal purchases.
  • Carrying personal debt on a business card is bad practice.
  • Use a credit card for the business as a whole, but only when the owner is personally comfortable with the credit arrangement.
  • Paying a supplier with a credit card covers the gap between a customer's payment and the payout from the store's payment processor.
  • The card balance then gives roughly 30 days or more to repay. So inventory can be ordered before sales money reaches the bank account.

Billing cycles and statement timing

1 min
  • Some credit cards have a 30-day billing cycle, while others bill in about 16 days. Amex is one of the latter in the example.
  • A card with a longer billing window gives more time before ad spend has to be paid.
  • The source describes Amex billing as roughly 30 days or more to repay.
  • Check the actual cycle on your statement. One example gives about 16 days for Amex.
  • Capital One Spark limits are based mainly on the owner's income and what the issuer will extend.
  • Very high monthly sales will not automatically unlock a matching limit.
  • Plan payments so the balance is cleared before the next statement closes.
  • Use the daily payoff method to make the balance clear before the statement closes.

Limits, estimators and charge card rules

1 min
  • American Express business card limits are not a fixed number.
  • The issuer sets them by purchase estimator (the bank's check of how much you are likely to spend) and risk. So a large monthly spend can still be declined.
  • Test a large purchase with the issuer's estimator before relying on it.
  • American Express charge cards must be paid in full each month.
  • In exchange, the issuer gives very high credit limits. No preset spending limit is stated, though a practical ceiling still exists.
  • (2023) The Gold and Platinum charge cards were described as having no preset spending limit.
  • They must still be paid in full every month.
  • Apply early for one as a second card when the business credit line is too small.
  • (2023) Qualifying for a US business card generally requires a US business with its information on file.
  • The 4X Amex reward on the first 150,000 USD of ad spend is stated without a time period in the source.
  • So it may not reset each year, as an earlier summary assumed.
  • Confirm the period with the issuer before planning spend around the cap.
  • The terms cited in the source reflect offers as of September 2021. These include the Amex and Capital One annual fees, the 4X and 2% earn rates, and the Capital One sign-up bonus.
  • Check current issuer terms before applying, since fees, earn rates and bonuses change.
  • The source states that the newer Capital One Spark Business card requires the balance to be paid in full each month.
  • Verify this with the issuer before assuming a revolving balance (a balance carried from month to month) is allowed.
  • Apply for a business credit card with a rewards program such as cash back or travel points. Ad and product spending can earn meaningful rewards.
  • The credit limit does not need to be high. Approval is what matters.

Redeeming points

1 min
  • Points can be redeemed (spent) for travel, gift cards, Amazon purchases, merchandise, PayPal payments and airline miles.
  • Airline transfers give the best value per point. The other options are simpler but lower value.
  • Redeeming points for Amazon purchases at checkout is a lower-value option than airline transfers.
  • It is a simple way to use points for ordinary purchases.
  • Amazon checkout accepts American Express (Amex, a credit card company) points when the Amex card is linked to the Amazon account.
  • At checkout, the buyer can choose to pay with points instead of dollars.
  • American Express points can be used at checkout with many merchants.
  • For example, a ride-hailing app such as Uber may offer to pay with points when it recognises the American Express card.
  • Only transfer American Express points to an airline partner.
  • Transferring to a hotel chain gives poor value per point.
  • Compare the points needed on the airline's own search before deciding where to move points.
  • American Express points transfer one-to-one to airline loyalty programmes such as Delta SkyMiles (Delta's airline miles program).
  • On Delta, a first-class one-way flight from the Minneapolis hub (the main airport the airline works from) to many US destinations cost roughly 25,000 to 70,000 SkyMiles.
  • The price depends on the destination and how close the booking date is.
  • Shorter routes such as Minneapolis to Denver sometimes cost under 20,000 miles.
  • Long routes and smaller airports cost more.
  • Using 100,000 Amex points for a single hotel night gives far less value than using the same points on a first-class domestic flight.
  • That flight would cost well over 1,000 USD in cash.
  • This comparison is the reason hotel transfers are avoided.

Portal or transfer

1 min
  • The American Express travel portal (Amex's own booking website) lets cardholders search flights, hotels and rental cars and pay with Amex points.
  • Compare its price with the airline's own points price before choosing between portal booking and a transfer.
  • When an airline award price (the price in points for a free seat) is close to the portal price for the same flight, booking through the portal can be preferable.
  • Booking through the portal keeps the airline's loyalty earning and status progress.
  • If the portal price is clearly higher, transfer the points to the airline instead.
  • One flight cost 56,000 Amex points in the Amex portal.
  • The same booking on Delta cost 46,000 Delta SkyMiles.
  • Transferring to Delta costs a small fee.
  • Booking on Delta earns more value for the airline loyalty account than the portal booking.
  • If the preferred airline is not on the American Express transfer list, transfer points to one of its partner airlines, such as a European partner.
  • Book the flight through that partner.
  • Check the partner's award availability (how many free seats it has) before transferring.
  • Flying roughly once a month on one airline and booking through the card portal when its price is close to the airline award price can build elite status (top loyalty level) without paying for flights.
  • The source reports reaching Delta Platinum Medallion (a high Delta loyalty level) this way.
  • A charge billed to a hotel room that posts to an American Express card as a travel charge can be covered with Amex points.
  • This works for purchases such as shopping inside a resort hotel that are billed to the guest room.

Funding supplier orders and payment methods

2 min

Timing and float

  • Float means using credit to pay the supplier before your customers' money arrives.
  • A business credit card can float supplier orders, with the balance paid off every day or two so no interest accrues (no interest builds up on what you owe).
  • Early on, with little credit available, supplier orders can be charged to a credit card.
  • Pay the card off each day as payment settles.
  • Customer payments to the bank take about 48 hours.
  • If no credit line (the amount a bank lets you borrow) exists, a debit card (a card that takes money straight from your bank account) can pay suppliers.
  • With a debit card, customer funds arrive about 48 hours later.
  • Paying a supplier immediately by ACH (a direct bank transfer between bank accounts) can speed up processing of the purchase order.
  • Use this when suppliers are slow to fulfil orders (slow to ship them).
  • Place supplier orders with a credit card where possible.
  • A credit card makes payments simple, and the card can earn points or cash back (money back on purchases).
  • As order volume and advertising spend (money spent on ads) grow on the same card, the rewards accumulate.
  • Free domestic ACH transfers (Brex, a business bank and card service) meant payments to suppliers arrived within about one day.
  • Recipients were notified within seconds.
  • This makes it practical to pay suppliers from a US company account (verify current terms).
  • (2024) Freight booking payment (paying for a shipping order): in the Payment section, click the "Stored Debit/Credit Card" button, which charges the company account automatically.
  • (2024) Leave the card dropdown on its preset card, tick the confirmation box, then click "Pay Now".

Choosing the payment method

  • Suppliers' payment options commonly include ACH transfers, checks and credit cards.
  • Bank transfers keep payment order-by-order, like credit terms.
  • Bank transfers eliminate the processing fee (the extra charge for handling the payment) that some suppliers charge on cards.
  • If a supplier requests a credit card processing fee (for example 4 percent), ask whether it accepts ACH or bank transfers instead.
  • Wise (an international money transfer service) and bank transfer are the two payment methods used to pay private suppliers per order.
  • Paying suppliers or CJ Dropshipping (a supplier that ships orders for you) through a wallet (a prepaid balance you load with money) makes little difference to the cost.
  • A wallet is useful only when you want a fixed budget for fulfilling orders.
  • Otherwise, paying directly by card or PayPal works the same way.
  • Some suppliers add extra fees when paid with American Express, which can cancel out the rewards.
  • In Australia, avoid Amex for supplier payments.
  • It is believed the same surcharge does not apply in the US.

Prepaying and card on file

  • Prepaying suppliers by card earns points on those purchases.
  • This only applies when you have a credit card in your name.
  • Replace a debit card on file (a card the supplier has saved to charge later) with a credit card once credit has been built.
  • The supplier keeps the card on file, so the card can be updated with the supplier later by email.

Personal liability, guarantees and co-signing

1 min
  • Suppliers generally do not care which credit card you use, as long as it processes orders.
  • A new business is usually personally liable (you yourself must pay) for its card until it builds its own credit history.
  • Put the business name on the card when possible.
  • You can update the card on file with a supplier by email later.
  • Amex personally guarantees you for any loans against the card.
  • If you take Shopify Capital (Shopify's business loan, which uses Amex), the business is liable, but you personally guarantee it (you promise to pay it yourself).
  • Review the terms and conditions carefully before using business credit for company loans.
  • (2023) For first-time business credit card applications, expect to co-sign personally (sign as a second responsible person) even though the card is in the business name.
  • (2023) Once business credit is established, you can remove yourself as co-signer.
  • A new business credit card usually still requires a personal co-signature and relies on personal credit in the early years.
  • A UPS Store mailbox address (a rented street address for mail) is a convenient option.
  • A standard forwarding address is an acceptable alternative.
  • A sole proprietor (a business run by one person with no separate company) can still open a separate card for business expenses only.
  • For an LLC (a limited liability company, a business type that separates you from its debts) or S-corp (a company type with a special tax status), a new business has no credit history.
  • The owner will usually have to co-sign the card.
  • If a business card application is denied while a credit freeze (a lock on your credit report that stops new checks) is active, lift the freeze and reapply.
  • Many banks offer business credit cards.
  • American Express and Chase were cited as popular options.

More rules worth knowing

1 min
  • AMEX Business Gold is used to maximize rewards on paid advertising.
  • It earns 4X points per dollar spent on ads.
  • It is suitable for businesses with significant ad budgets.
  • Separate virtual cards (card numbers that exist only online) can be created per advertising or software platform.
  • A virtual card can be closed immediately to stop recurring charges.
  • Closing a virtual card helps with free trial sign-ups.

Chargebacks, fraud prevention and processor holds

Section 18.15 · 15 of 24 in this chapter · 26 min read

In short

  • Keep chargebacks (customer reversals of card payments) under 1% of orders to avoid a payout hold.
  • Shopify can hold 25% of your payout money if you ship orders too slowly.
  • PayPal can hold your money for up to 180 days for compliance checks (rule checks).
  • Do not ship orders you cannot verify. A high risk rating usually means fraud.
  • Keep proof of delivery for every order.
More key points
  • PayPal can hold (keep back) or restrict (limit) your money. Call PayPal, send proof that orders were shipped and delivered, and keep tracking numbers updated for every order.
  • Move money out of PayPal often. Keep only a small balance there.
  • Use more than one payment method, so one ban does not freeze all your money.
  • Grow sales slowly, and never open a payment account in a country where you do not live.

Chargeback Rates and Shopify Reserves

3 min
  • Shopify Payments is Shopify's own payment service.
  • Shopify Payments places a reserve (money held back from your payouts, which are the payments sent to you) when your chargeback rate is above 1%.
  • A chargeback is when a customer asks their bank to reverse a payment. The chargeback rate is the share of your orders that end in a chargeback.
  • The reserve holds 10% of payouts. Keeping the rate under 1% avoids this hold.
  • Shopify Settings > Payments > Chargeback monitoring shows your current chargeback rate (example: 3.81%).
  • The same page shows whether payouts are held in reserve (example: 10% held due to a high number of chargebacks). Check this section to see whether you are over the threshold.
  • Two different holds exist. Do not confuse them.
  • The chargeback-rate reserve holds 10% of payouts and is tied to disputes (formal challenges by the bank).
  • The reserve payout for slow fulfillment holds 25% of payouts and is tied to delivery speed. Fulfillment means shipping and delivering your orders.
  • A reserve payout can hold 25% of your payout balance (the money waiting to be paid to you).
  • Shopify may do this when orders are not fulfilled fast enough, for example after about six weeks of slow fulfillment.
  • The hold can last 1, 2, 3 or 4 months depending on the case.
  • Shopify Finance > Payouts shows the payout balance and the reserve.
  • The reserve is the part of the payout balance set aside to cover disputes, refunds (money returned to customers) and chargeback risk (the chance of a chargeback).
  • Case: a store selling a niche product (a narrow, specialized product group) had a four-month reserve hold on payouts from roughly $30k in revenue, because orders were not fulfilled quickly enough.
  • The owner expected a payout of about $32k to $33k once the disputes the store won were recovered.
  • Takeaway: slow fulfillment led directly to a long cash freeze.
  • Case: a seller with unfulfilled orders had their Shopify Payments account banned after about two months, and the whole payout was lost.
  • A store with slow fulfillment can lose its payment processing (taking card payments) completely, not just a portion of payouts.
  • A public accusation by a rights holder (the owner of a brand or copyright) that a seller is a fraud can push loyal buyers to dispute charges with their bank.
  • Each dispute adds to the chargeback rate and its fees.
  • Threshold figures differ by source. Shopify Payments uses 1% as the reserve trigger.
  • Another source advises keeping the chargeback rate below about 1.5% of transactions.
  • Higher rates lead to fines and damage your payment processor profile (the record a payment company keeps on how risky your store is).
  • Use 1% as the working target on Shopify Payments.
  • Treat 1.5% as the outer limit cited elsewhere.
  • Each chargeback can incur an extra processor fee (a fee from the payment company) of $15 to $30, depending on the processor.
  • Older, 2021-22: A payment processor can hold back money as a reserve, for example an amount such as 60,000 USD, when refunds and chargebacks are high.
  • If the high level continues, the processor can close the account, and you can no longer take sales.
  • Older, 2019: A chargeback happens when a customer tells their bank that an order was fraud and the bank reverses the payment.
  • Chargebacks are the main reason payouts get held.

Cost of a Dispute

1 min
  • Shopify charges a fee of $15 per chargeback.
  • The full order amount is taken from the seller and returned to the customer.
  • Budget for both the fee and the lost revenue (set aside money for each) when calculating the cost of a dispute.
  • The fight window (the time you have to respond) for a chargeback is a few weeks, possibly up to about a month.
  • Respond within this window to contest the dispute.
  • Chargeback insurance (a paid policy that covers chargebacks) is only worth paying when a single event could ruin the business financially.
  • An occasional chargeback does not meet that condition.
  • The operator in this example (the person running the store) chose to keep that money instead of buying the insurance.

Chargeback Types and Dispute Response

1 min
  • Chargebacks come in two types: fraud with a stolen card, and customer dissatisfaction caused by a store error.
  • Each needs a different response.
  • Older, 2022: Avoid chargebacks caused by store errors through good customer support, honest communication and quick responses when problems occur.
  • Older, 2022: Chargebacks in favor of the customer are hard to reverse after proof of fulfillment.
  • Proof of fulfillment includes tracking (a code showing where the parcel is), delivery, a five-star review, ID, and FaceTime verification (a video call check).
  • A chargeback filed for "item not as described" or "fraud" is a different reason code (the code that says why the bank opened the dispute).
  • That type may require an attorney (a lawyer) or a small claims action (a simple court case for small sums).
  • Older, 2022: If a customer who received an item files a fraudulent chargeback (a false claim), consult an attorney.
  • A letter from an attorney or a small claims court can recover the money.
  • If a customer files a chargeback after a shipment, stop the shipment with the carrier (the shipping company) first.
  • Then submit evidence to Shopify.
  • Then contact the customer to check whether the chargeback was intentional.
  • Returning stock to the supplier (the company that provides your products) is a cost.
  • It is still better than delivering to a fraudulent customer.

Chargeback response procedure

  1. Stop the shipment with the shipping company.
  2. Submit evidence to Shopify support.
  3. Contact the customer to confirm whether the charge was intentional or a misunderstanding.
  4. If it was a misunderstanding, the customer can reverse the chargeback with their bank, and the shipment continues.
  5. If the customer does not respond, or it was fraud, arrange the return of the stock to the supplier.

  • The 2022 advice checks them before shipping.

    1. Read the fraud analysis reason shown in Shopify.
    2. Contact the customer to verify why the shipping address differs from their location.
    3. Optionally research the customer's email and phone number online to confirm they match.
    4. Cancel the order if verification fails.
  • Do not automatically decline orders that fail an AVS (Address Verification System, a check that compares the billing address and postal code with the bank's records) postal code check.

  • Older, 2021: Shopify Payments can hold a first high-value order for up to 120 days when the billing and delivery addresses differ.
  • In one case the hold applied to a 4,700 dollar order.
  • Contact the customer for an alternative payment before fulfilling.
  • Older, 2021: Handling a payment hold on an order with mismatched addresses:
    1. Contact the customer and confirm the order details.
    2. Ask the customer for an alternative payment method, such as PayPal.
    3. If the customer cannot pay another way, refund and cancel.
    4. Ask the payment provider for the reason and whether there is a threshold for address mismatch holds.
  • Older, 2021: When PayPal holds a payment, ask PayPal for the reason.
  • Check whether the account is new and whether a tracking number is required.

Verifying Suspicious Orders

1 min
  • Older, 2019: Fraud check for a suspicious order:
    1. Look for red flags (warning signs) such as fake-looking names, email addresses or phone numbers.
    2. Red flags also include an order far above your average order value (the average amount per order).
    3. Another red flag is an IP location (where the buyer's internet address points) that does not match the billing or shipping address.
    4. If you are still unsure, call the customer using the phone number on the order.
    5. Ask about the mismatch, for example the shipping state, billing state and IP location.
    6. Listen to whether the answers are consistent.
    7. Cancel the order if it is clearly fraudulent or the verification is not convincing.
  • Older, 2021: If an order triggers a fraud warning, verify it before shipping.
  • To verify: find the buyer online, check the phone number and IP address, and call the buyer.
  • Cancel any order that cannot be verified.
  • Never accept wires (direct bank transfers) or payments outside Shopify.
  • Older, 2022: Verify a suspicious order:
    1. Check the risk ranking that Shopify Payments assigns.
    2. Google the shipping address and check whether the customer's name is linked to it.
    3. Call the number given on the order.
    4. Fraudsters (people who commit fraud) usually do not answer, or the number is fake.
  • 2023: Handling a flagged high-risk order:
    1. Call the customer to find out why the order was flagged.
    2. Verify identity and order details.
    3. Process the order only once the risk is resolved.
    4. Decline the order if the customer cannot be verified.
  • Older, 2019: An order far above your average order value is a fraud warning sign.
  • Example: a $12,000 order when the average sale is about $500.
  • Check such an order before it is fulfilled.
  • Older, 2019: An order with a fake-looking name and an IP address from a country known as a common origin of online scams should be canceled if the suspicion is clear.
  • Cancel when the red flags are obvious and verification cannot resolve them.

Scam Patterns and Red Flags

2 min
  • Check payments (paper checks) and wire payment requests carry a high fraud risk.
  • Verify payments with the customer before goods are released.
  • Wire transfers (direct bank transfers) are legitimate for B2B orders (business-to-business sales), government entities and institutional purchases such as schools.
  • The scam pattern is an unsolicited buyer (one who contacts you uninvited) who makes an unusual request.
  • Unusual requests include: shipping to an unexpected country, using a freight forwarder (a company that moves goods for a buyer), or purchasing on behalf of someone else.
  • Another unusual request is mentioning an atypical (unusual) payment method upfront.
  • Decline international wire transfers from unverified buyers.
  • They carry over 99% probability of fraud.
  • Genuine customer inquiries focus on stock availability and delivery timeline, not payment methods.
  • If any hesitation arises about a wire request from an unknown buyer, refuse the order.
  • Overpayment scam example: a buyer requests a $20,000 wire transfer but sends $40,000, then claims it was an accident and asks for the overage (the extra amount) back.
  • After you refund, the original wire is reversed within 2-3 days because it came from a fraudulent account.
  • This leaves your account short by the full amount.
  • Always assume this pattern is fraudulent and refuse such requests.
  • Older, 2021: Large overseas orders (from other countries) that offer to pay shipping at double the product price are likely scams.
  • Verify the buyer's identity and organization before accepting such an order.
  • Older, 2020: Large orders from new buyers, such as a university purchase on 30-day terms (the buyer pays 30 days after the order), should be verified before they are accepted.
  • A safer approach is to have the supplier take the risk and pay a finder's fee (a fee for bringing in a deal) or commission (a share of each sale) of about 10% to 15%.
  • Older, 2021: Accepting orders by email from unknown customers who want to avoid Shopify checkout is a scam pattern.
  • Delete such requests.
  • Older, 2021: Do not ship to addresses in Dubai.
  • Do not allow sales outside your store's sales region.
  • Emails from buyers asking for Dubai delivery are often scams designed to cause losses.
  • Older, 2019: Red flags for a suspicious order include fake-looking names, email addresses or phone numbers.
  • They also include an order far above your average order value.
  • And they include an IP location that does not match the billing or shipping address.
  • Older, 2019: When red flags appear together with a fake-looking name and an IP address from a high-scam country (a country known for many online scams), cancel if verification fails.

PayPal Holds, Freezes and Rolling Reserves

5 min
  • PayPal (an online payment service) can hold funds (money) for up to 180 days for compliance checks (rule checks).
  • Exceeding 180 days requires proof of suspicious activity, which is very difficult to trigger.
  • Case: a seller accumulated $324,000 on a PayPal account held for exactly 180 days due to a compliance review.
  • The full amount was released after the hold period.
  • Without capital (money to work with) during that time, operations were strained.
  • If PayPal freezes funds for compliance and you need working capital for fulfillment, your only options are: taking a credit loan, finding a personal loan from a friend, or refunding customers.
  • There is no other mechanism to access frozen funds.
  • PayPal accounts can have $2,000 automatically deducted if the processor detects misinformation or policy violations claimed online.
  • This is a recently introduced rule.
  • PayPal processes 30-35% of typical ecommerce store transactions.
  • Reducing this dependency (relying on one payment method) is strategic due to recent policy tightening.
  • Older, 2018: PayPal often freezes an account when sales suddenly grow and asks for business documentation.
  • Expect this as a normal event once volume (the number of sales) rises.
  • Older, 2018: After a PayPal account is unlocked, PayPal may apply a rolling reserve (a share of each new payment held back for a set time) of 10 percent to new orders.
  • That money is held for 90 days before release.
  • Example: on a 1,000 dollar order, 100 dollars are held back for 90 days before that money is released.
  • Older, 2022: PayPal can hold funds for long periods on new accounts with high sales.
  • It may attach a rolling reserve to established accounts.
  • Keep PayPal as one payment option rather than the only method.
  • A share of buyers prefer it, and it increases total revenue.
  • PayPal can hold payments at the start of a store's sales.
  • It can also apply a second hold, called a delivery hold, until the product is delivered.
  • Money is only released after delivery, so plan cash flow (the timing of money in and out) with these holds in mind.
  • PayPal can hold funds from an order for up to 21 days when the account has processed few transactions.
  • Expect delayed payouts from a new PayPal account with little history.
  • PayPal can hold funds for up to 21 days... Shopify fraud protection: see above.
  • PayPal may lock accounts and hold funds, sometimes for two weeks or more, even after a large sale.
  • Plan for this risk, because it is not a question of whether it happens but when.
  • PayPal can hold funds in an account when orders look suspicious.
  • In one case the amount held reached $75,000 for up to a year before release.
  • Enabling auto sweep (automatic transfer of money to your bank) on a business PayPal account reduces the risk of money getting stuck.
  • Auto sweep does not eliminate holds.
  • PayPal can impose a withdrawal limit (a cap on how much you can take out).
  • In this example, the threshold was 18,000 EUR or USD: more than that could not be withdrawn in one go, and the cap applied per month.
  • To lift it, register a tracking app with PayPal to show the business is legitimate.
  • PayPal may withhold funds from stores without tracking numbers.
  • Older, 2021-22: The PayPal account should be connected to the primary business email, not a personal one.
  • If details such as name, address or email differ between the store, PayPal and other platforms, PayPal may freeze the account.
  • Logging into Shopify through a VPN (a tool that hides your real internet location) made the platform detect a login from two distant locations in a short time.
  • That triggered a fraud check.
  • The first payout was withheld for three months, which is very damaging to a business with high-ticket orders (expensive products).
  • Avoid logging into Shopify through a VPN and use a separate browser for admin work (store management tasks) instead.
  • When a payment processor withholds a payout, you may need to prove the item was delivered to get the funds released.
  • Escalating (raising the issue to a higher support level) with support may not speed this up.
  • So keep delivery proof for every order.
  • Older, 2021-22: Processing payments across borders, such as a US store paying out to a bank account in another country, goes against the terms of payment gateways (the payment services' rules) like PayPal and Shopify Payments.
  • They can withhold your funds for 90 to 120 days.
  • This is a serious problem for dropshipping (selling products that a supplier ships directly to the customer).

Resolving PayPal Holds and Restrictions

3 min
  • Older, 2018: To resolve a PayPal account hold (PayPal keeps your money for now), call repeatedly until you reach a useful representative (a support agent who can decide). Explain that you run an e-commerce business (you sell products online).
  • Older, 2018: Emails and live chat were not effective.
  • Older, 2019: Recovering a locked PayPal account (a locked account cannot send or receive money until PayPal reopens it):
    1. Send PayPal the proof of delivery (proof the customer received the order) and any other documents they request.
    2. Call PayPal directly, since email responses are slow and impersonal.
    3. Keep calling and speaking with different representatives, because a later call can reverse an earlier decision.
    4. Provide whatever documents are requested until the account is reopened.
  • Older, 2021: When PayPal holds a payment, ask PayPal for the reason.
  • Older, 2021: Check whether the account is new and whether a tracking number is required (a tracking number is a code that shows where a package is).
  • Older, 2022: If a PayPal account is suspended (temporarily shut off), keep the account under follow-up and contact PayPal regularly. Some suspended accounts are restored after reaching the right person.
  • Older, 2022: Meanwhile, keep other payment methods running, such as Shop Pay, Apple Pay, Google Pay and Klarna (other ways customers can pay).
  • Hold procedure, Source C: Procedure when PayPal places a hold on sales:
    1. Connect a tracking app (TrackShow was named) to PayPal and the store. Tracking numbers are then sent automatically when orders ship.
    2. When a hold appears, provide proof of shipment for the sample transactions PayPal requests. Examples are a supplier invoice (the bill from your supplier), tracking information or a shipping label.
    3. Expect the release after about 10 to 20 days once proof is accepted.
    4. Check the PayPal account regularly and resolve disputes early (a dispute is when a customer asks their payment company to take money back) to protect the account.
  • PayPal can permanently restrict a dropshipping account. Dropshipping means you sell products that a supplier ships directly to the customer.
  • PayPal may ask for fulfillment proof (proof the supplier shipped the order) and delivery proof. Sellers usually get these from their supplier.
  • Sending these documents does not guarantee the account is restored.
  • Recovering a restricted PayPal account:
    1. Send the fulfillment proof and delivery proof from the supplier when PayPal asks for them.
    2. Verify the PayPal account (prove your identity) with the registered company details.
    3. Keep syncing tracking numbers for every order. Syncing means keeping them matched automatically.
    4. Stay in contact with PayPal. Ask for the specific reason for the restriction, because some reviews are made by automated systems that a conversation can override.
    5. At the same time, open a second PayPal account with a different email address and verify it.
  • Step 5 conflicts with the advice on replacement accounts below. Both apply to different situations.
  • The second account in step 5 is a parallel option (used at the same time as the first account) during a restriction.
  • The replacement after a ban should wait until the cause is fixed.
  • Around 300 orders produced only one PayPal dispute. It was answered with a tracking number and documents.
  • Missing a registered company was given as a cause of restrictions. A registered company is a business officially recorded with the government.
  • PayPal accounts were verified with the registered company details. In the example, the company was a UK Ltd. (a limited company registered in the United Kingdom).
  • Not registering a company and not verifying the PayPal account led to about 15,000 USD being blocked.
  • Registering the company and verifying the account released the funds.

Tracking Sync

1 min

Tracking sync means keeping tracking numbers matched to your orders automatically.

  • Not updating tracking numbers on PayPal for each order is a likely trigger for restrictions. PayPal needs evidence that orders are being fulfilled (shipped and delivered).
  • Apps such as tracky.pay sync tracking numbers automatically from a supplier account like CJ Dropshipping to PayPal.
  • With these apps, every new order gets its tracking number without manual entry (without typing it by hand).
  • Connect a tracking app (TrackShow was named) to PayPal and the store. Tracking numbers are then sent automatically when orders ship.
  • To lift a PayPal withdrawal limit (a cap on how much you can move out to your bank), register a tracking app with PayPal. This shows the business is legitimate.
  • Keep delivery proof for every order.
  • Processors (companies that handle card payments) may require delivery proof before releasing withheld payouts (money they are holding back).

Withdrawing Funds Regularly

1 min
  • Prevent PayPal from holding funds by withdrawing regularly (move money to your bank), ideally daily.
  • Keep only a small balance in PayPal.
  • Transfer bulk funds (large amounts) to your business bank account.
  • PayPal may lock large balances for six months or longer.
  • Plan your cash flow (the movement of money in and out) around holds.
  • A hold on incoming payments at the start of sales delays your access to money.
  • A delivery hold keeps money locked until the order is delivered.
  • If frozen funds leave you short of working capital (money for daily running costs), the only options are a credit loan, a personal loan from a friend, or refunding customers (giving money back to buyers).

Diversifying Processors and Backup Payments

2 min

A processor is a company that handles card payments. A merchant account is the business account that receives those payments.

  • Use PayPal as an additional payment method next to Shopify Payments (Shopify's own payment service).
  • Do not use PayPal as your main merchant account.
  • PayPal can apply holds, such as seven days or 30 days of payout delay (waiting time before money reaches your bank), when volume (how much money comes in) jumps suddenly.
  • Keep PayPal as one payment option, not the only method.
  • A share of buyers prefer PayPal, and it increases total revenue (all the money you make).
  • Keeping all sales and working balances on a single payment account leaves the business exposed.
  • A ban (the account is shut down) can freeze funds for around 180 days.
  • Keep a backup payment option ready before problems start.
  • While a PayPal account is suspended, keep other payment methods running, such as Shop Pay, Apple Pay, Google Pay and Klarna.
  • Shopify Payments runs on Stripe (another payment company) in the background.
  • Shopify Payments has been reported to hold funds earlier than Stripe, around 20,000 to 30,000 USD in volume.
  • Stripe itself was reported to show holds around 35,000 to 40,000 USD.
  • Every processor can hold funds at scale (large amounts).
  • Stripe holds usually last about 30 days.
  • A hold can be extended by another 30 days if the dispute rate (the share of orders customers complain about) keeps rising.
  • Resolving holds quickly and lowering disputes can speed up the release and remove the hold.
  • Case: a Hong Kong company after PayPal holds. At about 30,000 to 40,000 USD in monthly PayPal volume, about 40,000 USD was held on a 21-day rolling hold (a hold where money is kept back for a set number of days).
  • The seller moved to a Hong Kong company with Hong Kong PayPal and Stripe accounts.
  • Later, the seller reported much higher volume without holds.
  • Opening a company in Hong Kong to get a PayPal account is a high-risk shortcut.
  • In one reported case, the setup cost about 700 USD, and the account was banned within a week.
  • In that same case, about 5,000 USD was held for 180 days.
  • Use a payment account that matches the country where the owner lives instead.
  • The two Hong Kong reports differ in outcome. The shortcut in ended in a ban, while the relocated seller in had accounts matching their new residence.
  • Use a payment account in the country where the owner actually lives.

Account Setup, Identity and Bans

1 min
  • Register a PayPal account in the country where the owner lives.
  • When you move, open a new account in the new country.
  • A mismatch between residence (where you live) and account location can contribute to bans and frozen funds.
  • Sales outside your store's region, and payouts across borders (money sent to other countries), expose the account to holds.
  • See and in the sections above.
  • Connect the PayPal account to the primary business email (your main company email address).
  • Keep name, address and email the same across the store, PayPal and other platforms.
  • After a payment account is banned, a replacement account in another country can be closed within days.
  • This happened with a US account opened two days after a ban.
  • Find out the cause of the ban and fix it before opening any new account for the same business.
  • Shopify Payments banned an account with unfulfilled orders (orders not yet shipped) after about two months.
  • The whole payout (all the money) was lost.

Scaling Volume Gradually

1 min
  • Scale the store gradually, meaning grow it slowly.
  • A sudden jump from a few thousand dollars to very large monthly volume can trigger a payment processor's risk review (a check by the processor).
  • Older, 2021: A new PayPal account went from zero to about $50,000 in sales within a few weeks.
  • Older, 2021: The rapid growth triggered a reserve (part of your money kept back) on the account.
  • A merchant account may place a reserve when chargebacks are high (customers reverse their card payments through their bank) or revenue rises very quickly on a new account.
  • Holds are likely when sales volume jumps sharply, for example from about 200 to 300 USD per day to 5,000 USD per day.
  • Match growth with proof of delivery.
  • At $100k-500k per month, chargebacks, payment processing holds and monthly processing limits become likely.
  • A payment processing agent (a paid helper who watches your payments) can monitor chargebacks, monthly volume and processing statistics, at about $8-12 per hour.
  • A payment processor can hold a first high-value order for up to 120 days when addresses differ.
  • Scaling into high-ticket products (expensive items) is safer with fraud tools (tools that catch fake orders) in place.
  • Older, 2019: Shopify fraud protection costs 1.2% per order.
  • Include this cost in your cost of scaling when you use it.
  • 2023: A new store with traditional merchant accounts that later added PayPal processed about $50,000 in sales in a short time.
  • PayPal flagged the account as high risk and held the balance for 180 days.
  • Sudden high volume on a new account can trigger holds.

Setting up Shopify payments and payment gateways

Section 18.12 · 12 of 24 in this chapter · 21 min read

In short

  • Shopify Payments is Shopify's own card payment service. Use it first, if your country supports it.
  • Check the country list before you build. If your country is not on it, use Stripe or PayPal instead.
  • Activate Shopify Payments under Settings > Payments before your store takes its first order.
  • Card fees come out of every sale. Count the net payout (the money that reaches you), not the order price.
  • New accounts can wait 21 days for their money. Plan your cash flow around that wait.
More key points
  • Shopify Payments (Shopify's own payment system) is needed for local currency and for selling in several countries. Without it, you are limited to one money type, such as USD (US dollars).
  • Open Settings > Markets and activate every country you sell to. If a country is inactive, buyers from there cannot pay.
  • Before you send visitors to the store, place a test order yourself and check that checkout works.
  • Keep your ID papers ready. A payment company can pause your store for a check at any time.

Shopify Payments: What It Is and What It Covers

1 min
  • Shopify Payments (Shopify's built-in card payment service) accepts credit cards, debit cards, Apple Pay (paying with a phone wallet) and Google Pay.
  • It runs on Stripe, a separate company that handles card processing and can request its own checks.
  • It accepts American Express, Discover and Mastercard, plus Apple Pay and Google Pay, without extra apps.
  • Shop Pay Installments (paying in parts) is built in. It is the equivalent of Klarna (a pay-later service).
  • The Shopify admin (the back-office dashboard) includes Shop Pay as a checkout option (the payment page of the store), payout tracking (money sent out to you), cashflow tracking (money coming in and going out), and chargeback management (handling customer card disputes that take money back).
  • It automatically shows options such as Apple Pay, Shop Pay and Mastercard at checkout.
  • Customer payments are deposited into the linked bank account. Customers can also use other payment methods alongside it.
  • It is managed under Payments in the Shopify admin. Analytics and finance reports are kept in the same Shopify business admin.
  • Shopify Payments is the preferred payment processor (the company that handles card payments). Linking a third-party merchant account (a separate account from another provider that takes card money) is possible but not recommended.
  • Use Shopify Payments whenever possible. Third-party processors add Shopify transaction fees (a fee per sale) on top of their own fees.
  • Setup needs only bank account details and takes about five minutes.
  • Setup is configured under the Shopify settings area and takes only a few minutes.
  • The bank details needed are the bank account number, the routing number (the bank's code for transfers) and the checking account number (an everyday bank account).
  • After setup, the processor accepts credit cards from customers worldwide.
  • After setup, the processor deposits the sales into the bank account.

Country Availability and Eligibility

2 min
  • Shopify Payments is available in specific countries only.
  • Older list, 2019: Merchants in Australia, Canada, Denmark, Germany, Hong Kong, Ireland, Japan, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Singapore, Spain, the UK and the US could use Shopify Payments. Businesses elsewhere were told to use Stripe.
  • Other list: Shopify Payments is available in the USA, Canada, Australia, Germany and the UK. In countries where it is not available, Shopify offers other payment options.
  • Shopify Payments works in dozens of countries, so banking limitations do not automatically block the store.
  • Check the country's availability before choosing another solution.
  • The Netherlands is an available country. A Dutch company with a Dutch bank account can use Shopify Payments to accept payments from customers anywhere in the world.
  • Availability may depend on the account's country of residence.
  • If it is not available, PayPal (an online payment service) can be added as a second payment option.
  • Shopify Payments is not always available to every seller, so check access before planning around it.
  • Where Shopify Payments is not available, such as Mexico at that time, use a payment gateway (the link that sends card payments from the shop to the bank) from the country's provider list.
  • Switch to Shopify Payments once it is available there.
  • If Shopify Payments is not available and 2Checkout (another payment company) cannot be used, connect the main local payment processor to Shopify.
  • Shopify connects to almost any processor, because it integrates with them (links to them).
  • Check the local processor's Shopify integration before signing up.
  • Shopify integrates with over 100 payment processors.
  • Stripe acceptance (how many payments go through) can differ by country.
  • Stripe India showed lower acceptance, and some banks blocked transactions. This led one seller to move to a company abroad.
  • Before launching in a new country, enter the shipping details for that country in checkout.
  • Also confirm that the payment providers work there. A checkout that fails for one target country loses sales before any ads are paid for.

Choosing a Payment Option

1 min

  • A payment gateway (payment processor) accepts card payments on an online store and forwards the funds to the seller's bank account.

  • Reliable access to one is the basic requirement for receiving store revenue (total money from sales).

  • Procedure to choose payment options for a store:

    1. Check which payment options are available in your country at shopify.com/payment-gateways.
    2. If Shopify Payments is available, start with it.
    3. If not, check Stripe as the alternative.
    4. Add PayPal (it needs a business account once business transactions begin, with company details and a fulfillment method, meaning how orders are shipped).
    5. If no suitable gateway is available at home, form a company in a country that offers them, such as the US or Hong Kong.

  • For beginners, use Shopify Payments if it is available, because it avoids most chargebacks and saves 1 to 2 percent in fees.

  • With Shopify Payments in a country where it is available, a store can often test products and scale to about 1,000 to 15,000 USD in monthly sales.

  • This works without a large company setup.

  • Starting out, the US owner's social security number (a US personal ID number) is usually enough to open it.

  • Enable Shopify Payments where it is available.

  • Stripe and PayPal are the other common and trusted processors. Shopify displays the methods supported by the processor you connect.

  • Alternatives when Shopify Payments is unavailable include Skrill (a payment service common in some Asian and African countries), plus Amazon Pay and Airwallex.

  • Amazon Pay and Airwallex require creating an account on their websites and connecting their API (a link that lets two systems talk to each other) to Shopify.

Activating Shopify Payments

1 min

  • Under Settings > Payments, click the button labeled Activate Shopify Payments.

  • Procedure to prepare the store before activation:

    1. Open Settings (gear icon, bottom-left corner).
    2. Open Store details and review the fields (store name, store email, store phone) so you know where to edit them later. Do not change the email or phone yet.
    3. Go to Settings > Payments and click Activate Shopify Payments.
    4. Choose Shopify Balance (a Shopify account that holds your money) as the deposit destination for the initial payouts.
    5. Leave all other settings at their defaults (the starting settings).

  • Procedure for activating Shopify Payments through Shopify Balance:

    1. Open Settings with the gear icon at the bottom left.
    2. Click Payments.
    3. Click Activate Shopify Payments.
    4. Answer the setup questions about where the money should go.
    5. Select a Shopify Balance account as the destination so payments land there instantly.
    6. Withdraw funds from the Shopify Balance account to your bank account.

  • Shopify Payments is set up inside Shopify so customers can pay by credit card, debit card or Apple Pay.

  • The money is paid out directly to the seller's bank account.

  • Of all the settings, activating Shopify Payments is the one that must be completed before the store is ready to take orders.

  • The billing address in Store details must match the address linked to the card on file with Shopify (the card saved with Shopify), since it is the account's billing address.

  • Create a separate Shopify Payments account for each store through the payment integration.

  • Do not link one Stripe account directly instead. Shopify Payments is powered by Stripe.

  • Each Shopify store needs its own Shopify account.

  • Several accounts can share one login email using single sign-on (one login that opens several accounts).

  • One merchant account number cannot be used across several websites.

Example: Activation in Australia

1 min

  • Shopify Payments in Australia costs 1.75% plus $0.30 per card transaction.

  • Other third-party payment gateways typically charge 2% per transaction.

  • Shopify Payments in Australia accepts major credit cards, Shop Pay, Apple Pay and Google Pay.

  • After activation, the Payments screen shows the last two digits of the connected payout bank account.

  • Procedure to activate Shopify Payments in Australia:

    1. Go to Settings > Payments.
    2. Click Activate Shopify Payments.
    3. Enter your bank account details (account numbers differ by country).
    4. Enter information about the products you will sell.
    5. Enter personal details and legal business name.
    6. Enter your business number (ABN, the Australian Business Number, a company ID in Australia; or a federal number such as the EIN, the US Employer Identification Number, a tax ID for US businesses).
    7. Submit and wait for Shopify to review the account.

  • Bank account fields for Shopify Payments and PayPal differ by country because banking systems are structured differently.

  • Fill in the fields the local form asks for, using the business bank account.

Setup Order and First-Day Readiness

1 min
  • Older advice, 2021. Set up your payment processors as part of the initial store build, so orders can be taken from the first day the store opens.
  • Older advice, 2021-22. Open the business bank account before connecting checkout payments during the store build, because the gateway setup needs it.
  • Payment gateways that take customer checkout payments must be linked to a business bank account, which receives the sales money.
  • Older advice, 2021-22. Checkout is not usable until at least one payment gateway is connected.
  • The recommended pair is Shopify Payments and PayPal.
  • Payment setup is the first gate to launch (the step that must be passed before the store can open for orders).
  • A merchant account is the intermediary (middleman) that accepts card payments and forwards the money to the seller's bank account.
  • Shopify Payments works with any bank and can be used without opening a separate merchant account at first.

Identity, Address and Account Verification

1 min
  • Older advice, 2021. Shopify Payments can be applied for in the payment section of the Shopify admin and is usually approved right away.
  • Shopify can check credit. If volume (how much you sell) grows, it may ask for identity verification (proof of who you are), such as a driver license or passport photo.
  • Account reserves (money the provider holds back) are uncommon.
  • Older advice, 2021-22. Shopify or Stripe may ask for further identification at any time after activation, even months later.
  • Provide the requested documents, and the account will be reviewed and activated.
  • Older advice, 2021-22. Both companies review new accounts because anyone can open a Shopify store.
  • The review aims to confirm the owner is a legitimate business that will deliver orders.
  • Older advice, 2020. If Shopify Payments places the account on standby (paused until checks finish) for identity checks, make sure the name on the account matches the name on the ID, including any middle name.
  • Update the account and reply to the verification request.
  • Processors ask for company documents mainly because a business that receives large amounts of money is treated as higher risk (more likely to cause losses for the provider).
  • Shopify Payments usually does not ask for a company until sales volumes become high.
  • Without proof of address, a seller cannot open Shopify Payments, so Stripe may be the only processor available.
  • In one case, the seller had PayPal restrict the account, and Stripe was used instead.
  • Stripe needs a tax number for the business owner or company during account approval. Every platform asks for the same tax number.
  • Have the EIN and tax residency documents (proof of where you pay tax) ready before applying.

Payout Destination and Schedule

1 min
  • A payout is money sent out to you. The deposit destination can be set to the Shopify Balance account or to a bank account.
  • Shopify Balance is recommended for the first payouts because funds are instantly accessible and can be transferred to a bank later.
  • Shopify Balance receives customer payments instantly, and money is then withdrawn to a bank account.
  • For beginners, this payout route is recommended over a direct bank payout.
  • The payout destination can be changed at any time.
  • Shopify Payments can be set to pay out directly to your own bank account, instead of through a third-party processor.
  • This example store uses Shopify Payments only, with no third-party gateway for payouts.
  • Older advice, 2020. Shopify Payments deposits into the bank account linked in Shopify.
  • You then pay each supplier yourself (the company that makes or ships your products) from that money.

Calculator and Checks

1 min
  • Check the gateway formula in the calculator against the payment rate your Shopify Payments account shows.
  • The example sets this at 5.5% of the order price plus $0.30.
  • Change the formula if your rate differs, because the gateway fee is the largest variable cost (a cost that changes with each sale) after COGS (cost of goods sold, what the product costs you) on a low-priced product.
  • Use the net payout (the money that actually reaches you after fees) in the finance sheet, not the gross order value (the full price the customer paid).
  • Worked example: a PayPal fee on a 500 order at 2.9% plus a 0.30 static fee (a fixed amount per sale): 500 × 2.9% = 14.50, plus 0.30 gives a processor fee of 14.80.
  • Use this same method to check any processor fee before entering it.
  • For a store that sells in one market only, the Shopify Markets fee input (the number you type in for it, Shopify Markets being Shopify's tool for selling in several countries) should stay at zero.
  • A store that uses Markets must weight the Markets fee (scale it) by the share of total revenue that comes from Markets orders.
  • Do not apply the full fee rate to all orders.

Plan Costs and Billing

1 min
  • Shopify Settings > Billing shows the upcoming bill.
  • The bill is issued at the threshold (a set sales limit, example: S$260) or at the end of the billing cycle (the period you are charged for), whichever comes first.
  • Settings > Plan: check the plan, the card rates and the billing cycle.
  • Shopify bills the store in cycles and charges for apps (add-on tools) and usage on the same account.
  • So app costs must be counted with the plan.
  • Shopify bills app charges and usage-based fees (fees based on how much you use), such as transaction fees, on the store account.
  • These are billed at the end of the billing cycle, or earlier once a usage threshold (a usage limit) is reached.
  • The Billing page shows the next bill date and the amount due. Check it before installing paid apps.

Stripe: Terms, Scaling and Account Rules

1 min
  • Stripe allows fast-shipping dropshipping (the supplier ships straight to the customer; orders arrive in 1-3 weeks).
  • Stripe does not allow slow-shipping dropshipping from overseas (orders take weeks, with high chargeback rates).
  • Dropshipping from US suppliers with fast delivery fits Stripe's terms.
  • Older advice, 2022. This applies to the terms above.
  • Older advice, 2021. Stripe may close accounts it classes as high risk.
  • Drop-shipping from China is one reason.
  • Check the provider's risk policy before building a store around a payment processor.
  • A Stripe account connected to a business bank account does not require a store URL linked to the LLC (limited liability company, a US business type) or a company web page.
  • An LLC does not need its own website to set up Stripe.
  • You can only use one payment processor at a time.
  • Shopify Payments is built on Stripe, so you cannot enable Shopify Payments and a separate Stripe account together.
  • Shopify Payments can cause problems once monthly revenue reaches about 20,000 to 30,000 dollars, or up to 50,000 to 60,000 per month, due to chargebacks.
  • Stripe is preferred for scaling stores (growing them) because it offers more payment features.
  • Once a store scales at high volume, consider Stripe or another processor for better features.
  • Stripe runs on the same infrastructure (the underlying systems) as Shopify Payments.
  • Older advice, 2020. If a processor stops supporting a store, check with the bank whether it is due to the industry.

Merchant Accounts and Gateways

1 min
  • A merchant account is the intermediary (middleman) that accepts customer card payments (credit or debit) and forwards the money to the seller's bank account.
  • Older advice, 2021. Authorize.net (a gateway service) is only a gateway, so it needs a separate merchant account at a bank or processor to deposit your money.
  • Start with Shopify Payments.
  • Older advice, 2021. When applying for a merchant account, state a realistic expected monthly volume, for example between $5,000 and $10,000.
  • Very high numbers look like more risk to the processor, and the volume can grow over time.
  • Older advice, 2021. If Shopify Payments is not available, open a merchant account directly with your own bank and connect it to Shopify.
  • You can connect it either through a direct integration or a gateway such as authorized.net.
  • Third-party processors such as Checkout.com that integrate with Shopify are a faster alternative.
  • Linking a third-party merchant account to Shopify is possible but not recommended.
  • Shopify Payments is the preferred processor.
  • Older advice, 2022. Shopify Payments has strict terms for supplements (vitamins and similar health products).
  • Sellers of such products may need a third-party merchant account instead.
  • Older advice, 2019: Shopify can work with other payment processors, but PayPal Payments Pro (a PayPal service for businesses) is not recommended.
  • You can instead open a merchant account through your bank and compare terms and rates.
  • Keeping Shopify Payments is simpler.

Other Payment Methods and Processors

1 min
  • Settings > Payments has a Manual payment methods section. It covers money taken outside the online store.
  • An order paid this way must be approved before it is fulfilled (sent out to the buyer). Older advice, 2021-22.
  • Settings > Payments also lists Third-party providers. These are other payment companies. They charge the provider's own rate (their own fee).
  • Settings > Payments also lists Alternative payment methods. These are extra ways for buyers to pay. They can be added on top of Shopify Payments or a third-party provider. Older advice, 2021-22.
  • Settings > Payments shows a "See all other providers" option. It lets you use a payment provider other than Shopify Payments.
  • Shopify Payments is still required to manage each market's base currency (the main money of that market). It is also required to offer local currency checkout (buyers pay in their own money).
  • So a different provider means giving those up.
  • PayPal (a payment company) needs a business account once business transactions begin.
  • You also need company details and a fulfillment method (how you ship orders).

Higher-Risk Processors and Restricted Products

1 min
  • A payment processor is the company that moves card money. High-volume payment processing (many payments at once) needs a higher-risk payment provider when sales spike suddenly (jump up fast).
  • PayPal and Stripe (another payment company) may freeze funds, which means they hold your money.
  • Authorized.net is named as a high-risk processor. It charges about 5-6%.
  • Payment processors may place a store under a standby review. This is a check that can happen at any time.
  • Keep your identity documents (ID papers) ready.
  • Stripe may close accounts it classes as high risk. Drop-shipping from China is one reason. (Drop-shipping means the supplier ships each order directly to the buyer.)
  • Check the provider's risk policy (their rules about which businesses they accept) before you build a store around a payment processor. Older advice, 2021.
  • Shopify Payments has strict terms for supplements (vitamins and similar products).
  • Sellers of such products may need a third-party merchant account instead. A merchant account is a special account that lets you accept card payments. Older advice, 2022.

Checkout Trust Signals

  • A store that only uses Stripe cannot set up markets. It must price everything in one currency, such as USD.

  • Local pricing shows customers prices in their local currency.

  • Without Shopify Payments, you cannot manage the base currency for a market.
  • Set the store currency in Settings to match the target market.
  • Without Shopify Payments (for example, when using Stripe), customers see only the store currency, such as USD.
  • With Shopify Payments, customers can check out in local currencies.
  • Use USD only for USD-targeted markets.
  • Set the store currency inside Shopify to your local currency, such as pounds for a UK seller.
  • Shopify Payments converts currency automatically for worldwide sales. Older advice, 2020.
  • If Shopify Payments is not available and another gateway (a payment connection) such as Stripe is used, checkout may show only the store currency, for example USD.
  • Shopify Payments lets customers pay in their local currency.
  • Use the local currency of each target market.
  • Use USD only when the store targets USD markets.

Procedure: Check Markets Before Sending Traffic

Traffic means visitors to your store.

  1. Go to Settings > Markets.
  2. Check which countries are active, for example United States, Canada, Australia, and United Kingdom.
  3. Activate every target country that is inactive.

Case: Inactive Markets Blocked Sales

1 min
  • Starting point: a newly created store had traffic and a high add-to-cart rate (how often visitors put items in their cart), but almost no sales.
  • Cause: only the primary market was active. Other countries were inactive, so buyers from them could not complete checkout.
  • Result: roughly 100 to 200 sales were lost within one hour before the market was activated.

Test Mode and Test Checkouts

1 min
  • If Shopify Payments is already set up, put Shopify Payments into test mode before placing a test order.
  • Test mode is a practice mode for payments. Use Shopify's own guide to set it up.
  • A test runs through the payment system without charging a real customer.
  • Go to Settings > Payments > Shopify Payments > Test mode and switch it on.
  • Place a test order to see how payments and orders flow through the store before launch.
  • To test payment providers cheaply, create a 95% discount code (a coupon that takes 95% off the price).
  • Then complete a real checkout using PayPal, Shopify Payments and Stripe, or the other providers you use.

Procedure: Test After Changing Currency or Payment Setup

1 min
  1. Make a test purchase yourself.
  2. Check that no error message appears at checkout.
  3. Repeat the test after provider updates. Payment providers sometimes update in ways that break checkout.

Fixing a Payment Error That Blocks Checkout

1 min

Procedure: Fix a Payment Error

  1. Check the Shopify orders to confirm customers add their details but fail to pay.
  2. Deactivate the payment provider in Shopify.
  3. Reactivate the payment provider.
  4. Create a 95% or 99% discount code (a coupon that takes that much off the price). Then place a test order yourself to confirm checkout works.
  • Uninstalling and reinstalling a broken payment provider app (remove it, then add it back) can fix setup errors.

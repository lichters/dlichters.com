Store policies and legal pages

Section 18.16 · 16 of 24 in this chapter · 52 min read

In short

  • Build five pages on your store before you open any ad or shop account.
  • The five pages are: privacy policy, terms, contact page, shipping policy, and refund/return policy.
  • A missing page can get your Google account suspended (paused).
  • Read every generated text yourself before you publish it.
  • Two working ways to contact you must appear on your contact page and in the footer, and both must be real. Email plus phone is the safe choice.
More key points
  • Remove every placeholder (dummy text, square brackets, "555" phone numbers) before you publish any policy.
  • Use the same policy text on your site pages and in the checkout settings, and link every policy in the footer.
  • Check each policy with AI, one page at a time. Send the pasted text, not the live site. Have a lawyer check custom cases.
  • Put the company name and email on every legal page.
  • Show the refund rules and shipping rules in checkout before visitors arrive.
  • Link the policy pages in the footer before you run any ads.
  • In Germany, the Impressum must be complete before the store goes live.
  • Your Terms of Service and Privacy Policy must be live before launch.

Store Policies and Legal Pages

1 min

  • Build five pages: privacy, terms, contact, shipping, refund/return. These cover every list above.
  • Add payment terms too. The cross-border list requires them.
  • Add About Us if you will approach suppliers.
  • Launch with all required pages live.
  • A missing privacy policy can get your Google Merchant Center account suspended. Repeated violations can lead to a ban (permanent closure).
  • Merchant Center flags your account by itself when pages are missing.
  • You must then request a manual review (a person checks it). This slows the process.
  • Create the pages first, before opening any ad or merchant account. This helps avoid suspensions and closures.
  • Google Merchant Center asks you to confirm that you read the policies. You also confirm that the account follows them.
  • These pages do not make the store fully compliant (following all the rules). Each platform handles other topics in its own setup.
  • You can refine policies after supplier deals are final. The launch goal is "supplier-ready" (working well enough for suppliers), not perfect.
  • Policies must be published on the site. They must match what you enter in Google Merchant Center.
  • Policies must be easy to find. Some ad platforms need easy access before they approve ads. Otherwise they may refuse to run them.
  • Privacy policy and terms of service must match what Google Ads requires.

Work order in Shopify

3 min

Compliance setup order (complete sequence)

  1. Generate the privacy policy with Shopify's privacy policy generator (a free tool that writes the text for you). Answer the company questions. Then request the document by email.
  2. Add the required extra privacy text to the generated policy, if it is missing.
    • A remarketing sentence (remarketing means showing ads again to past visitors) at the end of the introduction or closing.
    • A Google Analytics sentence (Google Analytics is Google's visitor-counting tool) as the last line of the third-party services section.
    • A Google "pref" cookie line as the last line of the cookies section. Cookies are small files saved in a visitor's browser.
  3. Generate the terms and conditions with Shopify's terms generator. Skip the trial (the free trial of Shopify). Enter company and website information. Receive the document by email.
  4. Review the generated terms, modify anything needed, then publish them on the store.
  5. Create or confirm a contact page with at least two contact methods, for example phone number and business email, optionally Shopify's contact form (a form visitors fill in).
  6. Create a shipping policy stating when orders ship, average transit time (time on the road), shipping cost, and any free or upgraded shipping options.
  7. Create a refund and return policy stating the refund rules, how to request a refund, how returns work, and how a return is started.
  8. Link all five pages in the footer menu.
  9. Add two contact methods to the footer.
  10. Confirm that accepted payment methods are displayed in the footer.

Publish order (second method)

  1. Go to Settings > Policies (the legal settings page).
  2. For each policy (return and refund, privacy, terms of service, shipping, contact information), either turn on the automated policy option (Shopify keeps the text up to date for you) or click Insert template (Shopify adds ready text that you must edit).
  3. Replace every placeholder (a marked blank such as a bracketed label) with your real business details: trading name (the name you trade under), business address, business phone number and contact information.
  4. Click Save, then Publish for each policy.

Generate with Shopify's free tools

  • Shopify has free privacy policy and terms generators. They ask for company information. They send the generated document by email, so you can paste it onto the policy pages.
  • Shopify Policy Generator (shopify.com/tools/policy-generator): enter company name, email address, business address and website link. Shopify emails a ready-made privacy policy or terms text that can be pasted without changes. Other generators on the same page create refund and terms texts.
  • Privacy generator, skip-trial step: at the bottom of the page, click the link labelled "I'll skip the Shopify trial now, just create my policy". Enter the company information and click "send me my privacy policy" to receive the generated document by email.
  • Generated text changes over time. Regenerate privacy and terms pages from the current generator. Replace older text when the generator updates.
  • Check the generated privacy policy regularly against the current store setup.
  • a privacy policy from Shopify's generator, used with only a few modifications, was roughly 95% standard Shopify text.

Privacy policy

2 min

Creating the page

  • Shopify admin steps (template route). Shopify admin is Shopify's back office.
    1. Click Settings in the bottom left corner of the Shopify Admin.
    2. Scroll to the bottom of the Settings page and click Policies.
    3. In the Privacy policy section, click Create from template.
    4. Read the highlighted instructions at the top of the generated text.
    5. Delete the highlighted instructions after reading them.
    6. Enter today's date as the last updated date.
    7. Replace the generic example domain with your store's actual URL (web address).
    8. Edit any other template parts that refer to your business.
  • Automated route: go to Settings > Policies, click Privacy policy, turn on the Use automated policy toggle at the top, then click Save. The same toggle is described as Turn on the automated policy option.

Editing the content

  • Delete the "notes to merchant" section for now. It applies only once you run ads. Update the privacy policy with ad-specific details before ads go live.
  • In the contact section, delete any phone number or physical location you do not yet have. Keep an email address there. The default is the email used at Shopify sign-up. It can be changed in the same place at any time.
  • Add the extra privacy text if it is missing: the remarketing sentence, the Google Analytics sentence and the Google "pref" cookie line described in the compliance order.
  • The privacy policy should cover data privacy and security as part of the full legal section.

Business name in the policy

  • The trade name (DBA, "doing business as", the name you sell under) is used as the store name in terms and privacy policies. The author gives no legal advice on whether the LLC name (the legal company name, such as a US limited liability company) must be included. Older (2023).
  • Conflicting values, both kept:
    • Practice: use the DBA as the store name and do not add the LLC name. Applies where the author's practice is followed.
    • Caution: the privacy policy may need to name both the store name and the legal LLC name, since they often differ. Unverified. A lawyer should confirm the exact wording required in your jurisdiction (the country or state where you operate). Older (2021).

Missing pieces

  • The ready-made policy set (terms, shipping, returns, customer support and data protection) contains no privacy policy text. Shopify's written policies list still expects a Privacy policy page, so write and publish it separately.

Terms of service

2 min

Creating the page

  • Shopify admin steps (Insert template route):
    1. Go to Settings > Policies.
    2. Click Insert template. Shopify generates the full policy.
    3. Replace any bracketed placeholders with your business name and support email.
    4. Optionally leave address and phone number blank.
    5. Click Save.
  • Full template procedure:
    1. Go to Settings > Policies.
    2. In the Terms of service section, click Create from template.
    3. Replace the sentence that names the operator of the website with your store name.
    4. Read the whole text and replace every generic item with your own information.
    5. Remove references to policies you have not created yet, such as the refund policy.
    6. Replace the link to the privacy policy with a real link to the Privacy Policy you just created.
    7. Fill in the contact information section with your details, or remove lines that do not apply.
    8. Click Save.
  • Shorter variant:
    1. Go to Settings > Policies and click Terms of service.
    2. Click Insert template at the top to have Shopify generate the template.
    3. Replace the link to privacy policy placeholder with a link to the Privacy Policy page you created.
    4. Replace the trading name placeholder with the store name.
    5. Replace the contact email placeholder with the support email address.
    6. Leave the business address and phone number fields empty if only an email address is provided.
    7. Click Save.

Both variants above describe the same Shopify route. Their wording differs, and both are kept.

What the terms must cover

  • Terms set the rules for using the website and buying products. They should cover payment, shipping, returns and warranties (promises to repair or replace).
  • The terms should state that the store may update them at any time and that changes are posted on the page. They should also cover correct use of the site, prohibited activities (things not allowed) and age restrictions.
  • Standard template text. Use it as a starting point and edit it to fit your business:
Terms and Conditions

By visiting our website and buying products from us, you agree to follow these terms and conditions. This includes correct use of the site, activities that are not allowed, and age restrictions. We may change these terms at any time, and any changes will be posted on this page.

Publishing and reading

  • The generated terms are the store's real legal terms, not placeholder text. Read them, adjust anything that does not fit the business, and only then publish.
  • In one store the generated terms were published unchanged. The owner must still read them before going live.
  • Templates are a starting point. Edit each one to match how your business handles orders, refunds and customer data before publishing.
  • Remove any reference to a policy you have not built yet. Link the privacy policy to the real page.

Shipping policy

3 min

Creating the policy (Shopify route)

  1. In Shopify Admin, go to Settings and select Policies. Open Shipping Policy.
  2. Get the Shipping Policy AI Prompt (a ready instruction for an AI writing tool) that starts with "You are a professional e-commerce operations copywriter". Copy it completely. The full text of this prompt is not included here, so take it verbatim from the prompt file and do not rewrite it.
  3. Fill in the Student Inputs (the blanks the prompt asks you to fill): store name, support email address (use support@yourdomain.com format), shipping region, handling time (time to pack and send the order), transit time (time on the road), delivery timeframe, and shipping method details.
  4. Use the standard timeframes below unless different circumstances apply.
  5. Create the policy using the prompt. Insert the generated policy into Shopify and save.
Stage Standard timeframe Ids
Handling (packing and sending) 3-5 business days (working days)
Transit (on the road) 3-7 business days
Total delivery 7-12 business days

  • These figures are rough estimates in business days. Before publishing, replace them with the delivery times your supplier and carrier (the delivery company) actually achieve. Promises the store cannot keep lead to customer complaints.
  • Which figure applies: the standard table applies to the usual case. The dropshipping table applies to supplier-fulfilled stores, as the source describes them. Both values are given here so you can choose the one that matches your supply chain.
  • Example values from 2026: orders leave in 2-3 working days and reach customers in 6-10 days.

What the shipping policy must contain

  • Shipping methods, estimated delivery times, shipping costs and international shipping details, and which customs duties (import taxes) or taxes the customer must pay.
  • Processing time, delivery times per country, shipping fees charged, and the domestic shipping time for the store's home country.
  • When orders ship, average transit time, shipping cost, and any free or upgraded shipping options.
  • Confirmation and tracking email, how shipping rates are calculated at checkout, which countries are served and how international shipping times can differ, and that customs or weather can delay delivery.
  • If the offer includes free shipping, state it clearly. Make the shipping cost terms transparent.
  • Google Shopping (Google's product listings) requires the customer to know the exact shipping cost before checkout begins. A free-shipping header must be matched by an explicit free-shipping statement in the shipping policy. Vague wording about shipping costs is a common reason for Merchant Center flags.
  • If products are built for one market, say so on the shipping page. For example, say that they need a 110V electrical system (the US power standard) and are certified only to US standards. This protects the store from electrical-compatibility complaints from buyers elsewhere. It is a condition-specific notice, not a general legal disclaimer. Older (2021-22).
  • Shipping and refund policies must be shown in checkout with the company name and at least an email address.
  • Text for the shipping policy prompt, with placeholders (marked blanks to fill in), from 2026:
Write a shipping policy for my Shopify store called [Your Store Name]. Orders are shipped within [X-X] working days and usually arrive within [X-X] working days. Cover the processing time, the confirmation and tracking email, how shipping rates are calculated at checkout, which countries are served and how international shipping times can differ, and that delivery can be delayed by customs or weather. Use [placeholders] for any values I have not given you.
  • Second shipping prompt, for a store with known figures, from 2026:
Draft a shipping policy for my Shopify store, [Your Store Name]. Orders leave our hands in 2-3 working days and reach customers in 6-10 days. The text must cover processing time, how checkout calculates shipping rates, international shipping, and the tracking email sent when an order ships. State that customs or weather can delay delivery. Put [placeholders] wherever I haven't supplied a figure.

Refund, return and exchange policy

4 min

Creating the policy (Shopify route)

  1. In Shopify Admin, go to Settings > Policies.
  2. Click Return and Refund Policy.
  3. Use the Return & Refund Policy AI Prompt (a ready instruction for an AI writing tool). Its full text is not included here, so take it verbatim from the prompt file.
  4. Fill in the Student Inputs (the blanks the prompt asks you to fill):
    • store name;
    • support email;
    • return window (the number of days a customer has to send an item back; recommended: 30 days);
    • condition requirements;
    • restocking fee (a charge for taking an item back into stock; recommended: none);
    • return shipping responsibility;
    • cancellation policy;
    • damage reporting timeframe (how long a customer has to report damage).
  5. Leave most other defaults as-is for now.
  6. Generate the policy.
  7. Paste into Shopify.
  8. Click Save.

Return window: values by context

Context Window Applies when Ids
Standard recommendation 30 days, no restocking fee Default for a new store
Dropshipping, defect cases 14 or 30 days money-back guarantee (you get your money back for faulty items) Defective (faulty), damaged or not-as-described items. Chinese suppliers usually do not accept returns ,
30-day guarantee in sales 30-day money-back guarantee Removes purchase risk (fear of losing money); shown near the purchase decision
EU shipping 14-day right to cancel or return for any reason Applies whenever the store ships into the EU (European Union), even if the normal window is shorter
  • Make the 30-day guarantee visible near the purchase decision.
  • If the store ships into the EU, keep the 14-day right to cancel or return for any reason in the returns policy. Keep it even when the normal return window is shorter.
  • Chinese suppliers usually do not accept returns. But they do refund or replace items that arrive damaged, defective or not as described. The return policy should therefore offer a 14-day or 30-day money-back guarantee for these cases only.
  • Dropshipping clause to adapt. Replace the bracketed placeholder with your support email and choose 14 or 30 days:
We provide a 14-day (or 30-day) money-back guarantee if you receive a defective, damaged product or if the product is not as described. Please contact us at [support email] and we will do our best to help. Please send us a photo or video as proof so our team can investigate. If our team approves your request, we will issue a full refund or a replacement, as agreed.

Return address and return conditions

  • For a new store without a return address, the owner's own address or a virtual address (a mail-forwarding address, not a real office) can be used as the return address (where items are sent back to). Customers pay the return shipping label (the prepaid postage sticker).
  • A returned item is accepted only if it is in good condition with intact (not broken), unopened packaging. Add these conditions to the return policy.
  • If you have no return address, remove or change that line in the generated policy.

Content of the refund policy

  • Rules for refunds, exchanges and returns, the conditions for returns, time limits and any restocking fees.
  • The policy should cover:
    • eligibility (who and what qualifies);
    • return window in days from purchase;
    • proof of purchase (a receipt or order proof);
    • non-returnable item types;
    • how refunds are processed and how long they take;
    • when partial refunds apply (a refund for part of an order);
    • when exchanges are allowed;
    • who pays return shipping and whether it is refundable;
    • sale-item rules;
    • how customers contact you.
  • Refund rules, how to request a refund, how returns work, and how a return is started.
  • Return prompt, from 2026:
Write a return and refund policy for my Shopify store called [Your Store Name]. Cover eligibility, the return window in days from the date of purchase, the proof of purchase requirement, non-returnable item types, how refunds are processed and how long they take, when partial refunds apply, when exchanges are allowed, who pays return shipping and whether it is refundable, the rules for sale items, and how customers can contact us. Use [placeholders] for any values I have not given you, and keep the wording plain and customer-friendly.

Specific product risks

  • Fashion products have high return rates, so return and shipping policies must be clear before launch. Suppliers should also agree to refund defective or wrong-size items.
  • Do not copy another store's policy. Write the refund, return and shipping text from your actual return window, conditions and exceptions. Check it against consumer rules (laws that protect buyers) in each country you sell to.
  • Before writing your own return and shipping policies, review established dropshipping stores' policies as a reference. Adapt the structure to your own supplier terms rather than copying their wording.
  • The policy should protect the business while being fair to customers.

Dropshipping return procedure

  • Open Shopify Admin > Settings > Policies.
  • Click the policy you need (for example Return and refund policy) to generate the default template.
  • Replace the store's contact email with your support email.
  • Insert a return address if you have one. Otherwise, remove or change that line.
  • Adjust any other line to your needs.
  • Copy the finished text into a page, or save it in the policies section.

Contact page and contact details

2 min

Contact page

  • Shopify, the online store software you use, gives every store a default contact page. Add its link to the footer, the strip at the bottom of every page.
  • The default page named Contact is already there: go to Online Store > Pages > Add page. If the store is locked with a password, remove the password before you check the page.
  • Create or confirm a contact page with at least two contact methods, for example a phone number and a business email. You can also add Shopify's contact form.
  • Use a business email, not a personal email, on the contact page and in the footer.
  • Use a support mailbox on the store's own domain (your web address), for example support@yourstorename.com. Use the same address in the return policy, shipping policy and contact page. Make sure someone reads it.

Filling the contact information section

  1. Open the policy in Shopify Settings > Policies, then scroll to the contact information section.
  2. Click the template option. A template is a ready-made text, and this one fills in your store details for you.
  3. Confirm the email address uses your real domain. It can be a support or sales address.
  4. Add a phone number, labelled as a call option.
  5. Add your hours of operation, for example 9am through 5pm EST (Eastern Standard Time, a US time zone).
  6. Check that there is no placeholder text (dummy text left in by mistake). Then click publish.
  • A physical address is not needed in this section.
  • For a US-based store, a valid hours entry can read 9am through 5pm EST, with the time zone stated. Only the contact section of the policies needs this. Customers rarely see it, but Google's crawler reads it. A crawler is Google's robot that reads your pages.

Real, working details

  • Every email address and phone number in policies and contact pages must be real and current.
  • In every policy page, use your correct real email address and real phone number. Remove any "555" placeholder phone number. Delete all bracketed or template filler text.

Footer, menus and visibility

2 min

Footer content

  • Create and link these pages in the footer menu before you launch traffic (visitors arriving from ads or search): privacy policy (use a Privacy Policy Generator), terms and conditions (use a Terms and Conditions Generator), contact us, shipping policy, refunds and exchanges.
  • Add two contact methods to the footer.
  • Show the accepted payment methods in the footer.
  • Link the refund, privacy, terms of service and shipping policy pages in the footer before you submit to Google.
  • The refund, shipping, privacy and terms pages should appear in the footer Quick Links menu, a small link menu, and must not be hidden. Make them easy to find before you run paid ads. These pages are required parts of the storefront (the part of the store customers see).
  • Put a footer link to the policy pages so customers can find them easily.
  • The footer phone number and address should match the Contact Us page details.

Creating the footer menu (Shopify)

  • Create the menu under Online Store > Navigation > Add menu. Name it, for example, "Our policies". Add a menu item for each policy page: Privacy policy, Terms of service, Shipping policy, Return and refund policy, and optionally Contact. Then click Save menu.
  • In the theme footer:
    1. Go to Online Store > Themes > Customize.
    2. Open the footer section in the theme editor.
    3. Click Add block, choose Menu, and select the policy menu.
    4. Rename the heading from "Quick links" to "Our policies".
    5. Check that all policy links appear in the footer, then save.
  • Main menu dropdown. A dropdown is a menu item that opens a list:
    1. In the main menu, click Add menu item and name it Our Company.
    2. Link it to the About Us page.
    3. Drag the item under Our Company so it becomes a dropdown.
    4. Add the child items Contact Us, Shipping, Easy Returns, Price Match, Privacy and Terms of Service. Link each one to its matching page.
    5. Click Save menu.

Easy to find

  • Make the refund and return policy clear and easy to find. The website footer is a recommended place for it.
  • Terms and privacy pages must be easy for customers to find. Some traffic platforms refuse to run ads without easy access.
  • Policies saved under Settings are also linked in the checkout footer. Add the pages and footer links afterwards, so visitors can find them.

Checkout legal settings

2 min

How the checkout gets its policies

  • The checkout (the page where customers pay) is hosted separately from your main site. Footer links do not appear there. So enter the legal policies separately, even though the checkout looks like it is on your domain. Older (2021-22).
  • Policies entered in Shopify's policy settings are the texts linked at checkout. Fill them in there first to meet the checkout requirement.
  • Policies in the legal settings are linked in the checkout footer. They do not appear automatically in the storefront footer, so add them to your online store menus.
  • Legal pages must be visible in the checkout, because customers enter personal and card details there.
  • Publish each policy after you save it. Each policy shows "No policy set" until you add one.

Copy the policy text into the checkout

  1. Go to Online Store > Pages and open the Refund policy page.
  2. Select all the text and copy it.
  3. Go to Settings > Legal and paste it into the Refund policy box.
  4. Repeat for the Privacy policy, Terms of service and Shipping policy pages.
  5. Make sure each policy in Legal matches the text on your site exactly.
  6. Click Save.
  • Do not keep two different versions of the same policy, one on the site and one in the checkout. Pages and the Legal section must contain identical text.
  • enter the return and shipping policy, privacy policy and terms of service in Settings > Legal. These feed the checkout page, and their text must match the corresponding site pages exactly.

Placeholders, sample values and accuracy

2 min

Placeholders to remove

  • Do not publish a template with unfilled placeholders, such as "(INSERT TRADING NAME)", "[INSERT BUSINESS ADDRESS]" or "[INSERT BUSINESS PHONE NUMBER]". Replace them with your real business details before you publish.
  • Templates contain bracketed placeholders. Replace each one with real store information before you publish. Leaving a placeholder, such as the trading name (the name you sell under) or the privacy policy link in the terms, makes the page incomplete.
  • Shopify's generated policy texts contain bracketed placeholders and instruction notes. Replace every bracket with your store name, contact email and address. Delete the notes before you publish.
  • Sample values left in a prompt's input fields, such as a template's example brand name, carry into the generated policy or About Us text. Replace or delete every sample value before you paste the output into Shopify.
  • Placeholder text, bracketed template fields and fake phone numbers such as 555-555-5555 are flagged by Google as signs of an unprofessional or incomplete store.
  • Shopify's policy generators insert placeholder text. Check and edit every generated policy.
  • Confirm there is no placeholder text before you publish.
  • Suppliers may click through your policies during review. A policy with placeholder links, missing sections or links to pages that do not exist makes the store look incomplete. Remove or fix every such item before you submit the store for approval.
  • Templates contain placeholders that must be replaced before submission. Review every generated policy under Shopify Admin > Settings > Policies.

Revising with real figures

  • After a generator (such as ChatGPT, an AI writing tool) writes a policy, reply in the same chat with your real figures. Ask for a revised version. Include the exact processing days, delivery days, return window and contact email in each revision, so the sample numbers are replaced.
  • In every policy page, replace the placeholders for the store name, the privacy policy link and the contact email, one at a time.

Accuracy and reality

  • Policies must describe what the store actually does. They must not just exist as placeholders. In most US states, the core legal pages (privacy policy, terms of service, return and refund policy) are binding legal documents.
  • Every page has to describe what the shop really does in practice.
  • Promises the store cannot keep lead to customer complaints. Replace rough delivery estimates with actual figures before you publish.
  • Conditional statements, such as financing availability, exclusions, sales tax by state, or a shipping restriction to certain states, must link to a page that explains the details. Without that, the visitor becomes confused and worried. Older (2021-22).
  • Statements such as "Some exclusions apply" or "No shipping to" a state, with no explanation or link, make a store look unreliable. State the restriction plainly and explain it, even when there is a good reason for it. Older (2021-22).
  • The price match guarantee can be stated clearly as applying to the listed price before coupon codes. This helps customers understand it before they order. Older (2023).

Google review requirements

1 min
  • Contact information that Google's crawlers read should list at least two contact methods, recommended as email plus phone, along with business hours. An address is not required for this business model. But the email and phone must be real and working.
  • Adding a short summary, such as a table or a few key points, to policy pages helps Google's crawlers read the key details, such as return and shipping terms.
  • Google Merchant Center (Google's account for listing your products) flags accounts automatically when required pages are missing. You must request a manual review.
  • Policy pages must match the policies entered in Google Merchant Center.
  • Google Ads (Google's paid advertising) requires privacy policy and terms of service pages that match its requirements. Check them every time the generator updates.
  • A missing privacy policy can get your Google Merchant Center account suspended (shut off). Repeated violations can lead to a permanent ban.

Country-specific content

1 min

  • If the store ships into the European Union, keep the 14-day right to cancel or return for any reason in the returns policy. This is a cooling-off right, meaning the customer can change their mind.
  • Check the consumer rules in each country you sell to when you write refund, return and shipping text.

Some countries require a legal notice page with your business details. The Impressum is that page. The rules below apply in Germany.

  • In Germany, an online store must show a legally required legal notice (Impressum) with the business information on the website.
  • Missing information can lead to warning letters (formal legal warnings) from lawyers and complaints from customers.
  • The Impressum must be complete before the store goes live (open to customers).
  • Check the legal requirements (legal rules) of the country you sell from and the countries you sell to.

Playbook › 9. Meta and Facebook ads

Campaign structure: CBO, ABO, objectives and budget setup

Section 9.9 · 9 of 22 in this chapter · 47 min read

In short

  • Meta ads have three layers: campaign, ad set and ad. Set each thing at the right layer.
  • For a new store, test interests with ABO, which gives each ad set a fixed budget.
  • Scale winners with CBO, which uses one budget for the whole campaign. Do not scale with ABO.
  • Sources disagree on structure and budget, so test both in your own account.
  • Wait for enough purchase data before you switch structures.
More key points
  • Test one idea at a time. Each ad set holds one interest and one message.
  • Choose Purchase as the goal for tests. Clicks and likes do not show who buys.
  • Use ABO (you set the money per ad set) for tests. Use CBO (one budget for the whole campaign) only after you know a winner.
  • Keep the account simple: a few campaigns, not dozens.
  • Keep winners, turn off losers, and stop tests after a short time.
  • Set the start to midnight on the next day, in the ad account's time zone, so the budget spreads over the day.
  • Start with the recommended setup. Change it only where it fails to make profit.
  • Do not connect an Instagram account to the Facebook ad.
  • Do not limit ads to certain hours. Facebook learns the best times in about 1-2 days.
  • Wait for "All edits saved" before leaving an ad page, so your last change is kept.

Account Hierarchy and Levels

2 min
  • Meta (the company behind Facebook and Instagram) runs Ads Manager, its ad tool, with three levels: campaign (the goal and budget strategy), ad set (budget, audience, placements, which are where the ad appears, and message destinations, where a click leads), and ad (identity, which is the page or account the ad comes from; creative, which is the picture, video or text; and the ad name). Settings must be made at the correct level, so a destination or audience change made at campaign level will not apply to the ad set.
  • The ad account (the account that holds all your ads) holds campaigns, campaigns hold ad sets, and ad sets hold the ads. Each campaign has one objective (the goal, such as sales), and each ad set holds one audience (the people the ad is shown to).
  • At campaign level the optional budget is set. At ad set level the countries, age, gender, interests (topics people like, used to find buyers), placements, schedule and, under ABO (Ad set Budget Optimization, a fixed budget set on each ad set), the budget are set. At ad level the media, copy (the words around the ad), headline and destination link are set. One campaign holds many ad sets, and each ad set holds many ads.
  • The campaign defines the objective (sales, traffic or leads), the ad set holds targeting (choosing who sees the ad: countries, audiences, placements, start date and time), and the ad combines the creative (video, image or carousel, which is a swipeable set of images) with the ad copy.
  • Settings are entered from the top level down, so campaign settings are set before the ad set and the ad.
  • Settings made at a higher level affect everything below it, so the structure must be understood before building.
  • In the Power Editor hierarchy (Meta's older advanced ad tool), the campaign represents the industry or theme, each ad set targets one precise interest (a topic a person likes, such as yoga), and each ad holds the creative shown in the feed (the main scrolling list of posts).
  • in a Facebook campaign, ad sets sit inside the campaign, ads sit inside the ad sets, and the audience is chosen at the ad set level. Name ad sets after their audience, for example website visitors within 7 days.

Campaign Objective and Buying Type

5 min
  • For new Meta sales campaigns, choose the Sales objective. Awareness (showing your brand to many people), traffic (clicks to a site), engagement (likes and comments), leads (sign-ups), and app promotion (app installs) are not recommended for direct-response ad accounts that aim to make money. Direct-response means ads that aim for an immediate sale or sign-up.
  • Set the objective that matches the real goal, such as conversions (tracked actions like a sale or sign-up) for leads or sales, or catalog sales (sales from a product list, called a catalog) for product sales. Choosing another objective, such as traffic or engagement while wanting sales, to steer who sees the ad is outdated.
  • Campaign objective: set Conversions, unless you are driving traffic for retargeting (showing ads again to people who already visited your site).
  • Choosing the purchase conversion objective targets audiences with high purchase intent (people likely to buy), so CPMs are higher than in engagement campaigns. CPM is the cost to show your ad 1,000 times. Interest targeting determines the first sales in early testing.
  • Create > Choose a buying type: Auction (Meta bids for each ad view automatically). Keep Auction for new sales campaigns and do not switch to Reach and frequency or other manual buying types.
  • Campaign details: keep Buying type on Auction, keep Campaign objective on Sales, and leave Product information untouched, because it only makes Meta suggest products to people and is not needed for a basic setup.
  • Reach and frequency campaigns use buying type Reach and Frequency and objective Reach. When asked, set how many impressions (views) per person per day and over how many days.
  • Total impressions per person = impressions per day multiplied by number of days. Example: 2 impressions per day for 7 days equals 14 impressions in total.

Sales Campaign Creation Procedure

  1. In Ads Manager, confirm you are working in the correct ad account.
  2. Click Create campaign.
  3. Choose Sales as the campaign objective.
  4. Choose Manual sales campaign instead of Advantage+ shopping campaign (Meta's automated sales campaign).
  5. Name the campaign with today's date, the product name and CBO.
  6. Turn on campaign budget optimization (CBO).
  7. Set the total test budget.
  8. Set the campaign bid strategy to highest volume (get the most results possible).
  9. Continue to the ad set level.

Sales Campaign Creation Procedure

  1. Click Create.
  2. Choose the Sales objective, because the main goal is driving sales.
  3. Click Continue and start with a manual sales campaign.
  4. Scroll down and switch off Advantage campaign budget (Meta's name for CBO), so no budget is set at campaign level during testing.
  5. Name the campaign: product name followed by 'audience testing campaign'.

Full Launch Procedure for a New Store

  1. Create a campaign with the objective Sales, choose Manual campaign setup (not Advantage+/CBO), and name it after the store.
  2. Set budget optimization to ABO (budget set per ad set) so each interest test gets the same budget.
  3. Confirm the Meta pixel (a small tracking code on your store that reports sales to Meta) is connected and that the pixel ID (the number that identifies your pixel, last three digits) matches the one shown in the Shopify Facebook app (the link between your Shopify store and Facebook).
  4. Set the conversion event to Purchase. 4a. Set the daily budget per ad set to $5 and schedule the start for the next day at 6:00 a.m. (the source's own choice; 12 p.m. or 12 a.m. also work).
  5. Set location to Switzerland, age minimum 25+, and leave the first ad set as Broad (no interests, a wide audience) with German as the language, because the ads are in German.
  6. Leave placements on Advantage+ placements (Meta chooses where the ad appears automatically).
  7. Select the correct Facebook Page and do not connect an Instagram account, so clicks go directly to the landing page (the page a visitor arrives on).
  8. Use manual upload (not a catalog) with a single image or video, write primary text, headline with the offer, and description, and set the CTA (call to action, the button text).
  9. Duplicate the ad set to create four ad sets with the same creative setup, each testing a different interest, then review and publish.

Campaign Setup for Creative and Audience Tests

  • Recommended testing budget and structure: start with the recommended testing budget and follow the recommended campaign structure, creatives and budgets. Do not improvise the structure on the first launch.
  • Keep one product per Meta ad campaign; every additional product you sell needs its own separate campaign so the algorithm (Meta's automatic system) can optimize for that product.

Budget Optimization: CBO Versus ABO

4 min

Definitions

  • CBO (Campaign Budget Optimization, now called Advantage campaign budget): one budget is set on the campaign, and Meta distributes it across the ad sets.
  • ABO (Ad set Budget Optimization): a budget is set on each ad set, and each ad set spends its own budget.
  • Principle: CBO is used for scaling (spending more on what already works) because Meta distributes one campaign budget across the best-performing ad sets. ABO is used for testing because it forces a fixed budget into each ad set.
  • Principle: ABO keeps spending under your control because a new pixel has no purchase data for Meta to optimize (improve the ads automatically) with.
  • CBO works by sending several copies of the same interest to different pockets of the audience (small groups inside the audience), so the campaign can shift budget to the pocket that performs best. For example, a $10 daily budget at a $10 CPM reaches about 1,000 people per day, which is a tiny share of a 30 million audience.
  • Under CBO, after about a week the campaign shifts most budget to the best interest ad set. If that ad set drops off, budget flows back to the others.

  • ABO is not a scaling method and should not be used by beginners to scale, because it burns the budget fast. Scaling with ABO is a mistake; move winners to a CBO for scaling.
  • ABO only makes sense with a large budget reserve (money kept aside beyond the daily spend).
  • Use CBO only for scaling with known winning interests and creatives (the ads themselves), because CBO would otherwise put most money on one interest and leave the rest of the test with none.
  • A new store that tests interests should use ABO rather than CBO, so that each interest receives the same fixed budget for a fair comparison. CBO is better used later when scaling winners.
  • To decide between CBO and ABO for your account, run the same proven interest in both structures for a set test period and compare ROAS (return on ad spend, money back per dollar spent) and purchases. Keep ABO for testing new interests, and use CBO for scaling only once an interest is already profitable on ABO.
  • Test both CBO and ABO in your own account, since the better structure depends on the specific ad account.
  • Use ABO campaigns for creative testing, because the budget is fixed per ad set and each creative can be read on its own. When a winner is scaled into CBO or cost-cap campaigns (a limit on what you pay per result), Meta controls the spend allocation, so put at least two to three creatives in each ad set to keep delivery from concentrating on a single ad.
  • In a separate ABO test campaign the budget is set at ad set level, so every creative gets spend regardless of early results. In a scaling CBO, some new creatives may get little or no spend, so you cannot tell whether they work.
  • Running an ABO scaling ad set alongside a separate testing ad set that targets the same interest usually makes the testing sets lose delivery (stop being shown).
  • Stay on CBO while testing, because ABO means funding every ad set manually, which takes a large budget. Let the algorithm allocate spend, and add bid or cost caps later once the pixel has data.
  • Switch to ABO only after the pixel has real purchase data.
  • Switch from CBO to ABO only after around 80-100 website purchases from the CBO, with rich data (a large amount of detail from your ads) from custom ads and high-intensity testing.
  • Move from ABO to CBO or other campaign types only after the pixel has collected good purchase data, because Meta can then identify likely buyers and allocate spend itself.
  • Use ABO only when testing several different products at once, and not as a beginner, because ABO uses up the budget quickly. Use CBO for a single product test and for scaling.
  • Lifetime budget optimization (a budget for the whole run of a campaign, not per day) is hardly ever recommended. It has specific uses, such as some Black Friday setups, and is only used in those cases.

Conflicting Position on the Test Budget Structure

1 min
  • Source D and other sources recommend a CBO test (Sales objective, CBO, highest volume) as the standard test structure.
  • Source C recommends ABO for testing and says CBO was unreliable for one product: CBO broke even (made no profit) or lost money, so ad set budgets were used instead.
  • Source C's Advantage+ campaign note (Advantage+ is Meta's automated campaign type): Advantage+ campaigns are scaled with the same strategy used for other campaigns.
  • Testing a CBO that holds only one winning creative limits ad delivery; add two to three creatives even when one ad keeps producing sales.

Case: CBO Failed, ABO Worked

1 min
  • Starting point: a profitable ad with a ROAS of roughly 2 to 3, and a testing-phase CPA (cost per acquisition, what one sale costs you) of about 10 to 50 dollars.
  • Step 1: the ad was duplicated into a CBO with three ads and a $200 budget, and CPA rose with almost no sales.
  • Step 2: a second CBO with a $20 budget and the same ads also failed.
  • Step 3: the ad in a separate testing campaign with a $5 budget performed well.
  • Result: CBO was unreliable for this product because Facebook shifts budget between ads and removing weaker ads disrupted delivery, so ABO was used instead.

Case: Budget Flow in a Seller's CBO

1 min
  • The seller used campaign budget optimization (CBO) on Facebook instead of ad set budgets (ABO). Most of the spend flowed to the broad interest and online-shopper audiences.

Mistakes With Per-Ad-Set Budgets

1 min
  • Budgets set per ad set make a profitable ad set stop at its daily cap (the most it may spend per day) while break-even ad sets keep spending. This forces constant manual reallocation (moving money by hand), so use CBO in campaigns that need it.
  • With ABO, a budget set per ad set is fixed, for example 25 dollars for each of four ad sets. Meta offers a setting that lets it move part of an ad set budget to other ad sets, which undermines manual control, so ABO is not recommended.

CBO Settings and Budget Figures

4 min

Setting Procedure

  • Set one total daily budget across all targeting groups (the groups of people you target) and let Facebook automatically allocate spending to the most profitable audiences and placements; for example, allocate $100/day and Facebook distributes where it performs best.
  • Campaign level: turn on Advantage+ campaign budget (CBO), enter the test budget there, since Meta then distributes that single campaign budget across the ad sets.
  • Budget > Budget strategy: choose Advantage+ campaign budget (CBO). Enter the daily amount in Daily budget in USD. Choosing CBO removes the ad set budget suggestions shown in Ads Manager.
  • The CBO toggle (the on/off switch) is off by default in the lead generation setup and must be turned on.
  • Ad set > Budget: check that the budget shown is the campaign budget, because an ad set still set to ABO will show its own budget. If so, go back and change it to Campaign budget optimization before continuing.
  • The Advantage+ sales campaign toggle is shown as on in the recommended setup for new sales campaigns, and the campaign-level budget is used. If an ad set shows that Advantage+ is off for Budget, that ad set is not using the campaign budget and should be checked before publishing.
  • Campaign level CBO test: choose the Sales objective, switch Campaign budget to Advantage+ campaign budget, and set the daily budget to a test amount. Use $50 for a normal test, with $75 or $100 for a more aggressive test; more spend yields data faster and bad campaigns can be cut earlier.
  • CBO campaign-level settings: turn off the Advantage+ sales campaign recommendations, leave Campaign bid strategy at its default, and set the Performance goal (the result Meta aims for) to Maximize number of conversions. Use Conversion event purchase (the tracked action) and confirm the correct pixel dataset (the stored data from your pixel) is selected.
  • Set the campaign to CBO and allow Meta to share the budget across the ad sets in the testing campaigns. This lets Meta move spend toward the ad sets that perform best.

Signals Meta Reads Under CBO

  • Meta's Advantage campaign budget (CBO) reads signals (numbers Meta checks) such as CTR (the share of viewers who click), CPA, CPC (cost per click), conversion volume and ROAS. It moves budget toward ads converting at lower cost and reduces budget for underperformers throughout each day. Spend is therefore treated as Meta's confidence signal for each ad.
  • With CBO turned on, Meta spreads the campaign budget across the ad sets to find more results. This makes the single campaign budget the one control you set for the test.

Budget Spend Behaviour

  • With a daily budget of 500 USD, Ads Manager shows an average spend of about 500 USD per day, a maximum daily spend of 875 USD (175 percent of the daily budget) and a maximum weekly spend of 3,500 USD (seven times the daily budget). Meta can spend more on some days and less on others, but it stays within the weekly cap.
  • With an Advantage campaign budget, the maximum weekly spend equals seven times the daily budget. Daily spend can vary above or below the average within the week, but the weekly total cannot exceed the cap. The example shows a maximum daily spend of $43.75 and a maximum weekly spend of $175, which matches a $25 daily budget.
  • Facebook has no minimum budget, and a daily budget of $1 is enough to keep a campaign active. Keep it running at this level until you have money to scale it back up.

  • Ad set > Ad set spending limits: tick the box and enter a daily minimum or a daily maximum for the ad set. Use this only when a specific spend limit is needed.

Testing Architecture

1 min

Testing Structures

2 min
  • Start a test on Meta with one ABO campaign. ABO means you set the budget on each ad set.
  • Use 6 to 10 ad sets in that campaign.
  • Each ad set targets a single interest, which is one topic people like, such as a hobby.
  • Spend $10 to $15 per day on each ad set.
  • Each ad set runs 3 to 5 different creatives. A creative is one ad: a picture or a video.
  • Testing campaign structure: an ABO campaign with 7 to 8 ad sets, each targeting one single interest.
  • Start each of those ad sets at about $15 budget.
  • Put three ads with different creatives inside each ad set.
  • Testing structure: one ABO campaign with five ad sets.
  • Each ad set targets exactly one interest.
  • Each ad set has at least three creatives (image or video).
  • That makes 15 ads in total.
  • Test three CBO campaigns. CBO means one budget for the whole campaign, and Meta splits it across the ad sets.
  • Set each CBO to the amount you are willing to spend.
  • When one CBO wins, increase its budget to reach more people from the same audience.
  • Launch three CBOs with the same budget and targeting.
  • Put four ad sets in each CBO, with identical settings.
  • After a few days, keep the one or two CBOs that perform.
  • Turn off the rest.
  • Launch the same ads in two separate CBO campaigns.
  • Turn on budget sharing so Meta can move money between ad sets.
  • Give each ad set one main concept.
  • Add 3 to 5 variations per ad set. Vary the hook (the first seconds that grab attention), the script or the angle (the reason or emotion behind the ad).
  • Start with a $75 budget.
  • Scale the budget fast in steps: $150, $300, $500, $750 and $1,000. Go higher if the results hold.
  • The goal of this phase is to find the winning creatives and angles.
  • Testing setup for two CBO testing lanes: a Main CBO prepared for bid cap stays unpublished. A bid cap is a maximum price you accept per purchase.
  • CBO 1 is for Kalo rips. A rip is a copy of a winning ad that is re-cut.
  • CBO 2 is for custom ads.
  • Testing lane: an ABO testing campaign on cost cap with tROAS or tCPA. A cost cap limits the price per result. tROAS (target return on ad spend) and tCPA (target cost per action) are price goals for the algorithm.
  • Use one ad set per creative batch.
  • Per creative, write 3 primary texts, 3 headlines and 1 description.
  • Set up the main CBO plus one testing CBO.
  • Put one concept in each ad set.
  • Put 3 to 5 creatives in each ad set.
  • The base Meta testing unit is one ad set.
  • That ad set holds one concept with 3 to 5 creatives.
  • All of it sits inside one CBO.

Country-Based Test Structures

  • For English-speaking markets (US, Australia, New Zealand and Canada), one interest per ad set at $5 worked better.
  • For German-speaking markets, several similar interests in one ad set at $5 worked better.
  • Choose the test structure per country. Do not use one template everywhere.

Testing Principles

2 min
  • A CBO with only one winning creative limits ad delivery (how many people the ad reaches).
  • Add two to three creatives, even if one ad keeps making sales.
  • More creatives give Facebook more options and usually improve overall results.
  • Use ABO for testing. It controls how much budget each interest gets.
  • Use CBO only for scaling, with interests and creatives you already know win.
  • Facebook prefers the older creative, the one with more engagement (likes, comments, shares) and sales data.
  • A new creative next to a proven winner gets little spend.
  • So always split them into separate ad sets.
  • Use a separate ad set for each new creative test.
  • Keep the winning ad set as a control (a fixed reference you compare against).
  • Then spend follows the new creative results, not the old winner.
  • For multi-product tests with ABO: all products in the same campaign and pixel should be in the same category.
  • A pixel is the tracking code that records sales from your store.
  • Example: all construction books, not a mix of nursing and plumbing.
  • Mixing categories under one pixel makes the test harder to read.
  • Meta's algorithm (the system that picks who sees the ad) can still handle the mix.
  • Testing campaigns are meant to run for a short window.
  • Testing both structures in your own account, with automatic rules running overnight. Automatic rules are settings that act on their own, so no manual checks are needed.
  • Source A's Sandbox campaign uses 2 ads per test: 1 control and 1 variation.
  • This split-tests creatives without affecting your core campaigns.
  • Mistake: using a single creative inside a CBO test limits delivery.
  • Power Five framework: start with the recommendations. Change things only where they fail to produce profit.

Multi-Product ABO Setup

  1. Create one campaign with Campaign budget off (Advantage+ campaign budget off). Advantage+ is Meta's automatic setup. Set the budget at ad set level.
  2. Set the Budget strategy to Ad set budget.
  3. Leave Ad set budget sharing and Advantage+ options at default, unless a test needs changes.
  4. A common starting budget is $50 per ad set for one product with three creatives.
  5. The money is divided per creative: $50 divided by 3 creatives.
  6. Products can start at $25, $50 or $100, depending on how hard you test each product.

Ad Set Budget Sharing (ABO)

  • When budget sharing is on, Meta can move up to 20% of an ad set's budget to other ad sets in the same campaign. Meta does this when it likely improves results.
  • To keep the budget strictly in each ad set for testing, turn it off.
  • Conflicting setting: Source D' CBO testing structure turns budget sharing on. Its ABO test turns it off for strict testing.
  • Use budget sharing on for CBO tests.
  • Use it off for strict ABO tests.

Scaling Architecture

1 min
  • Stay on CBO while scaling with concepts you know win.
  • The Main CBO holds only proven concepts, with stable spend. Winners are folded back in.
  • Scale proven concepts in the main CBO.
  • Test ripped winners in CBO 2.
  • Advantage+ campaigns are scaled the same way as other campaigns.
  • They use a single ad set.
  • When old creatives die out (stop working), add new creatives.
  • Scaling with ABO is a mistake.
  • Move winners to a CBO for scaling.
  • Use CBO for scaling only with known winning interests and creatives.
  • Otherwise CBO puts spend on one interest and starves the rest of the test.
  • Once an interest is already profitable on ABO, move it to CBO to scale.
  • When a winner is scaled into CBO or cost-cap campaigns, Meta controls how the spend is split.
  • Test with Meta's recommended budget first. Then scale.

Campaign Counts and Account Structure

2 min
  • Meta prefers a simple account with about 2 to 5 campaigns.
  • The practical setup here is 1 campaign, 1 ad set and 1 ad.
  • Avoid many complicated ad structures.
  • Use a simple account with a few campaigns and very broad targeting.
  • Do not build dozens or hundreds of hyper-segmented ad sets (ad sets cut into tiny audience slices).
  • Let the pixel find the buyers from its data.
  • Hyper-stacked targeting stacks many filters: interest, online-shopper behavior, household income above $100,000, cities, gender, age brackets and single placements.
  • This produced huge numbers of ad sets.
  • It needed about 50 hours per week from three people.
  • Use one CBO with one broad campaign.
  • Do not test different interests in it.
  • Do not add multiple account strategies.
  • Conflicting guidance: Source C's new-store procedure tests several interests in ABO ad sets inside one campaign. This goes against the one-campaign rule.
  • For a mature account with proven broad targeting, follow the one-CBO rule.
  • For a new store that must compare interests, run the ABO interest tests.
  • Power Five framework has five parts: auto advanced matching (Meta matches customer details to find buyers), a simple account with few broad campaigns, campaign budget optimization, automatic placements, and dynamic ads for remarketing (showing ads again to people who already visited).
  • Apply all five first.
  • Then change only the parts that fail to produce profit.

Testing Campaign Counts

  • Recommended setup: a main CBO prepared for bid cap that stays unpublished.
  • Add CBO 1 for Kalo rips and CBO 2 for custom ads.
  • Each extra CBO splits the data further.
  • Running two or more testing CBOs causes cannibalisation (your own campaigns compete with each other) in the short term.
  • This raises CPC (cost per click) and CPM (cost per 1,000 views).
  • It is still the fastest route to a winning creative.
  • Running several CBOs at once means your campaigns compete in the same auctions. CPC and CPM go up.
  • Accept this as a short-term cost for finding winners faster.
  • Testing campaigns are meant to run for a short window.
  • Run at most two testing CBOs in parallel.

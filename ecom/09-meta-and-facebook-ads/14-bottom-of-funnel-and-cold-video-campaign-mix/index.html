Bottom-of-Funnel and cold video campaign mix

Section 9.14 · 14 of 22 in this chapter · 16 min read

In short

  • BOF ads (ads for people who are almost ready to buy) work only when people already understand the product. If they don't, warm them up first with video ads.
  • Test with about $50 a day for 2 to 3 days. If the test is not profitable by day 3, stop.
  • Keep each format in its own budget group, so one format cannot take all the money.
  • When results stop growing, make new ads. Raising the budget on weak ads does not help.

Bottom-of-Funnel (BOF) Ads: When They Work

2 min
  • BOF means bottom-of-funnel, the last step before buying. BOF ads work for products that people already understand and can buy without explanation. Examples: phone cases, water bottles, posture belts and books that have already proven they sell. These products skip teaching and go straight to sales.
  • Before you launch BOF, ask: "Can this product sell without explanation?"
    • If yes, launch BOF.
    • If no, warm the audience first with educational video ads. Warm means people who already know you.
    • Build the pool first. The pool is the group of people you can show ads to. It must be big enough before BOF retargeting starts. Retargeting means showing ads again to people who already interacted with you.
  • If your product solves a problem people don't know about yet, warm cold audiences with video first. Cold audiences are people who don't know you. More than 50% of viewers automatically join the BOF pool.
  • For books:
    • If other brands have already run and proven the same book, BOF can work. The audience is already solution-aware, meaning they know a solution exists.
    • If the book is niche (for a small group) or new, warm it with video first.
    • If competitors have already built awareness, BOF image ads can catch sales from their audience that did not buy.
  • BOF is a harvesting channel. It collects sales. It is not a discovery channel, which finds new people. It shows the offer and benefits to people who already know the solution or have engaged with you. No spend goes toward explanation, so CPMs (cost per 1,000 views) stay low and ROAS rises. ROAS means return on ad spend, the money earned for each dollar spent.
  • Competitors' video ads can prepare buyers for you.
    • If they have warmed an audience to a solution, such as posture belts, some people may still not have bought.
    • Your BOF image ads can catch those almost-converted customers. Converted means bought.
    • They did the education. You harvest the sale with a simpler, cheaper approach.
  • Example: one personal brand earned $100,000 in its first six months using only BOF image ads, starting from zero. The product was easy to understand and not a niche supplement. The audience was solution-aware, so no warm-up video campaign was needed yet.

Writing BOF Creative

2 min
  • BOF ads should read like direct sales ads: simple, clear and selling right away. Tell the customer what they are buying and why, then ask them to buy. Skip storytelling. Storytelling is the opposite of educational ads.
  • For wearables (clothing or devices worn on the body) and health products, BOF ads work by addressing emotional change, not technical specifications.
    • Cold buyers respond to "you can have your life back" more than to "compression ratio of 360 degrees."
    • Testimonials (customer reviews), pain-point questions (questions about the problem) and risk removal (taking the risk away) all convert cold audiences.
  • Guarantees work in BOF because they remove the main objection of cold traffic. An objection is a reason not to buy. The main one is: "I don't know if this works."
    • Specific terms are more credible than open-ended guarantees. Examples: 30 nights, or a 7-day benefit window (the time until the customer should feel results).
    • The trial period must be long enough to test: 30+ days.
    • The benefit timeline must be realistic: 7 days for pain relief, not 1 day.
  • Pattern: BOF targets people who already know the product and are close to buying. It converts with a direct, number-based offer and a clear reason to act now. That removes the last reason to hesitate.
    • Formula: aware audience + specific number-based offer + clear reason to act now.
    • "Still comparing? Take 35% off your first order this week."
    • "Your size is still in stock: 20% off when you add the second copy."
  • Meta (the company behind Facebook and Instagram) uses an algorithm (an automatic system that picks who sees each ad). It gives different concepts to different customer types, based on scrolling behaviour and purchase history.
    • Skeptics (people who doubt) see testimonials.
    • Procrastinators (people who delay) see urgency.
    • Fence-sitters (people who can't decide) see value offers.
    • You do not need to segment manually. Create several concepts in separate ad sets and let Meta optimise.
  • Static hot ads (image ads that perform well) do not link to product pages. They build trust and authority with customer voices and link to the store. They complement the dynamic product ads (ads that show products automatically) that run alongside them.
  • Advertorial retargeting: an advertorial is an ad written like an article.
    • Visitors who read an advertorial but did not click through to the product can be retargeted with Meta ads.
    • This is a more qualified audience than cold traffic, meaning these people are more likely to buy.
    • It works best as a step in a warm-up path for lower-ticket products (cheaper items). Build a longer funnel (the path from first view to purchase) for expensive items.
  • Cold Facebook traffic should go to an editorial page (a page written like an article), not directly to the product page.
    • The editorial page's call-to-action button (CTA, the button that tells people what to do) links to the product page.
    • Facebook ads are good at catching impulse buyers (people who buy fast).
    • Sending all traffic straight to a product page leaves most prospects (possible buyers) unconverted, meaning they do not buy.

Campaign Structure and Separation

2 min
  • A campaign is one group of ads with one budget. Keep BOF as a separate campaign from cold video campaigns.
  • Inside one BOF campaign, create one ad set per concept. An ad set is a group of ads with one audience.
    • Concepts: testimonial, free gift, benefit, social proof (showing many buyers) and urgency (a reason to buy now).
    • Each ad set targets the same warm audience from a different psychological angle.
  • Put 4 to 8 ad variations with different hooks in each ad set. A hook is the first line or seconds that grab attention.
    • Meta measures which concept converts best.
    • It spreads budget across ad sets, so no single concept dominates spend.
  • CBO (Campaign Budget Optimization) means Meta splits one campaign budget across its ad sets for you. Use separate CBO campaigns for each format:
    • one for cold video
    • one for BOF (warm)
    • one for native ads, from week 2 onward, if used
  • Native ads are ads with long text that reads like an article. See the Native Ads section below.
  • Never mix formats in one CBO. If a high-performing video campaign shares a CBO with BOF ads, the BOF ads get no budget and no fair test.
  • Put the BOF statics and videos for the same product in different campaigns. Statics means image ads. If video ads share a campaign with them, they can starve the statics (take their budget), and the statics then die (stop getting results).
  • Grouping, which differs from the rule above: when you decide campaign structure, group native and BOF ads together as statics. Keep video ads in a separate campaign.
    • Conflict: says one CBO per format, including native. says native and BOF share a static campaign. Both are valid structures.
    • Use one CBO per format to keep each format's budget separate.
    • Use the statics grouping when you want native and BOF to compete for the same static budget.
  • ABO (you set the budget for each ad set yourself) or CBO matters less than having ads that actually win. Separate campaigns per format let you control budget and scale each format on its own. Scaling means raising the budget. Separation does not guarantee better results.

Budgets and Test Length

1 min
  • Starting budget for BOF testing: $50 USD per day.
  • Minimum viable test (the smallest test that still tells you something): $15-20 AUD (about $15 USD) per day.
  • Lower budgets often give higher ROAS, because the algorithm has more time to find your best audience.
  • Test BOF for 2 to 3 days. If it is not profitable by day 3, stop. Profitable means earning more than you spend.
    • Try new concepts or give up on this approach.
    • Do not keep spending on an unprofitable test beyond this window.
  • Starter setup for a product:
    • Kalodata rips (a product check in Kalodata, a tool that shows sales data) at $50/day. This is the top priority, because it proves the product sells.
    • BOF image ads at $50/day. This is a side test: low cost, quick results.
    • Both in separate CBOs for 2 to 3 days.
    • Native ads come in week 2, if profitable.
    • This spreads risk and tests three angles at once.
  • If your Kalodata rip campaign is not profitable after 2 to 3 days, test BOF image ads instead of pausing. The audience may be solution-aware and tired of competitors' videos, but has not bought yet. BOF is faster and cheaper to produce than video. It can capture warm-but-not-buying segments.
  • Scaling limit: when you scale to $200/day and reach breakeven (1.0X ROAS, meaning you earn back exactly what you spend), do not raise the budget further.
    • The constraint is the ads, not the budget.
    • Test new formats (BOF, native, advertorials, listicles (articles in list form)), new concepts or AI UGC. UGC means user-generated content: videos that look like real customers made them.
    • The biggest jumps come from new creative that works. Creative means the ad itself: image, video and text. Growth does not come from scaling mediocre ads.

Ad Set Rotation and Creative Fatigue

1 min
  • Creative fatigue means an ad stops working because people have seen it too often.
  • Typical BOF structure on an active brand (a brand running ads now): 30+ ad sets in total, with 15 to 20 active at any moment.
  • Introduce new concepts every 2 days. Keep winners and pause losers.
  • Pause fatigued ad sets rather than deleting them. Do not delete old ad sets. Disable them, and retest later if needed.
  • As new warm audiences enter your pool from video campaigns, retest disabled sets. See whether they perform better on the fresh audience.
  • Procedure for a new BOF campaign:
    1. Launch a separate CBO campaign for BOF. Not in the same CBO as videos.
    2. Create three starting ad sets: testimonial, free gift, benefit.
    3. Add 4 to 8 ads to each ad set.
    4. Set the daily budget to $50 (or a minimum of $15-20 AUD).
    5. Run for 2 to 3 days.
    6. If ROAS is profitable, keep running and introduce new concept variations weekly. If it is not profitable by day 3, stop and test new concepts or different products.
  • Ad type: BOF image ads perform as well as video ads at capturing warm, solution-aware audiences.
    • Images are faster and cheaper. About 20 minutes with AI prompts.
    • Videos take longer.
    • Use images to test quickly. Introduce video concepts once images prove the market.

How Video and BOF Work Together

1 min
  • Every viewer who watches 50% or more of a video is automatically added to a warm audience pool. These viewers then move into the BOF campaign. This grows the pool of buyers for your image ads without extra work.
  • BOF profitability compounds as the warm pool grows.
    • A larger pool gives BOF more impressions (views) at low CPMs, which raises ROAS.
    • Sustained cold spending (steady spending on cold audiences) increases BOF returns without extra effort.
    • This creates a self-reinforcing cycle, a loop that keeps feeding itself.
  • Video builds the audience and BOF harvests it.
    • BOF has a hard ceiling (a limit it cannot pass), typically $80K-$100K monthly revenue.
    • The real scale comes from video ads building a larger warm pool. BOF then monetises (turns into money) the result.
    • Together they can produce 5X or more revenue growth.
  • Example (personal brand): video campaigns were introduced at scale alongside existing BOF ads.
    • ROAS doubled.
    • Budget scaled from $300 per day to $1,000 per day using the same BOF ads, with no new creative.
    • The video campaign warmed the audience, and BOF captured the results.
  • Example (Brand A): the brand hit an $80,000 monthly revenue ceiling running only BOF ads.
    • When video ads at scale were added alongside the same BOF campaign, monthly revenue rose 5X to $400,000+.
    • Video prospected (found) cold audiences and warmed them. BOF captured the ready-to-buy segment.
  • A high number of BOF ads for a product signals existing demand. Those ads target warm audiences who already know the product. This means the product is easier to sell.

Funnel-Stage Creative Mix

1 min
  • TOF means top-of-funnel, the first step. Top-of-funnel (TOF) ads educate cold audiences who do not yet know the product. They simply show what it is.
  • A healthy product ad set includes a mix of top-, middle- and bottom-funnel ads. Middle-funnel means the middle step, for people who are warming up. Do not use only one stage.
  • Typical length: 1 to 10 seconds for BOF product ads. A much longer ad deserves a closer look before you assume it is BOF.
  • Skip a product if almost all its ads are BOF with no top- or middle-funnel variety. It gives too little variety to test with.
  • Inside one CBO, the mix is heavily BOF: about 90% of ads at that stage, with the rest at middle and top. Plan creatives around that mix.

Native Ads

1 min
  • Native ads are ads with a long primary text (the main text above the image) that reads like an article. They are a strong top-of-funnel format. Spot them by the long primary text, and copy their structure.
  • Native ads scale less than UGC or BOF ads. Run them as a side campaign for extra revenue.
    • Start them only after a winning product is confirmed.
    • Start them only after the pixel is warmed up. The pixel is a tracking code on your store that records visits and sales. Warmed up means it has collected enough data.
    • Only start them with budget set aside for testing.
  • Timing: native ads run from week 2 onward, in their own CBO, if the product is profitable.

Whitelist and Persona Pages

5 min

Concepts

  • A Facebook Page is the public identity above an ad. It has a page name, a profile picture and posts. For the customer, it is the face that speaks to them. The page you run an ad from shapes how it is perceived.
  • A brand page is the official Facebook page of your store or product brand, usually carrying the store name. Ads from it show the company speaking directly about its product.
  • A whitelist page is another Facebook page or creator identity that has given permission for ads to run under its name. This is called whitelisting.
    • The product stays the same, but a different person or page appears as the speaker.
    • Meta calls this arrangement Community Handled Partnership Ads. It manages permissions through its partnership tools.
  • Two whitelisting types:
    • Personal whitelisting uses a creator, influencer (someone with a following) or advocate (a supporter) persona that the audience trusts as a peer.
    • Third-party whitelisting uses an authority figure, such as a doctor for supplements, to lend credibility.
  • Persona means the character or identity the ad speaks as. Whitelist ads are usually written in the first person, from a named persona describing their use of the product. Some use a third-person page voice that describes the product. Both differ from the brand page's company voice.

Brand Page versus Whitelist Page

Brand page Whitelist / partner page
Who is talking Your brand Another approved page or creator
Product being sold Your product Can be the exact same product
How it feels The company is advertising A person or page is recommending
Main benefit Builds your brand Gives another angle or identity to test
  • Customers see ads all day. An ad straight from a brand often reads as plain advertising. An ad from a creator or relevant page feels more like a recommendation, and more organic (natural). So the creative feels more native.
  • Whitelist pages are used to:
    • test a different identity without changing the product
    • make creatives feel more native or personal
    • borrow a creator's credibility or style, once permission is granted
    • compare which page identity gets better results for the same product
  • A brand page and a whitelist page can promote the same product at the same time. This lets you compare two identities side by side.
  • Procedure:
    1. Start with the product you want to sell.
    2. Choose the face of the ad: your brand page or an approved partner page.
    3. Run the ad from the chosen page.
    4. Compare results across the pages.

Rules and Compliance

  • Only run ads under another person's or business's identity when they have granted permission through Meta's partnership or advertising tools. Never pretend to be someone else, copy their page, or use their identity without consent.
  • An invented persona can be your own whitelist page. It does not have to be an existing creator.
    • An invented persona has no real access to your ad account.
    • It must be treated as a separate identity.
  • A whitelist identity may not use the name of a real celebrity. Meta rejects those ads, and they are not approved. Use an invented name.
  • A whitelist page you build and own is presented as a fictional character instead of a company logo.
    • The ads run under that character's identity.
    • You keep the page, its audience and the pixel in your own account.
  • A character page is treated as a branded page with a personality, similar to a mascot brand (a brand with a made-up character, like a cartoon mascot).
    • It stays compliant only if the character is clearly a brand persona.
    • It must not imitate a real, identifiable person. Standard Meta ad policy still applies.
    • Check Meta's current Page authenticity and ad policies before scaling, since enforcement can change.
  • Mistake: using a real person's name, photo or likeness for the character can get the page or its ads rejected or disabled. Keep the persona fully fictional, with an AI-generated or licensed stock portrait (a licensed generic photo).
  • Invented personas are often shown with stock photos as profile pictures. A stock photo does not change the permission and compliance rules for whitelist pages.
  • Checking a page before you treat it as a possible identity:
    • Open its Facebook page view.
    • Check the Details section, the Links and Contact info fields, the follower count and the Photos section.
    • The page view also shows a "Not yet rated" or review status. This gives a quick sense of whether the identity looks like a real, active page.

Building the Page

  • Procedure, character assets built with AI:
    1. Attach your customer research and product documents to Claude (an AI chat tool).
    2. Paste the character-generator prompt.
    3. Let Claude read the research, pick the archetype (the basic personality type) and generate the character profile and assets.
    4. Copy the profile photo prompt, the cover photo prompt and the bio.
    5. Paste the two image prompts into ChatGPT (another AI tool) to generate the images.
    6. Upload the images and the bio to the new Facebook page.
  • Procedure, creating the page in Business Manager (Meta's admin tool for pages and ads):
    1. Create a new Facebook page inside Business Manager.
    2. Upload the profile photo.
    3. Upload the cover photo.
    4. Upload the bio.
    5. Start running ads from that page.
  • Setup is the same as a brand page: create the page, upload profile photo, cover photo and bio, then run ads from it. The AI prompts do most of the character work, so this is beginner-friendly.
  • One whitelist character can run all of a brand's ads without extra impersonation risk. Impersonation means pretending to be someone. A different page per person shown in UGC is also possible, but each character must not imitate a real person.

When to Use Whitelist Pages

  • Start with a whitelist page from day one of testing, rather than waiting for a winning ad. This is recommended on a tight budget, unless brand pages have already proven better for the product.
  • Make the whitelist page the default for the whole ad account, rather than running brand pages. A face instead of a logo means more people watch, fewer scroll past and more click. This adds up at scale.
  • Whitelist pages suit static ads well. A static image without a face needs only the character page.
    • UGC videos without faces can also use the whitelist page with no extra work.
    • Video ads with a face need the matching-look consideration.
  • Split test whitelist against brand page: run the same five ads once with each page.
    • This splits the budget in half.
    • Both tests need identical ads for a fair comparison.
    • With a small budget and few ad sets, run only the whitelist page.
  • If only a few ads are tested on a small budget, use the whitelist page for all of them and drop the brand page. This avoids splitting the budget across two pages.

Researching Competitor Pages

1 min
  • To see how competitors run whitelisting or persona pages, use the Meta Ad Library (Meta's public list of all running ads).
    1. Open the Meta Ad Library at facebook.com/ads/library.
    2. Set active status to Active.
    3. Set ad type to All ads.
    4. Set country to All.
    5. Set search type to Page.
    6. Sort by total impressions, descending (highest first), to see which pages run the most ads.
    7. Search by the competitor's page name, not by a product keyword.

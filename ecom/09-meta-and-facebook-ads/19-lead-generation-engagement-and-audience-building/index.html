Playbook › 9. Meta and Facebook ads

Lead generation, engagement and audience building

Section 9.19 · 19 of 22 in this chapter · 14 min read

In short

  • Build audiences in a repeating cycle. Engaged fans become the source for new look-alike audiences.
  • Free organic reach on Facebook pages is very low, so paid ads or a group are needed to reach people.
  • Engagement campaigns (likes, comments, shares) bring cheap clicks but rarely sales. Use them to test, not to sell.
  • For sales, use conversion campaigns that send Meta a purchase signal. Use engagement tests only for short, small-budget research.
  • Lead campaigns bring cheap contact forms, so keep the cost per lead below what a lead is worth.

Audience Building and Engagement Foundations

1 min
  • Audience building runs in a cycle. Repeat these four steps:
    1. Create a seed audience: a starter group of people who already engaged (followers who liked, commented or shared).
    2. Promote new content to the seed audience and to an engagement audience (people who interacted with your posts) to build awareness.
    3. Build look-alike audiences (new people who resemble your engaged followers) from the engaged followers, because look-alikes also engage with the content.
    4. Repeat until everyone in the target market knows the brand name.
  • Engagement compounds. When people like, comment or share, their friends see the post in their own feeds, so reach grows without paying for those extra viewers. Well-targeted content can create a snowball effect (growth that builds on itself) and very low cost per engagement.
  • Facebook shows the size of the audiences you have reached, so you can see how much of the interested market you have covered.
  • Keep reaching new people until the audience of interested people is largely exhausted.
  • Early audience building can run on about 5 dollars per day of ad spend (money paid to Facebook for ads). You can increase spend whenever you are ready. Spreading spend out is sensible while no product is for sale yet.
  • Engagement-objective campaigns (ads with the goal of likes, comments and shares) have no link or call to action (a button or line asking the viewer to do something) to a store. The ad text asks people to like and share, so the only goal is engagement.
  • A test of interest for a product without its own page: run an engagement ad instead of a conversion ad (an ad aimed at sales) to compare which product attracts the most attention. This is a test of interest, not a sales test.

Organic Reach Limits and Page Strategy

2 min
  • Organic reach means free views that you do not pay for. Facebook announced organic reach cuts to about 6% of the people who see a page's posts, and as low as 2% for business pages (figures as announced at the time).
  • Paid ads are therefore needed to reach a target audience.
  • Because few businesses pay to play, competition for paid placements (paid spots in feeds) stays lower.
  • A Facebook page is required to run ads, but a page has very low organic reach. Use a Facebook group (a community space for members) for most engagement and traffic (visitors sent to your site).
  • Let retargeting ads (ads shown to people who already interacted with you) invite engagers to join the group.
  • Relying on the organic reach of a Facebook page is a mistake. A post on a page with about 2,000 likes reached only around 100 people.
  • Paid ads or a group are needed to get posts in front of the audience.
  • Facebook organic reach is very low, so use Facebook and Instagram pages mainly to show the business is active and real when visitors search for it.
  • Regular posts on a Facebook page, even on old pages, barely reach anyone, so reach usually has to be paid for with a small boost (paying Facebook to show one post to more people). (Advice from 2024.)
  • Organic follower growth alone does little until paid campaigns already perform well. (Advice from 2024.)
  • Organic follower growth is easy, but converting followers into sales is hard. So organic page growth is usually a lower priority than paid traffic (visitors who arrive from ads).
  • It makes sense mainly after monthly paid spend has reached a satisfactory return on ad spend (ROAS: the sales earned for each dollar spent on ads). (Advice from 2024.)
  • Regular posting to Facebook is a positive signal for a fashion store, so keep up posting frequency and content types.
  • Post on social media a couple of times per week mainly so buyers can see the business is active and real before purchasing.
  • This is for conversion trust (buyers feeling safe enough to buy), not for reaching new people.
  • A Facebook community about the product topic can be built first. Use it to get ideas for content, share it and promote offers.
  • Content is then checked through the audience.

Algorithm Signals and Zapier Engagement Alerts

1 min
  • Facebook and Instagram algorithms (the automatic systems that choose which posts people see) prioritize fast initial engagement (likes, comments, shares) on new posts.
  • Automating team alerts to engage within minutes of publishing improves organic reach.
  • Create a dedicated Slack "Engagement" channel (a chat room in Slack, a team chat app).
  • Zapier (a tool that connects apps automatically) alerts team members (@channel, which pings everyone) whenever new Instagram or Facebook posts publish.
  • The alert requests likes and comments within their earliest convenience.
  • This leverages team assets to improve algorithmic reach at no cost.

Facebook Groups for Engagement and Sales

1 min

  • Use active niche Facebook groups (groups about your product area) to build sales:

    1. Identify groups in your niche that are active.
    2. Build authority by adding value to discussions and staying active.
    3. Promote your sales inside the group.

    This approach requires a personal account.

  • When looking for niche Facebook groups, choose groups with new posts at least a few times per week.

  • Less active groups produced few prospective customers (people who may buy) in this approach.

  • Participate actively in niche groups and add value on a regular basis before promoting sales.

  • This makes sales posts look like contributions rather than spam (unwanted posts). You can do it yourself or hire a social media manager.

  • Joining these groups generally requires a personal Facebook account.

  • If you do not want your personal account linked to the store, or you hand the work to someone else, create a separate personal account for this purpose.

Engagement Campaign Tests and Their Limits

2 min
  • Engagement campaigns to build a warm audience (people who already know the brand a little) can give cheaper clicks but lower buyer intent (how likely people are to buy).
  • They can also disturb the ad account and pixel data. The pixel is a small tracking code on your website that reports visits and sales back to Facebook.
  • Use them only for a short creative test (comparing ad images and texts) of 3 to 5 days at about $10 to $20.
  • Do not use them as a permanent part of the funnel (the path from first view to sale).
  • Engagement campaigns can show a CTR (click-through rate, the share of viewers who click) near 3 percent.
  • The same creatives in purchase campaigns often drop to about 1.2 to 2 percent.
  • Engagement audiences have lower buying intent and cheaper clicks. So use an engagement test only to rank creatives by CTR, not to forecast purchase results.
  • Keep it to a few days at about $10 to $20.
  • Page-like and engagement campaigns can quickly build follower counts, but followers and likes rarely turn into sales.
  • Targeting English-speaking tier-three countries (Meta's grouping of lower-cost countries) with about $20 over a week produces numbers, not revenue. (Advice from 2023.)
  • A Facebook page likes campaign with a few dollars per day for about a week can add followers and social proof (visible signs that others trust you, like follower counts).
  • Those users rarely become customers, so do not expect sales from them. (Advice from 2024.)
  • A boosted post does not train the pixel or ad account to find buyers well.
  • Keep the budget for proper conversion campaigns so the data is not wasted.
  • Example of a boosted reaction reel (a short video boosted for likes and reactions): a $55 spend produced 147 link clicks at $0.37 each.
  • The same reel had about 3,000 views, about 2,300 viewers and roughly 950 three-second video plays.
  • These are good engagement stats, but none of the clicks converted to a sale.

Engagement Campaign Setup

1 min

  • To run an engagement campaign on an existing Facebook post (Advice from 2024):

    1. Choose Engagement as the campaign goal.
    2. In the ad builder (Facebook's tool for making ads), click "use existing post" instead of creating a new ad.
    3. Select the page post.
    4. Set the budget to 3 to 5 dollars per day.

  • For a research engagement campaign on Meta (the company behind Facebook and Instagram):

    1. Create a new campaign for the research test.
    2. Set the optimization goal (what Meta aims to achieve) to engagement optimization, instead of sales and purchases.
    3. Post one simple open question from a whitelisting page (a page used to run the ad, not the brand page), written like a real person asking for advice.
    4. Set the budget to about $5 to $10 per day.
    5. Reply in the comments as the original poster, guiding the conversation with follow-up questions.
    6. Read every comment and aim for 200 or more comments.
    7. Note the main objections (reasons people hesitate), prior experiences with other brands and the wording people use.

  • For a research engagement campaign, set the optimization goal to engagement optimization.

  • Do not use sales and purchases, because the aim is to collect feedback from commenters rather than to drive purchases.

Lead Campaign Foundations and Measurement

1 min
  • Meta lead objectives (goals that collect contact details, called leads) fill the account with cheap form fills and low-quality traffic.
  • The algorithm (Meta's automatic system for showing ads) cannot optimize those leads toward purchases or return on ad spend.
  • Sending Meta a purchase conversion event (a signal from your site telling Meta that someone bought) lets the algorithm optimize ad sets (groups of ads with shared targeting and budget) toward buyers.
  • For giveaway funnels, a purchase objective therefore works better than a lead objective.
  • A Meta traffic campaign can send cold visitors (people who do not know the brand yet) to a content page that collects email opt-ins (sign-ups to receive emails).
  • The traffic objective usually brings low-quality cold visitors. (Advice from 2024.)
  • If such a campaign already performs acceptably, it can be left unchanged, but its budget should not be scaled up (increased).
  • Cold traffic lead generation campaigns typically run 6 to 12 months (half a year to a year).
  • After that, campaign performance may require moving to Sandbox split-testing (a separate test area for safely comparing ad versions).
  • The minimum starting daily budget for lead generation campaigns is $40 per day.
  • A lead ad can also bring in Facebook page likes, because viewers can like the advertising page while responding to the offer.
  • Treat this as a side benefit that grows the page audience, not as the main goal of the campaign.

Building a Lead Campaign in Power Editor

2 min
  • Power Editor (an older Facebook tool for managing many ads at once). Lead campaign steps:
    1. Open Power Editor and go to Ads.
    2. Click the plus icon to create a new campaign, ad set and ad.
    3. Name the campaign after the industry. Create a separate ad set for each precise interest, so you can split test (compare versions by showing each to part of the audience).
    4. In the ad, select the Facebook page to advertise from.
    5. Enter the website URL of the squeeze page (a single page built to collect contact details) or offer page.
    6. Upload the image. Fill in the text above the image, then the headline, link description and display link below the image.
    7. Check the preview, which shows how the ad will appear in the news feed.
    8. Create the conversion tracking pixel with the Leads event, and place it in the head section (the hidden top part of a web page's code) of the thank-you page (the page shown after someone signs up).
    9. Set the audience location to the market where your store ships. Select only one precise interest per ad set.
    10. Set optimization to optimized CPM (Meta's automatic setting that aims for the most views per money spent; CPM means cost per 1,000 views) with the default bid. Select the tracking pixel as the conversion.
    11. Set the ad set budget and upload the changes to Facebook.
    12. If the ad is denied, open a support ticket and ask which change would get it approved.
  • Lead ad set audience: select the location where your online store operates, such as the United States for a US store.
  • Select one precise interest per ad set. The right-hand panel shows the potential reach (how many people the audience can contain). For the example niche, this was about 560,000 people.
  • Lead ad set optimization: use optimized CPM with the default bid. Select the tracking pixel in the bottom section.
  • Manual or custom bidding (setting your own bid price) can be tested later, once the basics work.
  • Ad set budgets can start as low as 5 dollars per day.
  • Keep budgets low at the start, because many ad sets are split tested. You want to see early whether the site converts (turns visitors into buyers or sign-ups) before spending heavily.
  • The Facebook lead ad form has a call-to-action selector (the button text, such as "Sign Up") at the bottom of the ad settings.
  • A preview on the right side shows how the finished ad will appear in the news feed, so check it before publishing.
  • Cold lead generation campaign settings: buying type Auction (the standard way Meta buys ad space), objective Conversions for leads, and campaign budget optimization turned on.
  • Campaign budget optimization (CBO) means Meta spreads one budget across ad sets. The budget is set at campaign level, with an amount that suits the business.
  • Example for a yoga mat seller: cold lead generation can use one campaign with separate interest ad sets for yoga, pilates and a related athleisure brand (sportswear for everyday wear).
  • The campaign can also include a lookalike (new people who resemble past customers) built from past customers.
  • Under campaign budget optimization, Meta shifts spend toward whichever interest converts best after about a week.

Email List Building with Facebook Lead Ads

2 min

  • Facebook lead ad campaigns for building an email list (a list of addresses you can write to): set the campaign objective to Conversions, and the conversion event to Lead.

  • This lets people submit their details inside Facebook, without visiting the website. (Advice from 2024.)

  • Setting up a Facebook lead ad to grow an email list (Advice from 2024):

    1. Create a campaign with the objective set to Conversions.
    2. Set the conversion event to Lead.
    3. Use a cost cap (the most you will pay per result). Start low, around 5 USD per lead, and never exceed the value a lead is worth to the business (for example 10 USD).
    4. Offer a valuable lead magnet (a free gift for signing up), such as a buyer's guide that already converts on the store.
    5. Leads opt in on a Facebook form. Their data flows into Shopify (an online store platform), triggering the email flow (an automatic series of emails).
    6. Run for a few hundred leads, then compare the sales they generate against the lead cost.

  • Before paying for leads through Facebook lead ads, calculate what an email subscriber is worth in revenue.

  • Use an example such as exit-intent overlay sign-ups (a pop-up shown when a visitor is about to leave), measured over 30 to 60 days.

  • Set the cost cap below that value. (Advice from 2024.)

  • Facebook lead ads for growing an email list are an advanced tactic.

  • Use them only when a store already has a proven opt-in offer and a working email flow, not as a beginner step. (Advice from 2024.)

  • For a list-building campaign, run Conversion campaigns (not Lead Ads) with "lead" as the conversion event.

  • Send traffic to a custom landing page (your own page where users enter their email). This delivers higher-quality leads than Facebook's built-in lead forms.

  • To grow an email list, use a retargeting-led sequence:

    1. Run Facebook and Instagram retargeting ads to people who already visited the store.
    2. Run cold Facebook and Instagram ads that send new people to a top-of-funnel social opt-in (the first step: an easy sign-up offer on social media).
    3. Email the people who opted in.
    4. Send the email subscribers to a product page.

  • Run Facebook ads to collect emails for a free offer, such as a three-page giveaway. Then:

    1. Use the email list to push a low-cost digital subscription.
    2. Aim for the tripwire offer (a cheap first product meant to start a buyer relationship) to at least pay back the Facebook ads spent to collect the emails.
    3. Count physical sales and physical subscriptions as upside (extra gain) on top of that.

Lead Magnets and Targeting for Lead Ads

1 min
  • For a lead magnet ad on Facebook (an ad offering a free resource), target people by interests tied to the niche, its events and the brands or companies in it.
  • Do not target by buying intent (signs that a person is ready to buy).
  • Because these people are not searching to buy yet, the ad should offer a free resource rather than a discount.

B2B and Local Lead Examples

1 min
  • For B2B (business-to-business, selling to companies) or commercial products that are not aimed at consumers, such as restaurant equipment, use a branding campaign (ads that build name recognition) rather than a conversion campaign.
  • Facebook's interest and demographic targeting (choosing people by hobbies, age, gender or job) can find business owners.
  • This lets you build brand recognition over time.
  • Local dentist lead campaign, audience and ad settings: target the business's city with a radius of about 20 kilometers, ages 18 to 65 plus, and interests such as tooth beauty, salons and fitness.
  • The ad uses a real procedure video provided by the dentist.
  • The dentist's ad call-to-action uses a Facebook lead ad form. The form collects the prospect's (potential patient's) name, email and phone number, along with consent to use the data.
  • The lead then gets an option to book a free consultation at the dental practice.
  • Example from one test: a Facebook ad campaign for a dentist's Invisalign offer (clear plastic teeth aligners) generated 6 leads in its first days.
  • The ad cost about 8 to 9 Swiss francs or dollars per lead. Each lead had a potential value of 3,000 to 5,000 dollars.

Retargeting and Paid Traffic Priority

1 min
  • Paid ads are needed to reach a target audience.
  • The paid-traffic priority applies across the funnel. Organic page growth ranks below paid traffic.
  • This holds until monthly paid spend reaches a satisfactory return on ad spend.

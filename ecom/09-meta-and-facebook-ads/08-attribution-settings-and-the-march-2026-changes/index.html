Playbook › 9. Meta and Facebook ads

Attribution settings and the march 2026 changes

Section 9.8 · 8 of 22 in this chapter · 7 min read

In short

  • For almost every account, use standard attribution (the rule that decides which sales an ad gets credit for), not incremental attribution.
  • Only keep the 7-day click window. Set engaged-view and view-through to None.
  • Attribution settings lock once something is published, so check them before you press Publish.
  • From May 2026, new campaigns use 7-day click plus 1-day engage-through, set at ad set level.
  • Do not change a live ad set's setting. Build a new campaign and move over slowly.
This section has 6 parts. Tap one to open it.

Attribution Settings for Standard Setups

1 min

Standard vs. Incremental Attribution

  • Standard attribution is the setting for nearly all accounts. Attribution means the rule that decides which ad gets credit for a sale. Meta selects engaged-view by default. Engaged-view counts a sale from someone who watched an ad without clicking it. Change that default to None to keep only the 7-day click window.
  • Incremental attribution uses more complex models to estimate whether an ad caused a conversion. A conversion is a sale or other goal action. Standard attribution uses the click and view windows. A window is the number of days after a click or view that still counts. Standard attribution is the choice for nearly all accounts. Incremental attribution is only worth testing in specific cases.
  • For a CBO test ad set, keep Customer life cycle strategy on Get conversions from all audiences. CBO (campaign budget optimization) means Meta shares one budget across the ad sets in a campaign. Keep the Attribution model on Standard. The conversion event stays on Purchase.

Setting Standard Attribution Step by Step

  1. Open the ad set and go to Show more settings.
  2. Under Attribution setting, click the pencil (the edit icon) and select Standard attribution, not Incremental attribution.
  3. Open Standard attribution selections with its pencil.
  4. Change the default of 7-day click, 1-day engaged-view, 1-day view to 7-day click only.
  5. Set Engaged-view to None and View-through to None. View-through counts a sale from someone who saw an ad without clicking or engaging.

Setting Timing Rule

  • Attribution settings can only be changed before a campaign or ad set is published. Once published, they are locked. The only fix is to duplicate the item and start over. That is why you must check the settings before pressing Publish.

Attribution Windows: 7-Day Click vs. Engage-Through

1 min

Why the Window Matters for Reading Data

  • Meta attribution can arrive days late. When this was written, the attribution window was 7 days. A sale can appear after a campaign was switched off. The start date in the campaign name helps you read the data correctly.

What Changed on March 17, 2026

2 min

The Change in Reporting

  • On March 17, 2026, Meta redefined its attribution settings. The same ad performance is now reported differently. No new attribution windows were added. Ads Manager (Meta's ad dashboard) shows a grey notice at the top of the account about this change.
  • Before March 17, click-through attribution counted any interaction with an ad. That included link clicks, likes, comments, saves, shares, story swipe-ups, Shop Now clicks, and clicks on carousels or dynamic catalog ads. A carousel is an ad with several swipeable images. Dynamic catalog ads show products from your product feed.
  • After the change, click-through only counts real clicks. All other interactions moved into engage-through.
  • Engage-through now covers all engagement types, such as likes, comments, saves and shares. Engaged-view counts an ad view longer than a set time limit. That limit is reportedly around six to seven seconds, but this figure still needs confirmation.

Effect on Reported Revenue

  • Revenue moved from click-through into engage-through. Accounts that only report click-through saw conversions and revenue drop overnight. The ad performance itself did not change. Some accounts still report noticeably less revenue on click-through than before the update.
  • Adding click-through and engage-through together gives a number close to what was reported before March 17. That is why the combined setting became the new reference for evergreen account structures. Evergreen means a long-running setup that is not tied to one short promotion.

Recommended Settings After the Change

Situation Recommended setting Notes
New campaigns from May 2026 7-day click plus 1-day engage-through Set at ad set level. The 7-day click window was previously the only setting in use.
Many accounts after the March 2026 redefinition 1-day click plus 1-day engage-through Came closest to the performance previously reported by 7-day click alone. Reported numbers stayed slightly above historical levels even with this setting.
  • Recommended setting for new campaigns from May 2026: 7-day click plus 1-day engage-through. It is set at ad set level. The 7-day click window was previously the only setting in use.
  • After Meta's March 2026 redefinition, data from many accounts showed that 1-day click plus 1-day engage-through came closest to the performance previously reported by 7-day click alone. Even with this combined setting, reported numbers stayed slightly above the historical levels.

Transition Rules

2 min

Principles for Changing Live Accounts

  • You cannot change the attribution setting of a published ad set. So an existing asset cannot simply be switched to 7-day click plus 1-day engage-through. An asset means any ad set or campaign. The transition has to be done with new ad sets or campaigns.
  • Do not switch the whole ad account at once. That resets the learning phase of every asset and causes a large performance drop. The learning phase is the period when Meta tests an ad set to find buyers. The transition should happen gradually over a longer period.
  • Do not put an ad set with a new setting into the same CBO campaign as ad sets with the old setting. Meta moves most of the budget to the ad set with more attributed conversions. Test the new setting in a separate campaign instead.

Transition Plan by Account Type

Account type Pace Planned timeline Reason
Satisfied with performance Slow, new campaign runs alongside the old one About six months Little urgency, so the risk of a large drop is avoided.
Break-even Bigger changes acceptable Short Performance was weak to begin with, so there is little to lose.
Losing money on first order Fast switch with a short overlap About three days of overlap Little to lose, so bigger changes are acceptable.

Transition Procedure for a Satisfied Account

  1. Keep the existing campaign running with its current attribution setting.
  2. Create a new campaign that uses 7-day click plus 1-day engage-through at ad set level.
  3. Launch all new ad sets and every new batch of creative only in the new campaign. Never add the new setting to the existing campaign.
  4. Keep the original campaign running for as long as it produces a positive contribution margin (profit left after the direct costs of a sale). Let the new campaign take over gradually.
  5. Plan for a transition period of about six months.

Transition Procedure for an Account Losing Money on First Order

  1. Duplicate the entire campaign that is currently running.
  2. Switch the duplicate to 7-day click plus 1-day engage-through.
  3. Run the original and the duplicate side by side for about three days.
  4. Once the new campaign outperforms the old one, which usually happens almost immediately, turn off the original campaign.
  5. Run only the new campaign from then on. Send all new ad sets into it.

Transition Procedure for a Break-Even Account

  • Copy the current campaign. In the copy, change every attribution setting to 7-day click plus 1-day engage-through. Load all the new creative into that copy. From then on, treat the new campaign as the account's structure.
  • Another route is to copy the best-performing ad sets into a fresh campaign with the correct setting. Or start new ad sets inside the existing campaign with that setting. Meta will favor the new ad sets. That is fine here, because performance was weak to begin with.

Incremental Attribution (Older Advice, 2025 Onward)

1 min

What It Is

  • Incremental attribution is an optimization option available since around October or November 2025. It aims at incremental conversions, meaning sales that would not have happened without the ad. It uses machine learning (software that learns patterns from data) to predict whether an ad caused a purchase. After the March 2026 change, incremental reporting was higher than 7-day click in some accounts. That made it worth testing.
  • A purchase from someone who had never seen the brand before counts as incremental. A buyer who had already visited the site, added to cart, or was on the email list is unlikely to count. So results depend heavily on how many returning customers reach the account.
  • Some accounts show an option at ad set level to optimize purely for new customers under incremental attribution.
  • Incremental optimization worked well for large brands that already optimize for incrementality. It performed poorly for many smaller accounts when it first appeared in late 2025. Its results depend on how many returning customers the account already receives. Test it before you rely on it.

Test Procedure

  1. Create a separate campaign with your best-performing evergreen ad sets.
  2. Run it with 7-day click plus 1-day engage-through as the optimization setting.
  3. Note the incremental number that comes out of the campaign.
  4. Break down new customer ROAS (return on ad spend, meaning revenue per dollar spent, counted only for new buyers) by audience segment and by spend split.
  5. Keep incremental only if it produces a better new customer ROAS than the 7-day click plus 1-day engage-through campaign.

Customer Lifecycle Settings

1 min

Where the Settings Are

  • Customer lifecycle strategy sits at ad set level, near the top of the screen. It offers Acquire new customers with exclusions. For example, it can exclude buyers from the last 180 days, using the pixel (Meta's tracking code on your site) and an existing customer list.
  • To tell Meta which buyers count as existing customers, open Account settings and scroll down to the existing customers section.
  • Once this is set, the campaign reports the split between new and existing customers.

Status of the Setting

  • Customer lifecycle strategy has shown poor results as of May 29, 2026. It is not recommended for acquiring new customers. Incremental attribution is the better option to test for that goal.

Playbook › 9. Meta and Facebook ads

Why Meta, platform basics and when to start

Section 9.1 · 1 of 22 in this chapter · 18 min read

In short

  • Meta, the company behind Facebook and Instagram, is the best paid channel for beginners.
  • Focus on Facebook, and use at most two platforms in total.
  • Google first. Ads to people who already visited your store can start early, with small budgets.
  • Do not launch ads before your store is approved.
  • Keep budgets small at first, and grow only what sells.
More key points
  • Follow the steps in order. Do not skip ahead.
  • Start with Google Shopping. It shows product ads on Google to people who do not know your store yet.
  • Run Meta (Facebook and Instagram) ads only for people who already visited your store, with a small budget.
  • Do not run ads to new people on Meta until your sales goal and Google Shopping limit are reached.
  • Wait for sales of about 50,000 dollars per month before social shopping. Wait for a 10x return on ad spend before scaling Pinterest.

Why Meta is the main paid channel for beginners

2 min
  • Meta is the parent company of Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp and Oculus. So Facebook advertising now runs under the Meta name.
  • Meta Ads Manager is Meta's tool for building and running ads. It creates and runs ads on Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp and third-party websites and apps in the Audience Network (outside sites and apps that Meta also shows ads on).
  • Facebook Ads is a paid social traffic source (visitors you pay to bring from social media). It is one of the main paid channels for a Shopify store (an online shop platform).
  • Meta's algorithm (the automatic system that decides who sees an ad) is the oldest and most consistent at finding target customers. Other platforms' algorithms are built on Meta's data and approach. This makes Meta the most reliable choice for scaling (growing budget and sales).
  • Meta is the best platform for beginners among all paid traffic options. The reasons are consistent results, scalability and ease of use. Ban risks still exist.
  • Facebook still has the strongest algorithm compared with TikTok, Pinterest and Snapchat. A beginner should concentrate on Facebook, and use at most two platforms in total.
  • Facebook is the main channel when your core audience (your main group of buyers) is active on it. Target women over 40 on Facebook, and test Google Ads and TikTok ads later.
  • Customer acquisition (finding new buyers) is built on profitable ads on Meta (Facebook and Instagram). So ad performance decides whether the business can grow.
  • Paid traffic compared with organic (free reach from posts):
    • Results come within days, not weeks.
    • Audience targeting is precise.
    • You control when to spend, through day parting (showing ads only at set hours).
    • Budgets can grow fast, from small to large.
  • Facebook suits higher-ticket items (products with a high price) if the product can justify the price.
  • Products priced above roughly 40 USD and aimed at an audience over 35 are better tested on Facebook and Instagram. They can succeed there even when they fail on TikTok.
  • Facebook was the main platform and fastest route to scaling in one operator's experience (a person running a store), with TikTok second. Google Ads were only tested for the brand at that time.
  • Facebook was the main source of traffic and sales for one store. Keeping Facebook as the core channel and testing a second platform reduces risk.
  • In one test for a female audience, Facebook produced better results than Pinterest, so Facebook was the main channel. Pinterest was not tested and could be checked as a second channel.

Platform basics: how Meta ads reach people

1 min
  • Meta delivers an ad much like organic content (free posts that reach people without paid ads). Its algorithm finds viewers based on how people respond to the creative (the ad itself: image, video or text). The advertiser's only controls are audience restrictions (targeting: who may see the ad) and budget.
  • Engagement (likes, comments, shares and watching) drives delivery. Meta favours ads with strong engagement.
    • Higher engagement usually brings a lower CPM (cost per 1,000 views) and a lower cost per click (CPC).
    • Creative quality should create engagement naturally. Manipulating comments is an untested tactic.
  • Meta punishes content that viewers skip or ignore. This raises CPMs and lowers CTR (click-through rate, the share of viewers who click) and engagement. So a poorly matched creative or audience drives costs up.
  • Meta targets across three funnel stages (the path from stranger to buyer):
    • Top funnel: cold prospects (people who do not know your store yet) with a relevant problem.
    • Middle funnel: people who engaged, for example by viewing or commenting.
    • Bottom funnel: high-intent actions, such as add to cart (putting an item in the basket), initiated checkout (starting to pay) and purchase.
  • Facebook has fewer targeting options than Google because it restricts some audience data, such as members of certain groups. Targeting is less precise than on YouTube. A Facebook view can register when someone scrolls past a video.
  • Facebook's algorithm tends to deliver to people with stronger purchase intent (people more likely to buy).

How the auction works

1 min
  • Ads are sold through an auction (a contest for each ad spot). Advertisers compete for the chance to show an ad to one particular person. The top-ranking bid claims the spot. A bid is the sum an advertiser will spend to reach that audience.
  • Facebook runs an auction for each ad impression (each single showing of an ad). The price depends on competition for the same audience.
    • Bidding 5 cents above your maximum bid gives you priority in the auction.
    • You do not pay that maximum. You pay the auction price.
  • Example of a two-advertiser auction: each side raises its bid to win more impressions.
    • A bid of 0.50 dollars is outbid by 1.00 dollar.
    • Then it is outbid by 1.10 dollars.
    • Then it is outbid by 1.25 dollars.
  • Every raised bid means a higher price for the same audience. So an advertiser leaving the auction lowers the price for everyone who stays.

How delivery and testing work

1 min
  • A new video ad is first shown to a small test group, for example about 1,000 people. Meta optimises delivery (adjusts who sees it) toward the people who engage.
  • A strong creative earns more engagement and watch time (how long people watch) from that group. So Meta finds similar people, and reach grows.
  • A weak creative gets fewer engaged viewers, so reach shrinks.
  • Thresholds for pushing an ad to a larger audience (the bar an ad must pass):
    • About 3% CTR or about 5% engagement. Engagement includes likes, comments and watching the video for more than 10 seconds.
    • At later stages, the examples given were 3 to 5% CTR and 6 to 10% engagement.
  • Widening stages (the steps where an ad's audience gets bigger). The sources differ on the sizes:

  • Cold traffic from the Meta Traffic goal (it pays for visits to the site, not for sales) tends to be low quality. Do not scale it up. If it is already running and performing acceptably, leave it unchanged.

Automation features

1 min
  • Modern Facebook advertising uses five core automation features, known as Power5 (five built-in tools that run parts of your ads for you):
    1. Auto advanced matching
    2. Dynamic ads (ads that show each visitor the products they looked at)
    3. Automatic ad placement (Meta picks the spots where ads show)
    4. Account simplification (fewer settings to manage)
    5. Campaign budget optimization (one budget for the campaign, shared across its ads)
  • The strategy is to set your objective and let Facebook's system find profitable customers and distribute ads across all placements.

Facebook vs TikTok

1 min
  • TikTok ads are hard for beginners. Creatives (ad videos or images) suffer from creative fatigue (viewers get tired of them) after a few weeks.
  • TikTok also requires constant video production, while Facebook offers the more powerful algorithm.
  • Facebook is preferred over TikTok here because its algorithm is older and more stable. Older audiences (40+, 50+) are easy to target.
  • TikTok suits younger-demographic products such as beauty, jewelry, fashion, health and wellness, fitness and toys. Its CPMs (cost per 1,000 views) fluctuate more.
  • Facebook tends to deliver stronger purchase intent, while TikTok gives broader reach where audience quality depends more on the product.
  • Testing counts. Facebook needed about four to five tested products to find a clear winner. TikTok needed about seven to eight.
  • Overlap (the same people reached again by different ads) is also lower on TikTok, because its audience is very large.
  • Facebook gives more chances per product test than TikTok. The risk on Facebook is account bans, which can be reduced by following a correct account setup process.
  • Case: in the Apple Watch strap test (summer), Facebook CPA (cost per acquisition, the cost per sale) was too high, while TikTok CPA was good.
  • Platform choice should be tested. Facebook also serves Instagram, so it reaches younger users than assumed. TikTok may serve a younger audience better. If ads do not work on Facebook, testing on TikTok is a reasonable next step.
  • Once a product works for one or two months on one channel, test it on TikTok, Google, Pinterest and organic social. A product that sells to one audience usually sells to others too.
  • Native ads (ads that look like normal content on a site), Pinterest, Snapchat and Twitter ads are better treated as supplements (extras) to Facebook, not primary channels.

Facebook and Instagram

1 min
  • Facebook is the strongest platform for audiences over 30 and for reaching people outside major cities. Instagram works better for younger audiences in major cities.
  • Instagram ads are made in the same ad platform as Facebook, with the same terms and targeting options. So audiences built for Facebook can be reused.
  • Instagram is best for visual products that look attractive in an image or short video.
  • Facebook owns Instagram. Running both together is a two-in-one approach for paid social (paid ads on social apps).
  • Instagram placements (spots where ads show) are slightly easier to get approved, but the return was lower in one case.
  • Set up an Instagram account and post a few times per week from the beginning.
  • Do not use Instagram for paid expansion until sales exceed about 50,000 dollars per month from Google Shopping (Google's product ads) and Facebook dynamic remarketing (ads showing visitors the products they looked at).

Pinterest

2 min
  • Pinterest is not beginner-friendly. Its setup requires a pixel tag (a small code snippet that tracks visitors). It also takes substantial effort to understand its algorithm. This deters most beginning operators.
  • Pinterest is a discovery and search platform where users come for inspiration and to buy.
  • In the US, Pinterest's CPM (cost per 1,000 views) typically runs around $3 to $5, which is cheaper than most ad platforms.
  • Niches (narrow market groups) that fit well include home decor, fashion, beauty and health. About 60% of users are women.
  • Pinterest has rare bans. It accepts traditional dropshipping products (products shipped straight from a supplier) that other platforms often reject.
  • More than 25% of time spent on Pinterest is shopping-related.
  • Pinterest suits products for home decor, beauty, home improvement, inspiration, DIY and art. Results are less consistent than on Facebook or TikTok, but the platform is cheap to test.
  • First Pinterest campaign structure:
    • 1 campaign (a group of ads sharing one goal and budget)
    • 1 interest (stacked interests allowed, meaning several interests combined)
    • 3 creatives (three ad versions)
    • Campaign budget of $5 per day
    • Launch at 00:00 UTC (midnight in the UTC world time standard)
  • Pinterest has no ad set budget option (no ABO, where each ad group gets its own budget). The only structure is a campaign budget optimization (CBO) campaign, with one budget for the whole campaign.
  • Testing a product on Pinterest ads can start at about $5 per day.
  • Run at least 5 products at a time, about $25 per day in total.
  • Finding winners usually takes more than a week.
  • Older test budget: a Pinterest test budget can start at about 5 to 10 dollars per day.
  • Pinterest can be connected to a store, and small-budget Pinterest ads can be tested.
  • Pinterest ads are not a first channel. Use them only after Google organic (free Google results), Google Shopping and remarketing are dialled in (set up and working well). They can work for some stores.
  • Do not spend on Pinterest ads until Google Shopping and Meta ads are scaled as high as they can go, while keeping ROAS (return on ad spend, sales divided by ad cost) at 10x or above.
  • Pinterest organic posting (free posts) can be done earlier (2025).

Twitter (X)

1 min
  • Twitter (X) has lower CPM (cost per 1,000 views) than Meta, TikTok or Google, because few e-commerce advertisers use it.
  • Twitter (X) is not beginner-friendly:
    • The algorithm is unproven.
    • Shopify integration (a link between the platform and your Shopify store) is unavailable.
    • Pixel tracking is not yet implemented.
    • Tracking tags must be added manually.
    • Only certain product types succeed.
  • X/Twitter ads were reported to have cheap CPMs and good CTR (click-through rates), mainly for specific countries such as the US or Gulf states.
  • That was not tested yet. Validate it (check it) before committing budget.

YouTube, Audience Network and LinkedIn

1 min
  • YouTube ads are not a core early channel. Most stores should not start them in the first six months to a year, because other channels offer more opportunity.
  • The Audience Network shows ads on third-party news sites and partner websites that Meta controls.
  • A shopper who left a store can later see that store's ads there. This placement reaches past visitors again; it does not find new buyers.
  • LinkedIn ads are very expensive and have rarely been profitable for e-commerce. So they are not recommended for beginners.
  • Relationships with LinkedIn groups and their sponsors are a better way to reach a B2B (business-to-business) audience.

Platform choice by product and audience

2 min
  • Paid social (ads on social apps) is a separate channel group that includes Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest and Twitter. It is grouped apart from search traffic in the overall funnel plan.
  • Search platforms such as Google, Amazon and YouTube search reach only about 5 to 10 percent of the market.
  • About 90 percent of the market sits on social platforms such as Facebook, Instagram and Pinterest.
  • Each platform needs its own conversation and an offer adjusted to it. Facebook works best as a conversation-starting channel.
  • Social media is effective at reaching unaware prospects (people who do not know they need you) and turning them into problem-aware prospects (people who know they have a problem).
  • Social media is a weak way to find the small group of buyers ready to purchase now. Retargeting (showing ads to people who already visited) is the exception.
  • Facebook can work across about 20 industries.
  • Service businesses such as plumbing or bricklaying are harder to sell through ads, because the need is urgent, not social.
  • First decide whether your customers search for the product or discover it socially, then choose the platform.
  • Organic Instagram growth (free follower growth from posts) was realistic around 2019. But algorithms are more complex now.
  • So a modern brand typically needs paid advertising to reach buyers. Plan the marketing budget with paid media (paid ads) in mind from the start.
  • Paid social is not required for success. Dynamic remarketing is the main recommended use, and flashy video ads are not necessary to start.
  • Sending Facebook and Pinterest traffic directly to an Amazon product listing (the product page on Amazon) does not convert well (does not turn visitors into buyers well).
  • These platforms work best for content marketing (helpful posts that attract people), email list building and retargeting.
  • They do not work well for cold traffic sent to a marketplace listing. There, the customer cannot see the brand story or benefit from the community.
  • Facebook works well for product launches with heavy discounts and hyper-targeted audiences (very narrow audience groups).
  • Facebook users are in social mode, not buying mode. So ads need to build awareness and desire before purchase.
  • This makes Facebook better suited to cold traffic acquisition than Amazon's buying-mindset audience.
  • Facebook is currently difficult for beginners to test products on, because it is expensive and bans are common. In this case, Instagram placements were slightly easier to get approved, but the return was lower.

Channel order: Google first, Meta remarketing early

2 min
  • Google has buying intent because people search for what they want. Facebook targets broad audiences who are not actively searching.
  • This is why Google is the first platform to prioritise.
  • Facebook ads are useful for cold traffic. But for buyers who actively search for a product, Google ads deliver more profitable traffic.
  • Facebook is not dropped, but it is used with less priority.
  • Facebook ads are best used for remarketing to people who already visited the store. Broad audience targeting is weaker for finding new buyers.
  • Keep Facebook at about 10% of the total advertising budget.
  • Starting Facebook campaigns before Google Shopping is a poor order. Facebook is better used to capture visitors who left the store.
  • Meta remarketing is worth setting up early, even with a small budget. Return on ad spend is usually high when the budget is small and each sale is worth a lot.
  • Set up Meta remarketing once the Google negatives (negative keywords: search words that block your ads) are in place (2025).
  • Start Meta with remarketing ads only, and do not test cold Meta ads at the beginning.
  • Scale Meta after Google Shopping has reached its practical budget limit (2025).
  • Facebook ads should start early with very small budgets and be used for dynamic remarketing.
  • Start them when your Google ads go live.
  • Remarketing should run from day one, since it usually produces the most profitable ads.
  • But it cannot work without Google traffic feeding the audience.
  • Paid promotion of Facebook page posts, such as boosting (paying to show a post to more people) them to remarketing audiences of past visitors, should not start from day one.
  • Google Ads offers easier early wins for a new store. So paid Meta posts come after the organic page is set up and active.
  • Do not run Facebook awareness ads (ads to make people know your brand) while the store is still being built or before suppliers are uploaded.
  • Start them once the store has products and the pages are ready.
  • Do not set up Facebook or Google ad accounts until supplier products are live in the store, because ads need real products to send traffic to.
  • Do not launch Meta ads before the store has been reviewed and approved. Launching early is a common rookie (beginner) mistake that wastes ad spend.
  • Launch only after store approval.

When to hold off on cold traffic

1 min
  • Do not send cold Facebook traffic to the store until it generates at least 50,000 dollars in sales per month from Google Shopping and Facebook dynamic remarketing combined. Those channels are easier wins first.
  • Sources differ on the gate (the rule for when to start). The 2021 source uses the $50,000 monthly sales gate above.
  • The 2025 source says not to test cold Meta at the beginning. It says to scale Meta after Google Shopping reaches its practical budget limit.
  • Cold Meta traffic from the Traffic goal tends to be low quality. Do not scale it up.
  • Pinterest scaling gate: do not spend until Google Shopping and Meta are scaled, with ROAS at 10x or above.

When to hold off on social commerce

1 min
  • Social commerce means selling inside social apps. Use Facebook dynamic remarketing as one of the core paid channels before trying social commerce such as Instagram Shopping.
  • Instagram social commerce is not a requirement and can distract early on.
  • A store should reach about 50,000 dollars per month in revenue before testing social commerce.
  • As of August 2019, the Facebook Shop channel (Facebook's built-in shop) was not a proven extra revenue source. So ads that send traffic to the store site remain the priority.
  • Selling directly on Facebook is not recommended, because the platform is not built for it. Send traffic to your own store or sales page instead.
  • Facebook was rolling out seller listings (product listings inside Facebook), but at that time they were not yet reliable.
  • Selling directly inside a social platform was not recommended for Facebook at the time, because its seller listings were still unproven.
  • Pinterest had run on-platform selling for longer and was described as comparatively good for it.

Operator sequence for starting Meta

1 min

  1. Store reviewed and approved. Supplier products go live in the store.

    • Supplier: the company that provides the products you sell.
    • Live: visible to customers.

  2. Google ads go live. Start with Google Shopping for cold traffic.

    • Google ads: paid ads shown on Google.
    • Google Shopping: product ads with pictures and prices on Google.
    • Cold traffic: visitors who do not know your store yet.

  3. Set up Meta with remarketing ads only, at a small budget, once Google negatives are in place.

    • Meta: the company behind Facebook and Instagram. Its ad system is used here.
    • Remarketing ads: ads shown only to people who already visited your store.
    • Google negatives: blocked search words. Your Google ads will not show for these words.

  4. Create an Instagram account. Post a few times per week from the beginning, without paid expansion yet.

    • Instagram: a photo and video app owned by Meta.
    • Post: share a picture or video on your account.
    • Paid expansion: paying to reach more people. Do not do this yet.

  5. Start cold Meta traffic only after the revenue gate and Google Shopping limit are reached. Check against the sources above.

    • Cold Meta traffic: ads that reach new people who do not know your store.
    • Revenue gate: a sales level you must reach first.
    • Google Shopping limit: the set limit for Google Shopping.

  6. Start social commerce only after remarketing is strong and revenue is around 50,000 dollars per month.

    • Social commerce: selling products directly inside social apps.
    • Remarketing is strong: your remarketing ads already bring in good sales.

  7. Start Pinterest ads only after Google Shopping and Meta are scaled with a 10x return on ad spend or above.

    • Pinterest: a site where people save and share images.
    • Scaled: spending and earning more on the ads.
    • Return on ad spend (ROAS): money earned for each dollar spent on ads. 10x means 10 dollars back for every 1 dollar spent.
    • Pinterest organic posting, which means free posts without ads, can start earlier.

Further details

1 min
  • Bing search text ads: do not use until the store is already very profitable. Use a store-name campaign only to block competitors from taking branded traffic.
    • Bing: a search engine, like Google.
    • Text ads: short written ads in search results.
    • Store-name campaign: ads that show when people search for your store name.
    • Branded traffic: visitors who search for your store name.
    • Competitors: other stores selling similar products.

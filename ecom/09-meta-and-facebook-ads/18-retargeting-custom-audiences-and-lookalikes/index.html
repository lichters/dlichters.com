Playbook › 9. Meta and Facebook ads

Retargeting, custom audiences and lookalikes

Section 9.18 · 18 of 22 in this chapter · 46 min read

In short

  • Build six audiences from what people do on your store, before you start ads.
  • The Meta pixel (a small tracking code on your store) must be installed and linked first. Without it, store audiences cannot be built.
  • Check that all six audiences exist before you launch.
  • Building audiences costs no ad money.
  • Run new-customer ads (cold interest targeting) as the main plan. Retargeting, which shows ads to people who already know the store, is a smaller part.
More key points
  • Give retargeting about 10% of the ad budget. Adjust it by return (ROAS, the money earned per dollar spent).
  • Stop ads to recent buyers. Exclude everyone who bought in the last 180 days.
  • Create the Purchased 180 days audience before you publish, so Meta knows who already bought.
  • Showing the same ad to one person too often makes them tired of it. Change the ad instead of repeating it.
  • Add-to-cart audiences can stop selling without warning. Check their results every one to two days.
  • A new audience needs time to fill with people before you judge its size.
  • Start retargeting only after your ads have run for more than two weeks.
  • Connect the tracking code (pixel) first, or your website audiences will be empty.

Audience types and what they are for

2 min
  • A store audience is a group of people defined by actions on your store, such as visiting pages or buying.
  • Your store runs on Shopify (online store software). Once it is linked to Meta (the company behind Facebook and Instagram), those actions are sent to Meta.
  • Then ads can target people by how they behave.
  • Creating audiences costs no ad spend. They should already hold people by the time ads launch.
  • You need two store audience types: website visitor audiences (people who visited the site) and customer audiences (people who placed orders).
  • Custom audiences (groups you build from your own data) can come from customer contacts, website traffic or a mobile app.
  • For store audiences, select the website source.
  • Website visitor audiences are built from the tracking pixel. They can feed retargeting (showing ads again to people who already came) or a lookalike (a new group of people similar to your buyers).
  • Customer data files are a second source. An example is an export of Shopify customer emails.
  • The Audience menu offered four audience source options at the time.
  • Page and Instagram engagement audiences include people who liked, commented, shared or followed.
  • They can reach shoppers that Facebook can no longer track on the website since iOS 14 (an Apple phone update that limits tracking).
  • Engagement audiences are used for remarketing and as the base for lookalike audiences.
  • Engaged audience: people who added to cart (put an item in the basket), started checkout, visited the website, or are on the email list, but have not completed a purchase.
  • Existing customer audience: everyone who has made a purchase with the brand. They are tracked through the Facebook pixel or identified through the email list.
  • A window is the number of past days a person counts in. Warm audiences (people who already know your brand) with low traffic start with broad 180-day windows.
  • With high traffic, split them into 7, 30, 60 and 90 day windows.
  • The six core audiences, based on behavior and time frame:
    1. Visited website in last 180 days.
    2. Engaged on Facebook in last 180 days.
    3. Engaged on Instagram in last 180 days.
    4. Visited website in last 7 days.
    5. Made a purchase in last 7 days.
    6. Made a purchase in last 180 days.
  • High-ticket products (expensive items): build a custom audience from value purchases. For example, use buyers who spent more than the average order value (such as 100 USD). Then create lookalikes from it.

Pixel prerequisites

1 min
  1. The Meta pixel must already be installed on the Shopify store and linked to the Meta ad account (the account that runs ads) before store audiences can be built.
  2. Without that link, the pixel will not show as active (green) in the audience source field. Purchase events (records of sales) will not reach Meta.
  3. A Meta ad account should normally hold only one Meta pixel. Every website-based audience then uses that single pixel as its source.
  4. If more than one pixel appears, pick the one linked to the Shopify store and the ad account.

Building store audiences in Business Manager and Ads Manager

3 min

Business Manager is Meta's admin tool for businesses. Ads Manager is where ads are made and managed.

Business Manager route (procedure)

  1. Go to business.facebook.com.
  2. Click the three-line menu.
  3. Click Audiences.
  4. Create a website visitors audience named website visits 180 days, with the window set to 180 days.
  5. Create a Facebook engagement audience with a 180-day window.
  6. Create an Instagram engagement audience with a 180-day window.
  7. Create a website visitors audience with a 7-day window.
  8. Create purchaser audiences for the last 7 days and the last 180 days.

These audiences update automatically as people enter or leave each window.

Ads Manager route (procedure)

  1. Log in to Meta Business Manager.
  2. Click the three-line hamburger menu (the icon of three stacked lines) in the top left corner.
  3. Scroll down the side menu and click Audiences.
  4. If no audiences exist yet, three creation options appear. Choose Create custom audience (the top option).
  5. Under sources, check the box Website. The next screen opens automatically.

Website visitor audiences (procedure)

Retention is how many days back the audience looks.

  1. Start a new custom audience and select the source Website.
  2. Under source, select the store's pixel (the one linked to the store and the ad account). Confirm it shows green.
  3. Leave Events on the default All website visitors. Events are the actions the audience counts.
  4. Set Retention to 7 days and name the audience, following the naming pattern, for example website visitors - 7 days.
  5. Click the blue Create audience button at the bottom.
  6. Repeat steps 1 to 5 with Retention 30 and the name website visitors - 30 days.
  7. Repeat steps 1 to 5 with Retention 180 and the name website visitors - 180 days.

All other settings stay identical across the three audiences.

Customer (purchased) audiences (procedure)

  1. Click Create custom audience in the Audiences section.
  2. Select the source Website.
  3. Select the same pixel, which should be the only pixel in the ad account. Confirm it shows green.
  4. Under Events, open the dropdown and choose Purchase instead of All website visitors.
  5. Set Retention to 7 days and name the audience purchased - 7 days.
  6. Click Create audience.
  7. Repeat steps 1 to 6 with Retention 30 and the name purchased - 30 days.
  8. Repeat steps 1 to 6 with Retention 180 and the name purchased - 180 days.
  • Keeping the Events field on All website visitors builds a visitor audience, not a buyer audience.
  • Choose Purchase in the Events dropdown so only buyers are included.

Workaround for a new store (procedure)

Use this when Purchase does not appear under Events, because no sale has been recorded.

  1. In the Shopify back end (the admin area of your store), create a test product (any name, for example test product 123) priced at $1.
  2. Open the store as a customer and buy the test product.
  3. Wait until the purchase is recorded. Shopify then notifies Meta that an e-commerce purchase took place.
  4. Return to the Meta Audiences section and open the Events dropdown again. Purchase should now be listed.
  5. Build the purchase audiences with the same settings as above.

If the pixel was already active and sales had already come through, Purchase appears by default. No test product is needed.

  • Check all six in the Audiences section of Meta Business Manager before you start ads.

Engagement and video audiences

1 min
  • Video retargeting based on watch depth (how much of a video someone watched) is a classic approach.
  • People who watched 50 percent or 75 percent of a video are retargeted with an offer.
  • It still works after iOS, though fewer people are trackable.
  • 75 percent video view depth has performed best.
  • Stack audiences (combine them) in the middle of the funnel (the path from first look to buying) and bottom of the funnel. Middle: video viewers and page engagers. Bottom: add to cart and engaged with brand.
  • Test different view depths (25%, 50%, 75%). Or stack them into one large audience to maximize reach (how many different people see your ads).
  • A common retargeting option is people who watched 75% of a video.
  • Stack related audiences (video views, engagement, page views, added to cart) into one larger audience. Facebook tracks retargeting poorly, so one big audience helps.

Video-view custom audience (procedure)

  • Go to Ads Manager > All tools > Audiences > Create audience > Custom audience.

  • Select video engagement and choose the relevant video ads.

  • Set a people-who-watched-at-least-25%-of-video threshold (the minimum watch amount that counts).

  • Select a retention window. The common windows are 7, 14 and 30 days. 14 to 30 days is the usual range.

  • Engagement audiences as seeds (the starting group a lookalike is built from): a 1% lookalike built from purchase events shows high intent (a strong sign of buying). 75 percent video-view audiences may also work as a seed.

  • Page and Instagram engagement custom audiences: create them in the Audiences section with a window of 7, 14 or 30 days (30 days in the example).

  • Website audiences are built from pixel events such as add to cart, initiate checkout and purchase.

  • Engagement audience case: a Facebook page engagement audience (past 30 days, about 3,600 engagers) built a top 1% lookalike (the closest 1% of matches). It reached about 720,000 people.

Engaged audience and existing-customer segments

1 min

Engaged audience (procedure)

  1. Click Create custom audience.
  2. Select Website as the source, because that is where the actions happen.
  3. Click Next.
  4. Set the time window to 30 to 60 days.
  5. Select the three events: website visitors, add to cart and initiate checkouts.
  6. Click Create audience to add it to your engaged audience.
  • Include three events in the engaged audience: website visitors, add to cart and initiate checkout.
  • The custom audience form includes an Exclude people field with a limit of 0/50. It excludes certain audiences from the definition.
  • Engaged audiences are still treated as new customers for targeting and reporting, because they have not yet made a purchase.

Existing customers

  • Define existing customers as people who made a purchase in the last 180 days, based on the Facebook pixel.
  • Existing customers include email-list purchasers as well as pixel-tracked ones.

Syncing a Klaviyo purchaser segment (procedure)

Klaviyo is an email tool. A segment is a saved group of contacts.

  1. In Klaviyo, create a segment for everyone who made a purchase, so the segment updates continuously.
  2. Go to Klaviyo's APIs and integrations section (the settings for connecting other tools).
  3. Connect Facebook to Klaviyo.
  4. Follow Klaviyo's steps to complete the synchronization. Your Klaviyo audience will then sync as a Facebook custom audience.

Audience segment reporting

1 min

Setup and display

  • Audience segments let you define groups of customers. You then receive performance reporting broken down by those definitions.
  • This shows how ad spend (money spent on ads) flows between new and existing audiences.
  • In Ads Manager, go to Advertiser Settings > Audience Segments to define and edit the engaged audience and the existing customer audience.
  • At campaign level, the Audience segment reporting section displays the defined segments. Click Edit in advertiser settings from there to modify the definitions.
  • After changing segment definitions, allow a few minutes for the changes to reach reporting. The confirmation message states that changes typically happen within a few minutes.

Breakdown (procedure)

  1. Go to the campaign level in Ads Manager.
  2. Click Breakdown.
  3. Search for audience segments, or navigate to Demographics > Audience segments.
  4. Campaign performance is displayed broken down by new audience, engaged audience and existing customers.

Check of spend split (procedure)

  1. Break down your campaign by audience segments.
  2. Verify that the majority of ad spend goes to new and engaged audiences.
  3. If the majority does not go to new audiences, investigate two things. First, whether campaign exclusions exist. Second, whether they are configured tightly enough.
  • Rule: make sure the majority of ad spend goes to new and engaged audiences, not existing customers.

Customer list uploads and seed sources

2 min

Creating a custom audience from an email list (procedure)

  1. Go to Audiences in the Ads Manager sidebar and create a custom audience.
  2. Choose the data file option, then give the audience a name and description.
  3. Upload the file as a CSV (a spreadsheet file type) or TXT file. It must contain only one column of email addresses.
  4. If the audience shows as not ready, wait about 10 seconds, refresh the page and wait again. Processing can take a couple of hours.
  5. When it is ready, check how many profiles matched the email addresses.

Uploading a customer list

  • To upload a customer list: go to Audiences, click Create Audience, choose Custom Audience. Then select the existing customer list option instead of website traffic.
  • Facebook accepts email addresses or phone numbers. Names are not needed.
  • A customer list needs a minimum of 100 customers before Facebook will create a lookalike audience from it.
  • Upload the list. Facebook returns the audience in about 35 seconds.
  • Upload an existing customer list to Meta, even a small one of about 100 orders. This gives the pixel a head start in finding similar buyers.
  • Use a customer list uploaded to Facebook as a custom audience when running Advantage+ campaigns (Meta's automated campaign type).
  • In one example, broad targeting with the list still underperformed. So test it rather than assume it works.

Mistakes and match rates

  • A match rate is the share of your emails that Meta recognizes. Match rates are not guaranteed.
  • In one example, 1,300 of 2,000 emails (65%) matched.
  • Uploading an email list unrelated to your niche gives weak results. The lookalike built from it will not represent your buyers.
  • Do not use custom audiences in the early testing phase.
  • Start with interest targeting (choosing people by their hobbies and interests) and add custom audiences later.

Opted-in leads and LinkedIn

Opted-in leads are people who agreed to hear from you.

  1. Build your LinkedIn connections with the people who fit your target buyer.
  2. Export that contact list.
  3. Upload it to Facebook (and Twitter) as a custom audience.
  4. Market a product that is relevant to their office or role.
  • Cold ad sets (ads aimed at people who do not know you yet) use interest audiences and lookalikes.
  • Build lookalikes from customers when there is enough data. Otherwise, build them from opted-in leads. If needed, build them from website visitors.

Lookalike audiences

3 min

A lookalike is a new group of people who are similar to a group you already have. That group is the seed.

Creating a lookalike (procedure)

  1. Go to Audiences, choose create audience and then lookalike audience.
  2. Select the custom audience you created as the source.
  3. Select the country to target.
  4. Set the audience size to similarity, not reach (how many people it can touch). Reach-based optimization gave the opposite effect in testing.
  5. Create the audience and wait, usually a couple of hours, until it shows as ready.
  6. Enter the lookalike audience into the custom audience field of your precise interest ad set to narrow it. Do not target the lookalike alone.

Size bands and seeds

  • Choose the top 1% of similar people for the largest reach. Or choose narrower bands, such as the top 1 to 2% or top 2 to 4%, for closer matches.
  • Create the lookalike with engagers as the source. An example is people who engaged with the Facebook page in the past 30 days.
  • Split purchaser lookalikes into size ranges such as 0 to 1, 1 to 2 and 2 to 3 percent. Test which pool converts best.
  • The 1% band gives the largest reach. Start there before testing narrower bands.
  • Build lookalikes from both seed audiences and engagement audiences. Then rotate new content through all of them.
  • Lookalikes are a cheap way to start on Facebook without wasting money on guesses.
  • Lookalikes are less effective than before. One-percent lookalikes for purchase events still show high intent.
  • Build lookalikes after a product has several proven audiences, usually three to four winners (audiences that already bring sales).
  • Each winner can be scaled on its own. Lookalikes made from those winners help Meta find similar people at a larger size.

Reach and results

  • A lookalike built from a small customer list (even around 100 emails) typically landed near 2 million people.
  • It works best as a narrowing layer on top of an interest-based ad set. It is not a stand-alone target.
  • With a niche-relevant list, the combined audience was expected to fall to about 200,000 to 250,000 people.
  • In another example, a customer list of 100 or more people produced a lookalike of about 170,000 people. The maximum reach can go up to 2 million.
  • Narrowing that lookalike to people who also like a proven interest reduced it to about half or a third. But it made the audience far more targeted.
  • Run a split test between a precise interest campaign and a lookalike campaign. A split test shows two options side by side.
  • Compare cost per conversion (what one sale costs). Test the lookalike against interest targeting rather than assuming it is better.

Case: CrossFit equipment store

  • Starting point: the whole United States audience of about 150 to 250 million people.
  • Targeting a large niche brand page narrowed it to about 500,000 to 600,000 people. Adding related pages raised it to about 700,000 to 800,000.
  • The lead ad set on a single precise interest showed a potential audience of about 560,000 people.
  • A customer email list of about 2,000 addresses, mostly not CrossFit-related, matched 1,300 profiles (65%). It produced a lookalike of about 2.1 million people.
  • Combined with the precise interest ad set, the potential audience dropped to about 38,000, because the list was off-niche. A niche-relevant list was expected to bring it to about 200,000 to 250,000.
  • Over two days, the lookalike campaign reached a cost per conversion of 0.93 dollars. The precise interest campaign reached 1.20 dollars. That is a saving of about 30%. Savings of up to half were seen in other cases.

Formula and seeds (formula)

  • Lookalike audience: give Facebook a seed audience, such as a retargeting list of past site visitors. Ideally, use people who converted (bought).
  • Ask Facebook to find new people with similar attributes. These are cold users, people who have not visited the site yet.
  • An example lookalike reached 1.2 million people.

Size and sources: a note on conflicts

  • Lookalike size depends on the seed. One source gives the top 1% as the largest reach. Another shows about 2 million from a small generic list. A third shows about 170,000 from a list of 100 or more people.
  • Use the size band that matches your seed. Then test before scaling.
  • The processing time differs by source. A customer-list lookalike returned in about 35 seconds in one example. Another source said a couple of hours.
  • Expect a couple of hours for a lookalike to show as ready. Check the status before launch.

Retargeting by timing and funnel position

3 min
  • Retargeting (showing ads again to people who already visited or engaged with your store) from the store is limited.
  • Run cold interest targeting as the main strategy. Cold means new people who have never visited the store, chosen by their interests.
  • Email to a list of customers can drive strong revenue. Email to customers is a form of retargeting.
  • Use Facebook retargeting mainly during promotions, such as targeting past purchases.
  • The Facebook funnel (the path from first contact to purchase) has three stages.
  • The top of the funnel is the first stage. Use cold interest audiences there.
  • The middle of the funnel uses engagement audiences: people who interacted with your page, Instagram or ads.
  • The bottom of the funnel uses website visitors and add-to-cart audiences. Add-to-cart means the person put a product in their cart.
  • Cold interest targeting often outperformed retargeting after the iOS 14 update. iOS 14 is an Apple iPhone software update that limited ad tracking.
  • Prioritize interests that have worked before over retargeting channels.
  • Run cold interest targeting and retargeting as separate campaigns.
  • Target interests that have worked before.
  • Retarget website visitors, especially same-day visitors.
  • Also retarget form fillers (people who filled in a form), post engagers (people who interacted with a post), and anyone who visited the webpage.
  • Run multiple campaigns at the same time.
  • Retargeting can work on Facebook when an offer or message overcomes an objection. An objection is a reason not to buy.
  • Use testimonials (customer reviews) for trust problems.
  • Use a discount for price concerns.
  • Use proof for doubt that the product can solve the problem.
  • Showing the same ad again rarely works.
  • Retarget people who already acted on a page or post, such as page views or engagement.
  • Do not add extra age or demographic limits (limits by age, gender and similar traits). The audience was already qualified at the top of the funnel.
  • Target engaged shoppers as an audience. This was reported to perform well.
  • Interest targeting by job title was not tested, and the audience may be too small.
  • Facebook retargeting ads for store visitors are worth running. They have a specific setup.
  • Retargeting is a standard practice used by the store.
  • Set up Facebook remarketing audiences (saved groups of people to show ads to) before running remarketing ads. Remarketing is another word for retargeting.
  • Keep Facebook targeting simple at the start.
  • Dynamic retargeting (ads that automatically show each person the exact products they viewed) only needs site visitors.
  • Skip age ranges and interest-group testing.
  • Use an ad that shows the product the person previously viewed.
  • Segment remarketing by how long ago someone visited without buying. Segment means split into groups.
  • Recent visitors get direct-response ads (ads that push for a sale now) that bring them back to the product.
  • Older visitors get ads that build trust and credibility.
  • Shorter remarketing windows (the number of days back you look for visitors) are more profitable.
  • A visitor who left recently is more likely to buy than one who left months ago.
  • until Facebook is consistently profitable, run only dynamic product ad remarketing. A dynamic product ad (DPA) is an ad that shows products automatically from your product catalog.
  • Avoid promotions segmented for cold audiences.
  • The most successful stores also reach people who are not searching for their product. They do this through pre-sales engagement, which is content that builds interest before the sales pitch.
  • Search campaigns and site-visitor retargeting reach only people already in market. In market means already looking to buy.
  • So add cold content to grow beyond them.

Sales funnel procedure

1 min
  1. Serve pre-sales engagement content to a cold lookalike audience. A lookalike audience is new people who look like your existing buyers.
  2. If the visitor buys right away, the sale is done.
  3. If the visitor views a product but does not buy, enter dynamic product ad retargeting.
  4. If the visitor only reaches the home page, retarget with more commercial pre-sales content, such as comparison charts, buyer's guides (guides that help people choose), user stories, or uses of products the store sells.
  5. At the buying stage, use Product Listing Ads (PLAs, Google ads that show a product image and price), search text ads, and search audience ads.

Exclusions

2 min
  • Use exclusions (rules that hide ads from certain people) to keep specific audiences, such as recent buyers, out of campaigns that should not show them ads.
  • To stop showing ads to people who bought in the last 180 days, add a purchasers exclusion in the audience controls.
  • Exclusions matter most when many existing customers are being targeted.
  • Exclusions matter less when audiences are mostly new.
  • For a custom audience (a saved list of people) of past purchasers, exclude only the 180-day purchaser audience.
  • The 7-day and 30-day purchaser audiences are subsets (smaller groups inside a bigger group) of the 180-day audience. They need no separate exclusions.
  • Exclude website purchasers from the last 180 days.
  • Exclude existing Shopify customers (Shopify is an online store platform) from prospecting campaigns. Prospecting means reaching new people.
  • Use 7-day click and 1-day view attribution for these campaigns. Attribution is the rule that decides which ad gets credit for a sale. A 7-day click means the person clicked an ad within 7 days. A 1-day view means they saw an ad within 1 day.
  • Promotion Meta ads should repeat the message of the promotion's emails. Meta is the company behind Facebook and Instagram.
  • Show promotion ads to all website visitors from the last 180 days.
  • Exclude all customers who purchased in the last 30 days.
  • For Meta retargeting, use all website visitors from the last 180 days.
  • Exclude all customers from the last 30 days.
  • The same audience can show each promotion's offer several times within a three-month span.
  • Always exclude purchasers and website visitors from middle-of-funnel retargeting ad sets. An ad set is a group inside a campaign that shares one audience and budget.
  • Exclude purchasers from bottom-of-funnel audiences as well. This keeps audiences from overlapping, meaning the same people are not in two places.
  • Remove bias audiences (audiences that skew results) unless retargeting for a new product or special offer.
  • In the remarketing ad set, add Purchased 180 days as the ad exclusion.
  • Existing customers can show as undefined in reporting (the results screens).
  • Without the Purchased 180 days audience defined there, Meta does not know who has already bought.
  • Set the Purchased 180 days audience before publishing.

Advantage+ shopping and catalog campaigns

1 min

Advantage+ shopping

1 min
  • Advantage+ is Meta's set of automated campaign and ad features. Advantage+ shopping campaigns are one of them.
  • Advantage+ shopping campaigns take creatives (ad images and text) and a customer list.
  • They use broad targeting and let Meta find buyers.
  • When this was written, the cost was high, and significant budget was needed. Results were not yet proven for many advertisers.
  • Advantage+ shopping campaigns (Facebook's version of Google's Performance Max, an automated campaign type) performed well for the author.
  • They require a substantial budget to perform.
  • In Advantage+ campaigns, Facebook asks the advertiser what percentage should go to retargeting and what percentage to prospecting.
  • Facebook then assigns time to the retargeting audience.

Advantage+ catalog ads

1 min
  • Advantage+ catalog ads were formerly called dynamic ads.
  • They use machine learning (software that learns patterns from data) to scale ads automatically when a store has a broad range or a large volume of products.
  • Each person is shown the products they are most likely to want, including people who viewed products on the site.
  • The product catalog (your full list of products with names, images and prices) feeds dynamic ads.
  • The catalog must be linked to the campaign, or the carousel (a row of product cards) cannot show the viewed products.
  • Advantage+ catalog ads use the linked product catalog to show relevant products. They base this on each person's interests, intent (signs they want to buy) and actions.
  • For dynamic remarketing, keep this option switched on.

Remarketing campaign build

1 min

Campaign-level principles

2 min
  • A Meta remarketing campaign built this way serves ads on Facebook, Instagram, Messenger and the Audience Network (Meta's partner apps and websites) from one setup.
  • Meta adapts the single set of inputs to each placement's format. A placement is the spot where an ad appears.
  • The remarketing campaign depends on the website-visitor and purchaser audiences and the product catalog built earlier.
  • Do not start it until those are in place.
  • Manual sales setup is chosen over Advantage+ shopping because it gives precise control over who sees the ads.
  • For remarketing, this control matters more than Meta's broader automated targeting.
  • Keep the Advantage+ audience switched to original audience options for remarketing.
  • Advantage+ audience expands reach beyond the defined custom audiences. This breaks the control the campaign needs.
  • Keep Advantage placements on for remarketing. Advantage placements means Meta picks where ads show.
  • The audience is narrow, and Meta places ads where these people are.
  • Manual placements remain possible if you want to restrict the ads to a single platform.
  • Build the campaign as evergreen (it keeps running): no spending limit and no end date. This lets it keep selling as the audience fills.
  • The only regular change is the daily budget.
  • The Meta pixel (a tracking code on your store) records website activity.
  • Select it under Tracking with Website events, so visitor audiences and purchase conversions (sales Meta counts) can be built.
  • Choose the main pixel created earlier.
  • If several product catalogs are linked to the Meta account, the wrong one can be attached to the campaign.
  • Check the catalog dropdown (the menu of catalogs) and select the correct catalog.
  • For a dynamic remarketing campaign, set Buying type to Auction (bid-based buying) and Campaign objective to Sales.
  • Within Sales, the catalog sales result shows product-specific ads from the store catalog.
  • Other Sales results, such as Messenger, Instagram, WhatsApp and calls, serve different goals.
  • In a dynamic remarketing carousel, each viewer sees the products they most recently viewed on the store, pulled from the linked catalog.
  • This is shown rather than a general selection.
  • The audience and the catalog must be connected for the ads to match each person's browsing.

Campaign level (procedure)

1 min
  1. Log in to the Meta Ads account and click the green Create button.
  2. Keep Buying type on Auction.
  3. Set Campaign objective to Sales. If it is not preselected, click Sales.
  4. Click Continue.
  5. Choose Manual setup (manual sales campaign) instead of Advantage+ shopping campaign, then click Continue.
  6. Rename the campaign to a descriptive name, for example Store Name Dynamic Remarketing Campaign.
  7. Special ad categories: leave empty and scroll past. Special ad categories are topics Meta has extra rules for. Leave it empty unless the business falls under financial products and services, employment, housing, social issues, elections or politics.
  8. Campaign spending limits: leave empty, because the campaign is evergreen and runs until switched off manually.
  9. Keep Advantage+ catalog ads switched on and select the product catalog. If several catalogs are linked to the account, pick the correct one from the dropdown.
  10. Set the Advantage campaign budget (one budget Meta shares across the campaign's ad sets) as the daily budget. Use 10% of the Google Shopping daily budget. Google Shopping means Google's product ads.
  11. Keep Campaign bid strategy on Highest volume. The bid strategy is how Meta spends the budget to get results.
  12. Leave time-of-day bidding and day-of-week scheduling at their defaults. Time-of-day bidding means paying more at certain hours.
  13. Keep A/B testing off. A/B testing compares two versions against each other.
  14. Under Reporting, check that Existing customers shows Defined.
  15. If it does not, click the caret (the small arrow), search for the audience Purchased 180 days, and select it.

Ad set level (procedure)

2 min
  1. Click New sales ad set in the left navigation and rename it, for example Store Name Dynamic Remarketing Ad Set.
  2. Ignore the templates option.
  3. Set Performance goal to Maximize number of conversions and the conversion event to Purchase. The performance goal is what the ad set aims for. A conversion event is the action Meta counts as success.
  4. Under Show more settings, leave the attribution settings at default.
  5. Under Product set (the group of products the ads can show), keep All products.
  6. Do not narrow by product category or any other breakdown.
  7. Under Budget and scheduling (when ads run and how much they spend), do not set a future start date beyond immediate start. Set no end date and no spending limits. Leave everything at default.
  8. Under Audience controls (the settings that choose who sees the ad), set the location to the country where you do business only.
  9. Do not add other criteria, and do not set a minimum age.
  10. Under Advantage+ audience, click Switch to original audience options.
  11. Keep location on your business country, age range at the default 18 to 65+, and gender on all genders.
  12. Leave Advantage detailed targeting (demographics, interests, behaviors) completely blank.
  13. Under Custom audiences, click Search existing audiences and add Website visitors 180 days as the included audience.
  14. Under Ad exclusions, add the Purchased 180 days audience.
  15. Make sure the checkbox that lets Meta reach beyond the custom audience when it predicts better performance stays unchecked. Meta may tick it automatically when you add the exclusion.
  16. Keep Placements on Advantage placements.
  17. Leave Show more options at default.
  • Bidding objective conflict: the ad set procedure uses Maximize number of conversions with the Purchase event.
  • An older 2020 source says to set the bidding objective to Reach (showing ads to as many people as possible), not to Conversions, for Facebook remarketing.
  • Conversions would show ads only to the few people most likely to buy. That is too small for a remarketing audience.
  • Example: with 1,000 website visitors from the last 30 days, Reach shows the ad to all 1,000.
  • Conversions might show it to only about 50.
  • Use Reach if the audience is small and the goal is to fill it.
  • Use Maximize number of conversions with Purchase when the audience is large enough to learn from purchases.

Ad level (procedure)

2 min
  1. Click New sales ad and rename it, for example Store Name Dynamic Remarketing Ad.
  2. Keep Partnership ad switched off. A partnership ad runs under a partner's page.
  3. Under Identity, confirm the correct Facebook page and Instagram account are linked, so the ads show your business profile.
  4. Set Format to Carousel and keep Multi-advertiser ads on. Multi-advertiser ads is Meta's option to show your ads together with other advertisers' ads.
  5. Leave Show more options at default.
  6. Under Add creative, choose Multiple products and ignore product categories.
  7. Select the linked catalog and set the card display to Single image. This shows one image per catalog item.
  8. Leave Edit creative at default.
  9. Keep Headline on the dynamic product name field. This pulls the product name from the product feed (the file that lists your products).
  10. Product description: leave empty, or click the plus next to ad catalog feed to insert a feed field (a data column) such as price. Delete any field you do not want.
  11. Primary text: enter your text. Meta also generates AI rewrites as variations. Select the ones you like, and they will be tested alongside yours.
  12. Set Call to action to Shop now.
  13. Advantage+ creative: click Edit, toggle to All optimizations, click Save, and confirm it shows 8 out of 8 enhancements on. The default is 6 out of 8.
  14. Destinations (where clicks lead): switch from Manual destination to Advantage destination.
  15. Enter the store URL. Set Display link (the web address shown in the ad) to the domain without https. A domain is your store address, such as yourstore.com.
  16. Leave Override catalog deep links off. Deep links open a specific page inside an app.
  17. Leave Languages off, since ads only show where you do business.
  18. Leave App blank.
  19. Under Tracking, select Website events and choose your main Meta pixel.
  20. Promo codes: optional. Only add a code that will always stay active in the store.
  21. Click Advanced preview and review the placements.
  22. Click Publish.

Status sequence and budget changes

1 min
  1. The campaign shows Processing under Delivery (the column that shows whether ads are running).
  2. It then moves to In review. This can take about 30 minutes or a few hours for the first campaign on the account.
  3. Once approved, it becomes Active. Impressions (times an ad is shown) can take a few days to start on a store with low traffic. They then continue daily as the audience fills.
  • To change the budget later, open the campaign view in Meta Ads Manager (Meta's ad control screen). Click the pencil icon next to the daily budget, enter the new amount and publish.
  • Keep the budget at 10% of the current Google Shopping daily budget.

Why these settings

1 min
  • Manual sales setup is chosen over Advantage+ shopping because it gives precise control over who sees the ads.
  • Advantage+ audience stays on original audience options for remarketing, as described in the campaign-level principles above.

Dynamic product ad settings and CTR

1 min

CTR means click-through rate, the share of viewers who click the ad.

Facebook DPA remarketing

1 min
  • Send visitors back to the exact product page they viewed.
  • Start with a 2-day audience for people who visited the store recently.
  • If the audience is too small, expand to 7, 14 or 28 days.
  • Minimum audience size is 100 people.
  • If fewer than 100 people visit in the window, extend the time window, for example from 2 to 3 days.
  • Daily budget starting point: about 10% of the Google Shopping spend, for example 10 dollars with 100 dollars on Shopping.
  • Until Facebook is consistently profitable, run only dynamic product ad remarketing.
  • Shorter remarketing windows are more profitable.

Audience size and objective

1 min
  • If the dynamic product ad audience is small, optimize for link clicks instead of website conversions. Optimize means telling Meta what result to aim for.
  • This serves ads more often to people who click. That lifts the relevance score (Meta's rating of how well an ad fits its audience) and lowers CPC (cost per click).
  • Split retargeting audiences, for example by add-to-cart versus product view, only when the audience is large enough. Split means divide into separate groups.
  • A list of about 1,000 people is too small to split.
  • A cookie audience (people tracked by a small file stored in their browser) above about 10,000 can support separate campaigns.
  • When building a Facebook dynamic product ad retargeting audience for a Shopify store, enter the audience length in days in the audience settings.
  • For most advertisers, 30 days is a reasonable starting point. Adjust it by product price and sales cycle (the time from first look to purchase).
  • Improve audience duration: a higher product price supports a longer audience, a lower price a shorter one.

Improving DPA CTR (procedure)

1 min
  1. Update dynamic product ads every two weeks.
  2. Improve audience duration: a higher product price supports a longer audience, a lower price a shorter one.
  3. Use dynamic ad text (ad text filled in from product data).
  4. Optimize the product feed.
  5. Use ad overlays (text or graphics placed on top of an image).
  6. Use link clicks as the optimization target.
  • With dynamic product ads, the advertiser has little direct control over CTR. It must be improved indirectly through the product feed, audience and creative settings. Creative means the ad's images and text.

Placements and Google Merchant Center

1 min
  • Google Merchant Center is Google's product data tool. When its dynamic remarketing is disapproved (rejected by Google), use Meta retargeting.
  • Select the Audience Network placement, which is suitable for remarketing. It is not suitable for cold traffic.

Pattern: dynamic product remarketing (pattern)

1 min
  • Show a carousel of the exact products the visitor viewed on the store.
  • The ad text reminds them they have not finished checking out.
  • It offers a percentage-off coupon code.
  • The creative is pulled directly from the site.
  • Example variant: a shopper who viewed three ovens sees those three ovens with a 10% coupon.

where DPA belongs

1 min
  1. In Facebook Business Manager (Meta's business account tool), create custom audiences for website visitors in the past 7, 14 and 30 days.
  2. Create a remarketing campaign.
  3. Inside that campaign, create one ad set per audience and select that audience in each ad set.
  4. Place near-identical ads inside each ad set, changing some of the copy.
  • Use Facebook and Instagram dynamic product ads only for remarketing to past site visitors.
  • Do not use them for cold traffic (new people who have never visited) to the store until you have a proven system for that.

Budget share

1 min

Share of the total budget

1 min
  • Facebook remarketing should get about 10 percent of the total ad budget.
  • Example: if Google Shopping gets $30 per day, Facebook remarketing gets about $3 per day.
  • The daily budget for a Meta remarketing campaign should be 10% of the daily budget of your Google Shopping campaigns.
  • Example: a $50 per day Google Shopping budget means a $5 per day Meta budget.
  • Allocate about 10% of the budget to middle-of-funnel and bottom-of-funnel retargeting.
  • This is especially true once the account has reached about 100 purchases.
  • Keep cold interest targeting as the main strategy. Treat retargeting as a share of the budget, not the whole budget.

Conditions based on return

1 min
  • ROAS (return on ad spend, sales divided by ad cost) below 1x means Facebook retargeting should be reduced or ended. 1x means you earn back exactly what you spend.
  • Above 1x, you can keep retargeting but cap its share of the total budget.
  • For lost visitors (people who left without buying), use a Facebook dynamic product ad campaign for remarketing.
  • Use a small share of the budget, for example about $200 of $300 per month in the 90/10 split. A 90/10 split divides the budget into 90 for new people and 10 for retargeting.
  • The Facebook retargeting spend should not oversaturate people with ads (show them too many). Too many remarketing impressions lower returns.
  • A small budget of $20 to $30 per day, split between Facebook and Google remarketing, is a good way to bring back visitors who already showed interest.
  • Remarketing is the most profitable spend early on.
  • Keep retargeting campaigns to people who interacted with ads small. The example given has a maximum budget of 5 dollars.
  • Run retargeting for every traffic source (where visitors come from, such as Google or Facebook) that engages with the ads.
  • Thank-you video ad (an ad video that thanks the buyer) for new customers only: budget of about 5 dollars per day.
  • Filming and creating the video takes about 1 to 2 hours.
  • Use a simple phone-shot video thanking the customer and restating the shipping time.

How the numbers differ

1 min
  • The share of budget varies by source.
  • About 10% of total budget (2020).
  • About 10% of Google Shopping spend (2022).
  • About 10% of the Google Shopping daily budget (current remarketing setup).
  • About 10% for middle and bottom funnel once the account reaches about 100 purchases.
  • Use the 10% rule as the starting point in every case. Then adjust by ROAS.

Frequency, fatigue and window checks

1 min
  • Check frequency, which is how many times the same person sees your ad on average. When frequency is about five or higher, the same person sees the ad five times, which lowers performance and can lead to negative reports (people flagging the ad as unwanted). Change the creative (the ad itself: image, video or text), the angle (the reason or message behind the ad) or the proof (evidence such as reviews) rather than repeating the ad.
  • Bottom-of-funnel (BOF) retargeting, meaning ads for people close to buying, can have very high frequency, with people seeing the same ad around 10 times, which annoys them. Set frequency caps (limits on how often one person sees an ad) or manage the audience budget to avoid repetition.
  • Buyers often need six to eight contacts with a product before their first purchase, so repeated ad exposure can still turn viewers into buyers, even for products that seem saturated (the market has seen the same ads a lot).
  • For add-to-cart audiences (people who put an item in their basket but did not buy), check results every one to two days, because they can stop delivering sales suddenly. Turn off the audience, let others run, and turn it back on later. Test windows (how many days back the audience looks) of 30, 60, 90 and 180 days.
  • Lookalike audiences (new people who look like your existing customers) and retargeting audiences fill slowly after creation, so a new audience can show low numbers for a while. To test the problem, create a new 30-day website visitor audience (people who visited your site in the last 30 days) and compare its size with the old one.
  • A Meta (Facebook and Instagram) audience created later than the same Google audience shows fewer people, even with the same window, because audiences need time to fill. Give a new audience time before judging its size.

Creative rotation and promotions

1 min
  • For retargeting (showing ads again to people who already showed interest), rotate several different creatives (ads such as reviews, offers and testimonial videos) for the same audience, so people do not tire of the same content. One retargeting audience of around 100k (100,000 people) was mentioned with about 10 creatives added to it.
  • Warm audiences (people who visited or engaged recently) from the last 180 days can see a short carousel (a row of swipeable images) or static ad (a still image) that offers a free bonus product with a mattress or similar main purchase. Bonus-gift angles work well as one of the three to four ads in the static remarketing set (a group of still-image ads shown to the same audience), because they give a reason to buy without a direct discount.
  • Build the thank-you audience (people who just bought) from customers who purchased in the last 7 days, and the catch-all audience (a wide group of all past buyers) from customers in the last 180 days. Both are free to set up in the Facebook account.
  • A Facebook thank-you video ad, shown only to new customers, needs a budget of about 5 dollars per day. Budget is the money you set to spend each day.

Starting and restarting campaigns

1 min
  • Start a small Facebook remarketing campaign (a campaign is one ad project with its own goal and budget) early, but it needs clicks from Google first to have enough visitors to retarget.
  • If a remarketing campaign still does not spend (spend is the money the ads actually use), rebuild it. (Procedure, 2024.)
    1. Leave the existing remarketing campaign running or switch it off later, without changing anything else.
    2. In Meta, create a new remarketing campaign with the same settings as the old one.
    3. Use a 30-day audience window for it (the number of days back the audience looks), since the store's traffic is large enough to fill it.
    4. Check whether the new campaign starts spending. If it still does not spend, rebuild the campaign again.

Placements, page followers and reporting

1 min
  • The Facebook Business Page (your public company page on Facebook) follower list shows counts, not names, and that is normal. Facebook remarketing campaigns for the full product catalog (your complete list of products) should be running.
  • Page and Instagram engagement audiences (people who liked, commented or interacted with your page or account) can reach shoppers Facebook can no longer track on the website since iOS 14 (an Apple phone update that limits tracking). Use a 180-day window.
  • Retargeting based on video watch depth (how much of a video people watched) still works after iOS, though fewer people are trackable.

Store audience setup: summary of settings

1 min
  • Facebook: Audiences > Create custom audience > Website, to build website visitor lists for 7, 30 and 180 days. Use the 7-day and 30-day lists for dynamic remarketing (ads that show each visitor the products they viewed) and the 180-day list for monthly promotions. The 7-day and 30-day lists cost nothing to keep populated, so leave them running.
  • Purchaser audiences from the website Purchase event (a signal the store sends when someone buys): a 7-day list for exclusions (leaving people out of an audience) and a 180-day list for promotions. Once the store is linked to Facebook, purchases send the data automatically.
  • Instagram business profile: Create custom audience > Instagram business profile, select the Instagram account and set engagement criteria over 180 days. Name it for the purpose, for example Instagram engagement 180 days.
  • Engagement audiences: create a Facebook page engagement audience with a 180-day window, choosing the correct Page for each business in Business Manager (Meta's tool for managing pages and ad accounts).

Funnel stages and audience temperature

1 min

The funnel is the path a person takes from first seeing your brand to buying. Audience temperature describes how close people are to buying.

  • Cold audiences have not interacted yet and are reached through prospecting (showing ads to new people who do not know you).
  • Middle-of-funnel (MOF) audiences have engaged with ads, the page or the site, such as likes, comments, post engagement or site visits, without high-intent actions (strong signs they want to buy).
  • Bottom-of-funnel (BOF) audiences have taken high-intent actions such as add to cart, initiate checkout or purchase.

Video view audiences

1 min
  • Build separate video-view custom audiences (lists of people who watched your videos) at the 25%, 75% and 95% watch thresholds (how much of a video they watched), each with its own window.
  • The 25% audience is the usual retargeting base.
  • Viewers at 50% and above are the preferred seeds for lookalikes (the starting group Meta copies to find similar people).
  • Video audiences can be built from people who watched a set share of your videos, such as 95% or 75%, within a chosen number of days. Use this for video ad campaigns.

Website visitor audiences

1 min
  • The website audience option only shows pixel events (signals sent by the tracking code), so the pixel must be connected first. The pixel is a small code on your store that reports visits and purchases to Facebook.
  • For a retargeting audience of website visitors without buyers:
    1. Create a Website audience from pixel events with a 90-day window.
    2. Exclude the purchase audience with the same 90-day window.
  • When creating a website custom audience, the pixel is chosen at the top of the dialog (the pop-up box), and URL rules (rules that match web addresses) only use data from that pixel. Choose the correct pixel first, or the audience will be empty even if the URL rule is right.
  • The dropdown "Include people who meet ANY of the following criteria" controls how several rules combine. ANY includes people who match at least one rule, so check this setting whenever you stack more than one condition (add several conditions together).
  • Create a website custom audience:
    1. In Audiences, click Create a Custom Audience and choose Website.
    2. Under People who visited specific web pages, choose URL and an operator (a comparison word) such as contains, equals or doesn't contain.
    3. For Shopify product pages (Shopify is the store software), enter products as the URL value.
    4. Set the time range, for example the last 7 days, using Further refine by... in the past X days.
    5. Add a second URL condition to narrow the audience, for example product pages that also viewed a content page.
    6. Use Include More People or Exclude People as needed.
    7. Name the audience, optionally add a description, and click Create Audience.

Customer list audiences

1 min
  • Create a customer list audience:
    1. Accept Facebook's terms for customer files.
    2. Either paste the customer details in or upload a CSV file (a spreadsheet saved as plain text with commas between the values).
    3. Use the customer file template exported from Shopify. It does not include lifetime value (the total money one customer spends over time).
    4. Include at least first name, last name and email. Add zip code (postal code) and other fields if you have them, because more matching fields improve results.

Page engagement audiences

1 min
  • Page engagement audiences include people who engaged with your Page, any post or ad, or clicked any call-to-action button (the button that asks people to act). The example uses a window of 365 days.

Audiences overview

1 min
  • In Facebook Ads Manager (Meta's control panel for ads), go to Audiences. There are three options:
    • Create a Custom Audience: from customer list, website traffic, mobile app, Facebook page or video.
    • Create a Lookalike Audience: from page likers, conversion pixels or existing custom audiences.
    • Create a Saved Audience: from demographics (age, gender and place), interests and behaviours.
  • Facebook is said to hold up to about 4,000 data points per user (single facts about a person, such as an age or a like), and lookalike matching draws on that information.

Retargeting campaign timing and structure

1 min
  • Start retargeting only after the ads have run for more than two weeks, so the pixel and ad account (the account that pays for ads) hold enough warm-audience data.
  • ABO (Ad Set Budget Optimization, where the budget is set on each ad set) with one ad set per retargeting audience is the usual structure.
  • CBO (Campaign Budget Optimization, where one budget covers the whole campaign) can be tested as an alternative if it performs better.

Retargeting creative

1 min
  • Retargeting ads work best as UGC-style images (made to look like real customer content) or testimonial videos (customer praise videos) with an added offer such as 10% or 20% off. Warm audiences already know the product and mainly need trust and a reason to buy.

Step-by-step: retargeting traffic campaign with a static image ad

1 min
  1. In Ads Manager, create a new campaign and choose the objective Traffic (send people to a website).
  2. Enter a campaign name.
  3. Turn off Create Split Test (a test that compares two versions) and turn off Campaign Budget Optimization (CBO).
  4. Set the daily budget (test budget: 20 USD).
  5. Set the bid strategy to Lowest cost (Meta aims for the most results for your money). No bid cap (a maximum price you allow per result) or cost cap (an average price limit per result) is used in this setup.
  6. Create the ad set and name it to describe the audience, for example the 7 to 14 day product viewers.
  7. Set the destination to Website. Do not turn on dynamic creative (Meta automatically mixing ad parts), and do not add an offer (a discount or deal attached to the ad).
  8. Under audience, add the custom audience Product Viewers 14 Days and exclude Product Viewers 7 Days (leave those people out). This gives an audience of people who viewed products 7 to 14 days ago.
  9. Set location to the target country, and skip extra demographics (extra age or gender limits).
  10. Keep placements on Automatic (placements are where your ad appears, and Meta chooses them).
  11. Set optimization for ad delivery to Link clicks (clicks that take people to your website).
  12. Leave the schedule running continuously, unless it is a time-limited promotion that needs a start and end date.
  13. Create the ad: choose the Facebook page and the connected Instagram account so the ad can show on Instagram.
  14. Choose a single image or video, and add the media (your image or video file).
  15. Fill in primary text (the main ad text), headline and description, then set the destination website URL, display link (the short web address shown on the ad) and call to action (the button).

Lookalike audience principle

1 min
  • A lookalike audience (a new group of people who share traits with a source group) reaches new people who share characteristics with an existing source audience: customers, purchasers, video viewers, website visitors or engagers (people who interacted).
  • It is created from custom audiences only.
  • It is tested inside a campaign by selecting it at ad-set level.

Creating a lookalike audience

1 min
  • Create it from the Audiences section, or from a custom audience's actions menu (the list of options) by choosing Create lookalike.
  • Choose the source and the countries, for example United States, United Kingdom, Australia or Canada.
  • Select a percentage range: 0-2%, 2-4%, 4-6%, 6-8% or 8-10%. (A smaller percentage means people more similar to the source.) The 5 ranges give audiences of about 3 to 5 million people.

Lookalike source quality

1 min
  • Strong lookalike sources (the groups Meta copies) include 50%+ video viewers (people who watched at least half of a video), 30-day website visitors, Facebook and Instagram engagers, and purchasers.
  • Buyer lookalikes are the highest quality, so 0-1% and 1-2% lookalikes of purchasers are recommended.

Testing lookalikes

1 min
  • Test a lookalike audience in an audience testing campaign by adding it at ad-set level instead of any interest targeting (targeting by hobbies or topics). Winning creatives (the ads that perform best) go inside.
  • Add countries at ad-set level if they were not set in the lookalike. Otherwise the ad set is identical to a normal interest ad set.

Advantage+ Shopping and remarketing measurement

1 min
  • A new Meta Advantage+ Shopping campaign (Meta's automated shopping campaign type) was launched with a starting budget of $20/day and an expected target of more than 10x ROAS (ROAS is revenue divided by ad spend).
  • Updated creative for Meta remarketing ads was measured against a 90-day trailing CTR baseline of 5%, with the goal of raising CTR. CTR (click-through rate) is the share of people who see the ad and click it. A trailing baseline is the average of past results, used as the starting point for comparison.

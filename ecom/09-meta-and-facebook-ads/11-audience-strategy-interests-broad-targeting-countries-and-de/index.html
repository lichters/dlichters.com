Playbook › 9. Meta and Facebook ads

Audience strategy: interests, broad targeting, countries and demographics

Section 9.11 · 11 of 22 in this chapter · 45 min read

In short

  • Start with single-interest tests when your pixel or product is new.
  • Use broad targeting only after you have a warm audience: an email list of 100K+ people and months of pixel data.
  • Without a warm audience, broad ads make each click expensive. With a small budget, interest audiences may be the better start.
  • Check the audience size in Audience Insights before you spend money.
  • Pick interests with big audiences: 10 million or more, ideally 20 to 30 million.
More key points
  • Test one interest per ad set, so you can see which one makes sales.
  • Keep age and gender wide at first. Narrow later, when sales data shows the best group.
  • Check the per-country breakdown. Remove any country that makes the cost per click or view jump up with no sales.
  • Keep gender set to all genders. Narrow it only when the product is clearly for one gender.
  • Test one thing at a time (country, interest, gender or age), with equal budgets.
  • Judge interests by sales, not by clicks or attention. Then scale the winner step by step.
  • After each test, check the gender breakdown in Ads Manager.

Choosing the Audience Type: Broad, Interest, or Single Interest

1 min

A pixel is a small tracking code on your store. It records what visitors do, such as buying. A conversion is a sale or another goal you set. A warm audience means people who already know your store. A cold audience means people who have never visited it.

Audience Research Before Launch

1 min

Facebook Audience Insights

3 min

Facebook Audience Insights is a free Facebook tool. It shows the size, interests, age distribution (how ages are spread) and gender split (share of men and women) of an audience. It is used to segment (split into smaller groups) an audience before running ads. It also shows how much reach each interest combination offers.

  • Facebook Audience Insights is found at facebook.com/ads/audience_insights.
  • It shows the size and common interests of a chosen audience by country, age, gender and interests.
  • Use it even without running ads to learn about the market.
  • It also shows where your customers already spend their attention.
  • Results depend on the location the account appears to be in.
  • A VPN (a tool that changes your apparent location) can give you a different country if it is not set to the target market.
  • So check the location before drawing conclusions from the data.
  • Narrowing the age range too early can hide the real audience.
  • Start with the broad range and let the data show the characteristics.
  • Narrow only after the interest data shows a clear pattern.

Procedure, research an audience in Audience Insights:

  1. Set the location to the target country.
  2. Start broad: keep the age range at 18 to 65, keep all genders, and do not narrow the audience yet.
  3. Type relevant interest keywords into the interests field one at a time. Watch the audience size drop with each addition.
  4. Read the gender split and the age distribution that Facebook shows for the audience.
  5. Add more specific keywords to refine the niche. Then narrow the age range to the bracket that most of the audience falls into.
  6. Use the resulting audience as the seed audience (the starting group) for ads.

Reference numbers:

  • With the location set to the United States and ages 18 to 65, the interest 'home grilling' alone showed about 25 to 30 million people.
  • Each added interest shrinks the audience.
  • Restricting the age range to 18 to 24 dropped it to about 1 to 1.5 million people in one test.
  • Check the gender split at the broad level first. That grilling audience was roughly 42 to 46 percent men.
  • With the United States as location and no interests, the potential audience is about 150 to 250 million people.
  • Adding one large niche brand page narrowed it to about 500,000 to 600,000 people.
  • Adding several related pages raised it to about 700,000 to 800,000 people.

Procedure, research with brand pages:

  1. Open Audience Insights from the gear/tools menu in Facebook Ads Manager (Facebook's tool for running ads).
  2. Enter a precise interest, such as a well-known brand in your niche, in the precise interests field. Watch the potential audience size on the right.
  3. Open the Pages suggestions list at the bottom. It shows Facebook pages ranked by relevance to that brand.
  4. Add each relevant page to the additional interests field. Repeat until you have a large master list of related pages.
  5. Save the audience so you can reuse it when building lead campaigns (campaigns that collect contact details).

Scale check before cold campaigns:

  • Before launching cold Facebook campaigns (ads for people who have never visited your store), check the potential audience size.
  • This matters for a niche product such as running gear.
  • In the example, roughly 33 million people in the United States could be reached through Facebook ads.
  • Lists of pages and interest groups found through Facebook search can be used as targeting interests in Power Editor (an older Facebook ad tool).
  • They can reach new cold audiences that have not yet visited your store.

Market and Customer Research

2 min
  • Define a dream customer (your ideal buyer) before creating ads.
  • Then target that exact group by gender, age and country in each ad set.
  • In the example, the target group was women over 40 in specific countries.
  • Use persona research (a written profile of a typical customer) to choose ad targeting and creative angles (the main message of an ad).
  • The platforms and communities where the audience spends time show where to test ads.
  • These include Reddit, Quora, Facebook groups and Instagram.
  • They also show which language to use.
  • Make a list called Favorite Online Spaces: where your target customers spend time online.
  • Facebook is a common answer.
  • But specialist forums, relevant subreddits with active members (topic groups on Reddit), and Quora for question-based communities often reveal more precise places.
  • LinkedIn or Twitter often reveal more precise places for business audiences.
  • Older advice: Favorite Online Spaces should list where target customers spend time online, as above.

Target people before they become product aware:

  • Product aware means the person already knows and searches for your product type.
  • Target people before they become product aware.
  • The reason: the auction (the bidding for ad space) for people already searching for your product type is crowded and expensive.
  • So look for the earlier interest that leads to your product instead.
  • An Australian health and fitness business targeted people who follow a popular weight-loss TV show. That was roughly 500,000 people in Australia.
  • The common interests Audience Insights showed included celebrity trainers, clean eating recipes, a 12-week transformation program and an activewear brand.
  • These interests were less contested (less competition for the same people).
  • So bids around 39 cents (against a maximum of 19 cents) brought strong sales.

Procedure, chain from one interest to the next:

  1. Start with a broad interest that matches your customer's situation.
  2. Pull the common interests from Audience Insights.
  3. Test these interests with a small budget to see if the audience buys.
  4. If an interest converts, run the same process again from that interest to find the next audience.

Avatar Research from Ad Data

1 min

An avatar is a made-up profile of one typical buyer.

Procedure, ad data analysis for avatar research, in order:

  1. Gender: Check the spend share per gender (how much ad money goes to each).
  2. Check CPM (cost per 1,000 views), CPC (cost per click) and CTR (click-through rate, the share of viewers who click) for each gender.
  3. Check the conversion difference between genders.
  4. Identify the likely primary buyer, and whether a proxy buyer (someone who buys for another person) is involved.
  5. Age: Check spend, performance and conversion by cohort (a group sharing an age range).
  6. Look for under-served cohorts with high value: groups that get little attention but buy well.
  7. Think about likely life-stage explanations for each cohort.
  8. Think about likely distress by cohort.
  9. Time: Look at peak engagement and conversion peaks.
  10. Look at late-night activity as a possible sign of distress.
  11. Compare workday and weekend differences.
  12. Hypotheses: Write a primary avatar and secondary avatars.
  13. Add proxy-buyer signals and distress indicators from timing and engagement.

Proxy buyer add-on:

  • Identify who buys for whom and the relationships involved.
  • Estimate the percentage of buyers who are proxies, using ad data.
  • Build a full avatar for the proxy.
  • Add sections on their relationship to the end user (the person who uses the product).
  • Add sections on how their emotions differ from the direct user.
  • Add sections on what 'saving' the loved one would mean for their own identity.
  • Add sections on the hurtful things they fear hearing about the loved one.

Finding Interests

1 min

What Interests Are

1 min

Interest targeting means choosing people by what they like.

  • Interest targeting is about what the audience likes, follows, shares, comments on and engages with on Instagram and Facebook.
  • The aim is to find interests that lead to engagement (likes, comments, clicks), website visits and purchases.
  • There is no universally right or wrong interest, as long as it matches the target audience.
  • For example, do not target men and football for a makeup store.
  • Interests are based on what people follow on social media, not what they buy.
  • An interest includes everyone who engaged with the topic, including people who dislike it.
  • So interest targeting does not guarantee the right audience.
  • Interest targeting should follow what the ideal customer follows on social media, not only the product itself.
  • The interest does not need to be related to the product, as long as it reaches the right buyers.
  • Find interests where your buyers already spend time.
  • Think of the product, the customer it serves, and the brands, TV shows, magazines or celebrities that customer follows. Use those as interests.
  • A broad interest related to the product or the audience is enough.
  • Deep research is not needed.
  • Look beyond the product category.
  • Think about what else the target audience does: hobbies, parenting, work, brands, astrology, sports, food.
  • Start with obvious interests, then add less obvious ones.
  • Using interests that are only loosely related to the product leads to poor results.
  • Interests should reflect what people actually follow on social media and match the niche.
  • For example, use culturally specific interests for a specific audience.
  • Starting with interest targeting that is too broad, and running it for about a month without results, wastes budget.
  • Narrow the interests to match the audience once the product and offer (your deal, such as price and bonus) are in order.

Non-Obvious Interests That Sold

2 min
  • A shoe store found that its best interests were astrology, Kim Kardashian and makeup. None of them were directly related to shoes.
  • Its target audience was highly active on astrology content. When they saw the shoe ads, they visited the site and bought.
  • A shoe brand running ads in Australia found that its winning interest was astrology.
  • The reason: women over 40 who were interested in the brand were also interested in astrology.
  • The advertiser could scale that interest a lot (spend much more on it).
  • A non-obvious interest linked to the actual buyer can outperform the interests that look most relevant.
  • For women over 45 in Australia, the research surfaced health, fashion, spirituality and astrology as interests.
  • Astrology performed best for a shoe product.
  • For men's grooming products in Germany, football clubs such as FC Bayern and Dortmund worked as interests.
  • Interests that performed well for an Australian fashion store targeting women over 45 included discounts, Black Friday and astrology.
  • Very broad celebrity interests, such as famous singers, reached large audiences.
  • Those audiences did not buy a niche jewelry product.
  • Interests tied to the community the product serves produced results.
  • For example: a culture-specific style interest combined with a demographic filter for that community.
  • A travel accessory product tested broad first. Then it switched to an outdoor and camping audience.
  • The landing page (the page people arrive on after clicking) and creatives were changed to show the product in mountains and by the sea. Results improved.
  • For a floor mat product, the interest search used cleaning terms: 'Clean house' and 'Carpet cleaning'.
  • 'Carpet cleaning' had an audience of about 13 million.
  • Further ad sets then used other broad floor-related interests: 'Carpet dust mop', 'Mop' and 'Floor'.
  • Use one interest per ad set.
  • For a kids product, use interests such as kids, family, fatherhood, motherhood and parenting.
  • Unrelated interests, such as magazines, TV programs or cartoons, can also work. They are worth testing.
  • Problem-solving household and washroom products can work on Facebook with audiences aged about 25 to 30 and older.
  • They work because they recognize the everyday problem right away.
  • Target interests similar to the problem being solved, such as the game, the platform or related hobbies.
  • Match the audience age and gender to the buyer profile.

Interest Types by Affinity

1 min

Affinity means how strongly people connect with a topic.

  • For a higher-affinity audience, look for interests tied to big brands.
  • Also look for influencers that are still available as interests, and TV shows that match the product.
  • These show the user has a real connection with the product.
  • That is different from a single click on an interest.
  • Engaged shopper audiences (people who often buy online) have higher purchase intent (how likely they are to buy).
  • But they also raise CPM, because many brands target the same group.
  • Interests where other brands and dropshippers (sellers who ship products without holding stock) already convert have high CPMs.
  • The reason is competition.
  • Low-intent audiences such as engagement or link clicks have low CPMs.
  • But they rarely sell.
  • Start with the high-converting interests, even though they cost more per impression (one view of an ad).
  • Do not exclude dropshipper or AliExpress interests.
  • The exclusion adds a filter that raises CPM and shrinks the audience. In the example, it went from 100 million to 68 million.
  • It also made the ads more visible to spy tools (tools that show what other sellers advertise).
  • If audience filters like engaged shoppers perform poorly, switch to broad audiences.
  • Keep a main broad audience running alongside any interest audience.
  • Each extra filter on Facebook also increases the cost per impression.

Procedure: Finding the Next Interest

2 min

These steps combine four sources that describe the same workflow. Keep the ad set name in sync with the interest.

  1. Open the ad set that already has a working interest, or duplicate the ad set that is working.
  2. In the ad set's detailed targeting, search a broad term such as home decor, or open the interest section of the ad set.
  3. Click 'Suggestions' to get interest ideas from Meta. Under the working interest, choose one of the related interests Meta lists.
  4. Select one suggested interest and copy its exact name.
  5. Replace the interest in the copied ad set, or add it.
  6. Paste the name at the top of the ad set name. This way you always know which interest each ad set contains.
  7. Rename the ad set with the new interest name before publishing.
  8. Repeat with other interests, including adjacent themes such as flowers or bonsai for a home decor store.
  9. Click the cross (X) in the top left, and publish.
  10. Keep the first ad set Broad.
  11. Repeat with a different interest for each additional ad set.
  12. Repeat until you have 12 to 15 tested interests.

Additional rules:

  • Test several interests for the same product, even with a good CTR.
  • Some interests bring visitors who never buy.
  • Others convert.
  • Keep only the interests that produce sales.
  • Test many interests and ad variants (different versions of the same ad).
  • The goal is a lower CPA (cost per acquisition, the cost to win one sale) and a higher margin (profit left after costs).
  • Some interests bring strong results while others barely cover costs. So each one needs its own test.
  • Use Peeksta (peeksta.com, a tool that finds interests) to find niche-specific interests for single-interest targeting.
  • Interest choices matter even when platforms push broad.
  • In one test, a brand-specific interest, such as a snowboard brand audience, consistently beat broad targeting.
  • A pets interest performed better than a narrower dog-owner interest.
  • Test several interest levels before choosing one.
  • A high attention rate (how many people pay attention to your ad) does not show that an audience is buying.
  • In one test with two interests, the home and garden interest had a poor attention rate but produced the sales.
  • A PC-focused interest had about 40 percent attention and no add-to-carts (clicks on 'add to basket').
  • Judge interests by sales.
  • Interests can still be used with broad-level choices, such as 'cats' or 'pets' for a cat toy.
  • They matter less than before.
  • Facebook builds a profile of the audience from the ad creative (images and video), the copy (the written words) and who responds to it.
  • Naming the target audience in the copy helps the system find the right people.
  • For example, name an architect using a specific piece of software.

Discovering Interests from Adjacent Products

2 min

Adjacent means nearby or related.

  • For a pet product, a single-interest test ad set can target a broad interest such as 'I love dogs'.
  • Each test ad set then uses a different interest of the same kind.
  • Each one has an audience of at least 5M.
  • Interest targeting for a female fashion test uses these settings:
    • Gender = female.
    • Age = 40+ (the search uses 40 to 65+).
    • Country = one or more target countries.
  • Use the same age and gender settings as the research search. This way the test matches the product's proven audience.
  • Early tests used women 40+ as the audience.
  • Younger audiences were excluded when data for them looked weak.
  • When scaling, the winning group was narrowed to women 45+ interested in astrology in two cities.
  • That audience produced most of the sales.
  • For a fishing niche outside the usual women-focused audience, the test audience was set to men over 30.
  • Testing a demographic that differs from your core customer is a deliberate step. Do it when a niche shows strong results elsewhere.
  • Interests for a gendered product, such as men's jewelry, are needed when the seller has no prior success with that audience.
  • For those, see the procedure in the Gender section.
  • Postpartum audiences (mothers in the months after childbirth) suit hair loss products.
  • Targeting parents zero to 12 months after childbirth with a motherhood interest performed well.
  • Seasonal holiday audiences work only briefly.
  • Facebook's audience estimate for a new parents interest in Canada was about 30,000 to 40,000 people.
  • That size is too small for some products.
  • The same audience performed well for a hair product.
  • Small interest audiences can produce a few early sales and then stop.
  • One possible reason: the platform exhausts the easiest buyers first.
  • This is one possible explanation. The ad account data should decide.
  • Target the top-level interest related to the product.
  • Find where the buyer lives online and what they follow. Then move from broad to specific.
  • On Facebook, you can target people travelling between two countries for an event.
  • Open Facebook Ads detailed targeting and search for a travel behavior filter, such as people who travel from one country to another.
  • Another option is the filter for people who recently moved from the origin country to the destination country.
  • If neither returns a usable audience, a custom audience has to be built.
  • Instagram boosts (paid promotions of a post) can target a defined audience.
  • For example: women aged 25 to 55 interested in luxury products, tea and organic goods.
  • The aim is to find likely buyers, not to gather followers.

Stated Size Is Not the Active Audience

  • An interest that says 30 million does not show ads to all 30 million. With a purchase goal, the platform picks a much smaller group who showed buying behaviour. This small group is the "pocket" (the part of the audience that actually sees your ad). The active pocket is far smaller than the stated size.
  • Different interests can reach different pockets, and the pockets can overlap (the same people sit in both). Meta offers an audience overlap check (a tool that shows how many people two audiences share).

Testing Single Interests

3 min

Structure

This is the first test layout for a new Facebook campaign with a limited budget. A campaign is the top group. An ad set is a group inside it with its own targeting, budget and settings. An ad set holds one or more ads.

  1. Start with five ad sets, each with one interest.
  2. Or start with four interest ad sets and one broad ad set. Broad means no interests, only country and age.
  3. After sales come in and the store converts well, add more interests or ad sets.
  • Start interest testing with four or five interests, so the budget is spent wisely at the start. Add more interests only when the store has sales and a good conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy).
  • A first interest test usually covers about 5 to 10 interests. Often 2 to 3 or more winners come out of it. Keep adding new interests after that. This is called horizontal scaling: adding new interests side by side. The goal is a steady stream of new winning groups, not just the first winners.
  • The five ad sets compete with each other. Use one broad, related interest per ad set.

Launch settings for a new interest test:

  • Set the launch time to 12:00 AM (midnight) of the next day.
  • Use a starting budget of about $10.
  • Name the ad set after its interest.
  • When testing new interests, duplicate (copy) an existing ad set and change only its targeting.
  • For a test ad set in Switzerland, set location to Switzerland only, minimum age 25+, and language German when the ads are in German. Start with a Broad ad set without interests. Then add one interest per ad set.
  • Use one ad per ad set during testing. In Facebook testing, use a single creative per ad set. A creative is the image, video and text of an ad.

One Interest per Ad Set

  • Each testing ad set should hold exactly one interest. Then you can trace its results back to that one interest. Several interests in one ad set blur which audience responds. Keep the other settings identical, so only the interest differs.
  • Test one interest per ad set. Do not stack several interests. "Stacking" means putting several interests into one ad set. With stacked interests you cannot tell which one made the sale. Meanwhile, Meta keeps spending on the weaker ones.
  • Adding several interests to one testing ad set makes it impossible to tell which interest produced the purchases.
  • Stacking is not required. Test each interest in its own ad set first. Later, you can optionally combine winners into one ad set to see which setup performs better. There is no fixed right answer.
  • Meta has moved away from narrow stacked interests and now pushes broad targeting. Choose one interest per ad set only if that interest gives a better return on ad spend (ROAS, the money made per money spent) than broad targeting.
  • Keep creatives that perform acceptably across ad sets. Drop only the ones that perform badly, in a consistent way.

Test Design Examples

  • For a new store or new product test, test different ad copies and different interest audiences. The winning audience is found through testing. Specific interests often beat broad targeting. Here, "ad copy" means the main text of the Facebook ad, not the text on your website.
  • Test a new product with an interest test. Then use the interest that works best for scaling (growing spend on what works).

Broad Targeting and Creative as Targeting

3 min

Broad Is Valid, With Conditions

  • Facebook's algorithm can find the right buyers for a broad campaign on its own, if the creative is strong. So broad targeting is a valid start. Country is the main control. You learn the audience from the data the ads create.
  • Meta recommends broad targeting. In this case, the creative itself should identify the buyers. Once the creative does this well, you can go fully broad, with only a country selected.
  • Test broad targeting. Do not assume it works.
  • Facebook's broad targeting keeps improving, and interest targeting may be phased out. The relevance of the ad and creative matters more than fine interest selection. Even when platforms push broad targeting, a relevant narrow test can still win.
  • Facebook keeps removing detailed interest options and pushes toward broad targeting. So the relevance of the creative and landing page (the page a buyer arrives on) matters more. In one store, uploading a customer list into an Advantage+ campaign still gave broad delivery that did worse than the niche approach.
  • Once an interest or niche makes sales, narrow the creative and targeting to that niche. A catch-all ad is acceptable when the budget is low and the problem is common to many people.
  • After Apple's 2023 iOS update, tracking became weaker. Broad targeting with expanded audiences became the norm, and the creative now does most of the targeting. Meta expanded audience sizes by turning on detailed targeting in most accounts. Bigger audiences give the pixel (a tracking code on your store that reports sales to Meta) more data to optimise with.
  • Since the iOS 14 update, interest targeting has lost much of its precision. Meta pushes advertisers toward broad targeting. Broad targeting works best when the pixel has a lot of sales data.

Conflict: one source dates the tracking change to the 2023 iOS update. Another dates it to the iOS 14 update. Both describe the same shift toward broad targeting.

Creative Does the Targeting

  • The strongest long-term strategy is a creative that sells well on its own inside broad targeting. This holds no matter the learning phase (the first days when Meta tests and learns who responds). An ad that hits and sells is more important than tuning the algorithm.
  • The creative now does much of the targeting. The people, text and problem shown in an image or video tell Meta who to show it to. For example, an image of a person over 50 with the text "foot pain" pushes the ad toward that audience.
  • Facebook builds a profile of the audience from the ad creative, the copy and who responds to it.
  • Keep producing new creatives with different hooks (the first seconds or line that stops the scroll), angles and stories.
  • Keep the ad text, video and visuals relevant to the chosen interest. The algorithm reads them to decide who sees the ad.
  • Broad Advantage+ campaigns (Meta's AI-led campaign type) depend on ad quality, because Meta chooses the audience itself. Meta is moving toward broad targeting without interests. So creative quality becomes the main lever.
  • Since 2026, changing only the hook or primary text of a duplicated video ad no longer reaches a different audience. To reach several audiences, you need several distinct concepts. Viewers respond differently to short videos (around 30 seconds) and long videos (around 15 minutes).
  • Meta's Andromeda delivery system (Meta's system that picks who sees each ad) is described as LLM-based. An LLM is a large language model, the kind of AI behind chat tools. Andromeda shows ads to users at different awareness levels. So a campaign needs messages for several awareness stages, not one format repeated.
  • Running the same creatives across many accounts or sellers raises the risk that Meta flags your business.

Advantage+ Audience and Audience Controls

1 min
  • Advantage+ audience lets Meta's AI find people for the ad. Optional audience suggestions steer it toward people you expect to respond. With no suggestion, the AI searches on its own.
  • Ads Manager's estimated audience size (Meta's ad control panel) does not include Advantage+ audience options.
  • Under Suggest an audience, Meta can reach people beyond your audience controls when it expects better results. The suggestion field is optional. Leave it empty if you want strict targeting.
  • Use Advantage+ audience with broad targeting for the standard setup. Keep the default age range of 18 to 65+ and all genders, unless there is a very specific reason to restrict them.
  • Ad set > Audience > Audience definition: keep it on Broad. "Narrow" and "Further limit the reach of your ads" are not recommended. Normally, only the location and audience exclusions (people you block from seeing the ad) should be changed.
  • Ad set > Audience > Languages: a language restriction is available if ads should run in only one language. Leave it at the default for a standard setup.
  • In a product test, keep the ad set audience broad. Do not add detailed targeting or other audience controls. Restrict audience settings only for legal or practical reasons.
  • For worldwide targeting, Facebook can search wider than your strongest markets. See the Countries section below.
  • When testing several countries at once, break the data down by country. If one country pushes CPM and CPC (cost per click) up sharply, such as the US, remove that country from targeting.

Country Testing Workflow

Country testing workflow for a new product:

  1. Start with the Big Five markets: USA, Australia, New Zealand, Canada and UK.
  2. After a couple of days, break down the results by country.
  3. Remove countries with high CPM and no sales.
  4. Keep scaling the countries that work.

Procedure, one ad set per country, for a clean comparison:

  1. Create a separate ad set for each country you want to test.
  2. Use the same ad and the same copy in every ad set.
  3. Keep the ad sets in the same campaign and give each the same budget.
  4. Compare results per country after enough spend. Then the test shows which country responds best.
  • Targeting several countries in one ad set lets Facebook send most of the budget to the country that engages first. In one test, about 90% of the reach went to the UK. Australia and New Zealand got almost no spend.
  • Meta delivery optimises toward your chosen conversion goal. It moves spend to the ad set or country it predicts will convert best. So a multi-country ad set can send nearly all the budget to one country.
  • If your goal is to compare countries, rather than let the system choose, put each country in its own ad set. Use the same ad, the same copy and an equal budget.
  • Conflict: one ad set with several countries lets Meta choose the market, and spend will concentrate there. Separate ad sets per country are needed to compare countries cleanly.
  • When running several countries in one campaign, keep them in one campaign. Break the data down by country to find the best performers. Do not run a separate campaign per country.
  • When running several countries in one campaign, read the results by country in reporting, so each market can be judged on its own. To put the full budget behind one market, target only that country.
  • In a test, only the variable you are testing should differ between ad sets. Unequal budgets or different creatives make the comparison of countries or audiences invalid.
  • If a product sells in several countries, test it in several. Find the one where it converts best. Then focus the budget there.
  • If CPM is high in one country but sales still come from it, remove that country from the ad targeting and keep the others. Compare the country breakdown of your ad account to find the expensive market.
  • If one country pushes CPM and CPC up sharply, such as the US, remove that country from targeting.

Testing Outside the Main Market

  • Launch new ad sets that target countries outside your main market. For example, the UK, Canada and Australia, without the USA. This tests whether a mix of countries improves results. Test this in parallel. Do not cut existing ads to make room.
  • German-speaking neighbours such as Switzerland and Austria are a logical next step after a German market performs well. The German-language store and creatives can be reused there. Test them as separate markets with their own spend. Do not assume the German results carry over.
  • For very small countries, remove as much targeting as possible and go broad. Narrow targeting limits the audience. For larger markets, such as the US, Australia, Germany and other top-tier countries, specific interests can still help find relevant buyers.
  • When CPC or CPM is too high, the fix is a different, broader target audience, not one narrow audience. For example, expand from only the United States to a group of top-five English-speaking countries, such as the UK, New Zealand and Canada.

Worldwide Targeting

  • For worldwide targeting, keep the strongest English-speaking countries in the audience. Let Facebook search wider. Exclude countries with low buying power or high scam risk (many fake buyers or fake orders). These are Russia (hit or miss), India (scammy) and Brazil (scammy traffic). Include the top five English-speaking countries.

Country Data Example

  • On Facebook, mobile converted best among devices, followed by tablet, then desktop. In one example, desktop was three times as expensive per click and per lead (a person who gives their contact details) as mobile. Overall, it was roughly nine times as expensive. iOS converted higher than Android.

Geo-Targeting and Locations

1 min

Geo-targeting means choosing places for your ads.

  • Geo-targeting in Meta ads can target zip codes (postal codes), whole states, or a radius around a city. It does not have to be a whole country. This lets you limit ads to customers near a supplier's warehouse.
  • In the US, Facebook ads can target people by income, household size, political party and the last car they test drove. Geo-targeting can also reach people by their location in real time. This is useful for higher-priced offers. It is not available in every country.
  • In the Locations field, typing a country name returns a list of matching countries, regions, cities, states and addresses. You must select each suggestion from the list.
  • The Include dropdown adds the location to the audience. Browse or bulk entry can add several locations at once.
  • In the ad set's Locations field, add each target country with the Include control. Under the field, Meta shows the default option "People living in or recently in this location". Leave it as it is.

Language

1 min
  • Choose all languages or English as the language setting, unless your landing page and ads are in another language. If the store is in German, select Germany together with the German language.
  • Run ads in the language of the target market, for example German for a German-speaking audience. English ads weaken trust with local buyers.
  • If a product's language version is only English, run English ads. Match the ad language to the language of the product or game in each country.
  • Ad set > Audience > Languages: a language restriction is available if ads should run only in one language. Leave it at the default for a standard setup.
  • For a test ad set in Switzerland, set language to German when the ads are in German. Switzerland has German, French and Italian parts.

Gender

2 min
  • Keep gender set to all genders. Do not narrow it, because Meta (the company behind Facebook and Instagram) optimises (automatically adjusts who sees the ad to get the best results).
  • Narrow gender targeting (choosing who sees the ad) only when the creative (the ad's image, video and text) and the product are clearly for one gender, such as a men's grooming kit.
  • In Ads Manager (Meta's tool for running ads), open the breakdown menu (a menu that splits results into groups).
  • Select Breakdown by gender.
  • Check where budget (the money you spend on ads) and sales go.
  • A single gender often takes 70 to 90 percent of the budget, even with open targeting.
  • Other breakdowns in the same menu are By time and By delivery.
  • If the product is not clearly gender-specific, compare open targeting (no gender limit) with a single-gender split test (a test that compares two setups side by side, here one gender only).
  • Run 10 tests as an example of how many to compare.
  • Gifts can cross genders.
  • So the data decides which gender converts (makes a purchase).
  • Default gender in the standard setup is all genders.
  • Test a product made for one gender (a gendered product), such as men's jewelry, when you have no earlier sales with that audience:
  1. Start with broad interest targeting (a wide group chosen by general interests).
  2. Confirm the CTR (click-through rate: the share of people who see the ad and click it) is good. Also confirm the target gender engages (likes, comments or clicks) with the ad.
  3. Test interests that the target group typically follows, such as cars or sports, adjusted to each country.
  4. Run multiple countries in the same campaign (one group of ad sets with one goal). Then break the data down by country to see which performs best.
  • In Audience Insights (Meta's tool that shows who your audience is), check the gender split (the share of men and women) at the broad level first, before narrowing.
  • A grilling (barbecue) audience was roughly 42 to 46 percent men.

Keeping Variables Isolated

1 min
  • A variable is the one thing you change in a test.
  • In a test, only the variable under test should differ between ad sets (groups of ads with one audience and one budget).
  • Unequal budgets or different creatives make the comparison of countries or audiences invalid.
  • Each testing ad set should hold exactly one interest.
  • The other settings stay identical, so only the interest differs.
  • Test a single variable per run (one round of testing): country, interest, gender or age.
  • For a country test, use the same ad and copy (the ad text) in each ad set.
  • For a country test, give each ad set equal budgets.
  • When testing several interests with the same creative, keep the creative identical across ad sets where the test is about audience.
  • Keep creatives that perform acceptably across ad sets.
  • Drop only those that perform badly in a consistent way.
  • Test different ad copies and different interest audiences, because the winning audience is found through testing.
  • Keep ad set settings identical except the interest.
  • Duplicate an existing ad set (make a copy of it) and change only its targeting.
  • Single-gender split tests are run against open targeting, so the gender variable is the one under test.

Scaling Winning Audiences

1 min
  • Scaling means spending more on what already works.
  • Once an interest or niche (a small, specific group of buyers) produces sales, narrow the creative and targeting to that niche.
  • Interests that convert are used for scaling.
  • The interest that works best is used for scaling.
  • When scaling, the winning group can be narrowed.
  • In one case, the group was narrowed to women 45+ (45 and older) interested in astrology in two cities.
  • That audience produced most of the sales.
  • Targeting that works should be expanded horizontally (sideways, into new groups).
  • Keep adding new interests after the first winners, because the goal is a steady stream of new winning pockets (small groups that buy).
  • Scale the number of interests only once the store has sales and a good conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy).
  • Scale the winning audience by keeping the winners at the same ad settings.
  • Expand the number of interests only once sales are steady.
  • Interest choice is judged by sales, not by attention.
  • A small, narrow audience can produce a few early sales and then stop.
  • Check the ad account data to decide.
  • Broad targeting underperforms (does worse than expected) on some products.
  • If broad underperforms, move to broad interest audiences before falling back to no-interest targeting (choosing no interests at all).

Audience Size

1 min
  • For products that are not very specific or niche, use broad targeting with audiences of at least about 5 million people.
  • This applies especially when targeting top five or e-packet countries.
  • Let strong creatives define the audience through engagement data (likes, comments, clicks) and pixels (tracking code on your website that records visits and buys).

Creative as Targeting

1 min
  • Facebook builds a profile of who responds to an ad from its copy and creative.
  • A niche message (a message aimed at one small group), such as a specific profession with the problem named in its language, lets the algorithm find that audience faster.
  • The same kind of message makes the ad resonate (feel right to the reader) more.
  • When sales already come in and a niche is working, narrow the creative to that niche.
  • For example, use nurses or snowboarders in parks.
  • Catch-all ads are acceptable when budget is low and the problem is common to many groups.

Finding Interests

1 min
  • Facebook interests are determined by what people like and follow on their social media.
  • They are not determined by what people say about themselves.
  • An unrelated interest such as astrology can produce the best results if that is what the buyers follow.
  • To find new interests similar to a winner, open the ad set.
  • Scroll to Detailed targeting and click Suggestions.
  • Pick related interests with a good audience size.
  • For example, use a category interest such as home appliances for a cleaning product.
  • Look for interests that match the customer's life, not only the product.
  • Examples include family, hobbies or astrology, so the audience reflects who buys the product.
  • A post-purchase survey (a short questionnaire sent after the sale) can ask why the customer bought.
  • It can also ask what they plan to use the product for.
  • The answers can refine interest targeting for Facebook ads.

Interest Testing Process

1 min
  1. Find out who has already bought, including age, gender and country.
  2. List possible interests from their everyday life, such as hobbies, food, parenting, health, TV series and streaming shows.
  3. Test many different interests in parallel (at the same time), within the daily ad budget.
  4. Keep the interest that brings sales and scale it over a longer period.
  • Do not test too many interests at the same time.
  • The number that can be tested depends on the daily ad budget.
  • Running ten interests with a small budget gives too little data per interest.
  • For gift products, the person who pays is often not the person who uses the product.
  • Test interests that match the likely gift buyer's own life, such as what they follow and do.
  • Do not test only the recipient's profile (the person who gets the gift).
  • For a jewelry store, interests built around the product itself produced weak results.
  • Interests built around occasions, such as jewelry, anniversaries and relationship status, also produced weak results.
  • Fashion accessories worked better.
  • A fitting (suitable) interest was found only by testing many options.
  • Do not assume that buyers follow an interest just because it matches the product or occasion.
  • For a known buyer profile (a description of who buys) of UK women over 35, possible interests to test include parenting, motherhood, cooking, health and work and career topics.
  • Possible interests also include popular UK TV programmes or Netflix shows.
  • Use such a list as a starting set.
  • Keep only the interests that bring sales.

Country Selection

1 min
  • For a clean country test (a test with only one changed thing, the country), place each country in its own ad set.
  • Use the same ad, copy and campaign for each.
  • Control the spend per ad set with equal budgets.
  • Putting several countries in one ad set lets Facebook pick the winner by engagement.
  • It can also starve smaller markets (give them almost no ads).
  • When countries share one ad set, Facebook can send 90% of the spend to one country.
  • In that example the country was the UK.
  • Australia and New Zealand were left with almost nothing.

Keeping Variables Isolated

1 min
  • In a valid test, all variables must stay equal except the one being tested.
  • Unequal spend between two countries, such as 2 versus 5 per day, distorts (makes unfair) the comparison.
  • When you duplicate a winning interest into a new ad set to test a different interest, delete the older interest from the duplicate.
  • So the test isolates the new interest, and each test runs on its own.
  • This keeps the results of each test clean.

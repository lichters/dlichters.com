Playbook › 9. Meta and Facebook ads

Business manager, page, Instagram and ad account setup

Section 9.2 · 2 of 22 in this chapter · 27 min read

In short

  • Set up in this order before any ad: a Business Manager (Meta's admin panel for your business), an ad account, a Facebook Page, your store with its own web address, then Instagram.
  • Keep each business in its own Business Manager and ad account. Do not mix stores.
  • Use a payment card that works. A declined card stops all ads.
  • Add a second trusted person as admin, so you can still get in if your personal profile is lost.
  • Wait about 6 hours after you get a Meta invitation link before you join.
More key points
  • If you do not know the business on the invitation, decline it. Accepting adds you to its portfolio.
  • Prove you own your website with one DNS TXT record. You only need to do this once per domain.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication, a second login check such as a code on your phone.
  • Ad tax rules depend on the country, so check them with a tax professional.

Prerequisites and account structure

3 min

Five things must be ready before you run Meta ads. Meta is the company behind Facebook and Instagram.

  1. A warmed-up personal Facebook profile, meaning a profile that already has normal use. It shows Meta a real person, not a bot (an automatic program).
  2. A Business Manager, which is Meta's admin panel for your business. It is the control centre for your ads.
  3. An ad account inside the Business Manager. The ads run here.
  4. A finished Shopify store with its own domain. Shopify is the software that runs your online shop. The domain is your web address. The ads send people there.
  5. A warmed-up Facebook Page, which is the face of the brand. It makes people trust it more.

Incomplete setup causes most failed launches, not the ad itself.

Beginners who skip the prerequisites and backend setup (the work behind the scenes) often see:

  • An ad that does not spend, meaning it does not use its budget.
  • No data in reports.
  • Errors in the account.

Meta's ad algorithm (the system that picks who sees your ad) can find suitable buyers consistently. It needs a pixel. A pixel is a small tracking code on your store that sends purchase data to Meta.

Set up the account cleanly from the start. This avoids problems later.

Meta's setup order:

  1. Create the Facebook ad account.
  2. Create the Facebook page.
  3. Set up the pixel. This means: connect the domain, verify the domain (prove you own it), integrate the Shopify conversion API (a second way to send sales data from Shopify to Meta), and turn on the data switches (the settings that turn tracking on).
  4. Connect Facebook to Shopify.
  5. Connect Instagram and fill it with images.

A 2024 procedure for the store's account starts with the same core steps:

  1. Create the Business Manager account separately from your personal Facebook account.
  2. Create the ad account inside Business Manager.
  3. Create the store's Facebook page inside Business Manager, with you as admin (the person who controls it).
  4. If the account gets restricted (Meta limits it), contact support through facebook.com/business/help and choose Get support.

Creating the Business Manager

1 min

A Business Manager is free to create at business.facebook.com. **

Meta's Business Manager is needed to run ads. **

Creating a Business Manager for a new store (Source C method):

  1. Go to business.facebook.com while logged in with your personal Facebook account.
  2. Click Create an account.
  3. Enter the business name. Use your registered business name (the name on your official papers). If you have none, use your Shopify store name.
  4. Enter your name and a business email address. A regular email you check often is acceptable. Avoid using only the Shopify email.
  5. Click Submit to create the Business Manager.

Creating a Business Manager for a new store (Source A method):

  1. Open business.facebook.com in a new tab or browser.
  2. If you have no Business Manager yet, click Create a new account. If you already have one, open the account dropdown (a menu that opens from a list) in the left navigation. Click Create at the top of the list to make a new one for this business.
  3. Name the Business Manager after your store. Enter your business email, preferably the same email as your Google account.
  4. Log in. The top shows your business name. The middle section is empty until you add assets (things like pages and ad accounts).

Naming and email rules:

  • Name the Business Manager after the store.
  • Enter a business email address when creating it.
  • Use the same email as your Google account, so access stays consistent across platforms.

Do not submit a second Business Manager for the same business. If Meta reports that your business may already have one, continue with the existing account. Each business should have only one.

Business Manager limits

1 min

The number of ad accounts a Business Manager allows goes up with successful payment transactions. The sequence is 1, then 2, then 5, 7, 8 and finally 12 accounts.

If the Business Manager limit blocks creating another ad account, open a new Business Manager at business.facebook.com/create.

Before creating a new ad account, check the current ad account limit in Business Settings:

  • If the limit is not reached, create the new ad account there and add payment details.
  • If the limit is reached, open a new Business Manager at business.facebook.com/create.

By default, Facebook allows only one Business Manager per personal account. To create one, use business.facebook.com/create. If it is blocked, check the business details in Business settings and complete them. The account should look like a real business.

Business info also contains an Ad account creation limit field. It caps how many ad accounts the portfolio (the set of your business assets) can create. Check this value before planning to add more ad accounts under the same business. [L651.022]

Domain verification

1 min

Why verify

1 min

Domain verification shows Meta that you own the website that sends traffic and conversion data. Conversion data records purchases and sign-ups from your site. Data is shared between your site and Meta. This ownership check lets that data pass through.

A domain only needs to be verified once. The example account had several verified domains because it ran ads through several domains. An agency model would add domains differently.

Meta offers three domain verification options. The example used DNS verification. DNS is the system that tells the internet where your domain points. This option gives you a TXT record value. A TXT record is a short text note added to your domain's DNS settings. It must be added to the domain's DNS configuration.

Procedure

1 min
  1. Expand Brand safety in Business settings and click Domains.
  2. Click Add, choose Create a new domain, type the store URL starting with https, and click Add.
  3. Choose the DNS verification option and copy the text value shown.
  4. Open the domain settings of your store.
    • For a domain bought through Shopify, go to Sales channels > Online store > Domains.
    • Otherwise, go to the site where the domain is hosted.
    • Select the main domain, not the myshopify.com address. Myshopify.com is Shopify's default store address.
    • Then click Manage and open DNS settings.
  5. Click Add custom record, choose TXT, leave the Name as @, paste the copied value into the text value field and click Confirm. The @ symbol means the main domain.
  6. Return to Business Manager. Make sure you are still in the DNS verification section, and click Verify. If it shows a mismatch, wait a few minutes and try again.
  7. After it verifies, refresh the page to confirm the domain shows as verified.

Common errors and timing

1 min
  • Adding the DNS TXT record in the wrong place is a common error. Enter the main domain, not the myshopify.com address, or the record will not be found.
  • After adding the record, go back to the DNS verification option before clicking Verify. Verifying from the meta tag section fails. A meta tag is a hidden line of code in a website's header.
  • After adding a DNS TXT record, Meta may report a mismatch on the first Verify attempt.
  • Meta's documentation says DNS changes can take up to 72 hours to spread across the internet. In practice, it can take only a few minutes. In one setup, verification went through after about 2 minutes.

  • Before you create ads in Ads Manager, a notice asks you to review Meta's non-discrimination policy and click I accept. The policy prohibits ads that deny opportunities to individuals or groups based on protected characteristics such as race, religion, age, sex or disability.
  • Ads Manager is where campaigns, ad sets and ads are created and reviewed. A new ad account shows no campaigns and the message that no ads have been created yet, with a Create button to start.
  • When you open an invitation link while logged out, the Meta business login page offers Log in with Facebook, Log in with Instagram, Create new account, and Log in with a managed Meta account. Use the Facebook option to join a business portfolio with a personal Facebook profile.
  • In Business settings > Business options, the Pages and notifications toggle decides whether people in the portfolio see Page notifications on Facebook as well as in business tools. When it is off, notifications appear only in business tools. A separate Business notifications on Facebook toggle controls notifications about the portfolio's Pages on Facebook.
  • In Business settings > Business info, the Account section has an Ad account creation limit field. It caps how many ad accounts the portfolio can create. Check this value before you plan to add more ad accounts under the same business.
  • In Business settings > Ad accounts, each ad account row offers Assign people, Create ad, Assign partner and Open in Ads Manager. When you select an ad account, its ad account ID and owning business appear. Connected assets on that screen list the Pages and other assets linked to the ad account.
  • Full control on a Facebook Page means everything except sensitive actions. A person with Full control can manage the Page but cannot perform the restricted sensitive actions.
  • Full control on an ad account is broader. It includes ad account settings, finances and permissions. Assign ad account access with this difference in mind.
  • The "Finish setting up your Page" checklist on a new Facebook Page lists these sections: Category, Personal details, Details, Links, Services, Bio, About you, Pinned details, Book, Offers, Work and Education. Filling them in makes the Page look complete to visitors. Start with Category and Links, since those were required or are used most in the setup.
  • Use ad accounts in USD if possible. Meta charges at the same rate for each currency, but USD is the cheapest of USD, EUR and GBP, and Meta prefers spending in USD. Set the ad account currency to USD in Business Manager or the ad account settings, not via the credit card.
  • Changing an ad account's currency after creating it does not lose the Meta pixel. A pixel is a small tracking code that records visits and purchases on your site. The pixel can be attached to any ad account, regardless of currency.
  • Settings for Meta ad account currency: Business Manager or ad account settings > currency > USD. The card can stay in another currency, but your bank may charge a foreign conversion fee. A "business limit reached" message when you create a new ad account can come from many pages linked to one business. Removing pages from Business Manager is one option to avoid deleting them.

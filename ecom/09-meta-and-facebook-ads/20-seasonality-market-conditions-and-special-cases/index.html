Playbook › 9. Meta and Facebook ads

Seasonality, market conditions and special cases

Section 9.20 · 20 of 22 in this chapter · 11 min read

In short

  • Normal ROAS (sales earned per dollar spent on ads) is 5-7. In busy holiday seasons, 3.5-4.5 can also be normal.
  • Late-year ads cost more, so plan a bigger budget for October to December.
  • Use the 10x rule (ad budget = monthly sales target ÷ 10), but treat it as older advice from 2020.
  • Check the country's CPM (cost per 1,000 ad views). Stop paying for countries where CPM is high and sales are not coming in.
  • Judge expensive products only over weeks of data, not a few days.

Seasonal Planning and Q4 Cost Pressure

2 min
  • ROAS (return on ad spend: sales earned for each dollar spent on ads) of 5-7 is the normal baseline.
    • In peak seasons (the busiest selling times of year), 70-80% of market ad budgets go to holidays and seasonal events.
    • In those seasons, a drop to 3.5-4.5 is normal.
    • Constant competition keeps ad costs high.
  • Q4 (October to December, the last three months of the year) and Black Friday (the big sale day after US Thanksgiving) raise CPM (cost per 1,000 ad views).
    • Large brands spend heavily, so customer acquisition (winning new buyers) gets more expensive late in the year.
    • Plan budgets and expected costs with that increase in mind.
  • High CPMs on new products and new creative during peak periods, such as after Halloween, are normal. Creative means the ad image or video.
    • Big brands spend millions at the same time.
    • CPM falls as competitive spend normalizes.
  • Ad costs rise in Q4 because retailers and profitable businesses increase spending before year-end. They do this to reduce taxable profit (the profit tax is charged on).
    • Black Friday through Christmas traffic is therefore more expensive.
  • To prepare for higher Q4 costs:
    1. Move part of the normal November and December ad budget into earlier months, when costs are lower.
    2. Use that budget to build an audience of website visitors. An audience is a group of people your ads are shown to.
    3. Remarket (show ads again) to those visitors through Black Friday, Cyber Monday (the online sale day after Black Friday) and Christmas. Use both ads and emails, which is the lowest-cost advertising available.
  • For cold-weather products, raise ad spend as the cold season starts in the target market, not after demand has peaked.
    • Demand rises as temperatures fall, so the budget should follow that rise.
  • Increasing ad budgets is appropriate in a slow January when ROAS looks healthy.
    • Sales typically pick up the following week, after the post-Black Friday lull (the quiet period after the sales).
  • Dropshippers (sellers who sell products without holding stock, while the supplier ships) without warehouse stock cannot advertise during a supplier closure.
    • An operator with stock can gain share from competitors that cannot run ads.

Ad Budget Formulas

1 min
  • Monthly ad budget = target monthly revenue ÷ 10, because the target return on ad spend is 10x.
  • Daily budget = monthly ad budget ÷ 30.
  • Example: a 10,000 USD revenue target gives a 1,000 USD ad budget, about 33 USD per day.
  • Ad spend should not produce a return below 10x. Anything lower means the ads are losing money.
  • Example daily split for a 10,000 USD monthly revenue target:
Item Value
Monthly revenue target 10,000 USD
Monthly ad budget at 10x return 1,000 USD
Daily ad budget about 33 USD
Google Shopping (product listing ads, which show products in Google search) about 28 USD per day
Facebook remarketing 5 USD per day
  • Audit the ads every few days. An audit means checking the numbers and results.
  • Conflict on ROAS targets:
    • The 2020 source sets a 10x floor and says anything lower loses money.
    • The second source accepts a drop to 3.5-4.5 during peak seasons as normal.
    • The sources do not say which rule governs a given campaign.
  • Planning CPM: use about 15 USD CPM when estimating ad cost per 1,000 impressions (ad views) for the product's price point.

CPM Thresholds and Market Selection

1 min
  • Alarm threshold for a digital product (a product sold as a file, with no shipping): a Facebook CPM above roughly 20-25 is expensive.
    • Above 30, interactive ad formats are recommended to lower it. Interactive ads let people tap, comment, like or share.
  • Q4 testing rule: avoid testing in countries where CPM is above 35 USD, unless the product is already profitable there.
    • Prefer countries with lower CPM for testing.
  • The two thresholds apply to different decisions.
    • The 20-25 and 30 figures are general alarm and action levels.
    • The 35 USD figure is a cutoff for Q4 test markets.
  • Interactive ads that get comments, likes and shares from the audience tend to lower CPM.
  • Targeting a different market is the most basic way to reduce CPM, because some countries deliver much cheaper impressions.
  • Procedure: find the country with the highest CPM and check whether it delivers results.
    1. Open the ad account in Meta Ads Manager (Meta's tool for running Facebook and Instagram ads).
    2. On the right side, click Breakdown (a tool that splits results by group).
    3. Choose By delivery.
    4. Review age, gender and country to see which combination gives the best results.
    5. Stop spending on countries that show high CPM without good results.
  • Case: a digital product priced at 97 USD was tested with a Facebook ad set (a group of ads sharing one audience and settings) covering many countries. CPM was about 50-80 USD.
    • After switching to a single ad set with several English-speaking and European countries, CPM dropped to about 18.
    • The seller had three sales at that point.
  • Do not judge a campaign on CPM alone. The case above shows CPM changing with the country mix, but the sales count was still very small.

Advertiser Pullouts and Falling CPMs

2 min

  • Meta ad prices work like an auction (bidding, where the highest bids win).

    • The more advertisers bid for the same audience, the higher the price of each impression.
    • When a large group stops bidding, prices fall for everyone, including those who did not depend on that group.

  • Case, April 2025 (the report is dated Wednesday, April 30): Temu (a low-price online marketplace) stopped running Meta ads on April 16.

    • The expert estimated that a large share of US Meta ad spend came from Chinese sellers and US dropshippers sourcing from China.
    • That spend disappeared at once.
    • A CPM drop across Meta followed within about two weeks.
    • This is the expert's estimate, not a verified figure.

  • Temu was named as one of the large low-price sellers that spent heavily on Meta before pulling its ads in April.

    • The expert said Shein (an online fashion seller) and similar sellers were also major spenders.
    • Their pullback mattered too.
    • These are unverified estimates.

  • Unverified estimates in this section:

    • An analyst estimated that about 7 billion USD of Meta's 132 billion USD in 2023 ad revenue came from China. The expert assumed the real figure was higher in 2024 and 2025.
    • The expert estimated that more than 10% of the US Meta ad budget came from Chinese companies or US dropshippers sourcing from China, before tariffs (import taxes) and the ad pullback.

  • When ad costs fall because large advertisers pause, the same budget reaches more people.

    • This is temporary.
    • Prices are expected to return to normal if the tariffs or market shift that caused the pullback end.

  • Ad costs also fell during COVID, when many physical businesses such as retail stores and restaurants stopped advertising.

    • Any large group of advertisers pulling out lowers prices, whatever the reason.

  • Use falling CPMs to test ad goals that cost more when prices are high:

    • scaling spend on winning ads (raising the budget on ads that already sell)
    • lead generation ads that send visitors to an email opt-in page (a sign-up page for email)
    • ads that promote blog posts
    • campaigns that build social followers

    Do this only for stores that already have sales and profit, not for brand-new stores.

  • If you already run Meta ads, compare your current CPM with your CPM one, two and three months ago.

    • A lower CPM means the same budget reaches more people.

Holiday and Event Campaigns

2 min

  • Use the same ad format throughout the campaign: dark imagery with a simple sales banner overlay (text placed on top of the image).

    • Stage discounts: 50% off in pre-sale (an early sale before the main day), then 60% off on Black Friday itself.
    • The extra margin (profit per sale) is small, only 10 percentage points, but it drives urgency (pressure to buy now).

  • Run the same holiday deal creative in both the Instagram and Facebook news feeds (the main scrolling feed).

    • Use the same image and copy, adapted to each placement (where the ad appears).

  • State the exact end time in the body copy (the main text of the ad), such as midnight on the last day of the sale.

    • A fixed, real end point makes the urgency believable.

  • Send holiday deal ads to a dedicated sale landing page (a single web page the ad links to) for that event, such as a /black-friday URL, not the homepage.

    • This keeps the click focused on the one offer the ad promises.

  • For a promotion lasting only a few days, rotate several creatives in bright, different colors, plus one video variant.

    • The same audience then does not see one ad for the whole sale window.
    • Keep the same offer in every version and change only the visuals.

  • Run promotions on Reach and Frequency (a buying type that sets how many people see the ad and how often), not Conversions (a buying type that aims for sales).

    • Show them to warm audiences: people who already know your store. These are website visitors from the past 180 days, plus Facebook and Instagram engagers (people who liked, commented or clicked).
    • Do not exclude past customers.
    • Never show promotions to cold audiences, which are people who do not know your store yet.

  • Meta remarketing for holiday promotions:

    • Create one new campaign per holiday.
    • Use static image ads (still pictures), not dynamic remarketing ads (ads that automatically show the exact products a visitor looked at).
    • Audience: all website visitors from the past 180 days.
    • Exclusion (leaving people out of the audience): all customers who purchased in the past 30 days.

  • Retargeting worked well for large events such as Black Friday, Cyber Monday or Christmas, when site traffic is high.

    • Outside such events it may not work consistently, especially after Apple's iOS 14 update, which limited tracking.

  • Black Friday and Cyber Monday CPMs rise because competitors raise budgets, which makes targeting more expensive.

    • A useful response is an extended discount that starts the day after the event.

  • A Meta ad account that had spent steadily for about 1.5 years was allowed to scale to very large daily budgets when spend jumped.

    • A new account or newly set-up business manager (Meta's container for ad accounts) may face restrictions when spend increases quickly.
    • Build a steady spend history before planning a large sale.

  • For a holiday-themed campaign, restrict ads to the countries where the holiday is celebrated, instead of running them broadly.

    • For Cinco de Mayo, the example pointed to Mexico and the US.

Geographic and Gifting Niches

1 min
  • GCC markets (the Gulf Cooperation Council countries: Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE) showed a Facebook CPM of about 1 USD per 1,000 impressions.
    • This is cheap, but these markets still required cash-on-delivery (the customer pays when the parcel arrives) to convert (turn viewers into buyers).
  • For gift-type products in the holiday season, test a broad audience in one ad set.
    • Also test audiences such as grandparents or people with a friend who has a child, since they may buy as gifts.
    • Avoid overly narrow interests with this approach.

Digital Products

1 min

  • Validate the product with paid traffic (visitors from ads) before investing time in creating it.

    • Keep early costs low by using existing market videos.

  • Facebook is a strong platform for digital products, together with Pinterest and Etsy (online marketplaces for handmade and digital goods).

  • Pinterest and organic social media (free posts, not ads) can also drive traffic to digital products.

    • This works alongside paid Facebook and TikTok ads.

  • Run Facebook and TikTok ads for a digital product as you would for a physical product:

    • Start with interest-based targeting that matches the problem and audience profile, such as age, gender and related hobbies.
    • Use the cost per customer from these ads to decide the price and how much room the margin (profit per sale) allows.

  • Facebook ads are a recommended paid traffic source for affiliate funnels.

    • An affiliate funnel is a sales path where you earn a commission for sending buyers to another seller's product.
    • Free alternatives, if you want the first sale without spending on ads, are guest posts (articles written for other blogs), forum participation and active community engagement.

High-Ticket Products

1 min
  • High-ticket items are expensive products. For them, a lead campaign (an ad that collects contact details) can work better than sending cold traffic straight to a sale.
    • The lead campaign drives people into a funnel with email capture (collecting email addresses), followed by a drip sequence (a series of automatic emails sent over days).
    • Higher-priced products need more patience and more spend to gather enough data.
  • Touchpoints (each time a buyer sees your ad or message) before purchase:
    • Products around 50-60 USD may need about 7-10 touchpoints.
    • Products around 200 USD may need 20-30 touchpoints.
    • Expect a higher-priced product to take more creative exposure (how often a person sees an ad) and more time before results.
  • Example for a 200 USD product: one creative may need to be seen three or four times, and another about five times, before the buyer converts.
    • Plan the testing budget around that exposure.
  • About 300 USD of spend was cited as a rough reference for a campaign to start learning.
    • Higher-priced products typically need more spend and more time in market.
    • Do not judge a higher-ticket campaign on a few days of data.
  • Do not start a high-ticket product with a small test budget such as 10 EUR per day.
    • Collecting useful data at a higher cost per purchase takes too long.
  • For high-ticket products, organic social posts (free posts, not ads) rarely convert directly.
    • Post one or two times per week to show activity.
    • Focus budget on proven paid ads.
    • (Source dated 2024.)

Print-on-Demand Products

1 min
  • Print-on-demand products are printed only after a customer orders.
    • Facebook carousel ads (one ad with several swipeable images) and shopping ads were reported to work best.

Playbook › 9. Meta and Facebook ads

Ad creative, copy and placement formats

Section 9.13 · 13 of 22 in this chapter · 28 min read

In short

  • Primary Text and Headline are the two most important text parts, because they stay visible in every placement.
  • The first 1-2 lines of Primary Text must stop people from scrolling past.
  • Test the hook (the first 1-3 seconds of the ad), the style and the idea before you change small details.
  • Keep text on an ad image under 20% of the image, or the ad is rejected.
  • Test each new ad in its own ad set.
More key points
  • Make ideas that are truly different, not small word or colour changes.
  • Let each test run for 2 to 3 days before you judge it.
  • Judge the idea by profit, and the opening seconds by hook rate.

Ad Text Anatomy and Field Priorities

1 min
  • A Facebook ad has three text parts.
  • Primary Text is the main copy above the creative (the picture or video of the ad).
  • Headline is bold text below the creative.
  • Description is small text below the Headline.
  • The Description is often hidden on Reels and Stories placements.
  • A placement is a spot where the ad shows, such as the Feed, Reels or Stories. Reels and Stories are full-screen vertical videos.
  • Primary Text and Headline are the two most important elements.
  • They stay visible in all placements.
  • Description visibility is unreliable.
  • So optimise the first two first.
  • Viewers see only the first 1-2 lines of Primary Text.
  • They decide there whether to watch the video or skip it.
  • These lines must stop the scroll (stop people from scrolling past).
  • Use a bold claim, a direct question about a pain point, or a benefit statement.
  • Put the website URL (the web address) in the Primary Text field.
  • This lets users click from the copy straight to the store.
  • Users see the video first and the text overlay (words placed on top of the video) second.
  • Ad copy must not look pushy.
  • The whole ad should read as organic (like a normal, unpaid post) and native (matching the platform's look).
  • In a Facebook video ad, the headline and some call-to-action buttons (CTA, the button that tells people what to do, such as "Shop Now") below the image disappear.
  • So the website link belongs at the top of the Primary Text.

Primary Text Length and Templates

1 min
  • Long-form Primary Text runs more than 5-6 lines.
  • Short-form Primary Text runs 3-4 lines.
  • Choose copy length by audience age.
  • For 40+, use long-form copy.
  • For under 40, test short-format copy.
  • Conflicting age thresholds exist in the sources.
  • gives 40+ for long-form and under 40 for short-format.
  • gives above 40 or 50 for Facebook-style captions with product, solution and benefit messaging.
  • gives under 40 or 30 for TikTok-style UGC (user-generated content, which looks made by a regular customer).
  • Use the threshold that matches your audience data (your own numbers about who buys).

Long-form Primary Text template:

Line 1 (bold claim or problem): Introducing [Product Name], the ultimate solution for [Customer Problem].

Line 2-3 (agitation): Are you tired of [specific pain point]? We have got the perfect solution for you. Say goodbye to [problem] once and for all with our innovative [product category].

Line 4 (benefits): Key benefits: [Benefit 1] • [Benefit 2] • [Benefit 3]

Line 5 (offer): [Discount]% off, limited time only / [Discount]% off with code [CODE]

Line 6 (action + outcome): Shop now and transform your [problem] into [desired outcome].

Short-form Primary Text template:

Line 1 (problem question): Struggling with [pain point]?

Line 2 (solution intro): Say hello to [Product Name], your answer to [problem].

Line 3 (benefits): [Benefit 1] • [Benefit 2] • [Benefit 3]

Line 4 (exclusive offer): [Discount]% off, exclusive offer

Line 5 (CTA): Shop now

Headlines and Descriptions

1 min

Headline formula library (ready-made patterns):

**Attention-grabbing:** Discover [Product Name] [your solution to problem] OR Get ready to transform your life with [Product Name].

**Urgency + offer:** Limited time offer: [Discount]% off OR Hurry, [Product Name] sales ending soon. Grab [Product Name] now!

**Benefit-driven:** Experience [key benefit] with [Product Name] OR [Key benefit] way to [solve problem]

**Problem-solving:** Say goodbye to [problem] with [Product Name] OR Tired of [customer pain point]? Try [Product Name].
  • The Description is a small, often-hidden field.
  • Fill it with limited-time offer details, such as "Free shipping" or "30-day money-back guarantee".
  • Or fill it with social proof (signs that other people trust you), such as "Rated 4.8 out of 5 by 15,000+ happy customers".
  • Avoid long text.
  • On the Facebook news feed, the ad copy can be more detailed.
  • It can name both the problem and the solution the content offers.
  • The Headline and CTA stay the same as in other placements.

Writing and Sourcing Copy

1 min
  • Gather copy ideas from three sources.
    • Customer psychology research: the deepest desires and insecurities.
    • Direct customer interviews and feedback.
    • Competitor ad analysis via the Facebook Ads Library (a public database of running ads).
  • ChatGPT and Copy.ai are free tools for drafting Primary Text and Headlines.
  • Enter your product details and customer pain points (the problems customers want solved).
  • Or paste a competitor ad and ask for a rephrased version for your product.
  • Generate 3-5 variations.
  • Lead with the customer's transformation, not the mechanism (how the product works inside).
  • "Includes 360-degree compression technology and cooling materials" is weaker.
  • "Migraine relief you can feel in minutes" is stronger.
  • The stronger line works because the customer cares about the result.
  • Symptom-led headlines name the visible problem the buyer already feels, such as leaking, damage or lost time.
  • They do not name the root cause, because people recognise symptoms more easily than causes.
  • Formula: [symptom the buyer sees] + [what it costs them in time, money or image] + question.
  • Examples: "Shoes still soaking after your morning walk?" and "Keep finding ruined shirts after the rain? Stop losing money on replacements."
  • Avoid direct claims such as weight-loss guarantees or fitness guarantees.
  • This applies to captions, voiceover (spoken narration) and on-screen text.
  • Rephrase them so they do not read as a violation.

Hooks

1 min
  • Meta defines the hook (the attention-grabbing opening) as the first 1-3 seconds of an ad.
  • Other platforms and sources use different time definitions.
  • So the exact length matters less than the element that grabs attention immediately.
  • Hook length in the sources:
    • First 1-3 seconds: Meta's definition.
    • First 1-5 seconds: the proven hook to reuse from a competitor's ad.
    • First 3-5 seconds: the part that differs between creative variations in testing.
  • Test idea, style and hook before smaller variables (small details).
  • These three have the largest impact on results, even though they take more effort to change.
  • After Meta's Andromeda update (a change to how Meta shows ads), this lets the platform test a wide range of very different ads.
  • Use the Hook Test strategy on Meta, Facebook and Instagram.
  • Run several different ad hooks or angles (the viewpoint of the ad).
  • Keep the ones that scale (grow well when you spend more).
  • Judge hooks by hook rate (the share of viewers who keep watching past the hook).
  • Use hook rate to find the best-performing hook.

Offers, Urgency and Guarantees

2 min
  • Risk-reversal structure: a guarantee that takes the risk off the buyer.
  • It works because it removes fear of wasted money.
  • It sets clear expectations for when to judge results.
    1. State the specific benefit timeline ("relief in as little as 7 days").
    2. Include a money-back guarantee with an exact period ("30-night risk-free trial").
    3. Show the price clearly and prominently.
    • Examples: "Feel the difference in 5 days. 60-night money-back guarantee, $44.99" and "Sleep better by night one. 90-night satisfaction guarantee, $59.99 today."
  • Competitor comparison with negative indicators.
  • It works by making the alternative look worse.
  • It also makes your unique safety or benefit advantage clear.
    1. Display your product's key attributes with checkmarks or positive phrasing (upbeat wording).
    2. List what alternatives include that customers want to avoid, marked with X, "no" or "risk of".
    • Examples: "Natural formula ✓, Zero chemicals X, Long-lasting ✓, No rebound effect X" and "Effective immediately ✓, No grogginess X, No prescription X, No dependency ✓."
  • Urgency with specificity: vague urgency such as "soon" creates no action.
  • Specific deadlines trigger fear of missing out (FOMO).
    • State the end day, for example "Offer ends Sunday".
    • Or use "Today Only" or "Limited time only".
    • Or show inventory scarcity (low stock shown to buyers).
    • Examples: "Launch offer ends Thursday. Get 20% off now" and "This weekend only: buy now at launch price, $39.99."
  • Offer testing: start without an offer.
  • Then check visitor behaviour (what visitors do on the store).
  • Then test offers such as Buy one get the second item 20% off, Buy one get the second 50% off, or Buy two get the third free.
  • One operator's winning offer at the time was Buy one get the second item 20% off.
  • Place the offer text in the Primary Text of a single image ad, the most visible spot.
  • It also appears as the first line on Instagram.
  • For traffic from both Facebook and Instagram, put the offer in the Primary Text.
  • It can also go in other parts of the ad.
  • Run the first ads as conversion campaigns (campaigns aimed at sales).
  • Use urgency and fear-of-missing-out messaging, so first-time visitors buy and the product is validated (proven to sell).
  • Use retargeting (showing ads again to people who visited) and email to win back visitors who did not buy.
  • Use only evergreen promo codes (codes that never expire), such as a permanent 3% or 5% code created in Shopify (online store software).
  • Expiring or changing codes break remarketing (another word for retargeting) ads once they stop working.
  • Confirm codes are active before publishing.

Social Proof, Reviews and Comments

2 min
  • Include text-based customer reviews as quotes under the initial copy.
  • This applies to Facebook ads and other ads with enough copy space.
  • This adds credibility and increases curiosity and click-through motivation (the urge to click).
  • Review-led ad structure (an ad built around customer reviews):
    1. Place a headline that summarises a review above the body copy.
    2. Start the body copy with a review text, so the shopper sees social proof first.
  • A Facebook ad can carry four or five reviews in the top text.
  • It ends with a link back to the product.
  • Choose reviews that highlight different aspects of the product or service, so all key points are covered.
  • If many reviews praise the business itself, such as seller service or the buying experience, the trusted advisor ad can focus on the business.
  • A trusted advisor ad presents the business itself as the one to trust.
  • Product images can still be used.
  • The text should say plainly what the business is known for.
  • Social proof scale: "50K+ users" stated as "relieved and relaxed" gives concrete credibility.
  • Compare this with an unspecified "thousands of happy customers".
  • Reply to every comment with helpful answers, such as sizes or shipping times.
  • Do not pitch or post sales links.
  • Ask a follow-up question to keep the conversation going.
  • This builds engagement (likes, comments and shares) that lowers the CPM (cost per 1,000 views).
  • CPM was reported to fall from about $15 at launch to $5-$8 after building engagement with comments.
  • Typical dropshipping (selling products that a supplier ships for you) CPMs are $20-$30.
  • A new campaign can still show $20-$30 before it gets engagement.
  • An ad with zero comments looks untrustworthy.
  • So people do not ask questions or comment.
  • Seed the first comments and reviews yourself from the brand page at launch (place the first ones yourself).
  • Ads with high engagement, such as comments, likes and shares, get a lower CPM.
  • They reach more people for the same spend.
  • Creatives that invite viewers to comment or share can produce this effect.
  • Example: an ad inviting viewers to post a photo of their own pet in the comments triggered many comments and likes.
  • The higher engagement lowered the CPM, so the same budget reached more people.

Single Image Ads

1 min
  • Image ads are cheaper on Facebook and often convert better than video.
  • Use images for products such as jewellery, fashion and some lifestyle products.
  • Image ads often have a lower CPM than video, so they reach more people for the same budget.
  • Facebook may also show image ads more to users with a slow internet connection.
  • Video ads cost more but can be useful for retargeting.
  • Products that need no explanation can be sold with picture ads without video.
  • Examples of such products: home decor, clothing, shoes and jewellery.
  • Videos are needed for products that must be explained or demonstrated.
  • Scaling (growing spend and sales) with image ads alone is possible.
  • One operator reached seven-figure revenue (more than one million) over six-plus months.
  • Roughly 99% of those sales came from static image ads (still pictures).
  • That operator ran no video ads.
  • Image ads are easy to produce in Canva (an easy design tool).
  • They carry better targeting (choosing who sees the ad) than many alternatives.
  • For Facebook ads promoting clothing, image ads perform best compared with other formats.
  • Include natural, non-graphic photo ads alongside designed graphics.
  • Designed ads can still look like ads.
  • Carousel ads (several swipeable cards in one ad) suit lifestyle-heavy products such as fashion.
  • Single image ads can also be strong.
  • For a single image ad, Meta's Ad Setup > Format (the menu in Meta's ad builder) offers Single image or video.
  • Use Carousel only when several cards are needed on purpose.
  • Meme ad procedure:
    • Place a text on top of the image.
    • Add the product picture below.
    • Add an emoji (a small picture symbol).
    • Build these in Canva.
    • Test multiple texts with one image.

Video Ads

2 min
  • A Facebook video ad is built in almost the same way as a static image ad.
  • A static image version also works.
  • Video is generally preferred by audiences.
  • In the owner's clothing test, ads from 15 seconds to under 20 seconds tended to outperform one-minute videos.
  • In those short ads, the benefit came immediately.
  • They had better click-through (the share of viewers who click) and conversion (buying).
  • A before-and-after format showing the result early, then the product, drove more clicks.
  • A one-minute video still has value for retargeting viewers who watched more than 50% of it.
  • Repurposing a standard Facebook ad into a TikTok aspect ratio (frame shape) performed very badly.
  • Even uploaded as an organic video, it got fewer than 100 views.
  • Build creative native to each platform instead.
  • Early insight from the ad owner: the early Facebook ads worked because the advertiser was also a target customer.
  • That advertiser knew the audience's interests and followed the same creators.
  • Use a hook-pain-solution-benefit structure (hook, then pain point, then solution, then benefit).
  • Show the product's benefits directly.
  • Paid ads follow the same content logic as organic posts (unpaid posts).
  • The ad only adds paid distribution (paying to show it) and audience targeting.
  • Content that performs organically tends to perform as ads with the right targeting.
  • Transcript procedure for video ads. A transcript is the written text of the spoken words.
    1. Get the video transcribed with an automatic online transcription tool such as Rev.
    2. Paste the exact transcript into the ad copy at the top.
    • Reason: some people do not listen to audio, so they can read the message instead.
    • Always include a link to the page you want visitors to reach.
  • Rev is an online transcription tool that automatically turns video audio into text.
  • It is useful for producing ad copy from video.
  • Facebook ads do not offer a click-to-call button (a button that dials a phone number).
  • A call-to-action that asks people to phone the business must link to the website's contact page instead.
  • Use the contact page URL as the ad destination (the page the ad opens).
  • To promote an existing post, use the version that already has the engagement.
  • Likes, comments and views carry over from the original post, but the other platform's copy starts at zero.
  • Example: use the Facebook post if it has far more views than the Instagram copy.

Carousel, Catalog and Collection Ads

1 min
  • Carousel ads perform best on Facebook.
  • Carousels with five cards perform very well.
  • Catalog ads (ads that pull products from your product list automatically) also work well on Facebook.
  • Carousel setup:
    • Pick 3 to 5 product images. One operator used 4.
    • Add an offer and a link to each card. Free shipping text is optional.
    • Use the collection page link as the destination.
    • Add a headline and description to each card.
    • Use the same Primary Text as the single image ad.
    • CTA: Shop Now or See more.
  • Carousel ads perform well for product tests.
  • They show several product angles in one ad.
  • Use a single image or video for most Facebook retargeting ads.
  • Collection ads (a layout with a main video or slideshow and product tiles below) tend to underperform.
  • A carousel is an option only when several product views need to be shown.
  • Collection ads combine a video or slideshow at the top with dynamic product ads (ads that show the exact products a viewer looked at) underneath.
  • Shoppers can click through to the exact product they viewed.
  • This makes collection ads suitable for retargeting.

Slideshow Ads

1 min
  • Slideshow ad procedure in Meta Ads Manager (Meta's tool for building and running ads):
    1. Choose Create video instead of image or video.
    2. Select 4 product images.
    3. Click Edit and set the display time per image to 1 second.
    4. Arrange the image order.
    5. Click Play to preview.
    6. Click Save to computer and wait for the video file to finish building.
    7. Upload it, then add headline, description and Shop Now CTA, and check the link.

Specifications, Ratios and Safe Areas

3 min
  • The feed ad ratio (the shape of the frame, width to height) is 4:5.
  • In pixels (tiny dots that make up the picture), that is 1080 x 1350.
  • Reels, Stories and TikTok use 9:16 portrait (tall, phone-shaped).
  • Placing a 9:16 edit into a feed placement crops (cuts off the edges) it to 4:5.
  • So text outside the 4:5 area can be cut off.
  • Change the project ratio with the ratio button next to the playhead (the marker showing where you are in the timeline).
  • 9:16 is the usual portrait ratio for Reels, Stories and TikTok.
  • Build the edit in 9:16.
  • Keep all text and graphics within the 4:5 area.
  • That way the feed crop does not remove them.
  • Placing text high on the frame, near the face, is a common mistake.
  • Feed placements crop the top and bottom of a 9:16 edit.
  • Move the text down so it stays inside the 4:5 area.
  • Use a 4:5 overlay (a layer placed on top) or a locked guide layer while editing.
  • Blend modes for an overlay (how an overlay mixes with the picture below):
Blend mode Effect
Screen Removes dark areas and keeps light ones
Multiply Keeps dark areas and removes light ones
Soft light A softer version of these two
Darken Keeps only dark areas
Brighten Keeps only light areas
  • Lower the overlay opacity (how see-through the layer is) if the effect is too strong.
  • Facebook crops portrait video to 4:5 in feed placements.
  • Keep the main subject and captions near the centre of the frame.
  • Place captions higher than the bottom edge, so the crop does not cut them off.
  • Overlay banners (text bars placed on top) at the very top of a video can be cropped out.
  • This can happen when Facebook shows the feed version at 4:5.
  • Export a 4:5 feed version with sale banners inside the visible area.
  • Use a 9:16 version for Stories and Reels, where the top banner stays visible.
  • Ad image specification for link ads (ads that send people to a web page): exactly 1200 by 627 pixels.
  • No more than 20% of the image may be text.
  • Ads with more than 20% text on the image are rejected immediately.
  • Images with more than 20% text get the ad denied right away.
  • Keep text on the image well under that threshold.
  • Ads are also commonly denied in review.
  • That is why a support ticket (a request for help sent to the platform) is the next step.
  • Keep text on Facebook ad images under 20% of the image area.
  • Check with a grid overlay (a grid laid on top to measure) before launch.
  • Images that pass this rule have the best chance of approval.
  • Facebook ad images should be JPG or PNG (common picture file types).
  • Use square 1:1 for feed and in-stream video placements (video ads placed between other videos).
  • Use fullscreen vertical 9:16 for Stories.
  • Use an image with little or no overlaid text to maximise delivery (how many people the ad reaches).
  • In Ads Manager, Automatically Crop to Square creates a square version of an uploaded image for placements that need it.
  • Crop Image lets you adjust the crop manually for each placement.
  • A square ad uses a 1080 x 1080 pixel frame.
  • That is a 1:1 aspect ratio.
  • Set the frame size in the sequence settings of your editor (the video editing program).
  • Enter the horizontal and vertical pixel values for the ad format.
  • Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve (video editors) have an equivalent setting.
  • When Meta's ad preview shows dimension warnings for a 4:5 video, the warnings can be ignored in most cases.
  • The reason: a 4:5 file is served to most placements.
  • Check that the upload shows the dimensions as fine, then continue.

Placements, Previews and Destinations

2 min
  • Preview each ad on Instagram, the Facebook feed and Facebook Marketplace before publishing.
  • The same carousel or picture can look different in each placement.
  • Separate formats for placements (procedure):
    1. Upload the horizontal video.
    2. Click Edit video, then Edit placements.
    3. Upload the story-format version for Stories.
    • Using the same video in two formats (feed and story) is also acceptable.
  • For a video that runs on Facebook and Instagram in different formats, upload the horizontal version first.
  • Then click Edit video > Edit placements and upload the story version.
  • Feed placements on Facebook and Instagram were the best placements for the example account.
  • So switch from automatic placement (Meta chooses the spots for you) to manual placement once data shows feeds perform best.
  • The same image, headline and body copy can run on Instagram news feed placements.
  • Keeping one creative across Facebook and Instagram supports consistent recognition.
  • Facebook creates the Instagram version of a Facebook feed ad automatically.
  • So a separate Instagram ad does not have to be built.
  • The Instagram version is identical to the Facebook version unless you change it.
  • Facebook feed ads can use a short or long copy version.
  • Long copy gives more room for product detail.
  • A "View more" link lets readers expand it.
  • So the most important text should sit at the top.
  • On Instagram, only the first line of body copy shows before the "more" link.
  • The opening line must summarise what the ad is about, so the viewer clicks to read the rest.
  • Set the ad destination to the product page or landing page (the page the ad opens) for one-product stores.
  • Use a collection link only when customers should choose between several products, such as fashion or jewellery.
  • Set the ad CTA to Shop Now when the ad sends traffic to a collection page.
  • Use Shop Now with the product page link when the ad sends traffic to one product.
  • Multi-advertiser ads is on by default in the ad format section.
  • Meta can personalise the ad and resize the creative for each viewer.
  • Check the creative in the preview, because it can be resized.
  • Ad-level Languages can translate the ad text based on the viewer's location or default language.
  • It is optional and can stay off in a standard sales setup.

Meta Setup Settings

3 min
  • Under Ad creative setup, Meta allows up to five Primary Text versions. Primary Text is the main body text of the ad. It shows as "Primary text (1 of 5)".
  • Add more versions with the Add text option. Add more headlines with the Add headline option. A headline is the short title line of the ad.
  • When you enter more than one text, Meta shows the version it predicts will perform best.
  • The label reads "Not optimised" while this setting is off.
  • The Set up test option sits under the creative section.
  • It compares up to 7 different versions of one creative in a single test.
  • It is meant to make sure new test ads get delivery (Meta actually shows them to people).
  • Advantage+ creative enhancements are Meta's automatic changes to your ad.
  • The default is 6 of 8 enabled.
  • Choose All optimisations to turn all 8 on.
  • After saving, it shows 8 out of 8.
  • Dynamic creative testing (DCT) mixes many images with copy and headlines. Meta finds the best combinations.
  • Add a DCT ad as a new asset in a scaling CBO. CBO (campaign budget optimisation) means Meta splits one budget across the ad sets inside a campaign. A campaign is the top folder that holds ad sets.
  • This shows whether new creatives get spend (money put into showing them).
  • If it gets almost none, the creatives are likely not good enough.
  • Meta is phasing out Dynamic creative, which means it is slowly removing it.
  • While it is still available, using it switches on the Flexible ad format. This is an option where Meta changes the ad layout by itself.
  • So the Flexible option is turned off in this test setup.
  • Some advertisers build creatives as dark posts. A dark post is an unpublished post that appears only inside ads, not on the Page timeline (the Page's public feed).
  • The older method was still preferred for setting up ads.
  • Promotion and Sandbox ads run as dark posts. The Sandbox is a separate campaign for testing creatives.
  • They never appear on the Facebook Page.
  • Post only the content you want public, to the Page first.

Ad setup procedure for a creative test:

  1. Name the ad with today's date, product name, CBO, ad set name and the ad number, following the naming formula (your fixed naming rule).
  2. Choose Manual setup.
  3. Select the Facebook Page and the Instagram account that should appear as the ad identity. The ad identity is the name and profile picture people see on the ad.
  4. Choose the single image or video format and turn the Flexible option off.
  5. Open the Media box (where you add images and videos), choose Videos, and upload all the video creatives you have collected. Video creatives are the video ads you made.
  6. Paste the Primary Text and headlines, add one description (the small line under the headline), paste the landing page as the website URL, and select Shop Now as the CTA. The landing page is the page people reach after clicking. CTA means call to action, the button text.
  7. Duplicate the ad once for each creative so every creative has its own ad, then publish all ads.
  8. Uploading takes time, so wait for it to finish before step 6.

Creating the other two creatives in one ad set (an ad set is a group of ads that share one audience and budget):

  1. Click the three toggle buttons (the small menu buttons) on the first ad. Choose Duplicate, and repeat once more so there are three copies in total.
  2. Rename the copies Video 2 and Video 3.
  3. In each copy, open Ad creative, click Edit media, and click Change video. Select the next video. Image ads also work.
  4. Keep the Primary Text, headline, description and call to action identical to the first ad.

Creative Testing Structure

5 min
  • The Andromeda retrieval engine is Meta's system that decides who sees each ad. It was deployed in December 2024.
  • Creative now does the audience targeting.
  • So tight audience restrictions can hurt performance.
  • Campaigns need genuinely different creative concepts. A concept is one distinct idea for an ad.
  • Small changes, such as a word or a colour, do not count.
  • Campaigns with 8-15 truly different concepts are expected to outperform those with 2-3 variants of one idea.
  • A concept pairs one avatar with one angle. An avatar is a made-up picture of one buyer type. An angle is the reason or message used to reach that buyer.
  • An angle is one of these:
    • a specific pain point (a problem the buyer wants solved)
    • a variation in messaging (different wording of the message)
    • a demographic segment (a group defined by traits), such as ethnicity, age group or life stage
  • Test a new creative with the best existing audience, such as a winning interest or stacked interests. An interest is a Meta targeting group based on hobbies and topics. Stacked interests means several interests combined into one audience.
  • This way only the creative varies.
  • Reduce all other variables, including the offer and audience, to keep the test readable. Variables are the things that can change. The offer is the deal you make, such as a discount.
  • Moving a winning ad into a new ad set and forcing spend on it can raise CPM and CPC. CPM is the cost per 1,000 views. CPC is the cost per click.
  • This happens while other creatives get almost no spend.
  • Test untested creatives in a separate ad set. Then Facebook can learn which one performs. Do not push one ad.
  • Ads that get almost no spend in a campaign with a clear winner usually have weaker results in Facebook's data. This is normal.
  • Test them separately in their own ad set before deciding they are dead (no longer useful).
  • Putting several ads into one ad set makes Meta concentrate spend on one or two ads. Others get only cents.
  • Creative test results are then unreliable.
  • So use one ad per ad set during creative testing.

Creative testing structure with ABO:

  1. Create one new campaign with budget set at ad set level. This is called ABO (budget set per ad set, not for the whole campaign).
  2. Create nine ad sets, each with a daily budget of 5 USD.
  3. Put exactly one new ad into each ad set.
  4. Target a winning interest in some ad sets and a broad audience (a very wide group with few limits) in others.
  5. Let the campaign run. Compare the nine ads on performance, then keep the best one.

Creative testing with proven audiences:

  1. Build one creative testing campaign. Its ad sets are your winning audiences, one ad set per winning interest or custom audience. A custom audience is a group you build from your own data, such as past visitors.
  2. Add one new creative as three variations that differ only in the first 3-5 seconds. That opening is called the hook.
  3. Keep the same three variations in every ad set so the results can be compared across audiences.
  4. Treat a creative as a likely winner only if it has the best CTR in all winning ad sets, not just one. CTR means click-through rate: the share of people who see the ad and click it.
  5. Replace the older creative in the main campaigns with the confirmed winner. Main campaigns are the everyday ads that run for sales.
  • If one creative wins across all ad sets, use only that creative for scaling. Scaling means raising the budget on a winner to grow sales.
  • If no creative wins everywhere, let the audiences decide.
  • Different creatives may be relatable to different audiences.
  • So give the campaign several strong options.
  • Ad structure inside each ad set: one concept with its three hook variations.
  • Each ad set holds three ads, and every ad set carries the same three ads.
  • Two ads per ad set is the minimum.
  • Two to three is the recommended structure.
  • Three to four are also possible.
  • Let each creative test run for two to three days before judging it.
  • Judge the concept by profitability (whether it earns more than it costs).
  • Judge the hooks by hook rate (the share of people who keep watching past the opening).

Avatar testing procedure:

  1. Keep the problem concept fixed, and choose one audience to test it on.
  2. Create several avatar variants of the same audience. Vary them, for example, by ethnicity, language or gender, such as English-speaking, Spanish-speaking, Latino, or Spanish-speaking men and women.
  3. Test each avatar version with the same problem concept.
  4. Keep the version that performs best, and build more variants from it.
  • Test several creative formats at once. A format is the type of ad.
  • Examples include carousel (several swipeable images in one ad), meme-style image, slideshow and single picture ads.
  • Compare their CTR and spend. Keep the format with the strongest CTR.
  • Scale that one once it has enough spend.
  • Testing multiple creatives is effective.
  • Changing the pictures and adding the offer inside the image improved results for an ad set. That ad set had shown the same creative for a long time.
  • The Sandbox is a separate campaign for split-testing creatives. Split-testing means showing versions side by side to see which wins.
  • CBO tends to concentrate spend on the early winner.
  • So new ads in core campaigns rarely get a fair test.
  • Launch the Sandbox only after the core campaigns are live. Core campaigns are your main campaigns.
  • Test new creatives against the best-performing cold audience. A cold audience is people who do not know your brand yet.
  • When a new ad wins, move it into the core campaign and turn off the old one.
  • Launch a new campaign with a meaningful number of creatives, ideally between 10 and 20.
  • Use the time before launch to produce as many creatives as possible while saving cash.
  • A single ad set with about 13 creatives is a reasonable amount.
  • These can be four videos and nine static images (still pictures).
  • Spread them across different angles.
  • Diversify the angles, such as by gender.
  • Add variants over time.
  • Beginners should aim to launch more than 20 ads per month.
  • As a rough check, divide your monthly ad spend by 500 to 1,000 USD.
  • Creative and conversion matter more than the ad budget at the start. Conversion means a visitor completing a purchase.
  • Fix the page and the creative first.
  • Then increase spend so the test produces useful data.
  • Creative quality and offer quality have a bigger effect than the choice of scaling strategy.
  • They affect ROAS (return on ad spend: money earned per dollar spent) and scalability (how far an ad can grow).
  • A strong creative with a good offer and a strong sales page makes the audience and budget structure matter less.

Creative Fatigue and Refresh

1 min
  • Creative fatigue means an ad stops working because people have seen it too often.
  • Update and test creative ad variations every week.
  • Refresh campaign creatives every week.
  • Ads that get almost no spend in a campaign with a clear winner should be tested in their own ad set before being judged dead.
  • Do not copy a competitor's full creative.
  • Meta can detect duplicates. It can penalise your ad (reduce its reach) when the competitor is penalised.
  • You may reuse only the proven hook (about the first 1-5 seconds).
  • Then change the slides and clips, and add your logo and product name.
  • Alternatively, use custom content (made specially for your brand) or UGC (user-generated content, videos made by real customers or creators) when budget allows.
  • Meta's algorithm updates, such as Andromeda, are increasingly wary of AI-generated ads that look like spam.
  • Generic or mass-produced ads can lose reach (the number of people who see the ad).
  • So AI output must meet a real quality standard before going live.
  • A Dynamic Creative ad that gets almost no spend in a scaling CBO means the creatives are likely not good enough.
  • Replace them rather than adding more of the same.
  • Use the Sandbox to bring new creatives into core campaigns when they win. Then turn off the old one.

AI-Generated Creative

1 min
  • Meta may penalise or raise CPMs on AI-generated bottom-of-funnel ads uploaded directly. Bottom-of-funnel ads are aimed at people close to buying.
  • This happens because Meta prefers original content and reads image metadata (hidden file details).
  • If you make ads with ChatGPT, Claude or similar tools, upload the image to Canva and download it again before uploading to Meta. Canva is an online design tool.
  • The AI creative approach applies across product categories, including pet, fashion and pain relief.
  • The principles are not tied to one niche (one specific market).
  • So an advertiser can adapt them to any product.
  • A default AI answer (what a general-purpose AI says without special input), asked which resources to study for a system of effective Meta ad creative, relies mainly on Meta's own guidance.
  • It also relies on generic inputs such as reviews, the product itself, competitors and ROAS.
  • Generic sources like this do not reveal what actually works.
  • What actually works usually comes from testing over years.
  • Generic AI-made ads can lose reach on Meta. The quality bar in applies to every AI output before it goes live.

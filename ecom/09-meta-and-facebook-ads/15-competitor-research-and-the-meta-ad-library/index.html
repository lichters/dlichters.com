Competitor research and the Meta ad library

Section 9.15 · 15 of 22 in this chapter · 18 min read

In short

  • The free Meta Ad Library shows what your competitors run, how long each ad has run and what their offers look like.
  • An ad that has run for weeks or months is a good clue, but the library does not show sales, so never treat it as proof that a product sells.
  • Many identical ads from one brand, or many new ads in a short time, point to a winning product.
  • Ignore short tests: look for ads that have run at least 7 days.
  • Public numbers are estimates. Check them before you trust them.
More key points
  • Start with a list of 15 to 25 possible products.
  • Rank them by your own written rules, then test them one at a time.
  • Before testing, check how long each product's ads have run and where they run.
  • Pick each test's opening line using the product, the ad visuals and the ad text together.

Opening the Ad Library and searching

4 min
  • The Meta Ad Library is a free, public tool from Meta (the company that owns Facebook and Instagram). It lists the active ads (ads running right now) of any Facebook or Instagram page (a brand's public account), including video and image ads. It is sorted from newest to oldest by start date. Use it to study competitor creatives (the image or video plus the text of an ad), their offers and how long each ad has run.
  • It is the public archive of Facebook and Instagram ads (a stored record of ads, old and current). With the filters of a brand tool (settings that narrow the results), you can search it by country, language, niche (a product market or topic), media type (video or image), days running (how long an ad has run), page active ads (how many ads a page runs now) and ad creation date (when the ad was made).
  • Anyone can see every running ad. Sellers cannot remove their ads from it. So it shows competitors that third-party tools (outside software) may not show or may hide.
  • The library shows which ads run, their run dates and their creative angles (the main idea or approach of an ad). It does not show sales results. Some ads show no shop button or product details. Use it to study hooks (the first words or seconds that catch attention), formats and angles, not as proof that a product sells.
  • It does not show likes, comments or shares of an ad. Engagement (likes, comments and shares) must be checked another way. Check the advertiser's posts for high like counts, or use an ad spy tool (software that tracks other sellers' ads over time).
  • For most ads, the public library does not show CTR (click-through rate: the share of people who see an ad and click it) or spend (the money an advertiser puts into an ad). So filters by high CTR or high spend may not exist there. Judge candidates by visible signals such as engagement, comments and run length. Check which data fields the library actually displays before filtering.
  • It does not show which countries an advertiser targets (chooses to show ads to). Use other tools, or store and ad data, to find target countries.
  • Each ad card (the box that shows one ad's details) shows a Library ID (the unique number of each ad), the platforms it runs on (where it appears, such as Facebook or Instagram), the page name, the date it started, its running time and a line such as "3 ads use this creative and text."

Basic search procedure

  1. Open facebook.com/ads/library (or search "Facebook Ads Library").
  2. Select "All ads" from the dropdown menu (a list you open by clicking).
  3. Search for the competitor brand name or page name.
  4. If the page name does not appear in the dropdown, paste the competitor website URL (web address) instead.
  5. Click "View ads" to display all active or recent competitor ads.
  6. Record ad copy (the words in the ad) and creative patterns that repeat across 2 or more campaigns (groups of ads with one goal), since repetition indicates performance.
  7. Note the headline (the bold title line), the primary text structure (the main text above the image or video) and the opening hooks the competitor uses consistently.

Filter settings

  • Set Active status to Active ads so only currently running campaigns appear.
  • Set the Language filter to English (US) so results come from the English-speaking market you target.
  • When researching a US competitor, set the country to United States and the category to All ads.
  • The Active status filter is set to Active by default. Ads marked Inactive appear with that label. Set the filter to see only running ads, then read the Running Xd label (days the ad has been running) on each card.
  • Standard search settings: Active status Active; Ad type All ads; Country ALL; Media type All. Enter the product or niche keyword in the search field.
  • Use Show EU data for EU transparency information (information about ads shown in the EU) on an ad. Some ads carry a note that they are not active for some audiences because they did not meet certain requirements.
  • Use Save on a keyword search to return to the same list of competitor stores later.

Keyword searches for products

  • Enter one keyword at a time. Multiple keywords joined together are not supported.
  • Search word variations, such as singular and plural forms and synonyms (for example shoe, shoes, sneaker, sneakers), since different ads use different wording.
  • Filter by active ads, then review the results to find products being advertised.
  • A keyword search counts every ad that matches the word. The result number is broad and is not the exact count of one product's ads.

Verifying a tool's numbers

  1. Note the product's active ad count in the research tool.
  2. Open the same product in the Facebook Ads Library.
  3. Confirm that the listed ads are marked active and that the number matches.
  4. Check which countries the ads run in.
  5. Check the start date of the newest ad to confirm the product is still being tested or scaled (spending more on what works).

Verifying demand for a product

  1. Open the Meta Ad Library and search the product keyword or the brand's page name.
  2. Set Active status to Active ads and the country to United States.
  3. Check the Platforms icons to see where each ad runs.
  4. Note the Started running on date and the Library ID of each ad. Ads running for weeks or months are the strongest signal.
  5. Count the brand's active ads, then scroll to the bottom to see older tests.
  6. Open See ad details to read the full copy, the creative and the landing page link (the page a click opens).

Winning Hunter filters for the Meta Ad Library

2 min

  • Winning Hunter (an ad research tool) has a Meta Ad Library feature, labelled Meta Ads Library (recommended), which is the platform's main feature. Unlike ad spy tools that rank by likes and comments, it shows the ads themselves, so use it for ad research.

  • Filters, in order:

    1. Countries
    2. Language
    3. Video length (0s to 3600s)
    4. Niche
    5. Media type
    6. Page type
    7. Apps
    8. Themes
    9. Reach (Views) (how many different people saw an ad)
    10. Ad copy length (characters, 0 to 50000)
    11. Low Impressions (ads with few views)
    12. Days Running (0 to 365)
    13. Product Price Range (0 to 5000)
    14. Store Traffic (Monthly Visits) (how many visitors a store gets each month)
    15. Ad creation date
    16. Page Active Ads (0 to 10000)
    17. Last seen date
    18. Product/Collection Creation Date
    19. Page creation date

  • Further options: Sort By (Updated or Last Seen, the last day the tool saw the ad), Dropship Ads, Advertorials, Native ads, Quiz Funnels, Supplements, Top Branded, EU adspend, Save current filters and Reset. Use Save current filters for searches you run often.

  • Themes and Apps narrow pages by Shopify theme (the design template of a Shopify store; Shopify is a store-building platform) such as Dawn, Impulse or Impact, and by installed apps. Checkout integration filters such as Checkout Champ or Lasso/Whop checkout find stores that run a particular checkout provider (the service that handles payment on the checkout page).

  • A brand page has source switches for Meta Ads, Ad Library and Facebook Post Ads, plus a Global Ads or EU/UK Only toggle. Use EU/UK Only when the planned market is Europe or the UK, so regional ads are not mixed with global ones.

Filter minimums

Filter Value Purpose Source
Days running at least 7 days Removes short tests
Page active ads at least 200 Shows pages with scale
Ad creation date within the last 90 days Shows ads still working
Page Active Ads minimum 80 Finds large established competitors; the Checkout Champ filter returns fewer results with the same settings
Video length set a range Finds VSLs (video sales letters, long sales videos)
Ad copy length set characters Finds native ads (ads styled to look like normal content)

Finding newly launched Shopify pages

  1. Open the Page creation date filter and set the range to the last 30 days.
  2. Add the Shopify platform and the checkout filters you need, such as Apps or Themes.
  3. Sort by Last Seen and review the new pages that run ads.

Reading ad cards and detail views

  • An ad card shows EST. SPEND (estimated spend), REACH, the number of countries, active ads for the page and Est. Product Revenue (estimated sales from the product).
  • The ad detail view adds running days, duplicates (copies of the same ad run by other advertisers), format, gender, age range, first seen, last seen, landing page and estimated monthly traffic (visitors per month).
  • Quick actions: Find Competitors with, Visit Store, Page Ad Library, Download Video, Track Store, Fulfill with Ecomm..., Download Thumbnail and Transcribe ad with.... They move you from an ad to the store, its library page or its files in one step.

Tracking a competitor brand

2 min

Winning Hunter brand tracking procedure

  1. Copy the brand name or page name from the competitor's ad.
  2. Open Winning Hunter Brands, search for the name and click Follow.
  3. On the brand's page, use the View Page or Ad Library link to open the brand's Meta Ad Library page, and note the total results (130 in the example).
  4. Return to Brands and paste the Meta Ad Library link into the add-brand field so the tracker can gather the brand's live ads.
  5. Wait for the data to fill in, then review the brand's top hooks, angles and landing pages.

Brand tracker details

  • The Brands list shows each tracked brand's media mix (the share of video and image ads) as counts and percentages. It shows at a glance whether a competitor relies on video or static creatives (still images).
  • On a brand's detail page, the Global Ads and EU/UK Only switch limits the data to that scope. Visit Page opens the store page, Save saves the brand, and Ad Library opens its Meta Ad Library page. A Growth indicator shows whether the number of launched ads is Stable or changing.
  • Filter to active ads and read each ad's start date to judge how long it has run. A long run of active ads signals a working creative.
  • The Meta Ad Library, Pinterest Ads library and TikTok Ads library each show a competitor's active ads. Open the brand in Brand Tracker for its full ad set.
  • The Top Ads view ranks ads by ad rank (the tool's score for how well an ad performs) with up to 10 placements (the spots where an ad appears, such as feeds or stories). You can sort by Top Ad rank, Most Reach or Longest running. Rank #1 is the best, so a lower rank number means stronger performance.
  • On product cards, the number of active ads and the number of countries show how heavily a product is tested. A product with hundreds of active ads across dozens of countries has been tested far more than one with a handful, so start from the most heavily tested products.

Count discrepancies

  • The active-ad count in Winning Hunter can be lower than the total results on a Meta Ad Library page for the same advertiser. In the example, the library showed 130 results while Winning Hunter counted 124 active ads. Check both numbers when judging a competitor's scale.
  • A brand with around 130 active ads has likely validated its creatives and is a good reference. Use the ad count as a quick quality check.

Competitor research procedure

1 min
  1. Open the competitor's page in the Meta Ad Library.
  2. Review the active ads and filter for video ads to see the dominant format (the most common type of ad).
  3. Note each ad's hook, the problem it names, the proof it uses and the offer.
  4. Group the ads by angle, such as education, identity, social proof or offer.
  5. Save the strongest ads to a swipe board (a saved folder of example ads) for reference.
  • Use the Ad Library to see which ads competitors run, how long they have run and where the ads send people, such as an advertorial (an ad written like an article) or a product page.
  • Look at ad copy and video or image together when judging a winner. The Ad Library shows both the product and the winning creative, so it can reveal which hook or angle to copy with the product.
  • Set a list of 15 to 25 potential products from the Ad Library, then rank them by your defined product criteria.
  • Test products one by one. Some are tested by only one or two sellers and are not yet covered by spy tools.
  • Set a starting age range with an ad spy tool:
    1. Look up the product in the ad spy tool.
    2. Note the age range that competing advertisers target.
    3. Use that range only as a reference point for your own test.
    4. Keep the test broad, for example 18 to 65+, and narrow the age range only after sales data shows which group performs best.

Signals of a winner

5 min

Run time

Run time Reading Source
Under two weeks Weaker signal of success
7 days or more Filter floor that removes short tests
One month or more Likely profitable and worth studying
Weeks or months Stronger signal than a new ad; use as layout reference
Many months Strongest signal; study these first
  • An ad that keeps running for weeks or months is likely profitable, because advertisers do not keep paying for ads that lose money. Use run length as proof that the creative and offer work.
  • Long-running ads show which creatives the advertiser keeps. Check run dates and active ad counts on each ad card.

Duplicates and creative count

The thresholds below come from different sources and describe different things. Apply each where it fits; they are not interchangeable.

Count of ads or ad sets with the same creative and text Reading Source
Same copy in 2 or more campaigns at once Copy is performing; the advertiser would not keep investing otherwise
10 or more ads Likely in the scaling phase; product probably works
10 to 15 ads by one brand Creative is usually being scaled
13 or more ad sets with identical text Clear winner; treat as proof rather than a guess
15 or more ads Winning creative; sellers do not repeat weak creatives this often
  • The Ad Library count reflects how many ad sets (groups inside a campaign that share an audience and settings) use a creative, because one ad set cannot run the same ad twice. The campaign structure is campaign, then ad set, then ad (one creative inside an ad set). The count of ad sets is the signal to read.
  • Sellers usually scale a single creative only after the product has become profitable. Many identical ads from one advertiser point to a proven winner.
  • Duplicates on an ad show how many other advertisers use the same creative. Several duplicates mean the product or angle is already being copied.
  • A large number of active ad variations for the same product, and the same video running repeatedly over a long period, is a strong sign of profit. Repeated runs show the advertiser keeps spending because it returns money.

Active ad counts and scaling

  • Track how many active ads a competitor runs over several days. A jump, for example from about 50 to about 100 active ads in one day, signals many new launches and scaling. A competitor usually scales only a profitable product, so many new ads on one product is a strong signal.
  • Count active ads per single creative, not the total on the page. A page can show around 500 active ads overall while one creative runs only about 31. Only the per-creative count shows real scaling.
  • A competitor who launches new ads every day is actively scaling, which is a stronger signal than a static number. Check the start dates of the newest ads to see whether launches continue.
  • A competitor starting many new ads in a short time often signals a funnel (the path from ad to sale) being tested.
  • In the 2025 example, a brand with about 71 active ads that started in early July was still testing. The main brand behind the same product had about 650 active ads and was clearly scaling.
  • Large page counts are a filter tool: 200 active ads minimum for a page to show scale, 80 minimum to find large established competitors.

Page age

  1. Search the page name, or the store URL if the name does not turn up the page.
  2. Open one of the page's ads, then go to the About section to read the creation date.
  3. Read the page creation date in the About section, then open Page transparency (the page's info panel in the library) to see its ad activity.
  • A page created within the last few months that already runs many ads signals a new competitor testing a trend. An old page with a warmed pixel (a tracking code that has collected visitor and buyer data for a long time) and large purchase history gets better delivery and is harder to beat.
  • An older page is a stronger sign of success, but a new page with well-performing ads can still be worth testing.
  • A competitor whose page was created recently has not yet built a large purchaser audience (the people who have bought, tracked by the pixel) in its pixel, so a new entrant starts on roughly equal footing. This is a useful plus but not a mandatory requirement.

Still running

To check whether an ad is still running:

  1. Open the ad in the Ad Library.
  2. Set the comments sort to Newest.
  3. Look for comments from the last 24 to 48 hours. If comments still arrive, the ad is probably running and likely profitable.
  • Check that a competitor's ad is active before deciding to test its product.
  • To check a competitor's ad activity, open facebook.com/ads/library, select All, then All ads, and search for the page name. The results show that page's active ads.
  • The Facebook Ad Library lists a competitor's active ads and run dates, which shows how a store is scaling on Facebook.
  • For a product, check three things before deciding whether to test it: the active ad count, the date the ads started and the countries they run in.
  • The Facebook Ad Library is the basis for counting how many ads a competitor runs for one product.

Engagement

  • Shares are the most useful engagement signal on a competitor's ad. Likes and comments can be bought cheaply, while shares are hard to fake. A high share count is stronger proof of concept (evidence that a product idea can work) than many likes alone.
  • Many ads have comments turned off, so few comments or likes do not necessarily mean weak demand. Check sales data on AliExpress (a supplier marketplace) or other sources before judging a product by ad engagement.

Spend and reach estimates

  • Winning Hunter ad cards show an estimated spend and estimated reach for each ad. Read the two together to see which ads a competitor keeps funding, but treat both as estimates, not the advertiser's real spend.

Market signals

  • Competitor videos with sold-out messages, strong urgency or call-to-action text (a line that tells people what to do, such as "buy now") often indicate paid ads. Check the store page for ad-style elements to confirm.
  • When many competing stores run ads for the same product at once, do not assume the market is closed. A market can support several stores; only giant brands such as Apple or Amazon are real exceptions.
  • Political advertisers (ads about politics, which have their own rules) cannot switch ads off freely, so an active political ad does not prove profitability. Use active status and run duration as a profit signal only for ordinary, non-political ads.

Copying cautions

1 min
  • Copying a competitor's ad exactly does not reproduce its results, because their pixel data, audience (the people an ad is shown to) and timing differ from yours. The variables you control are your creative and your landing page.
  • Competitors who seem to succeed with simple drop-shipping videos (selling products that a supplier ships to the buyer) often have an older pixel with accumulated data, or they launched when competition for the product was low. Running the same video without that data or timing usually produces nothing.
  • Copying competitor ads and stores is a valid starting point for beginners but not a guaranteed success. If copying fails, change the angle (different problem, audience or context) rather than repeating the same creative.
  • If a copied ad or store does not sell, possible causes include the competitor having a longer-running pixel with more data, entering the market earlier, or an audience saturated (too many sellers already selling to them) with the product. The response is to test a new angle, not repeat the same copy.
  • Replicating winning creatives is a useful shortcut, and the winner can be adapted with a local voice-over (a spoken track over the video) or local captions (on-screen text in the local language) for a new market.
  • Launch new concepts with the competitor's ad only as a shortcut, then move on to your own content.

Ripping creatives

1 min

Ripping images from long-running ads

  1. Open the Meta Ad Library and search for the competitor page with the most active ads.
  2. Set Active status to Active ads.
  3. Set Media type to Images and memes.
  4. Set the Impressions by date range longer if older ads should be included, then Apply.
  5. Sort by total impressions (how many times the ads were shown).
  6. Open each ad and note its Started running on date.
  7. Right-click the image and save it to the desktop, or inspect the page and copy the image address when saving is not possible.

Video ripping

  • Upload downloaded videos directly to Meta Ads Manager (Meta's tool for running your own ads) without any editing. The same clean files can also be uploaded to TikTok Ads.
  • The time from ripping (downloading a copy of an ad's image or video) a video to launching the ad is about one to two hours. This speed is a main reason to use ripped creatives for early tests.
  • Facebook is stricter than TikTok about reused video. Replace copied videos with your own content as soon as a concept is proven, and use copied material only for short tests of one to two days.

Choosing which layouts to copy

  • Large established brands can run plain image ads and still sell, because brand recognition, customer lifetime value (how much one customer buys over time), referrals (recommendations from others) and organic demand (buyers who come without paid ads) do much of the work. A new store cannot rely on that. It should copy the structure of ads that perform, not the simplicity of big-brand ads.
  • Use long-running ads as layout references when building new creatives.

Choosing products from the research

1 min
  1. Build a list of 15 to 25 potential products from the Ad Library (Meta's public archive of ads, live and past).
  2. Rank the products by your defined product criteria (your own written rules for what makes a good product).
  3. Check these four things for each product, using the signals above.
    • Run length: how many days the ads have been running.
    • Active ad counts: how many ads are live right now.
    • Creative counts: how many different ad visuals (creatives) and videos exist.
    • Countries: where the ads are shown.
  4. Test the products one by one.
  5. Use the Ad Library's product, creative and copy (the ad's written text) data together when choosing the hook for each test. A hook is the first line or first few seconds that grabs attention.

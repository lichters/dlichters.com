Playbook › 15. Email and SMS

Browse abandonment, manual cart recovery and personal follow-up

Section 15.8 · 8 of 17 in this chapter · 12 min read

In short

  • Call first, then text when a customer leaves a cart unpaid. Keep the message personal.
  • Use many channels together: email, ads, texts and calls. Do not rely on one.
  • Track every unpaid cart in one sheet, and check your recovery rate.
  • Never fake who sends a message. A personal tone is fine.
  • Keep the standard reminder email running. Never replace it with a call or video.

Tracking abandoned carts (unpaid shopping baskets) and recovery results

1 min
  • Log every abandoned cart in one sheet, so you can measure recovery (winning the sale back).
Date Name Phone Number Email Cart Value Products Shipping To Recovered? Cart Link Notes
Abandonment date Customer name Contact phone Customer email $ amount Item list Destination Yes/No Cart URL Recovery method & result
  • Fill in every column for each abandoned cart, so you track recovery steps the same way every time.
  • Recovery rate = recovered carts (rows marked Yes) divided by all abandoned carts.
  • Use the recovery rate to check how well your campaigns work. A campaign is a set of emails sent to a group of people. The rate also shows where to improve.

Multi-touch (many contact points) recovery strategy

1 min
  • Abandoned checkout links (links that take the customer back to their unpaid order), automated email sequences (emails a system sends in a set order), SMS notifications (text messages), retargeting ads (ads shown again to people who already visited your store) and phone calls within minutes work together as one multi-touch strategy, not as alternatives.
  • Do not rely on Slack alerts alone. Slack is a team chat app. Send Slack alerts to sales staff, and also keep automated emails, retargeting ads and SMS (where legally permissible, meaning allowed by law) running for multi-channel reach (many channels at once).
  • Sources differ on SMS (text messages):
    • The older 2021 workflow used no SMS app (no software that sends texts for you). Staff call, then send the text by hand.
    • The current multi-channel rule adds SMS only where it is legally permissible.
    • Where no SMS tool (software that sends texts for you) is used, follow the manual call-and-text workflow below. Where SMS is legally permissible, add it to the multi-channel mix.

Staff alerts and lead notifications

2 min

A lead is a person who showed interest, for example by leaving an email address.

Internal notification (a message only your team sees) for manual lead follow-up (contacting leads by hand). Subject means the title line of an email. Body means the main text of an email.

To: [your email address]
Subject: 'new lead - [created at timestamp]'
Body: 'new lead created - [customer email address]'
  • Check the notifications every day, or per cycle (each set period you choose), and contact new subscribers (people who signed up for your emails) by hand.
  • Put created_at in the subject line. created_at is a data field that stores the time a lead arrived. This adds the timestamp of each lead's arrival. It makes it easy to sort and batch outreach (contact leads in groups) by day and hour.

Build a Gmail-to-Slack triage automation (an automatic flow that sorts Gmail messages and sends the important ones to Slack) in Zapier (a tool that links apps so they run on their own):

  1. Trigger on incoming Gmail messages. A trigger is the event that starts the flow. Authenticate the account (log in and allow access), then test the connection.
  2. Add an AI by Zapier step using the GPT-4 mini model (a type of AI model).
  3. Give the model a system prompt (hidden instructions that tell the AI how to behave) that classifies emails as 'REPLY' (legitimate messages needing action) or 'DON'T REPLY' (spam and promotional content). Pass the email body to the AI as context (reference text it reads).
  4. Validate the classification (check that the AI sorts correctly) by generating test output.
  5. Insert a Filter by Zapier step (a gate that only lets some results through) that passes only 'REPLY' results forward.
  6. Connect the filtered output to a Slack direct message (a private chat message to one person). Build the body from sender address, subject line and email body using variable insertion (placing data fields into the message automatically).
  7. Publish the automation to activate it.
  8. Optional: insert an AI step before Slack to generate draft responses (unsent reply texts). Then add a Gmail draft creation step (saves a reply in Gmail without sending it) to auto-populate response emails.

Older abandoned-cart alert workflow (2021):

  1. A Slack notification alerts staff to an abandoned cart.
  2. Staff call the customer.
  3. If there is no answer, staff send a text manually. No SMS app is used.

First contact with exit-intent pop-ups (boxes shown as a visitor leaves) and new leads

1 min
  • Follow up by hand with every email address captured (collected) through an exit-intent overlay (a pop-up shown when a visitor is about to leave) in the early stage. This builds first relationships. It turns website visitors into customers, who would otherwise be lost.
Hi [Customer Name], I'm [Your Name], owner of [Store Name]. I noticed you were checking out on our site recently and didn't complete your purchase. I'd love to know if there was an issue I can help with or if you have any questions-please feel free to respond to this email or call me directly at [Your Phone Number]. I'd really appreciate the chance to earn your business. Thanks!

Phone call, voicemail (a recorded message left when no one answers) and text sequence

1 min

Follow-up sequence (steps in a set order) for a customer who started checkout (the payment steps) but did not finish:

  1. Call the customer first.
  2. If there is no answer, leave a voicemail.
  3. About 10 minutes later, send a short, personal text by hand, with no spam or marketing tone.
  4. If the customer replies, continue the conversation. If not, stop without pressure.
Hi [customer name], this is [your name] from [store name]. I noticed you started to check out but did not finish your order. If you have any questions, I am standing by and happy to help. Just reply to this text and I will get back to you.
  • A personal text to an abandoned cart can do better than a generic (one-size-fits-all) abandoned-cart email. Use individual texts for high-value carts (carts worth a lot of money). Keep automated email sequences running alongside them.
  • Personal texts read like a real person reaching out. They earn more replies than automated messages. Make each text reflect that customer's actual situation rather than a fixed formula.
Hi {customer_name}, it's {your_name} from {store_name}. I noticed you have been looking at the {product_name} for a while, so I wanted to check in personally. Do you still want it, or is there anything I can answer for you?
  • Many customers prefer texts to calls, so texting is often more effective than calling, especially for owners who are not comfortable on the phone.
  • Use calls when a customer calls you first and you can pick up.
  • The sequence starts with a call, then a text if the lead does not answer.
  • Texts are the second step after an unanswered call.
  • If the owner is not comfortable on the phone, texting first is the stronger option.

Loom video follow-up for abandoned carts

2 min

Loom is a tool that records your screen and webcam (camera) into a shareable link.

  • When a lead asks a detailed product question, reply with a short Loom video of 30 to 60 seconds showing your screen and webcam. The personal video builds trust and turns leads into buyers.
  • Phone calls and texts are often ignored, because many people do not want to answer unknown calls.
  • A video sent by email gives a second route to a reply. That reply does not depend on the lead picking up.
  • A follow-up that does not show or name the exact product the lead left in the cart loses most of its personal effect.
  • Always pull up that product before recording.
  • Keep each Loom about 30 seconds to 1 minute. The goal is only to start a conversation.
  • The standard sequence stays in place: phone call first, then text if the lead does not answer. The standard abandoned-cart reminder email still goes out.
  • The Loom video is added on top of this sequence. It does not replace it.

Personalized Loom follow-up procedure:

  1. Open the products the lead left in the cart on your store, so they are visible on screen.
  2. Open Loom on your computer (Chrome extension, meaning an add-on for the Chrome browser, or desktop app).
  3. Turn on your webcam, which adds personalization.
  4. Allow microphone access (permission for Loom to use your mic).
  5. Select screen share (show your screen to the viewer).
  6. Start the recording, then speak the personal message while showing the cart products.
  7. Stop the recording.
  8. Copy the link that Loom generates. It is a loom.com link.
  9. Paste the link into a follow-up email with a personal greeting and an invitation to reply with questions. Then send it.

Email body for the Loom follow-up:

Subject: A quick video for you, [First Name]

Hi [First Name],

I noticed you were recently on [Store Name] and added [Product Name] to your cart, but you have not completed your order yet. I am the owner of [Store Name], so I recorded a short video for you, about [30 to 60] seconds, to say hello and check whether anything stopped you.

[Paste Loom link here]

If something held you back, such as a question, a delivery date you need, or a discount you were hoping for, just reply to this email. I am happy to help and to do what it takes to earn your order.

Thank you,
[Your Name]
[Store Name]

Spoken script for the video (keep it between 30 seconds and 1 minute):

Hi [First Name], I'm [Your Name], the owner of [Store Name]. I noticed you were recently on our website and added [Product Name] to your cart, but you did not finish checking out. I wanted to reach out personally and see whether there was a specific reason you did not complete the order, such as a question, a delivery date you need, or a discount you were hoping for. Just reply to the email this video came with, and I am happy to help and do what it takes to earn your business.
  • Offer a discount in a follow-up only if the store can actually afford it.
  • Do not promise a discount in the video or email just to get a reply.

Plain-text (simple, no design) and personal-looking emails

1 min

A template is a ready-made email design.

  • Some automated emails can go out as plain text with no designed template. They look more personal and get a higher response.
  • This format is used for a post-second-purchase flow (the emails sent after a customer's second order) and for abandoned-cart emails.
  • A plain-text email signed as sent from a phone is reported to work well.
  • But it misrepresents the sender. It gives a false picture of who sends it.
  • Check the legal and platform risk (the rules of law and of the sales site or app you use) before using it. Avoid it if it could be seen as misleading.
  • Rewrite automated messages so they read as if a person wrote them, not like a generic template.
  • Personal wording gets far more replies and thanks.
  • The personal tone is the goal.
  • Use it without a false sender identity (a fake name or fake sender).
  • A plain-text personal style is acceptable.
  • A signature that implies a phone-sent message needs the legal and platform check first.

Older guidance (2021), for visitors who sign up for an email coupon (a discount code sent by email) but do not buy:

  1. Check that the welcome emails are arriving.
  2. Check that the discount code is active and works at checkout.
  3. Send personal one-off emails (single emails, not bulk) to those leads. Ask whether they have questions before ordering.
  4. Use the replies to learn what is stopping them.

Older guidance (2022), a basic re-engagement email (an email to win back interest) for everyone who opted in (agreed to get emails) but has not bought. Send it to the whole list (every subscriber):

Hi [first name], are you still interested in [product name]? Reply yes or no.
  • Anyone who replies no is removed from the conversation.
  • Anyone who replies yes receives a manual follow-up (a personal email sent by hand) to close the sale.

Older procedure (2022), recovering abandoned carts with email:

  1. Open Shopify (online store software) and pull the list of abandoned carts.
  2. Send each customer a short personal question asking whether they are still interested in the product.
  3. Follow up manually with anyone who replies yes.

Re-engagement (winning back interest) and follow-up angles (new reasons to act)

1 min
  • Use email campaigns to re-engage visitors who did not buy on the first visit.
  • Email is the follow-up step after the first ad conversion (an ad visitor who buys) fails.
  • A follow-up should continue the same argument that created the interest.
  • A new pitch (a new sales message) weakens the impression already made.
  • Each follow-up must bring a new angle (a different approach) and a fresh reason to act.
  • Repeating the same letter brings steadily lower response.
  • Change the offer, proof or emotional appeal every time. Proof means evidence that it works. An emotional appeal is a message aimed at feelings.
  • Old unsold leads (people who never bought) can be a low-cost source of orders.
  • A follow-up to leads that salesmen had turned down still produced 4.5 percent orders when mailed with a new letter.
  • After several follow-ups bring little response, stop mailing that list and file the leads (put them aside) rather than sending more of the same.
  • Send a final mop-up letter (a last clean-up message to reach people who did not answer). It must be clearly different from the earlier ones. Send it before stopping.
  • Older retargeting discount pattern (2021-22). Retargeting means reaching people again after they visited. When a customer viewed a specific product but did not buy, email a large, clearly stated percentage discount on that same product.
    • Formula (a fixed recipe): product name, discount as a headline percentage, and regular price reference.
    • Why it works: the customer already showed interest. A big saving on a known product removes the main objection (the reason to say no), which is price.
    • Example one: "The jacket you loved: 25 percent off today", with the product image and the new price.
    • Example two: "Still thinking about the desk lamp? Save 20 percent", with a button linking to that product.

Backorder (order now, ship later) and stock-status (is it in stock) messages

1 min
  • Use automatic emails for out-of-stock status and expected delivery times. Customers then know what to expect without contacting support.
  • On the product page, near add-to-cart (the button that puts an item in the shopping basket), state: "Currently backordered with ETA [specific date]". ETA means estimated time of arrival (the expected date).
  • When the customer buys, email them to confirm the order and the ETA.
  • Text customers about stock discrepancies (gaps between the stock the store shows and the stock really on hand) or back-in-stock dates.
  • This keeps them informed and makes them more likely to order anyway.
  • A rough back-in-stock date by itself can lead to an order.

Confirmation email sent right after a customer buys a backordered product. Replace the bracketed placeholders (the fields in square brackets) before sending:

Subject: Your order [ORDER NUMBER] is backordered

Hi [FIRST NAME],

Thank you for your order [ORDER NUMBER]. The item [PRODUCT NAME] is currently backordered [OPTIONAL: due to REASON] and is expected back in stock on [EXACT DATE]. All orders ship first come, first served, and your order is on the list.

Please reply to confirm that you are willing to wait. If you would rather not wait, reply to this email and we will cancel your order for you.

Thank you for your patience,
[STORE NAME] team
  • After someone buys a backordered product, send a follow-up email confirming they saw the out-of-stock note and the ETA on the product page.
  • This protects against disputes (arguments) about delivery timing.

Post-Purchase, transactional and retention flows

Section 15.9 · 9 of 17 in this chapter · 41 min read

In short

  • Send a thank-you or order email right after the purchase. Use it to ask a question, ask for feedback, or make an offer.
  • Send first-time buyers and repeat buyers different emails. Never send them the same one.
  • Tell buyers the shipping time in the order confirmation. This cut about 70 percent of the store's customer emails.
  • Good service comes first. Repeat sales only come from customers who got good service.
  • Choose a starting timing for offers, then test and adjust it.
More key points
  • After a sale, ask the buyer one simple question: why did they buy?
  • Send extra offers (upsells) in four rounds: right after the sale, then at 15 to 30 days, 60 days and 120 days. Each round has 3 to 4 emails.
  • Ask for reviews about 14 days after purchase, never before the product arrives.
  • Remind customers about your loyalty and referral programs at least every three months.
  • Send a win-back email to buyers who have not bought for a while, with a discount.
  • Start a win-back only after your store has given good service.
  • After a sale, show your own stores first, then affiliate offers.
  • Give each main product its own email series, started by its own purchase.
  • Sources differ and many are older (2020 to 2022). Pick the rule that fits what you measure.

Core Principles of the Post-Purchase Flow

3 min
  • Transactional emails (automatic emails about an order) have extremely high open and click rates (how often people open and click them). They are a good place to make an offer, ask a question or request feedback.
  • Order and shipping confirmations (automatic emails that confirm an order and its dispatch) inform rather than persuade, so they do not need copywriting (sales wording written to convince).
  • Admin emails (service emails, such as order updates) are not copy emails (not written as sales messages). Their main purpose is service. An upsell (an offer for a bigger or extra product) can be added as copy if needed.
  • Shipping notifications (emails sent while a parcel moves) have very high open rates because customers are waiting for their parcel.
  • Shipping notifications are valuable for cross-selling (offering related products) and for building trust.
  • Default templates (standard ready-made messages) can be replaced with personal, friendly wording once the system works.
  • A post-purchase flow (a set of automatic emails sent after a sale) after the first order serves three purposes. It confirms the brand is legitimate, keeps the buyer informed about the order, and creates room for upsell and cross-sell offers.
  • Keep the message personal and include a link to the brand's social media profiles to grow followers.
  • After the first purchase, give customers time to use what they bought.
  • Then move them gradually up the value ladder (a path from cheaper products to more expensive ones) over a longer period.
  • The funnel type (the path a customer follows to buy) changes over time. For example, it can move from a free-plus-shipping buyer (someone who gets a product free and pays only for shipping) into an invisible funnel (a hidden sales path) or a two-step high-ticket backend program (an expensive offer sold after the first purchase).
  • Relationship building matters more than the first transaction.
  • Repeat purchases (buying again) only come when the store has delivered good service.
  • Without good service, announcing new products to past buyers by email and SMS (text messages) will not produce repeat sales.
  • Plan post-purchase sequences (planned series of messages) in advance.
  • Map the path from one purchase to the next: what to communicate, which next product offer fits, and when to make it.
  • Each product purchased should trigger (start) its own post-purchase sequence, different from the sequences for other products.
  • After a purchase, segment customers (sort them into groups) by what they bought and when they bought it.
  • Then send related product or service offers that bring them back and increase lifetime value (the total money a customer spends over time).
  • Use email to bring existing customers back at key moments, such as holidays and new product releases that match their interests.
  • This is part of the post-purchase funnel, not only of the remarketing funnel (ads or emails aimed at people who visited but did not buy).
  • Split the post-purchase flow into two tracks (separate paths): one for new customers and one for returning customers.
  • The post-purchase flow is aimed at customers who have already bought.
  • It offers a discount to encourage a further purchase, so it is a repeat-purchase flow, not a flow for new visitors.
  • Timing between post-purchase offers can be set at two weeks, one month or two months.
  • Choose a starting interval and adjust it through experimentation (testing and changing), because the right timing is not known in advance.
  • Reply emails (emails where customers write back) collect buyer insight (what buyers think and want).
  • Reply emails can also produce sales at the same time. Use them with buyers and with people who have not yet purchased.
  • Transactional emails can invite replies that reveal why a visitor did not buy, or gather feedback from customers who did.
  • Replies are a low-cost way to learn about objections (reasons people hesitate) and product satisfaction.

Order Confirmation and Shipping Confirmation

2 min
  • Send the order confirmation immediately after purchase.
  • Then send a separate shipping confirmation when the order ships, typically within minutes to about an hour.
  • Both should trigger (start) automatically from order and fulfillment events (steps such as an order placed or an order shipped).
  • Both are good places to add an offer, a question or a request for feedback.
  • Order confirmation steps:
    1. Send the order confirmation right after purchase.
    2. Send the shipping confirmation when the order ships.
    3. About three days after the customer receives the product, email them to ask how the product is going, whether they like it, whether they are happy, and whether they have questions.
    4. Invite them to hit reply to every one of these emails so they can answer.
  • Send one email immediately after a customer's first purchase, not an order confirmation.
  • Use it to present the next offer while the purchase is still fresh: a higher-priced item, a discount, a warranty (a promise to repair or replace) or a consultation.
  • Order confirmation text to adapt (a ready message you change to fit your store):
Thank you for your order. Items from [Brand or supplier A] are usually shipped within [X to Y] business days. Items from [Brand or supplier B] are usually shipped within [X to Y] business days. You will receive tracking updates by email as your order moves, and our team is here if you need anything.
  • Adding the expected shipping time for each brand to the order confirmation email eliminated about 70 percent of the store's customer emails.
  • This works because the customer gets the answer before they need to ask.
  • Show your own niche store offers (offers for one small, specific market) after a sale, through the order confirmation email and the tracking notification emails.
  • Showing them to visitors through Facebook retargeting (ads that follow people who visited before) before purchase is a gray area (not clearly allowed or banned). It sometimes works and sometimes does not, so test it per niche.

Shipping Expectations, Tracking and Delivery Alerts

2 min
  • Post-purchase shipping update flow, in this order:
    1. Connect the tracking app (AfterShip or similar, a tool that follows parcels) to Klaviyo (the email tool).
    2. Create a trigger email for the order-shipped status. A trigger is an event that starts an email.
    3. In the email, give the shipping update, the delivery timeframe (for example within a set number of days) and 2 to 3 lines of benefit-driven text (wording about what the product does for the buyer).
    4. Add a link to another collection (a group of products) with an extra discount for the customer's next order while they wait for delivery.
  • Shipping update emails put the customer in a positive mood because they confirm the order is moving and the store is trustworthy.
  • This is the moment to pitch (make a sales offer for) another product or collection with an extra discount, since the customer is most concerned about tracking.
  • The best offers work well inside fulfillment update emails (emails about packing and delivery progress).
  • So add offers to shipping and delivery updates.
  • Proactive delivery alerts (warnings sent before the customer asks) by SMS, Klaviyo, WhatsApp or another channel protect the customer experience.
  • Telling shoppers about a one-day delay early avoids the frustration of a late order with no update.
  • Klaviyo flows (automatic email sequences) can fire (start) on delivered and sent events from order tracking.
  • Each event is an extra touchpoint (a moment of contact) for upsells and cross-sells.
  • Proactive delivery delay SMS or email template (replace the placeholders, the blanks in curly braces). Send it as soon as the delay is known, before the customer asks where the parcel is:
Hi {first_name}, a quick update on order {order_number}. Your parcel is running about {delay_length} behind schedule. Your new estimated delivery date is {new_date}. You can follow it here: {tracking_link}. Thanks for your patience.
  • Add the same cross-promotion block (a box that promotes a store offer) to the AfterShip tracking notification templates.
  • Then every tracking update email also shows the related store offer.
  • AfterShip sends a customer email each time UPS, FedEx or another carrier (delivery company) updates the tracking status.
  • A customer who buys typically receives around four or five AfterShip tracking emails.
  • So the cross-promotion is shown several times at no extra cost.
  • The number depends on how many carrier tracking updates each shipment produces.
  • The Aftership delivery trigger only works if Aftership is installed and connected to Shopify (the online store software).
  • Without it, the flow must use the Ordered Product trigger (a trigger that starts when a product is bought) with a time delay instead.

Structure and Triggers

1 min
  • Thank-you flow trigger: Placed Order (the event that fires when a customer buys), with the first email sent 1 day after the order.
  • A first-time buyer sees the thank-you email after one day, using the condition order equals 1 (a rule that checks how many orders the customer has) to separate first purchases from repeat purchases.
  • Repeat customers get a separate email, so the two groups never receive the same message.
  • Split first-time and repeat buyers in the thank-you flow with a condition: Placed Order count equals 1 (all time) for first-time buyers.
  • Use a separate branch (a separate path) for repeat customers.
  • Sending the same thank-you email to first-time buyers and repeat buyers is a mistake.
  • Split them with a placed-order count condition and write a separate message for each group.
  • Thank-you flow structure:
    • Email 1 is sent one day after the order to first-time buyers.
    • A second email follows roughly two days later.
    • A third email can be added for returning customers.
    • Use a personalized message with a link to social media to gain followers.
    • Set all emails to Live (sent to customers) with Smart Sending (a Klaviyo setting that can delay emails) off and UTM tracking (tags added to links to measure clicks) on.
  • Post-purchase discount emails are spaced about 24 hours apart after the order is placed.
  • Email 1 goes out at once, email 2 about 24 hours later, and email 3 about 48 hours later.
  • Send the thank-you email separately as soon as the order is placed, so the discount sequence does not delay it.

Revenue and Tone

1 min
  • To earn extra revenue from thank-you emails, add a gift card (a prepaid voucher the customer can spend).
  • To earn extra revenue from thank-you emails, add a discounted offer for other products.
  • Two thank-you messages can differ greatly.
  • A message that thanks the customer in corporate language (formal company wording) feels distant.
  • A message in which the writer says they personally took the order, showed it to the company president and is grateful the customer chose them feels genuine and builds more warmth.
  • Use the first-person version (written as "I" or "we") for customer messages.
  • List VIP perks (special benefits for top buyers) in the thank-you email only if the store runs a real program that delivers every perk listed.
  • Perks such as discounts, early access and promo alerts (messages about sales) must all be real.
  • If the store does not have such a program, remove the list and keep a plain thank-you with the order tracking link.

Post-Purchase Discounts for the Next Order

1 min
  • The post-purchase flow has three emails offering 15% storewide (on the whole store) on the next cart (the basket a customer fills before paying).
  • The three emails have deadlines of last 3 days, last 48 hours and last 24 hours.
  • The third email states that the offer ends at midnight.
  • A separate thank-you email runs alongside it.
  • The post-purchase discount applies to the customer's next order, so it lowers the profit (money left after costs) of a repeat sale.
  • Before setting the 15% level, calculate the margin (profit as a share of the price) on a typical repeat order.
  • This confirms the sale still pays.

Handwritten Notes, Postcards and Physical Thank-Yous

1 min
  • Select one primary thank-you method (your main way to say thanks).
  • The options are pre-recorded personalized video messages (videos recorded in advance), handwritten or typed letters, or branded postcards (cards with your logo) with personalized messages and social media sharing instructions.
  • Order postcards in bulk (a large batch) at minimal cost.
  • Handwrite a personalized message on the back alongside a business image, and include branding elements (logo, colors and name) and instructions for customers to share on social media.
  • Include your logo, web address, and a phone or email contact on every handwritten note.
  • Customers often photograph notes and share them on social media or in messages, and the contact details make it easy for others to reach the business.
  • Top repeat customers, such as those who had bought four or more pairs, also received handwritten cards from the team.
  • This made the VIP group feel personally valued and supported repeat purchases.

Email-Only Sequence

1 min
  • Send a thank-you email after purchase, then a few days later ask why the customer bought, whether the product is helping, and whether it arrived in good condition.
  • Move the customer onto the main or house list (your main email list).
  • Send helpful tips on using the product, such as how to get the most from it.
  • Use post-purchase emails that resemble pre-purchase emails (emails sent before buying) to stay on the customer's radar (in their mind).
  • Include other products to sell to them over time.
  • Sending one thank-you email after purchase and then forgetting the customer wastes the post-purchase opportunity.
  • Buyers should be moved onto the main list and receive useful content.
  • One example order-based sequence contained 72 emails.
  • Some subscribers have received a daily email for over two months, and others for a full year.
  • Post-purchase sequence built as a Klaviyo flow triggered by a purchase:
    1. Send an order confirmation with shipping info, a short company intro and a mission statement (a short note on why the company exists).
    2. Send an education email, such as how to install or safely use the product.
    3. Send an email with engaging content, such as a helpful video or blog post.
    4. Send a dispatch email (an "your order has left" email) with tracking details, then send coupon codes that the buyer can use or share with friends and family.
    5. Send three testimonial requests, one for text, one for a photo and one for a video.
    6. Move the customer into the main broadcast list for regular updates and promotions.

Set-Up Steps for the First Order

1 min
  1. Send one email immediately after a customer's first purchase, not an order confirmation.
  2. In that email, make the next offer: a direct upsell to the higher-priced item with a discount or warranty, a consultation booking (a scheduled advice call), or a quantity increase such as 3 or 6 units instead of 1.
  3. If the upsell does not convert (does not lead to a sale), test a store-wide discount on the first order with a short deadline, such as one hour.
  4. After a second purchase, send a plain-text email flow (emails with no fancy design) that restates the brand's core claims (main promises).
  5. Build the flow in Klaviyo so it runs automatically.
  • Build a post-purchase email flow that runs automatically.
  • After the first purchase, send an email to test an offer (for example, a full-store discount with a short deadline).
  • After a second purchase, send a plain-text email flow that reinforces the brand message.
  • The flows should repeat the brand's core claims in each step.

Full Four-Sequence Upsell Campaign Per Core Product

3 min

Upsell email setup for each core product, in order:

  1. Sent immediately after the first purchase of the core product: 3 to 4 emails sent automatically in a row.
  2. Sent 15 to 30 days post-purchase: the same 3 to 4 emails again, with minor variations.
  3. Sent 60 days post-purchase: the same 3 to 4 emails again, with minor variations.
  4. Sent 120 days post-purchase: the same 3 to 4 emails again, with minor variations.
  • A full upsell email campaign uses four sequences per core product.
  • The first is immediately after purchase.
  • The others are at 15 to 30 days, at 60 days and at 120 days after purchase.
  • Each sequence contains 3 to 4 emails.
  • Formula: automated upsell emails per core product = 4 sequences × 3 to 4 emails each = 12 to 16 emails.
  • Across several core products the total multiplies accordingly.
  • It looks like a large number but comes from copying the same emails.
  • In Klaviyo, the four sequences can be built as one flow per product with four email sets inside it.
  • Or they can be built as four separate flows.
  • Either structure works as long as the emails are sent at the right intervals.
  • Each upsell sequence needs a condition (an "if" rule) that removes the customer from the flow if they have already bought the upsell product.
  • This way they stop receiving offers for something they own.
  • The 15 to 30 day window is a good time to re-offer an upsell (offer it again).
  • The core product has usually been delivered and the customer has started using it.
  • Add-ons (extra parts) and bundles (items sold together) are easier to value at this point.
  • The 15 to 30 day window also fits customers whose budget was stretched at the first order.
  • After the next payday or a freed-up credit card limit, they may be able to afford the upsell.
  • The 60-day and 120-day sends reach customers who have had more time with the product.
  • Their opening email (the first email in that send) should reintroduce the same upsell with a slightly different angle.
  • Reusing the exact wording from the 15 to 30 day send makes later sends feel stale.
  • Creating the sequences efficiently: duplicate (copy) the first upsell flow four times.
  • Then change only the opening email of each copy slightly. For example, change the framing of the offer for the later send date.
  • The products and the rest of the emails stay the same.
  • Duplicating an upsell flow works differently in each email platform.
  • Check how your tool copies flows, sequences or individual emails before building the four timed sets.
  • Only the opening email of each copy needs small wording changes.
  • The upsell product and the rest of the emails stay the same.
  • Setting up the full set of duplicated upsell flows is quick.
  • It can be spread over several days or a month alongside other work instead of being done in one long session.
  • Most email recipients never open a given message.
  • Average open rates (the share of emails that get opened) sit around 20 to 30 percent.
  • So repeating the same upsell offer across several sends reaches different people each time.
  • This raises total conversion (the share of people who buy).
  • Sending an upsell offer only once, and not sending enough emails overall, is a common error in email marketing.
  • A single send misses the majority of buyers who never open it.
  • Repetition is a core marketing principle.
  • The same call to action (the request to act, such as "Shop now") can be repeated several times on a page or across messages without harm.
  • It must stay respectful and consistent.
  • Upsell email sequences should run alongside the store's regular customer emails.
  • Those regular emails keep the relationship going between upsell sends.
  • Together they keep the brand top of mind and make each later upsell offer feel more natural.

Offer Templates and Patterns

2 min

Post-purchase follow-up for a low-ticket part (a cheap item) that belongs to a high-ticket product (an expensive item), sent immediately after the first order:

Hi [First name], thanks for your order of [low-ticket item]. It is a part for [high-ticket product]. As a thank-you, we would like to offer you a free consultation with one of our experts. You can book it here: [calendar link]. [Your brand name]

Warranty reminder email (a promise to repair or replace), sent automatically when the warranty checkbox was not ticked at checkout (the payment step):

Hi [First name], you did not add the [warranty name] to your order of [product name]. You can still add it here: [link] and get 25% off the warranty within [time limit]. If you have questions, just reply to this email. [Your brand name]
  • Missed add-on reminder pattern: [Name], you did not add [the add-on that protects or completes the purchase] to your order.
  • Here is [a specific discount] to add it now, with a time limit and a reply option.
  • This works because it frames the upsell as a reminder of a missed item rather than a new sale.
    • Example 1: Hi Sam, you did not add the extended care plan to your standing desk order. Get 20% off within 48 hours: [link].
    • Example 2: Hi Maya, you did not add the spare filter set to your air purifier order. Add it today for 15% off: [link].

Cross-sell email for repeat buyers (customers who bought before), sent 7 to 14 days after delivery together with the dynamic product ad (an ad that shows the product itself). Pair product X with product Y in the product catalog (your list of products) before sending, so the ad and the email recommend the same item:

Subject line: 'Pairs well with your {product X name}'

Hi {first name}, thanks again for your order of {product X name}. Many customers who love it also pick {product Y name}, so we thought you would like to see it. Your code {coupon code from the first email} still works until {expiry date} for {discount}. See the pick here: {link}.
  • Post-purchase messages can add a discounted offer for other products to thank-you emails.

Digital Bonuses and Access Links

1 min

A digital bonus is a free extra file or page. An access link is the web address that opens it.

  • Delivering a digital bonus after purchase with Klaviyo:
  1. Create a flow in Klaviyo triggered by a purchase of the product.
  2. Add an email that sends the access link.
  3. Use a Google Drive link (a link to a file in Google's storage) to PDFs or an unlisted YouTube video or unlisted page as the link. Unlisted means hidden from search, so only people with the link can see it.
  4. Test the flow before going live. Test means trying it yourself first.

Review, Photo and Testimonial Requests

4 min

A review is a customer's rating and written opinion. A testimonial is a short customer quote.

Timing

  • Send post-purchase email sequences over 14 days requesting text reviews, product photos and videos.
  • Incentivize submissions (offer a reward for them) with discount codes, gift cards or physical gifts to increase participation.
  • The photo or review request should be sent a few days after the delivery email.
  • It should not be sent on the day the product arrives.
  • Waiting gives the customer time to use the product before being asked.
  • A time delay (the wait before a message is sent) that is shorter than the real delivery time sends the request before the product has arrived.
  • Set the delay from the actual average shipping time and add a few days on top.
  • Judge.me's default review request email is sent 14 days after purchase.
  • Judge.me is a review app (an app that collects product reviews).
  • So the buyer should already have received the product.
  • Review requests are also sent after repeat purchases.
  • So customers who buy again are asked for a review too.
  • This keeps the review base (the total pile of reviews) growing over time.
  • Contact customers about three months after purchase to ask for their experience with the product.
  • Collect quotes from several customers into one evergreen blog post. Evergreen means content that stays useful for a long time.
  • Customer setup showcase pages can be created by year and updated as customers submit photos or reviews.

Building the Review Flow in Klaviyo

Klaviyo review request flow (Testimonial ask), step by step:

  1. In Klaviyo, open Flows and create a new flow, for example named Testimonial ask.
  2. Under the trigger, choose the metric Ordered Product (filters are optional). A metric is an event type that Klaviyo tracks.
  3. Add a time delay after the trigger that matches the normal delivery time plus a buffer (an extra safety margin). For example, use 10 days when shipping takes about 7 days.
  4. Add an Email action after the delay.
  5. Edit the email in the drag-and-drop builder (an editor where you move blocks with the mouse). Set the sender name, the subject line (the email's title line), images and text. Or use the HTML builder (a code-based editor) if needed.
  6. Save the email. Set the flow status to Live (switched on for real customers), then click Save and exit.
  7. The flow then sends automatically in the background without further work.

Aftership-based delivery flow (Aftership is a delivery tracking app):

  1. Install and connect Aftership to the Shopify store.
  2. In Klaviyo, open the prebuilt delivery flow that is triggered when a customer receives delivery via Aftership. It loads pre-built emails.
  3. Edit the flow so the photo or review email goes out a few days after the delivery email.
  4. Save the flow and set it to Live.
  • A flow that is saved but not set to Live never sends anything.
  • Always switch the flow to Live before saving and exiting.
  • In a Klaviyo flow with a time delay, each new buyer enters the flow on the trigger.
  • Each buyer then waits in a queue (a line) for the full delay before the email is sent.
  • One flow therefore handles every order automatically, with no manual sending per customer.
  • Klaviyo's built-in post-purchase flow can serve as a starting template for review request emails.
  • It can be edited to match the store's wording and timing.
  • Sending review requests through Klaviyo rather than through an app's automated review emails keeps the whole sequence in one place.
  • Klaviyo automates review requests and follow-up emails once the business has enough orders to justify automation.
  • Start with manual personal follow-up before automating.

Incentives

  • A reward for leaving a review must be automatically generated and tied to the review.
  • This stops the same customer from picking up the same incentive repeatedly.
  • Set the incentive in the review app's settings, not handled manually after each order.

To encourage photo reviews without offering a free product:

  1. Send a gift card, typically $25 or $50, that the buyer can use on the next purchase.
  2. Alternatively, send a small niche-related gift (a gift that fits your small, specific market) worth about $20 to $50.
  3. You can drop-ship it (the supplier sends it straight to the buyer) along with the order, if it is worth the cost.

Review Apps (Judge.me and Junip)

  • Judge.me and Junip are review apps.
  • Send product review requests through Klaviyo rather than through Judge.me's automated review emails.
  • Judge.me's emails are not a deal breaker (not a reason to stop) if the store is already happy with them.
  • Judge.me sends an automatic review request email to buyers.
  • It can be edited in the email template section with the built-in editor.
  • Keep the review request turned on so buyers are asked for reviews after they receive their order.
  • Judge.me sends a confirmation email to a customer after they leave a review.
  • It is part of the email templates and can be edited the same way.

Post-review actions flow (a flow that starts when a review is sent):

  1. Open Post-review actions in the Flows section. The flow is set to trigger when a review is submitted.
  2. Add an action to the post-review actions flow that sends the customer an email with their discount code.
  3. Connect a messaging app under Integrations (links to other tools) if you want the email or SMS (text message) action to be available.
  • Junip's Integrations screen offers messaging apps for post-review flows.
  • These include Postscript, Omnisend, Klaviyo, Drip and Shopify Flow.
  • Connecting one adds email or SMS functionality to the flow.

Surveys and Feedback Requests

2 min

Timing

  • Send the post-purchase survey email a few days after purchase (for example three or four days). A survey is a set of questions.
  • This is different from the review request, which is sent about two weeks after purchase.
  • Do not send both on the same day, to avoid overloading the customer.
  • Send the first survey request email about 14 days after purchase, adjusted to your typical delivery times.
  • The customer should have received the product first if the survey is meant to rate the product itself.
  • If the survey only covers the on-site experience (your website) or customer service, it can be sent closer to the purchase date.
  • Sending it further out avoids cluttering the early post-purchase email sequence.
  • The customer feedback survey email in the Source B sequence is sent 10+ days after purchase.
  • It is the final touchpoint (the last contact point with the customer). "10+" means 10 days or more.

Reminders

  • Every feedback or review request email should be followed by a reminder to those who have not responded yet.
  • A reminder can also reach people who already responded without harm.
  • Email open rates average about 30%, so a single email is often missed.

Survey Questions and Examples

Survey template (ask every caller or buyer in this order):

Before you wrap up, I have a few brief questions we put to every caller. While browsing our website, did anything seem broken or not work properly? [If yes: What was it?] Were you checking other websites while looking into this product? [If yes: Which ones?] Please share your candid opinion: did any of those sites appeal to you more, and if so, what made them stand out? What specifically did you like about them?
  • A post-purchase survey email can ask how the buyer experienced the business.
  • It can ask whether they compared other sites before buying, which sites those were, and what could be improved.
  • These questions reveal competitor comparisons (what rivals they looked at) and friction points (things that slow or annoy buyers).
  • The store can address those points.

Reporting Results to Customers

  • Send an email to the customer list once per quarter (every three months).
  • That email summarizes the survey results and the changes made in response.
  • For smaller businesses or fewer responses, every six months is sufficient.
  • Quarterly update email structure: state that many people have filled in the survey.
  • Share a few points they highlighted about the business.
  • Then explain what has been changed internally in response to that feedback.
  • Keep it short and factual.
  • This way customers see their feedback matters, and they get another reason to open future emails.
  • Automated survey emails can be sent through any email automation tool connected to your store.
  • The send point after purchase is your choice.
  • The full follow-up process can be outsourced to a virtual assistant (a remote helper) once it is set up.

Loyalty Program Reminders

1 min

A loyalty program gives points to customers for buying.

  • Tell customers about the loyalty program more than once, never only once.
  • Send the first mention at or soon after the first purchase.
  • Then remind customers who hold points at least every three months.
  • Loyalty program introduction email: sent 72 hours after purchase.
  • It is integrated into the welcome sequence (the set of first emails a new customer gets).

Loyalty reminder email flow:

  1. Write one reminder email that says the customer has loyalty points, explains how to use them, and suggests products they can buy with the points.
  2. Add it to the email marketing system as an automated flow.
  3. Set the flow trigger to send to customers with loyalty points once every three months, at a minimum.
  4. Activate the flow and leave it running.
  5. Review and adjust the wording occasionally, but do not rewrite it on a schedule.
  • Do not treat a loyalty program as a one-time announcement.
  • Customers forget the program quickly.
  • So repeated reminders are needed to produce repeat purchases (buying again).

Formula for a post-purchase message about a loyalty program:

  1. State the points earned on this order.
  2. Name one other action that earns more points, such as a review or a referral. A referral is when a customer brings in a new buyer.
  3. State what the points can become on the next order.

Customers are most receptive right after buying. A concrete balance with a clear next step is easier to act on than a general mention. Examples: Your order just added 150 points to your account. Leave a review on a product to earn more, then use the total on your next order. and Thanks for your order. You now have 320 points, enough for a discount on your next purchase. Refer a friend and you earn points when they buy.

Referral Program Emails

1 min
  • Promote the referral program several times so customers know it exists.
  • Include it in an email sent shortly after a customer's first purchase.
  • Referral program introduction email: sent 8+ days after purchase.
  • It is sent after post-purchase calls (follow-up talks with the buyer) are confirmed, when customers have had positive experiences.
  • Place referral program emails after customers have experienced positive post-purchase touchpoints (contact points), like support calls and loyalty rewards.
  • Customers primed by satisfaction (made ready by a good experience) are far more willing to enthusiastically refer your business.
  • Connecting referral rewards to the loyalty system makes the process smoother.
  • For example, you can award loyalty points for referrals.
  • Emails can then cover both programs.
  • This integration is helpful but not required.
  • Remind customers about the referral reward at least every three months by email.
  • Also mention the referral program in personalized post-purchase messages (messages written with the customer's details).
  • Reminders about a referral program give customers a simple way to pass something of value on to friends, family or social followers.
  • Framing the reminder around that benefit to the customer, not only the reward, is part of why it works.

Win-Back

1 min
  • A win-back email tries to bring back a customer who has stopped buying. Use it for customers who have not bought for a while.
  • Replace each placeholder in curly braces with your own detail. A placeholder is a blank you fill in. Checkout is the last step where the buyer pays.
Hey {billing first name}, it has been a while since your last purchase from {store name}, and we miss you. As a thank you, here is $15 off your next order. Use code {discount code} at checkout: {store URL}. We hope to see you back soon. Reply STOP to opt out.
  • Repeat purchases come only from good service. So a win-back offer should follow a store that has delivered good service.

Cross-Store and Own-Niche Promotion

1 min
  • An order confirmation email is the email sent right after a purchase.
  • A cross-promotion is an advertisement for another of your stores.
  • A cross-promotion in the order confirmation email reaches buyers after they have paid. So it does not hurt the conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy) of your main store. It also brings free traffic (visitors who arrive without paid ads) to your second store.
  • Add a note to the order confirmation email. It sends buyers to a related store with a return-customer discount code (a discount only for people who already bought from you). This drives sales across stores.
  • Cross-promoting two related stores:
    1. Add a note to the order confirmation email linking to the second store with a return-customer discount code.
    2. Once both stores run well, use monthly store-wide promotions (a sale on the whole store, see how to create promotions) to mix the audiences that have been customers the longest.
  • Show your own niche store offers after a sale. A niche is a small topic market. Show them in the order confirmation email and in the tracking notification emails (the shipping update emails sent as the parcel moves).
  • Post-sale value example: a $100 gun safe sale can become a $40 ebook sale, plus a recurring $30 per month subscription (a payment that repeats every month). Each buyer then brings in more money than the first sale. That makes a higher cost per click (what you pay each time someone clicks your ad) affordable.

Source Conflicts and Version Notes

2 min
  • Second survey or review timing: A survey is a short questionnaire for customers. The timing depends on what it measures. A survey on the product itself is sent about 14 days after purchase, after delivery. A survey on the on-site experience or customer service can be sent closer to purchase, at about 3 to 4 days. The Source B sequence places its feedback survey at 10+ days. Use the rule that matches what the survey measures.
  • Review request timing: Judge.me is an app that collects product reviews. Its default is 14 days after purchase. Klaviyo is an email tool. Use the Klaviyo delay for flows you build. Use the Judge.me default only if you keep Judge.me's request.
  • Review sending channel: The 2022 advice is to send review requests through Klaviyo, not through Judge.me's emails. The Judge.me email is not a deal breaker if the store is already satisfied with it. Keeping the request in Klaviyo keeps the whole sequence in one place.
  • Referral timing: A referral is a reward for customers who bring in new buyers. The Source B sequence sends the referral email at 8+ days, after the post-purchase calls (calls made to buyers after they buy) are confirmed. The later advice is to promote the referral program in an email shortly after the first purchase, and to repeat it. Both apply. Mention it shortly after purchase, then introduce it again once the customer has had a positive experience.
  • Thank-you discount level: The Clavio flow (an automatic email series) uses $15 off or 15% off. The thank-you template uses a 15% discount. The post-purchase storewide offer (a discount on the whole store right after buying) uses 15%. Choose the amount you can afford. Check the margin (the money you keep after paying the product cost) on a typical repeat order before setting 15%.
  • Second thank-you email timing: The structure gives the second email roughly two days after the first. The step-by-step procedure sends it 2 days after the order. Both describe a second email about two days later. So measure the gap from the event your flow uses.
  • Open rates: An open rate is the share of people who open an email. Average open rates are about 20 to 30 percent. Another figure gives about 30 percent. Either figure justifies repeating offers and reminders.
  • Upsell timing: The first-day sequence sends upsells at 1 hour, 25 hours, 48 hours and 60 hours after purchase. The long campaign repeats 3 to 4 emails immediately after purchase, then at 15 to 30 days, 60 days and 120 days. Both apply. The first runs in the first days. The second repeats the offer over months.

Flow structure per core product

1 min
  • A core product is your main product. A flow is an automatic email series. Do not build one flow for all core products.
  • Each core product needs its own flow with its own purchase trigger (the buy event that starts the emails). Otherwise the emails will not match the product the customer bought.

Post-purchase upsell email

1 min
  • Pattern:
    1. Thank the buyer for the core product just purchased.
    2. Name a related product that pairs well with it.
    3. Include a button that adds the product to the cart in one click.
  • Example templates:
Thanks for your order of the [core product]. The [related product] pairs well with it, so add it here in one click.
Your [core product] is on its way. Many buyers also add the [related product] at the same time. Tap here to add it to your order.

Repeat buyers

1 min
  • Customers who have already bought once should not receive the same offer again straight away.
  • If a repeat-purchase email (an email aimed at buyers who came back) does not convert (does not lead to a sale), the offer is probably wrong for them. Or they need useful content for a while before the next sales email.

Complementary products for past buyers

1 min
  • Complementary products are items that go well with what the buyer already bought. Past buyers can be contacted by email later to offer them. This turns a first purchase into a second and third sale.
  • This only works when your niche has a product range that suits the same customer.

Personalized recommendations with AI

1 min
  • AI is software that learns patterns from data. Automated, AI-powered email flows send personalized product recommendations, meaning suggestions chosen for each buyer.
  • They are used to keep customer retention (customers coming back again) high.

Playbook › 15. Email and SMS

Holiday, black Friday and promotion campaigns

Section 15.14 · 14 of 17 in this chapter · 31 min read

In short

  • Start warming your email and text list two months before the busy sales season, from about September or October.
  • Plan the email dates ahead, keep emails simple, and use the same discount code in every email.
  • Do not send sale emails to people who bought in the last 30 days or who are already in an automatic email series.
  • Make sure the sale end time you write matches the end time set in your store.
  • Send three emails in this order: first the benefit, then the reason to act, then the warning that the deal ends.
More key points
  • Use only real deadlines. Fake countdowns make people stop trusting your emails.
  • Replace every placeholder (a blank like X% or NAME) before you send. A leftover blank looks broken.
  • Write a new AI prompt for each sale. Use the exact prompts below.

Q4 and Seasonal Planning

5 min

Timing

  • Start preparing Q4 (the last three months of the year, when sales peak) at least two months before the peak.
  • Warm up your email and text message (SMS) list from about September or October. Your list is the people you can email or text.
  • Emails that ask readers to act (interaction-based emails) and surveys build the list before the sales period.
  • Use plain-text emails (simple text, no heavy design) with one link or one form.
  • Designed emails are more likely to land in the promotions tab (the inbox tab for marketing mail).
  • Surveys can be built with Jotform (an online form tool) and sent to subscribers (people on your list).
  • Plan the holiday content early. Steps:
    1. Create blog posts (short articles on your website) for major holidays (Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Christmas) 1-2 months before the event.
    2. Write articles about upcoming sales, new inventory (new products in stock), and limited-time promotions.
    3. Send an email to the list 2-4 weeks before the event with the article and promotional details.
    4. Aim to rank in Google search (show high in results) when customers search "Black Friday [niche]" or "Cyber Monday deals." Niche means your product area.
  • Run a campaign (one email sent to a group at one time) for every major event, such as Christmas or Black Friday.
  • Customers expect a discount at these times.
  • This adds to your regular weekly and monthly campaigns.
  • Use seasonal events such as Valentine's Day for promotions and extra emails.
  • Plan promotions and emails around holidays to create reasons to send more messages.
  • Plan a monthly promotion to the email list and build it into your regular calendar.
  • Monthly promotions lift sales (raise sales) and help the store grow (scale) into other sales platforms later.
  • Follow up holiday promotions in a later month.
  • A promotion for a holiday can close sales that a first visit did not convert (turn into a sale).
  • Match email promotions with the same offer on Meta (the company behind Facebook and Instagram ads) at the same time.
  • Past customers who bought months ago, such as six months back, should receive both the email and the Facebook promotion.

Event campaigns

  • If Klaviyo's template library (Klaviyo is an email tool; a template library is a set of ready-made email designs) has no template for a seasonal event, pick a template for a similar occasion and adapt it.
  • For example, use a New Year layout for Valentine's Day.
  • Replace the headline, offer text, and background color so the email matches the event.
  • A seasonal gift promotion, such as a Father's Day offer, fits stores whose products appeal to dads.
  • Promote it with a short series of emails to the subscriber list.
  • An event-based promotion sent to the email list, such as 5% or 10% off on May 5 for Cinco de Mayo, is a quick way to earn extra sales.
  • Event campaign for occasions such as Mother's Day or Valentine's Day, in this order:
    1. Choose a specific marketing angle (the main reason for the message) for the event.
    2. Send the first email to educate the audience and announce the upcoming offer, without a hard pitch (a direct push to buy).
    3. Send the next email with a benefit of the product and a sign-up request.
    4. Count the sign-ups to estimate the sales.
    5. Send the follow-up to sign-ups.
    6. Send a separate email to non-sign-ups asking why they did not sign up, with an extra benefit point.

Monthly promotion procedure

  1. Choose a high-ticket product (an expensive item) from a supplier (the company that provides the product) with good margins (good profit per sale).
  2. Send an email offering a discount on that supplier's products, for example 10 percent off.
  3. Link directly to the product collection (the page that groups products).

Black Friday and Cyber Monday announcements

  • Announce a Black Friday sale and a Cyber Monday sale on every channel you have.
  • Send an email to your list.
  • Post on social media.
  • Even a small email list or a small social following can produce extra sales from these promotions.
  • In 2019, Black Friday fell on November 29 and Cyber Monday on December 2.
  • Plan holiday promotions around these dates in the year they apply.

Abandoned cart contacts (2023)

  • Abandoned cart contacts (people who added items to the cart but did not buy) who are not subscribed to the main list can receive about one promotional email per month.
  • The promotion can be tied to a holiday or niche event.

Dated Promotion Calendars

6 min

Black Friday and Cyber Monday sequence

A launch means the sale goes live. Include SMS means also send a text message.

  1. November 24 (48 hours before launch): send "Going live in 48 hours. Last year everything sold out in 4 hours. You're among our first 100 subscribers, so take advantage." Include SMS.
  2. November 25 (24 hours before): send "Going live in 24 hours." Tease (show a hint of) a product image with a blurred price (for example, show "9.99" to create curiosity). Include SMS.
  3. November 26 (Black Friday, launch day): send the launch email and SMS with a link to the hidden category page.
  4. November 27 (day after Black Friday): send "We had huge success, received many requests, some items sold out. We just restocked limited pieces. Shop now."
  5. November 28 (Sunday): send "This is your last day. Only a few items left. Shop now." Send SMS.
  6. November 29 (day after): send "It's finished. Thank you for your trust and business." Send SMS.
  7. Monday (Cyber Monday, one week later): launch a new offer with a different discount structure.
    • Example: change from 50% off to 40% off plus a 50% cashback voucher (money back on the next purchase).
    • Send the new offer email and SMS.
    • Then send a 48-hour countdown.
    • Then send a last-chance email.
    • Then send a closure email.
  8. Optional Black Week extension (November 29-30, only 2 days): send an email plus SMS.
    • Then send a UGC email (UGC means user-generated content, posts made by customers).
    • Then send a closure email "It's over."

Black Friday sequence for a store

  1. Before the sale, send an email announcing Black Friday week and asking customers to decide now what they want.
  2. On the day, send the main announcement with the bigger discount.
  3. Retarget visitors who did not buy with a message saying they missed the offer.
  4. After the sale, send an email saying the sale has been extended for people who missed it.

Hidden Black Friday category page

  • Create a product category page (a page that lists one group of products).
  • The page is not linked in website navigation or menus (the links in your site menu).
  • It is only accessible via direct links from email, SMS, or retargeting ads.
  • Include all Black Friday-discounted products.
  • Include a countdown timer (a clock showing when the promotion ends).
  • Add low-stock quantity labels (labels showing how many are left) on each product to create urgency (a feeling that you must act now).

Welcome series and standard flows during BFCM

  • Standard email flows (automatic email series started by an action) remain active during Black Friday promotional campaigns.
  • Standard flows include abandoned cart sequences, welcome series (emails to new subscribers), and post-purchase emails (emails sent after buying).
  • Standard flows run in parallel (at the same time).
  • Do not pause them.
  • They operate on their own triggers (the action that starts them) and segments (groups of contacts chosen by rules).
  • They are separate from the VIP Black Friday list.
  • For ongoing welcome series, if new leads (new contacts) enter after Day 3 emails have started, pause emails 2 and 3 for those new subscribers.
  • This prevents clashes with Black Friday email blasts, which have priority.
  • Resume the welcome series after the promotion period ends.
  • The welcome series runs automatically on fixed dates (for example, Nov 1-3) regardless of when individual subscribers join.
  • For subscribers entering late (for example, Nov 22), pause emails 2 and 3 to avoid overlap with high-priority Black Friday blasts.
  • Resume normal sequences after promotions end.
  • Putting both rules together:
    • Standard flows stay active.
    • The welcome-series pause applies only to late joiners' emails 2 and 3 during the promotion period.

Campaigns versus flows

  • When time is short, use campaigns instead of flows for Black Friday emails.
  • Campaigns are simpler.
  • Send an initial email to the list, analyze results, then send a new campaign to the list minus unsubscribes (people who left the list).
  • There is no need for conditional flows (flows that change based on what a person does).

Christmas promotion

  1. Launch the holiday promotion email as early as possible, ideally right away.
  2. Include the message that products arrive before Christmas if ordered now.
  3. Send one or two reminder emails as the holiday gets closer.
  4. Do not send the main holiday promotion on the holiday itself, because most customers have already bought.

Five-day holiday promotion

  1. Send an email at the start of the promotion announcing the sale and including the discount code.
  2. Send an email in the middle of the window (the time period of the sale), or on a following day, saying the sale was extended to the final day.
  3. Send a last email stating that the code expires at midnight on the final day.
  4. Every email should include the code.
  • For a five-day promotion, spread three emails across the window.
  • Send the first email on the launch day.
  • Send the second email about two days later.
  • Send the third email on the final day.
  • Each email should match the stage of the promotion it is sent in.

One-week December promotion

  • For a one-week December promotion with three emails, send the gain email (focused on what the customer wins) on the first day.
  • Send the logic email (focused on reasons) two days later.
  • Send the fear email (focused on the risk of missing out) on the last day.
  • For example, December 8, December 10, and December 12.

High-ticket promotion calendar

  • Close offers on a weekday.
  • For more expensive, high-ticket products, the offer should close on a Friday.

Timers and extensions

  • Klaviyo email campaigns can include countdown timers.
  • The timers can be reset in each send.
  • When a sale is extended, update the timer and the matching offer text in every campaign.
  • This keeps the messages consistent.

Repeat buyers

  • Email customers who bought a single item with a targeted discount.
  • Invite them to buy a gift or a second item for a partner or family member.
  • This extends the value of first-time buyers in the holiday season.

Promotion Segments and Exclusions

2 min

Standard promotion segment (2024-2025 rule)

  • For a promotion email, send to all contacts in the list.
  • Leave out people who purchased in the last 30 days.
  • Leave out contacts currently in an evergreen automation (an always-on automatic email series), such as the abandoned cart flow.
  • This works with Shopify Email (the email tool built into Shopify) or Klaviyo.
  • Segment settings (a segment is a group of contacts chosen by rules):
    • Include: all contacts.
    • Exclude: contacts who purchased in the past 30 days.
    • Exclude: contacts currently in the abandoned cart flow (or any evergreen automation).
    • Segment size does not matter; a list of 500 or 10,000 contacts uses the same rules.
  • Promotional emails go to all contacts.
  • Two groups are left out: people who bought in the last 30 days, and people currently inside an evergreen automation.
  • Recent buyers and automated flows should not receive the promotion sequence.

VIP Early Access

2 min

BFCM VIP early access email

BFCM means Black Friday and Cyber Monday. FOMO means fear of missing out.

BFCM VIP Early Access Email: Subject emphasizes exclusivity (e.g., 'Your 24-Hour VIP Early Access Starts Now'). Body: Explain that subscribers get 24-hour first access to upcoming BFCM offers; this creates urgency and FOMO without requiring truly different pricing-simply send the email 24 hours before the public campaign launches. Landing: Direct to a dedicated BFCM collection or campaign page. Effect: Drives eager subscribers to site before the main sale begins.

VIP positioning for lead capture

  • For Black Friday lead capture (collecting sign-ups), use Facebook Creator Studio (Facebook's tool for managing posts and ads).
  • Use a VIP positioning (presenting the offer as VIP-only): "Enter details to get exclusive discounts up to 60% off with early access."
  • This entices email submission without over-promising.

VIP access sequence

  1. Start VIP access at least 72 hours before the sale begins.
  2. Give VIP access first to ambassadors (your most loyal fans who promote you).
  3. Then open it to organic followers (people who follow you without ads).
  4. Then open it to the email list.
  • Do not run VIP access paid for 30 days.

Trade-off of early access

  • Replacing a welcome discount pop-up (a box offering a discount when someone arrives on the site) with an early-access sign-up usually softens sales in the short term.
  • The trade-off is a larger list of interested buyers.
  • That list converts strongly once the sale starts.
  • The fair value exchange (what the subscriber gets back for their email) must be clear to the subscriber.

VIP pre-Black Friday email sequence

  1. Email 1 (day of signup): "Welcome to the Club."
  2. Email 2 (2 days before): "What you're going to Choose" (explore products).
  3. Email 3 (day before): "It's around the corner."
  4. Email 4 (day of): "Here's your exclusive early access."
  5. Mention in the final email that the discount releases to the public tomorrow, so act now.
  6. Final email on Black Friday: "Here we go, buy now."

Gain, Logic, Fear Sequences

3 min

Order and purpose

  • Each sale's emails follow the order Gain, Logic, Fear. Gain is the benefit. Logic is the reason to act. Fear is FOMO (fear of missing out).
  • The order moves the reader through different feelings. Those feelings lead to a purchase.
  • Three emails can follow Gain, Logic, Fear. Email 1 shows the benefit. Email 2 gives the reason it works. Email 3 shows the cost of not acting.
  • Gain, Logic, Fear is an older copywriting formula. Copywriting means writing words that sell.
  • AI tools can help adapt this formula to an e-commerce store (an online shop).

Gain-logic-fear sequence (2024 pattern)

  • Email 1 states what the buyer gains: the benefit plus the offer.
  • Email 2 gives a rational reason the offer is the smart choice.
  • Email 3 says the offer is ending now.
  • The order moves the reader from desire to reason to urgency (the feeling that you must act now).
  • This is why it converts (turns readers into buyers) across promotion types.
    • Example A (kitchen knives, Black Friday, the big sale day after US Thanksgiving):
      • Email 1: "Get 20% off the premium knife set, delivered before the holiday"
      • Email 2: "Why one quality knife set replaces five cheap ones"
      • Email 3: "Final hours: the 20% code ends at midnight"
    • Example B (pet beds, Cyber Monday, the online sale day after Black Friday):
      • Email 1: "Cyber Monday: 15% off orthopedic pet beds with fast shipping"
      • Email 2: "Why a supportive bed reduces night-time restlessness for older dogs"
      • Email 3: "Last chance: the 15% code expires tonight"

Formula with examples (2025)

  • A three-email promotion follows Gain, Logic, Fear.
  • Email 1 states what the buyer gains.
  • Email 2 explains why this offer is a sensible choice.
  • Email 3 says the offer is ending.
  • The timing creates urgency. The sequence works across most promotion types.
    • Example A:
      • Gain: 15 percent off plus delivery before the holiday weekend.
      • Logic: a gift that arrives on time saves a last-minute store trip.
      • Fear: the 15 percent code expires Sunday at midnight.
    • Example B:
      • Gain: a free carry strap with every yoga mat ordered this week.
      • Logic: the buyer avoids a shopping trip to a sports store.
      • Fear: the top color is limited and the strap offer ends Friday.

Closing-email patterns

  • Closing-reminder email: the subject line says the discount ends at a specific time.
  • The body gives the code (a word the buyer types at checkout) and a reason to act today.
  • Reuse the same subject line as the headline of the remarketing ads. Remarketing means showing ads to people who already visited your shop.
  • Example new subject lines: "Last Call: 20% Off Ends Tonight" and "Final Hours to Lock In Your Holiday Price".
  • Buy-by-date email: it states the last order date that still guarantees delivery before a holiday.
  • The reason is the delivery window (the time shipping needs), not the discount.
  • Example new subject lines: "Order by Dec 13 to Get It Before Christmas" and "Your Last Chance to Ship Before the Holidays".

Remarketing during an open window (high-ticket)

  • High-ticket means expensive products. The open window is the time the sale is running.
  1. Send one pre-promotion email before a high-ticket offer starts.
  2. Send one email per day during the promotion window.
  3. On the closing day, send at least two reminder emails.
  4. Run Facebook and Google remarketing ads to the same people who receive the emails, for maximum reach (how many people see it).

Remarketing timing

  • Start retargeting ads (ads for people who already visited) after roughly two to three weeks of traffic (visitors to your shop).
  • Start only once you have stats (numbers on who visits and what they do) to target from.
  • During an open promotion window, run remarketing alongside the emails, as in the procedure above.

Subject Lines by Holiday

4 min

A subject line is the title of an email. It decides whether people open it.

Formula and structure

  • Holiday subject line formula: event name or seasonal cue (a hint like "Spring"), then a concrete savings promise, then an optional emoji (a small picture symbol) or short action word.
  • The event cue shows why the email matters now.
  • The savings promise gives a reason to open it.
  • Examples in the same pattern: "Spring Clearance Starts Today: Save on Your Favorites" and "Summer Sale Is Live, Up to 30% Off Our Picks".
  • Write a separate set of subject lines for each seasonal moment (Halloween, Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Christmas, New Year).
  • Do not use one generic sale line for all of them.
  • Each moment has its own vocabulary and should match its own campaign (a planned set of emails for one goal).
  • Across a holiday campaign, mix three types of subject lines.
  • The three types are: direct deal statements, urgency lines (they push you to act now), and mood or holiday lines.
  • Varying the type keeps recipients from tuning out a repeated format.
  • Use one emoji per subject line as an accent, placed at the start or end.
  • Choose the emoji to match the season (ghost, cart, gift, fire, laptop).
  • Skip emojis when the brand or audience does not suit them.

Subject line patterns by holiday

  • Halloween: combine a spooky or playful theme (ghosts, treats versus tricks, monster-style wordplay) with a savings promise.
  • Use often one ghost or pumpkin emoji.
  • The theme makes the offer feel fun and fits the season, so the discount reads as a treat.
  • Examples: "Spooky Season Steals: Up to 30% Off Everything 🎃" and "Witch Better Believe These Deals Are Here 🧙".
  • Black Friday: use the words "Black Friday" with a savings or deal promise.
  • Add a sense of now, such as early access, the biggest sale, or a last chance.
  • Exclusivity ("just for you", "early access") and time-bound language make the email feel important.
  • Examples: "Black Friday Early Access: 25% Off Starts Right Now" and "Last Chance: Black Friday Prices End Tonight 🛍️".
  • Cyber Monday: name the day.
  • Pair it with either a countdown of hours left or a sitewide (whole store) percentage off.
  • Use "Extended" when the sale runs longer.
  • The online focus (laptop or phone emoji, "sitewide") signals that the whole store is in the sale.
  • Examples: "Cyber Monday: 12 Hours Left for 40% Off Sitewide 💻" and "Cyber Monday Extended: Our Deals Stay Live Through Tonight".
  • Christmas: blend holiday warmth (gifts, Santa, decorations, festive phrasing) with a savings message.
  • Add a deadline-style phrase such as "before they're gone" or "countdown".
  • The festive tone builds gift-buying urgency.
  • Examples: "Santa's Gift List Is Ready: Save 20% on Top Picks 🎁" and "Christmas Countdown: Grab Your Favorites Before Stock Runs Out 🎄".
  • New Year: frame the offer as a fresh start.
  • Use resolutions (yearly personal goals), "new year, new" phrasing, or "start the year" wording, plus a savings promise.
  • This ties the purchase to a personal goal, which makes the offer feel purposeful.
  • Examples: "New Year, Better Savings: Start 2027 With 15% Off 🎉" and "Your Resolution Starts Here: Save Big on Your Favorite Brands".

Placeholders and dates

  • A placeholder is a blank stand-in, such as X% or NAME.
  • Fill every placeholder before sending.
  • X%, XX% and similar must become the real discount.
  • NAME must become the merge field of your email tool (a tag that fills in each person's real name automatically).
  • Any year must be the current year.
  • A leftover placeholder in a live email is a visible error that damages trust.
  • Use placeholders in subject lines for the discount percentage (X%) and the year (20XX).
  • For personalization, use a NAME field that is filled per recipient.
  • Replace every placeholder with the real figure before the email is sent.
  • Do not leave an old year in a seasonal subject line, such as an outdated "Welcome 20XX" phrase from a previous year.
  • Update the year in every subject line for the current season before sending.

Reuse and extensions

  • Do not reuse the same subject line across different events.
  • Do not repeat an identical line in the next campaign.
  • Tie wording to the specific offer of each event so recipients do not see the same message twice.
  • When a sale is extended, send a follow-up with "Extended" wording.
  • Do not repeat the original subject line.
  • The change signals a new reason to open the email and keeps the offer credible.

Ready-made list

  • When AI-written email headlines are weak, use a proven list of holiday subject lines.
  • The list has about 97 lines, grouped by holiday: Halloween, Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Christmas, and New Year's.
  • Use them as email subject lines or ad headlines.

Scarcity, Urgency and Teasers

3 min

Scarcity means a limited supply. Urgency means a reason to act fast.

Urgency must be real

  • Urgency must be real.
  • Valid reasons include a webinar (an online seminar) starting tomorrow.
  • Valid reasons include a limited number of seats.
  • Valid reasons include a limited stock (how many items you have) of a physical product.
  • Valid reasons include a video being taken offline.
  • For evergreen products (items you always sell), use a special sale that ends soon.
  • Or use a coupon that expires in 24 hours.
  • Fake urgency backfires and destroys credibility (trust) once subscribers notice.
  • Invented deadlines teach readers to ignore future emails.
  • Only use deadlines you can honor (keep).
  • Do not claim urgency that is not real.
  • Lines such as "only 4 hours to go", "last day", or "extended" must match the actual sale timer or end date.
  • Use "before stock runs out" only when inventory (your total stock) is truly limited.
  • Subscribers who find a fake deadline stop trusting later messages.
  • Urgency phrases such as "hours left", "ends tonight", or "last day" should only be used when the deadline is real.
  • False countdowns damage trust.
  • False countdowns make later emails less effective.

Deadlines and expiry

  • Within each flow (a set of emails sent in order), every later email shortens the deadline.
  • The steps are: 3 days, then 48 hours, then 24 hours or a few hours.
  • The repeated shrinking window creates escalating urgency (a rising sense of pressure) without changing the offer itself.
  • Example: "Offer ends in 72 hours" followed by "Offer ends in 24 hours".
  • Example: "Your code expires this weekend" followed by "Last chance: expires at midnight tonight".
  • Set the send delays (the wait times between emails) so that each email arrives while its deadline statement is still true.
  • No fixed delays are given here, so derive the timing from the deadlines in the copy.
  • If an email says the offer ends at midnight, name the time zone (the clock of a region).
  • Make the coupon expire at exactly that time.
  • A midnight claim that differs from the real expiry damages trust the same way a false stock claim does.

Stock claims

  • Scarcity claims (signs that stock is low), such as "selling out like crazy", "inventory is running low", or "we might not be able to fulfill your order", must be true.
  • They must be true for the store at the time of sending.
  • False stock claims damage trust.
  • False stock claims can create refund (money given back) and complaint problems.
  • Use scarcity messaging in Black Friday campaigns, such as: "This exclusive discount releases to the public tomorrow. Act now to lock in the early-bird price before the public gets access."
  • This creates FOMO without over-exaggerating the offer.

Teasers

  • A teaser is a short preview that makes people curious. Teasing a larger promotion works in three steps:
  1. Send a teaser email to the broadcast list (everyone on your email list) a few days before the offer starts. For example, the Friday before a Monday start.
  2. Hint at the offer without giving the full details, so people start thinking about the purchase.
  3. Send the full offer at the start of the window.
  • Show a product image with a blurred price a day before launch to create curiosity (for example, "9.99").
  • For high-ticket offers (expensive products), send a single pre-promotion email before the offer starts.
  • That email says a big offer is coming.
  • It asks the audience to check their inbox first thing on the start day at a specific time.
  • Example: for leather sofas averaging $2,500, send the email the Friday before a Monday-to-Friday promotion.
  • This prepares buyers for a larger purchase.

AI Prompts for Sequences

4 min

A prompt is the text you type into an AI tool. A template is a pattern with blanks you fill in.

Building the prompt first (procedure)

  1. Write one message that includes the store domain (your web address), promotion dates, discount code, percentage off, delivery guarantee, and the send date of each email.
  2. Ask the AI to first write the best prompt for this task, instead of asking for the emails directly.
  3. Review and edit that generated prompt for your own store.
  4. Send the edited prompt, then ask for the sequence using the gain-logic-fear structure.
  5. Use a fresh, customized prompt for each promotion.
  6. Do not use one template for all promotions.

Remarketing and sequence build (procedure)

  1. Set the promotion's start date, end date, discount, and discount code.
  2. Add a delivery guarantee if the promotion has a deadline such as Christmas.
  3. Write a 3-email sequence.
    • Send one email on the first promotion day, one in the middle, and one on the last promotion day.
    • Example for a Monday to Friday window: Monday, Wednesday, Friday.
  4. Write remarketing ad copy for Meta (the company behind Facebook and Instagram) and Google Display (ads on websites in Google's network).
  5. Base the ad copy on the same email copy and offer details.

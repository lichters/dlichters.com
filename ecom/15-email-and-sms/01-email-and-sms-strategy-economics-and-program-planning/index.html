Playbook › 15. Email and SMS

Email and SMS strategy, economics and program planning

Section 15.1 · 1 of 17 in this chapter · 19 min read

In short

  • Email pays well: online shops earn about $40 for every $1 spent on it.
  • Build the basic automatic emails before launch and before running ads.
  • A shopper usually needs about seven contacts before buying, so keep in touch.
  • Repeat buyers are much cheaper to sell to than new buyers.
  • At first you need only the five core flows (automatic email sequences); skip extra emails.
More key points
  • Email can bring in about 10% to 30% of a store's sales, depending on the store and how well its emails are set up.
  • At $1,000 in store sales per month, email adds about $100 to $300 per month.
  • Aim for about $1 per month from each person on your email list. That is an older figure from 2022.
  • Repeat buyers cost far less to win than first-time buyers, so keep them coming back.

Why Email and SMS (Text Messages) Matter

1 min

Return on Investment (ROI: money earned back for money spent)

1 min
  • Online shops (e-commerce companies) earn roughly $40 in revenue for every $1 spent on email.
  • Email is therefore treated as a high-performing channel.
  • Sending promotion emails costs almost nothing beyond the email tool itself (the software that sends the emails).
  • A cited survey claims 89% of marketers see email as their main lead generation channel. Lead generation means getting the contact details of people who may buy.
  • The same survey claims email wins more customers than Facebook and Twitter combined.
  • Email matters most for a new business that still needs to grow its list (its collection of email addresses).
  • Email is one of four parts of the funnel (the steps from first visit to purchase). The others are Google Shopping ads (product ads on Google), organic search (free results from search engines) and retargeting ads (ads shown again to people who left).
  • Email marketing is part of the marketing system alongside paid search (ads shown in search results) and Google Shopping.
  • Organic search traffic (free visits from search engines) is included as a traffic source (visitors to the site).
  • Email complements retargeting as another way to stay in front of visitors who have not yet bought.
  • Email and retargeting together keep prospects (people who may buy) engaged between their first visit and their purchase.
  • This matters most for people in a longer buying process.
  • Once a list has grown, campaigns (one-off emails sent to a list) to it become an additional traffic and sales source alongside paid ads and organic content (free posts and pages).
  • This reduces dependence on paid traffic (visitors from ads) for repeat sales.
  • Email list growth starts slowly and compounds (grows on top of itself) as site traffic rises.
  • Email adds touchpoints (each contact a customer has with your brand) that build trust, especially before a first purchase.
  • Results compound over time, similar to SEO (search engine optimisation: getting found on Google for free).

Touchpoints and Trust

1 min
  • A prospect needs on average around seven touchpoints with an e-commerce business before buying.
  • A single visit is often not enough, so ongoing engagement (regular contact that keeps interest alive) is required.
  • Email is the place for ongoing, longer-form communication (longer messages sent over time).
  • This kind of email delivers knowledge, builds trust, demonstrates authority and builds a unique brand image.
  • It should not be used only for repeated product promotions and discounts.
  • Email is a personal channel.
  • Messages should read as if sent from one person to another, because people trust people more than faceless brands.
  • Email success depends on how consistently you send, how valuable the content is, and how many emails reach prospects and customers.
  • Software choice matters less; the tool is only a vehicle (a means to an end).
  • Many small and medium e-commerce businesses run no email marketing at all.
  • Even a basic setup puts a store ahead of these competitors.
  • Email is a channel cheaper than paid ads for repeat sales.

Repeat-Purchase Economics

1 min
  • A customer's second or third purchase typically costs far less to acquire (win over) than the first.
  • Repeat orders are more profitable and generate cash to put back into winning new first-time buyers.
  • A repeat sale to an existing customer carries no new acquisition cost (the money spent to win one new customer).
  • So the whole order value is close to pure profit.
  • Repeat purchases do not happen by accident.
  • They need defined processes and back-end marketing (messages to existing customers), kept separate from front-end acquisition marketing (messages to new visitors).
  • The aim is to turn first-time buyers into second and third-time buyers and eventually brand advocates (customers who recommend you).
  • Improving the autoresponder sequence (emails sent automatically in a set order) raises the value of every subscriber.
  • Higher value per subscriber lets you pay more to acquire the next subscriber and grow faster.
  • Email is one of the main levers (things you can pull to change results) for returning customers.
  • Build the list over time and use it for holiday promotions, new products and reminders.
  • Good opt-ins (sign-ups where people agree to receive emails) and email marketing raise the value of every paid click (a click from an ad).
  • In one large client account, email generated about 10% of total revenue linked to paid traffic.
  • Automation (emails that send by themselves) effort is low once set up.
  • Automated flows are built once and keep earning every month.
  • Most of the setup runs without ongoing work, except for testing and one step that needs more hands-on effort.

Ownership of the Customer Relationship

1 min
  • The customer relationship belongs to you, not the platform (the company whose software runs your store).
  • You can market to customers indefinitely (with no set end date), and they can return to purchase multiple times.
  • Email retargeting is one of the most powerful tools on Shopify (online store software).
  • Customers who give their email address become a permanent marketing asset you can reach indefinitely.
  • Promotions to your email list, plus traffic from several sources, help smooth out random swings in daily and weekly sales.
  • Promotions for cold traffic (people who do not know your store yet) are less effective than promotions sent to a list of past buyers or visitors.
  • Past buyers and visitors already know the store.

Campaigns and Flows

1 min

Definitions

1 min
  • Flows are automated sequences triggered by visitor or customer behaviour.
  • A trigger is the action that starts the sequence, such as signing up, abandoning checkout (leaving before paying), buying or receiving a shipment.
  • Flows turn interest into revenue without manual sending.
  • Campaigns are one-off sends tied to a date, event or launch, sent to a list or segment (a group within the list).
  • Campaigns are not triggered by website behaviour.
  • Flows are the better choice for behaviour-based follow-up.
  • A campaign sends a single email one time to your list.
  • An automation is triggered by a specific event (for example, abandoned checkout) and executes a sequence of steps automatically.
  • In Clavio (an email tool), flows are automated sequences that start from customer behaviour, such as a welcome series (emails sent to new subscribers), abandoned checkout and order confirmation.
  • Campaigns in Clavio are one-off messages sent manually to a chosen audience.

The Rule

1 min
  • All email campaigns except holidays and major sales events (such as Black Friday) must be automated flows.
  • Never send one-off campaigns for days such as National Pet Day.
  • Automation is where volume (how much) and leverage (results from little effort) come from.
  • Flows are tied to customer behaviour and deadlines.
  • Do not try to make flows reactive to news or trends; timely content belongs in campaigns.

Flow Design Requirements

1 min
  • Every flow needs three elements: a customer behaviour that triggers it, a clear objective, and a deadline.
  • Flow reference table:
Flow Behavior that triggers it Objective Deadline
Abandoned checkout Customer starts checkout Return and purchase 72-hour window, then the product goes back on the shelf
Welcome series Visitor wants to buy at a discount (wants a deal) Get the visitor to buy a product 72 hours, or until the coupon or discount code expires
  • Flow blueprints (ready-made plans) are intentionally generic.
  • Their value lies mainly in the gotchas (common traps), common mistakes, suppressions (rules that stop emails to someone) and exclusions (people left out).
  • Structure and copy still need adapting to each brand before use.
  • A flow for a high-ticket product (an expensive item, for example a $3,000 dining table) differs fundamentally from one for a low-ticket product (a cheap item, for example $40 jewellery).
  • Set timing, messaging, offers and sequence length (number of emails in order) by price point.

Which Flows to Launch First

1 min

Core Flow Set

1 min
  • Every e-commerce brand needs five automated email flows.
  • They are: a pop-up (a box on the website) with a welcome series, an abandoned checkout flow, a browser abandonment flow (for visitors who looked and left), an added-to-cart abandonment flow (for items left in the cart), and a post-purchase flow (emails after buying).
  • The five core flows (post-purchase, welcome series, abandoned cart, abandoned checkout and abandoned browse) generate at least 80% of total flow revenue.
  • They deserve the most attention when building or optimising a store's email setup.
  • Upsells (offers for a higher-priced item) and downsells (offers for a cheaper item) do not need dedicated flows.
  • Subscribers who receive regular campaigns with new angles and solutions will see upsell offers naturally.

Timing Against Launch

2 min
  • Set up abandoned cart and post-purchase email flows before the store goes live (opens to customers).
  • This means customers receive follow-up emails from the first sale (2025).
  • Klaviyo (an email tool) must have the abandoned cart flow and the welcome flow set up during store build, before traffic is sent to the store.
  • Start abandoned cart emails from the day the store first gets traffic, because shoppers who leave a cart can still come back.
  • Set up welcome and abandoned checkout flows at launch, before traffic is large.
  • Even 50-100 visitors can produce a few extra sales.
  • Missing these flows loses buyers who were already interested.
  • Sources differ on the gate (the condition that must be met before moving on) for capture (collecting email addresses) and follow-ups.
  • Build flows before go-live, as and advise.
  • Activate email capture and abandoned cart follow-ups only after live products are on the store, as advises (2023).
  • Set up email from the start for every product, because some buyers take time to decide.
  • It matters most for products priced $50 and above.
  • It also matters for stores with upsell or cross-sell products (cross-sell means offering a related extra product).
  • Klaviyo should be set up before running ads for a product.
  • Retargeting visitors who did not buy and capturing emails lowers customer acquisition cost (the cost to win one new customer).
  • It does this by converting more of the traffic already visiting the site.
  • Klaviyo should be set up before any ads run.
  • It turns visitors who already came to the store into buyers.
  • It lowers customer acquisition cost.
  • It builds a list for retention (keeping customers coming back) and for retargeting people who left without buying.
  • Email funnels should be built before scaling other traffic sources (growing spend and visitors).
  • Basic email campaigns are part of baseline store setup (the minimum setup), alongside Google Ads (paid ads on Google), payment processing (taking card payments) and remarketing (ads for past visitors).
  • Basic email campaigns should be set up before scaling traffic.
  • A new store should have a basic set of automated email flows in place before scaling traffic.
  • These are the minimum requirement for email marketing.
  • Newsletter sign-ups (sign-ups for regular news emails) and automated flows capture visitors who leave without buying.
  • They give the store a way to reach interested visitors who did not purchase on the first visit.
  • Start sending emails to subscribers (people on your list) as soon as they are on the list, regardless of which opt-in form or source brought them there.
  • Early sends build engagement (people opening and clicking) and begin producing returns while the list is small.
  • Email capture and automated sequences should be ready before subscriber numbers rise, so new subscribers get the right email immediately.
  • Set up email automations early, because they run continuously in the background.
  • Content marketing (creating useful articles and posts) can wait until the store has traffic and you know what is working.
  • Email marketing success starts with the basics.
  • Email campaigns form part of the marketing setup before traffic scales.

Phased Rollout

1 min
  • Use these phases as the planning order. Each phase starts only when its gate is met.

Phase 1: Build Before Launch

1 min
  • Set up the email sequences in this order:
    1. Plan the post-purchase and opt-in sequences (emails for people who agreed to join) in advance.
    2. Create any new list segmentations (groups within your list) needed to run them.
    3. Build the automations for each sequence.
  • Adapt any flow blueprint (ready-made plan) to your own store:
    1. Overlay your own branding (name and look), tone and messaging onto the blueprint (lay them on top of it).
    2. Overlay your own thoughts and store data (customer behaviour, price point, product type) onto the blueprint.
    3. Keep the parts that fit your store and discard the parts that do not.
  • Build three automated flows in the email service before anything else: abandoned cart, welcome and post-purchase.
  • Together they cover first contact with a new subscriber, recovery of unfinished checkouts, and the second order from existing buyers.
  • Build the email list and flows so that every visitor who opts in keeps hearing from the store.
  • Flows are not needed on the first day for every store.
  • They become an important revenue source later.

Phase 2: Capture and Launch

1 min
  • Opt-in and segmentation procedure (the steps for collecting and sorting sign-ups):
    1. Map the desired customer touch points from first hearing about the business to being ready to purchase.
    2. At each touch point, offer an option to join an email list or other list, such as push notifications (alerts sent to a phone or browser) or SMS by phone number. Do not rely on one pop-up for everyone; offer several ways to join across the site.
    3. Segment the list by the touch point where the subscriber entered.
    4. Send contextually relevant communication regularly over at least the first 30 days after joining, moving the prospect through the awareness stages (how much they know about you) from their entry point.
    5. Do not send the same emails to everyone, because subscribers are at different stages and need different content.
  • Implementation (setting it up) can take one to three months.
  • Rolling out the full email setup gradually is acceptable.
  • Consistent starting matters more than speed.
  • SMS (text messages to phones) works alongside email.
  • Set up SMS flows for the same triggers as email flows to recover lost sales, using SMSBump (a text-message app) in Source C's setup.
  • Set up only the basic automated emails needed to launch a new store as part of the initial build.
  • Defer advanced email marketing until after the first sales are made.

Phase 3: After First Sales

1 min
  • Advanced email marketing is a separate, later-stage layer on top of a basic email setup.
  • It is not a prerequisite for basic setup.
  • Set up email autoresponders for as many messages as possible, not only abandoned carts.
  • This way each repeated customer message is sent automatically.
  • Once a list passes 500 contacts, the email program should be profitable with at least about $1,000 in extra sales per month.
  • Check this against the Klaviyo revenue report (Klaviyo's report of money made from email).
  • Once live, send the holiday promotions to the full email list first.
  • Email lists drive a large share of extra revenue during holidays and monthly promotions.
  • A new store's list grows slowly, mostly from people who opt in through ads, so start building it early (2025).

Phase 4: Scale

1 min
  • Begin sending email promotions and retargeting campaigns once you have accumulated around 1,000 email subscribers.
  • Advanced email automations are worth implementing once a store is above roughly $100K in sales.
  • Below that level, simpler email flows are enough (2024).
  • Add the advanced flows (UGC generator, cross-sell after fulfilment) once the basic set is running.
  • UGC means user-generated content (pictures and reviews made by customers). Fulfilment means packing and shipping orders.

Program Management

1 min
  • Send copies of the emails you want to discuss, or paste their body text (the main message), before the call.
  • This lets the reviewer work out improvements in advance rather than reading them during the session.
  • Example (case): a workshop product launched to an email list of about 3,000 people with no prior sales history.
  • The team used reply emails to ask buyers why they bought and to learn prospects' objections (reasons they hesitate).
  • Insights from the replies went into the copy (written sales text) and the offer.
  • In its third month the workshop was generating about $90,000 in revenue from the small list.
  • The team noted that the reply emails provided both the data and the sales.
  • Competitor check (looking at a rival store): a competitor having no opt-in on its homepage does not prove it skips email marketing.
  • Check content pages and the checkout flow, because some stores email only visitors who take an action such as adding to cart.
  • Some e-commerce businesses send no email at all, so an empty inbox after signing up is itself useful information.
  • Record whether the competitor sends abandoned cart emails and any other follow-ups.

Outsourcing

1 min
  • Email writing can be outsourced (given to another person or company) if the operator does not enjoy writing.
  • This is fine as long as the full set of planned emails gets built and sent.
  • Email writing can be assigned to the same content writer role that produces blog posts, combined with other website copywriting.
  • This frees the owner from writing customer emails and copy.
  • A done-for-you email setup (an agency builds it for you) typically includes a welcome series for new subscribers, an abandoned cart campaign, and a post-purchase campaign that asks for referrals and reviews.
  • The technical setup in Klaviyo is also included, so emails fire (send automatically) at the right times (2023).
  • Find a trustworthy email agency by asking other store owners.
  • Use Upwork (a freelancer website) only as a last resort.
  • One operator's email list of about 50,000 subscribers produced strong, very profitable revenue.
  • This revenue came through regular sales campaigns run by an email agency.
  • Flow blueprints need adapting before use, so outsourced builds should still be checked against your own price point and data.

What to Defer

1 min
  • Advanced email marketing waits until after the first sales.
  • Add it as a later-stage layer.
  • Flows that matter later, not on the first day, can wait.
  • Win-back (bringing back people who stopped buying) and re-engagement do not need separate automated flows.
  • Regular campaigns sent to the full list already reach lapsed contacts (people who stopped buying).
  • In this context, win-back targets people who have not purchased in two to four weeks.
  • Do not stack extra win-back, re-engagement or upsell flows on top of a regular campaign schedule.
  • This floods subscribers with messages.
  • Keep the automated flows to the core five.
  • Browse abandonment, cross-sell and win-back flows come after the core three are running.
  • Advanced flows (UGC generator, cross-sell after fulfilment) come after the basic set.
  • Optional email topics beyond the abandoned cart sequence and promotional emails can be added later.
  • Content marketing waits until the store has traffic and you know what works.

Revenue Per Month and Per Contact

1 min

A contact is one person on your email list.

  • At $100 in monthly store revenue, email adds about $10 to $30 per month. At $1,000 monthly revenue, the same 10% to 30% range adds about $100 to $300 per month.
  • An extra $1,000 per month from email over six months adds up to $6,000 in extra profit. Small gains that repeat each month add up to big totals over time.
  • Your email list should earn at least about $1 per contact per month (2022, older figure). For example, 10,000 contacts should bring in roughly $10,000 per month from email with no ad spend (money spent on ads).
  • Email made about 30% of monthly revenue in the example business, part of it from monthly promotions.

SMS Benchmark

1 min

SMS means text messages sent to phones.

  • Text messages (SMS) worked as a retention tool (a way to keep existing customers buying) with a good return on ad spend (money earned for each dollar spent on ads). For one brand, about 2,000 USD of roughly 50,000 USD in revenue came from SMS.

Other Reference Values

1 min
  • The core five flows (the five main automatic emails) generate at least 80% of total flow revenue.
  • Email is often overlooked but can add 10% to 30% to monthly revenue.
  • A customer's second or third purchase typically costs far less to win than their first.
  • Email contacts raise the value of every subscriber. So when you improve your email sequences (series of automatic emails), you can pay more to get the next subscriber.

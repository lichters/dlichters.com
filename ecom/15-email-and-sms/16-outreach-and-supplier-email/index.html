Outreach and supplier email

Section 15.16 · 16 of 17 in this chapter · 12 min read

In short

  • Send from a personal address, never contact@, info@ or support@, or emails look like spam.
  • Start every new campaign at 20 emails per day and raise the limit slowly.
  • Always send three emails (one first, two follow-ups), because most replies come from the follow-ups.
  • Wait 5 days between each email and put an unsubscribe link in every one.
This section has 10 parts. Tap one to open it.

Mailbox setup and profile photo

1 min

  • Send outreach from a personal address. Use either a real name at your own domain, or a new personal mailbox on that domain. **

  • Generic addresses such as contact@, info@ or support@ look like spam. They lower the reply rate and the link-click rate, which means the share of people who reply or click a link. **

  • A profile photo is the picture shown next to your name in recipients' inboxes. It appears next to every email you send from that Google account. **

  • A real face makes outreach feel personal. It tends to raise the chance of a positive reply. It can be your own face or someone else's. **

  • If the profile photo upload fails, follow this procedure:

    1. Open your Google account, click the profile picture, choose a photo from your computer, crop it and set it as profile photo.
    2. If the upload fails, open the Google Admin console (the settings page for company Google accounts) and type 'profile edit' into the search bar, or go to Apps > Additional Google Services > Directory settings, then open Profile editing.
    3. Tick the Photo option ('Users can customise their public profile photo and cover photo').
    4. Click Save.
    5. Go back to the Admin console home page, wait, and try the photo upload again.
    6. If it still fails with an error code (a code number that shows what went wrong), leave it and retry later. Changes can take up to 24 hours to reach all users.

    **

Warm-up limits

1 min

  • Warm-up means raising your sending volume slowly, so the mailbox looks normal to Google. **

  • Sending hundreds of cold emails (unrequested emails to strangers) from a mailbox that normally sends little can make Google suspect unusual behaviour. Google may then suspend (block) the account, usually for a short time. **

  • Do not jump straight to hundreds of emails per day. Raise the daily volume gradually over time. **

  • Warm-up ramp for a new outreach mailbox or domain (a domain is your website address, such as yourname.com): **

    Phase Timing Max emails per day
    Start First campaigns 20
    Step 2 After about 2 weeks 50
    Step 3 After several more weeks 100
    Ceiling Keep increasing over time up to 300

    Mailshake (a cold email sending tool) spreads the batch across days up to the limit. A large list does not need to go out in one day. **

  • Start every new campaign with a sending limit of 20 emails per day. A large list can take a week or more to finish. That is expected. **

  • A steady trickle of replies is easier to manage than one large batch sent at once. **

  • Spreading sends over days also protects deliverability (how many emails actually reach the inbox). It keeps your inbox manageable. **

Signature and unsubscribe handling

2 min

  • A signature is the short block of text at the bottom of an email. Use this signature for every outreach email from the connected mailbox. Insert the link on the words 'click here' with the unsubscribe placeholder described in the setting below. **

    [Your name]
[Company name]
[Phone number]
If you'd like to stop receiving emails from us, click here to unsubscribe.

  • Keep the signature short and plain. Avoid large font sizes, colours and images. Do not add many links. **

  • Mailshake advises starting the signature with a blank line. A signature may be left empty if you prefer. **

  • Add the unsubscribe link by selecting the words 'click here' and inserting a link. Use {{unsubscribe_url}} as the URL. **

  • A placeholder is a stand-in code that the tool replaces with real data. Double curly brackets, like {{unsubscribe_url}}, mark one. **

  • Do not add an http prefix (the 'http://' start of a web address) in front of the placeholder. **

  • The {{unsubscribe_url}} placeholder lets Mailshake add the recipient to an unsubscribed list. It then stops future emails automatically, including follow-ups. **

  • An unsubscribe link (a way to opt out) in every outreach email means recipients are less likely to mark the email as spam. **

  • Putting it in the signature by default means it is never forgotten in a template. A visible opt-out is common practice and a courtesy. **

  • Mailchimp (another email tool) handles opt-outs automatically when a campaign contains its special unsubscribe link. It builds a list of contacts who unsubscribed. **

  • Outside Mailchimp, the Mailshake {{unsubscribe_url}} placeholder does the same job for the sending tool. **

  • In Mailshake, the left sidebar has an Unsubscribes section. It lists contacts who clicked the unsubscribe link. **

  • Check Unsubscribes before reusing a contact list. People who opted out must not be emailed again, including in follow-ups. **

  • If you receive emails from a business you are already signed up with, the signup email may differ from the one on your email list. **

  • Then send an unsubscribe request from the address that receives the emails. State that you are already a member. **

Cold outreach sequence

1 min

  • Do not use this approach for cold email to business decision makers (the people who approve purchases for a company). **

  • Reach trade customers (business buyers) through a dedicated trade page. The page has a contact form and a phone number. **

  • Cold outreach emails must be very short, simple and straight to the point. A stranger gives only a few seconds of attention. **

  • State the exact request in the first email. Avoid long explanations. **

  • Build a three-email outreach sequence (a set of emails sent in a fixed order) in this order: **

    1. Initial email: makes the first request. It is the most detailed of the three.
    2. Follow-up #1: a short reminder that refers back to the first email.
    3. Follow-up #2: a final, short message that closes the loop.

    Send all three. Many responses come only after the follow-ups. **

  • Always follow up on any outreach or sales request. Most positive responses come from follow-ups. **

  • Mailshake can automate the follow-up sends once they are added to the campaign. **

  • Wait 5 days between the initial email and each follow-up. Also wait 5 days between follow-up #1 and follow-up #2. **

  • Keep the default reply-chain setting unchanged. It groups emails as replies in the recipient's inbox, so all three appear in one thread. **

  • In the Mailshake campaign composer (the screen where you write the campaign), add follow-ups with ADD FOLLOW-UP. It sits next to the ADD DRIP and ADD ON CLICK options. **

  • Keep the default five-day wait between follow-ups and the default reply-chain setting. **

  • Use the recipient counter, such as 1 of 35 recipients, to step through each personalized email. Personalized means written for one person, with their name or details. **

  • Click NEXT only after checking each email. Edits are saved when you switch recipients or click NEXT. **

Subject lines

1 min
  • A subject line is the title of an email, shown first in the inbox. **
  • Keep subject lines simple and generic so they do not trigger spam filters (automatic checks that send emails to the spam folder). **
  • Use 'you' and questions to feel personal. Do not mention links, content, outreach or collaboration in the subject. Those words make people skip the email. **
  • Subject-line formula: a short, personal question or open phrase. It creates curiosity without revealing the request. **
  • Why it works: it looks like a human note, avoids spam trigger words and gets the email opened. The body of the email then makes the ask. **
  • Examples in this pattern: 'Would this fit your post on {{Topic}}?' and 'Any thoughts on an idea for your guide?' **
  • Each follow-up subject line starts with 're:' followed by the original subject line. **
  • This keeps all three emails in one visible thread for the recipient. **
  • Putting links, outreach words or product names in the subject line makes recipients skip the email. **
  • Avoid spammy wording in the subject line. It can send the email to spam. **

Outreach templates

2 min

  • A double-brace field, like {{Firstname}}, is a placeholder that Mailshake fills with data from your spreadsheet. **

  • A square-bracket prompt is your instruction. Swap it for your own words and remove the prompt itself. **

  • Here is the ready-to-send opening email. Leave every double-brace field as it is. Mailshake adds the signature automatically. **

    Subject: [Write your subject line following the subject-line pattern]

Hi {{Firstname/[Add a creative fallback here]}},

My name is [Name] and I write for [website], a site that [describe your site in one line].

I'm getting in touch about your article at {{Prospect URL}} [explain why a link to us is worth their time]

[Ask them to look at your link]

[Insert your link here]

[Ask them for feedback]

Thanks,
[Name]

  • In the introduction, state in one line what your site helps people do. Name the specific article you found, using the prospect URL field. **

  • In the same message, ask for a link and give a reason why it is worth the recipient's time. **

  • Here is the ready-to-send first follow-up. Leave the double-brace field as it is. Replace each square-bracket prompt with your own words. **

  • The subject line is 're:' followed by the original subject. **

    Subject: re: [Original subject line]

Hi {{name/creative fallback}},

[Introduce yourself again: your name and the site you write for]

[Ask whether they've had a chance to read my earlier email]

Thanks.

  • Here is the paste-ready template for follow-up #2, the final message. Keep the double-brace field unchanged. Replace each square-bracket instruction with your own text. **

  • Use the subject line 're:' followed by the original subject. **

    Subject: re: [Original subject line]

Hey {{name/creative fallback}},

[Remind them who you are and what you are asking them to look at]

[Ask if they got the chance to check out your proposal]

[State that this is the last message and that you will leave it there if they are not interested]

Thanks.

  • Final follow-up formula: say clearly that this is the last message. Also say you will leave it there if they are not interested. **

  • Why it works: it respects the recipient's time, reduces unsubscribes and closes the thread politely. **

  • Examples in this pattern: 'This is my last note, so I will leave it there if this is not a fit.' and 'Last check-in from me. If the idea is not for your site, no problem at all.' **

  • Early follow-up email for a contact who asked to talk by phone. Use it after a call. Keep the text as written:

    Today's discussion was valuable. I want to confirm it covered everything I need from you. Here are my contact details. Thank you for your time, and I'm eager to begin our working relationship.

    **

Supplier follow-up

1 min
  • An automated email sequence sends follow-up questions after each reply. (No date)
  • This shows which suppliers respond. Use it to get responsive suppliers (suppliers who answer) to reply in English.
  • If a supplier asks you to send an email after a phone call, send the day one email from the tool. You can copy it directly and edit it before sending.
  • If the day one email gets no reply, send the day two follow-up email. It refers to your earlier message, states the launch timing and asks for the application steps.
  • Formula for a supplier follow-up email: refer to your earlier message. State a near-term launch window. Commit to paying by credit card and following the supplier's pricing rules. Then ask for the application steps.
  • Why it works: it reminds the supplier of your request. It signals you are a low-risk new account. It makes the next reply easy.
    • Example 1: 'Following up on my note from last week. We launch in the coming months and want to present your brand properly. We will pay by card and follow your pricing policy. Could you send me the application steps?'
    • Example 2: 'Checking in on my email from Monday. Our store opens within a few weeks and we plan to represent your products carefully. We will pay by credit card and respect your listed prices. What are the next steps to apply?'

Composer fallbacks, checks and campaign settings

2 min
  • The composer is the screen where you write the email. A fallback is backup text shown when real data is missing. **
  • If the recipient's first name cannot be found, Mailshake shows the fallback text from the highlighted field. It does not show the field name. **
  • Write a neutral or creative fallback for every name placeholder. A raw field name looks broken. Fallbacks are never perfect, but they look better than an empty or raw field. **
  • The sender name in the email must match the name in the body. **
  • The sending address must match the website named in the introduction. **
  • Use only one from-address per campaign (the address that emails come from). Keep the signature personal. **
  • Before clicking Next in the composer, check that every text replacement field (a placeholder that gets swapped for real text) exists for all recipients. **
  • Mailshake warns you when some recipients are missing a field. Use only fields that all recipients have, or add a fallback. **
  • Review each email before sending: **
    1. Use the preview to read the subject, the body and the signature. Include the unsubscribe line that Mailshake adds.
    2. Confirm the email sounds like a human. Confirm the signature is correct and the call to action (the request you want the reader to act on) is clear.
    3. Send a test email and check it on a phone.
    4. Click Next.
  • Campaign options in Mailshake:
    • Track opens: records who opens the email. Turn it on if you want the data.
    • Track link clicks: records who clicks a link. Turn it on if you want the data.
    • Schedule this send: pick a start time in the local time zone.
    • Lead Catcher: a feature that captures replies. Leave it enabled so replies are saved. **
  • Accept the terms about anti-spam laws (laws that stop unwanted email) and the important notes. Then click Next to start. **
  • The composer can save a written email as a reusable template with SAVE AS TEMPLATE. **
  • CHOOSE TEMPLATE loads saved templates. Over time, a template library builds up inside the account. **
  • Use SEND TEST before launching. It sends the email to you so you can check it. **
  • Text pasted into the composer can carry colours or other formatting. Mailshake may then offer to auto clean the formatting, which removes that styling. **
  • You can decline that offer. Then check the preview. It shows the subject, body and signature as the recipient will see them. **

Pitfalls

1 min
  • Do not delete or rename the double-brace or bracketed fields. Doing so breaks personalization or leaves raw field names in the email. **
  • Only change the square-bracket instructions and the text around them. **
  • Long, detailed outreach emails reduce replies. Recipients give only brief attention. **
  • Keep the request in the first two or three lines. **

Customer support email

1 min
  • Answer customer support email within 24 hours, Monday through Friday. **
  • Customer emails can be answered any day of the week. **
  • Do not make phone calls to customers on Sundays. **
  • Contact-form messages (messages sent through the form on your website) and store inbox messages sent before a purchase are sales leads. A sales lead is a possible customer.
  • Treat these leads as valuable, possibly more valuable than a paid click.
  • Questions about options or features show demand. Repeated requests can be a signal to add that feature.
  • An AI Workflow is an automated process that uses AI to do a task, such as writing email replies.
  • When building one to generate automatic email replies, add as much context as possible to the system prompt. The system prompt is the hidden instruction that tells the AI how to behave.
  • Include: details about the recipient, your preferred response tone, how you usually handle similar requests, any specific business information the AI should reference, and guidelines about topics you do or do not discuss.
  • Rich context in the prompt greatly improves the relevance and quality of generated replies.

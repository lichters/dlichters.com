Welcome series and new subscriber nurture

Section 15.6 · 6 of 17 in this chapter · 31 min read

In short

  • A welcome series is a set of automatic emails. Each new subscriber gets it right after signing up.
  • Make the first email short and friendly. Give a discount that ends on a clear date.
  • Send an email every 2-3 days, not several a day. Stop once the person opens an email or buys.
  • Send the new-customer discount only to people who have never bought from you.
  • Only state a deadline you can keep.
More key points
  • Send new people a warm welcome email first. Say how often you will write, and give them a reason to open the next one.
  • Every free item you give away needs its own follow-up emails. Without them, people take the free item and never buy.
  • The first 30 days after someone joins are the most important. Interest is highest then, so send the strongest emails early.
  • Keep each welcome email short, with one clear offer and one button.

Welcome flow structure

5 min
  • A welcome series is the first automatic set of emails a new subscriber gets. A subscriber is someone who gave you their email address to receive your emails.
  • It starts when someone signs up through a pop-up (a box that appears on your website) or the footer newsletter form (the sign-up box at the bottom of the page).
  • Wheelio is an example of a pop-up app (a tool that creates pop-ups).
  • In Clavio (an email tool), the series starts when a visitor subscribes to a chosen list, such as Newsletter, through the sign-up pop-up.
  • The first email goes out immediately.
  • Later emails follow after set delays (waiting times).
  • In Klaviyo (an email tool), the series starts for each person who joins through a sign-up form.
  • The first email goes out right after subscription.
  • The three-day structure of Source C:
    1. Day 1: welcome email, sent immediately after subscription.
      • Keep it short and simple.
      • Thank the subscriber.
      • Explain that the newsletter keeps them updated with promotions and offers.
      • Include the brand's social media links.
      • Also send an SMS (text message) if your budget allows.
    2. Day 2: brand introduction email, sent 24 hours later.
      • Introduce the brand.
      • Go deep on product benefits, technology, design, and craftsmanship (the skill used to make the product).
      • Explain what sets you apart from competitors (other sellers).
      • For electronics, explain the technology benefits.
      • For jewelry, highlight the design, the designer's experience, and the handmade quality.
      • For any product, explain why it is worth buying.
    3. Day 3: user-generated content email (photos or posts made by customers).
      • Include a natural product photo (not a professional one).
      • Include a text testimonial (a customer's written praise) in this form: "We got an awesome comment on our Facebook/product page: [Customer name] faced [problem], tried our product, [their positive feedback]."
      • This adds social proof (showing that other people trust you) without video.
  • The welcome flow order for a community-style brand:
    1. Tell new subscribers they are joining a community, family or club.
    2. Offer a welcoming gift.
    3. If you have a collection (a group of products), add a collection block after the welcome content.
    4. Add your brand story in the footer.
  • A pop-up welcome flow with a 5-10% welcome kit discount:
    1. Trigger the flow (the event that starts it) when someone signs up through the pop-up or the footer newsletter form.
    2. Send the first email straight away.
      • Welcome them to the community, family or club.
      • Give a welcome kit discount of 5-10%.
      • Add a link to a collection.
      • Close with a short brand story in the footer.
    3. If the email is not opened after one day, send the same email again with the same offer.
    4. Keep sending emails every 2-3 days, not several times a day, until the subscriber opens an email or buys.
    5. Make the content educational (teaching something useful).
      • Address the customer's use case (how they use the product) and pain points (their problems).
      • Avoid hard sales pitches, such as "buy now" repeated in every email.
    6. End each email with at most one line of pitch, such as checking out the collection.
  • The welcome flow has three emails with a 10% storewide discount (applies to the whole store).
  • The deadline gets shorter in each email: last 3 days, then 48 hours, then 24 hours.
  • The same discount code is used in all three emails.
  • Some sellers run longer introduction sequences of 40 to 50 emails.
  • Choose the length you can realistically write and maintain.
  • A shorter sequence that gets finished is more useful than a longer one that is abandoned.
  • Stated lengths for welcome or introduction sequences differ by source. Use the length that fits your capacity.

  • The first emails must bond the subscriber with your Attractive Character (the likeable personality your brand shows in messages).
  • A boring first email usually means the subscriber stops opening your messages.
  • Place your most emotional story early in the welcome sequence.
  • This builds a bond before you pitch.
  • Readers who connect with the story tend to open and buy more.
  • The second email of a store welcome series should show:
    • Bestsellers (most-bought items), active deals or promotions.
    • Customer reviews or testimonials, so new subscribers can judge product quality.
    • It can also explain the store's mission (its purpose) and values, and how the products match them.
    • Keep it focused on buying decisions, not on follow-up actions.
  • The third email should build the relationship, not push a sale.
    • Invite subscribers to follow your social accounts or join a loyalty program (a rewards club).
    • Link useful help, such as product guides, size charts, or a customer service phone line, so they can buy with confidence.
    • Put the social follow request last.
    • Only include it if the store actually has social accounts.
  • The last welcome email shifts from brand talk to product.
    • A best-sellers block shows what the discount buys.
    • Use this switch when the message moves to the final deadline, so the subscriber sees products instead of another plea.
    • Only show items that are in stock.
  • Sample flow with timed steps.
    • A Time Delay step sets both the gap and the send time between emails.
    • Email one goes out on Day 0.
    • A Wait 3 days step puts email two on Day 3 at 12:00 PM.
    • A Wait 4 days step puts email three on Day 7 at 12:00 PM.
    • A noon send is a weak fit for welcome emails, so set the delay steps to send around 8 PM instead.

Timing and spacing

2 min
  • Trigger and first send:
    • In Shopify (an online store platform), insert a 1-minute wait after the email subscription trigger.
    • This lets processing finish before conditional checks run.
    • General, older: the first email after someone subscribes or asks about a product usually goes out after a delay of about 5 to 10 minutes.
    • Use the 1-minute wait in Shopify automations.
    • Use the 5-10 minute delay as the general reference.
  • Send time: schedule welcome emails for around 8 PM, not noon.
    • Lunchtime sends were judged a poor fit for this series.
    • Avoid a 12 PM send time after a multi-day wait.
  • Gap between welcome emails: several sources differ, so choose by offer deadline.
    • The second email moves from a 3-day delay to a 1-day delay.
    • A longer gap lets the buyer lose interest.
    • Email spacing of about 24 hours: email 1 on signup, email 2 about 24 hours later, email 3 about 48 hours later.
    • For a three-day offer, this keeps each email's deadline true.
    • After one day, 48 hours remain. After two days, 24 hours remain.
    • Lead-magnet flow (a free gift given for an email address): wait 24 hours between each further email.
    • Soap-opera sequence: five emails, sent once per day after signup.
    • Ongoing frequency: every 2-3 days, not several times a day, until the subscriber opens an email or buys.
  • Bestsellers and social follow-up in Klaviyo:
    • The bestsellers, offers and reviews email is sent 3 days after signup.
    • The follow-us-on-social-media email is sent 4 days after that.
    • So the social ask is the last message.
  • Source ambiguity: one Klaviyo procedure is unclear.
    • It labels the 3-day wait as "Email 1 is sent after 3 days' wait."
    • It then lists the bestsellers email as Email 2 after a 4-day wait.
    • This conflicts with the 3-day and 4-day timing above.
    • Read the 3-day wait as the delay before the bestsellers email.
    • Read the 4-day wait as the delay before the social email.
  • The 30-day master flow (a long main sequence) spreads early emails over the first days.
    • For prospects: Day 0, Day 2, Day 5.
    • For first-time buyers: Day 1, Day 7, Day 21.
  • Offer deadlines and timing are covered in the discount section below.

  • Deadline logic:
    • The welcome series deadline is 72 hours.
    • Or use the expiry date of the coupon or discount code, if that is the shorter limit.
    • Three-email flow: last 3 days, then 48 hours, then 24 hours.
    • In Clavio, email 1 states a three-day deadline.
    • In Clavio, the reminder (email 2) says the offer is available for 48 hours only.
    • Offer clock (a countdown shown in emails) in the Source E structure:
      • Welcome email with the discount code at 0 hours.
      • Bestseller showcase at 48 hours to go.
      • Social proof story at 24 hours to go.
      • Urgency email at 12 hours to go.
      • Last-chance email when the offer ends in 4 hours.
    • Only state a deadline you can keep.
    • A true deadline turns interest into action.
    • A fake one destroys trust.
  • Discount code formula for the welcome email:
    • The subject line names the offer and adds a deadline.
    • The body thanks the new subscriber.
    • The body shows the discount code in bold on a colored background.
    • The body states where to use it.
    • The body states when it expires.
    • The code is the reason the person subscribed, so it must be easy to find and clearly time-limited.
  • Examples from the same formula:
    • Subject "Your $5 welcome code ends Friday", with the code and a Shop now button.
    • Subject "Your 48 hours to claim $5 off start now", with a short thank-you line, the code, and a link to the best-selling collection.
  • Separate codes per channel: give each welcome channel (such as email or SMS) its own discount code.
    • Example: a 10% sitewide code for email and a 15% VIP code for SMS.
    • Separate codes show which channel brings redemptions (uses of the code).
    • They also make the offers easy to tell apart at checkout and in reporting.
  • Automatic application: before saving a discount link, confirm the discount is set to apply automatically at checkout.
    • The customer never has to type the code.
    • Use the link itself as the test.
    • The preview in Klaviyo does not make discount links clickable.
  • Use the same discount code in all emails of one flow.
  • Offer and reward messaging in the Clavio reminder:
    • The discount is valid until the stated time.
    • The subscriber can still load the cart after that.
  • Newer and older discount advice:
    • The 5% first coupon in the lead-magnet flow is from Source A, undated.
    • The 5-10% welcome kit is from Source C, undated.
    • Both apply to different flow types.

Filters: buyers, abandoners and channels

2 min
  • Audience for the discount: limit it to new subscribers who have never placed an order.
    • Then buyers do not receive the new-customer discount again.
  • Klaviyo audience setup: select the audience of new sign-ups who have never purchased.
    • Then choose a static coupon (one fixed code for everyone).
  • Klaviyo prebuilt flow (a ready-made flow from the tool):
    • It branches between subscribers who have never ordered and customers who have ordered at least once.
    • Customers with at least one order see a separate flow path.
    • So the template covers both groups.
  • Beginners should start with the prebuilt Klaviyo welcome flow.
  • Source E flow filter (a rule that decides who gets the flow):
    • Add the filter "Placed Order zero times since starting this flow".
    • Anyone who buys is removed from the welcome flow.
    • They stop receiving its emails.
    • So the flow only targets new customers.
  • SMS split: add a Conditional Split (a branch that sends people one way or another based on a rule).
    • Use the condition "Is not consented to receive SMS".
    • Put the SMS message on the No branch. The No branch means subscribers who did consent to SMS.
    • Let the Yes branch carry only the email path.
  • Shopify abandoned-cart exclusion: an abandoned cart is a cart someone filled but did not buy from.
    • Exclude customers tagged "abandoned cart" (a label on the customer record) from the welcome automation.
    • They enter a separate abandoned cart automation.
    • Sending both sequences creates duplicate emails and wastes effort.
  • Shopify rules used in the automation:
    • The first rule is Customer > Tags > does not include > abandoned cart.
    • The second rule is Customer > Amount spent > equals > 0 (they have never spent money).
  • Pop-up trigger: the welcome flow is triggered for people who enter their email in the pop-up installed on your store.
    • It is the first automated sequence a new subscriber receives.
  • Klaviyo newer flow experience: the pre-built welcome flow can be used with the newer experience.
    • Beginners should start with this pre-built flow.

2 min

Create a welcome email automation in Shopify Email (Shopify's built-in email tool):

  1. Open Shopify admin > Apps > Email.
  2. Click Create automation.
  3. Select Create custom automation (top right).
  4. Pick the trigger "Customer subscribed to email marketing".
  5. Click + to add a step.
  6. Choose Action.
  7. Select "Wait for a set amount of time".
  8. Set the duration to 1 minute.
  9. Add the next step.
  10. Choose Condition.
  11. Create the first rule: Customer > Tags > does not include > abandoned cart.
  12. Add a second rule: Customer > Amount spent > equals > 0.
  13. Add a step.
  14. Choose Action.
  15. Go to Installed app actions > Email > Send marketing email.
  16. Click Select template (a ready-made layout).
  17. Pick the "Welcome with discount" template.
  18. Click Select.
  19. Write the subject as "welcome to Source A merch, discount code inside".
  20. Verify the From field (the sender).
  21. Write the body: "Hey, we're so happy you're here. Enjoy this discount on your first purchase."
  22. Pick a discount code from the dropdown (example: save 10).
  23. Send a test email to yourself.
  24. Click Save.
  25. If prompted to select the discount code again, do so.
  26. Click + to add an internal notification.
  27. Choose Action.
  28. Keep Shopify selected.
  29. Pick Send internal email.
  30. Enter your email as the recipient.
  31. Set the subject to "new lead - [add variable: created at]".
  32. Write the message: "new lead created - [add variable: customer email]".
  33. At the top, rename "custom marketing automation" to "welcome email".
  34. Click Turn on workflow.
  35. Click Turn on.

Go-live checks for a welcome flow (switching it on for real):

  1. Confirm you are in the correct client Klaviyo account.
  2. Check that the trigger, the flow filter, the conditional split, and the SMS and email steps are all set.
  3. Turn off the client's other welcome flows, so two flows do not send to the same subscribers.
  4. Use Review and Turn On to switch the flow live.
  5. Notify the client that the flow is live.
  • Do not leave the client's old welcome flows running alongside the new one.
  • Subscribers would receive duplicate messages.
  • Confirm the old flows are switched off before going live.

Welcome email structure from Source C. For the welcome email formula, see the discount section.

Email 1 structure (soap opening, which sets the stage):

  • Start with a warm welcome from the character. The character is the voice your emails are written in.
  • Set expectations. Say how often emails arrive. Promise a valuable gift or lesson soon.
  • Use this formula:
    • Introduce yourself.
    • Share one personal milestone that shows you know your topic. This is your credibility, the reason people trust you.
    • State the send schedule, meaning when the next emails arrive.
    • Tease tomorrow's email in a P.S. (postscript, a short note at the end) with its subject line.
  • Subscribers know what to expect. They have a reason to open the next message.
  • Example 1, subject "Welcome: Day 1 of 5". Body: "Hi, I am Sam. I started my first online shop from my kitchen table. Tomorrow I will share the checklist I used to get the first 100 orders. P.S. Look for the subject line Day 2 of 5: The checklist."
  • Example 2, subject "Day 1 of 5: A quick hello from me". Body: "Welcome to the group. I will send one short email each day for the next five days. Tomorrow I will show you the one mistake that cost me my first six months."

Email 2 structure (high drama and backstory):

  • Open at the emotional peak of a story, the most intense moment.
  • Rewind to the events that led there. This is the backstory.
  • Stop at the wall you hit, the problem that blocked you.
  • Leave the answer for the next email.
  • The reader's situation should mirror the backstory.
  • Starting in the middle of a dramatic moment grabs attention faster than starting at the beginning.
  • Example 1, subject "Day 2 of 5: The night my account got frozen". Body: "I stared at the locked screen at midnight, knowing rent was due in four days. Two years earlier I had been sure this business was simple. Here is how I got stuck, and why the answer surprised me. Tomorrow I will show you exactly what I did."
  • Example 2, subject "Day 2 of 5: The month I almost quit". Body: "My order count had been flat for ninety days, and my savings were nearly gone. I had tried every tutorial I could find. I will tell you what finally broke the pattern tomorrow."

Email 3 structure (epiphany, a sudden realization):

  • Describe the moment you realized something.
  • This often happens when a simple observation shows a flaw in the old approach.
  • Connect the new insight directly to the product you sell.
  • End with a link to the offer.
  • The subscriber now sees the offer as the answer to the drama you built.
  • Example 1, subject "Day 3 of 5: The math that changed everything". Body: "I worked out how much I earned per hour compared with a single product that sold hundreds of copies a day. Selling a system once beat selling my time forever. The method behind it is what I want to show you. Here is the link: [link]."
  • Example 2, subject "Day 3 of 5: The tiny detail I had missed". Body: "One day I noticed that my best customers all came from the same place. That observation became my whole strategy. The short training that teaches it is here: [link]."

Email 4 structure (hidden benefits):

  • List two or three benefits the reader has not thought about. These are non-obvious benefits.
  • Phrase each one as: "Sure, you will get X, but you will also get Y."
  • Back each one with a short story.
  • Present the offer with a try-before-you-pay option, meaning a trial where they pay only if they like it.
  • Non-obvious benefits give a new reason to act and deepen the bond with the character.
  • Example 1, subject "Day 4 of 5: The benefit nobody mentions". Body: "Sure, you will earn more, but you will also get the freedom to take a Tuesday off. Last month I took three days away, and my store kept selling. Normally this course costs 300 USD, but you can take it free and pay only if you find it useful. Sign up here: [link]."
  • Example 2, subject "Day 4 of 5: More than money". Body: "Sure, you will grow your income, but you will also spend more evenings with your family. I did not expect that. The full program is free to try, and you only pay if it is worth it to you."

Email 5 structure (urgency and call to action; urgency means a reason to act now):

  • Give a real reason the offer will change or disappear. Examples: a webinar (live online class) starting tomorrow, or a discount ending.
  • State the price the reader will pay if they wait.
  • Give one clear link.
  • A true deadline turns interest into action. A fake one destroys trust.
  • Example 1, subject "Day 5 of 5: Last call". Body: "The free-access window closes tonight at midnight. After that the course returns to its full price of 3,000 USD. If you want to try the complete training before paying, grab your place here: [link]."
  • Example 2, subject "Day 5 of 5: Seats are almost gone". Body: "Only ten seats remain for Thursday's live session. After it starts, late entries pay the full fee. Reserve your seat now: [link]."

Welcome email principle: keep the content educational. Avoid hard sales pitches (pushy selling).

Soap-opera and story sequences

2 min
  • Purpose: the first emails after someone joins your list must bond the subscriber with your Attractive Character (the character your emails are built around).
  • A boring first email usually means the subscriber stops opening your messages.
  • Use the first sequence to build an emotional connection.
  • Soap opera sequence: five emails, sent once per day after signup. The emails tell a story over several days, like a TV soap.
    1. Email 1, set the stage: thank the subscriber, introduce yourself as the character, explain how often emails arrive, and promise a valuable gift or lesson in the next email.
    2. Email 2, open with high drama: start at the most intense moment, then tell the backstory that led there. Stop at the wall you hit. Open a loop for email 3 without giving the answer. (A loop is an open question that makes the reader want the answer.)
    3. Email 3, epiphany: reveal the discovery that changed things for you. Tie it directly to the offer, with a link.
    4. Email 4, hidden benefits: list two or three non-obvious benefits of the solution. Tell a parable (a short story with a lesson) that shows one. Present the offer with a risk-reducing try-before-you-pay option, meaning one that lowers the risk of buying.
    5. Email 5, urgency and call to action: give a real reason to act now. End with one clear call to action (the step you want them to take).
  • Open a loop in each email and close it in the next one.
    • Each email ends with a reason to open the following one.
    • Tease a result or secret without revealing the details. Give the answer later.
    • Repeat this pattern from email to email.
  • The five-email soap sequence is a short version of a longer introduction sequence. See the length table in the structure section.
  • Trust series before a higher-priced ask (asking for a bigger purchase):
    • Send a five-part email series. It tells the brand's story, the problems the brand solved, and how to solve them in the niche (a narrow market segment).
    • Then make the larger offer, once trust is established.
  • Emotional story placement: see the structure section.

30-day master flow

1 min
  • A flow is a set of automatic emails sent on a schedule.
  • The first 30 days after a subscriber joins or a customer buys are the most important engagement period (the time when people read and click the most).
  • Interest is highest then.
  • Use this period to build the relationship. Drive a first purchase for prospects (people who have not bought yet) or a repeat purchase for customers.
  • A 30-day master flow is one automated email series. It covers both pre-purchase (before buying) and post-purchase (after buying) contacts, no matter how the person entered your business. It is built so that the early emails carry the most engagement weight.
    • Example one: a 30-day flow for prospects who opted in (signed up to receive something) via a guide: welcome email on day 0, product story on day 2, first offer on day 5.
    • Example two: a 30-day flow for first-time buyers: order follow-up on day 1, usage tip on day 7, repeat-purchase offer on day 21.
  • Build the first 30-day master flow this way:
    • Start building it right away.
    • Add one email every day or every other day.
    • Accept that the complete flow will take a few weeks, not a single day.
  • Replicate an existing master flow for each new entry point to the email list (an entry point is a way people join your list).
    1. Copy the complete flow.
    2. Update only the specific emails that need to be more relevant to the new entry point.
    3. Keep shared early emails, such as the welcome email, unchanged.
  • Integrate introductory emails for the loyalty program (a reward program for repeat buyers), the referral program (a program where customers invite friends), and the customer feedback survey into your existing 30-day new customer welcome sequence. Then all new customers receive them automatically.

Content-based nurture sequences

1 min
  • A nurture sequence is a series of emails that builds trust and moves a lead (a person who gave their contact details) toward buying.
  • Send automated sequences to subscribers grouped by the content piece or product page they opted in on.
  • Emails lead down the purchase path (the steps toward buying) linked to that content.
  • The first few emails should follow the call to action in that content, such as buying a recommended product.
    • Example pattern: an opt-in from a guide comparing two products leads to emails that explain the guide's pick and then push that product.
  • Quiz and buyer's guide follow-up flow:
    1. The visitor completes a product quiz.
    2. A follow-up email sequence sends product recommendations based on the quiz answers.
    3. The sequence points the visitor to the buyer's guide (a guide that helps people choose what to buy).
    4. If the visitor downloads the buyer's guide first, the sequence points them to the quiz instead.
  • Giveaway leads: leads captured by the giveaway funnel (the path from a free prize offer to signing up) go into email nurture sequences. The sequences aim to convert them over time. These email flows also generate part of the measured revenue (the money you can track back to the emails).
  • To launch quickly, duplicate (copy) your existing welcome flows or welcome series. Run a similar funnel alongside them.

Lead-magnet follow-up flows

2 min
  • A lead magnet is a free item you give in return for an email address. Some courses call it a bribe.
  • Collecting opt-ins without a follow-up email sequence produces leads who never buy.
  • The money from a lead magnet comes from the follow-up. So every opt-in must enter a flow.
  • Build a separate flow for every lead magnet. Each requested item then receives its own follow-up sequence.
  • A single flow is enough at the start. Add more as you create more free items.
  • Lead-magnet email flow in Klaviyo:
    1. Immediately send the requested free item with the download link.
    2. Wait 24 hours.
    3. Send a welcome email introducing the company, with a first coupon (a discount code) such as 5 percent off.
    4. Wait 24 hours between each further email. Keep making offers.
  • A new lead should first go through a welcome flow of about four to five emails. After that, they move into the main broadcast list (the list that gets the same emails sent to everyone).
  • Beginners can start with about one broadcast email a week. Increase the frequency as the list grows.
  • Download-delivery emails (emails that send the free file) are a reference point:
    • In one published benchmark (a result from another seller's data), welcome and thank-you emails with a download link for a lead magnet had an 88.7% open rate (share of people who opened it).
    • They had a 35.07% click-through rate (share of people who clicked the link).
    • Use these figures to measure your own download-delivery emails.
  • The email that delivers a lead magnet is opened by most subscribers. So it can carry a second offer.
  • Use it as a second conversion point (a second chance to make a sale). For example, promote a membership discount or a related product.
  • Influencer digital products (digital products sold through an influencer):
    1. Send a five-email sequence that provides value first.
    2. Then send the new subscribers a 24-hour promotion with a coupon to convert them into buyers.

Ongoing welcome-series handling

1 min
  • Tag-based and purchase-based exits (ways to remove people from a flow). A tag is a label on a contact.
    • Keep the welcome flow limited to new subscribers.
    • Use the buyer and abandoner filters above (filters that sort people by what they did).
    • This stops the flow from mailing people who already bought, or who sit in a separate abandoned cart sequence (emails sent to people who left items in their cart).
  • Use separate codes per channel, such as one code for Instagram and one for email. Check redemption (use of the code) by channel in reporting.
  • Smart sending (a setting that changes when each person gets an email) and tracking:
    • Set Smart sending off.
    • Set UTM tracking (labels added to links so analytics, the reports that show visitor data, can see where visitors came from) on for every welcome email.
    • The flow then sends on the set schedule and shows its traffic in analytics.
  • Saving a reusable store template (a saved design you can reuse): save the first email as a template. Later emails then reuse the logo, images and layout.
  • Reviewing the flow after launch:
    • Check each email's delay (the wait before it sends).
    • Check the discount link and the send time.
    • Then review opened and clicked results.
  • Duplicate flows (copies of an existing flow):
    • Confirm that the old welcome flows are off before you switch the new flow on.
    • Notify the client that the flow is live.
  • Discount link test: test the link itself. The preview in Klaviyo does not make discount links clickable.
  • Ongoing frequency after the welcome series: continue with one message every 2-3 days until the subscriber opens an email or buys.
  • Ongoing sequence after the welcome series: move new leads from the welcome flow into the main broadcast list.

Further details

1 min
  • Build one autoresponder sequence (a set of emails sent automatically, in order, after signup). It delivers the bribe (the lead magnet) immediately, then follows up on a schedule.
  • Each email is scheduled for a specific day. So email one goes out on day one, email two on day two, and so on.

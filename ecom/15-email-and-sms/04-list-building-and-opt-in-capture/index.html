List building and opt-in capture

Section 15.4 · 4 of 17 in this chapter · 31 min read

In short

  • An email list is how you keep visitors who did not buy. Ask for their email, and offer something they want in return.
  • Use pop-ups that appear after a short delay, not the moment the page opens. Show them on phones too.
  • Make the discount code exact: the same name in the pop-up and in Shopify, and one clear amount.
  • Build the list before you need it. A Black Friday sale brings the most money once you already have a list.
  • Ask for the email with something useful, such as a guide. A discount alone is not enough.
More key points
  • Never buy email lists. Only email people who said yes to you.
  • Use double opt-in in Klaviyo, the email tool. People must click a confirmation link, which helps block fake sign-ups.
  • Name every list after the place its people came from.
  • Set up a welcome series before the pop-up goes live.

List Building and Opt-in Capture

1 min

Why the list matters

2 min
  • About 80 percent of store visitors leave without buying and never come back. Collect their email address or phone number so you can reach them again later (this is called retargeting, showing messages again to people who already visited). Email is the main way to turn these visitors into buyers.
  • 80 to 90 percent of website visitors leave without buying. A sign-up form that collects email addresses is the main way to win them back later.
  • A list built from visitors and offers is a traffic source of your own. It does not depend only on ads or organic social posts (free posts, not paid ads).
  • A subscriber list can be sold to again and again over time. So lead ads should first collect the opt-in (the visitor agreeing to join your list). Direct sales on the first click matter less.
  • Build an email list from visitors who have not bought yet. Use email to keep first-time buyers engaged, so they come back for a second and third purchase.
  • Use the email list you collect from visitors for follow-up. A visitor who does not buy on the first visit may still buy later.
  • Capture the visitor's email while they are on the store. Then send follow-up emails to encourage them to finish the purchase. This recovers buyers who did not buy on their first visit.
  • Use email campaigns (planned groups of emails) for visitors who did not buy. The list is an extra channel to bring back warm traffic (people who already know your store) after a cold ad (an ad shown to people who do not know you).
  • A large promotion, such as a Black Friday sale (a big shopping day in late November), brings the most money once a business has an established email list. Build that list from abandoned carts (shopping carts left without buying), exit-intent overlay sign-ups (pop-ups that appear as someone leaves) and remarketing audiences (groups of past visitors to show ads to).
  • An incentive (a reward) that gets visitors to opt in is tied to a working opt-in and email program. The source links this to a 10% boost in customer value.

Prospect lists versus customer lists

1 min
  • Prospect lists (people who have not bought yet) do not grow on their own the way customer lists do. They need deliberate opt-in offers and several list-building methods running at the same time. They take time to grow, so starting early and being persistent matters. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Customer lists grow by themselves through purchases. The list-building methods below target non-buyers only. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Seven proven methods build a prospect list: content opt-ins, discount offers, quizzes, surveys, contests and giveaways, sales event opt-ins, and lead magnets (a free gift for an email address) as the content-based version. Content opt-ins are the main workhorse. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Content opt-ins (a sign-up offered inside an article or guide) work best for visitors at the top and middle of the funnel (the path from first visit to purchase). These are people researching a problem who need teaching first. For visitors near the point of purchase they are often weak, because those visitors need less teaching. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Interest-based newsletters (regular emails on one topic) that come from top-of-funnel articles (early-stage articles that teach) are a useful way to capture visitors. Once trust is built, subscribers can receive discounts or new-launch messages. This turns anonymous readers into future buyers.
  • Capture email addresses in as many places on the site as possible. These emails are needed to remarket to shoppers who do not buy on the first visit. Older advice (2019).
  • Use email capture at several points, such as the footer (the bottom of every page) and an exit-intent overlay. This builds the list that later drives sales. Older advice (2022).
  • Use the collected emails for remarketing, not only as a newsletter list. Older advice (2018).
  • Collect emails with exit-intent overlays and at checkout. Use the list to make offers and to remarket to past buyers and opt-in visitors. Older advice (2019).

Choosing the offer

4 min

Match the offer to the goal

  • Match the opt-in offer to what subscribers want in the long run. Not only a first-order discount. A 5 or 10 percent coupon can work in the short term. An offer tied to the customer's real goal keeps subscribers close to the brand over time.
  • A bribe (the reward you offer) only earns an email address when it gives something the visitor already wants. Build it from the site's topic and the visitor's likely stage. Do not build it from what the affiliate (a partner who earns commission on sales) would like to promote.
  • Offer value that moves the customer toward the goal behind the product, not only the product itself. A podcast microphone buyer wants a successful show, so guides on launching and growing a podcast add value.
  • The list is a two-way exchange. The reader gets what they want. You get their attention and, eventually, their purchase.
  • Asking visitors openly for their email in an opt-in pop-up is right when the offer is truly useful and matches what the subscriber will receive. Visitors who want the content expect the request. Hesitating to ask weakens the list-building step. The promise in the pop-up must be exactly what the list delivers.
  • Use a community or cause in the offer. An offer that pairs a small incentive with a cause the reader already cares about gives a reason to subscribe that fits the article's topic.

Incentive types

  • Common incentive opt-in offers on Shopify (online store software) stores include 10% off the next order, $10 off the first order, and a spin-the-wheel pop-up. The wheel gives a random discount in exchange for an email address. A concrete discount makes signing up feel worth it. A generic newsletter invitation gives no clear reward.
  • A sign-up offer that gives a discount for an email address works because a clear, concrete reward makes signing up feel worth it. Two examples in the same pattern: Subscribe now and get 15% off your first purchase. and Join the list for free shipping on your next order.
  • The sign-up incentive is a discount, typically 5 percent, 5 dollars or 10 percent off, offered for the email address.
  • Offer an incentive such as 10% to 20% off for signing up on a sign-up form page.
  • Offer free shipping at the moment a visitor arrives, in exchange for their email address. In the example, this captured emails at a rate of about 20%.
  • If a discount does not fit the business, a buyer's guide (a helpful guide for choosing a product) or a product quiz can replace it as the reason to sign up. After signing up, an automated sequence (a set of emails sent on their own) follows.
  • Offer a valuable lead magnet, such as a buyer's guide or a quiz with results that unlock after entering an email. Use this when a discount alone does not work. Older advice (2022).
  • There are three list-building offer types for campaigns.
    1. Membership or subscription: exclusive member-only deals, limited spots (for example 100), free to join.
    2. Contest or giveaway: enter to win a high-value prize (for example a $1,000 gift card), subscribe to enter.
    3. Lead magnet (evergreen, meaning it stays useful over time): a valuable free resource (PDF, guide, template, checklist) usually priced at $19 to $29, available free for a limited time or limited quantity (for example 100 copies).
  • Replace the default footer sign-up text with a prize-entry line that names a monthly giveaway. People sign up for a chance to win more readily than for updates. Two examples: Subscribe for your chance to win a $100 gift card this month and Join the list to enter this month's giveaway of a free starter kit.

Lead magnets, buyer's guides, quizzes and giveaways

3 min

Lead magnet design

  • A lead magnet should give real value to the reader. A useful resource people want to keep works better over the long term than a single discount for a referring site (a site that sends you visitors). It should also look like a marketing brochure, with the company introduced and an offer included.
  • About 30 to 40 percent of people who opt in open the email and click the download link for a lead magnet. Make the content worth reading for that group. People who do not read it still receive the promotional emails.
  • A free downloadable buyer's guide for the product category can serve as a lead magnet, exchanged for an email sign-up. Adding a coupon after sign-up gives the prospect a second reason to buy. It also builds trust before Shopping ads (ads that show products in Google search) are shown.
  • A free buyer's guide for a niche (a narrow market) works well as a content asset (a useful piece of content). It captures emails you can use in promotions later. Older advice (2022).
  • Content pages should include an opt-in form to collect email leads (contacts who may buy), even when the page also links out to third-party sites. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Every blog post should offer readers a chance to join the email list. For example, offer a free download in exchange for their email address. Older advice (2021-22).

Buyer's guide procedures

Procedure A: lead magnet with a Klaviyo flow (an automatic email sequence). Older advice (2022).

  1. Create a buyer's guide PDF for the niche.
  2. Build an opt-in page on Shopify with an embedded Klaviyo sign-up box (a form built into the page) that asks for the email address and promises to send the guide there.
  3. Set the sign-up form to add the subscriber to a list named for the guide, such as buyer's guide, or to apply a tag (a label on the contact).
  4. Create a Klaviyo flow triggered when the tag is applied or the subscriber joins the list.
  5. Add a short wait of 5 minutes, then send the email containing the PDF.

Procedure B: Shopify page or exit-intent overlay. Older advice in the source year range (2023 source).

  1. Create a Shopify page titled Buyer's Guide with an opt-in form asking for an email address.
  2. Or set up an exit-intent overlay that appears as visitors leave and offers the guide for an email address.
  3. Connect the form to a Shopify email automation that sends the PDF guide to the subscriber.

Procedure C: buyer's guide on a dedicated page. Older advice (2022).

  1. Create a content page on the store that describes the buyer's guide. It should not be a product page.
  2. Embed the Klaviyo opt-in form on that page.
  3. Send the guide by email through Klaviyo when the visitor submits the form. You can also use the exit-intent overlay alongside it.

Exit-intent text for the buyer's guide, ready to use. Keep the email field as the only input, and send the guide automatically after submission. Older advice (2022).

Before you go, enter your email and we will send you our free buyer's guide.

Quizzes, surveys and contests

  • Offer a quiz with results that unlock after entering an email.
  • In a contest or giveaway, extra entries can be earned through actions such as social sharing and referrals (friends bringing friends). This increases the number of opt-ins. Promote the contest on social media to grow both entries and reach. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Instagram giveaways can collect email addresses for a new brand's list. Followers enter the draw by submitting their email. This suits a business that still needs to grow its audience before it has a large list. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Contest, giveaway and membership offers are set out in the three-offer structure above.

Sales event opt-ins

  • Opt-in form for a sales event: one field for an email address, a "Sign Up" button, and directly beneath the form this note. Older advice (2021-22).
*Submitting this form means you agree to get our emails, and you may opt out whenever you like.
  • A large promotion, such as a Black Friday sale, brings the most money once a business has an established email list.

Gamified capture

  • Besides standard sign-up boxes in the sidebar, footer and abandoned-cart emails, you can use exit-intent overlays and gamified spin-to-win pop-ups. Wheelio is one such tool. These offer a prize for an email address.
  • These tools collect leads from visitors who already come to the site. They add to the reach of paid traffic. They do not replace it.

Where to place capture points

3 min

Placement principles

  • Install an opt-in on the store before you publish content. Articles alone do not build an email list.
  • Put the email sign-up on the store even before you plan email campaigns. It collects subscribers into the Shopify admin (the store's back office) from day one. Keep the heading short and clear about what visitors get.
  • Each email opt-in on the site should have its own email list. Place specific opt-ins on relevant content pages. Review exit-intent pop-ups as an opt-in opportunity. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Map every page of the site and note which opt-ins already run there.
    1. Map every page of the site (home page, collection pages, blog page, blog posts, product pages) and note which opt-ins already run there.
    2. Identify the pages without any opt-in opportunity.
    3. Decide which opt-in to place on each gap, matched to the visitor's funnel stage.
  • Content opt-ins need their own list or segment (a group of contacts), with an automated email sequence behind them. The sequence should move the subscriber from the content toward a purchase. For example, it can lead someone who downloaded a how-to guide toward the relevant product. Older advice (2021-22).
  • A newsletter works as an opt-in when it is specific about its content and frequency. For example, one issue each month with a stated topic. Offer an immediate download or a sample of the newsletter on the page, so visitors can judge its value before they opt in. Older advice (2021-22).

Footer

  • Add an email opt-in to the footer for the mailing list. Niche theme stores (stores built on a narrow-topic design template) rely heavily on email to follow up, run holiday promotions and win repeat sales.
  • In the theme's Newsletter section, set the heading, subheading, email placeholder text (the grey hint inside the box) and button label. Include a short promise, such as stay updated with offers, so visitors know what they sign up for.
  • On a simple product store, place an email sign-up box in the footer with the heading Sign Up And Save and the line Get early access to new product releases and special discounts. Add a 40% OFF sale banner with Sale Ends SOON next to it to create urgency (a feeling that the offer ends soon). Ask visitors where they are based in the sign-up form.

Exit intent

  • An exit-intent overlay (a pop-up that appears as someone is about to leave) that collects emails is the main source of email leads for a store. It builds a list from real visitors who chose to opt in. Older advice (2024 source, same principle repeated across sources).
  • In the source example, the exit-intent overlay produced most of the collected emails.
  • Exit-intent overlays on the store help gather new email subscribers organically (without paid ads). This is how a list grows after launch, alongside abandoned cart contacts.
  • Exit-intent overlays capture visitors' email addresses when they try to leave. Together with the abandoned cart flow, they build the email list organically after launch. This works even if the store is new before Q4 (the last three months of the year).
  • Use an exit-intent overlay that captures emails and starts an automated follow-up sequence. Pair it with abandoned cart emails. Older advice (2020).
  • If you are not sure whether a pop-up comes from Shopify or Klaviyo, check Klaviyo first. A Klaviyo pop-up is visible there.

Blog and content pages

  • After an email signup section is added to the Manhattan (a store design template) blog post template and saved, the opt-in appears at the bottom of every blog post, below the content. It is visible on the live store after a page refresh (reloading the page).
  • Build an opt-in form on the content page, not only on product pages. This collects leads from visitors who are still researching.

Dedicated sign-up page

  • Build an email list with a sign-up form page on the Shopify store. It asks for email, name and optionally phone number, instead of a buy-now product page. Offer an incentive such as 10% to 20% off for signing up.
  • Send lead generation traffic to a page whose only goal is to collect name and email. Do not sell a product there. Ads that promote a discount or a best-seller page skip the list-building step. They lose the buyer who is not ready yet.

Pop-up set-up

5 min

Pop-up timing and triggers

Sources use different triggers (the action that makes the pop-up appear). Each is tied to the tool it describes. Use the setting that matches the platform you are setting up.

Tool or platform Trigger Ids
Clavio pop-up Exit intent, plus a 10-second delay after page load ,
Klaviyo pop-up Show after 10 seconds, plus a scroll rule at 30 to 50 percent of the page, with the match set to show only when all selected rules are met
Shopify theme pop-up Delay timer, for example after 60 seconds of visitor inactivity (not moving the mouse or scrolling)
  • A pop-up that appears immediately on landing is too aggressive and annoys visitors. A 10-second delay is preferred. Adding a scroll trigger at 30 to 50 percent means the visitor has seen half the content and is more likely interested in the offer.
  • Delaying the pop-up, and not showing it to repeat visitors, reduces annoyance. It keeps the opt-in rate (the share of visitors who sign up) stable. Older advice (2022).
  • The email pop-up in the theme settings asks visitors for their email address, so it is a lead capture tool. The exit pop-up does not ask for an email address. It only shows text, an optional image and an optional discount code.
  • In the same theme section you can choose an image, attach a discount code and change all of the pop-up text.

Clavio pop-up procedure

Clavio sign-up form (Clavio is a pop-up tool). This covers the set-up described in the sources.

  1. Open Sign-up forms in Clavio and pick a template that works on both desktop and mobile.
  2. Keep or rename the form (example name: Subscribe for coupon) and choose the list (example: newsletter).
  3. Click Create form.
  4. Replace the image with your logo. Change the headline and the button text so they state the discount. Add your own text where needed.
  5. Open Styles and choose Pop-up as the display type. Then set the background colour to match your store. Optional: add a background image.
  6. Open Targeting and behaviour. Choose Show based on rules, and set exit intent plus a 10-second delay after page load.
  7. Open the success screen. Enter the coupon code you created in Shopify (example: save five), and adjust the text and text colour.
  8. Click Publish at the top right, then Publish anyway. The pop-up is now live.
  • The same Clavio set-up in brief: open Sign up forms, choose a template that works on desktop and mobile, keep the list as Newsletter, set the form Styles to Pop-up. Under Targeting and behaviour, use Show based on rules with an exit-intent trigger and a 10-second delay after page load.

Klaviyo sign-up form

  • Klaviyo sign-up form creation (Klaviyo is an email marketing tool): Create > Email only > select the Newsletter list > Next > choose Pop-up > Next.
  • Do not include a teaser (a small preview box). Teasers can annoy mobile visitors. Add one only if the sign-up rate drops.
  • Pop-ups have the highest conversion among Klaviyo sign-up form types. Compare them with full-page and fly-out forms (a box that slides in from the side).
  • Set the form status from Draft to Live. Then check the layout in the mobile preview (how the form looks on a phone) before publishing.
  • Test the layout in the mobile preview, since most customers arrive from phones.
  • Creating a Klaviyo sign-up pop-up with a discount:
    1. Go to Signup forms in the left menu and pick a pop-up design.
    2. Name the form and add it to the newsletter subscriber list. Then click Create form.
    3. Replace the offer text with a fixed dollar amount, for example save $5 with your first order.
    4. Replace the image with a product photo or a gift image from unsplash.com (a free photo site).
    5. Set the success step to show the discount code you created in Shopify.
    6. Click Publish, then Publish form.
  • Klaviyo's embedded sign-up form on the store lets visitors enter their name and email before they download a lead magnet.
  • Klaviyo can collect names and email addresses from prospects on lead generation offer forms.

Other tools

  • SumoMe (sumo.com) is a free email opt-in pop-up tool. It can be added to Shopify, WooCommerce (another store platform) and most other platforms.
  • From the first day of the store, collect visitor emails with a pop-up tool such as Privy, so you can remarket to them later. Older advice (2018).
  • OptinMonster is an opt-in tool that can record which page or content piece a subscriber joined from. This makes it simple to place each subscriber into the matching list. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Optimunk's Email input goes to Klaviyo's email property (the email field of the contact). Its Phone input goes to phone_number. The SMS Club Invite input (an invitation to join text message updates) has no fixed Klaviyo target. You either choose a value from the list or type one in.
  • Under Custom value, send extra data with each sign-up: Campaign name, URL of campaign (the page where the pop-up appeared) and Variant name. Add more custom values if needed. The URL of campaign is set as the Root URL (the main web address the form sends back to).
  • Sending the URL of the page where the pop-up appeared lets follow-up emails return the visitor to that exact page. This is better than sending them to the homepage or a collection page. Those force the visitor to find the product again, which adds friction (steps that slow them down).

Shopify sign-up section

  • Email signup section: colour scheme set to accent 2 (a preset colour). The heading and short text can be edited, but the email form itself cannot be customised. Addresses entered there are stored as subscribers in the Shopify admin.

Pop-up and opt-in copy

1 min

Words for the boxes that pop up on your website. An opt-in is a clear yes from a visitor to get your emails.

Headline formulas

1 min
  • Formula for an opt-in pop-up headline. A pop-up is a box that appears on a web page. Name a concrete result the reader wants. Then ask for the email directly below it, in the same box, with a clear call to action (a short instruction, such as "Get it"). It works because it sells the result, not a discount. Asking for an email then feels like a fair trade.
  • New examples. Each one states the result, then asks for the email right after: See the exact five-step plan I used to cut product return rates in half. Enter your email below to get it. Or: Learn the three tools that cut my ad testing time by a third. Drop your email to receive the list.
  • Email sign-up headline pattern: name the community the subscriber joins, plus a fast benefit (a quick reward). An example benefit is inviting them to be first to see new arrivals. Example 1: Join the Crew and get first access to restocks. Example 2: Join the Club for early sale alerts.
  • Pattern: replace the generic newsletter line (a plain, vague line such as subscribe to our newsletter) with a short invitation. Newsletters are regular email updates. The invitation names the brand and its community or benefit. The brand name makes the line feel personal. The community word makes joining feel like belonging. Examples: Join the Harbor Crew and Get early access to [Brand] drops (drops are new product releases).
  • An email capture offer (a form that collects emails) works when it pairs a small incentive (a small reward) with a cause the reader already cares about. Examples: Get 15% off your first order and help fund a reef restoration project and Join our weekly rewilding newsletter and receive 12% off when you sign up for our forest-friendly range.

Weak and strong opt-in copy

1 min
  • Weak opt-in copy (vague sign-up text): Join our email list today to receive our newsletter and discounts.
  • Strong opt-in copy (specific sign-up text): Join our email list today and we'll send you our 50 page guide to sending emails that convert. To convert means to turn a visitor into a sign-up or a buyer.
  • The strong version names one concrete asset (a free item you hand over, here a guide) that solves a specific problem. Older advice (2021-22).

Standard pop-up copy (sign-up discount)

1 min

Copy means the written words on the page. Below is a starting text for a 10 percent sign-up discount. Adapt it to your brand.

Headline: "Want to get 10% off your first purchase?"
Sub-line: "Sign up to unlock your discount."
Email field placeholder: "Add your best email"
Button: "Reveal my discount"
Decline link: "No thanks, I'd rather pay full price"
After submitting, the success message should say "Thanks for signing up. Here is your discount code" followed by the code, with the confirmation line removed.

Exit-intent and pre-exit copy

An exit-intent pop-up appears when a visitor moves to leave the page. Pre-exit copy (the words shown just before they leave) is below, word for word (verbatim), for a pop-up that collects an email and unlocks a discount.

Pause before leaving: share your email address to unlock a special promotional discount from [STORE.COM].

1 min

A welcome message is the first note a new subscriber gets. This one confirms that the discount code is on its way.

Welcome to [Brand Name]. You're in. Your [10]% discount code is on its way to your inbox. If it hasn't arrived after a few minutes, check your spam or promotions folder, because our emails don't always land in the main inbox.

Pop-up principles

1 min
  • Asking visitors openly for their email in an opt-in pop-up is right when the offer is genuinely useful and matches what the subscriber will receive.
  • The email pop-up asks visitors for their email address. The exit pop-up does not.
  • Keep the heading short and clear about what visitors get.

Lead-gen ads and paid sources

2 min

Paid sources are visitors you pay to bring in, usually with ads. Lead ads aim to collect contact details. A lead is a person who has shown interest in your store.

  • Lead ads should collect email addresses first through an opt-in offer. Reason: a subscribed list can be sold to repeatedly over time.
  • Direct sales on the first click matter less than building the list. The first click means the visitor's first visit.
  • Offers such as a free gift or discount coupon are used to earn the opt-in.
  • Use conversion campaigns (ad campaigns that count one chosen action) with lead as the conversion event (the action the ad counts).
  • Do not use Facebook Lead Ads (ads with a sign-up form built into Facebook). Their lead form quality is significantly lower than landing page conversions (sign-ups on your own web page).
  • Lead form integration via Zapier (a tool that links apps automatically) to Klaviyo adds friction and can cause data sync problems (information going wrong between the two apps).
  • Lead generation traffic (visitors sent to leave their contact details) should go to a page whose only goal is to collect name and email, not to sell a product.
  • Use the lead-gen traffic pattern above before sending ads to a discount or best-seller page (the page of your top-selling product).
  • Procedure for collecting leads and sending remarketing (showing ads again to past visitors). Pixels are small tracking codes placed on your site. The steps are below. Older advice (2024).
1. Collect email addresses on the store.
2. Track visitors with pixels so they can be shown ads on Meta and Google that return them to the store.
3. Use abandoned cart and monthly promotion emails for email follow-up.
  • Ads that promote a discount or a best-seller page skip the list-building step, so send them to a collection page first (a store page that groups products).
  • Shopping ads (product ads with pictures and prices) are shown after a prospect (someone who may buy) has signed up for a buyer's guide and received a coupon.

Influencer and partner sources

1 min
  • Set up influencer incentives (rewards for an influencer, a person with a large social media following), such as exclusive coupons, free products or free training.
  • Design these rewards to drive sign-ups, not just traffic (visits) to the email newsletter.
  • This keeps the relationship going past the influencer's first promotion. It also allows long-term marketing to the audience by email.
  • Partner with other companies that have a list (their own email subscribers).
  • Run a drop (a joint product launch) with them, offering their audience an exclusive offer to get started.
  • The partner's audience must match your niche (the narrow group of buyers you sell to). Older advice (2021-22).
  • Ways to acquire first customers through email. Older advice (2021-22).
    1. Email your existing customers.
    2. Partner with other companies that have a list and run a drop with them, offering their audience an exclusive offer to get started.
  • Allowed email list sources are existing customers, abandoned checkouts (carts left before payment) and website pop-ups.
  • Before paying for more subscribers, measure how much revenue an exit-intent overlay (the pop-up that appears as someone leaves) brings in per sign-up.
  • Scale acquisition (spend more money to gain subscribers) only after an email campaign already converts well. Older advice (2024 source).
  • Import an email list from a previous employer only with permission, and only if the contacts are relevant to the niche.
  • Then import the list into Klaviyo and send a short introduction broadcast (one email sent to a whole list at once). Older advice (2021).

List structure, naming and sources

1 min

A list is a group of email addresses you can send to together. A segment is a smaller group inside a list.

Naming

1 min
  • Name lists to show their source clearly: Optimunk 10% OFF, Giveaway XYZ, Checkout, Customer. This makes acquisition history traceable (you can see how people joined) when reviewing performance.
  • Each content opt-in (a sign-up offered with a guide or article) needs its own list or segment, with its own automated email sequence (a set of emails sent in order) behind it.
  • Each email opt-in on the site should have its own email list.

Discount list flow

1 min

Procedure for a discount opt-in flow (the steps a discount sign-up follows), in this order. Older advice (2021-22).

  1. Create a dedicated list (a list used only for this purpose) for discount subscribers that feeds automatic discount reminder emails.
  2. Send a few emails to encourage completion of the sale if the visitor does not buy immediately.
  3. Add a rule that moves any subscriber who becomes a customer out of the discount list into the new customer list, so they stop receiving discount reminders.

Source tracking

1 min
  • Record which page or content piece (a guide, article or similar item) a subscriber joined from, so each subscriber lands in the matching list.
  • Send the page URL (the web address) with each sign-up, so follow-up can return the visitor to that page.

Consent settings

1 min

Consent means a clear yes from the person to receive your emails.

Opt-in process

1 min
  • The opt-in process (how a new subscriber gets added) is set per list under Klaviyo list > Settings > Consent > Opt-in process. Klaviyo is the email tool.
  • Double opt-in (the subscriber must click a confirmation link) is Klaviyo's recommendation.
  • Double opt-in requires confirmation. It helps block invalid (fake or wrong) and malicious (harmful) contacts.
  • It needs the list's confirmation email and the global SMS confirmation text (the standard text message that asks for a yes) kept up to date.
  • Single opt-in adds subscribers immediately, with no confirmation.
  • Klaviyo recommends regular list cleaning (removing dead or bad addresses) with single opt-in.
  • Changing the consent setting of a new list is easy to forget.
  • Check the opt-in process on every new pop-up list before the pop-up goes live. The setting decides whether subscribers must confirm.
  • The opt-in form note for a sales event (a sale period) tells visitors they agree to receive emails and that they may opt out at any time (they can unsubscribe whenever they want).

Unsubscribe behaviour

1 min
  • In Klaviyo, each list has an unsubscribe setting (what happens when someone clicks unsubscribe) under Settings > Unsubscribes.
  • It decides whether an unsubscribe from that list also removes the person from all future emails.
  • Check this option when you create a pop-up list. The account may apply a default (an automatic setting) that treats any unsubscribe as a full email unsubscribe.
  • Use the View settings link to review the account-level default (the setting for the whole account) before going live.

Go-live gate

1 min

A go-live gate is a check that must pass before launch.

  • Go live with the pop-up only after the Klaviyo welcome series (a set of automatic emails sent to new subscribers) is ready. Then new subscribers receive their first sequence right away.
  • If a welcome series already exists for the client, copy its structure and adapt it rather than building from scratch.

Never buy lists

1 min
  • Do not buy email lists to acquire customers. Older advice (2021-22).
  • Buying or renting email lists is a serious mistake.
  • Emailing people who never opted in causes spam complaints (reports that your email is junk).
  • Spam complaints can get your domain (your web address) blacklisted (blocked) from major inboxes such as Google and Yahoo.
  • Only email people who opted into your store list.
  • Purchased email lists cause spam complaints and damage your sending reputation (how much inboxes trust your emails). The people you want to reach then stop opening your emails.
  • Do not buy, rent or email purchased lists, or email people who do not know the brand.
  • Spam complaints quickly get the sending domain blacklisted, and legitimate emails stop reaching inboxes.
  • Never send cold emails (emails to people who never asked to hear from you) directly to purchased email lists. Instead:
    1. Upload the CSV (a spreadsheet file of email addresses) to Facebook or Google as a custom audience (an ad group built from your own list).
    2. Run ads sending them to your opt-in landing page (the web page where they sign up).
    3. Let them re-opt-in (sign up again).
    4. Then send emails to the engaged list (people who have shown interest).
  • This prevents domain blacklisting.
  • If you email a cold list with a low open rate (few people open the email), ISPs (internet providers' mail systems, such as Google, Hotmail and Outlook) read it as spam. They then blacklist your sender domain and block future emails.
  • Example: sending to 1,000 people with only 5 opens triggers ISP spam flags.
  • When uploading existing email lists to email platforms like Klaviyo, recipients may need to re-opt-in (sign up again), depending on setup.
  • Send in Blue (another email sending tool) allows bypassing this re-opt-in requirement if needed.

Following up new sign-ups

1 min

Automated follow-up

1 min
  • Use a welcome series, copied from an existing client series where one exists.
  • Follow-up sequence after a sign-up: send the welcome email and a discount reminder, then move the subscriber onward.
  • Use abandoned cart (a checkout someone started but did not finish) and monthly promotion emails for email follow-up.

Daily follow-up schedule

1 min

Procedure for follow-up of new email sign-ups. Older advice (2020).

  1. Email new sign-ups once a day during the first few days.
  2. After that, move them to the main broadcast list (the main list for regular emails).
  3. Send to the main broadcast list at least once per week, sometimes twice.

Manual outreach

1 min

Manual outreach (writing to people yourself, not through software) to new email sign-ups in the early stage. Older advice (2022).

  1. Take the email address from an exit-intent or opt-in capture.
  2. Send a short personal email from the owner that asks which products or questions the person has.
  3. Answer every reply to earn trust and start conversations.
  • Reply personally to new email leads (people who showed interest and may buy), asking if they have questions and offering help.
  • Manual outreach at the start builds trust and sales. It is especially useful before automation (emails sent by software) is in place.
  • Use this follow-up for a new email lead who signed up for your discount.
Hi [customer name], this is [your name], the owner of [store name]. I noticed you signed up for our [discount code] offer, and I wanted to see if you have any questions. If there is anything I can help you with, just reply to this email. I'm happy to help.
  • Use the email list you collect from visitors for follow-up, since a visitor who does not buy on the first visit may still buy later. Older advice (2019).

Further details

1 min
  • Collecting email sign-ups before launch (before the store opens) is not useful, because nobody finds the store before it goes live.
  • Audiences (groups of people saved inside ad platforms) built before launch do the job of building the pre-launch base (the early group of people ready for opening day).

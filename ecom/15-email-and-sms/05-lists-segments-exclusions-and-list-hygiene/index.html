Lists, segments, exclusions and list hygiene

Section 15.5 · 5 of 17 in this chapter · 14 min read

In short

  • A list is a sign-up group. A segment is a group found by rules, such as "bought twice."
  • Email the people who recently opened or clicked. Stop emailing people who never engage.
  • Keep recent buyers out of marketing emails for 7 days. Use 14 days if you want even less contact.
  • Always check who NOT to send to. This matters most.

List vs segment logic

2 min
  • A list is a data source. It shows where a subscriber came from. A subscriber is a person on your email list.
  • A segment is a rule-based profile. It defines who meets specific engagement criteria. Engagement means opening or clicking your emails.
  • A list is a group of profiles that opted in, such as the Newsletter list. Opting in means the person chose to get your emails.
  • A segment is a group of profiles filtered by conditions, such as past orders or behavior.
  • Both are found under Audience, then Lists and segments. Audience is the menu area in Klaviyo, the email tool.
  • Klaviyo provides pre-built lists such as Newsletter.
  • Create your own segments with Create list or segment under Audience > Lists and segments.
  • Klaviyo can read order history from Shopify or another connected store platform. Shopify is an online store builder. A connected platform sends its order data to Klaviyo.
  • This allows automatic segmentation by number of purchases and by products or product types bought.
  • Segments are used for customers. Separate lists based on opt-in source are often used for prospects. A prospect is someone who has not bought yet.
  • ** In Klaviyo, the only list to create for this setup is the broadcast list. A broadcast list is the main list that receives your mass emails.
  • Default segments that appear in new accounts are not needed, because automations do not use them. An automation is an email sent automatically after a trigger, such as a sign-up.
  • The broadcast list contains every subscriber who has not opted out, whatever the source.
  • Sources include abandoned cart, purchase, footer signup, or exit-intent signup. An abandoned cart means someone added items but did not buy. An exit-intent signup is a pop-up shown when someone moves to leave the site. A footer signup is a form at the bottom of the page.
  • These contacts receive all monthly promotions.
  • Split audiences into two kinds of lists.
  • Communication lists are email lists that receive your messages.
  • Invisible lists are used only for ad targeting and never receive email.
  • Keeping these apart keeps targeting precise.

Engagement-recency segments

2 min
  • Build overlapping recency segments. Recency means how recently someone engaged. Overlapping means one person can be in more than one segment.
  • Use windows of 7-day, 14-day, 0-30, 31-60, 61-90, and 90+.
  • The goal is to test audiences and rotate them across different engagement windows.
Segment Condition Use IDs
7-Day Hyper-Engaged (Opened Email at least 3 times in last 7 days) OR (Clicked Email at least 2 times in last 7 days) Rebuilding deliverability. Typical usage: 2 weeks. Send 3-7 emails within 7 days to this segment.
14-Day Super-Engaged (Opened Email at least 3 times in last 14 days) OR (Clicked Email at least 3 times in last 14 days) Deliverability rebuilding. If open rates reach 60-80%, continue for 3-4 sends, then expand to wider segments.
0-30 Days Engaged (Opened Email at least 1 time in 0-30 days) OR (Clicked Email at least 1 time in 0-30 days) Core segment for the regular sending strategy.
31-60 Days Engaged (Opened Email at least 1 time in 31-60 days) OR (Clicked Email at least 1 time in 31-60 days) Regular sending rotation.
61-90 Days and 90+ Days Engaged Same threshold: at least 1 open or click in the respective window Build matching segments for these windows.
All Active (Subscribed at least once over all time) OR (Received Email AND Can receive email marketing) Includes all active subscribers except suppressed addresses. Suppressed means blocked from receiving email.
  • The target open rate is above 30%. Open rate is the share of people who open your email.
  • When open rates fall below 30%, move to a more recent engagement window. This helps rebuild deliverability. Deliverability means whether your emails reach the inbox.
  • Email sending works like driving. Gradually expand your audience until open rates drop. Then revert to a tighter engagement window.
  • Find the sweet spot where volume and deliverability stay balanced. Check performance and adjust audience size to find it.
  • Keep emailing contacts who have not engaged only while deliverability stays good.
  • If deliverability weakens, stop mailing these contacts.

Behavior, purchase-count and VIP segments

3 min
  • Segmentation divides the list into groups based on behavior. Behavior includes pages viewed, time spent on the site, purchase history, and how the person joined the list.
  • Relevant messages to each group generally produce better results.
  • More segmentation usually means more valuable messages sent more often.
  • **
  • Email segmentation uses the same logic as retargeting audiences. Retargeting audiences are groups of people who showed interest, shown ads to bring them back.
  • Group people by their actions and interests. Each group then gets a message suited to where it is in the buying journey. The buying journey is the path from first interest to purchase.
  • **
  • These are the minimum segments to set up in the email software.
  • **
    1. Customer segments by purchase count: customers who bought once, customers who bought twice, and customers who bought three or more times.
    2. A segment of all customers who have bought.
    3. Product-based segments for best-selling products, or products with a clear upsell or repurchase cycle. An upsell is an offer to buy a better or extra version. A repurchase cycle is the time before someone needs to buy again, such as items that wear out or have a limited shelf life.
    4. Customer segments by product type if the store sells several product types.
    5. Prospect lists by opt-in source: one list per specific content opt-in, one per product page opt-in, and one per collection page or product type opt-in. A collection page groups similar products together.
    6. Add new segments or lists each time a new opt-in is added to the website.
  • Set up three audience segments in Klaviyo: buyers, non-buyers, and VIP customers.
  • A VIP customer means a customer who has bought more than once.
  • Exclude buyers from offer launches for products they already bought.
  • Example VIP segment: customers whose Placed order count is greater than one. This means two or more orders.
  • Use the VIP segment to target loyal buyers with product launches or early access.
  • VIP segment setup, step 1: under Audience, then Lists and segments, click Create list or segment and create a segment.
  • VIP segment setup, step 2: set the condition to the Placed Order metric with a count greater than 1. A metric is a trackable action, such as placing an order. Greater than 1 means two or more orders.
  • A VIP customer here is a customer with two or more orders. This means Placed Order count greater than 1.
  • Create Klaviyo segments for shoppers who bought Checkout Plus and for those who did not. Checkout Plus is a named product in this setup.
  • Then target these two groups differently.
  • The integration does not come out of the box, but it can be built.
  • After you find high-performing engagement segments (30%+ open rates), add product-specific conditions. This makes targeting more precise.
  • Product-specific conditions include "viewed products with [attribute]", "looked at [collection]", or "purchased from [collection]".
  • Example: add "AND viewed products containing protein" to the 0-30 Days Engaged segment. This targets engaged subscribers interested in protein. AND means both conditions must be true.
  • Audience segmentation produces 8 to 10 segments deployed in Klaviyo.
  • Build these segments from existing subscriber data without any extra tool.
  • Add a checklist of product interests to every reply card. A reply card is a paper card a customer sends back.
  • Also request name and address on that card.
  • Use the answers to build segments for later mailings.
  • A list built from stated interests produces more relevant follow-ups than a general list.
  • ** Email follow-up covers both pre-purchase and post-purchase contacts. Pre-purchase means before someone buys. Post-purchase means after.
  • Build and segment lists before purchase.
  • Send different emails to prospects and to buyers. This keeps them engaged and raises the chance of a completed sale.

Naming conventions

1 min
  • Use the naming convention brand name + double slash + segment name. For example: "e2 // 0-30 Days Engaged."
  • Apply this consistently across all segments.
  • Prefix all exclude segments with an asterisk (*). An exclude segment is a group you remove from a send.
  • The asterisk makes them instantly recognizable when you search for exclusions to apply in campaigns. Example: *e2 // Purchased Last 7 Days.
  • Name a new list after the campaign and its offers. For example: the campaign name plus "10% and 15%."
  • This lets you create separate lists later for split tests. A split test sends two versions to two groups to see which works better.
  • In Klaviyo, go to Lists & segments > Create list and give the list a name.

Core exclude segments

1 min
  • Apply these five core exclude segments to every email campaign. A campaign is one email or email send.
  • The five are: Purchased Last 7 Days (minimum; consider 14 days), Soft Bounce (3+ bounces), Spam Trap, and Spam Keywords.
  • A soft bounce is a temporary email failure, such as a full inbox.
  • This approach protects deliverability and maintains sender reputation. Sender reputation is how trusted your email address looks to email providers.
  • With proper list management and the five core exclude segments applied consistently, a well-maintained list should achieve over 30% open rate.
  • Focus email marketing on deciding whom NOT to send to rather than whom to send to.
  • Exclusions for bounces, inactive profiles, and suspicious keywords make sure messages reach engaged audiences.
  • Exclusions also protect your sender reputation.

Purchased Last 7 Days and Purchased Last 14 Days

1 min
  • Do not send marketing emails to customers within 5-14 days of purchase.
  • This period is seen as pushy and annoying.
  • Customers need time to receive and be happy with their order before you market to them again.
  • Create a Purchased Last 7 Days exclude segment. Its condition: placed order at least once in the last 7 days.
  • Adjust the timing based on shipping speed.
  • Purpose: prevent triggering customers with marketing right after a purchase.
Shipping speed Exclude window IDs
Customers receive products within 3 days 5 days
Standard shipping 7 days
Maximum aggressive purchase exclusion (minimal marketing contact with recent purchasers) 14 days (Purchased Last 14 Days: placed order at least once in the last 14 days)
  • Use the 14-day segment when you want minimal marketing contact with recent purchasers. This is a discount-driven choice.
  • Check flow structures for suppressions and exclusions. A flow is an automatic email sequence.
  • Check for customers who have already purchased or who are already in another flow.
  • These are key points that are easy to miss.

Soft Bounce

1 min
  • Create a Soft Bounce exclude segment for email addresses that have bounced at least 3 times. A bounce means an email could not be delivered.
  • Soft bounces significantly hurt deliverability.
  • In Klaviyo, the condition is "bounced at least three times overall time."
  • Always exclude profiles that have bounced twice or more.
  • Use a segment such as *Bounced x2 or more in the Don't send to field. The Don't send to field is where you add exclusions in a campaign.
  • In the example, this excluded 62 profiles.
  • This is a stricter campaign-level exclusion than the 3-bounce core segment.

Spam Trap

1 min
  • Spam traps are email addresses that Internet Service Providers (ISPs) use to monitor sender reputation. ISPs are companies like Google, Yahoo, and Outlook.
  • ISPs take over dormant accounts, which are accounts inactive for 5-10 years.
  • They sign these addresses up to mailing lists. Then they watch whether senders keep emailing them without engagement.
  • Senders who keep sending to spam traps get blacklisted. Blacklisted means blocked by email providers.
  • Create a Spam Trap exclude segment with these AND conditions together. AND means all of them must be true.
  • Viewed Product = zero times
  • Checkout Started = zero times
  • Placed Order = zero times
  • Opened Email = zero times
  • Clicked Email = zero times
  • Cannot Receive Marketing = true
  • Optional: add "subscribed more than 14 days ago" to exclude brand-new inactive profiles. This focuses the segment on established bot or fake accounts. A bot is an automated program, not a real person.
  • Profiles automatically exit this segment upon any engagement action.
  • Do not use "active on site" conditions in the spam trap segment. Bots and fraudulent accounts can appear active on site without real engagement.
  • Base spam trap detection on actual engagement behavior instead.
  • The test: zero emails opened AND zero emails clicked AND zero products viewed AND zero checkouts started AND cannot receive marketing.

Spam Keywords

1 min
  • Exclude emails containing these keywords. Create a segment with "Email contains [keyword]" conditions joined by OR. OR means any one match is enough.
  • These patterns mark non-personal or system accounts. They should never receive marketing.

Purchase exclusions for overlapping segments

1 min
  • Without exclusions, one person can appear in several overlapping segments.
  • That person can then receive conflicting or repeated messages.
  • Example: when emailing customers who bought once, exclude customers who bought twice from that audience.

Cleaning fake and unengaged subscribers

2 min
  • Invalid or misspelled email addresses self-correct. Each send attempt bounces.
  • After 3 or more soft bounces, your soft bounce segment excludes them from future campaigns.
  • This automatic removal improves list hygiene and sender reputation. List hygiene means keeping your list clean.
  • It works without requiring manual cleaning.
  • Clean fake newsletter subscribers in Klaviyo without double opt-in. Double opt-in means the person must confirm by a second click. Without it, you must clean manually. Double opt-in would drop most real subscribers.
  • Step 1: once a week, delete obviously fake contacts.
  • Step 2: once a month, run a report in Klaviyo for contacts who have not engaged with emails in the past 30 or 60 days.
  • Step 3: delete those contacts.
  • Build an exclusion segment for unengaged profiles at one struggling provider. Unengaged means they do not open or click.
  • Step 1: go to Audience > Lists & segments and click Create segment.
  • Step 2: name it with a single asterisk at the start, for example *Yahoo Unengaged. Avoid multiple asterisks.
  • Step 3: under Definition, choose What someone has done (or not done).
  • Step 4: set Person has > Opened Email zero times between 15 and 365 days ago. Add the same condition for Clicked Email. The profile must have neither opened nor clicked in that window.
  • Step 5: add an AND filter on the profile property email that contains yahoo.
  • Step 6: check the number under Preview details before saving. In the example, the preview showed about 2,446 profiles.
  • Step 7: click Create segment to save it.
  • In Klaviyo's Create segment screen, the Segment builder offers a Define with AI option. It sits next to the manual Definition builder.
  • The manual route starts with the Definition field. There you choose a condition such as What someone has done (or not done). Then you add Person has filters.
  • In a campaign's Recipients step, the Don't send to (optional) field takes exclusion segments.
  • The Estimated recipients count updates after you add them.
  • The Skip recently emailed profiles option uses Smart Sending. Smart Sending is Klaviyo's tool that avoids emailing the same person too often.
  • By default it skips profiles emailed in the past 16 hours.
  • You can change this window in Account Settings.
  • Check the Estimated recipients count in the Recipients step after adding each exclusion. It shows how many profiles remain after include and exclude segments.
  • In the example, the count was about 2,012 after excluding the bounced and Yahoo unengaged segments.
  • Put the exclusion segment into the Don't send to field of every new campaign.
  • Share it with the team so every email uses it automatically.
  • This keeps the fix in place without needing a manual check each time.

Send-to-engaged strategy

1 min
  • Use a three-part sending strategy.
  • Part 1: send to engaged subscribers first.
  • Part 2: tailor segments by product or behavior interests.
  • Part 3: exclude unengaged audiences. Most important is knowing who NOT to send to.
  • Segment by date range to control costs.
  • Create audience segments by date range. Example: a July-August cohort, and an August-September cohort. A cohort is a group of people who joined in the same period.
  • Send the same email campaigns to each segment separately.
  • Klaviyo charges per unique recipient per campaign. So segmented sends cost less than one large campaign.
  • This keeps you on the $150/month plan instead of scaling to $5,000/month.
  • Once you scale up to high-volume pricing, it is difficult to scale back down.
  • So plan segmentation from the start.

Avoiding overlap and conflicting messages

1 min
  • Split the customer list into segments.
  • Different customer groups then receive different emails.
  • Segmenting lets one email account serve several audiences without sending the same message to everyone.
  • ** Do not send all subscribers the same messages from one large list.
  • Split the total list into targeted segments. Each receives its own email flow based on actions taken on the site.
  • Actions include which product was bought or which content page the person opted in on.
  • ** Emails sent to a segment should be highly relevant to the opt-in point or purchase that placed the subscriber there.
  • Contextual, targeted flows are where the strongest email returns come from.
  • Before sending a segmented message, define the exact group the message is for.
  • Check whether that group overlaps with another segment.
  • If it does, add an exclusion for the overlapping segment.
  • Confirm that the audience now matches the intended message.

Audience sync to ads

1 min
  • In Klaviyo, sync an email list or segment to a Facebook custom audience. A custom audience is a list of people uploaded for ad targeting.
  • Subscribers are then shown ads even when they do not open email.
  • Use invisible lists only for ad targeting. They are never sent email.
  • Keeping them apart from communication lists keeps targeting precise.

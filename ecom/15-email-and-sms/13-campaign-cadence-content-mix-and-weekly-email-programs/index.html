Playbook › 15. Email and SMS

Campaign cadence, content mix and weekly email programs

Section 15.13 · 13 of 17 in this chapter · 20 min read

In short

  • Send more emails to new subscribers first, then send fewer as their interest cools.
  • Keep about 30% of your emails helpful and brand-building. The other 70% can sell.
  • Never send the same email unchanged twice, or it may land in spam.
  • Watch your unsubscribe and open numbers. They show when you send too often.
  • Do not send selling emails to people who just bought. They are not ready to buy again.
More key points
  • A sponsored email blast means you pay a partner to send your message to their email list.
  • The price usually goes up the more emails you send.
  • A sponsorship can be a series of emails, not just one.

Sending Frequency by Lifecycle Phase

1 min

A lifecycle phase is one stage in a subscriber's journey. A subscriber is a person on your email list.

Automated Sequences for New Subscribers

2 min
  • There is no fixed industry rule for how often to send email.
  • Use a hybrid approach, which means mixing two methods. Send many emails when a subscriber is new and very interested. Then send fewer as their interest cools.
  • A new subscriber's interest peaks right after they join or make a first purchase.
  • That window is the best time to sell or sell again.
  • After that, interest declines. So the attention and offers needed to keep the person engaged (paying attention and reacting) also shrink.
  • When someone joins, your email goals are to build trust and establish authority. Authority means showing you know your niche (your small market focus).
  • Your goals also include driving a first sale, an upsell (offering a bigger or extra product), or a repeat purchase.
  • Once trust and authority are built, far less effort is needed to maintain them.
  • Run each phase as its own automated sequence. An automated sequence is a set of emails that send by themselves, in order.
Phase Timing Automated emails per week Total in phase
Hot phase Days 0-30 4 16
Cooling phase Days 30-60 2-3 -
Long-term phase Day 60 onward 1 -
  • Hot phase (days 0-30): send 4 automated emails per week, 16 in total.
  • Engagement typically drops after day 30. So the chance of a sale falls for a while if nothing is sent or bought.
  • Cooling phase (days 30-60): reduce to 2 or 3 automated emails per week. Use a separate sequence for this phase.
  • Long-term phase (day 60 onward): send 1 automated email per week.
  • In the first 30 days, a subscriber gets 5 emails per week in total. That is 4 automated emails plus 1 blast email.
  • A blast email is a one-off email sent to many people at once.
  • In the hot phase, the subscriber is most receptive (open to buying). So you can put more offers in front of them.
  • As interest cools, cut offer frequency along with overall email volume.

Blast Emails

1 min
  • Blast emails are one-off sends. They are for a new blog post (a page of articles on your site), news, a new product or collection (a group of related products), or a sale such as Black Friday.
  • Blast emails are separate from automated emails.
  • On average, send about 1 blast email per week.
  • Send more during big sale events and fewer in quiet periods.
  • Blasts usually go to the whole list, whatever the lifecycle stage.
  • The exception is when a blast is aimed at a specific segment. A segment is a smaller group chosen by rules.

Touch Points and Persistence

1 min
  • A touch point is each time you reach a person with a message.
  • Plan for about 7 touch points before a typical person buys. More emails create more chances to sell.
  • Not every email has to be a sales email. Each one should be a chance to sell, or to give value that leads toward a sale.
  • Most sales happen after the 5th, 6th, or 7th follow-up. They rarely happen after the first few contacts.
  • So people often need to see a brand several times before buying.
  • If the emails give relevant value, send more rather than fewer.
  • Brands with genuinely useful content can email subscribers every day without harm.
  • This holds as long as unsubscribe rates (how many people leave) and open rates (how many people open) stay stable.
  • Judge frequency by these list health numbers, not by a fixed rule of thumb.
  • Content made for your website, organic social media (free posts, not ads), and retargeting (ads shown to people who already visited) can be reused in emails.
  • This keeps your sending frequency sustainable.
  • Each email can deliver a useful piece from a growing content library. A content library is a stored collection of ready content.

Long Nurture Series

1 min
  • A nurture series is a set of emails that builds trust slowly over time.
  • Long nurture series can be set to send daily for two months or a full year.
  • A daily cadence (send rhythm) is optional, not required for the flow to work. A flow is a set of emails triggered by something a visitor does.
  • Choose the frequency based on how much the subscriber gains from each message.

Content and Promotion Mix

1 min

Split Between Brand and Selling Content

1 min
  • Split email content roughly 30/70.
  • About 30% is brand-building content. This means educational and informative content that also entertains. The mix of information and fun is called infotainment. This content nurtures the audience and adds value.
  • About 70% is direct selling content.
  • Send the 30% brand-building content to every email segment, including recent buyers.
  • It builds the relationship without asking for a sale.
  • Do not send the 70% direct selling content to recent buyers.
  • They are not ready to buy again yet.
  • Quality comes from following the weekly mix.
  • Brand-building, educating, entertaining, and nurturing keep building brand value. They also keep content fresh enough to be opened and clicked.
  • The Problem-Solve format (a three-step selling email, shown below) can always be used. Customers always have problems to solve.

Example Content Mix

1 min
  • Each email can combine four elements:
    • Link to a new blog post or other content.
    • News relevant to your niche.
    • A product promotion, for example "buy this week save 2%".
    • An engagement request (asking the reader to act), for example "send us a photo of your product in your home".
  • The target email frequency is two emails per week.
  • Mix value content, such as blog links and niche news, with promotional offers.

Offers and Content Per Week

1 min
  • Release one promotional offer per week all year. Send it by email to your lead list.
  • This keeps people engaged. It also turns prospects (people who may buy) into customers.
  • Plan at least one relevant offer or piece of valuable content per week for every lead list. Ideally plan two.
  • Waiting until the next holiday to email a new subscriber lets them forget the brand.
  • Broadcast offers (one-off sends to the whole list) keep working as long as subscribers stay on the list.
  • Their response rates are lower than the first follow-up emails after a purchase.
  • Send them when a relevant new offer appears, or during big sales seasons.
  • The job of email is to sell more of what already sells to people who do not yet own it.
  • So top-selling products should be the core of regular campaigns. Include upsell pushes.

Content as a Library

1 min
  • Every new piece of content should be added to the email program.
  • A library of useful content gives the list something to read besides offers and promotions.
  • Without content, email has little to say beyond product pitches.
  • Content keeps subscribers engaged and warm (interested) between sales.
  • Email marketing is very hard to make effective without content.
  • A subscriber list needs more than product offers to stay engaged.
  • Send interview or video content to the email list. It adds backstory (the story behind the brand).
  • That backstory can convince buyers who are still undecided.
  • Email is for warming people already on the list.
  • A free weekly design sent by email keeps customers engaged while they wait for a physical product to ship.
  • The emails help the seller reach an engaged audience with high open rates.
  • They also help promote future offers.

Building and Maintaining the List

1 min

  • Build and maintain the list in this order:

    1. Offer incentives (coupons, free accessories, giveaways) to collect emails.
    2. Send 2+ promotional emails per week, separate from email funnels.
    3. Include new blog posts, product announcements, promotions, and industry news.
    4. Monitor open rates and reply engagement (how many people reply).
    5. Periodically test affiliate partnership offers. With these, you earn a fee when someone buys through your link. Use them to monetize (earn money from) the list further.

  • Email marketing sends promotions to a broadcast list. The goal is to bring people back to buy again.

  • Klaviyo (an email tool) can be used to build and manage this list.

Weekly Email Templates

1 min

What's Hot Weekly (WHW)

1 min
  • A hero image is the big main picture at the top of an email. A bestseller is a top-selling product.
What's Hot Weekly (WHW) email layout, in this order:
1. Large hero image at the top.
2. Six-product block headed by the trending products of the last 7 days, showing 6 products.
3. Three customer testimonials.
4. Shop bestsellers section at the bottom.
Keep this layout as one reusable template and change only the products each week.
  • Send the What's Hot Weekly email every Friday.
  • Build it once as a template (a ready layout you reuse). Then resend it each Friday by swapping the products.
  • This makes it easy to automate, meaning it can run by itself.
  • The six-product featured block can hold any of these:
    • The top six products by Shopify sales over the last 7 days. Shopify is the store platform.
    • The top six most-searched products.
    • Six products chosen manually.
    • A mix of these.
    • It can also feature a bundle (several products sold together at one price) or a deal of the week or month.
  • For a What's Hot Weekly campaign, place the selected products in the featured section at the top of the store.
  • Then move the other sections further down the page.
  • The featured block should match the products in the email. This way, visitors who click through see the same offer.

Hot Tips (brand-building, part of the 30% share)

1 min
  • A headline is the title line.
Hot Tips email, a brand-building email (part of the 30% share): a headline announcing three tips, then three short numbered tips on a topic your customers care about, each with a brief explanation of how to apply it. Keep it free of a sales push.

Problem-Solve (direct selling, part of the 70% share)

1 min
  1. Name one specific problem your target customer has.
  2. Agitate it. This means stirring up the pain by showing the cost or frustration of leaving the problem unsolved.
  3. Present your product as the solution to that problem.

Weekly Schedule for 3 Emails

1 min
  1. Monday or Tuesday: Problem-Solve email.
  2. Wednesday or Thursday: Hot Tips email.
  3. Friday: What's Hot Weekly email.
  4. Optional: resend an email on Saturday or Sunday, but only with changed content.

Repeats and Spam Risk

1 min
  • Never resend the same content unchanged.
  • When content matches what was recently sent, Google can route the email to spam (the junk folder).
  • Change the content each time an email is repeated.

Campaign Planning Workflow

1 min
  • The campaign calendar is built in Google Sheets, an online spreadsheet.
  • It is built with Claude Cowork, a tool, and the /email-strat-plan skill. A skill is a saved set of instructions for Claude.
  • Its inputs are brand-kit.json (a file that holds your brand rules) and the audience segments.
  • The output is a 30-day calendar with 4 campaigns.
  • These campaigns are then loaded into Klaviyo.

Newsletters and Content-Promotion Emails

1 min

Publish First, Then Email

1 min
  • Publish the article first. Then send the email to the list.
  • Readers then land on a live page, meaning a page that is already online.
  • Repeat this cycle with every new keyword. A keyword is a search term people type into Google.
  • Keep a steady weekly rhythm.

Article Email Formula (plain text, 4 to 6 sentences)

1 min
  • Structure: open with a question or teaser (a hint that makes people curious).
  • Link to the article, then repeat the same link once more.
  • End with a call to action. This is the one thing you ask the reader to do, such as checking out a new blog post.
  • Plain text has no fancy design. It reads like a personal note.
  • Short sentences move the reader quickly to the link.
  • Examples:
    • "Still unsure which hosting service fits a new podcast? I compared seven of them this week on price and ease of use. Read the full comparison here: [link]. Check the ranking and tell me which one you would pick. [link]"
    • "Still not sure a budget blender can handle frozen fruit? I tested four models for a month and noted what held up and what broke. See the results here: [link]. Let me know which one you would try first. [link]"

P.S. Line

1 min
  • End the article email with a P.S. line. This is a short extra line at the end.
  • The P.S. links to a second, related page, such as a product review.
  • The main call to action stays the same.
  • Readers who want more get one extra path toward a purchase. There is no second competing message.
  • Keep the P.S. to one sentence and one link.

Share Requests

1 min
  1. When a new piece of content is published, send it to your entire email list.
  2. Include past buyers. Include people who opted in (chose to join) through promotions on your site. Include exit-intent overlay sign-ups, which are people who joined from a pop-up shown when a visitor is about to leave. Include newsletter subscribers.
  3. Ask every recipient to share the content with someone who would benefit from it.
  • Promotion emails should ask readers to share the content in one of these ways:
    • On Facebook.
    • On X or Twitter.
    • By forwarding it by email.
    • By embedding it on their own sites.
  • The goal is for readers to consume the content and then pass it on. They should not just leave the site.
  • Sending content only once, without asking readers to share, wastes most of the promotion potential.
  • Content that is not worth sharing will not keep visitors on the page. Make it share-worthy first.

Reactive and Daily Story Emails

1 min

Reactive Campaigns

1 min
  • Campaigns are regular emails that are reactive, seasonal, trending, and relevant.
  • Reactive marketing means turning news or trends in your target market into brand content quickly, sometimes the next day.
  • An angle is the way you frame your product.
  • Reactive campaign formula: [relevant event] + [your product or angle] + [same-day or next-day send]
  • When a news event, trend, or season hits your target market, send a campaign that ties it to your product.
  • Do this within about a day.
  • It works because the audience already pays attention to the event. So the email feels timely, not like a generic promotion.
  • Examples:
    • Running shoes: a record marathon time makes headlines, so the next morning's email recommends a beginner shoe for people inspired to start running, with a 48-hour offer.
    • Coffee brand: a cold snap is forecast for the weekend, so the email promotes a hot-drink bundle sent Friday morning.

Daily Story Emails (Seinfeld-style)

2 min

Seinfeld-style means short stories from everyday life, like the TV show. The character is the storyteller in your emails.

  • After the soap sequence, Seinfeld-style emails are sent daily as broadcasts.

  • The soap sequence is a fixed series of story emails, like a TV soap opera.

  • The daily emails are usually entertaining stories from everyday life, rather than pure content.

  • Subscribers who finished the soap sequence move to a broadcast list. There they receive that day's email.

  • These emails are not part of a fixed auto-responder sequence. An auto-responder sends emails automatically, in a set order.

  • Soap sequence emails run as an auto-responder with a fixed order.

  • Daily Seinfeld emails are broadcasts. They can be scheduled ahead.

  • They are usually tied to real events in the character's life.

  • Keep the two systems separate in your email provider, the email tool you use.

  • Shift the emails to roughly 90% entertainment and 10% content.

  • In one case, this increased opens, clicks, and sales significantly. It was compared with a content-heavy approach.

  • Entertainment keeps subscribers reading after they have bonded with the character.

  • Brainstorm topics by asking:

    1. What is happening in the character's life right now?
    2. What recently happened that was embarrassing?
    3. How are you getting through the holiday season?
    4. Where are you planning your next vacation?
    5. What did you recently buy that you regret?
    6. What did you recently buy that you adore?
    7. What made you furious yesterday that you laugh about today?
    8. What did a child or pet get into yesterday?
    9. What funny past event taught a lesson?

  • Every story must lead back to an offer.

  • The offer can be your core product, another product of yours, or a partner's product.

  • Entertaining emails that never connect to an offer will not earn money. This is true however well they are written.

  • The tie-in is what turns storytelling into sales.

  • Pattern for Seinfeld emails:

    • A short everyday story.
    • A turn that exposes a lesson or mistake.
    • A natural bridge to an offer.

  • The subject line creates curiosity or a clear hook (an opening line that grabs attention).

  • The story keeps subscribers entertained every day.

  • The link makes every send commercially relevant.

  • Examples:

Example 1, subject '[True Story] I nearly lost a client over one spreadsheet'. Body: 'Yesterday a client called to say my report was wrong. I checked, and it was. The mistake came from a template I had trusted for years. That is why I now use the checklist in this guide: [link].'

Example 2, subject 'My dog ate the invoice (and what it taught me)'. Body: 'The dog chewed through the paper invoice this morning, which made me laugh. It also reminded me that I still send mine by post. The digital invoice tool I switched to last month is here: [link].'
  • Copy the daily entertaining email onto your blog. A blog is a page of articles on your site.
  • The same story then serves two purposes without extra writing. It leads readers toward a sale.
  • This is a quick way to keep the blog active.

Product Launch, Giveaway and Event Campaigns

1 min

Campaign Definition and Ideas

1 min
  • Campaigns are one-time email blasts. They go to a list or segment for events, launches, and seasonal offers.
  • Examples include Valentine's Day, Father's Day, Mother's Day, Christmas, a new product launch, or early access for VIP customers (your best customers).
  • Unlike flows, campaigns are not triggered by a visitor action.
  • They are sent to an existing audience on a set date.
  • Campaign ideas for a store with a consistent seller (a product that sells steadily):
    • Launches.
    • Offer launches.
    • Event launches, such as occasions like Mother's Day or Valentine's Day.
    • New product launches.
    • Specific collection promotions.
    • Bundle offers.
    • Giveaways and referral campaigns.
    • A weekly deal.
  • Each campaign should target a defined segment such as buyers, non-buyers, or VIPs.
  • Run 3-4 email campaigns a month from this list. Choose from offer launch, event launch, new product launch, specific collection promotion, giveaways, and deal of the week.

New Product and Category Announcements

1 min
  • New product categories can be announced by email and SMS (text messages) to existing customers.
  • Include the launch date and key features.
  • Repeat purchases from these customers need no ad spend (money paid for ads).
  • So the sale is close to pure profit. The acquisition cost (what you paid to win the customer) was already paid.
  • Good customer service is a precondition for this to work. A precondition is something that must be in place first.
  • Once you have a good customer email list, you can announce new products by email instead of paying for ads.
  • This makes your existing customer base a low-cost channel (a route to customers) for new product launches.
  • Email your customer list when you launch new products or promotions.
  • Do this even for a new private-label brand, which is a brand you sell under your own name.
  • Email drives traffic to products that Google Shopping (Google's product listings) rarely reaches.

New Product Launch Sequence

1 min
  1. One week before launch, send a VIP sign-up email. It announces the upcoming product with its features and images.
  2. Offer VIP sign-ups an extra discount on launch, for example 30% off an $80 product.
  3. Send three to four emails over the one-week period.
  4. Count the sign-ups to estimate how many purchases to expect. For example, 200 sign-ups out of 1,000 non-buyers.
  5. Send the next email to the people who signed up. Send a separate email to those who did not, asking why, and offering an extra benefit point.
  • Campaigns should warm up the audience before asking for the purchase.
  • Educate first. Then announce the launch. Then ask for the sign-up.
  • A direct buy request in the first email is too aggressive.

Weekly Deal

1 min
  • Weekly deal campaigns have a comparatively low conversion rate. Conversion rate is the share of people who buy.
  • They keep existing customers warm. They also stop customers from forgetting the brand after the first purchase.
  • Combine a benefit of a new product with a small extra discount each week.
  • A weekly 10% off deal is part of a monthly structure.
  • That structure is about four campaigns, plus one new product launch campaign per month.

Referral Giveaway

1 min
  1. Run the giveaway on social media. Ask followers to tag three friends and post to their stories.
  2. In the email, ask the customer to refer three people using a referral or affiliate link. This is a tracked link that shows who sent the customer.
  3. Reward the referrer when a referred contact signs up with an email or phone number.
  4. Add a larger reward when a referred contact buys.

Event Teaser

1 min
  • Add an event teaser at the bottom of a regular content email. A teaser is a short hint about what is coming:
PS: [Event name] is coming. [Link to signup page]
  • The linked page should ask only for an email address. This helps qualify (sort out) interested buyers.

Creating and Scheduling a Campaign in Klaviyo

1 min
  1. Open Campaigns and click Create campaign.
  2. Enter a campaign name, for example the event and year. Set the start date, for example 15 January for a Valentine's Day push. Click Save and continue.
  3. Select the recipients. These can be the Newsletter list or a segment such as VIP customers. Choose whether to exclude any groups. Choose whether to use tracking, which counts opens and clicks, and turn tracking on.
  4. Click Continue to content.
  5. Enter the subject line (the email's title) and the preview text (the short line shown after the title).
  6. Pick a template under Popular or from the template library. A template is a ready layout. For an event, choose the event template from the Popular library. If there is no event template, change a similar template and edit the event name.
  7. Change the background color to white or your brand color. Add text, images, or a dynamic product section. A dynamic product section is a block that fills itself with products from your store.
  8. Click Next.
  9. Turn Smart sending off. Smart sending is a Klaviyo timing setting.
  10. Choose Schedule or Send, then select Gradual send over several hours.
  11. Set the start time, for example 11 am Eastern. Then set the share of recipients per hour, for example 20 percent per hour.
  12. Click Schedule.
  • Gradual campaign sending means sending to a set share of recipients per hour. For example, 20 percent per hour. This is used instead of pushing all emails at one time.
  • Turn Smart sending off for campaigns too.
  • Klaviyo is the email platform where offer emails are scheduled and sent to the broadcast audience.
  • Scheduling of offers is done here alongside Google and Facebook.

Sponsored Blasts

1 min
  • Sponsored email blast: you pay a partner (another business you work with) to send your message to their email list (their list of saved email addresses). The price usually rises with the number of emails you send.
  • A sponsorship does not have to be a single email.
  • You can plan a series of emails with the partner. The series can promote your products, drive visitors (bring people) to your site, or point readers to your content.

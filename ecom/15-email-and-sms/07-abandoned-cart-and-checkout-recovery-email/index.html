Abandoned cart and checkout recovery (Email)

Section 15.7 · 7 of 17 in this chapter · 45 min read

In short

  • A shopper who starts paying and leaves is your best chance to win a sale back. Send them automatic messages.
  • Build the abandoned-cart message series before you spend more on ads or try other fixes.
  • Send the first message about 15 to 20 minutes after they leave, not right away.
  • Do not lead with a discount. Make the first messages about the product.
  • Do not push hard. The shopper has already decided to buy, so help them finish.
More key points
  • Do not offer a discount in the first email. Send a plain reminder with a link back to the cart first.
  • Hold the discount for a later email. It protects your profit (margin) from people who would have bought anyway.
  • Send the first reminder 15 to 20 minutes after the person leaves checkout (the payment page).
  • Keep every discount inside your margin. Use free shipping first if it costs less than 10% off.
  • Check the "Amount Spent" condition in Shopify before you rely on it. The sources disagree on it.

Purpose and Priority

3 min
  • An abandoned checkout happens when a customer adds products, starts checkout (the payment steps where they type in their details), and does not finish buying.
  • An abandoned cart flow (an automatic series of messages) is sent to shoppers who started checkout but did not finish the order.
  • The flow brings them back to the cart they already filled.
  • Visitors who add to cart and leave without buying waste paid traffic (visitors you pay for through ads).
  • Unless you follow up with them, that ad money is gone.
  • Automated reminder emails or SMS (text messages) let you contact them hours later.
  • That follow-up costs no extra ad cost.
  • Roughly 7 to 8 out of 10 shoppers who add products to their cart leave without buying.
  • So an abandoned-cart sequence reaches a large share of your site visitors.
  • Recovering about 25% of abandoned carts through an email sequence is a realistic goal once the flow is improved.
  • Abandoned cart recovery is among the cheapest ways to win back shoppers who added items to their cart.
  • Improving the abandoned-cart flow is one of the fastest ways to see results in an online store.
  • It works because it recovers sales that already had strong intent (the buyer clearly wanted the product).
  • Gains can show as lower cost per lead (what it costs to get one possible customer), better traffic quality (visitors more likely to buy) and a higher conversion rate (the share of visitors who buy).
  • A recovered buyer can become a repeat customer who leaves reviews and refers others.
  • Over time, that buyer can become a brand advocate (a fan who recommends you).
  • This long-term value makes the extra effort in recovery sequences worth it.
  • Set up the abandoned-cart email sequence before any other optimization (any other attempt to make the store work better).
  • Use it to learn why visitors leave.
  • Avoid discounts as the first lever (the first thing you try).
  • Discounts should be strategic and paired with something of higher value.
  • Set up the abandoned-cart sequence before you scale traffic (spend more on ads).
  • set up the abandoned-cart email sequence in Klaviyo (email software) from day one, before any traffic campaigns run.
  • once the store is established, run abandoned-cart follow-up as an automated email flow, for example one built in Klaviyo.
  • Klaviyo is used to set up abandoned-cart emails with customized messages and urgency (a reason to act now).
  • abandoned carts can turn into sales in the days after a visit, so configure these early.
  • an abandoned-cart email sequence is an automated message sent through an email autoresponder (a tool that sends emails on its own) when a shopper leaves a cart without buying.
  • Klaviyo can run abandoned-cart recovery.
  • It sends a notification to visitors who left items in their cart without completing the purchase.
  • Shopify's email or Klaviyo can both send abandoned-cart messages, and both work.
  • The author's team uses Klaviyo.
  • a person who added items and started checkout is the warmest prospect you will get (the most likely buyer).
  • So extra reminder emails are acceptable here.
  • Abandoned-cart messages are reminders about items left in the basket, not welcome emails.
  • Keep the copy focused on the cart and its contents.
  • Trust comes from the logo, the product block (the box showing the item) and clear next steps.
  • Trust does not come from a long introduction.
  • A shopper who abandoned a cart has already moved through the ad, the site, the product page and add-to-cart (the button that puts an item in the cart).
  • Their purchase intent (how strongly they want to buy) is high.
  • The messages should help them close the sale, not reintroduce the brand.
  • Abandoned-cart messages are bottom-of-funnel (close to buying). The funnel is the path from first visit to purchase.
  • Do not use top-of-funnel content (first-visit content) such as broad brand introductions.
  • Focus on trust, objection handling (answering doubts) and social proof (reviews showing others bought).
  • At this stage the buyer has already decided.
  • Messages should validate that decision, build trust and handle objections.
  • Pressure or a hard sell at the first contact tends to backfire.

Spacing Rules

1 min
  • Use 20-minute, 45-minute and 23-hour intervals (gaps between messages) rather than random timings.
  • The 23-hour wait hits the customer at the same time of day they abandoned.
  • That is when they are most likely to be free and receptive.
  • Space emails using multiples of 24 and 12 hours.
  • For example: 20 min + 45 min ≈ 1 hour, then 23 hours, 24 hours, 12 hours.
  • Avoid arbitrary intervals like 4 hours.
  • They hit customers at inconvenient times (for example 2 a.m.).
  • They also get lost in inbox clutter by morning.
  • Conflict: the Source A recommendation uses a 4-hour first delay.
  • The Source E path 2 pain email also waits 4 hours.
  • The Source E rule says to avoid 4-hour intervals.
  • Source E test data: 8.1 orders per 100 abandons with a 20-minute delay.
  • The same test shows 3.7 orders per 100 abandons with a 4-hour delay.
  • Use the Source E evidence where the 20-minute start is used.
  • The 4-hour delay remains the Source A recommendation for the first email.
  • Scheduled send time: in Klaviyo, use Time Delay with Wait until specific time (a step that waits for a set clock time).
  • This sends at a consistent time in the customer's timezone, for example 7 a.m..
  • This prevents emails sent at inconvenient hours.
  • Example from the same source: wait 13 hours to reach an 8 p.m. send time.
  • This creates midnight expiration urgency (pressure from a deadline).
  • Discount offer email at 7:00 am in the sequence.
  • Source C: the 48-hour email is optional.

Expiry Windows

1 min
  • Source E: the abandoned-checkout flow has a 72-hour window.
  • After that window, the product is returned to the shelf (back to normal stock) and the flow ends.
  • Source C: the cart discount should expire only after the final cart email.
  • Send email 4 a few hours before that expiry.
  • Derive the gaps between emails 1 to 3 from the expiry you set.
  • The cart copy does not fix those gaps.
  • Source B: label time-limited discounts clearly as limited, here for 3 days.
  • Reminders should show the remaining time, such as 24 hours and 4 hours.

Day-Based Countdown

1 min

Abandoned-cart email countdown over seven days:

  1. Day 5: send an email saying there are 72 hours left.
  2. Day 6: send an email saying there are 48 hours left.
  3. Day 7: send three emails within the day, at 12 hours, 6 hours and 1 hour left.
  4. Make clear in the final message that this is the last and best offer.

Full Sequence Procedures

2 min

Source C four-email abandoned checkout flow (in this order):

  1. Email 1: send it about 10 to 15 minutes after checkout is abandoned.
    • Wait at least 10 minutes, since entering card details takes 5-7 minutes. 20 minutes can also be tested.
    • Use a benefit-led headline (it leads with what the customer gains), not a salesy one.
    • Describe the customer's pain point and how they will feel after getting the product.
    • Add 3-4 bullet points on why they need it.
    • End with a benefit-driven call to action, such as "get yours today" or "make your partner happy by ordering now".
  2. Email 2: send it about 2 hours after email 1.
    • Use a subject line (the email title) that says the order is not yet complete.
    • Add 3-4 five-star reviews with images.
    • Add a link back to the order.
    • Add urgency, such as limited stock (for example only 50 pieces remaining), a price increase after 4-5 days, or "while stock lasts".
  3. Email 3: send it about 6 hours after email 2.
    • Use a new subject line that announces an exclusive deal with an extra 10% off.
    • Repeat the benefit points, reviews, headline and bullet points.
    • Restate the same offer.
  4. Final email: say the deal is about to end.
    • Give a final confirmation.
    • State that no further emails will follow.

Source C three-email abandoned checkout sequence:

  1. First email: explain the product's benefits.
    • Address the customer's pain point or current situation.
    • Explain why this product fits them.
    • Show the product with 3-4 bold headline points and a call to action.
  2. Second email: show image reviews and testimonials.
  3. Third email: make a better offer with an extra 10-20% off.

Source B abandoned-cart sequence:

  1. Reminder email immediately after abandonment, containing the product and a link back to checkout.
  2. Second reminder email 6 hours after the first, same type (product plus checkout link).
  3. Personal welcome-style email from the business owner 18 hours after the second reminder.
    • It asks whether you can help and invites a reply.
    • It does not push the product.
  4. Discount offer 24 hours later, limited to 3 days, with the product, a checkout link and the discount code.
  5. After 2 days without purchase, send one of two emails.
    • Either a helpful content email, such as a buyer's guide, if the product range is broad.
    • Or a discount reminder if the range is narrow, stating 24 hours remain to finish the purchase.
  6. 24 hours later, send a final discount reminder stating about 4 hours remain before the discount expires.
  7. Hand the contact over to the 30-day master flow for prospects (a long-running email series for leads), so they keep receiving emails.

Source A Shopify template sequence (overview):

  • The pre-built template automatically includes the customer's abandoned products in the first email.
  • It uses the color scheme set in the Messaging app settings.
  • The pre-built template is effective because Shopify designed it using performance data.
  • Keeping it unchanged is a safe choice.
  • Customize it only if you have specific improvements beyond the default version.

Desire vs Pain Messaging

1 min
  • Desire messaging focuses on what the customer wants to gain or feel.
  • Pain messaging focuses on the biggest problem the product solves.
  • Test both with a 50/50 split (half the visitors see each version) to find which resonates with your audience.
  • Effectiveness varies by product category and niche (the group of customers you sell to).
  • Desire messaging outperformed pain messaging for the first abandoned-cart email in the same Source E test.
  • Desire path A reached 83.8% open (opened the email), 16.2% click (clicked a link) and 8.1% order.
  • Pain path B reached 82.4% open, 5.9% click and 2.9% order.
  • Find the pain point by asking what goes wrong during the product's hardest real use.
  • Examples of hard use: long sessions in heat or a full working day.
  • Name that exact situation in the email so the reader recognises their own experience.
  • Pain-based example for bike gear: the subject line "Saddle Sores? Say No More!".
  • It targets the specific objection (uncomfortable clothing causing soreness).
  • It works better than generic benefits because it overcomes the biggest barrier to purchase in that category.
  • Objections are usually offer, price, lack of trust or uncertainty.
  • Address each objection in its own email.
  • Trust-based messaging focuses on trust, objection handling and social proof.
  • Do not spend those messages introducing the brand.
  • Source E offer email: tease what waits in the cart without naming the product.
  • This creates curiosity and gets opens.
  • Short versus story-driven pain copy after the 23-hour wait: see the benchmark results below.
  • Reminders must not suggest the customer forgot something.
  • That wording implies the item was not valuable to them.
  • Use assumptive wording instead, such as saying something went wrong at checkout.

Discount Rules

  • Hold the discount back until the second email.
  • This protects your profit (margin) from people who would have bought anyway.
  • Only people who still hesitate get the offer.
  • The most common mistake with abandoned carts (items left in the cart without buying) is offering a discount right away.
  • Start with a plain reminder and a checkout link instead.
  • Many people leave for reasons that have nothing to do with price. Examples: distraction, a lost connection, or an interruption.
  • Lead with value and the product before any discount.
  • Make discounts strategic. Pair them with something of higher value.
  • Use only the offer that is cheapest for your margin. Offer free shipping first. Use 10% off only if free shipping costs more.
  • For carts over $100, mention free shipping instead of one of the SMS (text message) value points.
  • Source C discount steps: none in email 1, then $5, then $10. The discount grows through the sequence (the series of automatic emails).
  • Say clearly when an offer is limited. Here, the limit is 3 days.
  • Reminders should show the time left, such as 24 hours and 4 hours.
  • Source C: email 1 has no discount. Email 2 has a small code. Email 3 has the largest code, and its deadline is stated.

Conditions, Splits and Filters

3 min

Send Conditions

A condition decides whether an email is sent to a person. A filter removes people from a group.

  • Second email conditions: the customer must not have placed an order since leaving the cart.
  • The customer must also not have started a new checkout.
  • First email conditions (Shopify template): the first email sends only if both are true.
  • Condition one: the customer has not placed an order since the workflow (the automatic process) started.
  • Condition two: at least one product from the abandoned cart is in stock.
  • Second email conditions (Shopify template): the second email sends only if the customer has no order since leaving the cart.
  • The last abandoned checkout must be more than 24 hours ago. This stops duplicate emails from many abandoned carts.
  • Purchase exclusion: if a customer buys before a delay timer (a countdown that waits before the next step) ends, they leave all remaining email steps automatically.
  • New checkout during a running automation: if a customer starts a new checkout before the first abandoned checkout automation finishes, that automation cancels without sending emails.
  • If the new checkout is also abandoned, the customer enters a fresh abandoned checkout automation for the new session. This allows re-entry without duplicate emails.
  • Second email spend filter: send the second abandoned checkout email only to customers who have spent over $0.00 before.
  • This filters out brand-new customers, who may not be ready for follow-up offers.
  • Conflict on the spend filter: the Shopify workflow sets the Amount Spent condition (the total money a customer has spent in the store) to "is equal to $0" (Customer > Amount Spent). It does this in its condition step and again in its review step.
  • The spend filter described in sends to customers who spent over $0.00.
  • The sources do not explain which rule applies in which case. Check the condition in Shopify before you rely on either one.
  • Branch by cart size: if the cart is over $100, mention free shipping in the SMS.
  • If the cart is under the threshold, add free shipping or add-on suggestions in email four.
  • Branch by product range: after 2 days without a purchase, check the product range.
  • If the range is broad, send a helpful content email, such as a buyer's guide.
  • If the range is narrow, send a discount reminder.

Splits

A split divides people into two paths. An A/B test (two versions tested on different people) compares the paths.

  • Source E 50/50 split: send the first email as an A/B test.
  • Path A uses desire messaging (talks about what the customer wants).
  • Path B uses pain messaging (talks about the problem the customer has).
  • Klaviyo setup for the split: use a Conditional Split (the flow sends people down one of two paths by a rule).
  • The rule is 'Is part of a 50% random sample'.
  • The live split was about 51% and 49%.
  • Path 1 (desire or objection email #1A): sent 20 minutes after checkout starts.
  • Path 2 (pain-point email #1B): sent 4 hours after checkout starts.
  • Conditional split for reminders: after the first email, check 'Has Opened Email zero times since starting this flow.'
  • If the email was not opened, send a 45-minute reminder with the same subject line.
  • If the email was opened, let the customer move on. Do not send a repeat email.
  • Source E test step: after the 23-hour wait, test short, direct pain copy against story-driven pain copy.

Copy and Subject Formulas

8 min

Subject-Line Formulas

The subject line is the email's title. A formula is a pattern you can fill in.

  • Formula for a cart-recovery subject line: suggest that something may have gone wrong at checkout.
  • Then promise a direct link back to the cart.
  • A question opener lowers pressure. It makes the customer feel less chased.
    • Example 1: "Did something go wrong at checkout? Here is your cart: {link}"
    • Example 2: "Quick question about your order, {first name}. Your cart is saved here: {link}"
  • Pattern for the last-chance subject line: put the exact hours left first. Then give the offer and its expiry.
    • Example from the author: "2 hours left: your 10% code ends tonight"
    • Example from the author: "Last call: your saved bag closes in 3 hours"
  • Pattern for the offer email subject: tease what waits in the cart, but do not name the product. This creates curiosity and gets opens (the email is opened).
    • Example from the author: "Something good is still sitting in your basket"
    • Example from the author: "Your favourite pick is still on hold"
  • Formula for the Source C email 1 subject line in the Clavio setup: write a friendly reminder that the cart is waiting.
    • Example of the same tone: "Still thinking it over? Your cart is saved for a few more hours."
  • Example for the second email: "Stock on your picks is running low. Here is 10 dollars off for the next hour."
  • Benefit-led first email: the sources give no example beyond the template. Set the subject to include the product name.
  • Discount offer example subject (Source E test): "You Won't Believe What's Waiting in Your Cart" with a 15% discount.
  • Discount reminder example subject (Source E scarcity test): "4 Hours Left: 15% Off Expires Soon"
  • Default subject line for the first abandoned checkout reminder email: "You left items at checkout"
  • The Source E sender name uses the brand and a monitored support address.
  • The subject "RE: About your order" makes the email look like a reply in the inbox.
  • Source C third email subject: "your cart is still waiting for you and so is our discount"
  • Day 3 to 4 email subject: "Your {product name} is still waiting for you"
  • "Did you forget something?" was used as the first abandoned-cart subject line.
  • Source E advises against wording reminders as if the customer forgot something.
  • So use the newer wording, not "Did you forget something?"
  • Source C second email subject: say the order is not yet complete.
  • Source C third email subject: announce an exclusive deal with an extra 10% off.

Copy Rules

Copy means the words in the email.

  • One strong headline is enough in the first abandoned cart email.
  • A sub-headline (a second line under the headline) can be dropped.
  • In this store's tests, the extra line added nothing. Remove it unless a split test proves otherwise.
  • Repeat the real deadline in the P.S. (postscript, the line at the bottom) of an offer email. Many readers scroll to the end.
  • Keep the P.S. deadline matching the countdown and the code expiry time.
  • Sign the offer and last-chance emails with the founders' first names. This adds a personal touch.
  • The sign-off should match the brand voice across the sequence.
  • Each abandoned-cart email should contain a direct button to the checkout link. The button sends the customer back to the exact cart in one click.
  • Copy the same link into each email in the sequence.
  • Text-based abandoned-cart emails with some images and lots of personal text tend to convert better. They feel personal and land in the primary inbox (the main folder, not spam or promotions) more often.
  • The personal welcome email in the cart sequence should ask a direct question and invite a reply. Do not repeat the product.
  • Its goal is to uncover objections. Examples: an unresolved question, or hesitation about a competitor.
  • A reply can lead to a personal conversation that moves the sale forward.
  • Personal message from the store owner: frame the second follow-up email as a personal message from the owner.
  • Offer direct help with questions. Invite the customer to reply.
  • Present it as a one-on-one conversation, not an automated broadcast. This raises engagement and invites a direct reply.
  • Insert the abandoned checkout products block into the second reminder email.
  • This shows the exact items the customer left behind. It works better than generic product suggestions or inspirational content.
  • Last-chance email build order:
    1. Copy the layout of the offer email.
    2. Change the subject to a countdown, such as "4 Hours Left: 15% Off Expires Soon".
    3. Update the headline to say time is almost up and the discount ends.
    4. Insert the product block of the items left behind between the headline and the CTA (call to action, the button that asks the customer to act).
    5. Add the final warning and the CTA to complete the order with the discount applied.
  • Source C email 1 copy: write it as a reminder.
  • Add a product benefit or a guarantee. Add a link back to the store.
  • Keep no discount in this first message.
  • Source E abandoned-cart copy should be benefit-led.
  • It should describe the customer's pain point and how they will feel after getting the product.
  • Use Shopify's AI tool to refine the first email template and improve its messaging.
  • For the first abandoned checkout email, use Shopify's Generate Text button (a built-in AI writing tool).
  • Give it a description, such as "You left items at checkout".
  • Give it a tone, such as playful.
  • Pick one of the suggested subject lines.

Tracking and Mistakes

2 min

Tracking and Go-Live Checks

  • Source C: for each email, set Smart sending to off, UTM tracking to on, and switch from Draft to Live.
  • Source C: set every email in the sequence to Live after cloning.
  • Shopify Admin: click Apply Changes and confirm to make the changes effective immediately.
  • Shopify status check: confirm the button shows 'Turn off workflow' to verify the automation is active.
  • Shopify Messaging: the automation now runs indefinitely once active.
  • Check each timer before going live.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving the default timers in place for welcome and abandoned cart flows means messages arrive too late or too late for the offer. Check each timer before going live.
  • Customizing the Shopify template without a specific improvement adds risk, because the pre-built template is built on performance data.
  • Offering a discount in the first message is the most common mistake; start with a plain reminder and a checkout link.
  • Stopping all emails after the abandoned-cart flow ends is a common error. Contacts who did not buy should move into the 30-day prospect master flow rather than being dropped, because they may still be in the market.
  • Do not word reminders as if the customer forgot something, because that implies the item was not valuable to them.
  • Do not restart the shopping journey in the early abandoned-cart messages by suggesting other products or new options. Doing so confuses a buyer who was ready to complete the purchase.
  • Do not add extra calls to action (social media icons, app download prompts or other links) to the first three abandoned-cart emails.
  • Do not add a second headline or sub-headline to the first email unless a split test proves it helps.
  • Do not send a last-chance email with a deadline that conflicts with the countdown or the code expiry time; keep the P.S. line consistent with both.
  • Do not keep a 4-hour first interval as a fixed rule for all emails; the Source E guidance avoids arbitrary 4-hour intervals that hit customers at inconvenient times.
  • Do not treat the abandoned cart flow as a one-off reminder; it is a series.
  • Do not leave an automation running with an expired discount; the discount should expire only after the final email, with email 4 sent a few hours before that expiry.
  • Do not send SMS to customers who did not opt in; SMS cannot recover their carts.
  • Do not use the abandoned-cart sequence to deliver top-of-funnel content or broad brand introductions.

Scope and Standard

1 min
  • Use an abandoned checkout flow with more than one email, not a single reminder. A typical flow runs five to seven emails spread across five to seven days.
  • If a store already runs an abandoned-cart flow, review it against the standard sequence: reminders, a personal owner email, a time-limited discount and a final reminder. Add only the missing emails rather than rebuilding the whole flow.
  • All advice in this section is older advice from Source B's 2021-22 course.

Why Emails Are Spaced

1 min
  • Abandoned-cart emails are spaced with waiting periods because many people do not check email right away.
  • Each step gives the shopper time to act before the next message is sent.

Sequence Timeline

1 min

The following schedule is derived from the moment of abandonment:

  1. 0 hours: first reminder with product and checkout link.
  2. 6 hours: second reminder of the same type.
  3. 24 hours: personal owner email asking whether you can help and inviting a reply.
  4. 48 hours: time-limited discount offer for 3 days.
  5. 96 hours: either a helpful content email (broad product range) or a discount reminder with 24 hours left (narrow product range).
  6. 120 hours: final discount reminder with about 4 hours left.
  7. After the final reminder, move the contact to the 30-day prospect master flow.

Per-Email Jobs

2 min

Reminders (0 hours and 6 hours). Both use the same plain text. Neither includes a discount.

Subject: You left {product_name} in your cart

Hi {first_name},

Your {product_name} is still in your cart and ready to order. You can finish your purchase here:
{checkout_link}

{business_name}

(Send the same email again 6 hours after the first one, using the same text. Do not include a discount in either reminder.)

Personal owner email (24 hours). Write it as a welcome-style email from the business owner, 18 hours after the second reminder, to a shopper who has not purchased. Use the following prompt text unchanged:

Write a personal welcome-style email from the business owner for an abandoned cart, 18 hours after the second reminder, to a shopper who has not purchased. Keep it short and written by a real person. Share a brief part of your story and what sets your business apart, and do not mention the abandoned product. End with one clear call to action: ask whether there is anything you can help with and ask the shopper to simply reply to this email. Use a plain-text style, no discount, no pressure.

Discount offer (48 hours). Send the time-limited discount with the product, the code and the checkout link. The offer lasts 3 days and ends on the expiry date. Use the following template unchanged:

Subject: {first_name}, your discount for {product_name} is ready

Hi {first_name},

You left {product_name} in your cart. We have set up a discount code for you: {discount_code}

Use the code at checkout here:
{checkout_link}

This offer is time limited and ends in 3 days, on {expiry_date}. After that the code will no longer work.

{business_name}

Narrow-range discount reminder (96 hours, 24 hours left). Use this email when the store has a narrow product range. Use the following prompt text unchanged:

Write a discount-reminder email for an abandoned cart, sent 24 hours after the discount offer when the store has a narrow product range. Include the product, a link back to checkout and the discount code, and state that 24 hours are left to finish the purchase. Keep it short and direct.

Broad-range content email (96 hours). Use this email when the store has a broad product range, in place of the discount reminder.

Final reminder (120 hours, about 4 hours left). Send a brief, factual final reminder. Use the following prompt text unchanged:

Write a final abandoned-cart reminder email, sent 4 hours before a discount expires. Include the product, a direct link back to checkout, the discount code, and a short, clear statement that the discount is about to expire in 4 hours. Keep it brief and factual, with no extra offers.

Discount Timing

1 min
  • The discount is offered at 48 hours and runs for 3 days.
  • The first two reminders contain no discount.
  • The narrow-range reminder is sent at 96 hours, when 24 hours are left.
  • The final reminder is sent at 120 hours, with about 4 hours left.
  • The source values do not fully align, so check them before building:
    • The 96-hour send matches the "24 hours left" wording, since the discount ends at 120 hours.
    • The wording "sent 24 hours after the discount offer" does not match the 96-hour timing. The offer is at 48 hours, so 24 hours after it would be 72 hours.
    • The 3-day discount from 48 hours ends at 120 hours. The final reminder at 120 hours is described as "about 4 hours left", which implies expiry near 124 hours.

Conditions and Splits

1 min
  • Product range decides the 96-hour email. A broad range receives a helpful content email. A narrow range receives a discount reminder with 24 hours left.
  • After the final reminder, the contact moves to the 30-day prospect master flow.

Personalisation Fields

1 min
  • Templates use these fields: {first_name}, {product_name}, {checkout_link}, {discount_code}, {expiry_date} and {business_name}.

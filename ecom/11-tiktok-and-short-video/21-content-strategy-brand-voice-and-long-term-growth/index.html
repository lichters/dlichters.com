Playbook › 11. TikTok and short video

Content strategy, brand voice and long-Term growth

Section 11.21 · 21 of 22 in this chapter · 5 min read

In short

  • Give each weekday one fixed theme, so followers learn when each type of post comes.
  • Short videos are the main way brands get seen. Show the product in use. Do not rely on static pictures.
  • Trends fade in about two to three weeks. Mix trend posts with content that stays useful for years.
  • Do not buy followers. Judge results over several posts, not one.
  • Keep posting. One viral hit does not last, and stopping after a success wastes it.

Content pillars and weekly plans

  • Give each weekday one content pillar (a fixed theme you post about that day), so your followers (people who follow your account) learn when each type of post appears.
  • Sample week for a short-video account (an account that posts short clips):
Day Pillar
Monday Behind-the-scenes routine videos (a look at your daily work)
Tuesday Practical hack videos (quick, useful tips)
Thursday Advice videos
Friday Trend videos (videos about topics many people are copying now)
  • The sample assigns no pillar to Wednesday, Saturday or Sunday.

Evergreen versus trend content

  • Post content that matches trending topics (topics many people are talking about now) regularly to boost visibility (how many people see your posts), but do not rely only on trends.
  • Organic (free reach, not paid ads) short-form content is not a quick result. Test several content types to see which ones work with your followers.
  • Create evergreen content (content that stays useful for a long time) that can be repurposed (reused on other platforms) for years.
  • Video trends on TikTok last about two to three weeks before momentum (the push that keeps views rising) fades, so change style and angle on that cycle.
  • A video that gets a million views can stop growing after a single day.
  • Keep producing new content and new viral angles (fresh ways to make a video spread fast to many people) to keep results coming.

Brand voice and format

  • Short-form video (short clips made for phones, like TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts) is now the main way brands get pushed on social media.
  • Static posts (still pictures and text) do little on these platforms, so brand content should be video showing the product in use.
  • Brands that win on social media tend not to take themselves too seriously.
  • Lighter, playful content usually performs better than polished corporate messaging (smooth, formal company talk).

Personal creator accounts and product accounts

  • Use a different rule for personal creator accounts (accounts run by one person who makes content) and product accounts (accounts about one product or brand).
  • A personal creator account should keep posting consistently.
  • A product or brand account that is not converting (not making sales or the action you want) should be abandoned, and the product moved to a new account after a short test.
  • Follower count matters little, so a product account with 10,000 to 70,000 followers may be dropped without much loss.
  • Switching an existing account to a different product can cost followers, because followers follow the account's identity (what the account stands for) and not only the content.
  • Start a new account when the product or niche (a narrow topic you focus on) changes significantly.

Follower thresholds (follower numbers that mark a new stage), with each applying to a different situation:

Situation Follower count Action Ids
Early phase, first base (your starting group of followers) About 1,000 Pass this mark with an easy angle (a simple viewpoint) first
Established base About 10,000 Move to higher-converting product angles (ways to present a product that sell more) and keep testing new ones
Product account not converting 10,000 to 70,000 May be dropped without much loss
  • The 1,000 and 10,000 marks guide an account that is growing and has a base, while the 10,000 to 70,000 drop rule applies only to a product account that is not converting.

Case: a creator with a personal account showing their own face, not a product account, passed 15,000 followers in about two weeks. After one video went viral, they uploaded several new videos and re-uploaded their best-performing videos as slightly changed versions with a new hook (the first seconds that grab attention).

Growth benchmarks (numbers to compare against) from the same case:

  • 1,000 followers within 15 days of the breakthrough (the moment growth suddenly jumps).
  • Then 10,000 and about 20,000 followers as viral videos continued.
  • Follower growth followed the viral videos rather than steady daily gains.

Volume benchmarks

Benchmark Value Ids
Successful account with consistent uploads (posting regularly), minimum About 150 to 200 uploaded videos
High-volume posting, over one year About 500 videos
  • Organic video can keep generating sales after it goes viral, without new uploads. A large catalog (all the videos you have posted) can produce a few sales each day.
  • Keep uploading anyway to keep traffic (visitors) growing.
  • After one viral video, do not stop uploading. Continue posting good quality content, because stopping after an organic success wastes the momentum.

AI-generated faceless content

  • One approach was to create about 60 faceless AI videos (AI-made videos with no person on screen) and publish them on YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and TikTok to test organic traffic before selling products through them.

General social media growth principles

  • Judge an influencer's (a person with many followers who promotes things) TikTok performance over several posts, not a single video, because short-form results rise and fall sharply from one video to the next.
  • A new account gets a broad mix of content, because the algorithm (the app's system that picks who sees each post) does not know what viewers want at first.
  • Train the feed (what the app shows you) on purpose by interacting (liking, commenting, sharing) with the content type you want to create, such as watching and engaging with similar videos.
  • Buying followers is not recommended, because TikTok can detect purchased or foreign accounts (accounts that were bought or come from another country), which can lead to shadowbanning (the app quietly hides your posts).
  • Purchased followers on Instagram can affect reels at first, but they do not provide lasting reach (viewers who keep seeing you over time).
  • Keep testing new angles constantly, since a good video can still break out at around 10,000 followers.

Live streaming

  • Consistent live streaming (broadcasting live on a regular schedule) is described as one of the few reliable ways to perform well on TikTok.
  • Live streaming takes a lot of effort and does not suit every brand, so weigh it against the team's capacity (how much work the team can handle) before committing.
  • TikTok's creator settings side panel (a menu on the side of the screen) separates shortcuts for Live, Subscriptions (a feature for fan subscriptions) and Series (a group of linked videos). These give creators quicker access to those monetization (earning money from your account) and content formats.

Branded challenges

  • Branded challenges (challenges run by a brand) were a major format in early TikTok, when the Explore page (TikTok's page that shows new posts to people) listed brand challenges. This describes early TikTok and is older advice.
  • A campaign built around a theme, product or hashtag (a tag word starting with #) works the same way: creators join it and the brand gains a pool of content (a set of posts it can use).

Warming up and launching a new account

Section 11.4 · 4 of 22 in this chapter · 7 min read

In short

  • A new account often gets an early boost. A quick spike of views does not prove people will buy.
  • Warm up the account first: wait 6-12 hours before the first upload.
  • Have 9 finished videos ready before you launch a new account.
  • If a new account gets fewer than 500 views in its first 24 hours, check the account and the content.
  • Judge the product at day 3-4, and only after you check real sales.
This section has 9 parts. Tap one to open it.

Choosing Between a New Account and an Existing One

1 min
  • A new account can reach more viewers than an older account with a similar follower count (number of people who follow it). This is true even when the video is only average.
  • Multiple accounts (several accounts run at once) are mainly useful for sellers who need reach (how many people see your video) quickly.
  • Established creators (people with a large, proven audience) usually do not need multiple accounts.
  • If an established account stays flat for a long period, testing the same product on a fresh account is a reasonable next step.
  • An older account with weak reach for months can be beaten by a new account that gets views right away.

Reusing an Existing Account for a Different Product

1 min

Use this procedure to make a reused account (an old account you use again) look like a fresh start.

  1. Change the account name.
  2. Set all old videos to private or delete them.
  3. Post the new product consistently, without skipping days.
  4. Expect slow views for one to two weeks while the algorithm (the app's system that decides who sees each video) adjusts to the new content.

Warm-Up Waiting Period

1 min

A warm-up means using an account in a normal way for a while before you post anything.

Standard warm-up procedure

  1. Create a TikTok account or warm up an existing one.
  2. Wait 6-12 hours before uploading the first video, or up to a week.
  3. During the wait, follow accounts in your niche (your small topic area), follow competitors, follow influencers, and engage with your target audience's content. Engage means like or comment on it.
  4. Do not upload videos or take any spammy actions (repeated or pushy actions that look like spam) during this period.
  5. After the warm-up, upload the first video. The account will receive views faster than a brand-new account with zero history.

New-email test procedure

Use this when you are confident a product is viable (able to sell well) but no account is gaining traction (getting views).

  1. Create a new TikTok account with a new email address.
  2. Wait 12-24 hours.
  3. During the wait, do not upload videos. Instead, follow accounts related to your product category and engage with their content.
  4. After 12-24 hours, upload your first video.
  5. Repeat this process 5-7 times if the product still struggles to gain traction.

Wait durations by source

Source means where the information came from.

Variant Wait before first upload Applies to Source

  • A warmed account that has engaged with other accounts before its first post gets the new account advantage (an early boost the app gives fresh accounts) sooner. It also performs better than an account that posted right away.
  • The advantage applies as long as no videos have been uploaded.

New-Account Advantage and Its Timing

1 min
  • A brand-new account can get an early distribution boost (the app shows its video to more people) during its first days or weeks.
  • A sudden spike of views on a fresh account does not prove that the product converts (turns viewers into buyers).
  • The timing starts from the first video upload, not from when the account was created. Plan the first upload to use the early window.
  • Duration: the sources differ.
  • One source says the advantage typically lasts about one week.
  • Another says the window lasts about one to two weeks.
  • Plan for the shorter figure as the guaranteed period. Treat two weeks as the upper bound (the most it could be).
  • Like-to-view ratio (likes compared to views; 1:20 means one like for every 20 views): new accounts can get away with weaker ratios, around 1:20 to 1:30.
  • Stronger push: new accounts can be pushed with weaker engagement ratios, such as 1:40 to 1:50, and sometimes even 1:100, depending on watch time (how long people watch).
  • Proven content on a new account is therefore treated as stronger than it really is.
  • Local-first distribution: new accounts are first shown to their local area.
  • Local targeting means showing a video only to people nearby.
  • Once a video passes about 1 million views, it is shown across many countries, and local targeting no longer applies.

Case: fast viral result on a second account

  • A product performed well on a first account for one week. So a second account was built on that proven product.
  • Paid Coins (TikTok coins you buy with money to boost a video) ran on the new account from its first day. By day three, it had gone viral (spread very fast) with millions of views.
  • The team uses this boost only in the first three days of a new account, since the advantage window lasts about one to two weeks.

Preparing Content Before the First Upload

1 min
  • Keep a content calendar (a plan of what you post and when) with at least 9 finished videos ready before launching a new account. Nine videos cover three days when three uploads are made per day.
  • Use the buffer (spare finished videos) to react to trends (popular topics) or strong comments without missing the minimum posting schedule.
  • A new account should upload at least three videos per day during its first one to two weeks.
  • Film more videos than you post so a buffer is always ready. For example, filming about nine videos per day produces three publishable videos in advance, which keeps daily posting consistent.

Launch Boost with Coins

1 min

A launch boost is a paid push at the start. It uses coins.

Coins procedure for a new account (first three days)

  1. On day one, put about $10 in coins on the best video of the day.
  2. Wait until midday and compare views across the day's videos.
  3. Put the next $10 in coins on the video performing best at that point. This may be a day-one video rather than a newer one.
  4. Repeat for three days, for about $30 total.
  5. If a video goes viral on day one or two, ease off (spend less on) the coins. The boost has broken the barrier (the hurdle) of a few thousand views and helped TikTok find the audience.

Expected Early View Benchmarks

1 min

A benchmark is a normal number to compare your result against. Growth trajectory means how views grow day by day.

Window Expected views How to use it Source
First 24 hours (minimum) 500-1,000 views for a posted video Floor (lowest acceptable level) for a new account with a posted video; below it, investigate
Initial test per video About 500 viewers Standard distribution test, applies to any reasonably presented video
Day 1 600-1,000 views Growth-trajectory benchmark
Day 2 1,000-2,000 views Growth-trajectory benchmark
Day 2 (floor) At least a few hundred views Minimum target in the first days; a lower floor than the trajectory above
Day 3 5,000-10,000 views Growth-trajectory benchmark
First video of a new account About 400-500 views Typical result, not a target
  • By day 3-4, this trajectory shows whether the product has viral potential (could spread very fast) or should be abandoned (stopped).
  • If views fall below these benchmarks, the cause is account problems or product-market misalignment, not content quality.
  • Account problems include shadow-banning (the app quietly hides your videos from most viewers) and insufficient warm-up.
  • Product-market misalignment means the product does not match the people who watch.

First 24-48 Hour Checks

1 min

Checks in order

  1. Do not judge a video at zero views too early. It can sit at zero for up to about 24 hours while the platform identifies the audience. Most views arrive within a few hours.
  2. Confirm the video is live (visible to viewers).
  3. Compare the first 24 hours against the minimum. If a new account with a posted video gets fewer than 500 views, investigate.
  4. Investigate for shadow-banning, spammy activity, poor content, or product-market misalignment.
  5. If none of these is found, create a new account and retry.
  6. If the first video gets under 100-500 views, especially after several uploads, the account may be shadow-banned or the content is weak.
  7. Decide on the product at day 3-4, based on the growth trajectory.
  8. Confirm actual sales before judging the product.

Thresholds and when each applies

A threshold is a line. When a video crosses it, you take action.

Threshold What it indicates When it applies Source
Fewer than 500 views in first 24 hours Investigate account and content New account with a posted video
Fewer than 500 views Platform issue (shadow-ban, spam, warm-up, restrictions), not content Initial distribution test
Under 100-500 views on first video Shadow-ban or weak content After several uploads
  • Poor content still clears (passes) the 500-viewer distribution threshold. A low count therefore points to the platform or account, not to content quality.

Checking Whether a Video Is Live

1 min

Procedure

  1. After uploading, tap Copy link.
  2. If a banner at the top says the video is under review (the app is checking it before it goes live), it is not yet published. Review can take a few minutes to a couple of hours.
  3. If you cannot copy the link, the video has not been fully reviewed.
  4. As a second method, have someone else look up the video on their phone.
  • A publishing delay of about 1.5-2 hours before the video appears publicly is normal.

Playbook › 11. TikTok and short video

Hooks, scripts and video structure

Section 11.6 · 6 of 22 in this chapter · 13 min read

In short

  • The first five seconds decide whether people keep watching. The hook (the opening that grabs attention) must work right away.
  • Keep videos at least 6 seconds long. Cut every slow moment.
  • Show the product clearly, in a way that feels like entertainment, not an ad.
  • Change the scene every 2 to 3 seconds so viewers do not get bored and scroll away.

Hook Design and First Seconds

1 min

The Opening Hook

1 min
  • The first five seconds decide whether viewers keep watching. A weak opening makes people swipe away (scroll off the video). The hook, the opening that grabs attention, must grab attention immediately.
  • Retention rate is the share of viewers who keep watching. It is treated as the most important signal for pushing a video, so the first seconds deserve the most effort. Some early drop-off is normal. The goal is to make the curve as flat as possible. The curve is the line on a chart that shows how many viewers stay over time.
  • Viewers often read on-screen text (words shown on the picture) or an emoji in the first seconds. Then they decide whether to keep watching. A short caption with a visible emoji in the opening frame (the first picture) can hold attention for the hook.
  • TikTok hooks must be more shocking than standard ad hooks. They should create curiosity that keeps the viewer watching. Other hook formats that work include before-and-after shots and point-of-view (POV) shots, which are filmed from the viewer's eyes.
  • A hook must promise something specific that the video then shows. Viewers stay to see whether it works.
  • Proven hook structures can be borrowed from product videos in other niches (other product types). The same opening works across different products. Copy the structure of the hook, not its exact wording. Then adapt the content to your product.
  • Virality means spreading fast and far on its own. It depends more on video quality than on luck. An engaging hook, such as a surprising question that makes viewers want to finish, is a key driver.
  • On TikTok, going viral depends on the hook, the wording, the angles (the viewpoint or story of the video) and the sound choices. Once these are found, repeat them across new videos.

Hook Pattern for a Short Product Video

1 min

Pattern: open with a short statement in the first seconds that makes viewers curious about a clear visible result. Keep the total length around seven seconds to raise watch time (how long people watch in total).

Example patterns to adapt to your own product:

  • "Your phone screen is dirtier than you think, see it clean in 7 seconds."
  • "This drawer organizer fits any kitchen sink, watch it fit in one try."

Hook Example from a Beauty Demo

1 min
  • A demonstration video (showing the product in use) for a beauty product that goes viral shows the product being used on screen. For example, it shows applying lashes to one eye while the other eye stays bare, for a side-by-side comparison. Viewers keep watching to see the result. This holds attention better than talking about ingredients or specifications (product details).

Native and Raw Entertainment Style

1 min

Core Principles

1 min
  • Native content looks like normal content, not an ad. Raw content looks real and unpolished. TikTok creatives (ads and video content) perform when they trigger an emotion, rather than reading like a sales pitch. The same principle can be applied to Facebook.
  • On TikTok, content is judged as entertainment rather than advertising. Viewers leave quickly if something looks like an ad. Hooks should lead with value or entertainment, and the product should appear inside that content, not be the focus. Skits (short acted scenes) with the product woven in tend to outperform straight product ads. Direct response styles (ads that push for a sale right away) that work on Facebook often fail here.
  • TikTok works as an entertainment platform rather than a social network. Users come to be entertained. A hook must create aspiration (a wish to be like or have something) or entertain before any product is shown. Ads that open with features or selling points (what the product does) are skipped quickly.
  • Content on TikTok should deliver one of three forms of value: aspiration, entertainment, or education. The ad must lead with one of these. Otherwise viewers feel sold to and leave.
  • Virality depends more on video quality than on luck.

Clean Versus Wild Style

1 min
  • Unconventional or overly wild video styles can go viral without converting well (without turning viewers into buyers). Viewers enjoy the spectacle but do not buy. Clean, professional-looking videos that show what the product does usually convert better.
  • Talk-heavy or dialogue-driven videos often get views but convert less than videos that show the product clearly. Use a talking-to-camera opening only when it leads into a demonstration.

Sound, Satisfaction and Creative Angles

1 min
  • Colorful, satisfying visuals with ASMR-style sound (soft, tingling sounds like tapping or crinkling) performed well on TikTok organically, especially with younger audiences. In one comparison, a candy-like colorful product outperformed a healthy-looking one.
  • Look for creative advantages in each product: ASMR-style sound, recreating a sound better than the original, cool transitions (smooth cuts between scenes), a clear wow moment, and relevant trends. Use these to stand out from similar videos.
  • A simple pencil product (Infinity pencil) went viral on TikTok, although it is not a high-converting product. Its success came from a trending sound (a popular audio clip) used at the right time. It also had a well-executed video with a good, aesthetically pleasing (nice-looking) background, and a sound that matched the video. The same product with a plain background would likely have performed much worse.

Case: Phone Case Demonstration

1 min
  • A phone case ad that works on TikTok can show the case being knocked, clicked and removed. It uses satisfying ASMR sounds and has no on-screen text or spoken sales pitch. The features are visible through the demonstration alone, so the viewer does not feel sold to.

Video Length and Clip Pacing

1 min

1 min

Hook-focused short product video Around 7 seconds
Dropshipping ads (ads for products shipped by a supplier, without your own stock) on TikTok 15 to 30 seconds, never more than one minute

  • Keep TikTok videos at least 6 seconds long. Anything shorter usually causes a large drop in retention and makes it harder for the platform to find the right audience. Very short videos can work occasionally.
  • Shorter videos are easier to keep watching and hit higher watch-time percentages. Start with videos around 6 seconds. Then gradually extend to about 10 seconds once you are comfortable with retention.
  • A clip of about five seconds can still perform well when it has strong visuals, clear sound and a styled background. The short length raises watch time and replays. This format works best when the product is satisfying to watch and the video loops easily (the end flows smoothly back into the start).
  • Video length for dropshipping ads on TikTok: aim for 15 to 30 seconds, and never exceed one minute. Two-minute ads only work for big brands with a famous founder who already has trust.
  • Making videos that run a minute or longer lowers average watch time. Viewers swipe away within seconds. Overly long videos are a common reason for weak retention.
  • Fake conversation videos, such as a customer and company exchange, work because viewers stay to see how the story ends. A longer video of around 15 seconds can raise watch time when the story is engaging.

Pacing Rules

1 min
  • Remove every dead moment and keep TikTok videos fast paced. Clips or sections under 3 seconds work well. The right length still depends on where each clip sits in the video.
  • Dull moments drive viewers to swipe away. Remove any pause or slow scene, even half a second. Longer videos should be edited down aggressively (cut hard) without removing the key content.
  • Change the camera angle, scene or use case every 2-3 seconds to hold attention. A clip that stays visually unchanged for more than two to three seconds causes people to scroll away.
  • When a video has longer clips, place them toward the end rather than the beginning. This eases viewers into the pace. Starting with long clips tends to lose attention early.

Editing Procedure for Trimming

1 min
  1. Cut every dull moment from the video, even if it is only a fraction of a second.
  2. Trim longer videos by keeping only the key content. A 20-second video may be cut to about 12 seconds.
  3. Open with a strong hook and avoid slow scenes that let viewers swipe.
  4. Have someone else or yourself rewatch the cut to find slow parts and make split-second edits.

Standard Product Video Structure

1 min

Structure for a 7 to 12 Second Product Video

1 min
  1. Hook clip, 1 to 3 seconds, ideally 1.5 to 2 seconds: on-screen text plus an unexpected background action.
  2. Product show clip: hold the product and spin it so viewers know what they are looking at.
  3. Demonstration clip: use the product on screen for about 2 to 3 seconds.
  4. Second demonstration clip: show the product in a different way, for example a different use or a different feature, for about 2 to 3 seconds.
  5. Further demonstration angles: keep adding demonstration angles so no single clip stays on screen too long. This stops viewers from swiping away out of boredom.

Longer Videos with a Foreshadow

1 min
  • If a video runs long, around 15 seconds, play the most interesting moment in the first second as a foreshadow (a short preview of the payoff). That opening clip must be very satisfying, because it makes viewers stay to see the full payoff.

Loops and Rewatch Logic

1 min
  • A seamless loop (the end flows back into the start) raises watch time. It is harder and more time-consuming to edit, so make it when the edit allows.
  • A video with a clear, satisfying ending that flows back to the start rewards rewatching. Combine this with a loop where the edit allows.
  • Rewatches and full completions matter more than likes for virality. A high rewatch rate pushes distribution (how far the platform shows the video) further, even when the like-to-view ratio (the share of viewers who press like) is modest.
  • A shocking ending near the end encourages rewatches.

Retention and Distribution Logic

1 min
  • TikTok shows a video to a small test audience first. It only expands distribution if watch time passes the threshold (the minimum mark). Watch time and full completion are the key performance signals (numbers that show how well a video does).
  • Watch time strongly affects reach (the number of people who see the video) and sales. High watch time keeps the video in circulation and gives viewers a reason to watch again.
  • Retention rate is treated as the most important signal for pushing a video.
  • Re-watches and full completions matter more than likes for virality.
  • Reusing the same intro and video structure over and over lets viewers recognize the content, and views drop over time. In the case described, repeated intros eventually caused an account to get almost no views.

Script and Story Formats

1 min

Story Script Template (Comment-Driving)

1 min 
Template: write a short-video story script that makes viewers comment at the end. Structure it in this order:

1. HOOK (face to camera, intense eye contact): one bold opening line that challenges the advice most people around you give.
2. CONFLICT: what you saw around you that made you doubt the common advice, and what happened when you shared your own goal.
3. TURNING POINT: the decision you made and how you began learning the skill.
4. CALL TO ACTION: ask viewers to comment a specific keyword, such as their country code, and promise to send them a free guide.

Use my story: [describe your background, location, starting situation, what you did and what changed]. Keep the tone emotional but humble, avoid bragging and close with the message that the goal is possible.

Write two completely different versions:
Version 1: emotional, personal 'mini series' style that emphasises pain, rebellion and redemption and builds emotional connection.
Version 2: bold, punchy, controversial 'debate starter' style that is faster paced, opinionated and built to trigger comments and shares, with a hook that challenges a common income benchmark.
  • Story-based scripts that end with a comment request get more replies when the call to action (a request to do something, here to comment) asks for one simple keyword and promises something specific in return. The hook and conflict should create tension first, so viewers want to see the outcome.

Debate-Starter Hook Formula

1 min

Pattern: name a common income figure that people use to judge what is realistic. Reject it as the limit of someone's goals, and set it against a dream worth protecting. It works because it creates immediate tension. It gives the viewer a position to agree or disagree with, which drives comments and shares.

Examples in this pattern:

  • "Don't let a $35K paycheck decide how big your store is allowed to grow."
  • "Stop taking money advice from someone who has never built anything they own."

Story and Scenario Videos

1 min
  • Story-based or scenario videos (short acted situations) can drive virality for a product. Viewers respond to relatable situations more than to plain product demonstrations.
  • Products applied to the face require the creator to show the face or part of it. This limits who can film the product comfortably. Check this first before choosing a face-care product for a faceless (no face shown) or privacy-focused account.

Talking-Head Videos

1 min
  • For talking-head videos (a person speaking to the camera), prop the phone up and film as if you were on a FaceTime call with a friend. Formats that suit this approach include a day-in-the-life clip, a founder routine, or answers to frequently asked product questions.
  • Talk-heavy or dialogue-driven videos often get views but convert less than videos that show the product clearly. Use a talking-to-camera opening only when it leads into a demonstration.

Scenario and Demonstration Examples

1 min
  • For a product meant for a pet or a baby, a stuffed animal or a doll can stand in for the real subject in short demonstration videos. This keeps the demo realistic while avoiding the need for a live child or animal.
  • Products applied to the face require the creator to show the face or part of it. Account for this before choosing a face-care product for a faceless or privacy-focused account.

Product-Specific Creative Angles

1 min
  • Look for creative advantages in each product: ASMR-style sound, recreating a sound better than the original, cool transitions, a clear wow moment, and relevant trends. Use these to stand out from similar videos.
  • Colorful, satisfying visuals with ASMR-style sound performed well on TikTok organically, especially with younger audiences.
  • Products whose use is visible through hands-on action, such as the phone case knocked, clicked and removed, can carry the video without speech or on-screen sales text.
  • A viral beauty demo applied lashes to one eye while the other stayed bare. This gave a side-by-side comparison that holds attention.
  • A simple, visually styled product can go viral through a trending sound used at the right time and an aesthetically pleasing background.

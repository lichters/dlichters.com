Instagram reels and trial reels

Section 11.19 · 19 of 22 in this chapter · 13 min read

In short

  • Post Reels consistently. New accounts can show zero views for weeks, so do not quit.
  • Remove watermarks (logos stamped on a video) before posting. Watermarked videos get shown to fewer people.
  • Reply to comments in the first few days after you post.
  • Trial Reels are shown only to people who do not follow you. Before reposting the same video, uncheck the cross-post boxes.
  • Keep Reels under 3 minutes, upload in the highest quality, and use only content you made or have rights to.

Account setup and first weeks

1 min
  • Create an Instagram account. A business account (a free account type that gives statistics) is ideal.
  • Reels (short vertical videos) are organic, meaning free to post and not paid ads.
  • A business account gives you analytics (statistics on your posts). It also lets you boost (pay to promote) posts.
  • A personal account is not required.
  • New accounts can show zero views for days or weeks before a video takes off.
  • Keep posting. Do not abandon the account.
  • A watermark (a logo or text stamped on the video) lowers the reach of only the one video it is on, not the whole account.
  • Growth on any platform depends on regular video posting.
  • Post Reels consistently.
  • When you start, follow accounts similar to yours. See what they post and find ideas.
  • Use the plus icon next to a followed account to find more suggested accounts.

Reach expectations and platform value

1 min
  • Reels can get more views than TikTok on new accounts.
  • In the owner's experience, TikTok converted better. "Conversion" means how many viewers buy.
  • A guess: the emotional content connects more with TikTok viewers.
  • Instagram Reels helps build your brand (how people recognize and trust you).
  • It also reaches buyers who need to see credibility (proof you are real and trustworthy) before they buy.
  • An Instagram follower is valued more than an equal TikTok follower.
  • For problem-solving products, Instagram may bring more sales.
  • Going viral (spreading very widely) is about as easy on Instagram with the same concept.
  • Follower counts are not the same between platforms. Compare reach instead. Reach comparison:
TikTok followers Approximate Instagram followers (similar reach) Source
about 30,000 5,000 to 10,000
100,000 15,000 to 20,000

Cross-posting TikTok videos to Reels

1 min

"Cross-posting" means posting the same video on another app.

  • Instagram can take four to five videos per day.
  • Reposting your existing TikTok videos there can bring views.
  • The example routine uses three per day. Each day's TikTok videos are reposted on Instagram the next day, keeping the same daily count.
  • The two numbers do not conflict. Four to five is the reported daily capacity. Three is the example routine.
  • Videos that did not go viral on TikTok sometimes perform on Instagram.
  • Cross-posting does not fix a weak angle (the main idea of the video).
  • In one case, a late cross-post reached only about 500 followers and did not convert sales.
  • Repurpose (reuse) strong TikTok videos as Reels.
  • A clip re-uploaded to a personal account with a trending sound set to zero volume was not restricted in the reported case. This held even after hundreds of uploads.
  • Restriction risk is still possible. Check account notices after each new upload type.

Procedure: moving a TikTok video to Reels with a trending sound

1 min

A "trending sound" is a popular audio clip many users pick right now.

  1. Download the TikTok video without watermark using SnapTik (a TikTok downloader tool).
  2. Upload the file to Instagram Reels.
  3. Open Instagram's audio library (its collection of sounds) and choose a trending sound.
  4. Add that sound and lower its volume to 0.
  5. Keep the video's own audio (the original TikTok sound) audible.
  6. The trending sound only signals the format to Instagram. It does not need to match the TikTok audio.

Note on conflicting audio advice: The detailed procedure keeps the original TikTok audio audible under a muted trending sound. The shorter routine says to keep the video unchanged but replace the original audio with a trending Instagram sound. Use the detailed procedure when the original audio matters to the video. The sources do not say which applies in other cases.

Caption and reply prompt

1 min
  • For Reels, write a short caption (the text under the post) with trending hashtags (words starting with # that group posts by topic).
  • End the caption with a prompt (a request that invites a reply). For example, ask viewers to comment a number or post a funny answer.
  • Contest prompts such as "funniest comment wins" bring many jokes unrelated to buying.
  • Judge comments by quality, not count.

Downloading and removing watermarks

1 min
  • To reuse your own TikTok videos, save them with a TikTok downloader such as SnapTik. Then upload the files to Instagram.
  • This avoids re-filming (shooting the same content again).
  • Remove the TikTok watermark before posting to Reels. Watermarked videos get suppressed (shown to fewer people).
  • In one test, a watermarked repost got almost no views.
  • The same video without the watermark reached a few thousand views. Other posts reached a few hundred.
  • Third-party watermarks lower reel performance.
  • This includes TikTok watermarks and a brand logo in a corner. Remove them before uploading.
  • Posting content you did not create, or without the rights (permission to use it), is a serious warning sign for Instagram.
  • If Instagram suspects this, the account can be taken down immediately.

Upload quality and format

1 min
  • Upload videos in the highest resolution available. Resolution means how sharp the picture is.
  • Instagram can compress (shrink) uploaded files. The Edits app is recommended for this.
  • InShot is a phone video editor app that you can use to export reels.
  • Export at 1080 resolution (full HD sharpness) where possible.
  • Instagram favors high-quality video. Low-quality video gets less distribution, meaning fewer people see it.
  • Film and edit with quality in mind.
  • Reels longer than 3 minutes hurt reel performance.
  • Reels below that limit can still work.
  • Test several formats before you know what works for your page. Try a reel of about 2.5 minutes, shorter reels, and carousel posts (several photos you swipe through).
  • Let the results decide which formats you keep.
  • When you publish a carousel, add a song.
  • Carousels with music can also appear in the Reels tab. This gives the carousel an extra way to be found.

Edits app

1 min

"Edits" is Instagram's free video editing app.

Recording and editing

1 min
  • Record in Edits only for footage you can easily reshoot (shoot again).
  • For time-sensitive or one-time shots, film with the phone's normal camera. A long in-app recording can be lost if the phone overheats or crashes.
  • The Edits camera has a green screen (it swaps the background for another image or video), a timer, and speed settings. It also has the usual camera controls.
  • The timeline (the editing strip) has these layers: Original audio, video clips, Text, Captions, Overlay, Sound FX, and Cutout. Each one gets its own track.
  • Each track can be turned on or off to preview the video without that element.
  • To trim (cut the start or end), drag the clip edges.
  • Use slip to shift the visible part of a clip without changing its length.
  • Overlay adds a second clip on top of the main video, like picture-in-picture (a small video inside the big one).
  • Add an overlay from the Overlay option on the timeline.
  • Filters (color styles) are the same as Instagram Story filters: None, Simple, Boost, Oslo, Paris, and Jakarta.
  • "Apply filter to all clips" applies the selected filter to every clip in the project.
  • Edits has AI tools that restyle or reimagine a video.
  • An "animate" AI feature was mentioned as newly released. Its location may have changed, so check the current menu before planning around it.
  • The Inspiration tab is a Reels-style feed. It mixes content popular with your followers and audio that is trending now.
  • Inspiration has no like, comment, follow, or send buttons. Use it only for ideas.

Export and sharing

1 min
  1. Finish the edit on the timeline.
  2. Tap Export (save the finished edit as a file).
  3. Keep the app open and the screen unlocked until the export finishes.
  4. The exported video is saved to your camera roll (your phone's photo and video gallery) automatically.
  5. Tap Share to Instagram and choose where to share the video next.

Edits watermark and share button

1 min
  • Videos shared directly from Edits to Instagram, instead of uploaded from the camera roll, carry a glowing "made with edits" watermark.
  • This is claimed to help distribution (how far a video spreads). No hard evidence was given. Treat it as a hypothesis (an idea to test).
  • Compare the reach of Edits-shared videos with camera-roll uploads before relying on the claim.
  • Instagram tends to push its newest in-app features. Adopting a new feature early can give a video extra distribution.
  • The boost from posting through Edits is not proven.
  • Videos shared from Edits are optimized for high-quality playback on Instagram.
  • The direct share button with the glowing "made with edits" icon is being phased out. It may already be gone on some accounts. Use it while it is still offered.

Trial Reels: principle

1 min
  • Trial Reels show a short video only to non-followers (people who do not follow you).
  • A Trial Reel stays off your profile grid (the photo grid on your profile page) at first. Your followers never see it there.
  • Use Trial Reels to test content styles outside your usual niche (topic) without risking your main audience.
  • According to the source, a Trial Reel is shown 100% to non-followers and 0% to existing followers.
  • So every upload reaches people who have never seen the account.
  • Brands that struggle to reach non-followers can use this to get a guaranteed share of new viewers.
  • Re-uploading the same proven video as a new Trial Reel gives it a fresh non-follower audience each time.
  • The main feed does not show that the same video was posted repeatedly.
  • Some creators repost a top video up to 10 times per day.
  • A Trial Reel can stay in trial and never be shared to the profile. Sharing is optional.
  • Share it to the main feed later only if it performs well.
  • A new feature usually gets an early visibility advantage (extra reach).
  • This boost is not guaranteed to last, because new feature pushes come and go.

Trial Reels: upload steps

1 min
  1. Open the new reel upload flow and select the video.
  2. Add the caption and usual settings: tags, location, audio, and an AI label if needed.
  3. Under Audience, select Trial.
  4. Leave the option to automatically share the reel to everyone in trial settings unchecked.
  5. Under Post to, uncheck the cross-post options for Threads and Facebook, and any other destination listed there.
  6. When the "Stop sharing on Threads?" pop-up appears, choose "Don't share this reel". This affects only this reel, not all reels.
  7. Tap Save draft or Share.

Trial Reels: settings

1 min
  • Audience > Trial: The reel is shown only to non-followers at first and does not appear on the profile.
  • If it performs well, the reel can be shared to everyone later.
  • "Share to everyone" checkbox (automatic sharing): Keep it unchecked when you post the same video repeatedly.
  • Otherwise, the reel is automatically added to your profile when it performs well.
  • Post to: The Threads (Public), Facebook (Public), and Your story options sit next to the account name.
  • Any destination left checked receives the same Trial Reel with every upload. Uncheck them for repeated posts.
  • Add AI Label: Instagram requires a label on certain realistic content made with AI (artificial intelligence).
  • Set the label during upload when it applies.
  • "Stop sharing on Threads?" prompt: Choose "Don't share this reel" to stop cross-posting for that one upload only.
  • Avoid "Stop sharing all reels." It applies to every reel on the account.

Trial Reels: mistakes to avoid

1 min
  • Leaving the Threads and Facebook boxes checked means every repeat upload also goes to those accounts.
  • Uncheck them, or choose "Don't share this reel," before publishing.
  • Turning on automatic sharing to the profile while reposting the same video makes the main feed show repeated copies.
  • Keep that option off for repeat uploads.

Trial Reels: starter routine

1 min
  1. List your top-performing videos from the main feed.
  2. Re-upload one of them as a Trial Reel each day, starting with one per day.
  3. Leave the reel in trial. Do not share it to the profile unless its insights beat your typical reel.
  4. "Insights" means the statistics for one post. Check the Trial Reel insights after several days. Then compare them with your typical reel.

Note on upload frequency: some creators repost a top video up to 10 times per day. The starter routine begins with one per day.

Engagement drivers

1 min

Early replies and conversation

1 min
  • Replying to comments in the first few days after a reel is posted is one of the strongest drivers of reel distribution on Instagram.
  • Follow-up replies help further.
  • Steps to engage with a new reel in its first days:
    1. Right after posting, open the notifications section.
    2. Filter the notifications by comments.
    3. Reply to every comment you can.
    4. When a commenter responds with another question, keep answering so the dialogue (back-and-forth conversation) stays open.
    5. Keep addressing new comments throughout the first few days.
  • The core Instagram recommendations combine early engagement with timing and quality. Early engagement includes replies to comments and DMs (direct messages, private messages).

Timing and consistency

1 min
  • After finding the hour when your audience is most active, post at that same time or very close to it on every upload.
  • Consistency in timing is part of the recommendation.

Commenting on other creators

1 min
  • Commenting on other creators' content is rewarded by the algorithm (Instagram's system that chooses what to show people).
  • A relevant or funny comment on a larger account's post can send significant traffic to your own page.

Engagement groups and trends

1 min
  • Engagement groups (creators who support each other's content) are a legitimate way to get early engagement.
  • They do not replace genuine engagement with your audience.
  • Trending audio and pop culture formats are worth using when they fit your brand.
  • They often drive short-term viral reach.
  • Do not let trends take over the whole posting plan.

"More like this" and "less like this"

1 min
  • On Instagram Reels, tap "more like this" on a video that matches what you want more of.
  • Or tap "less like this" on a video you don't want.
  • Each tap adjusts your personal algorithm.
  • The source describes these buttons as coming soon. Check whether they appear in your app before planning around them.

Threads and Facebook cross-posting

1 min
  • Turn on the cross-posting option for Threads (Meta's text app) and Facebook Reels inside Instagram before publishing.
  • The same post then goes to those platforms as well.
  • Publish the same video with the same caption on all platforms at the same time.
  • Budget no more than 10 minutes for this upload work.
  • Enable the Threads option on Instagram. Then every reel, carousel, or photo you post is automatically shared to Threads too.
  • The Threads audience is different from the Instagram audience. Posting there adds reach and a place for two-way conversation.
  • Treat Threads as an additional channel, not a copy-only destination.
  • Threads-to-Reels growth checklist. The title says nine strategies, but six are listed here:
    1. Post regularly; cross-posting is okay.
    2. Engage like it is a conversation.
    3. Comment on other people's content.
    4. Jump on trending audio and pop culture.
    5. Use niche-relevant hashtags.
    6. Experiment with formats.
  • These points apply to both Threads and Reels.

Instagram growth versus sales

  • A late cross-post reached about 500 followers and did not convert sales.
  • Cross-posting does not solve a weak angle.
  • Instagram Reels can get more views than TikTok. But in the owner's experience, TikTok converted better.
  • Reels helps with brand building and with buyers who need credibility first.
  • For problem-solving products, Instagram may bring more sales.

Testing structures, budgets and automated rules

Section 11.17 · 17 of 22 in this chapter · 7 min read

In short

  • Set the budget to Daily, never Lifetime.
  • Use ABO when each ad needs its own fixed share of money. Use CBO when one budget should cover the whole test.
  • Judge results over seven days, not one day.
  • Reset your account only when costs stay far too high, never between every test.
  • The budget trick gets an answer sooner. It does not make ads better.
This section has 10 parts. Tap one to open it.

Campaign budget type

1 min
  • Set the TikTok ad budget type to Daily, not Lifetime. A daily budget is an amount spent each day. A lifetime budget is one total amount spread over a set period. Lifetime budgets cause spending delays and inconsistent algorithm behavior (the platform's automatic system that decides who sees your ad), especially in high-competition periods such as Q4 (October to December, when many advertisers bid for buyers).

Campaign structure: ABO versus CBO

1 min

Two budget structures are used. They differ in where the budget is set, not in what they measure.

An ad group (also called an ad set) holds ads with their own audience and budget. A campaign is the top folder that holds ad groups.

Structure Where the budget is set Values in the sources ids
ABO (ad group budget) Each ad group gets a fixed amount $20 per ad group in a 7-15 video test; $20 per ad set on a product with a $10 margin ,
CBO (campaign budget) One budget at campaign level, divided by TikTok across the ad groups $50 per day split across five ads or ad groups; about 50 pounds or 50 Euro per day split across five ads ,,
  • ABO gives each ad set a fixed budget. CBO sets one budget for the whole campaign, and TikTok divides it across the ad sets. CBO is the recommended choice for the five-ad test.
  • The split follows the test design. Use ABO when each video needs a fixed, equal share of spend (7-15 videos at $20 each). Use CBO when one campaign tests five ads and TikTok should divide the total.
  • The five-ad CBO structure is one campaign with five ads. Use a $50 daily budget (about 50 pounds or 50 Euro) split between the five ads. Keep the same campaign running consistently. (merged: both ids describe the same structure)

Setting up CBO

  1. Go to the campaign level, turn on Campaign budget optimization (the setting that lets TikTok split one budget across ad groups), and set the budget. The example uses a campaign budget of $50 (50 Swiss francs in the Swiss account), spread across all ad sets.
  2. After the campaign is published, a duplicate button appears on the ad group. Click it four times to create five ad groups in total.

Budget trick: low campaign cap, high ad-set budgets

1 min

Procedure (use with the warning below)

  1. Create a TikTok Ads campaign and set a campaign budget limit (for example, $100 for daily spend).
  2. Inside that campaign, create ad sets and set each ad set's budget to $2000-$5000.
  3. The algorithm believes each ad set has more budget than the campaign allows, which forces faster spend.
  4. The campaign-level cap ensures you never exceed your intended daily spend.
  5. To increase overall spend, gradually raise the campaign-level limit in steps (for example, +$50 at a time).

What the trick does and does not do

  • The budget trick compresses the timeline for deciding whether a product is viable (worth selling). It does not improve results or ROAS (return on ad spend: sales money divided by ad money). Fast spending answers the question sooner. It does not change the outcome.
  • The result is the same whether you spend $500 gradually over five days or in rapid bursts. Only the timeline changes.

Warm-up spend

1 min
  • TikTok ad accounts often need a daily budget of around 50 pounds or dollars to warm up the pixel (a small tracking code on your store that reports sales back to the ad platform).
  • Turning the campaign off early can damage its learning. Keep the same budget running for several days without increasing or decreasing it.

Creative testing

2 min

A creative is the video or image in an ad. Ad copy is the written text that goes with it.

Keep everything except the video identical

  • When testing several creatives, keep the offer, ad copy and audience identical across all ads, so that only the video differs. Otherwise a difference in results cannot be traced to the creative.

Creative-led structure (newer approach)

  • With a fully broad audience (targeting wide open, with no interest filters), the platform finds a pocket of buyers for each creative. So the creative is the variable to test. A variable is the one thing you change.
  • The older setup used five ad sets, each with three to five videos and two to three ad copies, duplicated five times with the same creatives. It spread spend across shared assets (the videos and text pieces), so it did not show which single creative was the winner.
  • The newer setup uses 10 ad sets with one unique video each on a fully broad audience.
  • The reset procedure that uses this setup is under "Resetting accounts and pixels".

Video and ad-copy test (ABO)

  1. Start with 7 to 15 different videos.
  2. Create one campaign using ad group budgets (ABO, a budget set at ad group level).
  3. Set the budget at $20 per ad group.
  4. Use only the TikTok placement (the place where your ad shows).
  5. Set age to 25+ (adjust by product and gender).
  6. Use no interest targeting: completely broad targeting, with no interest filters.
  7. Place one different video in each ad group.
  8. Use an ad copy built from a short customer-language phrase (four or five words) naming the main benefit, and test several of them.

Product test with a budget limit

  1. Choose 4 to 5 interests (topics people like or follow, used to find buyers) that match the product. Narrow the targeting further when the product is niche (a small, specialized market).
  2. Set about $20 per ad set on a product with a $10 profit margin (money left per sale after costs).
  3. When the spend reaches twice the cost per acquisition (cost to get one buyer) without a profitable result, switch that interest off and cycle it out (drop it and try another).
  4. Spend about $200 to $300 per product with a well-made creative. If nothing works by then, the product is unlikely to take off.
  5. If the product still shows promise, try new creatives or a different marketing angle (the main reason a buyer should buy).
  6. Scale horizontally (add more ad sets or campaigns) or vertically (raise the budget on what works) once the test works.

Evaluation window and daily noise

1 min
  • Judge TikTok ad performance over a seven-day period rather than day by day. The evaluation window is the time you judge results over. Daily results on low budgets fluctuate and can average out. Short-term swings do not necessarily mean a fault or a glitch.
  • Example: a test with a $50 or 50 Euro daily budget produced five to six sales on the first day, with seven add-to-carts (a shopper putting an item in their cart) and five checkouts (shoppers starting to pay). That is about 71% from add-to-cart to checkout. On the following day it produced zero sales despite four or five checkouts and add-to-carts. This was put down to random daily variation.

Day-of-week effects

1 min
  • TikTok performance varies by day of the week. Monday-Wednesday typically underperform, and Thursday-Sunday perform better. Adjust expectations and automation rules accordingly.

Automated rules

1 min

Automated rules are settings that act on your ads by themselves.

Setting up rules in Ads Manager

  1. Go to Automated Rules in TikTok Ads Manager (the tool where you run ads), top right.
  2. Select "Manage Rules" or "Create New Rule."
  3. For beginners, use two pre-built rule types (ready-made templates):
    • (a) Control Budget: set a threshold (for example, "total cost in last 3 days > $X") and an action (turn off campaign or notify).
    • (b) Enhance Performance: if CTR (click-through rate: the share of viewers who click) is above 2-3% AND CPC (cost per click) is below $1, increase the daily budget by $50.
  4. Learn the interface before deploying, as wrong settings can disable profitable campaigns.

Kill rules based on break-even

Kill rules stop an ad automatically. Break-even is the point where ads stop losing money.

  1. Rule 1: If spend equals 2x break-even cost per purchase with zero sales, pause immediately.
  2. Rule 2: If spend exceeds $80 and ROAS falls below break-even ROAS, pause the ad set.

Hour-level waste and bidding

1 min
  • TikTok can spend budget in low-converting hours (hours when few viewers buy). Facebook's delivery algorithm (the system that picks when and whom to show ads) works differently. Check spend by hour when TikTok campaigns run.
  • Manual bidding (you set the price you pay yourself) is not needed on TikTok in most cases. The exception is dayparting (running ads only at chosen hours) blasts, which come later on.

Resetting accounts and pixels

1 min

A reset means starting again with a new ad account and a new pixel.

When a reset is justified

  • A reset to a new ad account with a new pixel is a fast fix when CPM (cost per 1,000 views) and CPC stay far outside benchmarks (normal ranges for your market). A new account showed better CPM and CPC after only $6.60 in spend.
  • This conflicts with the rule not to create a new account for each test. Reset only when CPM and CPC stay far outside benchmarks. Do not reset as routine practice between tests.

Reset procedure

  1. Create a new ad account with a new pixel.
  2. Launch 10 ad sets, each with one unique video (or one unique image) and a fully broad audience.
  3. Let them spend, then switch off the underperforming ad sets.
  4. When a winning creative appears, move it into its own campaign and duplicate that same creative into several ad sets.

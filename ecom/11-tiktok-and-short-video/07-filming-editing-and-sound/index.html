Filming, editing and sound

Section 11.7 · 7 of 22 in this chapter · 9 min read

In short

  • Edit on your phone, not in TikTok. Always keep the original file.
  • Use trending sounds early, while they are still rising, and only if they fit the product.
  • For paid ads, use only TikTok's commercial sound library, because regular music can cause copyright problems.
  • Most TikTok sellers present products poorly, so a clean, careful video can win views.
This section has 13 parts. Tap one to open it.

Filming Practice and Locations

1 min
  • Leave room around the subject when you frame the shot. Tight framing limits zoom (moving closer), pan (sliding the camera sideways) and transition (a change from one shot to the next) options.
  • Film in varied real places, such as a car, outdoors or while out and about. These look more engaging than the same indoor setting, such as a dining table or living room. Avoid glare (harsh reflected light).
  • Use sound-led footage where it fits. ASMR (videos built around soft, satisfying sounds) can make a bottle opener product more engaging. An example is the sound of opening a soda bottle.

Phone-First Workflow

1 min
  • Draft and edit on the phone. Desktop (a computer) cannot reliably add text, sounds or some editing features, so keep the phone as the main device for all TikTok work.
  • Do not edit in the TikTok studio or in-app editor. The original file is lost and cannot be reused in another edit. Use TikTok only for sounds, hashtags, text and text-to-speech (a computer voice that reads your words aloud).
  • Follow this production sequence:
    1. Record the video on the phone in 4K at 60 fps (4K is a very sharp picture; fps means frames per second, or how smooth motion looks). 1080p is acceptable.
    2. Edit the video in an external app such as Splice, keeping the original file.
    3. Export the finished video, which means saving it as a final file.
    4. Upload it to TikTok.
    5. In TikTok, add on-screen text, text-to-speech, music, filters and hashtags.
    6. Save as a draft or post, and keep several drafts queued for the upcoming days.
  • To add music, tap upload, pick the video, then tap the music icon at the top center of the screen and search for a sound. Trending sounds (popular right now) and copyright-cleared sounds (legal to use) are only available on a personal account.
  • Many TikTok sellers do products poorly. Cleaner editing, better hooks (the first seconds that grab attention) and removal of dull moments can win views over competitors. This is an opportunity for a clean, careful creator.

Premiere Pro

1 min
  • For 9:16 video (a tall, phone-shaped frame), keep a separate vertical sequence (a timeline with a tall frame) or workspace. Do not cut vertical clips in a landscape (wide) sequence. The sequence takes its frame ratio from the first clip, so set the ratio before adding footage.
  • Press C to switch to the Razor tool and cut a clip at the point you click. This is a fast way to remove dead time (empty or boring moments) from raw footage.

CapCut

1 min
  • Combine the recorded footage with the A-roll audio (the main voice or sound recorded with the footage). Create the final short video from the phone.

Splice

1 min
  • Use Splice to edit videos before upload.
  • Use Splice to add custom sound effects after the video edit is finished. Layer (stack) recorded sounds over the cut.

Keyframes and Transitions

1 min
  • A keyframe (a marker that saves a setting at one point in time) stores a value such as scale (size) or position. The editor creates the change between two keyframes. Movement only appears when at least two keyframes with different values are set.
  • Make zooms and moves long enough to show the change in speed and blur (smearing from fast motion) before judging the result. Very short transitions hide easing (the smooth speed-up and slow-down) and motion blur, so the effect looks weak or goes unnoticed.
  • In CapCut, set the transition curve between keyframes to a preset ease-in, ease-out or bounce option, or shape it in a graph. Start with the presets, because a hand-shaped bell-style ease curve is harder to get right.
  • Use transitions and trend formats that are current, such as a soft music intro that cuts into a fast track with a transition. Spend time inside the platform each day to see what is trending.

Vertical Framing and Reframing

1 min
  • A TikTok-style vertical ad uses a frame of 1080 x 1920 pixels (the dots that make up the picture), with a 9:16 aspect ratio (the shape of width to height).
  • After switching to a vertical frame, footage downloaded from TikTok can look smaller in the frame, because TikTok has compressed it (shrunk the file size). Scale the clip back up to fill the frame.
  • In CapCut's main menu, the Ratio option sets the project frame. For example, 1:1 is a square Instagram-style ad. After changing the ratio, resize the footage so it fills the new timeline frame.
  • Reframe horizontal footage (wide, side to side) for a vertical or square ad:
    1. Import the horizontal clip and place it on the timeline.
    2. Scale the clip up until the subject fills the vertical or square frame.
    3. Wait until the subject is clearly visible, then add a keyframe on position.
    4. Move the position keyframe so the frame follows the subject to the next keyframe.

TikTok-Style Text

1 min
  • In Premiere Pro, set the text color to black, add a white background box, add padding (empty space) around the text and round the corners of the box.
  • Add TikTok-style text in CapCut:
    1. Tap Text and add your text.
    2. Open Style and tap Canvas to create an instant background.
    3. Set the background to white and the text to black.
    4. Make the text bold.
    5. Pinch the text to resize it, and adjust the background height and width.
    6. Animate the text, for example with a bounce-in (the text pops in with a bouncing motion), if needed.

Sound Design and ASMR

1 min
  • Ambient ASMR sound (soft background sound), aesthetic backgrounds and colorful visuals keep viewers engaged. They help videos become memorable. Satisfying sounds, such as unwrapping or peeling, were cited as especially effective.
  • Recording your own sound effects can beat stock audio (ready-made sounds from a library). For example, record a stretching or pulling sound for a product. Original satisfying sounds were reported to perform better than generic sound effects.
  • Record your own satisfying sounds, for example a product being stretched or pulled. Place them on the edit to give the video original audio.
  • Follow this sound production procedure for product videos:
    1. Record the product's natural sound with the microphone as close as possible while filming.
    2. If the natural sound does not come out as wanted, record it again separately, for example on a voice memo.
    3. Recreate the sound after the video is done, and import it into Splice.
    4. Align the recording to the exact clip and frame where the sound happens.
    5. Balance the levels (the volume of each sound). Keep the product sound audible over the background music. For ASMR-focused content, lower the music and raise the original audio, or the other way around, depending on the goal.
  • Splice is used to import a separately recorded sound and align it to the matching frame of the video clip. This way, a re-created product sound fits the action.
  • A video has two sound layers to balance: the background music or trending audio, and the product's own sound. In ASMR-style videos, the product sound should stay clearly audible and not be drowned out by music.

Trending Sounds

2 min
  • Trending sounds can drive large organic reach (free, unpaid views). Videos using a rising sound are shown to people searching that sound. Find trending sounds early by opening a trending song and reviewing its top posts. These can show product videos already using it.
  • Hop on a new trending sound quickly. A sound and its hashtag can grow from thousands of users to hundreds of thousands or millions in a short time. Early adoption gives the best chance of being shown first in search.
  • An early video can rank near the top of the sound's page. As more users adopt the sound, that video is recommended first. The owner reported one video reaching 20 to 30 million views this way.
  • Hop onto a trending sound or hashtag early, while it is still growing.
  • Use trending sounds from the For You page (TikTok's main feed) rather than the viral tab (the page for the most viral content). Sounds that are already viral are often used by many accounts. Use a sound from the For You page only if it fits the video and the product.
  • Sounds taken from the For You page are often better than trending chart sounds, which saturate (fill up with copies) quickly. Choose sounds that are still in use but not yet overused.
  • Adopt a trending sound early, before many product accounts use it. A format that is already common among sellers gets less reach. Only use the sound if its meaning fits the product.
  • Trending songs tend to rise quickly and fade within days. If you plan to use a trending sound, post while it is still climbing, not after it has peaked.
  • Trending audio helps videos get found. Using a sound that is already trending places your video among the first ones shown for that audio. Dialogue or theme music from a trending series can be used, with the lines shown as on-screen text.
  • Organic TikTok growth depends on riding sounds before they peak. Jump onto a trending sound early, so viewers who click the sound also watch the other videos in that trend.
  • Cutting clips to the beat of the audio is a meaningful editing technique on TikTok, since sound is on by default. This differs from Facebook, where most viewers watch muted (with the sound off).
  • Trending sounds are a key factor in going viral. Check which sounds are currently trending. Use them in your own videos, provided the sound matches the video and is used while it is still popular.
  • Choose music whose beats or pauses line up with your transitions, and that matches the product and the timing of the video. A trending sound that has no relation to the product is unlikely to help.

Music Rules and Copyright

1 min
  • When a creator video runs as a TikTok paid ad, use only audio from TikTok's commercial soundtrack library (songs licensed for ads). Regular artist music in paid ads can lead to copyright problems (legal ownership claims).
  • For a video that will run as an ad, download royalty-free sounds (sounds you can use without paying each time) from a free library. Add them in Splice, then upload.
  • If a trending sound is copyrighted, find a bootleg (an unofficial copy) or re-uploaded version that sounds nearly identical. Use that instead. Sounds that complement the product also help the video.

Text-to-Speech

1 min
  • Hook videos (the opening seconds that grab attention) can be voiced with TikTok's built-in text-to-speech, using the "Jesse" voice. This keeps the hook from sounding flat.
  • Use the same voice across hook variations, so the format stays consistent for testing.
  • Add text-to-speech inside TikTok after uploading the exported video.

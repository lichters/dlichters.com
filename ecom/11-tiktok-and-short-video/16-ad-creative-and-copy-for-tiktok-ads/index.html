Ad creative and copy for TikTok ads

Section 11.16 · 16 of 22 in this chapter · 6 min read

In short

  • The video does about 90 percent of the work in a TikTok ad. The written words (copy) do only 10 percent.
  • TikTok ads need video. Still pictures do not work.
  • Make the ad look like normal TikTok posts, not a polished commercial.
  • Keep the text to one line. Longer text is hidden on phones.
  • Use recent videos for ads. Old viral videos get much less reach.

Ad Performance Weighting: Video Versus Copy

  • TikTok ad performance is about 90 percent decided by the video and 10 percent by the ad copy (the written words of the ad). The video carries the conversion weight, meaning how much it makes people buy.
  • TikTok requires video creatives (the ads themselves, shown as video). Images do not work on the platform.
  • People have already seen the product in the video within the first 3-5 seconds, so the copy's job is to close (get the click or the sale).

Offer and Copy Rules

  • Include the product offer or discount in TikTok ad copy in 9 out of 10 cases. The offer in the copy is the final trigger to click the shop button.
  • Keep TikTok ad copy to one line maximum. Longer text will not be visible on mobile.

Copy Strategies

The following template (a ready-made text pattern) is reproduced unchanged:

TikTok ad copy strategies (one line each):

**Offer-focused:** [Discount]% off, get yours today | [Discount]% off, limited time only | Buy one get one free, limited time only | Offer ending soon, shop now

**Problem-solution:** Say goodbye to [problem] | Tired of [pain point]? Here's the solution, get yours today.

**Question-based:** Struggling with [problem]? [Product name] is your answer.

**Meme/controversial:** Use punchy, pattern-breaking humor or provocative one-liners that stop scrolling.

Native Creative Style

  • TikTok ad videos should look like organic content (normal posts, not ads). "Native" means it fits in with the feed. The more native, fun and less salesy the video, the better the performance. Classic Facebook-style ad formats do not work on TikTok.
  • TikTok ads perform best with UGC (user-generated content, videos made by ordinary people) that already feels native to TikTok. Use different creatives for each platform.
  • Organic-looking, creator-style videos (made by everyday creators) were outperforming polished ads on TikTok Shop (TikTok's built-in store) in 2025. Use the top-selling videos as a reference for the format, pacing (speed of cuts) and hook (the first seconds that grab attention) of your own creatives.
  • GIF-style clips (short looping animations) and entertaining content often outperform static material (still images) in competitor ads on TikTok. Taglines (short catchy lines) that grab attention in the first seconds are worth noting and testing in your own creatives.
  • For digital products (downloads, not physical goods), one suggested approach is a hook showing someone with a pain point (a problem), followed by a transition to the product. This approach is not proven. Check the TikTok Creative Center (TikTok's free tool that shows ads that run) for competitor creatives.

Adapting Organic Posts and TikTok Shop Clips

  • Focus on organic growth (free reach from normal posts) first. Then promote the best-performing organic posts as ads. Ads performed poorly when the posts were not native to the platform.
  • When repurposing (reusing) an organic video for ads, adjust the text and add an offer with a call to action (CTA, the line that tells people what to do) at the end, such as an instruction to shop now. TikTok ads are judged on views and higher conversion.

Procedure: adapting a TikTok Shop video for a Meta ad (Meta is the company behind Facebook and Instagram)

  1. Download the video from the source tool only for study, then edit a new cut (a newly edited version) rather than running the original.
  2. Remove all TikTok Shop elements, such as the shop card, the product card and any shop-only buttons.
  3. Remove on-screen price overlays (prices printed on the video) that do not match your own selling price.
  4. Cut the video down, since the sample testimonial (a customer review) ran almost two minutes and most of it would not be needed on Meta.
  5. Keep the hook, the testimonial, the problem and solution, the benefits and the call to action.
  6. Add your own call to action and link to your store.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Do not run a TikTok Shop video on Meta unchanged. It still shows shop elements and a price that is not yours. Such mismatches confuse the viewer and can make the ad disconnect (feel unrelated) from your landing page (the page buyers reach after clicking).
  • Do not keep a video at close to two minutes when the hook and key claims fit in a much shorter cut. Long ads lose viewers before the call to action, so cut all filler and repeat lines.

Shop-badge example

  • Short vertical video ads (phone-height videos) for a seasonal one-size product can show a small TikTok Shop cart badge (a small cart icon) in one corner. This makes the ad look like it can be bought directly in the feed, which supports impulse purchases (quick buys made on a whim).

Static-to-Video Conversion

  • Once a static creative (a still-image ad) proves an offer, put that offer into a video ad. Video is more engaging but takes longer to produce.

Ad Length

  • Keep a TikTok ad under one minute. Short-form ads (short videos) for TikTok should be quicker and more punchy than longer feed ads. Aim for 30 to 45 seconds when the content allows.
  • The one-minute cap was the limit for Reels and Stories (short vertical video formats on Instagram and Facebook) at that time. Check current platform limits before relying on it.
  • A near-two-minute cut is too long when the hook and key claims fit in less time.
Format Target length Source
TikTok ad Under one minute; 30-45 seconds when content allows
Repurposed TikTok Shop testimonial for Meta Cut from almost two minutes to the hook, problem and solution, benefits and call to action

Sourcing Creatives

  • TikTok reduces the reach (how many people see it) of duplicate content, and the same logic applies to ads that reuse the same video. Fresh creatives are therefore needed for ads.
  • Use creatives uploaded recently, ideally within the last 7 days, that few competitors are using. A video with a good like rate (the share of viewers who press like) on PPAds (a tool that shows TikTok ad data) is a sign that it is getting strong engagement.
  • A video that has run for more than about 15 to 20 days, or a month, and has gone viral (spread very fast to many people) usually gets much less reach when reused in an ad, because TikTok has already analyzed it.
  • Example: a creator video posted three days earlier with about 23,000 to 30,000 views (not yet viral) was reused for ads and scaled successfully (spend was raised while results held), because TikTok had not yet picked it up.

Uploaded within the last 7 days, few competitors using it Preferred
About three days old, about 23,000-30,000 views, not yet viral Scaled successfully in the example

Creative Lifespan and Supply

  • Creatives on TikTok perform for 1-2 weeks before needing replacement. Plan for consistent creative supply (a steady flow of new ads) to maintain campaign performance (the results of your ad run).
  • Creative lifespan (how long an ad keeps working) on TikTok is about one week, sometimes up to two weeks.

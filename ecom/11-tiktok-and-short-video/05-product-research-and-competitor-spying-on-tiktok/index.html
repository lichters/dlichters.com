Product research and competitor spying on TikTok

Section 11.5 · 5 of 22 in this chapter · 7 min read

In short

  • Study what the top posts in your niche do, then copy their structure, not their content.
  • Judge a competitor by their latest video and the comments on it, not by their most-viewed video.
  • Use a separate account for product research, not your business account.
  • Save every viral video with its link, screenshot, and view count right away, because creators can delete them.
  • Use Sellercount and PPAds together to spy on TikTok ads, since each has a different library.
This section has 12 parts. Tap one to open it.

Finding Winning Hooks for Organic Content

1 min

Organic content means posts that are not paid ads. A hook is the opening moment that grabs attention. Your niche is the topic area of your product.

  1. Open TikTok's Creator Search, or search your niche keyword on TikTok.
  2. Look at posts with millions of views and note their intros, hooks, and how they grab attention.
  3. Filter (narrow down) results by most likes or views for a keyword, such as lamps, and study what the top posts do.
  4. Pull relevant niche hashtags (topic tags starting with #) and follow trending sounds (popular audio clips).
  5. Copy the structure, such as transitions (scene changes), hooks, or controversy (a claim that starts debate), not the exact content.

Creative Center and Video Ideas

1 min
  • The TikTok Creative Center is a tool for seeing what ads other brands run on TikTok.
  • The Creative Center has a video ideas section next to the search for top-performing ads (ads that get strong results) in a niche. Use it for filming ideas after you have found the winning hooks.

Checking Whether a Competitor Still Sells

1 min

A competitor is a seller of a similar product. Judge a competitor's TikTok product by its latest video, not its most-viewed one.

  1. Open the seller's most recent video, not the one with the most views.
  2. Read the comments in order of date and focus on those from the last week.
  3. Look for buying signals (signs that people are buying), such as "where can I buy this", "I just bought one", "I placed an order" or "I really want one".
  4. Look for complaints such as "where is my order" or "why hasn't my order shipped", which point to fulfillment problems (trouble getting orders delivered).
  5. Treat recent buying signals as evidence that the product is still selling, even if view counts are low.

Building a Research Feed on TikTok

1 min

A research feed is a stream of product posts that TikTok shows you. Use a separate account for product discovery, not your business account.

Procedure A (hashtag-based feed):

  1. Create a new TikTok account for research only.
  2. Search and follow pages for hashtags such as tiktokmademebuyit, Amazon finds, exposing dropshippers, Etsy finds and eBay finds.
  3. Like and comment on the content to show TikTok your interest.
  4. The For You page (TikTok's main feed of suggested videos) fills with similar product content over time, which turns the account into an organic research feed.

Procedure B (daily engagement feed):

  1. Create a new account that is separate from your business account.
  2. Like, share, comment on, and engage with (interact with) product videos every day.
  3. Keep doing this until the For You page of that account is filled with product content.
  4. Use this account to spot new trends, and keep it separate from your business account.

Spying on TikTok Ads

1 min

"Spying" here means looking at other sellers' ads. Sellercount and PPAds are spy tools for TikTok ads.

  • PPAds has a different library (a stored collection of ads) than Sellercount, so using both gives better coverage (finds more ads).
  • Use these filters in Sellercount or PPAds:
Filter Setting Reason Source
Post duration (how long the ad has been running) 2, then 3, then 4 weeks Many advertisers are moving from Facebook to TikTok
Engagement (how many people saw or reacted) 50k plus to 100k plus impressions Proof of concept (proof the idea works)
  • An ad with 50k to 200k plus impressions (times the ad was shown) suggests the advertiser has been spending roughly 500 to 1,000 dollars, which usually means the product is profitable (makes more money than it costs) for them.
  • Products with no clear profit signal should not be assumed profitable.

Finding Products That Sell Without an Audience

1 min
  • The Low-Follower Sales Videos filter shows videos that sell even though the creator has few followers.
  • A product that sells this way does not depend on an existing audience (people who already follow the brand), which makes it a stronger test candidate (a product worth testing first).

TikTok Shop and Video Reuse

1 min

TikTok Shop is TikTok's built-in store.

  • For a TikTok Shop video, the Magic AI search looks for Facebook ads that reuse (repurpose) the same video, sorted by thumbnail similarity (how alike the preview image looks).
  • If no similar ads appear, it is unlikely that anyone has reused the video in paid ads.
  • The Videos & Ads view lets you download all listed videos with one button.
  • Use this to save a whole competitor video set at once.

Reach Signals

1 min
  • TikTok open targeting (ads shown to a broad audience with few narrow settings) reaches roughly 500 million to 800 million people, sometimes up to about 1 billion people.
  • Because of this reach, high ad impression counts for a product are a positive sign for a seller.

Facebook Ad Library Cross-Checks

1 min

The Facebook Ad Library is Meta's public list of running ads.

When to use both platforms:

  • The Facebook Ad Library is not the only source of product video inspiration (ideas for product videos). TikTok searches can find better-performing videos for the same product.
  • Use both to find strong creatives (ad visuals and copy that work).

When to skip the Facebook checks:

Check TikTok product lists Replacement rule Source
Page creation date Skip None
Ad library scaling stages (how far an advertiser has grown their ads) Skip None
Ads created within the last 14 days with strong likes and plays (video views) Use Treat as proof the product works
  • For TikTok product lists, skip the Facebook checks because TikTok has no equivalent ad library page.
  • List at least about 10 products to rank (put in order).

Saving Viral Videos

1 min

Viral means spreading very widely.

  • Save the link, a screenshot and the view count of every viral video you research as soon as you see it.
  • Save immediately because the creator can later set videos to private (hidden) or delete them.
  • A previously working link that now fails, combined with a changed account name or domain (website address), signals that the account has pivoted (changed its direction).

Case 1: Two Look-Alike Bottle-Opener Stores

1 min

Starting point: A bottle-opener product was promoted by two TikTok accounts with about 41,000 and 19,000 followers. Each was linked to a Shopify store (an online shop platform) on a different domain (website address), and the two stores looked almost identical.

Evidence that one operator ran both (an operator is the person running the shop):

  • Store checks found no Terms of Service (the shop's rules for buyers), no Privacy Policy (how customer data is handled), and no full Contact Us page.
  • Both stores used a free default Shopify theme (a ready-made design template) with default colors.
  • The product cost about 5 USD with shipping from AliExpress (a supplier website).
  • Both stores listed the same contact email.
  • Both accounts posted at the same times with the same clips, so they were treated as one operator.

Timeline and sales estimate:

  • The older account began posting in June. The newer one began on 14 July, and about two weeks later one of its videos reached 1.7 million views.
  • The operator posted roughly once a day with gaps.
  • Sales were estimated only as a range of about 1,000 to 10,000 USD, since exact revenue cannot be seen from outside.

Store issues:

  • A leftover footer code line (hidden code at the bottom of the page) was visible.
  • The header (top of the page) was changed, but the default theme colors stayed in other sections.
  • There was no Shop Now button (a button that invites buyers to shop).

Case 2: Drinks Product With Gaps in Posting

1 min

Starting point: A seller of a drinks-related product started posting on 26 April, first with two or three videos a day.

Posting pattern and results:

  • After 27 April there were no uploads until 11 May.
  • A video that reused a clip from an earlier post got only about 600 views.
  • The seller's best video reached 700,000 views, and a second one reached 500,000 views.
  • The account had only a few tens of thousands of followers at most, yet it kept posting about twice a day.
  • Comments asking where to buy the product suggested sales were still coming in.

Store issues:

  • The store had a visible HTML/CSS footer code line (HTML and CSS are the code that builds a web page).
  • It had several pop-ups (small windows that open over the page) and heavy use of GIFs (short looping animations).
  • It used dark black and silver colors and lowercase brand lettering.
  • There was no Shop Now button, so buyers had to add to cart (put an item in the shopping basket) and then open a separate cart page.
  • The profile image did not show the product, which made the account look like a bot (an automated fake account).

Conclusion: The seller was probably still earning some sales despite low view counts. A new seller could beat those results with better consistency and cleaner content.

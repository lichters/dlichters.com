Playbook › 11. TikTok and short video

Engagement, comments and driving traffic to the store

Section 11.11 · 11 of 22 in this chapter · 9 min read

In short

  • Under 1,000 followers (people who follow your account), sales from your TikTok profile stay small. Build trust and views first.
  • Giveaways bring followers, but mostly people who want free items. They rarely buy. Use them only when your audience matches your product.
  • Never put a link in bio (a link on your profile) or a direct link in captions or videos. Point buyers to the product description and comments instead.
  • Creator payment programmes pay little per view. Product-linked content pays far more. Don't rely on the Creator Fund.
This section has 6 parts. Tap one to open it.

Followers Versus Sales

1 min
  • Below 1,000 followers (people who follow your account), direct sales from your TikTok profile are small. New viewers rarely trust a small account.
  • Sales usually pick up after the account passes 1,000 followers. A single video with roughly 5,000 to 10,000 views can be enough to reach that number.
  • Free-product and giveaway-style ideas grow followers and views fast.
  • The people who respond mostly want free items and rarely buy.
  • Use them for early momentum (early speed), not as your main sales idea.
  • Giveaway posts grew the follower count quickly. They mostly attracted people who wanted free items, not buyers.
  • Sales-focused videos that followed stayed under 5,000 views and drew little traffic.
  • Keep your content aimed at people who want the product.
  • Use giveaways only when your audience matches the product.
  • Engagement (likes, comments, follows and watching to the end) drives distribution (how far TikTok spreads your video).
  • When viewers like, follow, watch to the end and comment, TikTok sends the video to a larger audience.
  • Ads aimed at viewers who engage and finish watching tend to scale (grow bigger) better.

Audience Matching and Steering

1 min
  • Engaging with similar creators increases your exposure to their audience. Like, comment and follow creators in your niche (your topic area).
  • Steer the audience away from kids after a viral video (one that spreads very fast) attracts the wrong viewers:
    1. Find another viral video for a similar product and identify its angle (the main idea that makes it work).
    2. Test new content angles that appeal to adults.
    3. Use hashtags (topic tags starting with #) that adults engage with.
    4. Once an adult-focused angle goes viral, replicate it and reduce the youth-focused content.
  • Redirecting an existing audience with coins (TikTok's virtual gifts) is risky. It can drive away current viewers.
  • Use coins only when comments show a clear buying segment, such as all male commenters.
  • Target that segment, or an age group expected to buy.

Comments and Engagement

1 min
  • Reply to as many comments as possible.
  • Long comment sections raise watch time (how long people watch), and TikTok then pushes the video further.
  • Replying to comments with new videos and asking questions in comments drives engagement.
  • To reply to a comment with a video, tap the comment under your viral video and choose Make video.
  • The original comment then appears in the top-left corner of the recording screen. Place your reply video there.
  • The TikTok video reply lets you answer a specific comment with a new video.
  • Using it on a top comment that shares a theme can pull in viewers who already engage with that topic.
  • When a viral video's comments build around its angle, a follow-up video that responds to them can extend reach while the trend is active.
  • Only do this if the product really works. A retraction-style follow-up (a video that takes back your claim) can backfire.
  • Controversial content that sparks argument raises engagement and watch time.
  • The video keeps playing while people read and reply.
  • Some creators deliberately point viewers to the comments to drive interaction.
  • Small deliberate imperfections in a video or caption, such as a visible slip, can draw comments and boost engagement.
  • Keep them mild, because extreme behaviour can backfire on the account.
  • Do not copy your own video's link and share it on your own account.
  • TikTok can detect this self-spam (promoting your own content).
  • Share actions should come from genuine viewer behaviour, not self-promotion.

Calls to Action and Links

2 min

Calls to action

  • A call to action (CTA, a request that tells viewers what to do) can boost engagement.
  • Ask viewers to comment, share, save or tag.
  • Examples: ask viewers to save the video for later, send it to a gym partner, or tag a friend who needs the product.

Link rules

  • Do not put a link in bio or any direct link call to action in captions or on videos.
  • TikTok is believed to penalise it.
  • If a product link must be shown, a friend or second account can comment that they just bought it and that it is in the description.
  • That comment can be pinned to the top of the comment section.
  • Excessive use of link in bio calls tends to end in a sharp drop-off after an initial viral spike.
  • Shadow bans (hidden limits that cut your reach without notice) after large follower counts have been reported.
  • Play it safe with links. Do not risk an account that has reached a large following.
  • Avoid tricks such as misspelling "link in bio" in organic (natural, not paid) comments to get around algorithm limits (TikTok's rules for who sees what).
  • The effect is uncertain, and a mistake can damage an account that took a long time to build.
  • For commenters asking where to buy, redirect them to the link.
  • Do not use the standard link in bio wording.
  • Keep the product visible in the video so viewers have a reason to click.
  • Comment template for redirecting commenters:
Hey guys, if anyone is asking, you can find it in the product description. I just bought one, and it's in the description if you want one too.

Comment placement for the store link

  • TikTok removed the option to pin (fix at the top) comments.
  • The store link cannot be pinned any more.
  • Instead, be the first to comment, or comment under the most-liked comment, pointing viewers to the store.
  • A creator comment placed under the most-liked comment is shown above the other comments.
  • This makes it a reliable place to point viewers to the store.

Product link flagging

  • TikTok can flag a product page link when its URL (web address) has too many characters.
  • A flagged link may be blocked or fail.
  • Shortening the product page URL by removing characters can make the link work again.
  • A link problem can also be fixed in the Shopify store settings (Shopify is the platform your store runs on) or at the domain provider, such as GoDaddy (the company where you rent your web address).
  • A product link that TikTok flags or that fails may work on mobile (phone) while failing on desktop (computer).
  • Test the product page link on both device types before concluding it is broken.
  • Long URLs with many characters are a common cause of flagging.
  • Shortening the product page URL can restore the link.

Giveaways

1 min

Giveaway format

  • A giveaway format can drive comments.
  • Promise a free product to a commenter whose comment gets no likes within 24 hours.
  • Viewers often like each other's comments to avoid being the winner. This keeps the comment section active.

Fulfilment rule

  • Running giveaways you do not actually fulfil (deliver) damages trust.
  • It can lead to reports and violations, even if the giveaway drives comments.
  • If you run a giveaway, deliver the prize as promised.
  • If a giveaway is promoted, the prize must be delivered exactly as promised.

Running a giveaway for an account

  1. Post a link with a set entry date, here the 10th, for participants.
  2. Participants click or react to the link to enter a random wheel (a random picker).
  3. The wheel selects a winner. The result is announced on the set date, here the 15th.
  4. The winner receives the login details and can change the username, password and branding.

Account as prize

  • A giveaway account offered as a prize had more than 10,000 followers in the personal care category.
  • The account was about two to three weeks old.
  • Its followers were reported as organic (gained naturally, not bought) and real. This makes the account a stronger prize than a bought one.

Case: giveaway growth without sales

  • A store was open for one week after a TikTok video went viral.
  • The owner posted three to four videos a day, spaced four to six hours apart.
  • Most of the content was giveaways, meant to grow the account from zero toward 1,000 followers.
  • The account reached about 35,000 followers.
  • One video reached 22 million views within about 36 hours.
  • The store received about 1,220 sessions (visits) in the week.
  • There were 57 add-to-cart events (clicks on "add to cart") and 47 checkouts (people starting payment).
  • There were zero sales.
  • The owner blamed low-relevance traffic from curious viewers, and the product itself.
  • Then the owner stopped posting and closed the store.

Monetisation Programmes

3 min

Creator Next

  • Creator Next is TikTok's creator programme (a set of rules and perks for creators).
  • Access it via the top-right icon on the profile tab.
  • Requirements: 1,000 followers, 1,000 views in the last 30 days, at least 3 posts in the last 30 days, and age 18 or older.
  • Confirm the view requirement in the app, because the source is unclear.
  • The sources give a different follower figure for the shop playlist (a list of products viewers can buy).
  • At 10,000 followers, apply for TikTok's creator programme (written as Creator Next in one source) to get access to a shop playlist.
  • The playlist lets viewers see a "shop now" option.
  • Use 1,000 followers as the stated Creator Next requirement.
  • Use 10,000 followers as the figure to apply for the playlist.
  • Check current eligibility (whether you qualify) in the app.

Creator Fund

  • The Creator Fund is TikTok's payment pool for creators based on views.
  • Eligibility conflicts across sources.
  • One source reports that the Creator Fund requires at least 1,000 followers.
  • An account with 10,000 followers was still rejected in one case, and support replies were slow.
  • Verify the eligibility criteria yourself. Do not expect automatic approval.
  • Other sources state 10,000 followers as the requirement.
  • Use 10,000 as the planning figure.
  • Payouts: the Creator Fund was estimated at about 30 dollars per million views.
  • So 40 million views would be worth about 1,200 dollars.
  • This is only an estimate from community experience. Do not use it as a fixed revenue forecast.
  • The range given is about 20 to 40 dollars per million views.
  • It only pays meaningfully for accounts with large view counts.
  • Product accounts (accounts that sell a product) can earn about 1,000 to 3,000 dollars per million views through product-linked content.
  • This is worth much more than the Creator Fund.
  • Sources disagree on whether product accounts should apply.
  • For product accounts whose goal is sales, avoid the Creator Fund.
  • It risks view suppression (TikTok showing your videos to fewer people), which can hurt sales.
  • For accounts with about 10,000 followers whose videos already perform well, apply for the Creator Fund.
  • Also turn on sponsorships (paid brand deals).
  • Keep uploading well-performing videos, even without direct sales.
  • Late-year payouts may slow down because the fund runs out.
  • This can reduce views for creators in the fund.
  • Treat this as a risk to plan around, not a reliable income source.
  • Some sellers avoid the Creator Fund because they believe it limits organic views.
  • This is an unverified belief.
  • One seller estimated that a viral account could have earned about 30,000 to 40,000 dollars from it, but never applied.

Creator Marketplace

  • Creator Marketplace offers brand sponsor deals. It is not usually used for product accounts.
  • Large accounts have been offered around 2,000 to 3,000 dollars per sponsored video.
  • It is worth checking as a later revenue option.

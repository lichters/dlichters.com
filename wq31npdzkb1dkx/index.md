## Quick answers

**What is dropshipping?** You run an online shop, and a supplier packs and ships each order straight to your customer. You pay for ads and the product only after someone buys. It is a marketing business: your ads, store and service decide the profit. https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/dropshipping.md · https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ecom/business-model.md

**How do I find a product?** Pick products by data, not taste: find something that already sells, check the margin, skip what too many people already run, and test fast and often. Plan one or two new product tests per week. Only test a product with 70% or more gross margin and at least $50 gross margin per order. A competitor ad running 7 days or more is a sign the seller makes money or at least breaks even. As a beginner, sell one single product, not a bundle. https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ecom/niche-and-products.md

**What do I compute before the first ad?** Your break-even: the cost per purchase (CPA) at which a sale stops making money, or the break-even ROAS (average order value divided by gross profit per order). In our store a 69.99 bed had a break-even CPA of 47.01, a ROAS of 1.49. Every offer tier keeps at least 67% gross margin (price minus product and shipping, before ads). Count Shopify fees, payment fees (about 2.9% plus $0.30), refunds and taxes as costs. https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ecom/numbers.md · https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ops/playbook-full.md

**How do I start Meta ads?** Your own Business Manager, ad account, page and pixel, connected to Shopify. Sales objective, Purchase as the conversion event, broad targeting, automatic placements. Start with one CBO campaign at about $50 a day; every ad set gets one concept with 3 to 5 creatives. Our playbook: warm up a brand-new ad account (one engagement ad at about 20 a day, stop at 8 to 10 spent), spend about 10 to 20 on day 1 and 20 to 30 on day 2, then up to 3 CBO campaigns at 50 a day, one concept each. https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ecom/meta-ads.md · https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ops/playbook-full.md

**When do I turn an ad off?** First 48 hours: touch nothing except technical problems. Decide only on purchases and CPA. Kill an ad set after 72 hours with no purchase or a CPA above break-even, and launch a new concept as a new ad set. Emergency brake before 72 hours: only at 0 purchases and twice the break-even CPA spent. Kill a product after 7 days of traffic with 0 purchases. The course pages say: leave a new campaign alone for at least 3 days; $50 spent with zero sales on day 1 is not enough data. https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ops/playbook-full.md · https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ecom/meta-ads.md · https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ecom/numbers.md

**How do I scale?** Course pages: raise a winning budget by 15 to 20% at a time, then wait about 3 days. Our ladder: raise only at ROAS 2.0 or better; from 50 a day the steps are 50, 75, 110, 150, 200, 275, 350, then +25% per raise. Never raise every day. Profit falls after a raise: one step back and hold 48 hours. Scale only what already works. https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ecom/meta-ads.md · https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ops/playbook-full.md · https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ecom/scaling.md

**How many creatives?** Our playbook: start with at least 12, aim for 21 (12 images, 9 videos); a new hook on an old image is not a new creative; refill with one new concept as a new ad set, never into a running ad set. The message sells, not the format: use the customer's own words and judge creatives by hook rate and website purchases. https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ops/playbook-full.md · https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ecom/creatives.md

**What should the store look like?** Clean, simple and trustworthy; edit it in mobile view (about 90% of traffic comes from phones); send ad traffic straight to the product page; start on the Basic plan; run one test checkout before you spend money on ads. https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ecom/shopify-store.md

**How do I price and bundle?** Aim for about 70% margin on each product (at least 67 to 69%). Quantity breaks (buy 1, 2, 3) are enough for most products; every bigger bundle needs a lower price per item. Only real deadlines and real scarcity. Our store: 69.99 / 119.00 / 167.98 for 1 / 2 / 3 beds, free US shipping, no strike-through price. https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ecom/offers-and-pricing.md · https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ops/playbook-full.md

**Which supplier?** A China agent or private supplier who ships each order with no minimum order quantity. Never promise 3 to 5 day delivery from China; set 12 to 21 or 10 to 14 business days at checkout. At least two suppliers for the main product, read every invoice, samples from every production run. https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ecom/suppliers.md

**Customer service and chargebacks?** Ship fast, answer fast and refund before a customer goes to the bank, because a chargeback rate above 1% can freeze 10% of your payouts. https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ecom/customer-service.md

**Money, taxes, payments?** Keep business money separate, use Shopify Payments, publish honest policies you can keep, hold cash for slow payouts, and ask a local accountant about taxes early. Profit is not cash: a payout can reach your bank days after the order. https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ecom/legal-money.md · https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/ecom/numbers.md

**Which business model should I start with?** The table of the models we tested, with results, is in the page below ("What our own tests showed"). Every test that failed failed mainly from too little experience; each model can still work today. https://dlichters.com/wq31npdzkb1dkx/business-start.md

# Start your first business

Pick one skill, turn it into one small offer that others already sell, find the first 10 customers by hand, and decide how long each test runs before you start.

## How to start

1. **Pick one skill you can practise every day.** Video editing, drawing, writing, coding, fitness, fixing things.
2. **Turn it into one small offer.** Thumbnails for small YouTubers, a simple app.
3. **Check that someone already sells it.** If others make money with it, there is demand. If nobody sells it, that is usually a warning, not a gap.
4. **Find your first 10 customers by hand.** Ask what they would pay for. Asking feels scary, but people notice you much less than you think, and one week of practice makes the fear of a "no" smaller. Write your first sentence down and count asks, not yeses.
5. **Decide your test length before you start.** For content or a shop, think in months. Write the date down and do not switch before it.
6. **Measure one number every day.** Videos posted, people asked, sales, euros. If it never moves, change one thing, not the whole business.

## Mistakes

- **Watching instead of doing.** Business videos feel like work. They are not.
- **Fake shortcuts.** Fake personas, fake reviews, lying about your results, spam, betting. They hurt people, get accounts banned, and teach you nothing.
- **Jumping to the next idea after a few days.** Several of our early tests ended after days, not months (print on demand, vending machine).
